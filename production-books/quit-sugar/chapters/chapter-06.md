Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

IN THIS CHAPTER
A wet morning market. Warm bread torn with fingers. A plain favourite meal eaten hungry. An evening table with laughter. A clean plate pushed back.

*Hunger met with real food is joy itself — BAD SUGAR never made it sweeter.*

Welcome Back, Fuel-Killed

Welcome back, investigator. You walk in different from the reader who opened this book.

You came in counting good days and blaming yourself for bad ones. You left the last room forgiven. You know now your Mondays did not fail because you are weak. They failed because the method was false. You know the afternoon buzz was debt dressed as wages. The lift never lent. It took, and called the taking lift.

I honour that seeing, because it clears the chair beside you for what we do now. We do not fight here. We do not argue harm. We eat.

You tell me, quietly, in the voice you use when no one lectures you, that you fear what comes next. If the sweet lift was a fraud, what is left on the plate? Will eating turn grey? Will joy thin to chewing fuel? Will love leave the table when bright parcels leave?

I felt that fear myself. I believed dessert was love and hunger was trouble. I believed my appetite needed sweet handling or it would howl all day. I believed a meal without a bright ending was labour, and a day without grazing was empty.

The fact is the opposite. Hunger is a friend. Satisfaction is a signal you can trust with your eyes closed. Real food eaten hungry is favourite enough to make you smile without thinking.

Sit with me and eat one ordinary meal the way we were built to eat, and you will feel it in your own mouth.

The Grey Fear

You say, “But I love food because of sweet things. Take those away and what is left to love?”

I print your words warmly, because we all spoke them. “A treat when you feel you deserve one.” “Celebrating with chocolate.” “Without something sweet, dinner is dull.” “Sugary somethings to get me through the evening as well as the afternoon.”

Look at what those sentences confess. They confess that love now lives at the very end of the meal, in a small bright parcel, while the meal itself has become labour to get through. The soup is eaten quickly. The bread is counted. The vegetables are endured. All attention waits for the parcel. Is that love of eating? Or is it love starved and narrowed to one bite?

We learned to live like that, all of us caught in the sweet loop. We grazed all day on doses that shouted louder than hunger, then sat to dinner already coated and foggy, then woke hunger with a sweet hit and called the waking appetite. We forgot what clear hunger feels like. We forgot what stopping at satisfied feels like. No wonder we fear the table will grey. We have not met the table clearly for years.

You say the roller coaster is the only colour. Without the high-low-high-low, life will be flat. Food will be beige. Evenings will stretch long with nothing to look forward to.

I ask you, friend to friend, with your own evenings open before you:

When you ate truly hungry after a long walk, did plain bread ever taste dull?

When you laughed through a whole meal with people you love, was the brightest minute the wrapper minute?

When you stopped at satisfied and pushed the plate away with a sigh, did you need a rule to tell you to stop?

There is only one honest answer, and your body already knows it. Hunger makes simple things marvellous. Company makes them glow. Satisfaction closes them cleanly. The dose only shouted over that music and then claimed it wrote the song.

You fear you will lose pleasure. The fact is you will lose shouting and find music. You will lose coating and find taste. You will lose fog and find flavour. That is not loss. That is return.

Hunger Is Not The Enemy

We were taught to fear hunger. A hollow opens under the ribs and attention runs straight to panic. Eat something RIGHT NOW. Silence it. Cover it. Never let it grow.

I tell you as one escaped: hunger is not a fire to smother. It is an invitation.

Real hunger arrives slowly, like light rising. It does not twitch. It does not shake. It does not point one finger at one bright packet. It opens. It says, food will be good now. It sharpens smell. It makes the mouth water at bread. It makes the stomach ready and calm. It makes time slow in a kind way, so the coming meal matters.

You remember this from childhood, even if you buried it. The small child who stops mid-bite to run off and play — that is hunger and satisfaction before rules touched them.

That child did not count. He did not earn. He did not punish. He ate because hollow called, he glowed while eating, he stopped while juice still shone on his chin because play called louder. No guilt followed him to the garden. No fog followed him to bed.

We were that child. The signal still lives in you, undamaged, under years of noise. Doses can shout over it. They cannot cut it.

I lived years without hearing it. I ate by clock and by mood and by wrapper, full but never settled, empty but never clear-hungry. The first morning I felt true hunger again, low and slow and kind, I almost wept at the shop, because I recognised an old friend walking toward me down the aisle.

You will recognise him too. Not through effort. Through hearing.

Learn the difference in your own body, because your body teaches faster than any lecture.

Clear hunger grows slowly over hours. It welcomes many foods. Bread appeals. Soup appeals. Apples appeal. It waits without panic while you cook. It sharpens as you chop and stir.

Sweet urgency arrives fast after fullness. It welcomes one thing only. It does not want soup. It does not want bread. It wants bright, soft, quick sweetness, and it wants it now. It twitches in the hands. It narrows attention to cupboard and drawer. It fades for minutes after a hit and returns louder within the hour.

Bodies do not speak in brand names. Loops do.

Ask yourself which voice you have been obeying at ten at night. Was it the slow opening that says food will be good? Or the quick narrowing that says sweet only, now, fast? Which voice leaves you settled after? Which voice leaves you coated and restless and reaching again?

There is only one honest answer. The slow voice feeds. The quick voice bills.

Hunger never punished you. The loop taught you to fear hunger so you would feed the loop. Take the loop away and hunger stands revealed as what it always was: appetite awake, ready to enjoy.

The Market Morning

Come with me on an ordinary morning, no ceremony, no new rules for living.

Air cold on the face. Stalls wet from rain. Greens piled high, dark and shining. Carrots with earth still on them. Apples blushing in crates. Tomatoes smelling of vine even in cool air. A baker’s table at the corner breathing warm. Mushrooms with forest smell. Leeks thick as arms. Peppers glossy. Cheese with rind the colour of straw. Fish on ice with bright eyes. Hands red from cold, breath showing, canvas bags bumping knees.

You have not eaten since night. Hollow has opened, honest and calm. You are hungry.

Tear the warm bread. The crust cracks. Steam rises. Smell fills the nose before taste touches tongue. You bite. Crumb soft, crust singing, chew deep and nutty. You close your eyes without deciding to. No icing needed. No drizzle needed. Hunger seasoned it perfectly.

Pick an apple. Cold, firm, snap loud. Juice runs. Sweetness bright and clear, wrapped in crunch and water and skin, arriving and then leaving cleanly. You eat it standing, coat collar up, feet cold on pavement, heart warm. No second apple shouts. One is glory. Hunger met, pleasure full.

Walk the stalls slowly. Let the nose lead. Smell the dill. Smell the basil even in winter glasshouse. Smell the oranges piled like suns. Smell the fresh coriander, sharp and green. Notice how smell wakes when hollow is open. Notice how the mouth waters without panic. That watering is joy beginning, not trouble beginning.

Buy what you fancy for supper. Not what a chart orders. What your nose wants when hungry. Leeks. Peppers. Mushrooms. A hunk of cheese. Fresh bread again. Fruit for after. If you live with any condition where changing what you eat carries risk, talk to your clinician and follow their word first.

Carry the bag home swinging. Notice you are already happy, and no dose has touched your mouth.

That happiness is not virtue. It is appetite awake in the world.

Tell me honestly, with the bag bumping your leg: did you miss bright parcels among those colours? Did green need coating? Did red need drizzle? Did warm crust need a wrapper to be marvellous?

You know. Colour was there before wrapper. Smell was there before wrapper. Hunger made them loud. The market did not grey without doses. It glowed because hunger met it clearly.

We trapped eaters told ourselves markets were for other people, virtuous people, thin people, people who do not understand pleasure. The fact is markets are for hungry people. You are a hungry person. The market is yours.

The Favourite Meal Eaten Hungry

Evening now. Light low. Kitchen warm. No performance.

Cook simply. Slice vegetables thick so they keep bite. Salt honest. Oil shining. Bread on the board. Fruit washed in a bowl. Nothing weighed. Nothing counted. Nothing feared. Steam on the windows. Knife thocking on wood. Pan hissing. Hands busy, mind easy.

Sit when hungry. Truly hungry, not twitchy-hungry, not clock-hungry, not sad-hungry. The slow hollow that says now.

Eat. First bite noticed. Chew. Taste. The pepper sweet where heat meets flesh. The leek buttery where soft meets salt. The potato earthy where skin crisps. The bread tearing, again that crack. The mouth alive, the nose open, the belly warming bite by bite.

Talk. Laugh. Listen. Let company season as company always did. Notice how flavour rises when attention is easy, not when sugar shouts. Notice how a story makes the next bite better. Notice how laughter makes chewing lighter. That is not imagination. That is appetite joined to life, the way it was built to join.

Halfway through, pause. The edge has left hunger. Glow has arrived. You are no longer ravenous, not yet full. Food tastes brightest here, in the middle, where hunger still lights but satisfaction begins to gather. The bread tastes most itself here. The vegetables sing here. Time slows here.

Eat on a little. Then stop when satisfied. You will know. The next forkful does not call with the same music. The belly says enough, quietly, without thunder. The mouth is content. The mind drifts to talk, to evening, to rest. The plate can wait.

Push back. Sigh. Smile without deciding to. The evening is yours now, not the cupboard’s. No coating calls for washing down. No fog gathers behind eyes. No second reach stirs the hands. You get on with enjoying your life, full and clear, free to talk, to walk, to rest, to sleep deep.

Tell me honestly: what in that hour needed BAD SUGAR?

Was the bread dull without icing? Was the apple poor without syrup? Was laughter thin without chocolate at the end? Was the pause in the middle less bright because no parcel waited after?

You know. We all know when we dare to replay without flinching. The scene gave everything. Hunger gave the seasoning. Satisfaction gave the close. Love lived in the hands that cooked and the faces that laughed, not in a parcel handed after.

Your body knows how to do this without teaching. It makes steady fuel from ordinary meals and carries you between them without drama. Plain food eaten hungry holds you for hours. You eat, you stop, you forget food and live. You do not need a sweet wire to stay alight. Eat when hungry and the light steadies by itself.

That steadiness is marvellous. That ease is wonderful. That clear evening is exciting in the quiet way real excitement comes — not buzz, but glow.

Fruit Is Not The Question

You say, cleverly, as we all did when cornered, “Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. If an apple is sweet, why blame a bar?”

I honour the sharpness. It sounds like logic. It is the loop changing clothes.

Taste them side by side in memory and the body answers before argument.

An apple eaten hungry: crunch, juice, bright sweetness arriving inside water and fibre and chew, satisfaction rising cleanly, one enough, two a feast, ten unthinkable. The mouth freshens after. The belly settles. Attention moves on to living.

A bar eaten full: rush, coating, want sharpening with each bite, satisfaction never arriving, one never one, box empty before regret ends. The mouth coats after. The belly churns. Attention narrows to more.

Same word, sweet, two different experiences. One belongs to hunger met. The other belongs to want re-fired. Your mouth knows the difference even when words blur it.

No one sits at midnight haunted by apples. No one hides apple skins in shame. No one says, “I feel like i cant stop,” with an orchard. The loss of control lives where the trap lives, not where hunger lives.

So enjoy sweetness where hunger finds it — in fruit, in vegetables grown ripe, in a plain meal that turns sweet on the tongue when you are hungry enough to taste it. A carrot roasted till edges caramelise. Peppers softened till they glow. Onions melted till they sweeten. Peas bursting. Corn milky. That enjoyment is not a slip. It is proof the instrument works. Real food enjoyed as favourite leaves no bill after.

I speak plainly here because I will never moralise your mouth. I do not divide the world into pure and sinful bites. I do not hand you a list of allowed and forbidden joys. Sweetness in fruit and in ordinary meals belongs to joy. Eat it rejoicing when hungry, stop at satisfied, and let pleasure do what pleasure does — pass cleanly and leave gratitude.

The only sweetness that ever billed you was BAD SUGAR eaten as hits between hunger and beyond satisfaction. Doses taken full that shouted for more before swallowing ended. Fixes that re-fired want right where satisfaction should have settled. Leave those hits and eating returns to itself. Nothing is forbidden to the satisfied eater. Everything is savoured by the hungry one.

Ask yourself with an apple in one hand and memory of a box in the other:

Which sweet lets you stop with a smile? Which sweet stops you from stopping?

Which sweet leaves the mouth fresh? Which sweet leaves the mouth coated and calling?

Which sweet do you remember with warmth? Which sweet do you remember with fog?

There is only one honest answer. Your body gave it years ago. Trust it now.

Satisfaction Knows When

We lost trust in stopping. The box taught us stopping is impossible. The evening taught us one becomes all. So we armed ourselves with rules, counts, forbidden days, good weeks. “I’ve managed to be good this week.” Good. Bad. Naughty. Clean. A whole courtroom carried to the table.

I tell you plainly: the courtroom was built by the loop, not by you.

Watch satisfaction in a free meal and you will see it needs no police.

It speaks quietly. Interest softens. Chewing slows by itself. The next bite looks pleasant but not urgent. The belly feels easy. The mind drifts to talk, to evening, to rest. That drift is the signal. Follow it and you stop, not through virtue, through completion.

We all knew this as children and we all relearn it in one meal when hunger is clear. No weighing teaches it faster. No counting guards it better. The body closes the meal the way night closes day, without vote.

BAD SUGAR broke that close by shouting louder than the quiet voice. Each hit re-fired want right where satisfaction should have settled, so full never read as full. Remove the shouting and the whisper is heard again at once.

Try it in memory on your market supper. No one told you when to put the fork down. You put it down because music eased. That ease is authority. Trust it and it strengthens each time you obey it. Hunger calls clearly. Satisfaction answers clearly. You stand between them, eating what you want, free from punishment on either side.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as promise, not as rule. It does not tell you what to want. It tells you wanting can be trusted once shouting stops. It does not tell you when hunger should come. It tells you hunger will come clearly and leave clearly. It does not threaten you if you mishear once. It tells you mishearing teaches hearing, without courtroom, without shame.

You say, “But I cannot trust myself. I have proven I cannot stop.”

I answer, you have proven you cannot stop shouting. No one can stop shouting by listening harder to shouting. Stop the shouting and stopping happens by itself. The box proved nothing about you. It proved everything about doses taken full. A free meal proves everything about you. Try the free meal hungry and watch who stops easily. You do.

That is not a diet. I prescribe no menu, no good against bad, no pure against sinful. I hand you back ears to hear with. Eat hungry. Stop satisfied. Enjoy real food. The rest is living.

Love Was Never In The Wrapper

You say, last and softest, “But dessert is love. My mother gave it. I give it. Without it, how do I show tenderness?”

I honour the tenderness, friend. I felt it too. The birthday candles. The cinema hand finding yours in the dark with chocolate between. The tired child offered something sweet after a hard day. To question that feels like questioning love itself.

Look closer, with love still in the room, and see where love actually lived.

Mother’s hand on your hair while you ate — was the love in the hand or in the parcel? The laughter round the birthday table while candles dripped — did the glow come from wax and icing, or from faces gathered for you? The evening warmth, shoulder to shoulder, story unfolding, funny scene breaking tension — did sweetness make the laugh, or did the laugh make sweetness seem bright? The tired child held and heard — did comfort come from sugar, or from being held and heard while sugar happened to be there?

Isolate the variable and credit truly lands. Keep the hand, keep the faces, keep the shoulder and the laugh, keep being held, lift the dose clean away. Does love thin? Or does it stand clearer, undistracted, uncoated, unhurried by want for more?

You know. We all know when we dare to replay without flinching. The meal gave. Company gave. Hunger met gave. The dose sneaked a ride on giving and then billed you after in fog and craving.

Real love at the table feeds hunger and honours satisfaction. It offers food when hollow calls. It lets stopping be easy. It never says love must shout louder than the body or punish the eater after. Offer that love to yourself and to others and eating becomes marvellous again, wonderful in its plainness, exciting in its freshness, brilliant in its simplicity.

Picture the birthday without fog. Cake? No, picture the table. Faces leaning in. Candles trembling. Breath held for the wish. Laughter breaking. Hunger met by food you love, satisfaction closing cleanly, evening long and clear, child asleep without restless tossing, morning bright without coated tongue. Where in that picture is loss? What colour left because bright parcels left? None. Colour deepened because fog left.

Picture the sofa without box. Blanket. Story. Shoulder warm against shoulder. Tea steaming. Body satisfied from supper, mind easy, hands still, laughter unhurried. No wrapper rustle to punctuate talk. No regret rising under sweetness. No second reach while credits roll. Is that grey? Or is that tenderness undistracted?

You call dessert love because love was present when dessert was present, and the dose claimed the credit. Take the credit back and give it where it belongs: to hands, faces, laughter, hunger met. The dose gave nothing. The moment gave everything.

Hunger makes food marvellous.

Read that again, because your freedom sups here. Not icing. Not drizzle. Not parcel at the end. Hunger. Plain, slow, honest hunger, met with real food you fancy, stopped at satisfied, never punished. That is favourite. That was always favourite before noise taught you otherwise.

So come to the table hungry, eater. Come rejoicing, not fearing. The grey you feared was fog from old hits, lifting now with each clear meal. Colour returns bite by bite — green biting, red juicing, brown crust cracking, faces glowing. You will not miss joy. You will pity the evenings you mistook shouting for singing.

You came to this room fearing food joy was sugar. You leave having inhabited ordinary hunger satisfied by a real meal as favourite. The body leads. The meal satisfies. Love stays where love always lived.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

SUMMARY
- Hunger is a kind invitation to enjoy food, not trouble to silence with a sweet hit.
- Plain food eaten truly hungry tastes favourite without any bright parcel at the end.
- Satisfaction arrives quietly in the middle of a meal and can be trusted to close it.
- Sweetness inside fruit and ordinary meals belongs to joy and leaves no shouting after.
- Love at the table lived in hands, faces, laughter and hunger met, not in the wrapper.
- Eating without punishment returns colour, ease and steady evenings.
- The body makes steady fuel from ordinary meals and carries you clearly between them.
