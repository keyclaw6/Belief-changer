Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A kitchen table with a photographed list — A closed book beside an empty plate — A plain meal waiting ahead, eaten hungry — Morning light on an ordinary street

*You have nothing left to study and nothing left to mourn, only a free life to live and sweetness to enjoy again.*

### There Is Nothing More To Learn

You came to this book believing you would need rules to carry around forever.

"How will I cope at birthdays?" "What will I do at the cinema?" "Will I need to keep reading and re-reading to hold myself clear?" I hear that whisper because we all heard it. We believed freedom would be heavy luggage we must drag behind us, page after page, year after year.

The fact is, you carry nothing now.

We carried the weight before. We carried the hunt into every morning, the bright paper into every bag, the fog into every afternoon, the rustle into every evening. We called that normal and told ourselves a free eater must live by vigilance. Now look with your own eyes as witness.

When you woke clear this morning, did you need a lesson to stay clear till lunch? When you walked the shop with clear eyes, did you need a lecture to walk past paper? When you ate supper hungry and left it at satisfied, did you need a rulebook to close the meal?

Only one honest answer fits. Living free needs no teaching. Living trapped needed constant teaching — scores, wages, promises, tomorrows, allowances, apologies.

So close the school. You are not a pupil on probation. You are an eater returned to nature. Hunger guides. Satisfaction closes. Real food pleases. That is not a system to memorize. That is the body you were born with, speaking clearly at last.

I know how strange that sounds after years of diet weeks and Monday restarts. We were trained to believe that without a plan we would fall. We kept lists. We kept scores. We told ourselves we had been good all week and therefore something sweet was owed, then told ourselves we had been bad and therefore something sweet was forfeited. Good or bad, the paper won. The scoreboard itself was part of the fraud.

There is no scoreboard now. There is hunger, honest and open, welcoming many foods. There is satisfaction, quiet and warm, closing the meal. There is taste, bright again once fog lifts. You do not manage those signals. You meet them. A free eater does not perform eating correctly. A free eater eats and lives.

Do you worry you will forget? You will not forget seeing. You do not forget that night is dark and morning is light. You do not need to rehearse it. You saw that BAD SUGAR never fed hunger, only borrowed brightness and repaid flatness. You saw that the lift made the low. You saw that one bright bite calls the next, not because you are weak, but because the con is built that way. Seeing stays.

Do you worry you will need to keep this book open on the kitchen table forever? Use it as you would use a door you have walked through. Photograph the small list at the end and put the book away. Let the pages shrink behind you with every clear hour. I closed my own last page years ago and expected a great emptiness to follow. What followed was washing, shopping, cooking, sleeping, waking. Ordinary days, lived bright. Yours will shrink the same way. That is not carelessness. That is cure.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar, are pregnant, or carry medical risk around food, keep your clinician in charge and follow their advice while you enjoy this freedom.

There is no second course. There is no advanced level. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have followed them. Now live.

Ask once more, with your own life as witness.

Did rules ever keep you clear for long, or did rules keep the hunt alive by making paper precious? Did freedom ever come from watching yourself harder, or did freedom come the moment you saw clearly and stopped watching?

Only one honest answer fits. Watching kept the prisoner watching the bars. Seeing opened the door.

Leave the classroom gladly. Breakfast waits. Hunger will call. Satisfaction will answer. No bell needs to ring in between.

### Get On With Enjoying Your Life

This is my order to you, given with joy, not with warning.

Get on with enjoying your life.

Take the holiday. Cook the supper. Meet friends. Laugh at the film. Walk the morning street with light feet. Talk of work, of love, of weather, of anything real. Let BAD SUGAR become background noise while foreground life grows loud and bright.

We once believed joy lived in bright paper and life without it would turn grey. The fact is, paper borrowed brightness and repaid greyness. Mornings fogged. Afternoons dipped. Evenings rustled. The debt ran underneath every laugh. Now no debt runs. Taste sharpens. Hunger sharpens. Joy sharpens. Marvellous how little ceremony freedom needs — food, water, rest, warmth, people — and how fully life thanks you when fog leaves.

Do not stand at the open door admiring the daylight. Walk through it. Do not count clear days. Inhabit clear days. Do not test freedom by staring at hunters. Pity them quietly and walk past easy, happy to be free among shelves that once hunted you.

Go everywhere. Meet everyone. Change nothing else in your life to stay free. Same kettle. Same bus. Same desk. Same sofa with lamplight soft on it. Freedom lives inside the old routine with one fraud removed. That is why it is easy. That is why it holds.

You will sit at birthday tables groaning with gloss. Sit easy. The joy in the room was never in the gloss. The joy was people, music, laughter, candles, belonging. Watch the children loud on frosting then weeping an hour later, the adults bright for ten minutes then foggy and short. Does that look like a gift being received? You know the toll because we all paid it. Take fruit gladly. Laugh fully. Leave the gloss untouched without a speech. No warning. No contempt. Warm to the person always.

You will sit in cinemas while chocolate passes hand to hand in the dark. Sit back and let the story carry you, mouth clean, belly calm, mind present. The film gives the laughter and the closeness. Paper only rode along and charged for the ride. You can love films more now, not less, because your mouth is not busy chasing fade.

You will hear the office talk of treats earned and weeks been good. Smile warmly and talk of work, of weekend, of anything real. Goodness needs no edible wage. Hunger needs food. Tiredness needs rest. Leave their paper to their evening. Your ease says more than any warning.

And do not preach to any of them. The fastest way to reopen your own doubt is to argue someone else out of theirs. They will defend what you attack, and their defence will echo in your ears on a tired evening. Let visible ease recruit. Eat gladly. Laugh fully. Leave sweets untouched without comment, or take fruit gladly when offered, with a clear "Lovely, I will have the peach." If someone presses — "Go on, one won't hurt, don't be boring" — answer light and closed. "I used to live on those and I feel brilliant free of them. I am happy as I am." Smile. Change the subject. You owe no debate. Freedom needs no defence.

What will you do with the hours the hunt once ate? Live them. We spent years thinking about sweets, hunting sweets, regretting sweets, planning to quit sweets tomorrow. That thinking filled evenings, filled queues, filled quiet moments in traffic. Now the room is empty and bright. Fill it with living — supper, bath, bed, talk, walk, work, laughter — never with dosing. Call the friend. Cook the soup that takes an hour. Read the book. Sleep soft. Wake clear. The day did not need rebuilding. Only the fraud left it.

When a sweet thought crosses — wrapper bright, smell warm, old memory glowing — you know what to do. You will not fight it. You will not mourn it. You will not debate it. You will answer with the joyful shout that has become your natural reply, and feel relief flood warm:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Each firing makes the next firing faster, till thoughts pass like weather and leave the sky clear. No vigilance. No armour. Life lived.

Do you feel a faint flatness around ten? Smile. It is weather passing. Drink water. Have your lunch when hunger calls. Do not turn a passing cloud into a verdict on freedom. A free eater does not feel bright every hour. A free eater feels clear underneath every hour, bright or dull, because no debt is running.

Ask, with your own mouth as witness.

When you ate that bright bar at ten in the old days, did you feel true morning energy return, or did you feel a loud ten minutes followed by a longer thin hour and a second reach? When you eat now and stop at satisfied, do you feel scolded and heavy, or light and ready to get on with enjoying your life?

Only one honest answer fits. The old ten o'clock dose borrowed. The plain morning restores.

That is guarding without vigilance. Thoughts cannot move your hand. Only belief moves the hand. While belief stays dead, thoughts pass like clouds and leave the sky clear. You do not suppress thoughts. You reframe them with joy. You do not avoid life. You walk through it free.

Rejoice. Celebrate. Feel relief rise whenever paper crosses your mind. Pity the hunt inside the sharing. Feel marvellous lightness in ordinary aisles. Feel wonderful calm at ordinary tables. Feel exciting newness in ordinary mornings that ask nothing of you but hunger met and left at satisfied. This is happy to be free, not merely free. There is a world of difference, and you live on the bright side of it now.

### The Sweetness That Was Handed Back

Hear the last truth, kept for this hour, because only a free eater can hear it rightly.

You did not lose sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

We were conned into believing BAD SUGAR was sweetness itself — love, reward, comfort, celebration. Doses coated the tongue, borrowed hunger, shouted over meals, then left the mouth flat and calling for more. The eater you were before paper taught its shout knew sweetness without shouting: hunger met, juice bursting, pleasure quieting into satisfied, then running off to play. That eater never left you. That eater was buried under tolls.

Think of the earliest sweet you remember, before the hunt began. Fruit eaten outdoors with juice on your chin. Bread warm from the oven. Milk cold. Berries sharp and bright. No fog after. No second reach hammering underneath fullness. No rustle calling from the cupboard. Hunger opened wide. Satisfaction closed softly. That was sweetness by nature, and nature never withdrew it. Paper covered it.

The trap took real sweetness, sold it back in bright paper at a cruel price, and called the theft a treat. It told you love comes wrapped. It told you celebration comes frosted. It told you comfort comes in a bar. It told you energy comes in a can. Every telling narrowed life toward one paper while the wide world of taste waited outside, patient. Now the debt is cancelled. Hunger is sweet again. Fruit is sweet again. Supper is sweet again. Morning air is sweet again. No paper needed to sweeten what is sweet by nature.

This was never about quitting a pleasure. This was about growing back into the eater you were before the con found you.

You grew out of the cage; you did not white-knuckle your way out of it. A child grows and the small coat no longer fits, and no one mourns the coat. You have grown and the bright paper no longer fits, and there is nothing to mourn. Growth does not ache with loss. Growth feels light. Growth feels right. You are larger now than the hunt that once held you, and you cannot fit back inside it without bending yourself small again. Why would you?

Look ahead with your own heart as witness and choose.

Which life do you want for the rest of your life? The toll paid daily, the lift-crash-repeat, the box emptied before the film ends, the morning fog, the evening hunt, the bright paper calling forever, flat and thirsty underneath every hour?

Or the clear life for the rest of your life? Mornings met hungry. Meals tasted fully. Evenings soft without rustle. Shops simple. Mind quiet. Joy unborrowed. Belly calm and warm. Mouth clean. Head clear.

Only one honest answer fits. And you have already chosen it.

So eat. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Eat gladly. Laugh fully. Leave bright paper untouched without comment, or take fruit gladly when offered. Sleep soft. Wake clear. Let mornings prove it. Let hunger prove it. Let shops prove it. Let evenings prove it. Each ordinary day returns what was taken — taste bright, belly calm, mind quiet.

You are free. You will stay free while belief stays dead, and belief stays dead because you see. No loss was suffered, because nothing real was offered. There was no pleasure to bury and no friend to miss. There was a fraud to leave, and you have left it. Seeing that, there is nothing to miss and everything to live, bright and plain and yours.

Put the book down. Leave it closed. Go to the meal ahead hungry and meet it with joy. The road stays bright because you keep your eyes on where you are going, not on the rumble behind you. The book shrinks. Life grows. Sweetness, stolen for years, sits back on your own table where it belonged all along.

Go now. Get on with enjoying your life.
