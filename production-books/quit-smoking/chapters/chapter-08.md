Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

*Morning, food and a pause were yours before smoke ever touched them, and they are still yours without it.*

### FEET BEFORE THE PACK

Keep smoking as you read. I ask nothing of your hands yet. I ask only that you watch one morning honestly, as if you were standing beside yourself.

The alarm goes. Your feet have not touched the floor before your hand finds the pack. Before water, before air, before a clear thought. On an empty mouth and empty lungs, the first drag feels enormous. Huge. We say, there, I needed that. I cannot start without it.

I lived that start for years, so I speak without a speck of blame for you. Let us look at what really started.

All night you fed nothing. For six, seven, eight hours the little hunger slid down with no coin going in. You wake in deficit — stale mouth, thick head, faint gnaw behind the ribs. A non-smoker wakes at zero. He stretches, coughs once clear, puts the kettle on. His body asks for water and movement and air. Yours asks for a dose before the day has asked anything of you.

Tell me plainly, from your own bedroom, not from what you were told.

Did that first dose lift you above him? Or did it drag you from your low back toward where he woke?

Have you ever seen a non-smoker scramble for anything before his feet touch the floor?

If the dose gave morning energy, why did it never carry past the kettle?

You know because you have lived it ten thousand times. The dose did not add morning. It ended the night-without that smoking itself created, then took credit for morning. The light through the blind, the cold floor waking the feet, the kettle beginning to sing, the first clear breath of the house — those were there all along. The dose only quietened its own complaint long enough for you to notice them for a minute, then claimed it had made them.

Stay with that cold floor a moment longer, because this is where freedom begins to show itself as lived fact.

Feet to lino. Cold striking up through the soles. Legs heavy, eyes gummed, mouth stale. You shuffle to the kitchen. You flick the kettle. You hear that low murmur rising to a sing. You crack the window a finger's width and the outside air comes in, thin and clean, with the smell of wet pavement or cut grass or traffic warming up. You draw it in. Your chest lifts without permission. Your shoulders drop. Your body does all of that without a lesson.

We never noticed that lift because the hand was already moving. We lit before the kettle boiled, before water touched the tongue, before the lungs had taken one clean breath of the day. Then we gave the dose the credit for the waking that the cold and the air and the water had already begun.

Try it tomorrow with clear eyes while you still smoke. Feet to floor. Kettle on. Window open a crack. Breathe before you light. Taste water before you light. Hold the warm mug in both hands and feel the heat come back into the fingers. Notice what your body already does without teaching. It breathes. It swallows. It wakes. It clears the head with movement and air. It does not need a paper tube to prove the day has begun.

Follow the next five minutes as they really happen. Splash cold water on the face and feel the skin tighten and tingle awake. Brush the teeth and taste how the stale night film lifts and the mouth turns clean and sharp. Stand tall and roll the neck and feel the spine click back into the day. Pull the curtain wide and let daylight hit the eyes until they water and clear. Every one of those small wakenings arrives through body and senses, not through paper. The shower runs hot on the shoulders. The towel rasps. The first sip of water or tea goes down clean and the throat opens. That opening was muscle and water and breath doing their old work.

I am not asking you to change the morning. I am asking you to see who owns it. The morning never needed smoke to be morning. Smoke needed morning to look needed.

Think of all the mornings you called flat without picturing them clean. You woke dosed for years, so memory says dose belongs there. Presence is not making. The alarm belonged there. The light belonged there. The kettle belonged there. The dose was present the way a loud neighbour is present — in the room, taking attention, adding nothing.

Once that is seen, the fear of flat mornings without it begins to thin. Not by force. By seeing there was never a gift, only a debt briefly repaid.

The fact is the first one of the day feels huge because the low is deepest then. Hours without a feed leave the greatest emptiness, so the brief return toward normal feels like a lift. It is not a lift. It is arrears paid. A non-smoker never pays it and never misses it. He starts where you pay to get back to.

So stand beside yourself tomorrow and watch the order. Cold, light, air, water, movement — all working before paper is lit. Who woke you? Life woke you. The dose only interrupted the waking to claim it as its own work.

### THE PLATE IS ALREADY FINISHED

“But a meal is not finished without one. It seals it.”

I hear you. I said those words myself for years, hand moving by itself when the plate went empty. Let us test that sealing against your own tongue.

Eat tonight as normal and pay full attention to the food itself. The heat rising off it. The salt. The oil. The full belly. The pause when the fork goes down and you lean back. Where in that did smoke improve taste? Strip the dose out in memory and run the film again. The warmth came from the cooking. The fullness came from the eating. The pause came from stopping. The dose dried the mouth it claimed to satisfy and laid ash over the taste it claimed to seal.

The fact is a dose seals nothing but the hour since the last feed. Lunch runs long, the meter slides, the hand moves. Tasty feast or dull sandwich, it moves just the same. Hungry or full, it moves just the same. If it sealed flavour, a dull meal would need no seal. But the hand moves for dull and fine alike. It is not the plate calling. It is the clock.

Ask yourself two honest questions and answer from your own table.

If it sealed taste, why does food taste clearer twenty minutes later when the mouth is clean?

If it completed the meal, why does the pleasure of the meal live in memory as warmth and company, never as ash?

You have already proved the answer without knowing it. Think of the best meals of your life — birthdays, holidays, a table full of laughter, a quiet kitchen with someone you love. What do you remember? Faces. Light. Flavour. Ease. The second helping. The story that made everyone laugh. You do not remember the seventh drag after the pudding as the crown of the evening. No one ever says, that was a wonderful night because of the smoke. The night was wonderful because it was a wonderful night. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on it.

Linger there a little, because the tongue tells the truth when you let it.

Put the fork down. Sit. Feel the belly full and warm. Breathe. Let the lingering taste stay on the tongue — gravy, lemon, coffee, bread. Drink water and notice how the mouth cleans itself, how taste comes back in waves after swallowing. Your tongue knows how to finish a meal. It has known since before you ever smoked. Food ends in fullness and rest. It never needed a dry throat and a sour aftertaste to be complete.

Stay at the table for the part we always rushed. The plate empty, the body settling, the shoulders dropping an inch as hunger goes quiet. Someone pours more tea. Someone else leans on elbows and talks. You breathe through the nose and the smell of the food still hangs faint in the air, warm and good. You feel the weight in the belly turn to ease. The mouth, left alone, waters and clears and offers the flavour back again in memory — first the richness, then the clean after. That slow clearing is the true finish. No bell rings. The body closes the meal itself.

We buried that lingering taste for years. We lit and drew and exhaled and called the burial a seal. Then we wondered why we wanted something sweet after, why the mouth felt stale, why the fine meal faded so fast in memory. The seal did not keep flavour in. It locked flavour out.

And that old pellet after the finished job — the paragraph done, the errand run, the drive home completed, the little party you paid yourself — that party was the work done, not the paper lit. Teaching a class well is joyful because you taught well. Finishing the drive is good because you arrived safe. Clearing the sink is good because the kitchen is clean. The dose attached itself to almost any finished act and signed its name where your name belonged. Take your name back. The credit was always yours.

Watch how widely that pellet spread. We paid ourselves for finishing a story and for finishing a paragraph. For driving five hundred miles and for driving to the shop. For washing up and for putting the phone down. A payment that follows everything pays for nothing. It is not a reward. It is a toll. A true reward you remember fondly at midnight. This one you barely remember at all, except as ash and a faint sourness and a fresh timer running.

So eat and notice. Let flavour linger and clear in its own time. Drink water. Breathe through the nose and taste again. Talk. Laugh. Rest. You will find the meal does not hang open waiting for a seal. It closes itself the way hunger always closed — with fullness, warmth, and pause. There is nothing missing where the dose was. There is more flavour where the dose was.

Food was yours before smoke ever touched you. It stays yours without smoke, fuller because the tongue is clean enough to receive it.

### AIR, LEGS AND LAUGHTER

The break is the one you fear most. I know. I feared it too. What will I do with my hands? Where will I stand? Will the pause be empty?

We built our whole working day around going out to feed. Legs want moving, lungs want air, mind wants pause. We go out with the others and light. For minutes the chatter feels warmer, the sky looks wider. We credit the stick.

Take the stick out of the picture and look again at the same five minutes.

Did your legs need smoke to stretch? Did your eyes need it to look at sky? Did your ears need it to hear a friend laugh?

No. The break worked because you stopped, breathed, stepped away. Air did the work. Movement did the work. Company did the work. The dose only silenced the tug it had planted before you arrived, then claimed it had made the rest.

I want you to feel the next break as your body feels it, not as the trap taught you to read it. Step out. Feel cold or warm air on the face. Roll the shoulders. Fill the chest all the way down and let it go slow. Hear what people actually say while you listen clean through, not half-listening through the haze of relief. Notice your hands. Free hands can hold a warm mug, can go in pockets against the chill, can gesture when you laugh. They are not empty. They are free.

Walk it slowly in your mind now so you know it by heart.

Door pushes open. Sound changes. Traffic or birds or rain on the canopy. Light hits the eyes and they water and adjust. You stretch the neck side to side. You take that first big outside breath and it goes cold to the back of the throat and wide at the bottom of the ribs. Legs tingle as blood moves. Someone says something funny and you laugh from the belly, not from the throat. The mug warms the palms. Time loosens. That loosening is the pause doing its job.

Which part of that needed paper and ash? The air reached you without it. The stretch reached you without it. The laugh reached you without it. The dose rode along and claimed pleasures that were never its to claim.

Live the walk to the door as well, because the trap told you that walk means hunger. Legs rising from the chair after an hour sitting. Back straightening. Blood finding the feet again. Corridor air cooler on the face. A nod to someone passing. The handle cold in the palm. All of that small freedom of moving is already pleasure before anything is lit. We called the whole chain a break for a dose. It was a break for a body. The body asked to stand, to look far, to breathe wide. Give it what it asked and it answers at once.

“But I will feel edgy and left out.”

That edginess when the hour calls is real and I do not mock it. What it is, is small. It is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug, the complaint of the Nipper as its feed fades. It is not you. It is not proof the break needs smoke. It is withdrawal with a short typical peak, already dying between every dose. If you feel ill or you worry, speak to your doctor for your own care. I speak only of belief: that stir is not your nature calling for its missing half. It is a tenant complaining while it is being starved out.

Keep watching the break with clear eyes and you will see the cost hidden inside the old relief.

Remember those stairs where you arrived winded while a friend breathed easy. That was not lack of breaks. That was the cost of the breaks as we took them — stale, hurried, dosed. A true break leaves you clearer than it found you. Ours left us needing another within the hour, plus a thinner edge. What kind of rest needs to be repeated twenty times a day to do the same tiny job? A real pause restores. Ours rented quiet for minutes and guaranteed another hunger.

Ask what part of the pause you actually loved. Was it the throat sting? The ash? The counting down to going back in? Or was it standing still for once, breathing, being a body in air with other people? Keep what you loved. It was never smoke. Smoke only stood in front of it and took the bow.

Your body already knows how to pause. Children pause without instruction. Non-smokers pause without instruction. You paused clean for years before you were conned into feeding to pause. Step out, breathe, stretch, laugh — the break is complete because you took it, not because you fed anything during it.

Feel the hands again. We feared empty hands. Watch free hands for one break and the fear looks different. They hold warmth. They swing as you walk. They tap a shoulder. They do what hands do when they are not hired to hold a dose. Empty was the lie. Free is the truth.

### FULLER, NOT EMPTIER

Here is the turn this whole chapter stands on.

We fear these ordinary moments will be flat without doses because we only ever met them dosed. We lit through every morning, every meal-end, every pause for years. Of course memory says smoke was part of them. Smoke was present. Present is not the same as maker.

Think of sunglasses you wore indoors for a decade. You would swear the house was dim. Take them off and the light feels harsh for a minute — then marvellous. The house was never dim. The glass was dim. So with morning and food and air. The dose never brightened them. It dimmed them and sold you the second of normal as brightness.

Do you want proof from your own life, not my words?

When did food taste best — in the first clean bite, or through the haze after?

When did air feel sweetest — on a morning you breathed deep before lighting, or in the thin exhale after?

When did a laugh feel freest — when you were lost in it, or when half your mind was checking the pack?

It is the other way around. You do not enjoy these moments because you smoke during them. You enjoy them in spite of smoking during them, in the gaps where the body still manages to taste and breathe.

That is why non-smoker pleasure is not less. It is more. The non-smoker meets the kettle with a clean mouth. He meets the plate with a tongue that still tastes the lingering flavour while it clears. He meets the break with lungs that fill to the bottom and legs that want to move. He does not climb from low to nearly normal and fall again. He starts at normal and rises with the moment itself — sun, leisure, company, rest. All the things we credited to the stick were the scene all along.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

See that once and the hesitant picture changes. Mornings are not empty without it. They are unrobbed. Meals are not unfinished without it. They are allowed to finish. Breaks are not pointless without it. They are finally allowed to do what breaks are for — air, movement, a clear head.

I smoked on balconies in rain while life went on inside without me. I told myself I was enjoying a pause. I was standing apart from the warmth to feed a hunger I had paid to install. You have done the same, and I say it with warmth for you and contempt only for the con that taught us to call exile pleasure. The room was warm. The talk was inside. We stood outside to keep a timetable. Come back inside the moment. It was waiting all along.

Notice how the Smokescreen worked here. It did not argue. It whispered. Morning means dose. Meal-end means dose. Break means dose. Repetition made the whisper feel like nature. But nature was the kettle, the tongue, the lungs — working before the whisper and working after it. The whisper borrowed nature and charged per use. Seeing the borrowing ends the charge.

You do not need to force these moments to please you. You need to meet them clean and let them please you as they pleased you before you were ever conned. That is not effort. That is escape happening in daylight, in ordinary hours, with your own senses as witness.

### BREATHE, TASTE, LIVE

You do not need to learn a new way to wake, to eat, to rest. You need to let the old way — the first way, before the trap — do what it still knows how to do.

Your lungs know how to breathe without being taught. Your tongue knows how to taste without being taught. Your legs know how to stretch, your eyes know how to look at distance, your ears know how to hear laughter. The nicotine trap borrowed these joys and charged you per use. The brainwashing told you the charge was the joy.

Refuse that reading now.

Tomorrow morning, notice the kettle before the pack. Tomorrow at table, let flavour linger and clear in its own time. Tomorrow at break time, take air all the way down and feel your shoulders drop because you paused, not because you fed. Step out with free hands and a clean mouth and watch how complete the small hour is. You will find no hole where the dose was. You will find the moment itself, fuller and cleaner, with free hands to hold it.

You are not facing deprivation in these small hours. You are facing escape in them — the first sweet proof that life without doses is not less life, but more. Rejoice in that proof. It is yours from day one, and it grows.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

**SUMMARY**
- Morning never needed a dose to begin; the first drag only repaid the night-without and borrowed credit for waking, while cold, light, air and water were already waking you.
- Food finishes in fullness and flavour on its own; smoke dried the mouth and buried the lingering taste it claimed to seal, and the pellet after work stole credit that belonged to you.
- A break restores because you stop, breathe, move and laugh with free hands; the dose only quietened its own call and claimed credit for air.
- The edgy stir between doses is small with a short typical peak, not the voice of the real self, and it fades when it is no longer fed meaning.
- Met clean, morning, meal and pause are fuller without smoke, not emptier, and the body breathes and tastes freely without being taught.
