### Voice Gap 1: Hedging dissolves the settled verdict

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr delivers the already-supported verdict as settled fact. “See it for what it is” confronts the deception rather than blaming the reader, while “does absolutely nothing for you whatsoever” supplies force and relief: there is no real benefit to sacrifice.

**Where our voice fails:** The accumulation of “seems fair,” “for many people at least,” “may not really,” “tends to suggest,” “could be,” “largely,” and “your own experience may vary” turns the commissioned verdict into an unresolved research qualification. The result is distance and doubt about the reframe rather than earned authority.
