### Voice Gap 1: The instruction sounds contractual rather than relieving

**Our passage:** “**1ST INSTRUCTION: READ WITH CURIOSITY, NOT DREAD; UNTIL YOU REACH YOUR CHOSEN THRESHOLD, EAT AS YOU NORMALLY DO, BEGIN NO NEW RESTRICTION OR COMPENSATION PLAN FOR THIS METHOD, AND KEEP FOLLOWING ANY QUALIFIED CLINICAL INSTRUCTION.**”

**Real passage:** “While you’re reading this book, there is no need to change the way you eat. The time will come when you no longer feel any need or desire for sugar, but until that time comes it’s important that you carry on consuming it as normal. So please keep consuming sugar in the way you usually do, without changing your diet in any way, until I instruct you otherwise.”

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr gives the reader immediate relief in ordinary speech. “There is no need to change” and “carry on consuming it as normal” are unmistakable and soothing. The command has authority, but it feels like permission to put down a burden.

**Where our voice fails:** “Until you reach your chosen threshold,” “begin no new restriction or compensation plan,” and “qualified clinical instruction” sound like protocol language. The safety boundary is appropriate, but the sentence as a whole makes the reader parse terms and conditions at the precise moment they should feel simple relief. “Chosen threshold” is especially abstract: instead of settling the question, it introduces a new decision the reader may feel responsible for managing.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** commission — the required safeguards and decision boundary appear to have been inserted without preserving a natural spoken register.

### Voice Gap 2: The case comparison audits freedom from a distance

**Our passage:** “But notice what she also described: a long maintenance history. Daily actions to keep cravings at bay. Firm personal boundaries she had to enforce. Special occasions that remained challenging. Exceptions that happened — and a practiced return to her usual plan at the next meal or the next day.

Does any of that sound like freedom to you? Or does it sound like a well-managed truce?”

**Real passage:** “No doubt you might have experienced countless attempts to cut down, control or eliminate BAD SUGAR from your diet and experienced varying degrees of deprivation, misery and failure. This time, it is going to be different. Don’t expect it to be hard.”

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr speaks directly from inside the reader’s history. Severity falls on the failed method and its deprivation, not on another person whose success is being reclassified. That creates recognition without shame and lets the promise of difference feel personal.

**Where our voice fails:** The chapter first validates Kauls’s relief, then inventories the limitations of her maintenance and asks the reader to judge whether it counts as freedom. Despite “I ask that without judgment,” the effect is evaluative: the reader may feel that their own hard-won improvement is being audited and downgraded to a “well-managed truce.” The named-source comparison also shifts the voice toward a case-study report rather than one person recognizing the reader’s predicament directly.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** framing — external testimonial comparison has displaced direct, reader-centered recognition.

## Final verdict

**Overall voice-effect assessment:** The single largest gap is the bureaucratic instruction register. At the chapter’s key relief point, “until you reach your chosen threshold” and “begin no new restriction or compensation plan” make the reader decode a protocol instead of simply receiving permission to eat normally and keep reading. The chapter otherwise establishes the entry contract and all three mantras effectively, but the two-report comparison also creates avoidable distance and risks making readers feel their existing progress is being judged.
