Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The grey plate fear — Wet market morning, torn warm bread — Hunger as a clear bell — The plain favourite meal — The clean finish of enough — Love in faces and hunger met

*Hunger met with real food is the feast itself — BAD SUGAR never made it better.*

**THE GREY PLATE FEAR**

You have seen the afternoon for what it was. The buzz never gave power. It muted for ten minutes the heaviness the earlier dose had left behind, then left fog deeper than before.

A new fear now steps forward, soft and reasonable, and it sounds like love.

“Pudding is love.”

“Without something sweet, eating will be grey.”

“I am a comfort eater. Dessert is my favourite part.”

I know that voice. We all lived inside it. We called the sweet course the point of the meal, the reward, the warmth. We said food without a fix would be dull, worthy, joyless. Of course you believe it — that is why the thought of a life without BAD SUGAR still tightens the chest a little.

Let me ask you, warmly and plainly, as one eater to another.

When did icing alone, eaten cold and solitary over the sink, ever feel like love? When did a whole box, eaten past comfort while the evening soured, ever leave you grateful and glowing? And when did true stomach hunger, met after honest waiting, ever leave you emptier than that box did?

You know the answers because you lived them. The solitary dose never felt like love. The box never felt like favourite. The grey did not come from missing sweetness. The grey came from eating without hunger and eating past satisfaction, dose on dose, while taste blurred and joy thinned.

The fact is, eating itself was never grey. We painted it grey by burying hunger and fullness under hits.

Think of the evenings we describe to ourselves as proof. We say, I love to eat, I live to eat, take away my sweet and what is left? And we picture a plate, pale and steamed and joyless, eaten because we must, chewing from duty while part of us mourns. That picture is not memory. That picture is fear painting a future it has never visited.

Ask what that picture leaves out. It leaves out hunger. It leaves out company. It leaves out rest after work, laughter, bread passed hand to hand, the body saying thank you, done. It leaves out everything that ever made a meal a meal, and keeps only the wrapper at the end, as if the wrapper were the feast.

We did the same with mornings. We woke with a mouth already loud, reached for something quick and sweet before hunger could even speak clearly, blurred the day’s first true appetite under a hit, then told ourselves we had no appetite for breakfast. No wonder breakfast felt grey. We never arrived hungry enough to taste it.

We did the same at lunch. We ate fast, half-standing, eyes elsewhere, sweetened away the edge before hunger could guide us, then chased fullness past the signal and called the daze comfort. No wonder lunch felt grey. We ate without the two great seasonings that make any food sing — true need and clean stop.

Do you see the trick? The loop first steals hunger, then steals satisfaction, then points at the flat meal it created and says, you see, without me food is flat. It takes the colour, then sells back a bright label and claims the colour lived in the label.

I tell you with total certainty, from lived escape and from watching thousands rediscover it: colour never lived in the label. Colour lives in the eater. Colour lives in a belly that is ready, a mouth that is awake, a table where enough is allowed to arrive.

Let this chapter do one job and one job only. Not to frighten. Not to lecture. To inhabit. To put you back inside hunger, inside satisfaction, inside real food eaten as favourite, so you feel in your body what argument alone cannot give — that eating without BAD SUGAR is not a smaller life. It is a larger one.

Keep an open mind and come hungry with me.

**HUNGER AS A CLEAR BELL**

There is a feeling we forgot how to hear.

Not mouth-noise. Not head-noise. Not the sudden, twitchy call for something sweet that arrives at ten to three and at ten at night and insists it is urgent. That call is loud, nagging, restless. Real hunger is nothing like it.

Real hunger is low and clear. A hollow readiness in the belly. An emptiness that feels clean, not panicked. A quiet bell that says, eat now, and food will be good. It grows slowly. It waits. It does not shout brand names. It does not point at the cupboard. It opens the whole world of food and says, come.

We buried that bell for years. We answered every stirring with a quick dose before it could ripen. We feared that hollow and misread it as emergency. We topped up before we were empty, then topped again, so hunger never got to finish its sentence.

Tell me honestly. When did you last sit down properly hungry — that clear, hollow, ready feeling that says eat now — and let that hunger choose the pace?

I had forgotten. You have forgotten. We all forgot together, because the loop trains us to fear any gap. An hour without a hit feels like danger. A growl feels like failure. We pat it down with biscuits, with cola, with handfuls from jars, and call ourselves well-fed while the bell never rings.

Hunger is not an enemy. Hunger is the body’s welcome. It is appetite in its true form — not craving for one narrow hit, but openness to food itself. It sharpens flavour the way morning sharpens light. It makes simple things vivid. It makes enough knowable, because only a belly that began empty can feel clearly when it fills.

Watch what happens when you allow it. The morning goes a little longer without a top-up. The belly speaks. First a whisper, then a clear note. No panic. No shaking drama. Just readiness. You notice smells more. Bread smells more like bread. Apples smell bright. Soup smells deep. The mouth wakes. This is not suffering. This is the overture.

And here is the settled physiology that makes this no mystery and no risk for a healthy eater. The fact is your body makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food, so a life without BAD SUGAR is no deprivation — if you live with diabetes or take anything that affects blood sugar, follow your clinician first. You run on meals, not on hits. Hunger is proof the system works, not proof it is broken.

Do you trust a bell you have not heard for years? You can. It never left. It was only drowned. Leave a gap, let the gap ripen, and the bell returns within days, clear as ever.

Ask yourself and answer honestly. If hunger were truly unbearable, why do the hungriest bites taste best? If hunger were danger, why does food eaten hungry satisfy deeply, while food eaten blunted leaves you hunting? If hunger needed sweetening away, why do free eaters welcome it as the start of pleasure?

One short verdict: hunger is not a hole to plug. It is an appetite to meet.

**WET MARKET MORNING, TORN WARM BREAD**

Come with me on an ordinary morning.

Wet greens piled high. Carrots with earth still on them. Tomatoes that smell of sun. Apples, pears, berries, sharp and sweet in their own bright way. Mushrooms, peppers, onions, herbs that perfume the hands. Bread torn open while still warm, steam rising, crust crackling in the hand. Eggs, cheese, beans, rice, fish, meat for those who want them, plain yoghurt, olive oil, butter that melts into crumb. Nothing grand. Nothing measured. Nothing counted or weighed. Real food, bought hungry, carried home hungry.

I want you to inhabit this, not admire it from outside. Feel the bag heavy on the arm. Feel the cool air on the face. Hear the market talk, the tear of paper, the thump of loaves. This is not a prescription. I am giving you no plan, no rules, no good and bad lists. I am showing you a world that is already yours.

Now cook simply and eat hungry.

Take vegetables bright and hot, cooked enough to sweeten them and no more. Take broth that steams the glasses. Take potatoes roasted till edges crisp. Take the bread, chew it, taste wheat and fire. Take fish with lemon, chicken with garlic, beans with oil and salt. Take fruit for what it is, juicy and alive. Notice. Savour. Eat at the pace hunger sets.

Taste what hunger does. A hungry mouth finds depth in a carrot, music in crust, perfume in a pear. Salt sings. Texture speaks. Warmth spreads. The first bites are vivid beyond any wrapper promise, because the mouth is awake and the belly is ready and nothing is blurred.

We ate this meal for years without seeing it. We wolfed lunch while scrolling, talked over dinner without tasting, sweetened away the edge before hunger could speak, then blurred the rest with a dose and wondered why food bored us. No wonder we called dessert love. We never let the meal speak.

Let it speak now. Sit. Breathe. Taste. Put the fork down between bites if you like. Talk. Laugh. Return. Notice how flavour changes as hunger softens — bright at first, then deep, then gentle. That change is the body guiding you. Follow it.

And fruit? Let me settle this kindly, because the loop loves to whisper, fruit is sugar too so nothing matters, eat the bar. The fact is, natural sweetness inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals belongs to the meal, to hunger met and pleasure given. An apple is not a dose. Berries in yoghurt are not a fix. A pear after bread is not the loop. No one needs to fear them or count them or moralize them away. Honour hunger, honour enough, and fruit sits where it always sat — inside eating, inside joy, not inside the trap.

Do you fear that without a sweet hit you will never feel lifted at table? Watch. Hunger lifts. Warmth lifts. Rest lifts. The first hunger met lifts more cleanly than any buzz, and leaves no fog behind to pay.

One short verdict: real food eaten hungry needs no bright label. It is bright.

**THE CLEAN FINISH OF ENOUGH**

There is a second feeling we forgot, kinder even than hunger.

Enough.

Not stuffed. Not regretting. Not loosening clothes and vowing to be good tomorrow. Enough is clean, light, settled. The hollow fills. The edge leaves hunger. Pleasure turns from more to done. The body says thank you, and the mind turns gladly to other things.

We lost enough the same way we lost hunger — by eating past it on purpose, dose on dose, while the signal waved unheard. The loop teaches contempt for enough. It calls it missing out. It says one more bite will complete what the last bite failed to complete. It promises satisfaction one hit beyond the place where satisfaction lives.

Think of the box evenings we all know. We permitted a sliver, then the rustle, then the row, then the box light and crumbs, regret arriving before swallowing ends. Did that ever feel like enough? Or did it feel like being driven past enough with the brakes cut, watching the stop go by?

Compare that driving with a meal eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied. You rise from the table light and settled, mind clear, hands ready for the day. No loud mouth calling for more. No fog. No bargaining. Food stays where food belongs — in its hour — and you walk free into the next hour without a leash.

This is the practice I commend to you, not as a rule to white-knuckle, but as a joy to inhabit, spoken once and kept forever:

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that in your body, not your head. Eat what you want — no fear, no virtue, no clock. When you are hungry — let the clear bell call you. Stop when you are satisfied — let enough close the meal. And never punish yourself again — no penance, no pay-back, no good-week/bad-week ledger. Like that small child with the peach who stops mid-bite to run off.

That stopping is not loss. It is freedom in miniature. The eater who can stop trusts food will be there tomorrow, trusts hunger will return, trusts pleasure does not need to be stockpiled. That trust is what the loop stole, and that trust is what returns the moment you honour enough twice, three times, and feel the calm that follows.

Ask yourself. When did stopping at enough ever leave you grieving? When did eating past enough ever leave you grateful? When did a clean finish ever call for another dose, the way a blurred finish always does?

You know because you lived both. The clean finish closes. The blurred finish hunts.

Practise the close. Mid-meal, pause. Ask the belly, not the head, are we still hollow? If yes, eat with joy. If the edge has gone, if taste has softened from vivid to pleasant, if the body feels warmed and settled, stop. Not from virtue. From satisfaction. Push the plate with pleasure, not with effort. Turn to company, to work, to walk, to day. Feel how food, honoured, releases you.

One short verdict: enough is not missing out. Enough is arrival.

**NOON SOUP, EVENING BREAD, MORNING FRUIT**

Let me walk you through a day lived this way, so it is not theory.

Noon. Properly hungry after a morning of work and air. Not mouth-noisy, belly-ready. You sit to soup — deep, hot, salty, with bread for dipping. You taste the first spoonful fully. Steam, pepper, oil. You chew the crust, hear it crack. You eat till the hollow fills, till the spoon slows on its own. You stop. You breathe. The mind is clear. The hands are steady. No fog asks for a drawer. The afternoon begins light.

Afternoon. You stand, stretch, drink water, look from the window. The three o’clock hour that once owned you passes as an hour. Perhaps tiredness visits — honest tiredness from work, from short sleep, from long screens. You meet it as tiredness, not as hunger. Rest, air, movement answer it. Food waits for hunger. No bargaining, no grazing to get through. The bell will ring when it is time, and you trust it.

Evening. Hunger returns, clear and kind. You cook simply or share what others cooked. Vegetables, grains, fish or meat or beans, oil, salt, lemon, herbs. You eat together if you can, slowly if you cannot. You notice. You savour. You stop at satisfied. Fruit follows if wanted — berries, apple slices, pear, grapes — eaten as part of the meal, enjoyed, finished. Tea, talk, rest. No box calls from the cupboard because no hollow was left unmet and no finish was blurred.

Morning. You wake light, not heavy with last night’s box. Hunger arrives in its own time. Perhaps early, perhaps late. You answer when it calls — warm porridge with fruit, eggs and toast, yoghurt and honeyed warmth of real food, bread and cheese, whatever you truly want. You eat till enough. You rise ready.

Do you see what is missing from this day? Not joy. Not flavour. Not love. What is missing is fog, hunting, regret, bargaining, the tug between mouth-noise and fear. What is missing is the flat hunt that once passed for appetite.

We feared this day would be grey. Live it once in imagination and tell me where the grey lives. In bright soup? In warm bread? In hunger met? In enough honoured? The grey lived only in the picture fear painted, never in the day itself.

I lived the grey years. I ate fast, ate blunted, ate past, then blamed food for boring me. I called myself a comfort eater and meant a wrapper eater. I said dessert was my favourite and meant the ten-minute mute was my relief. Of course I believed eating would be grey without hits — I had made every meal grey with hits, then credited the hits with the only colour I allowed.

You are not that eater now. You are the eater who lets hunger open and enough close, and finds the middle vivid.

**LOVE WAS THE MEAL**

If dessert was love, why did love vanish the moment hunger and company were removed?

Take your fondest food memory and strip it honestly. Sunday lunch loud with talk. Bread passed hand to hand. Laughter. Hunger after a walk. Steam, faces, being held and fed. The sweet course arrived last and got the applause, but where did the warmth live? In icing? Or in faces, in hunger met together, in rest after effort?

Isolate the variable and the answer frees you. The same dose eaten lonely in a car never warmed anyone. The same faces, same hunger, same rest, with water and fruit and bread, still warms to the bone. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on hunger, on company, on rest, and claiming the credit for the glow.

It comforts stress? No — company comforts, warmth comforts, being fed when hungry comforts. It rewards virtue? No — rest rewards, pride rewards, the pause rewards. Celebrating with chocolate never made the celebration. The celebration made the chocolate glow for a minute, then left you flat while the laughter went on without it.

We say, treat when you feel you deserve one, and picture virtue paid in icing. Tell me honestly. When you had been good all week, what did you truly want — icing, or rest, or praise, or ease, or to be held and told well done? And when the icing was eaten, did virtue feel paid, or did it feel postponed, with a new debt to pay from Monday?

We say, sugary somethings to get me through, and picture kindness in a wrapper. Tell me honestly. What got you through — the wrapper, or the pause, the stand, the breath, the kind word, the meal you finally allowed yourself when truly hungry? Give the credit back where it belongs and the wrapper stands naked.

The meal gave the joy. The dose only took the credit.

That is the whole inversion of appetite. BAD SUGAR never added flavour to food. It dulled hunger, blurred satisfaction, then sold back a brief bright hit as if brightness belonged to it. Take away the hits and brightness does not leave. Brightness returns, because hunger and satisfaction are brightness.

You lose no favourite. You regain the eater who knows favourite from the inside — hungry, satisfied, free to enjoy real food as favourite.

** TABLE MANNERS OF A FREE EATER**

Let me give you the table manners of freedom, not as rules to fear, but as habits of joy you already know.

Come hungry. Do not graze to silence the bell. Let the hollow ripen till it rings clear. If the mouth calls before the belly, smile at the noise and wait for the bell. It will come.

Eat what you truly want. Not what the week earned, not what the mood permits, not what the wrapper shouts. What the belly asks for when it is ready — soup, bread, vegetables, grains, fruit, meat, fish, cheese, oil, salt. Real food in its plenty. No counting. No clock. No penance after.

Taste. Put eyes on food. Smell before spoon. Chew. Notice the arc from vivid to pleasant to enough. Talk if with others. Rest if alone. Let the meal be a meal, not fuel while scrolling.

Stop at enough. When the hollow fills, when pleasure softens, when the body feels warmed and settled, stop with pleasure. You are not missing the best bite. You already had it — the hungry bite. The rest beyond enough never improved it yet.

Never punish. No good week, no bad week. No pay-back runs, no skipped meals to atone, no lectures in the mirror. Food is not a court. Hunger is not a crime. Enough is not virtue. They are nature, returning.

Drink water gladly. Move after eating if you wish. Rest if you wish. Let food settle into energy without chasing it with a hit. Feel how clean the hour after enough feels, compared with the loud hour after a box.

Do you fear you will eat too much without sweet rules? Watch. Rules made binges. Hunger and enough end them. The eater who honours both eats less hunting and more joy, not by effort but by nature. The body knows its measure better than any wrapper ever did.

Do you fear you will miss the sweet taste? Taste honestly. Real sweetness never left — in berries, in pears, in roasted carrots, in warm bread, in milk, in corn. It arrives inside hunger met, vivid and sufficient, without fog after. You do not need to chase it. You need only meet it hungry.

One short verdict: the table was never the trap. The hits at the table were.

**WHY THIS DAY FREES TOMORROW**

There is a reason this chapter sits where it sits, at the centre, warming while demolition rests.

You cannot fight your way to freedom on an empty belly and a blurred finish. You inhabit your way there, meal by meal, hunger by hunger, enough by enough, till the body itself testifies that joy never lived in the hit.

Every clean meal is evidence the court cannot refute. Every hunger met says, I am fed without fog. Every enough honoured says, I can stop with joy. That evidence accumulates quietly, without effort, and carries you toward the final vow on a tide of lived proof, not on promises.

Marvellous relief waits here, and it is present, not future. Not after weeks. Not after a milestone. Now — at the next hunger, at the next meal. The immediate gain is a table that brightens, a belly that trusts you again, a mind that leaves food at food’s hour and turns free to life.

Rejoice in that. Celebrate the return of appetite. We were conned into fearing hunger and worshipping hits. We now see hunger as friend, satisfaction as close, real food as favourite. That seeing is not loss. That seeing is homecoming.

Ask the closing questions and answer as the eater you now are.

If sweets made food joyful, why did the most wrapper-heavy years feel the least joyful at table? If hits were love, why was love strongest where hits were smallest — in hunger, in company, in rest? If eating without hits is grey, why does every hunger met in this chapter glow brighter in memory than any box?

You know. We know. I know. The glow was never in the wrapper. The glow was in us, hungry, together, fed.

Hold that glow. Trust it at the next bell. Let enough close you cleanly. Wonderful eating, free eating, happy eating stands ready — not as a reward for struggle, but as nature resuming the moment hits stop drowning it.

**6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION**
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**

* Food without sweet hits is not grey — hunger met and fullness honoured make an ordinary meal bright and complete.
* True stomach hunger is a clear, calm readiness that sharpens taste and opens all real food to joy.
* Clean satisfaction closes eating with lightness — the hollow filled, pleasure turned to enough, no fog or further hunting.
* The body runs well on ordinary meals and makes what it needs from them, and fruit inside a meal belongs to joy.
* Warmth in eating lived in hunger met together, in company, rest and care — the sweet hit only rode along and took the credit.
* Eating what you want when hungry, stopping at satisfied and never punishing yourself restores food as favourite.
* The next hunger is a welcome, the next enough is an arrival, and the table brightens the moment it is trusted.
