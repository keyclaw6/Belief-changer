### Voice Gap 1: Hedging dissolves the method promise

**Our passage:** “This method has helped a lot of people, and there is a good chance it can help you too. Results naturally vary, and it might not be entirely easy for everyone, but many readers report finding it easier than they expected.”

**Real passage:** “This book will help you to understand the truth about BAD SUGAR and take you through a proven method to cut it out of your diet completely and permanently, without leaving you feeling deprived or requiring any willpower. In fact, it will be easy.”

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr makes a direct, definite promise to the reader: the method will produce understanding, freedom, permanence, and relief from deprivation and willpower. “In fact, it will be easy” carries earned authority and confronts the reader’s fear plainly.

**Where our voice fails:** “There is a good chance,” “results naturally vary,” “might not,” and “many readers report” turn the promise into a cautious testimonial disclaimer. The reader is left wondering whether the method itself can be trusted and whether they may be one of the people for whom it fails. Instead of relief, the passage creates distance and uncertainty.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** writer-prompt — an apparent demand for cautious claim language has overridden the assigned need for a firm method promise.
