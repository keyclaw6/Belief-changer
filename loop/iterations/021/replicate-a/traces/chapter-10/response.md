Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

IN THIS CHAPTER
Friday calendar with a red circle. A cliff edge with a chalk line. Wrapper rustle to empty box. A seatbelt clicked in a parked car.

*There is no safe sweet to keep, there is only the Trap fed or left to starve.*

The party stayed bright without the bar.

THE NEGOTIATOR WAKES UP

Now the voice changes its tune.

It no longer argues that BAD SUGAR helps you. It argues for a smaller cage. “All right, the daily dosing is slavery. But surely just a treat, once a week is fine.” “I don't keep it in the house, so I am safe.” “I will quit tomorrow, after the birthday, after the holidays.” “Just one. I will wean off.”

We all know that voice. We have all spoken it. It sounds reasonable. It sounds moderate. It sounds like control.

The fact is it is the same old fraud in a tidy suit. This is the Sweet Con bargaining for its life.

You have already seen that BAD SUGAR never lent you energy or love or reward. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you. If that is true every day, why would it stop being true on Friday? If a dose gives nothing on Tuesday, what magic does the calendar lend it on Saturday?

Ask what honest answer you can give:

If the Friday sweet is harmless, why do you think about it all week?

If just one is easy, why has just one ever ended with an empty box?

And if tomorrow is a better day to be free, what does today give you that freedom would spoil?

There is no honest answer that leaves the exception standing. A special sweet is not a way out of the Sugar Trap. It is the Sugar Trap with a ribbon on it.

THE FRIDAY THAT ATE THE WEEK

Live one Friday-allowance week and watch the mechanism with your own eyes.

Monday you are good. Tuesday you are good and a little proud. Wednesday the picture of Friday pudding starts to glow. Thursday you are already tasting it at your desk. You tell yourself anticipation is half the pleasure. You walk past the shop and smile because Friday you may.

Friday comes. You eat it fast, half guilty, half rushed, already mourning that it is over while it is still in your mouth. Then Saturday feels flat and a little cheated, and a small voice says Thursday was hardly Friday, Saturday is near enough, Monday is far away.

By the next week the circle has widened. Friday plus the birthday. Friday plus the tired Thursday. Friday plus the leftover Sunday. The line you drew to contain the Trap becomes the line the Trap uses to pull you back.

I am not judging you. We were all brainwashed to call this moderation. Look at what moderation does in practice. It makes the dose precious. Forbidden fruit tastes sweeter because it is forbidden, not because it is sweet. A permitted sliver fires the thought of the next before hunger has spoken, and you know that rush of I feel like i cant stop once the pack is open. That rush is not your weakness. It is the signature of the Trap. Once the first deliberate dose is in, the Nibbler grumbles for its feed and the Con supplies the excuse.

The yo-yo of rule-then-binge is the form of the Trap itself. Tighten the rule and the binge grows. Loosen the rule and the dosing grows. Either way the Trap grows.

THE CLIFF

Let me show you the bargain in daylight.

A friend takes you to a cliff. White rock, long drop, sea banging below. He points to a chalk line three steps back from the edge and says, cheerful as you like, jump off here provided you fall only a few metres.

You would laugh in his face. You cannot jump off a cliff a little. The fall does not obey the chalk line. The cliff does not care about your good intentions.

Telling yourself you will take BAD SUGAR only on Fridays, only one, only as a weaned-down dose, is that same chalk line. You stand on the edge by choice and promise the drop will respect your diary.

“But sugar is not a cliff,” the negotiator says. “A biscuit will not kill me.”

No. A biscuit will not throw you on the rocks that afternoon. It will do something quieter and surer. It will keep the belief alive that a dose carries value, and while that belief lives the craving lives, and while the craving lives you live in the tug between wanting and fearing. That is the fall. Not a crash. A return to slavery with a weekly view.

If there is nothing to gain from a dose often, there is no point in a dose seldom. A nothing does not become a something because you save it for Friday.

JUST ONE, NOT IN THE HOUSE, TOMORROW

Take the three cleverest carve-outs and hold each to the light for ten seconds.

“Just one.” You know that wrapper rustle to empty box. One was never one in the Trap. One is the trigger that wakes the whole chain. The mouth wants another before the first is swallowed, not from hunger, but from the loop the first relit. To plan just one is to plan a binge and call it restraint.

“I don't keep it in the house.” Whose house? The shop on the corner keeps it for you. The desk drawer keeps it for you. The petrol station, the cinema, the birthday table keep it for you. We never needed a cupboard to stay trapped. We carried the Con in our heads, and the world laid doses at every turn. Moving the box moves nothing. Killing the belief kills everything.

“I'll quit tomorrow, after the birthday, after the holidays.” Tomorrow never has fewer sweets than today. Birthdays breed birthdays. Holidays breed holidays. The calendar is full of reasons by design. And ask the trap question: if the special sweet were truly harmless, why would you be reading a book about escaping it? The promise of tomorrow is the Trap begging for one more day of rent.

I hear the deeper objection behind these, the one aimed at this book itself. “This all-or-nothing talk is extreme. You are turning a biscuit into dogma. Surely a free person can take a little.”

I love that objection because it proves the point. Who taught you that freedom means keeping a little of the cage? A free person does not need a little dose. A trapped person needs permission for a little dose. Totality sounds extreme only while the Con still whispers that something of value is being left behind. Once you see there is nothing of value in the dose, totality is not strict. It is obvious. It is relief.

THE SEATBELT

Now hear the margin clearly, because a clear margin makes you strong and a blurry margin keeps you scared.

Your body can cope with an accidental crumb. A trace in a sauce, a misread label, a polite bite taken before you knew. The body shrugs at accidents. What the mind cannot afford is a deliberate exception.

Think of a seatbelt. You click it in a parked car for the jolt you never plan, not so you can steer into walls. The belt is there for accidents, never a licence to drive erratically.

So it is with BAD SUGAR. An accidental blip changes nothing unless you let the Sweet Con back in to preach over it. “See, you failed, you may as well carry on.” That sermon is the only danger. Without that sermon, a blip is dust on the shoe, brushed off, forgotten. With that sermon, a blip becomes a relapse, not by chemistry, but by belief.

So guard the belief, never police your day in panic. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that moves blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any food-related risk, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief change only. For everyone, the rule is simple in the head and calm in the body: no deliberate dose, no mourning, no fright over accidents.

Do you feel how much easier that is than moderation? Moderation asks you to decide every hour, to count, to bargain, to be good. Totality asks you to decide once, clearly, and then get on with enjoying your life. One decision ended. A thousand daily negotiations ended with it. That is why free eaters feel light while cutters-down feel tired.

You do not need a house rule. You do not need a Friday. You do not need tomorrow. You need no safe sweet because you need no sweet at all to be whole, and the tiny grumble of the Nibbler dies rapidly unfed once the Con stops feeding it stories.

10. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

SUMMARY
- A Friday allowance makes the dose glow brighter all week and spreads to Thursday and Saturday.
- Just one carries the binge with it, not from weakness but as the signature of the Trap.
- Keeping it out of the house moves the box while the belief stays in the head.
- Tomorrow never comes emptier, birthdays breed birthdays by design.
- An accidental crumb is dust on the shoe when the belief stays dead, a deliberate exception feeds the whole loop alive again.
