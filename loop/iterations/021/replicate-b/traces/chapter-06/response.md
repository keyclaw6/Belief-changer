Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A morning market stall, wet greens and warm bread
- A plain favourite meal eaten properly hungry
- A bowl of fruit, a full belly and an easy stop

*Real hunger met by real food is the feast itself — sweetness never made it better.*

The afternoon buzz is seen for what it was.

Now a quieter fear remains, and it deserves an honest answer. You say to me, in your own voice:

“But food will be grey without my sweet treat. Dessert is love. Pudding is the best part.”

I hear you. We have all said it. We sat at good tables, full of good company, and told ourselves the joy came from the sugar at the end. We called the last dose the reward for eating well, the proof we had deserved something.

Let me ask you three plain questions, and answer as an investigator would.

When has plain food tasted best to you — when you were hungry, or when you were already stuffed with snacks?
Did you need a topping to love lunch as a small child running in from play?
Has the evening dose ever left you truly satisfied, wanting nothing more?

You know the answers. Hunger makes food sing. The child knows it. And the dose never satisfies.

The Grey Fear

We fear that without BAD SUGAR doses, eating will lose its colour. No more pleasure. Just chewing to stay alive. That fear keeps many good people paying for fix after fix.

The fact is the opposite is true.

Think of your own best meals. Not the hurried bar at the desk. Not the box emptied in front of late television. The meals you remember. A Sunday roast when you came in cold and hungry. Bread torn with friends. Fish eaten by the sea. Was it the white dust on top that made those moments shine?

No. It was hunger met. It was company kept. It was flavour noticed. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on a pleasure that belonged to the moment.

BAD SUGAR does not brighten food. It dulls it. Each sweet hit coats the mouth, blunts appetite, rushes you past satisfied into stuffed and still wanting. You eat more and taste less. You finish the plate and reach for more while enjoying less. That is not love of food. That is the loop talking.

You do not need to be told what to eat. I am no diet teacher and this is no eating plan. Your body already knows how to ask and how to stop, if you let it speak.

The Market Meal

Come with me to do one ordinary thing, fully noticed.

Morning. You wake properly hungry. Belly empty, mouth clean, head clear. You walk to the market. No rules in your head. No good-food sermon. Only hunger, honest and simple.

Look. Wet lettuces. Tomatoes warm from the van. Fresh bread with a dark crust that cracks. Apples with real perfume. Cheese that smells of the field. Nothing fancy. Nothing labelled. Just real food, made to be eaten hungry.

You choose what appeals to you. You take it home. You cook it simply or eat it as it is. You sit down. You eat.

First bite. Notice it. The crunch. The juice. The salt. The warmth spreading out from the stomach. Second bite. Slower. You talk. You laugh. You pause.

Halfway through, something marvellous happens. The sharp edge of hunger softens. Flavours grow rounder. You eat a little more. Then you stop. Not because a clock tells you. Not because a rule allows you. Because you are satisfied. The body says, enough, thank you. Pleasure complete.

Stay with that stop. It is not deprivation. It is arrival.

That is eating as favourite. Not as fuel calculation. Not as reward earned. As hunger met by food, noticed and enjoyed, then left when done — like the little one who drops the peach mid-bite and runs off to play.

When did you last stop like that after a dose? The dose never lets you stop. One leads to rustle, to handful, to regret before the swallow ends. The meal lets you stop because the meal answers the need. The dose cannot stop because the dose is not answering hunger at all.

Hunger and Satisfaction Know the Way

Hunger is not an enemy to silence with a quick fix. Hunger is the seasoning no packet can copy. It makes plain things taste wonderful. Satisfaction is not a limit to fight. It is the bell that tells you the joy is done.

We lost trust in both because we kept interrupting them. We fed the 11am dip with a bar. We fed the 3pm dip with biscuits. We fed the evening with spoon after spoon. Then we wondered why meals felt flat and why we never felt truly done.

It is the other way around. Meals felt flat because the doses stood between hunger and satisfaction. Remove the interruption and the signal returns on its own. Quickly. Without training. Without effort.

Your body makes its own steady glucose from plain food. That is plain physiology, not a promise. Take away the sweet hits and you are not left empty. You are left with the system that ran you before the loop taught you to doubt it. If you live with diabetes or take medicine that touches blood sugar, follow your clinician first — this is about belief, not medical care.

And hear this clearly, because no one here moralises about sweetness. A ripe peach is sweet. Berries are sweet. An apple after hunger is sweet in a way no wrapper ever matched. Enjoying that sweetness is part of freedom, not a threat to it. The target was never flavour or joy. The target was the refined dose that steals flavour and joy and sells them back.

So taste again, hungry. Eat what appeals to you. Stop when the body says enough. Notice how much more you taste when the mouth is not coated and the blood is not yo-yoing. Notice how company sounds funnier, how the evening feels lighter, how the belly rests easy.

Is life grey without the dose? Ask your own mouth. The meal eaten hungry, savoured and stopped at satisfied, is in full colour. The dose eaten full, hurried and regretted, is the grey thing.

You were never afraid of losing joy. You were afraid of losing the interruption that kept joy from arriving.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is not a rule to memorise. It is what the body does when left alone. No counting. No guilt. No penance after. Hunger calls. Food answers. Satisfaction closes the meal. You get on with enjoying your life.

I promise you with the authority of one who has walked out and watched thousands walk out: real food, eaten hungry, will become your favourite. Not because you force yourself to love it. Because you finally taste it.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy was never in the sweet dose, it was in hunger met, flavour noticed and company kept.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry satisfies fully, while a dose eaten full leaves wanting more.
- Hunger makes plain food taste wonderful and satisfaction tells you joy is complete without rules or penance.
- The body keeps steady energy from plain meals on its own, with clinical care left to your clinician where needed.
- Ripe fruit and real meals keep all their sweetness to enjoy, the refined hit added nothing to them.
- Stopping at satisfied is arrival, not loss, and eating returns to pleasure without effort.
