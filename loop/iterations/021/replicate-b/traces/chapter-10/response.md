Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A Friday allowance that brightens Monday and spreads to Thursday and Saturday
- A cliff edge with permission to fall only a little way
- The box empty before the swallow ends
- A seatbelt clicked for a jolt, never for wild driving
- The house with nothing in the cupboard and everything in the mind
- Tomorrow morning that never arrives

*There is no careful little corner you can keep inside BAD SUGAR and stay free.*

The prettiest one kept nothing for the dose.

And now the mind does what every trapped mind does at this point. It bargains.

I hear you because we all bargained there, you and I. The fog is lifting. The cinema glow belongs to people. The afternoon lift stands exposed. The belief is dying. And just as freedom opens its hand, a small reasonable voice steps forward with a pen and a little contract.

"All right. I see it. But surely I can keep just the Fridays? Surely one tiny one will not matter? Surely I will start properly tomorrow, after the birthday, after the holiday? Surely I can keep the house clear and have it only outside?"

That voice sounds moderate. It sounds mature. It is the voice of the trap negotiating its own survival.

Let us look at it kindly and completely, because once you see what a carve-out does, you will never want one.

THE FRIDAY THAT ATE THE WEEK

Take the most respectable bargain of all. The Friday rule.

You decide: no BAD SUGAR all week, then Friday night, feet up, a proper dessert, a bar, a pudding you love. Controlled. Civilised. Only once a week.

Watch that week as investigator, hour by hour, exactly as you have lived it before.

Monday starts bright. Tuesday holds. Wednesday afternoon a thought crosses: only two days to Friday. The thought feels sweet. Thursday you find yourself planning. Which shop? Which flavour? Big bar or small box? You picture the first bite in detail. Friday morning you wake a little lighter because tonight is the night. The day drags in a pleasant ache.

Tell me honestly. What has happened to the other six days?

They have become a corridor leading to a door. Food during the week loses colour because colour has been stored up for Friday. Hunger stops being a clean signal and becomes a countdown. You are not free six days and indulgent one day. You are hungry in mind for seven days for the sake of one evening.

Then Friday comes. You unwrap with ceremony. The first bite flares brighter than any bite ever flared when you ate daily. Of course it does. Six days of waiting have polished it. Six days of nothing have taught your mouth that this is precious beyond price.

Is that bite smaller for being rare? Does waiting make the glow weaker or stronger? Does forbidden fruit taste duller or brighter?

You know the honest answers. Waiting makes it blaze. Rarity makes it sacred. The rule that was meant to shrink it has gilded it.

And then, ten minutes later, the old familiar slide. One was meant to be one. The glow calls for a second to hold it. The second dulls, so a third is needed to chase the first. Soon there is the box empty before the swallow ends, and a hot wave of regret before the taste has even left the tongue.

I say this without a shred of blame for you. That slide is not weakness. When a permitted sliver is eaten while the belief still glows, the craving carries the binge. The loss of control is the signature of the trap, never proof that you are broken beyond repair. You are not helpless. You are hooked by a belief that one exception has kept alive.

Then comes Saturday morning with its second bargain. "Well, I ruined Friday, so I may as well enjoy Saturday and start again Monday." Then Thursday whispers, "One little one now will take the edge off till Friday." The Friday island floods from both sides until the week is water again.

Did the rule make you freer or busier in mind? Did you think about BAD SUGAR less or a hundred times more? Was the dose smaller across the month, or larger, brighter, and more bossy than ever?

Only honest answers count, and you know them. The allowance did not contain the trap. The allowance fed it.

That is the first lock on the door: cut down to once a week and you do not weaken the desire. You marinade it.

THE CLIFF

Let me show you the same truth in a picture you cannot unsee.

A man stands at the edge of a tall white cliff. The sea bangs far below. A cheerful friend puts a hand on his shoulder and says: "Jump. It is quite safe, provided you fall only three metres and then stop in mid-air."

You would stare at him. You would ask if he had lost his reason. You would say: falling is falling. You cannot order gravity to pause because you made a rule.

That is exactly what "just one" asks of the mind.

BAD SUGAR inside the Sugar Trap is a cliff, not a slope. The belief that a dose gives a genuine lift, a genuine reward, a genuine comfort, is gravity. Once you step off with a deliberate bite while that belief lives, you fall. The Nibbler wakes and grumbles. The Sweet Con supplies the script: that was good, have another, you have started so you may as well enjoy it. Attention narrows. The hand moves before the decision.

Would you call that friend wise for adding: "Well, jump only on Fridays"? Would a net made of promises catch you halfway down? If the fall is certain, is a small permitted fall safer or madder than a big one?

It is madder, because it dresses danger as control.

I have watched strong, clever, kind people stand on that edge with house-rules in hand. One square. One day. One brand. Only outside the house. Only with coffee. Only when sad. Only when happy. Every rule broke the same way, not because the people were weak but because the rule kept the belief upright. While the mind believes the dose carries joy, the mouth will finish the job.

There is no safe sweet inside BAD SUGAR. There is no careful fall. There is only the cliff, and there is solid ground with your back to it.

JUST ONE, JUST OUTSIDE, JUST TOMORROW

Now let us take the other bargains one by one, briefly and warmly, because they all share one throat.

"Just one tiny one cannot hurt."

Tiny to the eye is huge to the belief. A tiny dose revives the big idea: this does something for me. It re-teaches the mouth that relief lives in a wrapper. It wakes the little creature and hands it a meal, then leaves you to hear it grumble for hours afterward. And what does one prove? If one truly gave nothing, why beg for one? If one truly gave something, why stop at one? The plea defeats itself either way.

"It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you."

Read that slowly and let it cover even the tiny one. It rots teeth through the smallness. It spikes then drags the bright attention you brought to the hour. It teaches every eye watching you that love comes in a wrapper. It does all that TO you, quietly, in a minute, while you tell yourself it was nothing. What did the tiny one do FOR the hour that the same hour without it would lack? Name one thing. You cannot name it, because there is nothing to name.

"I do not keep it in the house. That proves I am in control."

I honour the effort in that rule. We all tried it. The cupboard bare, the drawer clean, the will shining on Monday.

Where did the thought live while the cupboard stayed empty? Did the bare shelf make the shop brighter or duller? Did you walk past the aisle quicker or slower, with less chatter in the head or more?

You know. The house was empty and the mind was full. The checkout ambush glowed like a lighthouse. The petrol station glowed. The office tin glowed. Keeping it outside the front door only moved the altar to the street and made every walk a pilgrimage. Control of the cupboard is not freedom of the mind. Freedom is when the thought itself brings relief that you need never queue for that fix again.

"I will start tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holiday."

Tomorrow is the kindest lie the trap ever told you.

Ask yourself as investigator. Will the dose be less precious after the birthday cake has re-crowned it? Will the belief be weaker after one more demonstration that celebration needs sugar? Will Monday's mouth be calmer after a weekend farewell tour of every favourite?

Does a farewell tour loosen love or deepen it? Does one more dance with the jailer make the cell easier to leave? If tomorrow were truly easier, why does tomorrow never come?

It never comes because delay is use wearing a mask. Every tomorrow pushes the same belief one day deeper: this is too dear to leave today. A free person does not need a perfect date on the calendar. A trapped person uses the calendar to avoid seeing they are trapped.

"I will wean down slowly. Cut down gently."

Gentle to whom? Each smaller hit between longer gaps burns brighter in memory. Each gap teaches anticipation. The on-and-off schedule itself drives the binge volume higher than steady grazing ever did. We know this shape from life: forbid then permit, starve then feast, and the feast grows wilder. The yo-yo is not your character. The yo-yo is the form of the trap when you pull at it with rules.

Does tapering make the final step smaller or does it make every step glow? Does a man escape a pit by climbing halfway out each day and sliding back each night to practise?

You escape by seeing there is nothing down there you need, and stepping onto solid ground with eyes open.

THE SEATBELT

At this point a frightened question rises, and I welcome it.

"Do you mean one crumb will poison me forever? Must I live in panic?"

No. Listen carefully, because this distinction hands you ease for life.

Your body is marvellous. It can cope with an accidental blip - a sauce thickened without your knowledge, a crumb in a shared kitchen, a mistaken bite you spit and laugh at. That jolt passes like a bump in the road. If you live with diabetes, take medicines that touch sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice, this book only changes what the dose means to you.

Think of the belt that clicks across your chest when you drive. It sits there quietly for the jolt you never planned. It does not invite you to steer into walls. It does not excuse wild driving. It saves you when life bumps, precisely because you drive straight the rest of the time.

So it is with freedom from BAD SUGAR. The body absorbs the accident. The mind must never steer into the dose on purpose. A deliberate "special sweet" is not a bump. It is driving at the wall to test the belt. The belt holds, but the belief wakes, dusts itself down, and starts whispering again in the voice of the Sweet Con.

Do you see the kindness in this? You need never panic about perfection. You need only guard the belief. Let an accident be an accident: notice it, smile that it gave you nothing, rejoice you are free, and get on with enjoying your life. A slip revives nothing unless you let the old idea back in with it.

Guard the belief, and the behaviour looks after itself.

THIS SOUNDS FANATICAL

And now the last objection, the one aimed not at sugar but at me.

"This totality talk sounds extreme. No exceptions ever? That is fanatical. That is just another diet tyranny. You are brainwashing me."

I love that challenge. Bring it fully into the light.

Who is demanding tyranny here? The man who says you may eat abundantly of real food when hungry, stop at satisfaction, never punish yourself, walk mornings and shops and birthdays with a light heart - or the voice that says you must count days, police cupboards, earn Fridays, mourn Mondays, measure squares, and think about a bar a hundred times a day?

Which life feels tighter in the chest? Which feels wider?

Totality inside the Sugar Trap is not narrowness. It is the wide road. One clear line - no deliberate BAD SUGAR - ends a thousand negotiations. The mind goes quiet because there is nothing left to debate. Choice returns because the false choice has gone. That is ease, not force. No white knuckles are needed to avoid a movie that holds no interest for you. That is what freedom feels like when the belief dies.

Fanaticism clings to something precious and calls it forbidden. I am showing you there is nothing precious there to cling to. How can it be fanatical to leave what gave nothing? How can it be tyranny to walk away from slavery rejoicing?

If I am brainwashing you, test me the way you would test any honest investigator. Take one ordinary week without a kept sweet and watch: hunger cleaner, afternoons clearer, evenings calmer, shops duller in the sweet aisle and brighter everywhere else. No dread is required. The proof arrives in lived hours, not in my promises.

You do not need to believe me. You need only see it, and seeing is already escape.

11. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

Hear that as good news, not as a scolding. One line ends a lifetime of tug and creep. Keep nothing back for the trap to breed in, and the Nibbler starves without a sound while you get on with the marvellous business of living.

You stand now where every free eater has stood, you and I together. The bargains lie broken at the cliff edge. The Friday glow has dimmed back to ordinary wrapper. Tomorrow has lost its mask. The house rule has shown its true address, which was always the mind, never the cupboard.

Will you carry one glowing coal in your pocket and wonder why your coat keeps smoking? Or will you empty the pocket, laugh at how heavy one small coal felt, and feel the wonderful lightness of having nowhere to fall?

Choose solid ground. Choose it now, wholly, cheerfully, for the rest of your free life. There is no loss here to mourn. There is only a con to leave, and relief to celebrate.

BRING NO SWEET WITH YOU INTO FREEDOM, AND FREEDOM BRINGS EVERYTHING WITH IT.

**SUMMARY**
- A Friday allowance makes the dose brighter through waiting and spreads across the week until the rule owns the mind.
- One deliberate bite reawakens the idea that the dose helps, and the binge that follows is the trap working, not a flaw in you.
- Empty cupboards, slow weaning and tomorrow promises keep the thought alive while moving the struggle from place to place.
- An accidental taste passes like a road bump, a kept special sweet steers the mind back to the cliff.
- Total clarity feels light because debate ends, hunger and satisfaction return to their natural ease.
