## C. CONTESTED — do NOT overstate (the credibility armor for the framing stage)

This section is the discipline the doctrine requires. These are the claims the framing stage must NOT inflate.

- **CANCER:** Sugar does not *directly* cause cancer (Wikipedia/WCRF; see S-019#E-002). The cancer link is mediated by obesity and possibly by insulin/IGF-1 signaling. Pancreatic cancer has a 22% per-25g-fructose increment (Huang 2023, class III) — modest, observational. **Do not write** "sugar causes cancer" as a flat claim. The book's correct claim is: *the obesity that sugar drives causes cancer, and the insulin/IGF-1 axis is a plausible secondary mechanism.*

- **ADDICTION:** Sugar is not a validated DSM/ICD addiction in humans. It does activate dopaminergic reward pathways, and glucose supplementation reduces nicotine withdrawal (van Amsterdam 2025). **Do not write** "sugar is as addictive as cocaine" (the overstated popular framing). The correct claim is: *the sugar loop shares mechanism with substance-reward pathways, but the clinical-addiction claim is not settled — and "addiction" framing can be misused to make quitting sound harder than the clean-baseline approach actually is.*

- **FRUCTOSE / LIVER (ISOCALORIC):** The Chung 2014 AHRQ review (S-017) found that at isocaloric exchange, fructose/HFCS/sucrose do not increase liver fat vs other carbohydrates. The Schwarz 2017 / Lustig 2016 trials (S-008, S-009) found dramatic liver fat reduction in 9 days of isocaloric fructose restriction, but in *already-obese* children. **Do not write** "fructose is uniquely hepatotoxic independent of calories" as a general population claim. The correct claim is: *in obese/at-risk populations, even isocaloric fructose reduction reverses liver fat within 9 days — but the book should not overstate this as a universal claim about healthy adults.*

- **DEMENTIA:** Jouni 2025 found RR 1.49 (1.03–2.15) but I²=79% — high heterogeneity. **Do not write** "sugar causes Alzheimer's" as a flat claim. The correct claim is: *the observed association is substantial but heterogeneous; mechanistic plausibility exists (insulin resistance, vascular injury); causality is not established.*

- **SUGAR VS. FAT — HISTORICAL RECEIPT, NOT MODERN CONSENSUS:** Kearns 2016 documents the 1965 SRF-funded NEJM review. This is a historical receipt, not a current scientific debate. **Do not write** as if the 1965 framing is the present scientific consensus. The current consensus is that *both* excess sugar and excess saturated fat (and excess total calories, and ultra-processed food) contribute to cardiometabolic risk.

- **QUITTING EVIDENCE — SUGAR-SPECIFIC:** There is no clean meta-analysis of "sugar cessation" as such (a search for `sugar cessation intervention systematic review abstinence` returned only 3 results, none with strong RCT design for sugar specifically). What we have is:
  - SSB reduction RCTs (S-004, S-011, S-012) show that reducing sugar-sweetened beverages causes weight loss and improves biomarkers.
  - Isocaloric fructose restriction (S-008, S-009) shows rapid metabolic improvement.
  - **No clean, large RCT of "eliminate added sugar entirely from diet" exists.** The book should claim "the evidence is strong that reducing added sugar improves metabolic markers within days" — not "there are RCTs showing total sugar abstinence cures X."

- **HALL 2019 — ULTRA-PROCESSED ≠ SUGAR ALONE:** Hall's inpatient RCT shows that ultra-processed foods (which are typically high in added sugar, fat, and salt) cause overconsumption. The trial was not designed to isolate sugar. **Do not write** "the Hall 2019 trial proved sugar makes you overeat." The correct claim is: *the Hall trial proves ultra-processed food (sugar-laden and otherwise) defeats normal satiety, which is the relevant environmental context for a sugar problem.*
