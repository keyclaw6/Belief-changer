Chapter 9
THE BARGAIN THAT KEEPS THE CAGE

It is Monday morning and you are in control.

You open the desk drawer and look at the neat row you laid out last night. Five small things for five working days. One sweet thing a day. Nothing at the house to tempt you in the evening because you were firm in the shop. Weekends are your S-days, the ones that begin with S, when the rule relaxes, because a life with no sweet at all does not bear thinking about. This time the rule is sensible. This time the amounts are small. This time you have learned from January.

Monday goes exactly as drawn. You eat your one at half past three with tea. You feel virtuous closing the drawer. You tell yourself this proves it. You can handle it. You are not one of those people who cannot stop. You have a rule and you kept it.

Tuesday goes almost as drawn. The one is eaten a little earlier, at half past two, standing rather than sitting, while answering something dull. The drawer clicks shut. A faint tug follows you back to the screen. You notice it and you translate it as proof the rule is working. Only a small tug. Manageable. This is what moderation feels like from the inside.

Wednesday the drawer opens at eleven. You tell yourself you will split the one and save half for the afternoon. You eat half. You fold the wrapper with care. You put the half back. You look at it three times before one o'clock. At ten past one you eat the half telling yourself afternoons are the hard part and mornings should not count. At four o'clock you borrow tomorrow's. Only borrowed. You will skip Friday to balance it. The arithmetic feels honest while you do it.

Thursday there is no arithmetic. There is the drawer hanging open and the sweet gone before lunch and the swift trip to the machine and the second sweet eaten walking back so no one sees the wrapper and the resolve to be strict from Monday. There is the evening shop where you buy a little extra because the weekend is coming and S-days permit desserts twice plus the late pizza eaten as sweets. There is Sunday night on the sofa with the empty tub and the old sentence arriving on schedule.

I have no control. I may as well start again next week.

Stay with that week a moment longer than is comfortable, because this chapter lives inside it. Not in the weekend tub. In the Monday drawer. In the rule itself.

*The rule does not control the loop. The rule builds the timetable the loop runs on.*

IN THIS CHAPTER: the neat Monday drawer, why the rule breaks by Thursday, why one is never one, the cliff with the kind limit, what totality really asks of you

The Neat Monday Drawer

Let us begin where you are strongest, not where you are weakest, because your strongest week is my best evidence.

Why did you need the drawer at all.

If one sweet thing a day were a free choice, happily taken and happily left, you would not need a row laid out like medicine. You would not need to count days that begin with S. You would not need to clear the house on Sunday night as if the house were the problem. You do not lay out five apples on Sunday night and congratulate yourself for eating only one a day. You do not borrow tomorrow's apple at four o'clock and hide the core. The drawer with its arithmetic, its borrowing, its balancing, its Monday virtue and Thursday wreckage, is not the behaviour of a person enjoying a pleasure. It is the behaviour of a person inside the Bad Sugar trap negotiating rations with it.

Ask the plain question you have never asked while laying out the row.

Who is the rule for.

It is not for your body. Your body asked for lunch at one and supper at seven and told you plainly when each was done. It is not for your appetite. Appetite does not count weekdays. The rule is for the tug-of-war inside your head between the part that wants the dose and the part that fears the dose. The rule promises to satisfy both. A little permission to quiet the wanting. A little limit to quiet the fear. Everyone around you calls that balance. Everyone around you believes a kept rule proves control.

Did the rule quiet either voice in your week.

The wanting did not quiet. It learned the timetable. It knocked at half past three on Monday and at half past two on Tuesday and at eleven on Wednesday, earlier each day, louder each day, because a rationed dose teaches the mouth to wait and to watch the clock. The fear did not quiet either. It kept the ledger. Borrowed from Thursday. Owe Friday. Start again Monday. I have been good this week. I will start tomorrow. You know that dialect by heart because you have spoken it for years. Just a treat. Once a week is fine. One sweet thing a day. Special occasions. Each phrase sounded like control at noon and read like permission at midnight.

We have all lived that ledger. I have lived it. I laid out my own neat rows and set my own kind limits and told myself this time was different because this time the number was smaller. The number was never the point. The bargain was the point. I will keep the sweet but tame it. I will keep the pleasure but lose the cost. It feels like wisdom. It feels like maturity. The adult between the abstainer and the binge eater, steering the middle course.

Ask what the middle course carried you to by Thursday. Not by S-day. By Thursday. If the rule proved control, why did the drawer hang open two days early. If moderation were control, why did the most controlled week you can remember still end with the tub and the pizza and the sentence about starting again.

You did not break the rule. The rule broke you against its own timetable. That is our subject. Not your weakness. The bargain.

Why The Rule Breaks By Thursday

There is a sentence I want you to hear in your own voice, because you have said it or thought it a hundred times without noticing its two halves.

Anything sweet was forbidden in my mind, and after I ate what I knew was too much I thought what is the point anymore.

Hear the two halves click together like a lock.

First half. Forbidden. The rule makes the sweet precious. The drawer makes the drawer glow. The S-day makes Saturday shimmer from Tuesday onward. What is counted becomes coveted. What is rationed becomes radiant. The sweet you barely noticed when it sat in the office kitchen all day becomes magnificent when it is your one and you must make it last until tomorrow. That is not your character reaching for the magnificent. That is the deprivation inside the bargain manufacturing value that was never in the bite.

Second half. What is the point anymore. The hopelessness after the slip. Once the one has become two, the ledger says the week is spoiled, the rule is blown, Monday is far away, so tonight may as well be large. The restriction manufactured the binge and the hopelessness finished it. The perfect recipe, exactly as hooked eaters describe it when they speak honestly. Forbid, slip, despair, flood.

Ask yourself how many Thursdays ran that recipe while you blamed Thursday.

How many weeks did the weekday restraint manufacture the weekend release. How many S-days that began as desserts twice ended as desserts twice plus the late box plus the Monday vow. How many I do not keep it in the house weeks ended in the shop on Thursday buying enough for a house of six. You called each collapse a failure of yours. Read back to front, each collapse was a success of the form. Intermittent sweet, held back then released, the one-treat yo-yo, the weekday rule and the weekend flood, produces bingeing where steady eating does not. The schedule manufactures the loop.

Hear how precisely that fits your drawer, because your drawer is that schedule in miniature.

Monday restraint. Tuesday watch. Wednesday borrow. Thursday flood. Weekend permission. Monday vow. Restraint then release then restraint. The very rhythm that teaches the mouth to escalate. Given steady access without counting, the mouth nibbles and stops. Given one special hour then nothing then one special hour again, the mouth learns to take more in the hour, faster in the hour, with more noise in the hour, because the hour may not come again. That is why Monday's one felt small and Thursday's three felt necessary. The rule did not contain the wanting. The rule trained it.

This is why I say the bargain keeps the cage, and why I say it with warmth for the person who struck the bargain. Who would not strike it. The whole street told you moderation is maturity. The whole shelf told you a little of what you fancy is wisdom. The Willpower Method told you failure means you need a stricter rule, a smaller number, a firmer drawer. You were conned, not foolish. The confidence trick was complete before you bought your first drawer tidy, and every Monday since has been the trick renewing itself in your own sensible voice.

But seen, the bargain loses its power to sound like you. Next time the sensible voice says once a week is fine, you will hear what it is. Not reasoning. Script. The Sweet Talker in a grey suit, selling the timetable that feeds the Sugar Mite its daily drip.

Why One Is Never One

We can now meet the sentence this whole book has been walking toward, the kindest and most dangerous sentence in your dialect.

Just one. One sweet thing a day. One on S-days. One to be sociable. One because it has been long enough.

Hear how innocent it sounds. It does not sound like brainwashing. It sounds like arithmetic. One is small. One is containable. One proves you are not hooked. That is why we must meet it head-on and not around the edges, because while one survives intact the trap survives intact.

Ask the sentence what it means by one.

One taste. Yes. You can handle one taste. One bite tastes of what it tastes of. No one disputes it. The laboratories and your own midnight box agree on this narrower point. The pleasure of the bite is small and fragile and fades by the middle. By the fourth the liking has thinned. By the sixth you are barely meeting what you eat.

One firing. No. You cannot handle one firing by handling one taste, because the firing does not ask your permission. One binge-like hit re-triggers the signal that lights want. One lift guarantees its dip. One dip sharpens the faint gnaw under the ribs. One guest, as you saw in the afternoon house, texts his stupid friends until the evening is full. That is not poetry. That is the second-meal pull you have lived without naming. Sweet breakfast, hungry by ten, snacking by eleven, sagging by three, raiding by dark. One did that. One opened the door.

Ask your own days for the evidence, because they will answer faster than my pages.

On the weeks the first dose arrived early, did the day stay neat or did it sprawl. On the days the packet opened at three, did the evening hold or did it lean toward the fridge light. On the nights the raid ran, did the next morning arrive level or foggy and calling for a sweet start to lift the fog the night had left. You know the answers. You lived them. One sweet guest called the rest. The loop fed itself the way a fire feeds on air.

And ask your own cupboard the question that ends the arithmetic for good.

If one were ever one, where are your half-packages. Show me the drawer with four of Monday's five still sleeping in a row on Friday. Show me the S-day tub with one spoon gone and the lid back on. Show me the bar with one square missing, kept for months. Look in your kitchen. Does that kitchen exist. Or does every opened thing finish, every rule need renewing, every one need its next.

One is too much because it is never enough. You have never said that last one aloud, but you have lived it in the dark with crumbs on you. The first bites heavenly, the rest a chase after the first, the packet ending the meal because your body never closed it. Once triggered, the craving carries the binge. That carry is not your character. It is the signature of the loop. Escalation over time is the signature. More in the first hour than before. More to feel less. The family size where one bar used to close the evening. Tolerance in plain clothes, exactly as you described it before you had a name for it when you said it does not hit like it used to.

It is the other way around. One is never one because one reopens the loop that makes one feel like never enough.

This is not a scolding. There is warmth here for the person who believed the sentence, because the sentence was sold to you as freedom. A little freedom each day. A little joy kept. Who would not buy that. But the little freedom was the cage with the door left ajar so you would walk back in daily to prove you could walk out. A cage you re-enter daily is not moderation. It is tenancy.

The Cliff With The Kind Limit

Picture the cliff at the edge of town, the one the children dare each other to jump from in summer.

A sensible man arrives with a clipboard and a kind face. He does not tell you jumping is safe. He tells you jumping is manageable provided you observe the limit. You may jump off the cliff, he says, provided you fall no more than a few metres. One small fall a day. Weekends, two. Special cliffs, as many as you like because the view is lovely. He marks the air with his pencil a few metres down. Land above this line and you prove control.

You laugh, and you should laugh, because the kind limit is absurd. The fall does not negotiate with the pencil. The drop is the drop. A few metres is still the cliff doing what cliffs do. The only way to observe his limit is never to step off, and the only honest sentence is the one he will not write on his clipboard. Do not jump.

That is the moderation bargain in one picture. Keep it and you will see why this book insists on totality, and why the insistence is wonderful news rather than doom.

BAD SUGAR is the cliff. The dose is the step off. The kind limit, one a day, weekends only, special ones kept, is the pencil mark in mid-air. The fall, the firing and the dip and the gnaw and the texts to the stupid friends, does not negotiate with your number. It runs its course once the step is taken. The weekday rule and the S-day and the borrowed half and the balanced Friday are all pencil marks. The drop laughs at pencils.

Ask the pincer question that closes every pencil mark for good, because it has only one honest answer whichever claw you take.

If there is a genuine benefit in doing it often, why do you spend your weeks trying to do it rarely. And if there is no benefit in doing it often, what is the point of doing it occasionally either.

Take the first claw. If the sweet truly carried you, truly paid you, truly loved you back, rationing it would be perverse. You do not ration air or water or sleep or laughter. You do not lay out five breaths for five days. The very existence of your drawer confesses what your mouth still denies. There is no good in it worth having often.

Take the second claw. If there is no good in it often, the occasional one keeps the belief alive for nothing. One kept sweet means the Sweet Talker keeps his vote. One kept ritual means the Mite keeps its timetable. One kept exception means the brainwashing that says treat and comfort keeps its embassy in your head. You pay the full price of the trap, the watching and the wanting and the Thursday flood, to keep a pleasure that inspection showed was relief from the last dose.

There is the totality logic landed without scolding and without gloom. Total cessation of BAD SUGAR is the only stable state, not because you are weak and need a wall, but because the thing offers nothing worth keeping occasionally once it offers nothing worth keeping often. The cliff does not need a kinder pencil. It needs no jumpers.

Hear the relief inside that severity, because severity without relief would be the old method talking.

You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. That sentence sounded outrageous in the first chapter when the treat still glittered. Read it now beside the open drawer. What does the rule ask you to lose. The counting. The borrowing. The Thursday wreckage. The S-day flood. The Monday vow. The tug-of-war between wanting and fear that never quieted no matter how sensible the number. That is the loss. A cage with kind limits. And what do you gain. The end of negotiation itself. No drawer to police. No pencil to obey. No Thursday to dread. All you have to do is follow all the instructions, and the instruction at the hinge of this chapter is the simplest you have been given since we began.

9. REFUSE THE MODERATION BARGAIN.
One is never one.

Carry That Seeing Into The Shop

You stand now where the midnight box left you, and the ground has hardened usefully underfoot. Hunger trusted. Satisfaction heard. The rescue seen as the perpetrator. The house party seen hiding in a single guest. The drawer seen as the timetable. Keep that seeing with you into the days ahead, because the bargains crowd thickest where the rule used to protect you.

When the bright thought arrives in the shop queue, one for the road, run it back to front before it runs you. What low is this lift relieving, and which dose dug that low. What fire is this hose answering, and who held the matches that morning. What texts will this one guest send, and do I want the house full tonight. What will Thursday say about Monday's arithmetic. Asked once, honestly, in your own aisle with your own hand halfway out, the inversion holds. The fix fires the want and the dip demands the next. There is nothing to handle in one bite. There is only a loop to stop re-entering.

And hear the kind voice for what it is when it returns, because it will return in your own tone.

I can keep just the special ones. I can do S-days only. I can keep it out of the house and be safe. Each one sounds like you at your calmest. Each one is the Talker knocking with his clipboard, pencil ready, offering a kinder fall. You will know him now. Oh. There you are again. You set the fire on Monday and now you are selling the hose for Thursday.

Let the evening speak what the drawer never let it say. The weeks you remember as happy were not happy because the S-day tub was large. They were happy because the people were near and the leisure was real and the meal closed clean. The spoon was only sneaking a ride. The weeks you remember as ashamed were not ashamed because you are broken. They were the bargain running its rails. Restraint manufacturing release. Release manufacturing vow. Vow manufacturing restraint. Round and round, exactly as the trap was built to run, exactly as your own Thursdays testified before you knew what they testified.

Do not mistake this chapter for hardness. It is the kindest chapter so far, though it speaks sternly, because it returns to you the hours the negotiation stole. Think of the mental rent you have paid for years. The counting. The planning. The hiding. The balancing. The starting again. The whole whispering bureaucracy of one. Totality evicts that bureaucracy in a stroke. No number to remember. No line to patrol. No pencil falling from a great height. Real food stays fully yours. Whole fruit stays fully yours. Meals eaten as meals stay fully yours, watched and enjoyed and closed. What goes is only BAD SUGAR, the boxed and bottled and engineered sweet that never fed you, only fired you. Eat what you love from what is left, and the drawer can hold pens again.

We meet next the swap that says a cleaner sweet escapes the form, and the delay that says tomorrow is kinder than today. We meet them with the loop running plainly before us and no rule left to hide behind. The schedule, not your character, said one becomes the box. The cliff, not your courage, says the kind limit cannot hold.

SUMMARY

- The Monday drawer proved the trap rather than control. Counting, borrowing, balancing and S-days are the behaviour of a person negotiating rations, not enjoying a pleasure.
- Restriction plus hopelessness manufactures the binge. The rule makes the sweet precious, the slip then reads as a spoiled week, and the spoiled week floods. Forbid, slip, despair, flood.
- The schedule manufactures the loop. Restraint-then-release teaches the mouth to take more in the hour, faster in the hour, which is why Monday's one felt small and Thursday's three felt necessary.
- One is never one because one reopens the loop that makes one feel like never enough. One taste is containable but one firing re-triggers want, guarantees its dip, and texts the stupid friends until the house is full.
- Moderation is the kind man with a pencil at the cliff, permitting the jump provided the fall is small. The fall does not negotiate. If there is no benefit often, there is no point occasionally.
- Total cessation of BAD SUGAR is the only stable state. What goes is only the engineered sweet. Real meals and whole fruit stay fully yours, and the end of negotiation is gain, not loss.
