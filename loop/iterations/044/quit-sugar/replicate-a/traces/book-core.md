## 1. Book core

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of refined/added sugar and junk carbs — the craving–snacking loop and its grip, not nutrition pedantry.

**Reader (planner-facing state):** An adult who eats sweet treats daily, calls it a sweet tooth or a deserved break, has run moderation rules, cheat days, sugar-free swaps and Monday restarts and watched each collapse into the box, the drawer or the night kitchen, and suspects the approach was wrong rather than the person.

**Load-bearing false belief:** Bad sugar is a pleasure, a treat, a fuel and a comfort that makes life sweeter, and life without it would be deprivation.

**Through-line:** The lift is the low. Every perceived gift of bad sugar — energy, reward, love, taste — is relief from a discomfort bad sugar itself created; seen once, the treat stands exposed as the trap, and leaving it is escape, not sacrifice.

**Format:** Full-length Easyway book, 65200 words, 18 cards (front matter + 14 argument chapters + testimony bridge + vow + ordinary life + short recap). Long testimony in the main flow in its own room. Short photographable instruction recap to close. Raw citations quarantined to back matter; the scare stays in the argument.

**Redefinition (Good-X / Bad-X line):** Eating cannot be quit. BAD SUGAR can. Boxed in Ch 1, CAPS name carries the definition in every sentence thereafter.
> BAD SUGAR = refined/added free sugars plus the fast junk carbs that behave like them in the loop: sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, pastries, sugary drinks and juices, sweetened yoghurts and cereals, white bread–pizza–chips binges eaten as sweets, and honey/syrup/agave/diet-sweet swaps used to keep the sweet hit alive. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy, grains eaten as meals, and real food cooked at home are not the target and are never counted.
**Margin-for-error doctrine:** The body can cope with an occasional accidental blip; the mind cannot cope with a deliberate exception. A slip revives nothing unless the Sweet Talker is let back in to rename it permission. Guard the belief, not the behavior. The buffer is a seatbelt: there for accidents, never for driving erratically.

**Clinical / eating-disorder safety perimeter:** General adult edition. Not medical nutrition therapy, not diabetes management advice, not eating-disorder treatment, not a weight-loss program. Readers on medication, with diabetes, with a history of eating disorders, pregnant, or under medical supervision involve their professional for any bodily specifics; the book argues belief only. One plan-wide advisory (CA-SAFE) printed once in front matter; one plain spoken doctor sentence on the vow card only. No instruction carries a clinical tail.

**Fork decisions (style-guide §4, Carr defaults executed exactly as Carr practices them):**
- **Fork 1 — inner monster: FULL CARR PERSONIFICATION.** Two original behavior-fitted mechanism characters, frozen as mantras: the trivial physical creature THE SUGAR MITE, starved to death in days, whose death throes are revelled in; and the belief-system that feeds it THE SWEET TALKER, the lifelong brainwashing voice that says treat/fuel/comfort. Craving is external, small, already dying — never a mighty enemy demanding willpower.
- **Fork 2 — outcome: TOTAL CESSATION OF BAD SUGAR, COMMANDED WITH CHEERFUL CERTAINTY.** Follow ALL the instructions is the contract, never apologized for. Moderation foreclosed with the pincer (if there is no benefit in doing it often, there is no point doing it occasionally either) and the cliff-jump image. Whole fruit and real food remain fully permitted; the line is the redefinition, not abstinence from eating.
- **Fork 3 — science weight: HARD FACTS AT FULL CARR FORCE, THEN RELIEF.** Obesity, teeth, heart/stroke, liver load, mood linkage stated flat as settled fact where the plan assigns them, then the reader is told not to change from fear. Facts arrive as "The fact is…", never as hedged citations. The scare is never sent to an appendix.
- **Fork 4 — villain: TWO VILLAINS, BOTH HIT HARD.** The engineered food industry that built bliss-point, vanishing-density, everywhere-availability treats to grab stomach share; and the named anti-method THE WILLPOWER METHOD with its diets, rules, swaps and restarts that kept the reader in the trap. Contempt for both; warmth for the reader always.
- **Fork 5 — void: CHANGE NOTHING ELSE IN YOUR LIFE.** The body returns to its natural baseline on its own; the reader inhabits every old context confidently to prove freedom. No replacement system, no void-filling program. Hunger, satisfaction and real food as favourite are encountered as authority, not taught as curriculum.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene:** The birthday table — a molten chocolate dessert set laughing in the middle of the family, everyone lit warm, the reader lifting the spoon as love made edible. Met head-on late; every drop of its pleasure reassigned to the people, the leisure, the evening — the spoon was only sneaking a ride.

**Destination state:** Whenever the thought of BAD SUGAR crosses the mind, the reader feels relief and gaiety that they no longer eat it — free from the first day of freedom, happy to be free for the rest of their life, pitying (never envying) eaters, never reopening the decision, forgiving a slip as a warning without licensing a repeat.

**Saved ending reframe (appears only in the final movement):** The empty dessert plate is not an absence at the table. It is proof the host is finally home.

**Moment-of-revelation prediction:** About the third week, the reader walks past the breakroom plate, pours tea, sits to work steady through the afternoon, and only at dusk realizes they forgot to want anything — then the family celebration where they dance through pudding and remember the figs they forgot to taste, because the evening outshone the course.
