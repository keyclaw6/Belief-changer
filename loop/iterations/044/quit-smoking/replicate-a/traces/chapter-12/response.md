Chapter 12

THE LAST ORDINARY CIGARETTE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the evening lane with hedges and kerbs, the pack with two left, the watched light, the shut door, the thought that means gladness.

*You close nothing you ever needed. You shut a door that only ever took.*

You have lived a Tuesday with full hands. Coffee woke without paper. The corridor turned on legs and air. Dinner closed on hunger answered. The dusk lane held on trot and lamps and cool air. You saw the night give while paper billed. You saw the day itemise plain what built each hour and what only ever charged for building.

A smaller voice speaks now, and I know it well because we all hear it at this kerb.

It says you understand it all in the head but the hand still shakes at the finish. It says reading about the shut door is one thing and shutting it is another. It says tonight is not the night, tomorrow will be kinder, one more pack will soften the parting.

I tell you plainly from your own Tuesday that the voice is not you. It is the last whisper of old weather before clear air, and clear air is already here.

ARE YOU CHAMPING AT THE BIT

I ask you straight, as one smoker to another who has stood where you stand. Do you feel ready to shut it. Are you champing at the bit to finish. Does the thought of never tending another burn bring a lift in the chest that has nothing to do with fright.

If the answer is yes, read on with both eyes open, because tonight is yours.

If the answer is no, if there is a drag in the belly and a wish to keep one corner back, do not push forward on grit. Put this book down and read it once more from the start. The drag means one room has not yet done its work, not that you lack anything in character. We never force a door that still looks like a friend. We look again until the friend shows as the biller, and then the hand shuts it gladly. No scolding here. No clock ticking. The lane will wait while you see whole.

You say you fear the craving will start the minute you say this is it. I have heard that fear from smokers of twenty a day and smokers of forty, from doorway hiders and dawn coughers and midnight drivers. We all learned to read the minute of decision as the minute the hunger begins. We said the vow itself calls the tug.

Watch that minute from your own evenings and the reading falls apart.

What calls the tug. The bell rang every hour long before this book, vow or no vow. The vow adds no wire to the bell. Did it ever ring softer for the smoker who promised tomorrow. Your own years answer without my voice raised. The bell rang on its own clock. Naming never wound it tighter.

You say a part of you still loves the light. I carried that love through eleven failures, and Tuesday has already answered it hour by hour. The cup warmed. Talk knit. Food sealed. Paper stood beside the three and sent the bill.

Look at tonight plain and you have nothing to lose and everything to gain from seeing it to its end. Count what stays. The coffee stays. The bread stays. The corridor mates stay. The soup stays. The files stay. The mince and the onions stay. The dog stays. The lamps stay. The kerb breath stays. Count what goes. Ash goes. Cough goes. Tending goes. Paying goes. Hunting the open kiosk goes. Counting the pack goes. Splitting the knot goes. The arithmetic is not close. It is a landslide, and the landslide runs all one way.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You have followed them this far with open eyes. One stands between you and the shut door, and it is the kindest of the set.

TWO LEFT IN THE PACK

Take the evening lane tonight with the dog and the pack as I describe it, because this walk is not like the night corner with drink and talk and offered ends. That corner is settled and gone. This lane is its own kerbs and hedges, its own bins out for morning, its own cat on the wall, its own lamps humming on one by one. No foil torn open by another hand. No buzz lent by the glass. Nothing here to blur the watching. The lane holds you and the dog and the last of the supply, and the supply is two.

Carry two and no more. No carton on the side. No farewell stock laid in. We do no binge farewell here. Two tells the truth without theatre. The dog pulls glad at the gate and settles to a trot. Houses glow one by one. The lead hangs loose in the hand that used to divide itself between lead and burn.

Walk the first stretch with no light. Let the legs swing loose after dinner. Let the cool air rinse the kitchen warmth from the face. Let the dog read the hedges nose low and tail high. Let the mind run the day back start to finish, coffee and corridor and plate, and find each hour whole. Notice how the hands belong to the lead alone. Notice how the eyes stay up for lamps and cats and the fox at the alley mouth. Notice how the chest takes the lane air down deep without tending. We believed legs needed company of paper on this lane, and the lane proves legs keep their own company with the dog.

If you take medication or live with any medical or mental-health condition, speak to your doctor about stopping and follow their advice.

That sentence stands once and plain outside the argument, the way a kerb stands outside the walk, there to be stepped on without breaking stride. The rest of this lane is belief and attention, and attention does the closing.

That weather of stories that taught you to hear the tug as hunger for a friend was the Big Fog, and tonight you walk out from under it with eyes open. Name it once as you walk and leave it behind you on the pavement like a dropped receipt. The hedges do not believe it. The lamps do not believe it. The dog does not believe it. Only the old weather said they needed smoke to be walked past gladly, and weather clears.

Check the pocket at the corner where the car passes. Feel the pack thin with two inside. In the trap years the pocket ordered the lane. Tonight the pocket asks nothing. The lane decides. The thin pack rides along like a letter to be posted, not like a master to be obeyed.

Stop at the bench past the alley mouth where the hedges run high. Take out the pack and weigh it in the palm. A day's supply of doses in paper jackets. We believed the gold and the white meant choice, and the palm proves the weight is paper around a dose that only ever topped its own fall.

SMOKE IT WITH OPEN EYES

Light one now, the second to last, and smoke it the way the patio audit taught, with full attention and no company to lend it glow. This is not a treat. This is a watching. Draw and watch what follows the way you watched coffee and talk and food.

Mark the flick. The lighter flares and dies. The end glows and dirties. The first draw scratches faint and the chest tightens to take it. The tongue takes ash over the dinner that still lingers. The nose takes sting over the lane air that ran clean a minute ago. The dog sneezes once and steps aside from the cloud. The lamp shows the cloud thin and blue and gone in three seconds. Three seconds of throat bite for an hour of tending. We called those seconds heaven, and the lane grades them without flattery.

Ask from the bench without my voice raised. Did the draw swing the legs. Did it cool the face after steam. Did it deepen the talk that is not even here to be deepened. Did it add one lamp to the street or one wag to the tail. Your own feet know. Legs swung before the flick. Air cooled before the flick. The lane stood whole before the flick. Paper built none and billed the standing.

Smoke it to its middle and watch the middle. The glow creeps. The ash lengthens. The hand divides again between lead and burn. The eyes drop from hedges to ember length. We believed dividing doubled the walk, and dividing thinned it. Whole attention keeps the lane. Split attention keeps the ash.

Smoke it to its end and watch the end. The draw grows hot and harsh. The taste sours to wet cardboard. The mouth dries. The last draw is stubbed underfoot with a hiss on damp pavement. Look at it lying there while the lamp lights it plain. That sodden end is what the bill bought.

Now breathe the kerb breath the book has taught your body to trust. While the car passes let one deep pull of night air go down to the bottom of the chest and out slow. Mark how the chest opens past the grit. Mark how the shoulders drop. Mark how the dog sits glad beside you and pants. We believed that long exhale belonged to paper, and the kerb proves the breath belonged to lungs left to their work. One clean pull grades the lane better than years of talk about lanes.

Leave the stub where it lies. Walk on with one left and feel how little the hand hunts for it once the watching is done. The flick kept nothing. The middle kept nothing. The end kept nothing. What joins never broke the lane. What broke the lane never joined.

You will hear a small clerk voice as you walk. It says the smoked one was bad but the last one will be good. Hear that clerk kindly and set the tally down. Did any dose of twenty years ever give what the clerk promises for the next. Your own bench has answered inside the hour. A watched dose cannot be mourned because mourning needs haze and haze cannot stand watching.

CLOSE THE DOOR

Walk the home stretch with the last dose riding thin and the house coming back into view. Windows warm. The lead loose. Each hour stands ahead in its own clothes, wanted for itself. The hunger for those hours sits clean in the chest.

Stand at your own gate. Take out the last one and look at it whole in the lamplight. We believed that neatness meant harmless company, and the bench proved neatness wraps a dose that only ever rents back its own leaving. It steadied nothing that legs and air had not steadied. It sealed nothing that food had not sealed.

Light it and finish it with the same open eyes, not to enjoy it but to close it. Let the ugliness stand unblurred to the stub. No deep farewell pulls. A last ordinary dose smoked ordinary and found ordinary-ugly, stubbed ordinary underfoot by the gate. The supply stands at none.

Speak the vow now standing at the gate with the dog at your heel and the house warm before you. Speak it aloud into the cool air so the ears hear what the mind decides. I pin no borrowed poetry on you. Say it in your own plain words that carry one meaning: never another dose, not one puff, not one borrowed end, not one kept special.

I spoke mine like this at my own gate after twenty-two years, with rain on the skylight behind me and mince on the stove inside: I have seen it whole and I shut the door. The trap had me. It has me no longer.

Say yours. Say it once. Mean it the way Tuesday meant it, hour by hour, not the way fright means it for a night.

12. SMOKE THE LAST ONE AND CLOSE THE DOOR
You are free the moment it goes out.

Put it out and know what the sentence means while the smoke still thins. No waiting week. No trial month. No stair of days to climb before the name fits. The bell loses its payer the moment the payer stops paying. The weather clears the moment the weather is seen. The door shuts the moment the hand shuts it. Congratulations. You have done the only hard-looking thing in this book, which was looking, and looking is done.

Crush the pack in the palm and hear the cardboard give. The foil tears. The empty jackets crack. Drop the crushed pack in the bin by the gate where bins stand out for morning. Hear it land on peel and paper with a light sound. That light sound is the heaviest weight of years leaving the pocket. The pocket stands thin and quiet against the leg. The leg walks on without orders. Picture mornings with hands busy at the kettle for the rest of your life and the count gone, the pocket holding keys and coins, the bin holding the crushed gold, the lane holding the two stubs already washing flat in rain. That picture is not a distant prize for suffering. It is what remains when the bill stops being paid.

Go in with the dog and coil the lead on its hook. The dog drinks long at the bowl and circles to sleep with a sigh that runs the length of the body. The plates stand on the rack where dinner left them, still faintly warm at the rim. The kitchen holds its small order and waits for your hands. Wash the ash from the fingers with soap and hot water and smell the soap stay. Rinse the mouth with cold water and taste water stay. Stand at the window over the sink and look at the dark street and one lit window across the way. The house narrows to lamplight and talk low from the next room. The shut door sounds whole tonight. Whole doors need no seal past their own click.

THE THOUGHT THAT MEANS GLADNESS

I tell you now what to do with the thought when it comes, because come it will, and its coming has frightened more quitters than any tug.

Tomorrow the kettle will click and a thought will cross like a familiar stepping through the kitchen: a dose with this coffee. The day after, the corridor knot will loosen and the thought will cross: a dose at this hinge. The week after, a plate will be pushed forward and the thought will cross: a dose to seal this. In the weeks after, the same old hours will send it crossing again, faint as breath on glass.

We were taught to read that crossing as danger, as proof the want lives on, as the start of the slide. We said a thought means desire. We said desire means doom. We said the only safety is never to think it, and then we thought it and called ourselves lost.

The fact is the crossing proves the opposite once the door stands shut.

Only a shut door can be knocked on. Only an unfed bell can ring faint through a closed door. The thought crosses because old wiring fires once in its old hour, not because the hour needs paper. It arrives uninvited, thin, brief, gone by the next task if left alone. It carries no order. It asks no obedience. It is a knock, not a command. A knock on a shut door proves the door stands shut.

Do you want proof from your own lane. When the bench dose burned, did the hedges ask for another. When the stub hissed, did the dog ask for another. When the kerb breath opened, did the chest ask for another. Your own walk answers without my voice raised. Nothing in the lane asked. Only old wiring knocked once, and knocking is not needing.

So take the knock for what it is and answer it with the gladness it has earned. Do not argue with it. Do not push it down. Do not count its visits on a calendar. Let it come and meet it with one spoken sentence inside the chest, said the way Tuesday was lived, plain and whole.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it the first time now at the sink with soap still on the fingers, so the mouth learns the shape before the morning. Say it aloud into the small kitchen and hear how different it sounds from the old clerk tally. The clerk counted loss. This counts gain. The clerk billed. This receipts. The clerk mourned a friend. This greets a shut door.

Say it tomorrow at the kettle when the coffee thought crosses. The cup steams. The bread browns. The tug thins while you drink. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The sentence fits the kitchen because the kitchen proved it.

Say it in the corridor when the hinge thought crosses. Legs loosen. Air clears. Company knits. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The sentence fits the knot because the knot proved it.

Say it at the table when the seal thought crosses. Hunger answers. Flavours lay down. Fullness sends its plain signal. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The sentence fits the plate because the plate proved it.

Say it on the lane when the company thought crosses. Legs swing. Air rinses. The dog pulls glad. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The sentence fits the hedges because the hedges proved it.

Each saying does two jobs at once. It names the knock as knock, not need, and it turns the knock into a bell for gladness. The thought that used to open the pack now shuts the door tighter. The wiring fires and the gladness answers, and after a few answers the wiring learns new work. Mornings come with the sentence ready. Hinges come with the sentence ready. Meals come with the sentence ready. Lanes come with the sentence ready. What arrived as a threat leaves as a reminder, and reminders thin faster than threats.

Keep that sentence for whenever paper crosses the mind. Keep it word for word and loud inside. Never trim it. Never soften it. Never swap its shout for a sigh. Its power sits in arriving exactly the same every time, so it fires unasked after a while the way the old bell fired. One fixed reply beats a hundred reasoned replies, because the door needs a bolt, not a debate.

You ask if one smoked in weakness later would prove the door never shut. I answer from your own stubs, not from scolding. A stub picked up proves only that old wiring knocked louder once, not that the lane needs paper. The shut door stands unless you rehang the belief that paper gives. Guard the belief and the door guards itself. Never puff to test the bolt. Testing hangs the bell back on its hook. Leave the bolt alone and let the sentence do the guarding.

SUMMARY

- Tuesday lived whole proves the hours build themselves, and the shut door keeps what Tuesday built.
- Two doses watched with open eyes show ash and tending and grit, and watching leaves nothing to mourn.
- The vow spoken once at the gate shuts the supply at none, and the shutting holds from the moment the last dose goes out.
- A returning thought is a knock on a shut door, not an order, and one fixed glad reply turns every knock tighter.
- The Big Fog weather clears when seen, and the lane ahead runs on legs and lamps and talk and food.
- Tonight holds the kettle and the plates and the dog and the bed, and tomorrow keeps each hour in its own place for the rest of your life.
