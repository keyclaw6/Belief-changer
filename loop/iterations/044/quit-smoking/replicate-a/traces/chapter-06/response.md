Chapter 6

HEAVEN THAT ENDS IN SHAME

IN THIS CHAPTER: the back step at dusk, the lighter flick, the first draw, the glass ashtray, the bathroom mirror, and the drawer pack kept from sight.

*Heaven that ends in shame inside the same minute was never heaven, only relief misnamed.*

You have watched the finished work keep its warmth while the draw stood beside the claim. Keep that watched day beside you, because this chapter tests the defence you hold most dear. Not the pressure hour. Not the small finishes. The light itself. The second you would defend if every other reason fell.

Evening brings the back step.

You push the door with your shoulder and the cool air meets the face. The yard holds its fences and its washing lines and the neighbour television through brick. The kettle still ticks behind you in the kitchen. Your fingers find the pack in the apron pocket with no vote taken. You tap one out and set the pack on the step. You flick the lighter and the flame stands upright in the dusk. You shield it with the palm. The tip catches and glows. You draw.

The first draw pulls warm across the tongue and down. The shoulders drop a fraction. The chest loosens a notch. The noise in the head quiets for a breath. The dusk looks kinder for a blink. Your hand rests on the knee with the stick balanced between the fingers. The smoke goes up past the gutter and thins.

I know that step. I stood on it for years with the lighter already warm from the pocket. We smokers of cold evenings all learned that flame cupped in the palm. The door half open. The kitchen light behind. The first pull. We called the minute by its brightest second, and we believed the name told the truth about the whole.

Stay on the step with me, because the whole argument of this chapter lives inside one lit minute and the chapter will not leave that minute until the minute has spoken.

THE FLICK AND THE FIRST DRAW

Take the minute apart slowly, because the eye misses the trick when it hurries past the only part you ever loved.

The pack lies on the step with its cellophane torn at the corner. The foil shows the torn mouth where the fingers dig. The stick sits white between your fingers with its filter firm at the lip. The lighter carries its thumb wheel worn smooth from years of flicks. The flame rises yellow with its blue root. The tip crackles faintly as the paper takes. The first smoke rolls warm over the tongue with its dry edge and its familiar bite. The throat takes it with a small catch that you learned to read as arrival. The chest takes it deeper on the second pull. The shoulders lower. The jaw unclenches. The hands steady.

That is the heaven you defend. I do not mock it. I stood where you stand and I felt what you feel. The minute narrows to the pulls and the pulls narrow to the loosening. The day has pressed and nagged and the step gives you a doorway out of the kitchen noise. The flame answers the thumb. The draw answers the flame. Something in the chest answers the draw. Call it what you called it for years. Heaven. I will not take the word from your mouth before we have timed what the word covers.

Watch what the word covers, because the word covers seconds and the minute keeps the books.

The loosening arrives with the first two draws. Name its length honestly from your own step. It does not arrive as a slow afternoon that builds. It arrives as a sharp softening that peaks while the tip still glows bright. Before the ash has grown a fingernail, the brightest part has already passed. By the middle of the stick the draw tastes thinner and hotter. The tongue finds the dry edge turning sour. The throat finds the catch turning rough. The chest holds less loosening with each pull. By the last third you draw from habit toward the filter while the mind has already left the step for the washing up and the keys and the morning. The stick that opened as heaven now burns as duty toward the butt.

Look down at the glass ashtray while the minute closes, because the ashtray keeps what the heaven kept.

The ash lies grey with its black rim where the paper burned uneven. The butt shows the brown stain creeping up the filter. The smoke still threads from the pressed end with its stale tail. The fingers carry the smell into the kitchen when you lift the kettle. The tongue carries the sour film into the tea. The throat carries the small burn into the night cough. Ask what part of the heaven stayed past the pressing out. The glow died. The warmth died. The loosening died within minutes of the lighting. What stayed was ash and smell and film, carried forward while the heaven stayed behind in the first draws.

I speak your defence in your own words here, so you know I hear it before we test it.

"the moment I light up I'm in heaven, but feel shitty afterwards. So whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker"

I do not mock that sentence. I lived inside it for years. It feels true in the hands because the first half is true in the body. The light does bring a rush of softening. The after does bring a sinking. The love does fasten on the light. The hate does fasten on the smoker who must carry the after. This chapter honours the first half fully while it tests what the first half proves. The heaven is real as a feeling. The question is what the feeling belongs to.

WHAT YOU DEFEND

Stay with the love a while longer, because the love deserves a fair hearing before the audit and a fair hearing makes the verdict stand.

You love the flick. The thumb learns the wheel the way the hand learns a tap. The flame answers in wind. The cupped palm makes a small room for fire in the open air. There is craft in it after twenty years. The tap of the pack. The choice of the straight one. The turn of the stick between the lips. The first crackle. We smokers practised that craft ten thousand times until the craft felt like comfort. I loved the craft too. The hands knew their work and the work quieted the hands.

You love the first draw. The smoke comes warm where the evening came cool. The tongue meets its old bite the way the ear meets an old song. The chest meets the pull the way the bed meets the back at night. For two or three pulls the world narrows kindly. The quarrel from lunch thins. The bills on the dresser thin. The morning cough thins from memory. The step holds only flame and breath and dusk. If heaven means a brief softening after pressure, then the step gives you its small4456 heaven and the name feels earned.

You love the pause the light buys. The kitchen can wait. The phone can wait. The talk can wait. The stick gives you a reason to stand outside the noise with your own breath for company. We smokers called that pause ours. I called it mine on every step and every kerb and every car bonnet from here to the coast. The pause felt like kindness in a day that gave little.

Ask what the love fastens on, because the love fastens on seconds and the seconds need timing before they can testify.

Did you ever love the tenth draw the way you loved the first two. Did you ever hold the middle of the stick to the light and praise its taste. Did you ever finish a stick and sit back fed the way a meal feeds, with the body quiet for hours. You know the answer from ten thousand steps. The love never spread past the lighting. It lived in the flick and the first pulls and died before the filter browned. A love that dies before the butt cools needs a harder look than praise, because the dying tells you where the love lived.

Think of the smoker you were at the start, because the start remembers what the middle forgot. The first sticks tasted foul and the head swam and the throat burned and the eyes watered. No one called that heaven. We coughed through the school wall and the park bench and the cold bus shelter and kept at it until the cough learned manners. The heaven story arrived years later, after the body learned to read the softening as pleasure. We built a heaven out of a softening. The softening taught us where our joys supposedly lived. The softening needs cross-examining, because softening and enjoyment share a minute but not a source.

THE SHAME INSIDE THE SAME MINUTE

Now watch the same minute to its end, because the shame you already feel arrives faster than memory admits and the speed accuses without my voice raised.

The stick still burns between your fingers while the aftertaste gathers. The tongue finds the sour film under the bite. The lips find the dry cling. The nose finds the smoke turning from warm to stale in the nostrils. The throat clears once, then again. The chest holds a faint heaviness where the loosening stood. The hand that loved the flick now plans the hiding. Pocket the pack before going in. Wash the hands before the child climbs up. Chew the mint before the meeting. Spray the coat before the car lift. The minute that opened with flame now closes with housekeeping against discovery.

Walk with me to the bathroom mirror, because the mirror keeps what the step hides.

You press the butt out in the glass dish and the thread of smoke rises accusingly straight. You step inside and shut the door softly so no one hears the tap running. You look at the face that loved the light. The eyes look a fraction duller. The skin looks a fraction greyer in the strip light. The teeth show their yellow rim when you pull the lip. The hair carries the smell when you lift a strand. You run the water cold and wash the hands twice. You brush the teeth hard and the paste cannot lift the film. You breathe into the palm and test it and look away. The shame does not arrive at midnight when the chest tightens. It arrives while the butt still smoulders in the dish outside. Heaven at the lighter. Hiding at the tap. Both inside five minutes.

Hear the hate in your own sentence again, because the hate testifies alongside the love and both need hearing.

You said you hate being a smoker while you love smoking. Ask what that split means from the mirror. The love fastens on seconds of softening. The hate fastens on everything else the minute carries. The money burned for the softening. The smell worked into the curtains and the car seats. The cough kept through winter. The stairs that wind. The drawer pack kept from a partner. The morning throat that needs clearing before speech. The small lies told at work about the long break. The fear kept quiet at the chest pain. The love owns the lighting. The hate owns the day that holds the lighting. Count the minutes each owns. Seconds for the love. Hours for the hate. That arithmetic is not my rhetoric. It is your own bathroom tap running while the dish still smoulders.

Ask yourself, and answer honestly. If the stick gave heaven, why does heaven need hiding. Do you hide a meal that fed you. Do you wash a song from your hair. Do you breathe into your palm after a walk to test whether the walk shamed you. You know the answer, and the answer holds without argument. What needs hiding after seconds of softening was never heaven. It was softening with a bill attached, and the bill arrives before the flame dies.

TIME THE HEAVEN

There is a warm feeling at the light and this chapter has honoured it. Now time it, because timing tells you what the feeling belongs to and timing cannot be argued with once seen.

The smoked dose reaches the brain in seconds and its sharp edge leaves in minutes. That is why the credited heaven arrives like a topped level rather than building like satisfaction. It lands fast. It stays briefly. Before the stick is out, the faint gnaw has already begun to gather again, the sense that something will soon be missing. A kind word warms you for hours. A finished job pleases you when you meet it again at night. A meal holds you till morning. What kind of heaven must be topped twenty times a day to keep the day in heaven.

Watch closer still, because the trap hides in the timing and the timing hides in plain sight on your own step.

We smoked through the day at our usual pace. Each stick delivered its dose and let it fade. By the back step at dusk, with the kettle still ticking behind, the level had fallen again and the body called for its top-up in the language we learned to call hunger for enjoyment. Light feet. Bright ache. A sense of due. We called that the evening calling for its heaven. The fact is the evening supplied the dusk and the pause and the cool air, and the falling level supplied the hunger on top of them. Then the next dose paused the hunger for minutes and we thanked the dose for giving heaven. We felt a debt pause and we called the pause heaven.

Light a second stick in the mind eye to be sure the first did not cheat you, because the minute keeps its own score and the score repeats.

The step sits dusk-cool. You flick again. Glow, draw, lean on the rail, gaze on the fences, tap, draw, press. The chest loosens the same fraction. The tongue sours the same way. The throat catches the same way. The shame gathers the same way at the tap. The sentence of heaven reads the same and fades the same. If the first stick gave enjoyment, the second should show the enjoyment doubled, the way a second helping doubles a meal pleasure or a second chorus doubles a song. Show me where the doubling sits. In the tongue. In the chest. In the dusk past the minutes. You cannot, because the audit runs the same both times. The dusk gives the kindness. The pause gives the quiet. The stick bills for the pause. We smokers paid the same toll twice in one evening and thanked the toll-keeper twice for the road our own step built.

Ask what the heaven belongs to, because we credited the pellet with the whole step for years.

The body loves a pause after noise. The lungs love cool air after a close kitchen. The eyes love dusk after strip light. The hands love rest after carrying. These loves predate every pack. The child who stood on the step with cocoa and watched the bats cut the dusk knew the pause without a lighter in the house. The girl who leaned on the rail after washing up and felt the cool on her forearms knew the softening before she knew the brand. The boy who stood outside the dance hall for air between songs knew the lift with empty pockets. You knew the lift at eight years old with scraped knees and no paper tube carrying your name. Did your nature change since. Did your nerves forget how to soften at dusk until smoke touched them. Did your chest lose the power to quiet at a pause until fire fed it. You know the answer. The pause is older than the lighting and larger than the lighting, and the lighting has lived off the pause age like a lodger claiming the house.

THE DAY AROUND THE HEAVEN

Now lift the eyes from the minute to the day that holds twenty such minutes, because the day accuses without raising its voice and the accusation completes the inversion.

Count the sticks honestly from your own pocket, not from my arithmetic. Morning stick at the kettle. Mid-morning stick at the railings. Lunch stick at the wall. Afternoon stick at the car. Tea stick at the step. Evening stick at the television. Night stick at the bed window. The day becomes a row of small bells, and we believed the bells rang for pleasure. The fact is the bells rang for the level, and the level wore the mask of pleasure to collect the credit. Morning stick. Midday stick. Dusk stick. The gaps between them taught us where our heavens supposedly lived. The gaps were the teacher, and the teacher measured doses and not delights.

Look at what the day pays for seconds, because the price runs the other way around from the story and the running tells you which way the cause flows.

It pays money burned for the pause. It pays smell worked into the hair washed that morning. It pays ash on the step swept that morning. It pays minutes chopped from each live hour into flick and draw and tap and wash and hide. It pays the small servitude of checking the pocket before the errand, counting the sticks before the drive, planning the evening around the shop shut, keeping the drawer pack turned from sight. Much done to the day in that chain. Nothing done for the day that the day did not already hold. The dusk gave the cool. The pause gave the quiet. The kitchen gave the tea waiting. The pellet gave the pause of its own debt and signed the whole step.

Follow the servitude past the step, because the minute bills the whole day and the day shows the bill plainly to any eye that totals it.

The morning starts with the count. Fingers tap the pack before the kettle boils. Enough for the day. Enough for the drive. Enough for the evening in. The mind runs the small arithmetic while the tea brews. Shop shut at ten. Kiosk shut at eleven. Garage open through the night at the far junction with its hard lights. We smokers ran that count ten thousand mornings and called it habit. The fact is the count was guard duty, and the guard watched a paper box the way a nurse watches a drip. The non-smoker in the same kitchen runs no count. The kettle boils. The tea pours. The day opens. Nothing missing. Nothing guarded. Our morning opened with stock-taking before speech, and we called the stock-taking pleasure.

The day bends around supply in smaller ways the eye learns to miss. The long meeting gets its break timed to the wall outside. The drive gets its window cracked in rain. The meal gets its chair nearest the door. The cinema gets its seat on the aisle for the quick slip. The visit to the non-smoking house gets its quick step into the garden between courses with apologies and lighter palmed. Each bend looks tiny alone. Total them across twenty years and the bends draw the map of the day more truly than the diary does. The diary says work and meals and company. The map says where the next light could be lit without challenge. A heaven that redraws the map around its own feeding was never heaven. It was tenancy, with the tenant paying in hours for seconds.

Ask what the supply watch ever gave back past the pause of its own calling. Did the count make the tea taste richer. Did the cracked window in rain make the drive kinder. Did the aisle seat make the film deeper. You know the answer from your own diary. The watch added nothing to the work or the meal or the company. It taxed each one and signed the margin while the day did the living.

I know this is hard to accept, and I welcome the protest, because the protest shows you still feel the loosening in the chest and you take the feeling seriously. Keep the feeling beside you. I am not asking you to deny what you felt. I am asking you to watch what the feeling belonged to. The loosening belonged to the pause of a debt. The kindness belonged to the step. Two events shared one minute for years, and one minute of sharing fooled us into one authorship.

WHAT IT DOES TO YOU

There is a last defence the lit minute raises, and it sounds like loyalty.

"You do not understand. The pack stayed with me through every hard hour. The step. The railings. The car. The wall. Take those lights away and the hours will feel bare. The dusk will lose its kindness."

I hear the fear inside that defence. No one wants a bare dusk. So hear my answer with the warmth I hold for you. The dusk never belonged to the lights, and the lights never kept the dusk company. They kept the debt company while the dusk did the kindness.

Hold the butt up before you press it out and audit it the way we audited the finished work, because the audit runs the same every lit hour. The glow. The ash. The sour end. The brown filter. None of it stayed with you past the smell on the fingers, and the step never needed any of it to be kind. The dusk knows. The rail knows. The step knows. The tea knows.

There is a smaller voice inside the loyalty that names the desk and the pen, and it needs answering before the minute closes, because it borrows the names of clever men to dress the stick as thought.

"You do not understand. The writers smoked. The thinkers smoked. The bright ones at the table with their papers smoked through the night. The stick must feed the mind, or why would sharp minds keep it burning."

I hear the respect inside that voice. We all watched a clever hand lift a stick above a page and we joined the dots wrongly. So watch the desk honestly from your own nights. The sentence came after the pause, not from the smoke. The mind turned the problem over while the hands were busy with flame and the eyes rested on dusk. Then the next dose paused the faint gnaw and the pause felt like arrival of thought. We gave the page warmth to the stick, and the stick kept the books the way it kept them at the classroom door. Ask the page itself. Did the verb grow truer while you drew. Did the argument tighten while the tip burned. Did the blank fill faster in the smoking years than in the clear school years before the first pack, when the mind ran bright on sleep and food and curiosity alone. You know the answer. The mind made the line. The stick billed for the pause around the making.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

Say those sentences on the step and hear how much work the small words do. Much done to the tongue in that minute. Much done to the throat. Much done to the pocket. Much done to the mirror face that must be washed. Nothing done for the dusk that the dusk did not already give. Nothing done for the pause that the pause did not already hold. Nothing done for the chest past minutes of softening that the next fall reclaims. That is not a slogan. That is the lit minute totalled in its own coin.

It was never a genuine pleasure or crutch.

Say that sentence at the glass dish and hear how much work those five words do. A genuine pleasure would have enriched the step and left you fuller after the butt. A crutch would have held you up where you would have fallen without it. This object did neither. It borrowed the dusk cool and sold it back as its own. It borrowed the pause quiet and sold it back as its own. It borrowed the loosening of a debt pause and sold it back as heaven. It is not pleasure and not support. It is the story that the topping up is pleasure and support, told so often by so many voices that we mistook the telling for feeling.

Ask yourself, and answer honestly. Has the dusk ever refused to cool until you lit. Has the pause ever refused to quiet until smoke touched the step. Has the tea ever refused to warm until the dish filled. You know the answer, and the answer breaks the heaven story at its hinge. The step never waited for your lighter. It stood kind on its own cool while you stood on it giving the lighter the credit for the kindness.

The minute that ends in hiding was never heaven. It was relief misnamed, and the misnaming kept the servitude dressed as love.

You have timed the minute. The hour that holds such minutes back to back still waits to be seen, and the seeing of the hour follows from the seeing of the minute the way morning follows dusk. Bring me that timed minute answered honestly from your own step, and the rest follows.
