Chapter 10

THE LAST NIGHT ON EARTH

IN THIS CHAPTER: the crowded bar with glasses ringing, the lit doorway to the buzzing corner, the offered pack, the slide toward morning, the late air on the walk home.

*The night gave everything and the cigarette gave nothing, and one kept keeps the chain.*

The street holds Friday the way a glass holds light. The bar door stands open and warm air rolls out with music and talk. Inside the glasses ring and leave wet circles on wood. Laughter moves table to table. Coats pile on hooks. Someone buys a round and the tray comes back wet and shining. The hands wrap round cold glass. The throat takes the first cold pull of beer. The shoulders drop an inch after the week. We believed this hour belonged to smoke because smoke always stood beside it, and we never thought to ask what the hour held on its own.

I stood in that doorway a thousand nights with a pack soft in the pocket. I know the pull of that corner from the inside, and I tell you plainly that I loved those nights and feared this book could not follow me there. I feared the yard most of all, because the yard felt like proof no argument could touch.

You feel that fear tonight with drink aboard, and you have every right to feel it. This is the corner you call your last night on earth, the one place you believe the case for smoke still stands. Come and stand in it with me once with open eyes, and you will keep the night and lose only the billing.

THE BUZZING CORNER

Follow me out through the side door with your drink still in your hand, because the corner explains the whole claim.

The side door opens off the laugh and the music. The night air comes cool on the face after the warm room. The yard holds six or seven of us under a string of small lights. The wall gives back the talk. The ashtray on the ledge holds crushed ends and ash. The lighter clicks and flares and shows faces close together. The talk runs fast and kind. Someone tells the story of the week and we all lean in. The drink sits warm and bright in the chest. The buzz of company lifts the evening a foot off the ground. We believed the lift came from the lit ends moving hand to mouth, and we credited the stick with the warmth that belonged to drink and talk and late air.

Look closely at that corner with open eyes and the credit changes hands.

What makes the laugh roll. The beer aboard loosens the tongue and the week drops off the shoulders. The friends lean close and the talk turns quick and fond. The music leaks through the door and keeps time under the talk. The night air sharpens the face after the warm room. The lift from the week sits in the chest like light after long hours. Take the lit stick out of the picture and nothing leaves the corner past paper and ash. Leave the friends and the drink and the talk and the late air in place and the corner still sings. The stick added no story and no laugh. It only ever sneaked the ride while the night did the work.

We all lived that corner as the warm place of the night. We smokers of birthdays and Fridays and football nights all learned to read the yard as home. We said the room inside was only half the night. We said the yard held the real talk. We said non-smokers missed the best of it while we stood close under the lights. The fact is the yard held the talk because we carried our talk there, not because paper burned there. The non-smoker at the table kept the laugh going while we stepped out. The drink kept its glow while we stood in cool air. What we called missing out was stepping out, and the stepping out was taught, not chosen.

Think of buying the round, because the round shows who gives. The hand lifts glasses from the bar, wet and shining, and carries them careful through the crowd. Faces lift. Thanks sound. The first pull goes down cold together. Nobody checks who stepped out before raising. The round binds the table while the yard scatters it. We believed the yard held the bond, and the round proves the bond sat at the table all along. The table kept no ash and lost no story. The yard kept ash and lost the punchline to wind.

Think of the music, because music never needed paper to lift feet. The bass comes through the floorboards. Someone drums fingers on glass. Someone sings the chorus wrong and the table corrects them laughing. The shoulders move. The feet tap. The drink keeps time. We believed smoke sharpened that lift, and the lift runs whole with clear hands. Take paper out and the song stays. Take song out and paper holds nothing.

Stand a minute in the doorway and watch the two rooms together, because the doorway tells the truth both rooms hide.

Inside, the table holds the round and the talk and the laugh with no tending at all. Hands hold glass. Eyes meet. The story runs start to finish without a pause to mind ash. Outside, the same talk runs but cut through with small errands. The lighter fails and the story pauses. The wind takes the flame and the punchline waits. Ash lengthens and eyes drop to tap it. The ember nears fingers and the hand lifts without asking the mind. We believed the yard deepened the night, and the doorway shows the yard thinned it. The same friends. The same beer. The same hour. One room whole. One room fidgeting around a burn.

Ask and answer honestly from your own Fridays. Did the stick ever buy the round. Did the stick ever tell the story that made the table roar. Did the stick ever lay its hand on your shoulder when the week had cut you. You know from your own nights that the answer stands without my voice raised. The drink loosened. The friends held. The late air cleared the head. The stick stood beside the giving and took the applause for giving.

Think of the lake weekends that taught the same lesson in daylight, because daylight shows what lamplight hides. Friday jars in a cool bag. Sun on shoulders. Water slapping the dock. Talk loose and long with nowhere to be. We believed those slow afternoons proved smoke belonged to leisure, and we lit one after the swim and one with the cold drink and one while the sun dropped. Watch that afternoon with open eyes and the credit moves again. Sun warmed. Water cooled. Leisure loosened. Company held. The stick sat beside sun and water the way it sat beside beer and talk, billing hours it never built.

That lit corner was the nicotine trap in evening dress.

It dressed in friendship so we would not see the taking. It borrowed the light from the string of bulbs. It borrowed the warmth from the beer. It borrowed the closeness from friends with their heads together. Strip the borrowed clothes and the corner stands plain. Friends and drink and talk and late air give. Paper and ash take. One gives the night. The other bills the night by the dose.

THE OFFER

Stay in the corner a little longer, because here comes the hand with the pack and the sentence that has closed a thousand evenings.

The talk runs high. The second round arrives and glasses sweat on the ledge beside the ashtray. Someone laughs with the head back and the throat open. Someone else leans on the wall with shoulders loose. The pack comes out with the foil torn and the row neat. The hand shakes it open and holds it out. The gold foil catches the light. The row of white ends stands neat and offered. The voice comes easy and kind, with drink aboard and no malice in it.

“Go on. One won't hurt. Don't be smober tonight.”

We all heard that voice from a mate and from inside our own chest at once. We smokers of offered ends all knew the double pull. The wish to belong without a speech. The wish to prove we could take one and leave it. The fear of standing as the lone figure outside the circle while the lights buzz. We believed refusing meant preaching or sulking or killing our own night, and we believed one could sit apart from the chain, a small guest that would leave before midnight.

I took that offered one more times than I can count. I know how kind the hand looks and how small the request sounds, and I tell you the smallness is the lie.

You stand there with glass in one hand and the pack under your nose, and the mind runs its quick arithmetic. You tell yourself you have been good for weeks. You tell yourself drink explains a little looseness. You tell yourself nobody keeps score on a Friday. You tell yourself you can join the circle without joining the chain. I know that arithmetic because I ran it in that exact yard with that exact glass sweating in my hand, and I watched it fail in the same hour every time.

Hold that offered stick up to the light of the corner and watch what it asks.

Does one sit apart. The first draw scratches faintly and the chest coughs a fraction before it settles. The head swims a touch with beer aboard. The hand finds its old shape at once, two fingers up, elbow tucked, the way the hand always sat in that yard. The talk goes on but part of the mind steps back to tend the burn, to watch the ash length, to time the next pull. The second stick comes easier because the hand has remembered its post. The third comes with the next round because rounds and lights have always walked together. By half past eleven the offered one has grown brothers and the pack in the pocket has thinned. What arrived as one leaves as attendance through the evening.

Did the talk need that tending. Did the laugh pause until the ash fell. Did the friends lean closer because paper burned. Your own evenings have already answered at their hinge. The tending split the attention while the night gave. The tending lent its small fidget to hands that were happy holding glass. The tending billed the drink for a lift the drink had already given.

Hear how the offer sounds in your own mouth when you turn it over, because your mouth knows the lines by heart.

“This is my last night on earth, so let me have this.” Last nights on earth are made of sun and talk and drink and touch, not of paper. A last night deserves full hands and full attention, not half a mind on ash.

“I HAD to. Everyone was lighting.” Had never lived in that yard. Want lived there, dressed as had by years of talk. The circle never ordered your hand. The hand obeyed a story while the circle laughed on without orders.

“I will bum one cig and then Febreeze the hell out of myself before home.” The spray covers the coat while the hour stays billed. Morning knows what the coat tried to hide, and the hand knows before morning.

Think of bumming cigs as the yard teaches it, because borrowing proves the taking. The hand held out. The eyes hopeful. The voice light, as if asking for nothing. One borrowed. One more borrowed. One for the walk between bars. We believed borrowing proved lightness, a hand free of owning, and borrowing proved only that the hour owned the hand. A hand that must borrow through the night is not a hand enjoying a guest. It is a hand paying by the hour while calling the payments company.

Think of cigarette candies on the bar, because sweetness shows what paper is not. Someone puts the tin down and the table takes one each. Mint and orange. The mouth wakes. Talk laughs at the sour one. Nobody steps out to enjoy the sweet. Nobody tends a burn to keep it. The sweet gives and goes. We knew at once the sweet gave, and we knew because no tending stood beside it. Paper never passed that test in any yard.

It was never a genuine pleasure or crutch.

It posed as pleasure while it split the night, and it posed as belonging while it pulled you half a step out of belonging to tend it. The pleasure lived in the company and the drink and the music leaking through the door. The belonging lived in the heads together and the story told well. The stick stood beside both and charged admission to what was already yours.

ONE WON'T HURT

Now watch the slide, because one carries the whole chain inside it and the morning reads it back plain.

The yard thins a little after midnight. The lights buzz louder in the quiet. Someone goes in for another round and the door swings and shuts and swings again. The talk turns slower and kinder. The pack goes round again and this time you take without being asked. The hand dips sure. The lighter flares sure. The draw runs deep because the chest has relearned the road in an hour. We believed a social rule could hold the line where the day rule never held, and we told ourselves weekends need not count and party smoke sat apart from daily smoke.

“I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way, thinking after five months I could control myself and just have one.”

That sentence passes hand to hand among people who kept the social rule, and it passes because the rule breaks the same way every time. Five months clear, then a wedding yard, then one to be sociable, then the chain back by the week after. One a month at parties, then one each Friday, then a pack bought for a lake weekend that creeps into Tuesday. Found ends in a coat pocket kept for emergency. A borrowed one on a night out that buys the next pack. A whole pack smoked through on a summer trip and the morning vow that one won't hurt again. We believed each return proved a weak moment, and each return proved only that one kept keeps the caller awake.

Think of the party ladder, because the ladder shows the slide rung by rung. One a month at weddings, laughed off. One each Friday with the same mates, counted as social. Two each Friday, one borrowed and one owned. A ten-pack bought to stop bumming and carried for company. A twenty bought for the lake weekend so nobody must drive late. The twenty open on the kitchen table Monday morning with three gone and the week already ringing. We believed each rung sat apart from the next, and each rung taught the foot the next. Ladders are climbed one rung at a time, and this ladder is climbed without looking down until the roof.

Think of found ends, because finding proves the chain never slept. A stale one in a winter coat, smoked on the doorstep in rain. A crushed one in a bag, straightened and lit at the bus stop. A borrowed one from a stranger outside the station, asked for with a small shame that drink had washed away the night before. We believed finding proved chance, and finding proved the hand still watched for paper while the mind claimed months clear. Clear hands never search coats. Watched coats prove the story still stood.

Follow one kept Friday through to morning, because morning reads the night without flattery.

Half past one and the throat feels lined with ash. Half past two and the mouth tastes stale beside the toothpaste. The coat smells of the yard and gets hung by the door. The head aches faintly behind the eyes from drink and heat and tending. The alarm comes hard. The chest feels heavy on the first deep breath of the day. The mind reaches at once for the old arithmetic to cover the slide. It was only social. It was only drink. It was only Friday. It will not count on Monday. We believed Monday could hold a line Friday erased, and Monday always paid Friday's bill with interest.

Ask what one ever kept apart from the chain, because your own diary answers without dressing.

Did one ever stay one through the month. Did the social rule ever hold past the third Friday without new print in small type. Did weekends ever stay fenced while the level rang through the week. Did the lake pack ever stay at the lake. You know the slide from your own years. One teaches the hand its post again. The post teaches the hour its ring again. The ring teaches the mind its story again. Three parts turning inside one lit yard. What must be topped by the hour cannot be kept as a guest. It takes the spare room and then the house.

Hear the two whispers that come with drink aboard and set them both down kindly.

“Just one or two wouldn't hurt tonight. You have been good for months.” Good needs no proof by paper. Months clear prove the night never needed the dose, not that the dose has turned harmless. A clear chest reading its own calm never owed a test to a lit yard.

“Everyone is lighting. You don't want to be the only one not smoking.” The only one not tending a burn is the only one fully in the talk. The circle never lost a member to clear hands. It loses members to stepped-out tending while the story goes on inside.

“What is the point to life without these little parties.” The little parties were the talk and the drink and the laugh and the late air, and those parties stand taller with full attention. The point never lived in ash. The ash only billed the point.

Keep the bar. Keep the friends. Keep the drink in your hand through the whole evening. I do not ask you to avoid the house where you laugh or the mates who love you or the beer that loosens the week. Avoiding never taught eyes to see. Seeing lets you stand in the same yard with the same glass and watch the offered row pass without a pang, because the pang was lent by a story and the story has broken.

THE LATE HOUR

Walk home with me through late air, because the evening closes where its strongest sweetness lives and the claim must meet that sweetness too.

The bar shuts its lights one by one. The chairs go up on tables. The till rings last. The street stands empty and shining after rain. The talk thins to two voices. Your shoes sound loud on the pavement. The air comes cool and clean into the chest after the yard. The town sleeps with windows dark. A taxi passes with light on. A cat crosses a wall. The key turns in your own door and warmth meets you. The bed waits. The partner waits. Closeness waits, given and taken with full attention, skin and breath and laugh low in the dark. Afterwards the room holds quiet and the heart beats slow and the body rests whole. We believed the hour after called for a lit end to seal it, and we reached for the pack on the table beside the bed.

I reached that way for years and called the reach part of love. I know how deep the quiet runs and how loudly the old story speaks inside it, and I tell you the story lied in its softest voice there.

Watch what the lit end does to that quiet when it enters.

The lighter flares harsh in the dark room. The first draw dries the mouth that was soft. The chest that lay calm takes in heat and coughs faintly and settles heavier. The partner turns away a fraction from the smell. The arm that held close lifts to hold paper away from hair and sheets. The mind that rested whole steps back to count ash and mind the burn near cloth. The afterglow that belonged to touch and talk and breath together thins while tending takes its small cut. The night that gave everything gets billed at its sweetest hour for nothing given.

We told ourselves the seal proved the act complete, the way we told ourselves the yard proved the talk complete. We smokers of late hours all learned to pair the two and call the pairing nature. We said the draw lengthened the calm. We said the haze held the warmth a little longer. We said habit smokes after love were different from day smokes, softer and almost tender. The fact is the pairing thinned what it claimed to seal. Calm needs no heat to stay calm. Warmth needs no ash to stay warm. Tenderness needs no tending apart from touch.

Reassign every drop of that hour to where it belongs and the hour stands taller.

The sweetness lived in touch. The rest lived in the long day closed and the loved voice close in the dark. The calm lived in the body lying easy and the late air still in the lungs from the walk home. The drink had loosened, the talk had knit, the walk had cleared, the closeness had sealed. Paper added no warmth to warmth. It only ever sneaked the ride while love did the work, the way it sneaked the ride beside beer and talk in the yard.

Ask and answer from that quiet room without my voice raised. Did the burn ever deepen the touch. Did the ash ever lengthen the rest. Did the smell ever bring the shoulder closer. Did the flare ever soften the dark. Your own nights know. What joins never interrupted. What interrupts never joined.

Think of the walk home as the night grades it, because the walk grades honestly. The pavement shines. The air cools the face. The chest pulls deep without thinking and the head clears a fraction with each block. Talk turns soft and true with nobody to please. The hand swings free at the side holding nothing, and the mind rests on the evening whole. We believed the yard had lifted that walk, and the walk lifts because the yard is behind. Add a lit end to that walk and the grading falls. The hand tends. The chest tightens faintly. The talk pauses for draws. The clear air gets billed at the hour it gave. The walk that graded high gets marked down by paper.

Think of push through as the morning teaches it, because mornings after teach without flattery. The alarm rings. The mouth tastes stale. The head feels wrapped. The hands move slow with the kettle. The mind says push through the day and the night was worth it. We believed worth could be pushed into a grey morning, and the morning shows the night borrowed against it. A night that leaves the morning owing never gave what it charged. A night kept whole leaves the kettle singing and the head clear and no pushing needed.

The yard and the bedroom tell one story together. The night gives through company and drink and talk and late air and touch. The dose takes through hand and mouth and hour and leaves the giving smaller. See the taking once and the corner turns pitiful, not tempting. Pity the row of white ends standing neat in foil. They must borrow light and love to look like anything at all.

Think of the closet smoker the morning after, because the morning after explains why the late hour matters. The pack hidden in a boot. The ends buried deep in the bin under coffee grounds. The coat shaken at the door before coming in. The mint chewed hard on the walk home. We believed hiding proved control, and hiding proved only that shame had joined the billing. A night that needs hiding the next day never gave what it promised. A night kept whole needs no burying and no spray and no arithmetic at dawn.

KEEP THE NIGHT

Hold the whole evening in one hand now that each hour has spoken, because the evening decides the rule.

The bar with its ringing glasses. The yard with its lights and talk. The offered pack moving hand to hand. The slide toward midnight with rounds and lights walking together. The walk home through clean air. The quiet room with breath slow. Every hour gave through sun and leisure and company in their night shapes, through drink and talk and late air and touch. Every hour got billed where paper entered. Keep the giving hours whole and the billing shows plain beside them. What gives stays. What bills goes.

We spent years guarding a special corner where the argument could not follow. We said stress and reward and lighting joy might fall, but the drink-lit night with friends would stand. We said this corner proved the stick gave somewhere, somehow, to someone. The fact is the corner proves the opposite once watched whole. The strongest corner falls the hardest, and its fall leaves no foothold anywhere. If the night that held everything good needed nothing from paper, no hour needs anything from paper.

You keep the night. You keep the laugh that rolls table to table. You keep the beer cold in the hand. You keep the heads together under the lights. You keep the walk home with shoes loud on wet pavement. You keep the quiet room and the slow heart. You keep dancing and singing and late talk on the doorstep and the cold air bright in the face. Bring me one real thing any kept one adds past the pause of its own debt, watched from your own yard and your own bedroom, and the rest follows. No kept one answers that call without borrowing.

Think of next Friday arriving with the same lights, because the next Friday proves the keeping. The same mates gather. The same door stands open. The same round comes wet to the table. The yard fills with talk and the pack goes round. Your hands hold glass. Your eyes stay on the story. Your chest stays whole through the chorus. The offer passes and the hand does not lift, not from holding back but from seeing plain. The talk runs on without a gap where tending stood. The walk home grades high. The quiet room stays whole. We believed such Fridays belonged to paper, and one such Friday watched whole teaches more than years of talk about them.

10. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE
One kept means the belief is intact.

Carry that weighed evening through your next Friday and watch who moves the hand now that the corner stands read. The door swings. The music leaks. The glasses sweat. The pack goes round. The talk runs. The late air waits. Watch without blame on any face, because no mate built the taking and no friend lends it power now. The taking lent itself borrowed clothes while the giving did the work. Leave the borrowed clothes on the floor of the yard and walk home with clear hands through clean air.

SUMMARY

- The buzzing corner gave through friends and drink and talk and late air, while paper stood beside the giving and took the credit.
- An offered stick never sits apart, and the hand that takes one relearns its post within the hour.
- One kept through drink and company grows into the evening and then into the week, because what must be topped cannot be kept as a guest.
- The lake afternoons and the late quiet after closeness belong to sun and touch and rest, and paper thins both while adding nothing.
- Keeping the bar and the friends and the drink leaves the night whole, and the corner turns pitiful once seen whole.
- A special one kept means the old story still stands, and the story standing means the hand returns.
