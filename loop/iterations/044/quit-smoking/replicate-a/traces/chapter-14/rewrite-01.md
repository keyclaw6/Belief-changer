Chapter 14

FREE, AND ON WITH LIFE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kerb with the book nearly shut, the thirteen lines in one picture, and the life waiting past the last page.

*The cigarette never joined your life, it only ever interrupted it — you are resuming the life that was always yours.*

You stand at the kerb with the book nearly shut in your hand and the bag light on your arm. Buses pass. A dog pulls glad at its lead. The morning air goes down to the bottom of the chest and out slow. There is nothing more to study. There is only the accounting to close and the street to walk.

We began with your pocket full and your chest tight, and we end with your hands whole on your own goods. You know where every claimed gift went when it was watched. You know what paid the bill and what sent it. The work is done in the mind, and the mind has turned.

YOUR OWN ACCOUNT

Look back at your own week with open eyes and close the account in your own words.

Did the mornings ask for paper. Did talk ask for paper. Your own mouth knows the answer now. Nothing in the hours asked. Only old wiring knocked, and knocking is not needing.

You can speak the sum plain, because you lived it: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain, and you felt the truth of it in rooms, not in promises.

Take your own inventory slowly, the way a shopkeeper counts the till at night, and let each hour speak for itself.

The wedding table spoke first. You sat through long speeches with salt on the chicken and warmth in the face from wine. Talk ran fast about work and children and the new flat. You stayed inside when chairs pushed back toward the lawn. You watched from the warm room while small fires were tended apart from the music. You danced badly and laughed till your sides ached. You held the cousin close for the photograph. You drank water between glasses because the head likes to stay clear for jokes. Nothing in that long afternoon stood up and called for a light. The glow kept its own company from the first plate to the last dance.

The grey desk spoke next. The rain ran on the bus window. The letter with bad news lay open. The deadline moved forward. The phone rang with a voice that wanted more than you could give. The chest tightened in the old way. You finished the letter. You answered the phone. You drank the water on the desk. You walked to the window and breathed down deep and let the shoulders drop. You sorted the papers into what must be done today and what can wait till morning, and you did the first pile with the lamp on. No paper beside the work paid one line of it. Hands on work lightened the load because hands on work always lighten it.

The bar stool spoke next. Dark wood and low lamps and glasses chiming. Talk of football and the cousin's baby and the price of beer. Peanuts with the first round. The long story about the boss with the second. The offered pack slid across late in the evening with the old grin beside it. You stayed at the table and heard the story to its end. You turned back with warmth and no sermon and kept the evening whole. The company lifted the night because company always lifted such nights. Late air on the walk home tasted of rain and chips from the van. The talk carried because talk always carried.

The till queue spoke last. Bright shutters and warm bread in paper bags. Coins counted once for milk and bread and soap. The girl at the till smiled tired and you smiled back. The pocket held keys and coins, quiet against the leg. The hand stayed whole on the basket. You passed the glowing rows unbought and the passing felt ordinary, which is exactly the proof. Bread fed. Coins paid. Legs carried. Mornings moved on errands because mornings always moved on errands.

Put the four rooms together and the account closes itself. Weddings laughed without tending. Hard hours passed without pardon. Bars warmed without a kept stick. Shops moved without a dose in the pocket to make them safe. Each room gave what it always gave. Each hour held when held whole. That is how a reader knows the sum is his own, not mine lent to him. He did not take my word for it. He watched his own days answer.

I smoked through years of mornings and deadlines and late talk, certain the day could not hold without a light beside it. I tell you from the other side of the shut door that the day holds better whole. Coffee warms. Talk lifts. Work knits when the hands stay on it. The meal closes. The walk clears the head. Paper built none of it and billed the standing.

Every past failure belonged to "the Willpower Method", which sold suffering as the price and then sold the puff as cure for the suffering it made. You owe that voice nothing. You were conned, not weak, and the con seen whole cannot hold you.

You did what the book asked from the first page: "All you have to do is follow all the instructions." You kept them, and the keeping brought you here to the kerb with the reply ready.

WHAT NEVER JOINED

Here is the last turn to fix, and it needs no new proof.

The cigarette never joined your life, it only ever interrupted it. It broke the talk to tend the fire. It broke the work to feed the hour. The walk was stopped to stand apart from the laughter. The night was billed to keep company that never needed billing. Take the breaks away and nothing is missing. The talk runs on. The work runs on.

You are not ending anything. You are resuming the non-smoker you always were underneath the years of tending. The child who ran without counting breath. The talker who laughed without stepping out. The worker who finished and smiled at the finished thing. That life never left. It waited while paper stood beside it sending the bill, and now the standing is done.

See what resumption means past this kerb, tomorrow and the day after, with the settled understanding already assumed.

Tomorrow morning the alarm goes and the feet touch the floor and the hand does not reach for the pack first. The kettle boils. Coffee warms the mouth. The shower runs hot and the chest takes its first deep pull of the day without grit at the bottom. You dress with both hands on your own clothes. Keys and coins go quiet into the pocket. The door shuts behind you and the street takes you. No errand stands between you and the day. No hour needs paying before it can begin.

At work the pressure comes as it always comes, because work is work, and you meet it with whole attention. The phone rings. The papers pile. The deadline shortens. You drink water. You sort the pile. You do the first thing, then the next. When the chest tightens you let the shoulders drop and breathe down deep and keep your hands on the task. The knot loosens because knots loosen when left to work and breath. Nobody steps out to tend anything. Nobody divides the hour between task and burn. The morning stays in one piece because you stayed in one piece inside it.

At midday the meal closes as meals close. Bread tastes of bread. Soup warms the hands round the bowl. Talk with the others runs its course and ends where it ends. Nobody leaves the table to stand apart. Nobody watches the clock for the hour to release them. The plate clears and the afternoon begins with the mouth clean.

In the evening the walk clears the head as walks clear heads. The dog pulls glad at its lead. Hedges hang damp after rain. Lamps come on yellow along the pavement. Legs keep their own steady company. Breath goes down to the bottom of the chest and out slow. The eyes stay up for traffic and faces. Nobody counts the distance in sticks. Nobody borrows the lane to feed anything.

Later in the week a wedding invitation comes, or a bar call, or bad news in a letter, and each arrives as life arrives, not as a trial set for you. You go inside each hour with the same whole hands. You stay where the warmth sits. You leave when you wish to leave. The thought may cross, thinner each time, and the reply answers inside the chest and the week moves on. That is resumption. Not a new life built from parts. The old life with the interruptions taken out, running as it always ran underneath.

The small complaint you felt was the Ash Mite starving because you stopped feeding it. Its complaint thinned while you lived, and the thinning proved the starving neared its end. Let it lie unfed.

What lifted above those days was the Big Fog clearing, and you never need to walk back into it. The weather that made the Mite look like a friend has lifted. Leave the weather behind.

Keep one question for any fond thought that crosses in the months ahead. Ask kindly, from your own plate and your own till: was that ever "a genuine pleasure or crutch", or did it only rent back what it took first. Your own hours answer. "It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you." The question keeps the door shut because the answer never changed.

You carry this horizon now as your own: mornings and work and talk and late air, yours to live clear for the rest of your life.

GO OUT AND LIVE

Whenever the thought crosses, call it by its permanent name. That whisper is the nicotine trap knocking with its old line. Hear it kindly. Name it. It is a knock, not a command. It is old wiring firing, not need speaking.

Answer it inside the chest with the one reply that shuts the door tighter.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it loud inside while the street moves on. The sentence fits because your own days proved it. Each answering makes the next knock thinner, until the knock comes seldom and leaves fast.

Ask from your own kerb and let your own street answer. Did the wedding laughter need tending to lift. Did the bar talk need billing to warm. Did the queue need a dose to move. Your own week has answered three times over, and the fourth answer stands on the pavement with the bag light in your hand.

Did the grey hour need rescue to pass. Your own desk answered with the lamp on and the papers sorted. The letter stayed. The deadline stayed. The voice on the phone stayed. Paper fixed none and billed the standing, and the hour passed because hours pass when lived whole.

Pity smokers briefly when you see them tending small fires apart from the laughter, and let the pity pass. No envy. No sermon. Let visible gladness do what argument cannot.

You do not need to count visits on a calendar. You do not need to hold your breath past old hours. You need only the one fixed reply, word for word and loud inside, at weddings and desks and bars and tills, until it fires unasked the way the old bell fired. Mornings come with the sentence ready. Hard hours come with the sentence ready. Company comes with the sentence ready. Queues come with the sentence ready. The sameness is the bolt.

Now shut this book and go and live.

SUMMARY

- KEEP AN OPEN MIND — You questioned what you thought you knew and the rest followed.
- DO NOT STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL WE FINISH — You saw the device whole before you shut the door.
- ASK ONLY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU — You asked for one real benefit and found the scales empty.
- LET THE NEXT PRESSURE TEST THE STORY — You watched a hard hour pass and saw paper fix nothing it named.
- LET THE FINISHED THING BE THE PARTY — You let the paragraph and the class and the grocery run stand as the celebration.
- STOP CALLING RELIEF ENJOYMENT — You timed the lit seconds and found the shame inside the same minute.
- TIME THE RELIEF AND WATCH IT LEAVE — You timed ten seconds up and watched the clock start again.
- BLAME THE DEALER, NEVER YOURSELF — You were conned, not weak, and anger sits at the workbench, not the mirror.
- IGNORE EVERY METHOD THAT ASKS FOR SUFFERING — You left every door that needs misery to the old voice that sold it.
- NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE — One kept means the belief stands intact, and the chain rebuilds link by link.
- CHANGE NOTHING ELSE IN YOUR LIFE — You lived coffee and breaks and closed meals and the dog walk and proved the day holds whole.
- SMOKE THE LAST ONE AND CLOSE THE DOOR — You smoked the last one with eyes open and you are free the moment it went out.
- NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION — A thought is not an order, and one fixed reply bolts every knock.
