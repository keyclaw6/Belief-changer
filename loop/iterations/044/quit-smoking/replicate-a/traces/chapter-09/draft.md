Chapter 9

EVERY DOOR MARKED EXIT

IN THIS CHAPTER: the bathroom drawer in morning light, the winter car with its locked trunk, the brown paper bag on the back seat, the kitchen clock, the drawer receipts.

*Every soft way keeps the leash on, so the only way out is to leave the whole trap shut behind you.*

Put your hand on the bathroom drawer with me before the house wakes, because this drawer holds every receipt for softer ways.

The bathroom holds its morning shape. The mirror shows sleep still in the face. The tap runs cold then warm. The cabinet stands open with cotton and blades and a cracked cup holding brushes. Below the sink the drawer sticks a fraction before it gives, wood on wood, and inside lies the small museum we all kept. Foil sheets of gum with two squares punched out. A half-used card of patches with the backing half peeled and stuck to itself. A folded leaflet with a stepped chart and a quit date circled and crossed and circled again. A tin of mints bought to cover the hour. The drawer smells of mint and plastic and old paper. The hand knows each object by touch in the half-light. The mind knows the price of each object to the pound. We believed the drawer held tools that would carry us out a side door when the front door felt too much.

We all kept that drawer. We smokers of kitchens and cabs and night shifts all ran the same errand of hope before speech. Which aid this time. Which strength. Which step. Which flavour of gum that would make the hour pass without the stick. Which patch that would sit under the shirt at work while the hands learned to forget. We asked the chemist in a low voice. We read the backs of boxes in the aisle while others passed with baskets. We told ourselves we were being sensible and gradual and kind to ourselves. We told ourselves the front door demanded too much at once and a side door would treat us gently. The fact is the drawer never held a door at all. It held leashes of different lengths, and each leash kept the same neck.

I kept that drawer for years. I charted steps down with a pencil and kept the chart like a ledger of virtue. I chewed the gum until the jaw ached and called the ache progress. I wore the patch under the shirt and lifted the corner to check it was still there, the way you check a ticket in the pocket on the way to the station. I told myself the dose through the skin or the gum was cleaner than the dose through smoke. I told myself less smoke meant less trap. The drawer took my money and my mornings and gave me back the same hour with a different object in the mouth. What kept me was never the paper or the gum. What kept me was the story that some softer way would do what only seeing could do.

THE DRAWER AND THE TRUNK

Stand with me at the drawer a moment longer, because the drawer explains the trunk and the trunk explains the drawer, and both explain why softer never softened.

The drawer in our house sat below the sink where the pipes knocked when the heat came on. The top tray held plasters and a thermometer with a dead battery. The lower space held the museum. Patches in two strengths, the higher strength bought first with confidence, the lower strength bought later when confidence thinned. Gum in two flavours, mint bought for freshness, fruit bought when mint began to taste like medicine. A rubber band around three vouchers for money off the next box. A receipt folded small with the total circled. The hand opened this drawer more mornings than it opened the book on the shelf. The eye read the stepped chart while the kettle sang in the kitchen. The mind did small arithmetic about steps and weeks while the chest did its own arithmetic about the hour.

We ran that arithmetic ten thousand mornings and called it planning. We said this week twelve sticks with gum between. We said next week eight sticks with the patch on. We said weekends need not count because weekends were social. We said the drawer proved we were serious because serious people buy tools. The neighbour with no drawer runs no arithmetic. The mug pours. The coat buttons. The door shuts. Nothing stepped. Nothing chewed between. Our mornings opened with counting and chewing and sticking, and we called the counting care.

Now follow the drawer out to the winter car, because the drawer never stayed in the bathroom when night came.

January holds the street in iron. The breath shows at the door. The bins stand with frost on the lids. The car sits at the kerb with frost feathered across the glass and the wipers frozen to the screen. The key turns stiff in the lock. The trunk lifts with a sigh and the cold pours out. Inside, under a folded blanket and a bag of salt for the path, lies a brown paper bag folded twice at the mouth. Inside the bag lie twenty sticks in their pack with the cellophane torn, kept for emergency, kept for the morning after the patch, kept for the night the gum runs out. The fingers go numb finding it. The nose runs in the cold. The shoes slip a fraction on the slick pavement. The bag crackles loud in the still street. You know this run from your own winters, and you believed the run proved the drawer was working because you needed less.

We all ran that winter run. We smokers of frost and late hours all knew the map of open garages and the price of petrol against the price of one more pack. We told ourselves the run was the exception that proved the rule. We told ourselves we smoked less that week, so the bag would last, so the drawer deserved credit. We told ourselves the cold proved our commitment because only a serious person would drive for a crutch they meant to throw away. The fact is the run proves the opposite of what we paid it to prove. A hand that must drive at one in the morning through frost for a paper bag is not a hand being carried out by steps and gum. It is a hand paying the meter on its knees while the drawer takes the applause.

Ask what the drawer ever removed past the smoke from the room, because the room answers without my voice raised.

Did the drawer remove the half-hour call. Did the gum remove the count before speech. Did the patch remove the palm weighing four for the afternoon and two for the evening and one for the kettle. You know the answer from your own diary. The call stayed. The count stayed. The weighing stayed. What changed was the object between the calls, and the calls learned to wait for both objects. The mouth chewed. The skin itched under the square. The chest waited. The clock rang. The hand fed one leash or the other and called the choice progress while the level ordered both.

Hold the brown bag beside the drawer and watch the credit change hands. The drawer cost pounds and promised steps. The bag cost pounds and promised covering. Both promised a gentler road with the same destination. Both kept the destination at arm's length by keeping the level alive between payments. The level falls. The story translates. The hand obeys. Three parts turning like wheels inside one bathroom and one frozen street. We believed different objects meant different roads. Different objects meant the same wheel with different paint.

THE SHOPPER

I speak your present state plainly here, so you know I see the shopper without calling the shopper weak.

You have shopped every method the way we all shopped. Cutting down with rules. Gum and patches with charts. The vapour stick with its charger on the counter. Tomorrow with its clean date. The mild pack with its gold word. The social rule that weekends need not count. The private rule that three a day is hardly smoking at all. Each method arrived with its own promise and its own receipt. Each method asked for a small suffering now in exchange for a larger life later. Each method kept you busy measuring while the trap kept you.

We shopped because shopping felt like action while the dread of the front door grew. We compared boxes the way we compared coats, as if fit decided the outcome. We asked mates what worked for them and collected answers like keys on a ring. One mate swore by the gum. One mate swore by the vapour. One mate swore by Sundays only. One mate swore by the new year. We turned the keys over in the palm and chose the shiniest. We told ourselves a strong will would turn the key if we pushed hard enough through the grey days. We told ourselves failure meant we chose the wrong key or pushed too softly. The blame sat on our shoulders from the first turning, and the trap counted on the shoulders to carry it.

A conned hand is not a weak hand. A tutored doubt is not a verdict on your worth. The dealer built the falling level and the story that named the fall as your nature, and the drawer sold you leashes while the bench stayed out of sight. You shopped in good faith among doors that all opened inward. No wonder the basket filled while the room stayed shut.

Look once with open eyes at the whole menu together, and you have nothing to lose and everything to gain from seeing it plain.

Does any method on that menu ask you to suffer through grey days while it keeps the dose alive in another form. Does any method keep the count and the clock and the palm-weighing exactly where they were. Does any method leave the story intact that the stick gives something no drawer can replace. Answer from your own drawer and your own trunk run, not from my voice. What keeps the level keeps the caller. What keeps the caller keeps the hand. What keeps the hand keeps paying, whatever the object between the payments. That needs no argument from me once the drawer and the trunk stand side by side.

Think of paying for sticks for the rest of your life against one plain afternoon of seeing, because one plain afternoon decides which of those two accounts you keep.

WHAT THE TRIALS SAY AND WHAT THE KITCHENS SAY

There are two different rooms where people count quitting, and the rooms answer different questions, so let each room speak for itself without borrowing the other's books.

In one room, men in white count trial volunteers under lamps. They give one group the licensed gum or patch and another group a dummy with no dose, and they count who holds past half a year. In that counted room, the group with the dose holds in larger numbers than the group with the dummy. The fact is plain and I state it flat. A dose through skin or gum can lift the half-year count against a dummy in counted rooms. That room counts what happens under lamps with volunteers and visits and charts. It does not count your kitchen. It does not promise your hands. It does not order your drawer.

In the other room, the kitchen counts what people actually do when no lamp watches. In that room, most who attempt to stop do so without aids, with bare hands and the book on the shelf or nothing at all, and a small handful in a hundred hold past half a year. That is the single counting sentence of this book about how common either road is, stated once and left there. It counts attempts as people live them, with frost and bills and night shifts inside the count. It does not say you cannot hold. It does not say the drawer decides. It does not say bare hands never hold. Two rooms. Two counts. Two different questions. What lifts a count under lamps never told your hands they were doomed, and what most kitchens attempt never told your drawer it was destiny.

Why does that separation matter to your hands tonight. Because the trap loves a collapsed count. It loves to take the lamp count and read it as your sentence. It loves to take the kitchen count and read it as proof the front door never opens. It loves to set the two counts against each other until you shop again rather than see. I know that collapsed reading feels like sense after twenty receipts. It feels like prudence to buy another box when the numbers sound thin. Welcome that doubt without obeying it. Doubt is the weather clearing, not the verdict. The numbers never owned your eyes. Your eyes own the numbers once each count stays in its own room.

Ask, and answer honestly from your own house. Has the lamp count ever entered your bathroom and closed the drawer for you. Has the kitchen count ever driven your car through frost for you. Your hands drove. Your hands opened. Your hands paid. Counts never lifted a hand. Seeing lifts the hand by removing the story that moved it. Neither count gives orders. Neither count writes your name. Neither count stands between you and the door once the story breaks.

WHISKY FOR GIN

There is a sentence passed hand to hand among people who found the drawer empty, and I lay it here whole because one sentence ends the gum story without my voice raised.

Quitting cigarettes with nicotine gum is quitting whisky by drinking gin.

Hold that sentence beside the drawer and let it do its work. The whisky drinker who moves to gin at noon and gin at night has changed the glass, not the thirst. The clock still rings. The palm still measures. The evening still waits for its pour. The glass teaches the hand to keep lifting while the tongue learns new words for the same lift. No one calls that move quitting drink. No one says the gin glass broke the bar tab because the liquid runs clear. Everyone sees at once that the thirst stayed loyal while the label changed.

The gum and the patch work that glass change in paper and skin. The mouth chews between sticks while the level waits for its top. The skin drinks under the square while the chest waits for its lift. The hand learns two hungers in place of one, checking the drawer and checking the pack, guarding the gum and guarding the bag. The day runs longer with more objects to count and the same level to serve. Morning gum. Midday stick. Evening patch edge lifting at the corner. Night drawer opened again to see what strength remains. You know this double count from your own weeks, and you called the doubling proof of a serious attempter. The doubling was attendance at two counters while the same master rang the bell.

Did the new glass ever teach the morning to open without counting. Did the new glass ever teach the trunk to stay shut through frost. Did the new glass ever return the paragraph or the meal or the talk as the party itself. Your own diary has already answered at its hinge. What must be topped by the clock never turns harmless by the vehicle. It teaches the hand to top through more doors while the coat takes the credit.

The boxes sit where hands left them. I speak only of the story that sold the boxes as doors. A door that keeps the level alive keeps the caller alive. A caller kept alive keeps the hand. That is not a slogan. That is the drawer totalled in its own coin beside the brown bag in frost.

THE BRIEF DREAD

Now we face the dread that sells every softer way, because every softer way promises to spare you days you fear you cannot pass.

You fear the days after the last stick the way we all feared them. The snap at small noises. The hollow behind the ribs by mid-morning. The hands looking for work at the hour. The sleep thin on the first nights. The temper short with people you love. You picture weeks of grey stretching without a pause, and the picture alone sends the hand to the drawer for covering. I do not mock that picture. I lived inside it for years and paid every softer way to soften it.

Here is the plain shape of those days as bodies live them, stated flat without dressing. The restless empty clamour may begin within hours of the last dose. For most, it sharpens across the first days, crests about two or three days after the last use, then thins across the coming weeks until it goes. Not every body to the same hour and not every day to the same shade, and no one can hand you your mornings timed to the minute. That is the typical course, held as typical, not as your sentence. It is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical crown, not proof the stick was your self calling for its missing part.

We mistook that clamour for ourselves because the weather taught us to hear it that way. A lifetime of talk and pictures taught that smoke steadies and settles and rewards, so when the level fell and the chest complained, we heard our own nature asking for aid. The weather fed the hollow a meaning. The meaning fed the hand the lighter. The hand fed the level its dose. Three parts turning like wheels inside one kitchen. The level falls. The weather translates. The hand obeys. Name the middle wheel and the whole machine slows. The complaint is the level falling, not your character failing. A falling level never proved a missing gift. It proved a debt coming due that the last dose laid.

Ask what those few days ever weighed past the story that wrapped them, because the scales answer without my voice raised.

Did the hollow ever stop the tea pouring. Did the edginess ever stop the bus running or the bread rising or the child laughing at the table. Did three grey mornings ever weigh more than counting and paying and driving through frost without end. Your own weeks have already broken that bargain at its hinge. What passes in days never matched what repeats without end while the story holds. The dread looked large because the story lent it size. The story borrowed its size from every softer way that promised to spare you and thereby taught you the days must be unpassable alone.

Do not change from fear of those days. I do not want you seeing because the crest frightens you or the hollow sickens you. Fear never kept eyes loyal to seeing. Keep your eyes on the workmanship while the fright thins. The method takes care of fear the way daylight takes care of a locked door once seen. The seeing does the work the dread never did.

CUTTING DOWN

Cutting down arrives dressed as prudence, and prudence deserves a straight look before the evening closes.

The rules sound wise in the mouth. Twelve today. Eight tomorrow. None before noon. None at work. Only with coffee. Only with drink. Only at weekends. The chart goes on the fridge with magnets. The ticks gather through the day like small virtues. The hand learns to watch the clock for permission the way a dog watches the kitchen door. Eleven forty with one left until noon becomes a small triumph of waiting. Eleven fifty-nine becomes a ceremony of the hand hovering. Noon strikes and the draw runs deeper and longer than any draw ran before the rules, because waiting taught the lungs to drink harder once allowed. The evening then pays for the morning's virtue with interest, stick upon stick, while the chart takes the credit for the morning and the night takes the blame for the night.

We all lived that chart. We smokers of rules and ticks all learned the same lesson while calling it different names. The fewer the allowed sticks, the larger each stick looms in the mind. The larger each looms, the harder the wait bites. The harder the wait bites, the deeper the topping once allowed. The deeper the topping, the louder the next wait calls. Waiting never loosened the knot. Waiting pulled it tight and polished each allowed stick until it shone like a small sun. The day filled not with living but with watching for leave to live. Obeying the clock never bought an hour. It rented each hour back from the fall the last dose laid, while the kitchen did the living that got billed as smoke.

Cutting down is jumping off the cliff provided you fall no more than a few metres.

See the fraud at once from the doorway. No climber calls that bargain prudence. No guide sells tickets for half a fall. The drop does not negotiate with rulers and charts. Either you step back from the edge whole or you meet the rocks below. Praising the ruler for marking metres on the drop would be madness while the ground still waits. The chart marks metres on a fall and calls the marks mercy while the level still orders the landing.

That tutored bargain is the weather you breathed, because the bargain kept the hand loyal while the bench kept the dose. A lifetime of talk taught you that less poison means more control, and that control proves character. The weather fed the hollow a meaning. The meaning fed the hand the chart. The hand fed the level its fix on schedule. Three parts turning again. The level falls. The weather translates. The hand obeys on tick. You obeyed because the waiting felt like virtue while the topping stayed out of sight.

There is no stable middle where the trap stays half-fed and the mind stays whole. If there were no gift in smoking often, there would be no point in smoking seldom either. What gives nothing often gives nothing seldom, and what must be topped by the hour cannot be tamed by the ruler. One kept means the story stands. The story standing means the hand returns. That is the totality this room lands without my voice raised. Leave the whole device shut and the counting ends. Keep one small window open and the wind keeps the house cold.

THE DOORS

Now walk the corridor slowly with me and read each door as you pass, because each door carries its promise in paint and its hinge on the inside.

“I'll just cut down to three a day. That is hardly smoking at all.” Three keeps the clock and the wait and the noon ceremony. Three teaches deeper pulls and longer waits and brighter halos around each allowed stick. The chart grows while the level stays. A small number never broke a wheel that runs on topping.

“I'll use the patch and the gum to step down. The chart makes it gradual and kind.” Gradual keeps the level alive through more doors while the mind watches steps in place of seeing. The mouth chews. The skin drinks. The chest waits. Two counters collect while one bell rings. Kindness that keeps the caller is not kindness to your hands.

“I'll move to the vapour stick. No smoke, so no trap.” The vapour keeps the hand lifting to the mouth on the hour and the level topped between lifts. The charger sits beside the bed where the pack sat. The count moves from sticks to pods while the palm still weighs before speech. A different vehicle never closed a road that runs on dosing.

“I'll start tomorrow, fresh with the new month. Today is the wrong day.” Tomorrow keeps today safe for topping while it spends tomorrow before it arrives. The fresh date arrives with the same hands and the same weather and the same level. The calendar never changed a story. Hands change the story and the calendar follows.

“A few a day is not a big deal. Different for me because I hardly inhale.” Few keeps the same wheel turning fewer times while the mind pays full price in watching. The lungs still queue. The palm still counts. The trunk still waits through frost for its brown bag. Small numbers never made a leash a bracelet.

“I'm different. I smoke for the taste and the pause, not the dose.” Taste never drove at one in the morning through frost for flavour. Pause never needed a brown bag under a blanket. The run proves the driver while the tongue takes the applause. We all believed our own flavour story until the trunk run read it back without garnish.

“Mine are mild, so the risk sits lighter and control sits closer.” Mild changes the coat, not the clock. The hand learns its way back to the level while calling the finding liking. What must be topped by the hour never turned tame by the word along the edge.

“I need the crutch for nerves. Without it I would snap at everyone.” Nerves never lived in the stick. The stick created the edginess it rented back as calm, then charged the day for the loan. Watch the next pressure pass with the story broken and see who steadies whose hands.

Each door above carries the same hinge once seen. Each promises less pain for the same payments. Each asks for measuring and waiting and guarding while the level orders all three. Each leaves the story standing that the stick gives something no ordinary hour holds. Pull that story and every door shows its inside hinge at once. Leave that story and every door keeps you shopping.

I speak that flatly from your own drawer and your own frost, without dressing it as a charge from books, because the drawer and the frost prove it by themselves. No lamp count opened your drawer for you. No chart closed your trunk for you. Your hands opened. Your hands drove. Your hands can leave both shut once the story breaks. The doors never held the power. The story lent them power while the level rang.

The voice that whispers for one more softer attempt is not your prudence. It is the Willpower Method speaking through old receipts, selling suffering as the price and measuring as the cure. Hear it kindly and set it down. A method that needs misery to work belongs with the trap that made the misery, not with your hands.

9. IGNORE EVERY METHOD THAT ASKS FOR SUFFERING
If it needs misery, it belongs to the trap.

Carry that weighed drawer through one more morning and watch who moves the hand now that every door stands read. The mirror shows sleep leaving the face. The tap runs warm. The drawer sticks then gives. The foil lies where the hand left it. The chart hangs where the pencil left it. The trunk waits cold at the kerb with its brown bag folded twice. The clock starts its quiet count toward the next hollow. Watch the count without blame on the mirror, because the mirror never built the clock. Demand one real thing any softer way adds past the pause of its own debt. Keep the kitchen. Keep the pause. Keep the tea. Bring me that watched morning answered honestly from your own rooms, and the rest follows.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Picture mornings with your hands free for the rest of your life and the count gone, the drawer holding plasters again, the trunk holding blankets and salt, the brown bag absent without a vote each night. That picture is not a distant prize for measured suffering. It is what remains when the story breaks and the doors show their hinges. No soft door opens outward. Every soft door locks inward. Leave them shut and walk past.

SUMMARY

- The drawer of aids and charts kept the count and the clock exactly where they were, while the hand learned more objects to guard.
- The winter run for the brown bag proves the level still ordered the hand, whatever the drawer promised.
- No number from another room decides what your hands do once the story breaks.
- Swapping the vehicle keeps the thirst, the hour, and the palm-weighing in place.
- The hollow after the last dose crests early for most and then thins, and the hollow is a falling level, not your nature asking for aid.
- Cutting down polishes each allowed stick and tightens the wait, so the ruler never tamed what runs on topping.
- Every softer door opens inward on the same wheel, and leaving the story breaks the shopping.
