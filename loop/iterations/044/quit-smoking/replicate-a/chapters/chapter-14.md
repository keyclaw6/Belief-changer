Chapter 14

FREE, AND ON WITH LIFE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kerb with the book nearly shut, the account closed in your own words, the last turn that fixes, and the street to walk.

*The cigarette never joined your life, it only ever interrupted it — you are resuming the life that was always yours.*

You stand at the kerb with the book nearly shut in your hand and the bag light on your arm. Buses pass. A dog pulls glad at its lead. The morning air goes down to the bottom of the chest and out slow. There is nothing more to study. There is only the accounting to close and the street to walk.

We began with your pocket full and your chest tight, and we end with your hands whole on your own goods. You know where every claimed gift went when it was watched. You know what paid the bill and what sent it. The work is done in the mind, and the mind has turned.

Stand a moment longer at this kerb before you shut the cover. Feel the weight of the bag on the arm and the quiet of the pocket against the leg. Keys and coins. Nothing else. The street runs its ordinary business past you, buses and prams and men sweeping steps, and none of it asks anything of you. You have read the whole of it. You have watched the whole of it. The cover is nearly closed because there is nothing left inside it you have not lived.

Hold the book a breath longer and feel how light it has grown. In the first pages it carried a promise that sounded too large, and you carried it warily, braced for the old bill. Now the pages behind you weigh nothing, because every line in them has passed into your own mouth and your own hours. What remains ahead is not another lesson. It is the street itself, already under your feet, asking only to be walked whole.

YOUR OWN ACCOUNT

Your wedding table and grey desk and bar stool and till queue each gave the same answer when lived whole, and your own days carry the sum.

Look back at your own week with open eyes and close the account in your own words.

Did the mornings ask for paper. Did talk ask for paper. Your own mouth knows the answer now. Nothing in the hours asked. Only old wiring knocked, and knocking is not needing.

You can speak the sum plain, because you lived it: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain, and you felt the truth of it in rooms, not in promises.

Let that sum sit beside you at the kerb the way the bag sits light on the arm. You did not take my word for any of it. You watched laughter hold without tending. You watched a hard hour pass without pardon. You watched company warm without a kept stick. You watched a morning move without a dose in the pocket to make it safe. Each hour gave what it always gave. Each hour held when held whole. That is how a reader knows the sum is his own, and not mine lent to him. The mouth that answers is yours. The days that answered were yours.

I smoked through years of mornings and deadlines and late talk, certain the day could not hold without a light beside it. I tell you from the other side of the shut door that the day holds better whole. Coffee warms. Talk lifts. Work knits when the hands stay on it. The meal closes. The walk clears the head. Paper built none of it and billed the standing.

Ask it once more from your own kerb and hear how short the answer runs. When the hours were lived whole, what stood up and called for a light. Your own street answers without my voice raised. Nothing stood up. Nothing called. Only the old bell rang from habit, and a bell ringing from habit is not a voice asking from need.

Every past failure belonged to "the Willpower Method", which sold suffering as the price and then sold the puff as cure for the suffering it made. You owe that voice nothing. You were conned, not weak, and the con seen whole cannot hold you.

You did what the book asked from the first page: "All you have to do is follow all the instructions." You kept them, and the keeping brought you here to the kerb with the reply ready.

WHAT NEVER JOINED

Here is the last turn to fix, and it needs no new proof.

The cigarette never joined your life, it only ever interrupted it. It broke the talk to tend the fire. It broke the work to feed the hour. The walk was stopped to stand apart from the laughter. The night was billed to keep company that never needed billing. Take the breaks away and nothing is missing. The talk runs on. The work runs on.

You are not ending anything. You are resuming the non-smoker you always were underneath the years of tending. The child who ran without counting breath. The talker who laughed without stepping out. The worker who finished and smiled at the finished thing. That life never left. It waited while paper stood beside it sending the bill, and now the standing is done.

See what resumption means with the settled understanding already assumed. It means the talk you love runs start to finish without a gap cut in its middle. It means the work you do stays in one piece because you stay in one piece inside it. It means the legs under you keep their own steady company and the chest takes what it needs without grit at the bottom. Nothing here is new. Nothing here is built from parts. The old life runs as it always ran underneath, with the interruptions taken out.

That is why this kerb feels ordinary, and why the ordinary is exactly the proof. The bag sits light. The pocket holds keys and coins, quiet against the leg. The air goes down deep and out slow, and the shoulders drop past the grit. The eyes stay up for traffic and faces. Nobody divides anything between basket and burn. Nobody watches a clock for release. The morning moves on legs and errands because mornings always moved on legs and errands, and you are inside your own morning again.

Stand inside that ordinary and let it speak once more, because it speaks louder than any pledge. The legs that carry you to the shops carried you before paper billed the carrying. The lungs that pull the morning air pulled it before paper charged for the pull. The mouth that laughs at the table laughed before paper taxed the laughing. Nothing was added by the years of tending. Only a bill was added, and a gap cut where no gap belonged. The gap is closed. The bill has stopped. What walks the pavement now is the walker who was always underneath, whole from kerb to kerb.

Mark how little resumption asks of you. It asks no ceremony past this page. It asks no new arrangement of your days. It asks only that you walk the street that is already under your feet, with whole hands on your own goods, and let the hours do what hours do when lived whole. Coffee warms the mouth. Bread tastes of bread. Talk lifts because talk always lifted. Work lightens pile by pile because hands on work always lighten it. Late air clears the head because late air always cleared it. Paper built none of it. Paper only stood beside it sending the bill, and the billing has stopped.

The small complaint you felt was the Ash Mite starving because you stopped feeding it. Its complaint thinned while you lived, and the thinning proved the starving neared its end. Let it lie unfed.

What lifted above those days was the Big Fog clearing, and you never need to walk back into it. The weather that made the Mite look like a friend has lifted. Leave the weather behind.

Keep one question for any fond thought that crosses in the months ahead. Ask kindly, from your own plate and your own till: was that ever "a genuine pleasure or crutch", or did it only rent back what it took first. Your own hours answer. "It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you." The question keeps the door shut because the answer never changed.

You carry this horizon now as your own: mornings and work and talk and late air, yours to live clear for the rest of your life.

GO OUT AND LIVE

Whenever the thought crosses, call it by its permanent name. That whisper is the nicotine trap knocking with its old line. Hear it kindly. Name it. It is a knock, not a command. It is old wiring firing, not need speaking.

Answer it inside the chest with the one reply that shuts the door tighter.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it loud inside while the street moves on. The sentence fits because your own days proved it. Each answering makes the next knock thinner, until the knock comes seldom and leaves fast.

Ask from your own kerb and let your own street answer. Did the wedding laughter need tending to lift. Did the bar talk need billing to warm. Did the queue need a dose to move. Your own week has answered, and the answer stands on the pavement with the bag light in your hand.

Did the grey hour need rescue to pass. Your own desk answered with the lamp on and the papers sorted. The letter stayed. The deadline stayed. The voice on the phone stayed. Paper fixed none and billed the standing, and the hour passed because hours pass when lived whole.

Pity smokers briefly when you see them tending small fires apart from the laughter, and let the pity pass. No envy. No sermon. Let visible gladness do what argument cannot. You know the pavement they pace between rounds, and you know the bill they pay to pace it. The pity is warm because you stood there, and it passes because you stand here now, with the bag light and the chest open and the whole evening yours.

You do not need to count visits on a calendar. You do not need to hold your breath past old hours. You need only the one fixed reply, word for word and loud inside, at weddings and desks and bars and tills, until it fires unasked the way the old bell fired. Every old hour meets the same reply, and you will meet each knock with the sentence ready. The sameness is the bolt.

Now shut this book and go and live. The street is under your feet. The day is yours start to finish. Walk it whole.

Go where the warmth sits and stay while the warmth lasts. Leave when you wish to leave, with the mouth clean and the hands whole. Let the talk run its course and end where it ends. Let the work knit pile by pile and the meal close when it closes. Let the evening air clear the head on the way home. There is no gap to tend between any of these, and no bell to obey inside any of them. The life past the last page is the life you already proved, hour by hour, and it asks nothing of you but to be lived.

SUMMARY

- KEEP AN OPEN MIND — You questioned what you thought you knew and the rest followed.
- DO NOT STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL WE FINISH — You saw the device whole before you shut the door.
- ASK ONLY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU — You asked for one real benefit and found the scales empty.
- LET THE NEXT PRESSURE TEST THE STORY — You watched a hard hour pass and saw paper fix nothing it named.
- LET THE FINISHED THING BE THE PARTY — You let the paragraph and the class and the grocery run stand as the celebration.
- STOP CALLING RELIEF ENJOYMENT — You timed the lit seconds and found the shame inside the same minute.
- TIME THE RELIEF AND WATCH IT LEAVE — You timed ten seconds up and watched the clock start again.
- BLAME THE DEALER, NEVER YOURSELF — You were conned, not weak, and anger sits at the workbench, not the mirror.
- IGNORE EVERY METHOD THAT ASKS FOR SUFFERING — You left every door that needs misery to the old voice that sold it.
- NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE — One kept means the belief stands intact, and the chain rebuilds link by link.
- CHANGE NOTHING ELSE IN YOUR LIFE — You lived coffee and breaks and closed meals and the dog walk and proved the day holds whole.
- SMOKE THE LAST ONE AND CLOSE THE DOOR — You smoked the last one with eyes open and you are free the moment it went out.
- NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION — A thought is not an order, and one fixed reply bolts every knock.
