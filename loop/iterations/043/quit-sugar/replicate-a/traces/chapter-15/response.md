Chapter 15
THE PHOTOGRAPH

The kitchen is quiet. The cup is washed and turned mouth-down on the cloth. The chair is pushed back from the table where you spoke. The book lies closed beside the crumbs you brushed aside.

You have walked the mornings. You have walked the aisle smiling. You have sat with coffee while cake sat near. You have come through a late day with rain on the coat. You have met the offered plate and the passing thought. The days did what days do. They proved.

Take the page with the list on it. Fold it once. Tear it clean along the fold. Put it in the pocket where money goes. Leave the book closed.

IN THIS CHAPTER: the closed book, what stands plain, the list in the pocket, life.

*you have not lost your sweet tooth — you have got your taste back.*

THE CLOSED BOOK

Sit a minute before you stand. Let the room tell you where you are.

The kettle is cold now. Light lies across the table boards. A fly walks the window and leaves. From the street comes a bus sighing and children calling and a shop shutter rolling. Nothing in the room asks for anything. Hunger will call when hunger calls. Till then the mouth is clean and the head is clear and the hands are your own.

We lived years with a bright tail tied to every hour. Morning needed sweetening. Three o'clock needed rescuing. Work finished needed rewarding. Kindness needed wrapping in shine. Company needed cake beside it to count as company. We believed all of it at once and called it appetite.

Look at those hours now with the mornings behind you.

Morning wakes without hunting. The tongue reports bread as bread and fruit as fruit and water as water. Three o'clock arrives and passes without remark. The pause in a hard day soothes because pausing soothes. A good week pleases because the week pleased. Loving hands please because they are loving hands. The meal closes because the belly answers. The day holds because nothing now digs under it.

It never was a genuine treat or lift.

That one plain sentence closes the whole account. All the packets and bars and bottles and bowls promised lift and delivered billing. All the bright beginnings billed the afternoon. All the rewards fined. All the comforts left the ache standing. Seeing it once is seeing it for good. What is seen cannot be unseen.

You stay out of the Sugar Trap.

Speak it plainly between you and yourself as you fold the page. Not as a boast. As a fact like the address on your door. The door stood open from the first pages and you stepped through it at the table. The days since proved the stepping. The list in your pocket keeps the stepping plain.

If a small voice says this feels too simple to last, hear it kindly and let it pass. Simple was the truth all along. The tangle was the con. You do not need to carry the book to carry the seeing. You carry the list and the mouth and the afternoons that testified.

A thought may cross this evening. It will wear your voice. It will say one small sweet mouthful would round the evening off.

Meet it with one bright sentence and a smile and turn back to the room.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

That is the whole reply. You do not follow the thought down its corridor. You do not count reasons. The sentence fires because the work behind it was done at the table and in the days after the table. The thought passes like cloud. The sky behind it is the one you chose.

WHAT YOU NOW KNOW PLAIN

There is nothing more to learn about bright packets. There is only what you now know in the body to keep plain.

You know the afternoon. Watched like a detective, it testified. The drawer that promised rescue dug the hole it sold the ladder for. The meal feeds. The bright tail taxes. The head clears when nothing coats the tongue. The hands stay in the day when no hourly lesson calls them away.

You know the pause. Taken at the kerb with water, at the window with breath, in the kitchen with hands washed, it drops the shoulders on its own. The wrapper never added to the pause. It only rode along and billed afterwards. Take the break. Leave the wrapper to its own silence.

You know the wage. Paid in shine after a good week, it left heaviness behind the eyes and talk thinned to chewing. A wage that leaves the worker poorer never paid anyone. The week's pleasure belonged to the week. The pay belongs to pride that needs no coating.

You know the hands. Grandmother's tin, the bakery smell on cold air, payday candy, the birthday candles, the neighbour's plate held out warm — the love was the people. Tenderness stays exactly where it was. Rooms, laughter, hands that cooked, friends that stayed late. The packet only photobombed them. Thank the hands. Keep the people. Let the shine go with the wrapping.

You know the table. Eaten slowly when hunger calls and left when the belly answers, ordinary food satisfies down to the ground. Eat what you want; BAD SUGAR is off the menu. Hunger comes and goes. Satisfaction answers and holds. No counting, no sums in the aisle, no bargaining with the palm while the queue waits. Your body already knows the portion. The tongue, unfiltered, reports plainly again.

You know the single. Weighed in the palm at the till, one small bar looked apart from the whole. It never was apart. One reopens what thousands could not close. The bright signal does not count portions. It counts answers. Leave it unanswered and the lessons thin to nothing. Answer once and the hourly lessons resume. There is no single apart from the whole.

You know the costume. Cans ranked in the fridge door, boxes marked free of this or light in that, jars of amber poured as nature — the loop changes costume, not character. Keeping the sweetness keeps the wanting. Leaving the sweetness leaves the wanting to dry. Drink water. Eat clean. Let the mouth learn plain again.

You know the day. Candlelit and shared, with music and beloved faces, it shone before any packet arrived. The candles were never the cake. Sun, leisure, company, talk, dancing where dancing is on — those owners made the sweetness. The packet only rode along. Let the day be sweet in its owners.

You know the strugglers. Heard at work, at family tables, in queues, their stories all confess the same method. White lines held till Sunday night swept them away. Calendars marked with permissions that slid. Boxes hidden and counted. Mornings vowed and evenings swept. That hard grind belonged to the Willpower Method. Its strugglers prove the method, never the person. You hold no line. You keep a verdict. The difference shows in the shoulders and in the sleep.

I tell you from having sat with readers at many such tables that this knowing holds. Not because you memorised it. Because you lived it in mornings and aisles and cafés and late days and offered plates. Lived knowing does not fade like a lesson. It prints deeper with every calm passing until the ground holds without thinking about the ground.

THE LIST

Photograph this page before you tear it. Keep the picture where you keep what matters. Keep the paper in the pocket till the pocket wears thin. You will soon know it by heart. Till then let the heart be helped.

These are the words you lived. They stand in the order you met them. Speak them as orders, kindly and flat. They cost nothing and they keep everything.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND. Nothing I ask will hurt you.
2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET. The belief must change first.
3. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD. You are walking out, not giving in.
4. WATCH YOUR AFTERNOON LIKE A DETECTIVE. The slump will testify against the drawer.
5. TAKE THE BREAK, LEAVE THE WRAPPER. The pause was always the relief.
6. STOP PAYING YOURSELF IN POISON. A reward that punishes is a fine, not a wage.
7. THANK THE HANDS, NOT THE SUGAR. The love was the people.
8. EAT WHEN YOU ARE HUNGRY AND STOP WHEN YOU ARE SATISFIED. Your body already knows the portion.
9. NEVER NEGOTIATE THE SINGLE. One reopens what thousands could not close.
10. REFUSE THE SWAP, NOT JUST THE SUGAR. The loop changes costume, not character.
11. LET THE DAY BE SWEET WITHOUT IT. The candles were never the cake.
12. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY FORCE. Their struggle is their method's confession.
13. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY MEAL AND MAKE THE VOW. Speak it aloud, then you are free.
14. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION. A thought is weather; the verdict stands.

Eat what you want; BAD SUGAR is off the menu.

WHAT STAYS

What stays is ordinary. That is the wonder of it.

Mornings steady. You wake before the alarm by a breath and put feet to cold floor. Kettle, bread, fruit, water. Hunger answered sitting down. Talk at the table that stays talk. Plates pushed back without fog. The old state you will remember without visiting: tired, heavy and foggy. The present state you will live without remarking: clear, fed, upright, ready for the street.

Shops quiet. You walk in for milk and bread and greens and walk out with milk and bread and greens. Shelves call in their old dialect and the call sounds thinner, like a radio left on in another room. Pity rises where envy once lived. Those bright rows bill strangers now. You feel for them as you would feel for anyone still paying a tax they believe is a wage. You walk past smiling.

Meals closed. Coffee tastes of coffee. Soup tastes of soup. An orange opens in the hand with its own sharp perfume. Laughter tastes of laughter. Evenings tick quietly with cups washed and cloths folded and messages answered. Three o'clock passes without remark until you forget it ever needed remarking.

You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. Keep it so.

This keeping is not a performance. You do not count days. You do not bar cupboards. You do not tell friends what sits in their packets. Preaching breeds defence in friends and drags the new footing back into argument. Let your own settled plate do whatever talking is needed. If they ask another day, you will answer plainly then. Today the talk stays on the baby and the landlord and the holiday plan.

You walked out easily, immediately and permanently.

That sentence is not a boast. It is a timetable kept. Easy because nothing was taken. Immediate because freedom began at the vow. Permanent because the verdict stands while the thought is weather. Mornings will come steady like this for the rest of your life.

Picture it plain, not as a dream but as days. The same table holding a hundred ordinary meals that end with talk, not with reaching. The same three o'clock passing daily without remark. The same friends passing plates while you take what feeds and leave what bills, with no empty place to fear and no apartness to mourn. The same pocket holding the folded list till the folds soften and the ink fades, kept after fading because it marks the week you stopped paying.

If a blip finds you — sauce sweetened without your knowing, a bite taken in the dark before the mind woke, a label read too fast in a strange shop — do not turn accident into verdict. Your body copes with an occasional blip. What must never return is the decision to keep taking it. Note the coating. Drink water. Eat clean at the next hunger. Keep the vow intact. A stumble on the kerb is not a fall down the stair. Only reopening the decision makes it a fall, and you will not reopen it.

LIFE

The chair goes in. The kitchen stands clear.

The table is cleared. The vow stands. The list is in the pocket. The mouth is clean. The afternoon ahead is yours without billing. The evening ahead is yours without hunting. Work, love, food, talk, sleep — all stand where they stood, brighter for having nothing tied to them.

Go on with enjoying your life.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

SUMMARY

- The bright packet never lifted; it billed, and the billing has stopped.
- Mornings wake clean, afternoons hold without rescue, and hunger answered plainly satisfies.
- The pause soothes, the week pleases, the people love, the meal feeds — the shine added nothing to any of them.
- The single is the whole, the swap is the same loop in costume, and the hard grind belonged to the wrong method.
- The list sits in the pocket, the verdict stands, and taste came back plain.
- The book closes here and life opens here, steady and outward and done.
