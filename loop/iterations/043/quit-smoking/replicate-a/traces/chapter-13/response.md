Chapter 13
THE SPARE THAT LOCKS THE DOOR

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen table with the ashtray and the last tube, the drawer and the coat pocket and the car cubby where spares hide, the smoke watched close for its ugliness, the vow spoken out loud, and the morning after with nothing kept.

*A spare does not save you from craving. It orders the craving to stay.*

THE LAST CLUTCH

The table is clear except for what you placed there. An ashtray washed clean. A lighter. The pack from your pocket with two or three tubes left. You know this table. You have sat at it a thousand mornings with coffee going cold while you lit before thought. You know the drawer to the left where a sealed pack waits for emergencies. You know the coat pocket where a crushed pack waits behind receipts. You know the cubby in the car where a spare waits for the road. Tonight we gather all of them into daylight.

I know the clutch that holds one back, because we all held it. You say it softly, even now, after Tuesdays have been walked whole and grand evenings have been seen through. “I see it all. I am ready to end. But let me keep one sealed pack in the drawer, just for emergencies. Not to smoke. Only to know it is there. What if the night turns hard. What if work breaks. What if I panic.”

I answer without any scolding, because we all whispered that sentence. A man who trusts the bridge behind him is gone does not carry a plank on his back for emergencies. He walks on.

Ask what exactly you believe that sealed pack would do for you in an emergency.

When did a sealed pack ever stop an emergency, and when did it start one by whispering hourly that an emergency will come. If the pack protects you, why do you think about it while coffee pours and while talk runs and while work holds. Would a guard who loves you stand outside your door all night tapping to remind you that thieves may come, or would you call that tapping itself the thief of sleep.

You believe the spare calms you because you know it is there. I tell you the spare keeps you nervy because you know it is there. While it sits sealed in the drawer it does its work without burning. It tells the mind the question is still open. It tells the nerves to listen for trouble. It tells every hard hour to report itself as proof that the drawer must be opened. We lived with that listening for years and called it need.

Consider how we learned to read safety backwards.

We all knew the hour when the pack ran low. Shops shut. Rain hard. Night late. Chest tight at the thought of morning with none. Was that tightness proof tubes help in emergencies, or was it proof tubes manufacture the very emergency they claim to solve. The drawer spare was invented to quiet that manufactured panic. It quiets nothing. It rents the panic a room and charges by the night. A prisoner who keeps his own spare key in his shoe does not sleep better for knowing the key is there. He sleeps as a prisoner who knows the door is still locked and the key is his own work.

Ask the only honest questions this clutch permits.

Did any emergency in your life ever end because paper burned, or did the emergency end because people acted and time passed and you acted with them while paper burned beside the acting and claimed the ending. Would a rope that pulls you back into the cell prove a lifeline because you hold it while drowning in the cellar the rope guards, or does a lifeline prove itself by leading out. Would you keep a sealed bottle of the sickness that put you in bed in order to feel safe from sickness.

You believe a safety net proves sense. I tell you safeties keep the prison.

This is your exit from the nicotine trap. A trap with a spare key inside the cell is still a locked cell, and a man who throws the spare onto the table with the rest has already stepped through the door.

For years the Smokescreen taught you to read that hourly listening as wisdom. Wisdom is seeing the listening was installed, and lifting it out with the packs.

NO SINGLE ONE

There is a second voice behind the spare, quieter and more reasonable, and I will meet it head on because it has fooled kind people for months at a stretch.

“I will keep no pack. I will end now. But surely after months clean, one tube at a wedding, one draw with drinks among old friends, will not drag me back. Surely control grows with time.”

I have heard that sentence from men and women who had been clean five months and spoke with pride, and I have spoken its cousin in my own early bargains. We all know how the story ran after. One lighting offered as a test. Throat harsh again after months clear. Head light for minutes. Then the old twinge back by morning, then the old listening, then the old pack in the pocket by the weekend. Not because one tube contains a lifetime of poison in a single pull. Because one tube re-admits the belief that tubes do something for you, and belief re-arms the whole schedule.

Ask what that one would prove.

If tubes gave nothing in years of daily use, what would one tube give at a wedding except a brief quieting of the hunger that years of use installed and months clear had starved. If little means nothing, why pay for little with the return of hourly listening. Would a man who has walked out of a cellar into daylight climb back for a minute to test whether darkness still darkens, or does the test itself prove the old reading still rules him.

You believe time makes one safe. I tell you time makes you safe while the belief stays dead, and one keeps the belief alive.

The fact is plain from our own mouths across years of trying. The smoker who learned the hard way after months clean names it settled. One drags the chain back. Not as doom over you. Hear me well on this, because I speak to you warmly and not as a judge. If you ever light after this night, you have not proved yourself weak and you have not lost your worth. You have only fed the old reading again. Kill the reading again and walk out again. The door does not lock from the outside. But do not test the door by stepping inside to see if locks still lock. There is nothing inside worth testing.

You risk nothing by reading on, and you stand to win your freedom.

That line was true on the first page and it is true on this last threshold. You have tested every claim with eyes open. Coffee tasted cleaner with a clear mouth. Breaks rested through air and distance. Meals closed through fullness. Roads ran under hands. Coats smelled of nothing. Stairs climbed on breath alone. What exactly would one borrowed lighting add to that whole, except film on a clean tongue and a clock set ticking toward the next doorway.

See the pincer closed. If there is no benefit in much, there is no benefit in little. If much only fed hunger, little only feeds hunger in smaller coin. The moderate bargain is the same con with neater wrapping.

SMOKE IT AWAKE

Bring all of it to the table now. Not as a feast. As an inventory.

Open the drawer and lift the sealed spare you kept for emergencies. Turn the coat pockets out and lay the crushed pack with its two bent tubes beside it. Step to the car and open the cubby and lift the spare that rode every mile with you. Lift the kitchen shelf pack, the bag pack, the desk pack. Shake ashtrays into the bin and wash them. Lay lighters beside packs. Look at the pile as an object. This is not a friend gathered for farewell. This is the dispenser laid bare in daylight.

Count what the pile cost in money and hours and breath and hiding, not to frighten you into change, for I do not want you changing from fear. Count it to see the rescuer plain. The pile never carried you through emergencies. You carried the pile through emergencies and paid for the carrying.

Now take one tube for the last ordinary act. Only one. No special brand. No last-night-on-earth glow. No slow farewell with music and dim light. A plain tube from the pile, smoked in plain light at the table, with attention fixed on what it actually does. This is ceremonial attention, not a dare to smoke more. We end by watching, not by indulging.

Light as you always lit, and watch what you once called pleasure with eyes that have learned the reading.

Draw and notice paper heat first, not flavour. The mouth fills with hot, dry, faintly sweet chemical smoke that bites the back of the throat. The tongue, washed clean since morning coffee, films over within two pulls. Taste thins as film thickens. The nose fills with stale acrid air that clings to hairs and cloth. The chest takes the pull shallowly and coughs to limit it. Fingers take on the smell within a minute. The ember shortens and ash lengthens and must be tended. Tap. Ash falls. The hand returns to mouth on schedule.

Ask while the tube still burns what it gives.

Does bitterness sharpen coffee still warm beside the ashtray, or does coffee taste blunted beside it. Does the head clear, or does a faint dizziness pass for clearing against a background of nightly hunger briefly thinned. Does the hand rest holding and flicking and guarding from wind, or does the hand labour in small chores while claiming rest. Would a true pleasure need to be chased to the bitter last pulls that taste of hot filter and burnt paper, or does chasing to the bitter end prove hunger, not pleasure.

You believe lighting is heaven. I tell you lighting is replenishment mistaken for heaven, and replenishment watched close looks like what it is.

Smoke to the middle and keep watching. The early dizziness thins. A flat, slightly queasy pressure sits behind the eyes. Breath shortens. The mouth asks for water to rinse. The room smells changed. The coat on the chair takes on the sour sweet stale film you once aired for hours. The fingers that will touch food and faces carry tray smell. None of this is scolding from me. I stood in this smoke for years and called it company. I ask only that you see company weighed on true scales. A companion that films the tongue that tastes, narrows the breath that climbs, and perfumes curtains with stale air proves what kind of companion.

You hear the Ember Mite complain faintly as you watch, and that complaining is useful to hear plainly at last. A thin gnaw under the ribs. A sense of hands wanting business. A thought arriving in your own voice saying this last one soothes. Hear it for what it is. Not proof tubes help. Proof a trivial physical hunger, unfed for an hour, asks briefly to be fed, and the old reading rushes to translate the asking as need. For years the Smokescreen translated that gnaw as pleasure and relief. Tonight translation ends.

All that is left after the pile is gathered is a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling. That feeling is not you missing a friend. That feeling is old business finishing. It rises briefly, it thins while you drink and move and speak, it peaks within a few days for most and then subsides into quiet. You do not need to answer it. You only need to recognise it when it visits in the coming days and let the day go on whole around it.

Finish the tube if the tube still burns, or stub it midway if the ugliness has spoken enough. There is no rule that demands suffering to the filter. Crush it fully. Look at the crushed tube and the stained filter and the grey ash. Smell fingers and air. This is not nostalgia. This is evidence for the nose. The pile on the table is not a row of little parties. It is a row of doses that never rose above the normal of a man who never lit.

Sweep the pile now. Packs, spares, crushed tubes, lighters kept for lighting alone. Tie the bag. Carry it to the bin outside the door and leave it there. Wash the ashtray and shelve it clean for coins or keys. Shake the coat at the door and hang it to air. Open the window a minute and let outside air take the room back. Stand back and see the table clear. Coffee beside a clean tray tastes of coffee. Breath tastes of air. Hands hold nothing and are therefore at rest.

ARE YOU READY

Sit a minute before the vow, because the vow matters and I want it spoken from seeing, not from strain.

Are you ready. You should be champing at the bit to be done. You have seen the spare as a lock, not a lifeline. You have seen one as a chain, not a test. You have smoked awake and watched replenishment without its paint. You have walked Tuesdays whole. If any part still pulls, read that part again before you speak. Wait an hour and watch again if any part still pulls, and speak only when you see clearly. If you still believe some hour truly needs, return to that hour and watch again until the hour answers. Do not speak the vow from will. Speak it from sight.

If you take daily medicine or live with a health condition, keep your doctor in the picture as you stop.

I speak to you now as a man who stood where you stand, with the bin full outside and the window open and coffee cooling. I smoked for years, failed by patches and gum and stern promises, and ended not by fighting harder but by seeing that tubes never did anything for me at all. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. That line was the contract on the early pages and it is the contract on this page. Keep an open mind to the end. Smoke no more after the final tube. Judge nothing by a smoker lighting up. Remember what tubes do to smokers, not for them.

Speak the vow out loud, standing or sitting, with the table clear before you. Mean it as a statement of fact, not as a wish.

8. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND VOW NEVER TO SMOKE AGAIN
Know there is nothing to give up.

Say it in your own voice after me. “I have smoked my final tube. I have seen what it did to me and nothing for me. I vow never to smoke again.” Say the words fully. Hear them in the room. The vow is solemn because it marks a true ending, and it is joyful because the ending is an opening.

You are a non-smoker as of now. Not from tomorrow. Not after three days. Not when craving thins. Now. The pile is in the bin. The drawer holds no spare. The coat holds no spare. The car holds no spare. The belief that held them is dead, and with it the schedule dies. You leave smoking easily, at once and for good.

Let the next thought come whenever it comes, tomorrow at coffee, at the doorway at half past ten, after the meal, on the road, in the queue. It will come in your own voice and wear your own memories. “Just one. One will not hurt.” Do not argue with it and do not push it away. Answer it with the full weight of what you have seen, compressed into the joy it deserves.

Think of coffee tasted to the grounds. Think of the break taken in air and sky. Think of the meal closed by fullness. Think of the coat smelling of nothing and stairs climbed on breath alone. Think of the pile in the bin and the tube watched awake to ash. Then let the answer rise as lived relief, not as a slogan learned.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it under breath or aloud, and feel what the words point to. Not a boast. A report. The thought that once ordered a lighting now triggers the recognition that there is nothing to light for. In the background the Ember Mite grumbles faintly and starves a little more with each unanswered hour. In those hours the Smokescreen, which once translated every gnaw as proof of need, has dissolved, and no translation returns unless you invite it back. Each time the thought comes and is answered this way, the answering grows quicker and the thought grows thinner until it visits rarely and then no more.

Stand now and live the evening as it is with nothing burning. Drink water and notice how water rinses a clear mouth. Talk a minute with whoever shares the house. Step to the door and breathe at the kerb. Air meets the face cold or warm. Traffic passes. Sky holds its evening colour. Lungs fill fully without tending. Hands rest in pockets or hold warmth. No ember times the minute. No ash asks tending. The day closes whole because days close themselves.

SUMMARY

- A sealed spare does not guard against hard hours. It keeps the question open and orders nerves to listen for trouble.
- One tube after months clean does not test control. It re-admits the belief that tubes do something, and belief re-arms the schedule.
- The final tube, smoked awake in plain light, shows hot paper and filmed tongue and tray smell, not pleasure or help.
- What remains after the pile is in the bin is brief old business in the body, which thins while you drink and move and speak.
- The vow spoken from seeing marks an instant change of identity, and the next thought of lighting is answered with joy.
