IN THIS CHAPTER: the table cleared after coffee, the folded list in a pocket, the street door open.

THE DAY YOU OWN

This morning the kettle boiled and the cup warmed the hands and the day started itself. No paper timed the hour. No ember ordered feet to the door. Coffee tasted where it lands when nothing films the tongue. The second mouthful went deeper because taste had somewhere to land. The window stood cracked and air met the face and the street sounded its ordinary starting.

We spent years paying by the hour to interrupt days like this and calling the interruption the day. You have watched that reading fall apart at a clear table. You do not need to watch it fall again. What was seen stays seen when you live it.

Your ordinary Tuesday going on whole is the proof kept in the hands. The shop bell rings and the queue moves slow and you wait as a man waits when no clock ticks in him. Feet on tile. Jars on shelves. Talk at the till. Milk and bread paid for and carried home with the bag light on the wrist. Work holds and then breaks and you step to the doorway where air and distance and talk do the resting. No ash asks tending. No butt needs hiding. Evening comes and glasses clink and faces warm and stories lengthen and no one leaves mid-sentence to feed anything. Pavement cools under shoes on the way home. The coat holds night air and nothing else. The stairs climb on breath alone. The door shuts on a day without a gap that needed paper.

I stood in those hours for years and believed each one needed lighting to close. Morning needed it to start. The queue needed it to wait through. The doorway needed it to be kind. The table needed it to seal. I tell you now from the other side of that reading: they close themselves. Sit where you always sat. Drink as you always drank. Laugh as you always laughed. Go with them if you always went. Change nothing else to prove anything. The hours you feared to meet are the hours that prove, by continuing whole, that nothing was taken.

Water rinses a clear mouth. Food stops when hunger stops and the meal closes itself. Sleep comes with lungs unfilmed and mornings start cleaner for the same reason. None of this is learned. It is the day walked before, now walked without tending.

When a thought of lighting visits in your own voice tomorrow at coffee or at the doorway or after the meal, let it come and let it pass. A thought is not an order. It is old reading knocking once to see if anyone still translates. You do not argue and you do not push. Pushing makes a small thought mighty. You see and you answer with joy and you go on with the story being told. Each answering leaves the next visit thinner until visits grow rare and then no more.

WHAT FITS IN A POCKET

You will not carry chapters in your head. You carry lines short enough to speak under breath while the kettle boils or the queue shuffles or talk runs at the table. They are not lessons to study and not rules to strain after. They are what you already saw, kept in the order you saw it, so a tired hour cannot shuffle them.

Fold this list and keep it where cellophane used to sit. Photograph it if you wish so it sits in the telephone beside faces of people you love. Read it straight when the old voice knocks. Read it in the voice you would use to remind a friend of something plain you both watched. Then put it back and pour another cup and turn back to work and talk. The lines do their work quietly while you live.

SUMMARY

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Let the argument finish before you decide.

2. KEEP SMOKING EXACTLY AS NORMAL
Do not stop or cut down until your final cigarette.

3. BEGIN WITH EXCITEMENT, NOT DREAD
You are escaping, not losing.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Anyone who found it hard used the wrong method.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY STRAIN
Their struggle was the method, not the truth about stopping.

7. JUDGE NOTHING BY A SMOKER LIGHTING UP
A lit cigarette tells you nothing about pleasure.

8. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND VOW NEVER TO SMOKE AGAIN
Know there is nothing to give up.

9. PITY, NEVER ENVY, THE SMOKER YOU SEE
Remember what it does to them, not for them.

WHAT YOU STOPPED PAYING

The table stands now with the pile in the bin and the packet gone, and what the coins were buying across all those years stands plain beside it. The pause was in stopping work. The seal was in food and company and leisure. The help was hunger briefly quieted that hunger itself had installed. The payment bought back for minutes what the payment had taken for hours.

The exchange stands plain once and then counting ends. Month after month you laid rent money on a counter for a room you already owned. The walls were yours before paper. The air was yours before paper. Morning and shop and doorway and table were yours before paper and stayed yours while paper charged admission to stand in them. The door stands open now without a hand held out. You live across the whole floor while the till keeps its bright rows for others.
