Chapter 7
THE SECOND THE MATCH MOVES

IN THIS CHAPTER: the minute you light and call heaven, the taste that follows it, the coat shaken at the door and the car aired at the kerb, the coffee cans of ends by the back step, the mild pack weighed in the palm, and the meter on the corner that keeps taking coins.

*The heaven you feel at the lighter lasts seconds because it is hunger fed, not joy made.*

THE MINUTE YOU CALL HEAVEN

I know the minute you mean, because I lived it thousands of times and defended it the way you defend it now.

You have gone two hours without. A meal has run long. A meeting has run long. A train has run long. Your hands have grown light and restless in a way you barely name. You step to the doorway or the yard or the car, you tap the pack, you put the roll between your lips, you work the lighter, and the first pull comes in hot and full.

The moment I light up I am in heaven. That is how smokers put it when they speak honestly, and I do not argue with the sentence. I felt it. You feel it. The shoulders drop. The gnaw stops. The head clears for a breath. After the long gap the smoke feels kind.

Stay with that minute a little longer than praise allows, because the minute tells on itself when watched whole.

Light the same roll after five minutes of steady feeding and there is no heaven in it. It tastes hot and hurried. You half finish it talking and stub it without thanks. Light the first of the day after sleep and it can feel like rescue. Light the fourth in an hour and it feels like nothing much. If the roll carried heaven inside the paper, every lighting would carry the same heaven. It does not. The heaven arrives tallest after the longest hunger and thinnest after the shortest. You know this from your own pocket without my telling.

Did the paper change between those lightings, or did the interval change. Did the tobacco learn kindness overnight, or did the body go longest without its dose overnight. Would the same first pull feel like heaven if the last twenty thousand pulls had never taught the hands to ask.

Ask what kind of heaven needs hunger first in order to be felt. Food tastes good to the hungry and to the full, though hunger sharpens it. Music moves the tired and the rested. A warm room welcomes whoever enters. Your lighting heaven moves only one kind of watcher, the one whose last dose has faded, and it moves him exactly where the fade stands tallest. That is not the shape of a pleasure. That is the shape of a gap closed.

I say this without mocking your heaven, because mocking would be mocking you, and you deserve better than mockery. The quiet after that first pull is honest quiet. The shoulders do drop. The gnaw does stop. The lie is not in the feeling. The lie is in the signature under it. The hour signed the receipt, when the fading dose wrote the bill.

Watch what follows the heaven in the same ten minutes, because you already know the second half and file it under nothing.

The second half of the roll never tastes like the first second. It tastes hot at the back. It bites. The last pull tastes bitter and you take it anyway, then stub with a small twist and a faint disgust you hide even from yourself. The mouth needs water. The fingers smell. You shake the coat at the door. You wind the car window down though the evening is cold. You chew something. You spray something. You stand a little apart for the next talk so no one leans into the cloud round your hair. At home the empties tell the same story plainer. Coffee cans of butts by the back step. The ashtray emptied late so the kitchen will not smell in the morning. The pack hidden from the child, the ends carried to the outside bin, the coat hung apart from the clean washing.

You read that evening as a true pleasure or a true crutch.

Read that sentence over the ashtray and test which office the lighting claims. If it stood as pleasure, the pleasure would spread across the roll and across the hour after it, the way a good meal spreads warmth across the talk after it and also across a quiet lunch alone. If it stood as a prop, the hour after would stand richer than the hour before. Watch your own ten minutes and you find neither. The first seconds soothe. The rest irritates. The hour after carries no lift, only the start of the next fade. Only the interval is marked, and the marking is called heaven.

Did the roll sweeten the air round your coat, or did it sour the air round your coat. Did it sweeten the car for the next drive, or did the next drive begin with windows down and talk about airing. Did it leave the mouth cleaner for the next meal, or did the next meal begin with brushing and water. You know the answers because your own shaking and spraying wrote them. The aftertaste is not an accident beside the heaven. It is the same act seen whole.

You say you love smoking and hate being a smoker, and I take both halves at face value. Of course the lighting feels loved. It ends the gnaw fastest. Of course the being feels hated. The gnaw returns, the smell stays, the hiding goes on, the paying goes on. We all lived that split and thought the split proved we were weak or strange. It proves nothing about you. It proves the heaven is moment-only, a second of feeding inside a day of carrying.

That split second is the nicotine trap.

Read that sentence plainly over the lit end and let it hold. A hold that gives seconds of quiet and takes hours of carrying looks exactly like pleasure from inside the seconds. From outside the ten minutes it looks like metering. The body meters the day into gaps. The mind thanks the gap-closer for the seconds the gap pauses. We all thanked it, and the thanks felt honest, because the quiet after a pull is honest quiet. It is only not what it claims to be.

THE SHAME THAT FOLLOWS IS EVIDENCE, NOT CHARACTER

Stay with the hating half, because smokers misread it as proof something is wrong with them, and the misreading keeps the hold shut.

You light among friends and feel high for a breath. Then the car needs airing. Then the coat needs shaking. Then the hands need washing before the child climbs up. Then the can by the step needs emptying before visitors come. You feel shitty afterwards, as smokers say when no one polished is listening, and you think the shittiness proves you lack control or cleanliness or will.

I tell you as settled fact that the shittiness proves the roll did its work on you, not that you failed at yours.

A true pleasure leaves no hiding. A meal you loved leaves plates, not shame. A walk you loved leaves tired legs, not spray. A talk you loved leaves a warm head, not a coat hung apart. Your lighting leaves hiding in every room it enters. The hiding is not your character. It is the trace of what was burned.

Consider the non-smoker who stood beside you at the same doorway and breathed the same cold.

You believe he missed the heaven and kept a grey minute. I ask you to watch his minute closer and credit him with nothing extra. He stood in the same cold. He watched the same traffic. He felt no heaven at the start because no hunger stood in him to be fed, and he felt no shittiness after because no smoke stood in him to be carried. He met one rhythm, cold and talk in their own clothes. You met two rhythms, cold and talk plus the hourly hunger that must be fed inside each pause. The roll quiets the second rhythm and leaves the first untouched, then claims credit for the whole air.

Did the cold need paper in order to be borne, or did lungs built for air bear it without paper. Did the pause need fire in order to be rested in, or did the sitter rest in it without fire. Did the talk after stand warmer for the lighting, or did it stand exactly where talk stands when no lighting cuts it.

You think the heaven proves the roll adds. I tell you the heaven proves the roll subtracts first and rents back a second of normal. The shame that follows is the receipt for the renting, paid in smell and hiding and ends in cans.

We misread those cans at the time, and the misreading kept the hold shut. We told ourselves the cans proved we loved it too much to stop, rather than seeing the cans proved we carried something we did not love enough to keep in the room. We credited the later lighting with rescue, though the morning coat before it had already told the cost. We praised the window for air, forgetting who filled the room with smoke.

Ask what remains when the pulls are taken out of the lighting and the lighting is left standing.

The doorway remains. The cold remains. The stretching remains. The looking at lit streets remains. The pause remains. They hold nothing that needed paper and fire to be borne. What does not remain is the second load, the hourly gnaw that turned an ordinary pause into something that felt unusable without lighting. You will meet the same pauses with one gnaw fewer, not with one joy fewer. That is not a loss to dread. That is a load laid down.

THE METER ON THE CORNER

Follow the paying itself, because the paying exposes the control story better than any talk about pleasure.

You tell yourself you control it, and I do not blame you for the sentence, because I spoke it in the same tone for years. I told myself it was not a big deal since I did not smoke that much. A few a day. Never in the house. Never before noon, except on hard days. Healthy otherwise. My vice, my choice. Different for me. Less hooked than the heavy crowd.

I ask you to walk with me past a meter on a town corner and watch dependence dressed as preference.

A driver parks a car he loves. At the kerb stands a grey post with a slot. Feed it coins or the car gets marked, then towed. He feeds it through the day. He learns its rate. He sets alarms for its rate. He hurries back from meals to feed it. He keeps coins for it in every pocket. He knows which meters take cards and which jam. Ask him if he loves the meter and he laughs. Of course he does not love it. He feeds it because the loss behind it feels worse. No one calls his feeding a hobby. No one calls the meter his pleasure or his crutch. It is a tax on parking, hated and paid.

Now learn that the man who sold him the car bolted the meter to the kerb himself, keeps the coins, and tells him meters are part of driving.

You feed through the day the same way. You keep supply in every pocket. You learn rates, how long a meeting can run, how long a film can run, how long a flight can run before the gnaw turns loud. You hurry back from tables to feed. You cut evenings into coins. You call the feeding pleasure when the gap before it was long, and control when the gaps are kept short and neat. The meter does not pause for neatness. It only runs quieter when fed on time.

Did you choose the rate, or did the last dose set the rate. Did you choose the hurry back from the table, or did the fade set the hurry. Did the non-smoker at the same table check his pockets before the long evening, or did he sit through without checking because no meter stood over his seat.

You believe a few a day proves light control. I tell you a few a day proves the same meter running on a longer leash. The leash is longer between checks, the coins fewer, the hurry smaller, but the parking is still taxed. The heavy crowd feeds every forty minutes and calls it need. You feed five times a day and call it choice. The meter takes both feedings with the same slot.

That tax is the nicotine trap speaking in coins.

Read that sentence at the kerb and test it against your own counting. If feeding proved liking, the driver would praise the post. He does not. If fewer coins proved no meter, the car would stand safe unfed. It does not. Leave the car unfed past the hour and the mark appears, then the tow. Leave the body unfed past the hour and the gnaw appears, then the edginess that sends the hand to the pocket. The feeding is not preference wearing work clothes. It is obedience wearing the clothes of preference.

And here is the grace I owe you plainly, because stern words without grace would be bullying and I will not bully you.

Whether you smoke five or forty, whether you roll or buy boxed, whether you took it up young to stand with friends or late to fill work pauses, whether you run hard each morning or climb stairs winded, the line holds the same. The count never measured your strength. It measured the rate you were taught to pay. I have watched heavy men and light women meet the same minute when the meter showed, the same hand moving before thought finished, the same quiet after feeding misread as character. Whatever number you carry stays a number, not a verdict. The method asks no change in number first, only a clear look at what the number pays.

Ask what the meter ever gave the car besides leaving it where it already stood.

The street gave the standing. The car gave the travel. The driver gave the parking. The meter gave nothing but the absence of the tow it threatened. Your lighting gives nothing but the absence of the gnaw it renewed. Once the marks are sorted, the day keeps only its rooms and talk, none of which ever asked for coins in a slot.

THE MILD PACK IN THE PALM

Stay with the mild pack itself, because it carries the second half of the control story, the safer-brand bargain, and the bargain needs weighing.

You keep the light pack or the mild pack or the low-number pack as proof of care. The numbers on the side read lower. The taste reads smoother. The throat reads kinder. You tell yourself the smoother smoke must take less, and a smaller take must mean a smaller hold. You point to the pack and read it as moderation made visible.

I ask you to weigh that pack in the palm and test it with attention, because the hand knows what the numbers hide.

Take the pack as you take it a hundred times a day and feel its weight whole. Note the colour, pale and clean, made to look airy. Note the filter with its ring of tiny holes, too neat to notice unless looked for. Note how light the roll feels between fingers, how easy the draw feels on the first pull, how the smoke feels thin and almost clean. You believe the thinness proves less entering, when the thinness proves air entering alongside smoke.

Now smoke one with full attention from lighting to stub and watch what the body does without asking leave.

You pull a little deeper because the thin smoke invites a deeper pull. You hold a little longer because the smoother taste allows a longer hold. You take a few pulls more because the kinder throat does not warn you off. Your fingers and lips cover the tiny holes without meaning to, the way a hand covers a draught without teaching. You finish sooner and reach sooner for the next, or you finish slower and pull harder to get the same close. None of this is plotted. It is hunger doing arithmetic the mind does not watch.

Did the machine that gave the pack its numbers smoke the way you smoke. A machine takes the same small sip each time, through the same open holes, at the same pace, with no gnaw inside it and no memory of the last dose. You smoke with a gnaw inside you and a memory of relief, through holes half covered by holding, at the pace hunger sets. The machine reads lower because it is not hungry. You read the pack as lower while the body reads hunger and makes up the pull.

Consider two evenings you already lived, though you misread them at the time.

On the mild pack you stood out as often, or more often, and pulled longer on the smoother rolls, and called the smoothness kindness. On the full pack you stood out as often and coughed sooner, and called the cough proof the milds were kinder. You filed both evenings under care and missed what they shared. Both evenings cut the night into the same coins. Both evenings left the same cans. Both evenings began with hunger and ended with quiet mis-signed. The count did not follow the number on the side. It followed the gnaw underneath.

I do not claim every smoker makes up the pull to the same drop, because bodies differ and hours differ and no honest watcher can promise identical arithmetic for all. I claim what your own evenings already showed without instruments. Machine numbers fall on paper while human taking does not fall the same way, because people deepen and lengthen and repeat without deciding to. That is enough to break the bargain. A bargain that depends on you smoking like a machine breaks the first time hunger holds the roll.

Ask what kind of safer needs harder pulling in order to feel the same.

A safer that safed would ask less of lungs, not more. Warm milk asks less of the throat than hot spirit cut with water that lets you drink deeper. Quiet asks less of ears than muffled hammering that lets you stand closer. Your mild asks more pulls, deeper and longer, through a smoother mask that lets the pull run deep. The mask is flavour, not mercy. The throat reads kindness while the lungs receive the same taking dressed in air.

Try the palm test again tomorrow with the new question in hand when the old thought shouts loudest. Notice how each honest look makes the next mild plainer. The first pull tastes more openly of air mixed with paper. The deeper pull tastes more openly of hunger steering the hand. The quiet after feels more openly like feeding, not like care. The edge between grows more openly like a rate, not like taste.

You believe the pale pack proves you have tamed the hold. I tell you the pale pack proves the hold tames the pulling without asking leave. The colour changed. The slot stayed.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Read that verdict over the weighed pack and let it close the bargain. It stains the coat it promised to spare. It fills the can it promised to lighten. It taxes the day it promised to ease. It teaches the fingers to cover holes it calls ventilation. Once the marks are sorted, the mild keeps only its paler paper and smoother lie, neither of which ever asked to be paid for in pulls.

WHAT THE LIGHTING NEVER GAVE

Run one day backwards with me to test the reading, because the hold hates backwards watching.

Take a day you counted as proof of pleasure and control. The morning roll with coffee that started the engine. The work-break roll that cleared the head. The after-meal roll that sealed the food. The night roll with drink that crowned the talk. You point to the row and read it as four small heavens held by choice, milds keeping the taking light.

Now run the same day with the credit reassigned and watch how neatly it fits.

You woke with hours of no doses behind sleep, so the hunger stood high and the first pulls only brought you near normal. The morning allowed steady feeding between tasks, so no hunger grew tall enough to feel like crisis. Each pause arrived with a hunger near its peak inside it, because pauses come when intervals end. Each lighting quieted the hunger inside the pause and let you mistake the quiet for help. The count ran as the intervals ran, not as joys deserved seals. The milds ran smoother, so the pulls ran deeper, and the deeper pulls kept the rate where the rate always stood.

You believe the count proves enjoyment steered by care. I tell you the count proves schedule inside liking.

Think of the pause that seems to argue back. The roll after food that feels most like a seal. I answer that food closes a meal on its own, coffee warms on its own, talk loosens on its own, rest restores on its own. The seal you feel is the hunger ending inside the closing, two closings cresting together while only one gets thanked. The match is timing wearing the clothes of sealing.

Did the lighting change the day, or did it change the hunger inside the watcher. Did the glow arrive because joy was made, or because the dose arrived on schedule and faded on schedule and the fading demanded another arrival. Would the same warm pauses have needed the same lighting if the last twenty thousand lightings had never taught the body to ask.

You know the answers because your own intervals wrote them. The day stayed. The dose came and went. The pause that never knew the lighting never asks for it.

I do not ask you to smoke or not to smoke this minute, to change your brand or your count, to hide your pack or display it. Keep your day exactly as it stands. Meet the same pauses at the same hours. Let coffee stand where it stood. The book asks only clear sight in the lighting minute. Watch with the new question in hand when the old thought shouts loudest. Notice how the staying minute holds whole without lighting. The food keeps its close. The pause keeps its rest. The talk keeps its thread. The lighting keeps only its seconds, hot and hurried, none of which ever needed a day to be carried in.

Hold that verdict while you meet the next hunger, because the next hunger will test it within hours.

You will feel the gnaw rise after the meal or the meeting, and the hand will lift before thought finishes, and the voice will speak in your own tone, reasonable and small. I loved each one. Life is short. I would light now if I truly loved it. I smoke little. It is different for me. Answer each with what the ten minutes taught. Loved each lighting, or loved the seconds that ended hunger. Short life, or short quiet rented at long carrying. Would light now for joy, or would feed now to still the gnaw. Little smoking, or same meter on a longer leash. Different, or same hunger in different clothes.

Ask what the lighting ever did for the day besides feed its own fading, and refuse every answer that points at the meal or the pause. The meal did the closing. The pause did the resting. The people did the holding. Once the marks are sorted, the day keeps only its work and rest, neither of which ever asked for fire to be shared.

SUMMARY

- The minute called heaven arrives tallest after the longest gap and thinnest after steady feeding, which shows it is hunger fed rather than joy made.
- The same ten minutes leave bitter taste, coated smell, shaken coats, aired cars and hidden cans, which shows the act takes across the hour what it rents for seconds.
- The hating half is not proof you are weak, because the trace belongs to what was burned rather than to character.
- Feeding a few a day still feeds on a rate set by the last dose, which shows control counts coins while the meter keeps the slot.
- Pale packs with tiny holes read lower on machines that sip without hunger, while people pull deeper, hold longer and cover the holes without meaning to, which leaves human taking not lowered the same way.
- Smoother taste lets the pull run deeper, which shows mildness masks flavour rather than removes taking, and no brand is a safer way to keep feeding.
