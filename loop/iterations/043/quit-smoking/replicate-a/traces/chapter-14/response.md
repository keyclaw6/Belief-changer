Chapter 14
MORNINGS, SHOPS, DRINK WITH FRIENDS

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen in morning light with coffee poured, the corner shop and the queue that moves slow, the work doorway at half past ten, a table with friends and glasses, and the night pavement home with nothing burning.

*Your first free days ask nothing new of you. Live them on the seeing you already own.*

MORNING COFFEE

You wake and the hand moves before thought. For years it moved toward the pocket and the pack. This morning it moves toward the kettle and the cup, and for half a second the old pairing tugs and says coffee means lighting.

Let the pairing tug and pass. Pour as you always pour. Sit where you always sit. The kitchen holds its morning smell of toast and wash water. The cup warms the palms. The first mouthful spreads bitter and full across a tongue that no film covered in the night. Notice how coffee tastes when nothing films it. The second mouthful tastes deeper because taste has somewhere to land.

We all lived the morning chain where coughing stood before feet and several tubes burned before the day began. That chain taught us to read morning as proof we needed. Morning proves nothing except that a body wakes thirsty and a mind wakes into habit. Drink slowly and let the day start itself.

There may be a thin restlessness under the ribs while the cup cools. Hands may want business. A thought may arrive in your own voice saying one with coffee would seal it.

That thin gnaw is only the Ember Mite complaining faintly as it starves.

Hear it plainly and go on pouring. It rises briefly and thins while you drink and move and speak. Stand and rinse the cup. Open the window a crack and breathe at the sill. Air meets the face. The street sounds its ordinary starting. Lungs fill without tending. Hands hold warmth and then are at rest. No ember times the minute. The morning closes its first hour whole because mornings close themselves.

Ask while the cup still steams what a tube would add to this whole. Would bitterness sharpen coffee, or blunt it beside hot paper. Would the head clear, or take on a faint dizziness that passes for clearing. Would the hand rest holding and flicking and guarding ash, or does the hand rest now holding nothing. You know the answer because you smoked awake at the table and watched. There is no need to rehearse the watching. Live the answer.

THE SHOP QUEUE

On the second morning you walk to the corner shop. Milk, bread, whatever the house needs. The bell rings as you push the door. The shelf wall stands bright. Behind the counter the gantry glows with packs in neat rows, and for a second the old walk runs itself: coins counted, brand named, cellophane cracked on the way out.

Walk past it and feel what passing means now. You are not passing something precious. You are passing doses that never rose above the normal of a man who never lit. The price tickets say more than price. They say money paid daily for the privilege of staying listening. Let the tickets stay there for others for now. Your pockets keep their coins.

Take your place in the queue. The queue moves slow. A child counts sweets. An old man counts change twice. The till beeps. For years every wait meant a lighting or the planning of one. The wait felt too long because hourly listening made every wait too long.

Wait as a man waits when no clock ticks in him. Look at the jars. Feel feet on tile. What you feel in the chest while waiting is only a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling that thins while you stand and breathe.

It is not a hole that needs filling. It is old business finishing. It visits, it thins, it passes while the queue shuffles forward. You do not need to answer it. You only need to recognise it and let the queue go on whole around it.

At the till, take your milk and bread and pay. The hand that once slid coins for packs slides coins for food and keeps the change. Step outside with the bag light on the wrist. Great, I will carry this home and pour another cup. Speak it inwardly in the positive, as a man naming what he will have. Name what you will have, never what you will do without. The mouth that tastes milk and bread and coffee needs no other tending. The shop errand ends at the doorstep with the bag unpacked and the day unbroken.

THE DOORWAY AT HALF PAST TEN

Work holds until half past ten and then breaks. Chairs push back. Voices rise. Feet move toward the same doorway where for years you stood shoulder to shoulder in cold and rain, trading lighters and minutes, finishing one and lighting half of another before going back in.

Go with them if you always went, or stay a minute and then go. Change nothing else in life to prove anything. The break belongs to air and distance and talk, not to paper. Step out and stand where you always stood. The wall holds its old warmth or cold. Traffic passes. Sky holds its colour. Talk runs about the morning's work and the evening's plans.

Someone lights beside you. Smoke drifts. For half a second the old credit rushes forward and says the break works because of that lighting. Watch where the break truly lives. It lives in stopping work, in stretching legs, in laughing at the story told badly twice, in air taken fully. You took those breaks for years while paper burned beside the taking and claimed the rest. You know who did the resting.

I stood in that doorway for years and believed the doorway needed lighting to be kind. I tell you now as a man who stands there with nothing burning: the doorway is kinder with a clear mouth. Breath goes down whole. No ash asks tending. No butt needs hiding. Talk does not pause to drag and flick.

If the thought visits while talk runs — just one here, no one would mind, this is the hour that proves it — do not argue with it and do not push it away. Pushing makes a small thought mighty. Arguing reopens a question this book has closed. Let the thought arrive in your own voice and answer it with the full weight of what you have seen, compressed into joy.

You will meet that answering fully in a page or two. For now know this settled handling: a thought is not an order. A thought is old reading knocking once to see if anyone still translates. Leave the door shut and go on with the story being told. The story is better heard with clear breath.

DRINK WITH FRIENDS

Evening comes and friends gather round a table with glasses. Beer or wine or coffee, whatever the house pours. Talk runs loose. Laughter comes quicker. For years this was the hour we feared most to meet without tubes, because we believed drink and company and lighting were one pleasure braided together.

Take your seat early and settle. Hold your glass in the hand that once held tubes between sips. Drink as you always drank. Laugh as you always laughed. The night belongs to people, the exile belonged to paper. You remember that old smoking-area hub from nights out, and you remember standing outside while talk went on inside.

Do not restage it tonight by stepping out to test yourself. Sit inside where talk lives. Notice how the table holds without gaps. Glasses clink. Faces warm. Stories lengthen. No one times the minute by ember. No one leaves mid-sentence to feed anything. The seal of the evening is fullness and company and leisure, and all of it was there on nights you credited paper for carrying.

Across the table a friend lights. Then another is offered one and takes it with the old line we all spoke: one will not hurt with drinks. Watch without any scolding, because we all spoke that line and we all believed it kind.

See what it does to them, not for them. The lighter flares. The face tightens for the pull. The talk pauses for drag and flick. The eyes water faintly and then pretend they do not. The coat on the chair takes on the sour sweet stale film that will ride home and sleep in cloth. The hand labours in small chores while claiming rest. Minutes leak toward the door. The purse leaks toward the gantry. The nerves listen for the next gap to slip away again.

Ask in silence the only honest question this sight permits. If tubes gave pleasure here, why does the smoker spend the evening interrupting pleasure to feed them. If tubes gave company here, why does the smoker leave company hourly to stand apart. Would a true seal need to be renewed every forty minutes, or does renewal prove hunger, not sealing.

You feel no pang of being left out, because nothing is being shared except a schedule. What you feel is steadier and warmer. You pity the schedule and you love the man caught in it, as I loved friends while I stood with them and as I love them now standing apart in sight.

9. PITY, NEVER ENVY, THE SMOKER YOU SEE
Remember what it does to them, not for them.

Hold that line as your standing answer for every lit tube you will ever watch again. It is not coldness. Coldness would mock or lecture. Warmth sees a fellow prisoner tapping his own bars and wishes him the same daylight. You do not envy a man for paying hourly to interrupt his own evening. You are glad the interrupting has left your evening.

Speak nothing of this at the table. This matters more than it sounds, so hear me well. Do not preach, do not explain the trap, do not correct the smoker beside you, even from love. Preaching breeds defence in friends and drags you back into argument that reopens your own settled question. The friend who hears a lecture hears that you still take tubes seriously enough to fight over. The friend who sees you sit whole through drink and talk and laughter with nothing burning, asking for nothing, missing nothing, hears without a word that evenings close themselves.

If asked, answer short and glad and then return to talk. I quit and I am glad to be out. Say it once and let visible ease do the rest of the recruiting. Turn the talk back to the story, the game, the week. Pour another round. Great, I will have another coffee while you finish that story. Name what you will have. Let the night run.

Walk home when the night thins. Pavement cool under shoes. Air full in the chest at the kerb. Houses quiet. Your coat smells of night air and nothing else. Stairs climb on breath alone. The door shuts on a day that held morning and shop and work and friends without a single gap that needed paper. Your ordinary Tuesday going on whole asks for no comment. It proves itself by continuing.

WHEN THE THOUGHT VISITS

In these first days the thought will visit. Tomorrow at coffee. At the doorway. After the meal. On the road. In the queue. It will come in your own voice and wear your own memories. Have one. Just one. One will not hurt. You have been so good, one would be a reward. The night is hard, one would take care of it.

We all hear it. The visit proves nothing except that old reading was read for years and echoes a few times before silence. Do not mourn the echo as a lost friend. Rejoice at a dead enemy knocking once to see if anyone answers.

Do not suppress and do not obey. Do what you did at the table with the pile: see and answer. Think of coffee tasted to the grounds. Think of the break taken in air and talk. Think of the queue waited through whole. Think of the meal closed by fullness. Think of the tube watched awake to ash. Then let the answer rise as lived relief, not as a slogan learned.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it under breath or aloud, and feel what the words point to. Not a boast. A report. The thought that once ordered a lighting now triggers the recognition that there is nothing to light for. Each time the thought comes and is answered this way, the answering grows quicker and the thought grows thinner until it visits rarely and then no more.

Guard one handling above all in these days. Never reopen the decision. The decision was made from seeing at a clear table with the pile in the bin. A passing tug does not make seeing unseen. A hard hour does not make a dose helpful. A drink does not make one safe. If the mind says let me weigh it again, answer that weighing is the trap knocking, and go on with coffee, work, talk. The question stays shut because the answer stays seen.

Keep your hands to what hands do. Drink water and notice how water rinses a clear mouth. Eat when hungry and stop when full and let the meal close itself. Take the break outside and breathe. Drive and wait and queue without timing by ember. Wear the coat that smells of nothing. Climb without pause. Sleep will come easier with lungs unfilmed, and mornings will start cleaner for the same reason. None of this is a new method to learn. It is the day you already walked, now walked without tending.

IF YOU EVER LIGHT

Hear this once so no slip can ever grow teeth, and then put it aside and live.

If on some strained evening after drinks you take a lit tube offered fast before thought catches, do not stand in shame and do not say you have blown it and may as well fetch a pack. Shame is the old collapse that turns one tug into a chain. One lighting does not prove you weak and does not prove tubes precious. It proves only that old reading, re-admitted for a minute, orders the old schedule again.

See it as a warning, not as a failure. A rumble strip at the road edge warns; it does not drive the car into the ditch. Crush the tube fully. Rinse the mouth. Smell fingers and air and remember the watching at the table. Kill the reading again on the spot: that pull quieted nothing except the hunger that years of use installed, and hunger re-fed is not pleasure re-found. Walk back inside to talk and glasses. Do not fetch and do not test again to see if you got away with it. Getting away is not tested by stepping back into the cell to check whether locks still lock. There is nothing inside worth testing.

You have stopped paying rent for a room that was yours all along. The room holds morning and shop and doorway and table and pavement. No landlord knocks hourly now. Keep the door shut and live in the whole of it.

SUMMARY

- Mornings run whole on coffee and air and starting, with no lighting needed to seal them.
- Shops and queues are waited through as they are, with the restless edge recognised as old business thinning.
- Breaks rest through stopping and talk and distance, not through paper burning beside the rest.
- Drink with friends belongs to people and laughter, and a lit tube watched close shows interruption, not pleasure.
- A thought of lighting is answered with joy, never argued with or pushed away, and grows thinner each time it is answered.
- Preaching is left aside, and visible ease does whatever recruiting is ever needed.
- A slip, if one ever comes, is a warning to see clearer, never proof of weakness and never leave to fetch more.
