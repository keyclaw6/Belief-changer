Chapter 8
THE WINDOW THE JAILER RENTS YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER: the ten seconds after a pull when the head clears, the twenty minutes after when the gnaw starts again, the restless second day when the hands feel wrong, the airless cellar with one barred window, and the small gnaw named plainly for what it is.

*The emptiness you feel between cigarettes was made by cigarettes, and it passes as hunger passes.*

THE TEN SECONDS THAT FOOLED YOU

Watch one lighting with a clock in your hand, because the clock tells what praise hides.

You lift the roll, you draw, the smoke goes deep, and within seconds the gnaw loosens. The shoulders drop a finger's width. The head clears for a breath. The snarl that stood behind the eyes thins. Smokers know this timing in their bones though they never timed it. The change comes fast, almost with the breath itself, and for that breath you thank the roll with your whole chest.

I do not argue with the speed. I lived it. You live it.

The speed is the confession.

Take the same roll and watch the next twenty minutes with the same plain eyes. The quiet does not hold. It thins first at the edges, then in the middle. A lightness comes back into the hands. Attention shortens. Something faint stirs low down, like hunger heard through a wall. At forty minutes the stirring has a voice. At an hour the voice has hands and it reaches for the pocket. You light again, and again the quiet comes within seconds, and again it thins within minutes.

Did the first roll solve anything outside the body, or did it quiet something inside the body for minutes. Did the trouble you smoked to meet change shape while you stood at the doorway, or did the gnaw inside the pause change shape while the trouble stood untouched. Would the same pull feel like rescue if the last dose had been five minutes before, when no gnaw stood to be quieted.

You know from your own pocket that it would not. A roll lit on top of steady feeding tastes hot and hurried and means little. A roll lit after sleep or after a long meeting or after a long film feels like kindness itself. The paper did not change between those lightings. The interval changed. The hunger changed. The taller the hunger before, the taller the thank-you after.

That is replenishment wearing the clothes of help.

The smoke carries its dose to the brain fast, fast enough that the credit lands on the lighting minute. Then the acute lift fades fast with it, and with the fading goes the brief sense of reward. That rapid rise and rapid fall is why the hand must go back. Not because the day keeps wounding you in new places, but because the dose keeps leaving the blood as fast as it entered. Each quiet carries its own ending tucked inside it.

Ask what kind of helper fixes by fading. Food holds the walker for hours. Water holds the thirsty mouth for hours. Rest holds the tired legs through the night. Your helper holds for minutes and then asks to be hired again. A helper that must be hired every hour is not a helper. It is a rate.

You read that rate as a true pleasure or a true crutch.

Read it again over the ashtray with the clock still running. If it stood as pleasure, the pleasure would spread past the first seconds and sweeten the hour after, the way a good meal warms the talk after it and a good rest steadies the hands after it. If it stood as a prop, the hour after would stand stronger than the hour before. Watch your own hour and you find neither. The first seconds soothe. The minutes after thin. The hour after carries no lift, only the start of the next fade. The roll marks only the interval, and the marking is praised as rescue.

I tell you as settled fact that the praise belongs to timing, not to tobacco.

That circle is the nicotine trap.

Read that sentence with the clock and let it hold. A circle that quiets within seconds and nags within minutes looks exactly like relief from inside the seconds. From outside the hour it looks like metering. The body meters the day into gaps. The mind thanks the gap-closer for the seconds the gap pauses. We all thanked it, and the thanks felt honest, because the quiet after a pull is honest quiet. It is only not what it claims to be.

THE HAND THAT MOVES BEFORE THOUGHT

Follow the hand itself, because the hand learned lessons the mind never voted on.

You finish eating and the hand moves. The phone rings and the hand checks the pocket. Talk pauses and the hand taps the pack. Work sticks and the hand reaches. A door opens to cold air and the hand knows the doorway drill. None of this feels like deciding. It feels like being yourself. The movement arrives in your own tone, reasonable and small, almost kind. Now. This pause needs one. This talk needs sealing. This knot needs clearing.

I ask you to test whose tone that is.

Did you decide at twelve that meals must close with fire, or did thousands of closings with fire teach the pause to ask for fire. Did you decide that talk must be sealed with smoke, or did thousands of seals teach the talk to feel unsealed without it. Did you decide that a stuck hour needs a lit roll, or did thousands of stuck hours with a lit roll teach stuckness to wear the clothes of needing. Would the same pauses have asked the same way if the last twenty thousand lightings had never paired the pause with the pull.

You know the answer because you watched animals learn faster than men admit. Ring a bell at feeding for a month and the mouth waters at the bell. Pair a doorway with a dose for years and the doorway waters the hands. The cue is not need. It is pairing.

This is why the emptiness feels like self. When pharmacology and cues arrive together, the reader names the bundle as personality. I am edgy without it. I cannot settle. I cannot think. I lose my humour. My day has a hole in it. I take each sentence at face value and I tell you each sentence mis-signs the author.

The edginess is two debts arriving in the same coat. One debt is the fading dose, faint and physical, already thinning. The other debt is the learned hour, the cue that says this minute always held smoke. Together they speak in your voice, and because the voice sounds like you, you obey it as you.

Consider the non-smoker who eats the same meal and meets the same pause.

He closes the plate and sits. He feels full and a little slow and glad the talk can run on. No hand taps. No pocket check. No sense that the minute lacks a seal. He meets the same stuck hour and stretches and drinks water and stares out the window and goes back in. He meets the same ringing phone without a lit roll between ring and answer. You believe he bears his pauses by nature while you must prop yours by paper. I tell you he bears his pauses without a second hunger inside them, while you bear yours with a second hunger stitched inside each one. The roll quiets the stitching for seconds and leaves the cloth untouched, then claims credit for the whole coat.

Did the pause need paper in order to be borne, or did lungs built for air bear pauses for years before paper taught them to ask. Did the meal need fire in order to close, or did the belly close it while the lighting rode along unthanked until it learned to demand thanks. Did the talk need smoke in order to hold, or did people hold talk while smoke cut it into coins.

You think the cue proves the roll adds. I tell you the cue proves the roll arrived so often it now knocks before entering.

Watch three ordinary cues tomorrow with the new question in hand and test the reading without changing your day. When the plate is pushed back, notice the hand lift and ask whether belly or interval spoke. When talk pauses, notice the tap and ask whether talk or timing asked. When work sticks, notice the reach and ask whether the knot or the gnaw moved first. Notice how each honest look makes the next cue plainer. The plate keeps its fullness. The talk keeps its thread. The work keeps its knot. The hand keeps only its habit of answering hunger with fire, none of which ever proved the pause needed burning.

Ask what remains when the pulls are taken out of the pause and the pause is left standing.

The doorway remains. The cold remains. The stretching remains. The looking at lit streets remains. The waiting for the kettle remains. They hold nothing that needed paper and fire to be borne. What does not remain is the second load, the hourly gnaw that turned an ordinary pause into something that felt unusable without lighting. You will meet the same pauses with one gnaw fewer, not with one joy fewer.

NAME THE WEATHER TRULY

Stay with the feeling itself, because the feeling is the witness the trap calls to court, and the witness lies about its name.

Name it as you feel it when you cannot light at once. It is not pain. It is not illness. It does not lay you flat. It sits low and vague. The belly feels a touch hollow though food sits in it. The chest feels a touch restless though no trouble stands in front of you. Attention will not settle on one line. The hands want business. The temper shortens by half an inch. Music irritates a shade sooner. Waiting irritates a shade sooner. A small voice says something is missing, and because the voice floats without a label, the mind pastes the nearest label to hand. I need a cigarette to be myself.

I know that float, because I floated in it for years and misnamed it every time.

I ask you to hold the float still for one plain minute and give it its true size.

Recall the worst hour you ever feared from stopping, the picture painted by talk and memory. Shaking walls. Sleepless nights. A body crying out. A mind unhinged. Now set beside it what you actually felt on the mornings you could not light at once, on the long flights, the long films, the long mornings in waiting rooms with no smoking signs. You felt hollow, yes. You felt edgy, yes. You felt the hands seek. But you stood. You talked. You waited. You did sums. You carried bags. You laughed at something though the gnaw sat underneath. The feeling never once stopped the day. It only shaded the day.

That shading has a true name, and I want you to learn it by wearing it once with eyes open.

Tomorrow, when the interval runs a little long, do not light at once and do not preach at yourself. Sit where you sit. Drink water. Keep your hands busy with plain business. Notice the hollow arrive faintly, not fiercely. Notice the empty sit lightly, not heavily. Notice the edgy edge the feeling, a faint irritability round an otherwise ordinary hour. Notice how it comes in waves, a minute louder, three minutes quieter, never a straight rising wall. Notice how food, talk, walking, washing, work with the hands all thin it without paper. Notice how the next lighting would have ended it fastest, not because lighting heals, but because feeding answers hunger fastest.

What you just met, watched whole, is a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling.

Read that line slowly and let it replace the old label. It is faint, not fierce, and faint things starve fast. It is empty, not broken, and empty things fill from ordinary hours. It is slightly edgy, not unhinged, and slight edges blunt within days. Call it by this size and the court hears a different witness. Not I am missing myself. Only my last dose faded and my cues knocked at once.

Did a faint thing ever prove a mighty need, or did a loud label make a faint thing sound mighty. Did an empty hour after a dose ever prove the dose gave fullness, or did it prove the dose taught emptiness between doses. Did a slight edge ever prove you cannot think without smoke, or did it prove hunger makes thinking feel scratchy until fed, the way any hunger does.

You believe the float proves the roll completes you. I tell you the float proves the roll empties you on schedule.

That twinge is the Ember Mite complaining.

Read that sentence plainly and let the size settle. A mite complains faintly when unfed. It does not roar. It does not tear. It gnaws a little and then it thins. You mistook its complaint for your character because the complaint arrived in your chest. Once named small, it stays small, and small things are starved by time, not by battle.

THE CELLAR AND THE RENTED WINDOW

Follow the inversion itself into a cellar, because the mind holds pictures longer than sentences.

Picture a broad man with keys who keeps a lodging cellar under a public house. The cellar is airless. The walls sweat. The one barred window stands shut. After an hour down there any lodger gets a dull head, a thick mouth, a wish for air. The man knows the airless hour makes the ache. He built the cellar so.

At the turn of the hour he climbs down, sets his lamp on a crate, and rents you the window for a minute. He works the bar, the crack opens, cold air cuts in, your head clears for a breath, you thank him from your chest. He nods like a helper, shuts the bar again, climbs back up, and leaves you to thicken toward the next hour. You count him kind. You count the minute of air as his gift. You fear the hours without his visits more than you fear the cellar itself.

I ask you to look once at the keys in his hand.

Did the window give you air, or did the cellar take your air and the window rent a minute of it back. Did your head clear because the man heals heads, or because the crack paused the thickening his shut room keeps making. Would you have needed his visits at all if you slept upstairs where windows stand open and heads clear on their own.

You know the answers because the picture will not let you dodge. The helper is the jailer. The cure is the pause in the harm. The thank-you belongs upstairs, not downstairs.

Now set the lodging beside your own day and watch how neatly the keys fit.

The cigarette is the jailer with the keys. It locks you each hour in its own airless minute by letting its dose fade, and the fading lays the dull hollow and the slight edge across an otherwise ordinary pause. Then it climbs down and rents you the window crack for seconds, the fast quiet after the pull, and shuts it again as the dose fades. You thank it for air. You fear the hours without its visits. You call the fear proof you need it to be yourself.

Did the lighting clear your head, or did it pause the thickening its own fading keeps making. Did it steady your temper, or did it still the edginess its own interval keeps renewing. Did it open the pause, or did it crack for seconds a room it keeps shut for hours.

You think the window proves the jailer helps. I tell you the window proves the jailer locks.

That reading is the Smokescreen talking when it calls the jailer a friend.

Read that sentence at the foot of the cellar steps and test which office the thank-you claims. The Smokescreen learned its lines from years of pairing, from talk that called the roll a comfort and a prop and a seal. It reads the Mite's faint complaint as proof of pleasure, relief, or need, the way a lodger reads the rented crack as proof the jailer loves him. The Mite gnaws faintly. The Smokescreen translates loudly. Between them they turn a rented minute into a life story.

Ask what the lodging costs across a year when priced in air.

The upstairs sleeper breathes all night without thanks and wakes clear. The cellar lodger breathes in cracks and pays by the hour and thanks the keeper for each crack. Both breathed the same town air. One paid rent on what the other owned by sleeping upstairs. Once the marks are sorted, the window keeps only its bars and its minute, neither of which ever asked to be praised as kindness.

DAY TWO AND THE SHORT PEAK

Meet the day smokers fear most, because fear paints it taller than it stands.

You have watched smokers on their second day without steady feeding and you have marked the signs. Sleep broken. Temper short. Hands seeking. Food tasting flat or too sharp. Talk harder to follow. A hollow that will not sit still. You watched and you thought, there stands proof. Take the roll away and the man comes apart. The roll must have held him together.

I tell you as settled fact that the second day proves the dose leaving, not the self leaving.

When the last dose fades and no new dose follows, the body notes the absence. Within hours a restlessness can begin. For most it sharpens across the first few days, often standing tallest around the second or third day, then it thins across the days after and usually loosens its hold within a few weeks. Hunger waves come and go. Cues knock loudest where pairing ran deepest, after meals, in pauses, at doorways. None of this arrives as straight torture for all, and no honest watcher can hand you your hour-by-hour chart in advance. Bodies differ. Hours differ. What holds across most is the shape, a short typical peak and then a thinning, not a new personality installed for life.

Did a short peak ever prove a lifelong need, or did it prove a brief debt being settled. Did the second-day snarl ever prove smoke made you kind, or did it prove fading makes anyone scratchy until the fading passes, the way hunger makes any waiter short until fed. Did the flat taste ever prove the roll flavoured life, or did it prove senses dulled for years take days to rinse clear.

You believe day two shows who you are without it. I tell you day two shows what its leaving feels like while it leaves.

That hollow on day two is the Ember Mite starving, not you wasting.

Read that sentence through the restless afternoon and let the pity fall where it belongs. A starving mite complains loudest before it quiets. Its loudest hour is not its strongest hour. It is its last full voice. You do not answer a dying complaint with feeding, the way you do not answer a lodging bill from a jailer by praising the window. You let the faint gnaw pass through ordinary business, and each wave passed thins the next.

Consider what the Smokescreen does with day two, because it works hardest when the Mite speaks loudest.

It says, see, you cannot settle. It says, see, you snap at people you love. It says, see, work needs its clearer. It says, see, this is your true edgy self and the roll kept you human. Each sentence reads pharmacology as personality. Each sentence turns a thinning wave into a life verdict.

That verdict is the Smokescreen reading hunger as identity.

Read that sentence on the second evening and test it against your own longer record. Were you born edgy in pauses, or did pauses grow edgy after years of paired doses. Were you born unable to close a meal, or did closings grow unclosable after thousands of seals with fire. Did the non-smoker beside you settle his second day without paper because nature favoured him, or because no Mite complained in him and no screen translated for him.

You think the storm proves the roll was ballast. I tell you the storm proves the ballast was stones tied on by the roll, now being untied.

Watch the storm pass the way storms pass when no one feeds them. Eat when hungry. Drink water. Sleep when tired. Keep the hands in plain business. Change nothing else in your life this week, neither your coffee nor your walk nor your seat at the table. Notice the waves space themselves. A bad hour, then two plain hours. A sharp morning, then a dull afternoon that asks for nothing. Notice how each honest look makes the next wave plainer. The hollow arrives fainter. The empty sits lighter. The edge blunts sooner. The day keeps its rooms and talk, none of which ever asked for fire to be shared.

Ask what day two ever gave besides proof of leaving.

The plate gave fullness. The walk gave air. The talk gave warmth. The night gave sleep, broken once, then deeper. The gnaw gave only its complaint, faint and brief against the day. Once the marks are sorted, the fear keeps only its painting, tall shadows on a short wall, none of which ever measured the man who cast them.

WHAT THE LOOP NEVER LENT

Run one timed day backwards with me to test the reading, because the hold hates backwards watching.

Take a day you counted as proof the roll steadies you. The morning roll that started the engine. The mid-morning roll that cleared the knot. The after-meal roll that closed the food. The evening roll that crowned the talk. You point to the row and read it as four steadies dealt by a steady hand.

Now run the same day with the credit reassigned and watch how neatly it fits.

You woke with hours of no doses behind sleep, so hunger stood high and the first pulls only brought you near normal. The mid-morning pause arrived when the last dose had faded, so lighting quieted the fade inside the pause and let you mistake the quiet for clearing. The after-meal cue knocked because thousands of meals closed with fire, so feeding stilled the knock and let you mistake the stilling for sealing. The evening talk ran with intervals underneath it, so each lighting paused the gnaw inside the talk and let you mistake the pause for crowning. The count ran as intervals and cues ran, not as helps deserved thanks. The quiet ran seconds, the fading ran minutes, the pairing ran years, and the three together wrote a life story on a dosing clock.

You believe the row proves the roll steadied the day. I tell you the row proves the day steadied itself while the roll metered hunger inside it.

Did the lighting change the day, or did it change the hunger inside the watcher. Did the glow arrive because steadiness was made, or because the dose arrived on schedule and faded on schedule and the fading demanded another arrival. Would the same warm pauses have needed the same lighting if the last twenty thousand lightings had never taught the body to ask.

You know the answers because your own intervals wrote them. The day stayed. The dose came and went. The pause that never knew the lighting never asks for it.

I do not ask you to light or not to light this minute, to change your hour or your count, to hide your pack or display it. Keep your day exactly as it stands. Meet the same pauses at the same hours. Let the meal stand where it stood. Let talk run where it ran. The book asks only clear sight in the timed minute. Watch with the new question in hand when the old thought shouts loudest. Notice how the staying minute holds whole without lighting. The food keeps its close. The pause keeps its rest. The talk keeps its thread. The lighting keeps only its seconds, hot and hurried, none of which ever needed a day to be carried in.

Hold that verdict while you meet the next hollow, because the next hollow will test it within hours.

You will feel the gnaw rise after the meal or the meeting, and the hand will lift before thought finishes, and the voice will speak in your own tone, reasonable and small. I cannot settle without one. I am not myself. I would light now if I truly loved the taste. Answer each with what the clock taught. Cannot settle, or dose faded on schedule. Not myself, or faint hunger plus old cue speaking as self. Would light for taste, or would feed to still the gnaw fastest. Each answer returns the credit where it belongs. The meal did the closing. The pause did the resting. The people did the holding. The roll did only the renting, seconds of air in a room it keeps shut.

Ask what the loop ever lent the day besides its own fading answered briefly.

The street lent the walking. The plate lent the fullness. The hands lent the work. The talk lent the warmth. Looked at straight, the day keeps only its work and rest, neither of which ever asked for fire to be shared.

SUMMARY

- The quiet after a pull arrives within seconds and thins within minutes, which shows relief timed to replenishment rather than help that holds.
- The hand moving at meals, pauses, and stuck hours shows pairing learned by repetition, not a self that needs smoke to settle.
- The inner weather watched whole is a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling, faint rather than fierce and brief rather than lifelong.
- The cigarette locks the cellar and rents the window crack, which shows rescue claimed by the same hand that keeps the ache returning.
- The restless second day with its short typical peak that usually loosens within weeks shows a dose leaving, not a personality revealed.
- Hunger plus cues speaking in your voice is hunger misread as self, and once named small it starves by time while ordinary hours carry the day.
