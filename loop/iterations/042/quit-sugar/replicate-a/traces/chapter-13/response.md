Chapter 13
LOOK BACK ONCE, THEN LIVE

IN THIS CHAPTER: a kitchen table in morning light, the drawer that stays shut at three, a closed supper, the list to keep in your pocket, and one last truth about sweetness.

*You walked out of the trap with clear eyes, and now you live the days that prove it.*

The table stands plain in morning light. A cup steams beside a folded list. The kitchen holds quiet after years of rustle and hide. No packet leans against the bread. No wrapper waits in a pocket. Light moves across wood and shows dust that mornings hid when eyes woke coated. You know this quiet because you lived the noise that came before, and knowing sits deeper than pictures.

You sit with both hands around warmth and listen to breath moving even in the chest. Hunger stirs honest and narrow, asking for breakfast rather than flaring for brightness. Taste waits clean on the tongue. The head feels clear behind the eyes. I tell you plainly that such plainness tells more than any argument could add, because lived proof outranks told proof wherever doubt talks.

### THE TABLE IN MORNING LIGHT

We sat for years at this same table with sweet tea and a biscuit beside coffee, calling that hunger though hunger never asked for brightness. We told ourselves the day needed paint before it began, and we paid for paint with fog that lasted till eleven. We reached for brightness before feet touched floor, and we called the reach appetite though appetite waits for food and thanks it. You know that early bargain because you struck it daily, and you see now that the bargain billed the morning before the morning began.

You sit here now with plain tongue and clear head, and the difference answers before thought forms words. Hunger comes quiet and wide, asking for food rather than pointing at one shelf, and you meet it with bread and eggs and fruit that quiet and stay quiet. Taste stays bright from first bite to last because no coating dulls the tongue midway, and fullness speaks at its ordinary hour with thanks. You stop because the meal closed, not because a rule ordered stopping, and closed meals need no tally to stand. I tell you plainly that ordinary light stands where the fight was supposed to stand, and lived mornings outrank told mornings wherever doubt talks.

We were conned with warmth, never weak with shame, and the con that set the bright rows never softens though warmth toward the hand never wavers. We called the packet normal because brainwashing called it normal, and the till line looked the same for every shopper though sight differed underneath. We watched coins for bread while never tallying what doses took from afternoons, and we hid wrappers after midnight while calling the hide a choice. The reality is that brightness never fed work, it only billed it, and fed afternoons closed without a visit though hours ran the same length and work pressed the same weight. You see tuning now where you once saw choice, and seeing frees the hand without strain.

You walk the bright aisle without quickening and without slowing, the way people pass shelves that hold nothing they need. Packets stand quiet under shop light. Bottles glow where bottles glowed. Colours call and words vow and pictures show happy tables that never stood in any aisle. You know where that happiness lived, because the bright table taught it and the celebration taught it again. It lived in faces and talk and leisure and hunger met with thanks, never in coating. Coating rode. Faces shone. Shine stayed with faces when packets left the table. That sight walks with you here though you carry no table with you.

A hand reaches beside you for a family packet, and pity steadies your seat before envy can form, soft and unpreached. You look at that hand without scorn and without hunger, remembering years you lived inside that reach. You see tired shoulders buying rescue that billed later. You see a person conned with warmth, never a person weak with shame. Warmth steadies. Hardness clarifies. Clarified eyes stay kind without one sermon spoken. I tell you that kindness holds because you sat inside that reach and called riding shining, and now you know riding never shone though faces shone beside it.

"What if this fails and the afternoons pull me back."

When that line arrives in your own voice, answer it at once with the line you carry now, remembering that you have nothing to lose and everything to gain by keeping sight clear. You stepped out easily, immediately and permanently when you finished that last ordinary sweet and spoke the vow. Hooked would have meant grey afternoons for the rest of your life, and walking clear means bright mornings for the rest of your life. Mouths that coated past pleasure tell more than pictures that crowned before first bite, and credit returns to food and hours where it always lived. Did the drawer ever close the drawer, or did each visit open further visits though promises stood beside it. Did sweet mornings ever carry you further than sweet evenings carried you, or did both lift for minutes and bill for hours. Only honest answers stand, and honest answers leave brightness where brightness always lived, in pictures that needed dawn to look kind.

Afternoon comes with its old hour, half past two sliding toward three, and light slants lower through blinds while chairs creak down the hall. Tiredness touches the shoulders lightly, honest tiredness from hours lived rather than fog from brightness billed. You drink cold water slowly and feel it move clean down. You open the window and let air move across the face. You step outside for five minutes and let legs carry weight that sat too long. Talk thins and eyes blink slower and breath shortens a fraction, and the body asks for air and movement and perhaps food in its own voice rather than in pictures. Pictures once translated every dip as proof brightness must come. Bodies never said so. Bodies said rest a little, move a little, drink, eat if hunger calls. That difference holds through the whole slope down to supper without billing anything.

Evening gathers and the kitchen fills with steam and smell that owes nothing to packets. Pots knock and a knife ticks on board and onions soften to gold. Meat browns and holds its juices. Greens brighten in boiling water then drain and shine with butter. Bread warms in the oven and crust cracks under thumb. Hunger sits honest at the table, neither raging nor absent, the kind of hunger that thanks food rather than chases brightness. You eat slowly and let each mouthful report fairly. Soup warms the throat and spreads heat outward. Potatoes break soft and take salt cleanly. Fish flakes and tastes of sea and lemon. Greens bite then yield. Taste stays bright because no coating dulls the tongue midway. Fullness speaks with plain sufficiency and you stop with thanks. The sink runs hot and cloth wipes and cupboards close and the kitchen settles into order that holds till morning. No wrapper hides in a pocket. No packet waits in a drawer for a raid when the house sleeps. Feet stay in bed and sleep comes even and deep. You know unbroken nights prove more than promises point to, because nights prove though words sleep.

### THE LIST TO KEEP

If you opened here first, close these pages and start at the beginning so the lines below mean what they say. The list means nothing lifted from its ground. Read from the first table through the last sweet, live the mornings and aisles and suppers in order, and then keep this pocket list as speech, not as study. You know a list kept in the pocket steadies the hand only when the eyes saw why each line stands.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
2. DON'T TRY TO QUIT BAD SUGAR YET
3. START WITH EXCITEMENT, NOT DREAD
4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
6. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY THE WILLPOWER METHOD
7. EAT REAL FOOD WHENEVER YOU ARE HUNGRY
8. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE
9. PITY, DON'T ENVY, ANYONE STILL TRAPPED
10. FINISH YOUR LAST ORDINARY SWEET AND MAKE THE VOW

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. The lines stand bare so a photograph holds them. No line needs a tale beside it now because each line cashed an argument the pages already won. Hunger trusts real food and stops at full. One exception keeps the whole trap alive though promises stand beside it. Pity steadies the seat where envy once pulled. The last ordinary sweet closed the door and the vow spoke freedom in one breath. You carry these ten as owned speech for hours when old lines surface, brief and warm and final.

Take Tuesday coming, with sight already steady and nothing left to argue.

Work lies plain and the known hour turns. Shoulders carry honest weight from hours used. Memory lights the old drawer for a moment, then dims. We all met such moments with packets for years and named the meeting need though need never stood there. I am telling you this because you may meet the moment and expect debate, and find speech standing where debate used to stand.

The line comes in your own tongue, wearing your voice. "sugary somethings to get me through." "a pick-me-up." "I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling."

You leave debate shut. You leave pushing and counting out of the room. You speak from the pocket, short and kind and finished. You speak EAT REAL FOOD WHENEVER YOU ARE HUNGRY where the tug speaks. Hunger gets food and thanks, or thirst gets water, or limbs get air outside for minutes, and hands go back to work. Then you speak inside with full chest, FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The tug spoke of missing. The reply speaks of a dead foe. The tug spoke of rescue in coating. The reply speaks of people and hours carrying rescue all along. Papers line up straight and the hour walks past with no call answered. Nothing here proves again what mornings already proved. Speech holds because seeing holds.

You learn how short the exchange runs once seeing stays shut. No weighing starts. No voices gather for and against. One sentence said from the pocket, then the shout said as your own. Thirst answers water without translation. Hunger answers food and quiets. Legs answer a short walk and return lighter. Hands answer work and stack pages straight. The drawer memory dims further by four o'clock and the desk holds only work and water and sloping light. We all feared such hours would drag without brightness to lift them, and I am telling you they move quicker without a second visit to plan and hide. Quicker hours need no praise spoken aloud. They pass and supper waits ahead with taste kept clean.

Take Saturday coming, with the same pocket speech ready.

Friends sit close and dishes move and voices cross. A kind hand stretches bright wrapping toward you, smiling welcome. We all lifted such wrapping for years to show we belonged, and named belonging fun though fun sat in company. I am telling you belonging asks no wrapping now.

The line comes dressed for ceremony, gentle and fair. "special occasions." "just one." "You must be a lot of fun."

You leave long reasons shut. You leave preaching out of the room. You speak from the pocket where the tug speaks, NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE. A kept seat never kept empty, and promises beside it never held it empty. You meet the kind hand with warmth for the holder and firmness for the trick that filled the aisle. Then you speak inside, FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The tug spoke of ceremony owing coating. The reply speaks of company owning light all along. Voices go on and shoulders ease and mirth rises with no aid from lips. Nothing here proves again what the feast already taught. Pity keeps the chair soft with no sermon, and envy never lifts its head.

You learn how kind refusal runs once seeing stays shut. No lecture starts. No face falls. Hands stay calm round a glass of water while talk unwinds. Stories carry their own warmth and shoulders loosen round the table. We all feared such tables would thin without coating to crown them, and I am telling you talk runs thicker when mouths stay clean. Thicker talk needs no comment spoken aloud. It carries the evening and the kitchen later settles without wrappers to hide.

This is what the ten do in days ahead. No study sent with you, no count, no strain. A single bare sentence said aloud inside where the old sentence rose, then the great shout said as your own. Dip-hour meets a food sentence and the shout. Feast offer meets a wholeness sentence and the shout. Lines once wearing your voice come tagged now and fall quiet while days walk plain. No wrestling starts because wrestling lived in years when seeing stayed dim. Seeing shut the quarrel. Hands stay calm. Mouths keep clean. Heads keep bright for work and supper and talk.

### ONE LAST TRUTH ABOUT SWEETNESS

Celebration taught the hardest lesson and taught it clean. Birthday cake stood rich on the menu under low light while talk ran warm around it. Wedding cake passed from hand to hand with a joke and pressure to look fun. Mousse arrived because no one wished to sit out. First bites shone bright and then the arc turned. Bites laboured past taste. Want stayed lit while taste thinned. A forced cookie tasted more sugar than chocolate and the afternoon asked for a nap after. You watched smiles cover the gap though no one at the table watched the arc. The reality is that people lent the light and the packet borrowed it, and borrowed light never owned the room though the room shone.

Did the packet sweeten that table, or did faces and talk and leisure and hunger met sweeten it while the packet rode. When the last bite turned mechanical and too sweet, what stayed sweet then, the coating or the company that stayed bright after. When morning came clear without fog and the drawer stayed shut till supper, what carried the day, the dose or the food and air and rest that asked nothing. Only honest answers count here, and honest answers move credit where it belongs without shame and without boast. People shone. Hours held. Food fed. Pictures pulled. Pull ended when sight closed though hours stayed long.

Sweetness was never in the packet. Life was sweet underneath and the packet was covering it.

Read that twice and let it sit without armour, because it lands different from every line that came before. We chased brightness believing brightness lived in coating, and coating only dimmed the tongue that would have tasted morning plain. Strawberries cracked clean. Bread smelled of crust. Hunger thanked and fullness closed. Talk ran unaided. Light hit the kitchen and stayed kind without paint. The packet did not add sweet to sweet life, it laid a film across sweet life and then sold relief from the film. Once seen, it cannot be unseen. Coating rode. Faces shone.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say that where the thought once stood and hear how it changes office in one breath. Thought said loss calls. Answer says enemy died. Thought said rescue waits in the packet. Answer says rescue stood in people and hours all along. Each answer arrives as celebration rather than as reasoning, and celebration reframes rather than suppresses. If a crumb ever passes lips without orders, spit the strangeness kindly, note how coating dulls where real food brightens, drink water, eat when hunger calls, and walk on with the next clear morning. The belt holds for accidents, never for driving erratically, and belief guards the road while driving stays plain. You meet old lines pre-labelled now and they lose command though days stay ordinary.

Get on with enjoying your life.
