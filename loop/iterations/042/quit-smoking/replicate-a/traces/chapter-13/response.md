Chapter 13

THE LAST ORDINARY CIGARETTE

The pack lies on the table beside a chipped mug and a lighter with teeth marks on the plastic. Evening light comes sideways through the kitchen window and lays a thin shine on the cellophane. The room smells faintly of yesterday's coat hung by the door. Outside, the same fence, the same flags, the same chair you know so well stand empty in the cooling air.

You have your ordinary brand in your pocket or in the shop drawer, the one you always buy, not a mild one, not a strong one, not a farewell treat. Your hands know its weight. Your mouth knows its first harshness and the papery dryness after. For years those small objects ran the hour. Tonight they sit quiet while you read these last pages before the step.

You come here with the witness still warm in mind. A man sat in the same chair through timed years and through clear years and found the clear years whole. Mornings with kettle and open door. Meals closed in peace. Nights without splitting in two. Money left in the jar. Breath easy on twelve stairs. You have shut each old sentence in turn and heard who spoke it. The mind stands convinced and the body asks for its own hour of proof.

IN THIS CHAPTER: the quiet table before the step, the honest check of readiness, the one ordinary cigarette seen plain, and the vow spoken once.

*You close nothing precious tonight; you finish paying for a want that was never yours.*

Are You Champing To Finish

Let us check honestly before anything is lit, because honesty now saves all argument later.

Do you feel a lift when you think of never lighting again. Not dread. Not a heavy sense of duty. A lift, an eagerness to be done, a lightness at the thought of mornings with nothing timed, evenings in one room, the pocket patted and found empty and found glad. Are you champing to finish and to walk out into that plain day.

Most readers who have seen what you have seen answer yes without pause. The hours held. Coffee held. The break outside held. The meal held. The night among lit ones held. The six old sentences lost their voice one by one. The witness showed years holding the same way. What remains to fear. The small tug is seen for what it is. The large story is seen for what it is. The design is seen for what it is. The drawer of aids is seen for what it is. The bar for the future is in the hand. Of course you want to finish.

If that lift is in you, read on to the hour itself. You are ready.

You can tell the lift from excitement that still hides fear. Fear says perhaps I can manage without, perhaps tomorrow. The lift says there is nothing to manage and nothing to postpone. Fear counts hours ahead. The lift sees hours ahead already whole. Fear asks what if mornings feel flat. The lift remembers mornings tested and found fuller. If your chest feels light at the thought of never paying again, if the thought of the shop counter passed without purchase brings quiet gladness rather than a pang, trust that lightness. It is seeing speaking, not mood. Mood shifts with weather. Seeing stays because it was earned hour by hour.

If it is not in you, if some part still mourns, still fears mornings flat, still believes one pleasure would be lost, still hears a whisper that tomorrow would be a kinder day to stop, do not force the step. Put this chapter down and read the book again from the start, slowly, with the same open mind you brought the first time. Do you remember how we began. You risk nothing by reading on, and you stand to win back your whole life. That promise held for every hour we tested, and it holds for the re-read too. Let the seeing ripen until eagerness comes on its own.

I tell you this with all the firmness I carry. Do not push yourself over the line with a clenched jaw. Do not summon force to cover doubt. Doubt means some thread of the old story still looks true, and force never cut a story. Seeing cuts it. When you see fully, finishing feels like putting down a heavy bag on the doorstep, not like throwing away food. Wait for that feeling by re-seeing, and it comes. It always comes when the mind is allowed to look again.

Ask yourself plain now, with no one watching.

Does any cigarette still look like a friend to you, or does each one look like rent paid for an hour of quiet that was already yours. When coffee steams, do you picture the cup fuller with smoke or cleaner without. When talk runs late, do you hear the evening asking for fire or asking only for voices. When trouble presses, do you believe paper and leaf solve anything, or do you see they only pause their own echo. When you picture yourself in a year, do you see someone missing something, or someone whole who wonders why the question ever seemed hard.

If the honest answers are rent, cleaner, voices, echo, whole, you are ready. The door stands open and you have only to walk through.

One Ordinary Cigarette, Seen Plain

Take now one ordinary cigarette of your ordinary brand. Not two. Not a last pack to say goodbye through the night. One ordinary one, smoked alone with attention, is the whole ceremony. This is not permission to smoke. This is the ending of smoking, done with eyes open so no false glow survives it.

Choose a quiet minute. Sit where you often sat, at the table or on the patio step, with nothing else claiming you. Light it the way you always light it and do not hurry.

Watch what it actually does, not what memory tells you it does.

The first draw scratches. You know this scratch so well you stopped tasting it years ago. Taste it now. Hot paper, dry leaf, a chemical bite at the back of the throat that makes the chest pull short. The mouth fills with warm smoke that coats the tongue and leaves it duller for coffee after. The nose fills with a smell you have learned not to notice in coats and curtains and hair. Notice it now. Ask what good there is at all in that taste and that smell apart from the easing they promise.

That easing comes, as it always comes, a few seconds in. Shoulders drop a finger-width. The faint edginess that had been growing since the previous one quietens. Notice how small that change is when watched. Not joy. Not calm poured in. Only a thin hunger pausing because it was fed. You see now where that hunger came from. We built it together hour by hour, each one planting the seed of the next, until the day looked like need and need looked like pleasure.

Smoke it halfway and look at the ash. Grey, flaking, already dead at one end and already demanding the other end be finished. The fingers hold a faint yellow memory that soap must wash out. The stomach knows nothing of it. The heart knows only a quicker beat it did not ask for. The mind, which came to the chair with its own thoughts about the evening, finds its thoughts narrowed to when the next one may come. Is this a friend. Is this a reward. Is this a helper with trouble.

Notice how the second half burns hotter and harsher than the first. The draw grows shorter because the throat asks for less, yet the hand lifts again because timing asks for more. The eyes sting a little when smoke turns in wind. The throat clears. The tongue seeks water. The body gives its plain report at every pull while the old story talks over it about relief. Listen to the body now, not the story. The body never called this pleasure. It called it intrusion, and it cleared, coughed, and dulled itself to carry it.

Smoke it to the filter with the same watching and ask the questions that end all mourning.

What did this one add that the minute lacked before it was lit. Did the light grow warmer because of it. Did the air grow cleaner. Did the trouble of the day grow smaller except for the thin hunger it paused. Did the coffee taste fuller or duller. Did you feel more yourself or more divided, half in the minute and half in the burn.

You know the answers because you have tested every hour already. The light stayed the light. The air stayed the air, only smoked. The trouble stayed the trouble, only joined by hunger. The cup stayed the cup, only filmed. You stayed you, only timed. There is no cigarette that does not feed the trap. This ordinary one feeds it too, in grief and in joy, alone and among friends, first of the day and last of the night. Seen plain, it has no gift left to offer and no sorrow left to charge for leaving.

Let it go out fully. Put the butt in the ashtray and look at it cold. That cold end is what the romance always was when the hunger was not speaking through it. Ugliness watched without hunger is only ugliness. Remember its smell on the fingers as you wash your hands clean. That smell is the truth the hunger painted over for years.

Do not keep the pack for comfort. Do not keep one for emergency. An emergency supply is a kept question, and there is no question left. Break what remains and put it where rubbish goes, not where memory keeps relics. You are not throwing away pleasure. You are throwing away the dose unit that charged admission to pleasures that were always yours.

The Vow

Stand now, with clean hands and a clear head, at the threshold itself.

You have checked readiness honestly. You have watched one ordinary one with eyes open and found nothing to keep. The witness has shown years holding whole on the other side. The doors behind you stand shut. There is only the speaking left, and then life.

Follow your doctor's guidance about any illness or medicine, and if you feel unwell seek care promptly.

Speak your vow aloud if you can, quietly if others sleep, inside if speaking aloud cannot be done, but speak it as a solemn promise that divides life, not as a wish. Say what you mean and mean it to the bottom.

I will never smoke again. Not one puff. Not in joy and not in trouble. Not with drink and not with coffee. Not offered with love and not stolen in shame. I am done.

Say it once, slowly, and hear your own voice finish it. That voice is yours, not the old story borrowing your mouth. The old story spoke in whispers of one and tomorrow and different. You speak now in a full sentence that leaves no crack.

Hear the instruction that carries this hour, and keep it whole:

1. SMOKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY CIGARETTE AND MAKE THE VOW. Then never take another puff.
You speak it tonight because there is nothing left to keep.

This is not a threat and not a test of strength. It is a description of what seeing does. When you see there is nothing to keep, you keep nothing. When you see each one only feeds the same want, you feed it no more. The second sentence follows the first the way morning follows night, not by effort but by order.

And now hear the conferral, flat and final, from one who has watched thousands cross where you stand.

You are free as of now. Not after three days. Not after three weeks. Not when some count is reached. Now. The belief changed, so the want lost its root. What remains in the body is only a faint echo dying for lack of feeding, and an echo needs no mourning and no management. It needs only to be left unfed while you live.

Do you feel how different this is from every other attempt. Before, you stopped while still believing something precious was lost, and loss must be carried. Now you stop knowing nothing was ever given, and nothing needs carrying. Before, you waited to become free. Now you are told what is true. You already are.

Before, each morning asked a question. Can I get through today. Before, each night asked another. Did I count the hours. Those questions kept the thing alive as something missed and measured. Tonight no question is set. The morning will ask only what mornings ask. What shall I eat, what shall I do first, how does the air smell through the open door. The night will ask only what nights ask. Who shall I see, what story shall I hear to its end. Freedom that must be counted each day is still timing by another name. Freedom conferred whole tonight needs no count, because there is nothing left to count.

What To Think From Now On

From tonight, whenever smoking crosses your mind, in whatever hour, you will know what to do with the thought. Do not push it down. Do not argue with it for an hour. Do not fill the minute with chewing or pacing or any busy hands to prove strength. Look at it, know the speaker, and turn it into proof.

The thought comes dressed as memory. Cup, then reach. Door, then yard. Laugh, then light. Stress, then cry for care. It lifts for a second in its old hour and asks to be taken as order. It is not order. It is echo. The body remembers timing the way a room remembers a clock after the clock is taken down, ticking in the quiet for a night or two until silence settles into the walls.

Answer it the way free people answer a dead claim, with gladness, not with debate.

Think of the full cup in your hand, tasted to the bottom. Think of the whole evening in one room, heard to its end. Think of the stairs climbed without thought, breath simply there. Think of the jar where coin stays without striving. Think of mornings by the thousand waiting plain and whole. Then let the cheerful verdict rise on its own, from the chest, not from the throat.

AT LAST! I'M FREE!

Say it inside or aloud and mean the exclamation. At last, because years of timing ended tonight. Free, because no chain remains to be loosened link by link. The thought that came as temptation leaves as reminder. Each visit makes the next thinner, because each visit ends in gladness instead of hunger. Within days whole stretches pass without a single visit. Within weeks you sometimes go long and remember only when something like this book crosses your mind. That thinning is not you growing stronger against a mighty foe. That thinning is a small hunger starving while you live, easily, at once, and for good.

Keep that last line the way you keep your name. You did not stop piece by piece. You did not stop for a while to see how it goes. You stopped whole, tonight, on seeing, and wholeness needs no repair. When drink is warm and talk runs late, wholeness holds. When work piles and sleep runs short, wholeness holds. When the mirror asks who you are without paper, wholeness answers. Undivided. The same joke, the same timing, the same kindness after midnight, only without the timed burn standing beside them.

If a hard minute ever speaks louder, ask its own questions and watch it shrink.

Did smoke ever solve trouble, or did it only pause its own echo while trouble waited. Did smoke ever make company, or did it only stand nearby while people made company. Did smoke ever make the cup fuller, or did it only film the mouth that tasted. You have answered these in lived hours, not in lines. Let the lived hours answer again. They answer the same way every time, because truth does not change its story with the weather.

Go now into ordinary life with empty hands and open eyes. Drink your coffee to the bottom. Take your break outside with nothing lit. Close your meals in peace. Walk through old yards and old roads without dodging them. Change nothing else in how you live. The quiet comes because the need is gone, not because the world was narrowed. That is how you prove to your own body what your mind already knows, and the proof arrives daily without being chased.

Tomorrow morning the kettle boils the same. The shop opens the same. Food tastes of itself. Drink tastes of itself. Work calls the same. People laugh the same. You meet each hour as before, only undivided, with no split in the evening and no reach in the morning. If some old picture lifts for a second, you know its name and its answer, and you go on without pause. That going on is the whole of what follows. No second ceremony. No long guard. Only life, taken whole, day after day, until the thought of timing feels like a story about someone else.

I congratulate you, not as one who endured, but as one who escaped. You did not climb out through force. You saw, you spoke, and you stepped over. The chair waits on the patio. The mug waits on the low table. The sun comes over the right shoulder. Everything you loved about those hours waits intact, only without the fire that once timed them.

You are free. Get on with enjoying the life that was always yours underneath the smoke.

SUMMARY

- Readiness is eagerness to finish, and doubt is answered by re-reading until seeing is full, never by force.
- One ordinary cigarette watched with attention shows only harshness, dullness, and a small hunger pausing, not a gift.
- The cold butt and the smell on the fingers tell the truth hunger painted over for years.
- The pack kept is a question kept, and there is no question left to keep.
- The vow spoken once divides life, and freedom starts at that speaking, not at a future count.
- Any thought that visits later is echo, not order, and it thins when met with gladness.
- The joyful verdict turns each memory into proof that escape already happened.
- Ordinary hours hold whole without tribute, and wholeness needs no repair.
- The life ahead is the same life, only undivided, with mornings, meals, and nights returned.
