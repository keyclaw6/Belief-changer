Chapter 10

ORDINARY MOMENTS, INTACT

You left the drawer open behind you on the kitchen table and you walk into the morning with empty hands and open eyes. The pack still sits in the pocket while you read, untouched for now, because we agreed to change how you see before we change what you do. The street outside looks the same as yesterday. The same kettle. The same stairs. The same shop bell. The same evening noise through a doorway. Only the seeing is different, and that changes everything you meet in those hours.

I know what question walks beside you into that morning.

Very well, you say. I see the drawer held one door painted four colours. I see cutting down makes each puff sacred. I see total stop is the only stable ground. But you do not know my mornings. Coffee without a cigarette feels like a letter without a greeting. The break at work without smoke feels like standing about with nothing to do with my hands. The meal at night feels open at the end, as if someone left the door ajar. Tell me those hours will hold. Do not tell me about chemicals or counts. Let me live a day and find it complete.

That is fair. I am not going to argue you into mornings. I am going to walk you through them, hour by hour, until you feel them hold under your own feet.

IN THIS CHAPTER: the first coffee savored to the grounds, the work break taken outside with nothing lit, the meal closed in peace, and the night out with drinks and laughter met head on.

*Your coffee, your break, your closed meal and your night with friends were always complete in themselves, and you can inhabit every one of them favoured, with nothing lit.*

The First Coffee, Savored Whole

Come with me to the kitchen before the house wakes.

The kettle clicks off. The light through the blind lies in thin bars across the oilcloth. You spoon the coffee the way you always do, two heaped, and the steam gets up and warms the face while you pour. The first mouthful is hot and bitter and full, the way it always was. The second mouthful settles you into the chair. The quiet sits around you, kind and unhurried, before the day starts asking.

Watch what used to happen here.

In the old days the cup stood beside the ashtray. You lifted the cup, you set it down, you reached before thought finished. The hand knew the path so well it moved while the eyes still looked out of the window. The first puff went up with the first sip, the two smokes mingling in the cold air from the cracked window, coffee smoke and tobacco smoke braided together until memory tied them into one thing. You called that first pair the start of the day. You believed the coffee needed its companion to be coffee.

Now sit in the same chair on the same morning and drink the same cup to the grounds, with nothing beside the cup but the spoon.

What do you taste.

You taste coffee, hotter and cleaner than you remember. You feel the hands wrap the mug and take its heat. You look out at the same grey roofs, and the day comes toward you whole, not through a veil.

Ask the plain questions while the cup is still warm in the hands.

Did the cigarette add heat to the mug. Did it add bitter to the bean or quiet to the kitchen or light to the window. Did it make the chair kinder or the ten minutes longer or the mind clearer for the day ahead. Or did it stand beside a pleasure that was already complete and let the steam and the quiet and the first waking of the body be credited to its own thin smoke.

You know the answer because the mouth knows it before thought argues.

The pleasure was the coffee, the warmth, the pause, the body waking, the day not yet started. The smoke only arrived at the same hour and claimed the hour as its own. Morning did not work because you lit. Morning worked although you lit, and paid a small tax of cough and hurry and stale air on the coat for the privilege of working.

Drink the cup slow this morning in your mind and feel how it ends.

It ends with the grounds at the bottom and the warmth low in the belly and the mind ready. No clock starts ticking toward the next one. The kitchen stays clean. The mouth stays clean. The start stays yours.

You feared coffee would feel empty with nothing lit. Find it fuller. Fuller because nothing stands between you and it, fuller because the taste is taste and not ash laid over taste, fuller because ten quiet minutes are ten quiet minutes and not ten minutes measured by how fast a stick burns down.

That is the first ordinary hour, inhabited, not explained. The morning works. It works better with the tribute removed.

The Break Outside With Nothing Lit

Mid-morning the legs grow restless and the eyes grow dry. The screen blurs. The till talk fades into a hum. The foreman calls time, the office kettle goes on again, the yard door bangs. Someone says break, and the old path lights up in the head like a lit corridor.

Watch the old break plain.

You pushed back the chair, you patted the pocket to check the pack was there, you walked not for air but for the corner where air was permitted to be breathed through smoke. You stood with shoulders up against the cold, you lit fast against the wind, you pulled deep because the morning had been long without. The talk around you curled with the smoke. Five minutes, ten minutes, the stub pressed out under the heel, the hands smelling of it all the way back to the desk, the mouth dry for coffee to wash the taste away. You called that rest. You called that fresh air.

Now take the same break at the same hour with the same people, with nothing in the pocket to check and nothing in the hand to shield from wind.

Push back the chair and feel the legs stretch as you stand. Walk to the door because the legs want walking and the eyes want distance. Step outside and take the cold full in the face. The air comes in clean to the bottom of the chest. The talk goes on around you, easy and idle, football and prices and the foreman's van.

Stand there the whole ten minutes and notice what rest actually is.

Rest is the back straightening after bending, the eyes looking far after looking near, the shoulders dropping, the laugh at the joke. None of it asked for fire. The break began when you stood and it ends when you go back in, complete at both ends.

I hear the small fear that speaks halfway through that first real break.

What do I do with my hands. How do I stand here without the thing to hold. Will the others think me odd, standing empty-handed while they light.

Let me answer straight, as one who stood that same concrete with the same empty hands.

Your hands know how to be hands. They go in the pockets. They hold the mug. No one watches them but you. And if someone holds the pack out, you smile and say no thanks, I am good out here. You stand where you always stood, and you walk back in with the lungs full of air.

Ask while the cold still sits fresh on the cheeks.

Did the old break rest you, or did it divide you. Half the mind on the talk and half on the burn rate. Was that rest, or attendance at a small appointment you never made but never missed.

The new break rests because nothing divides it. Ten minutes are ten minutes, taken, not paid for.

That is the second ordinary hour. The break holds. It holds better without the tax.

The Meal Closed In Peace

Evening. The plate empties. The belly fills and warms. The table holds the small wreckage of a good meal, crumbs, a knife laid across, a glass with a finger left in the bottom.

Every smoker knows this edge of the day. Meal closed, coffee poured, hand expecting the paired companion. The chair pushed back a fraction. The body sighing with fullness. The thought arriving before the hand moves, something to close this, something to seal the fullness and turn eating into evening.

Inhabit it now without the seal, and find the seal was never needed.

Pour the coffee the way you like it. Take the first sip while the taste of food still lives faint on the tongue. Lean back that inch the body asks for. The fullness spreads and settles of its own accord.

That peace is the close. It needs no stamp.

Ask what the old seal ever sealed.

Did the smoke make the food richer after the plate was empty. Or did it lay a sharp note across a finished pleasure and call the sting the finish. Food ends in fullness. Evening ends in rest. The smoke only scribbled over it.

Stay at the table five minutes longer in your mind than the old hunger allowed, and feel how the hour completes itself.

The coffee cools a fraction. The talk goes on without you watching the door. Nothing is ajar. Everything is shut gently, plate to belly to rest.

You feared meals would feel open without the close. Find them closed deeper, because nothing interrupts the body's own closing.

That is the third ordinary hour. The meal stands. It stands favoured without tribute.

The Night Out You Feared Most

Now I take you where you most feared the test would come, because I will not leave the strongest case standing behind us like a dog we tiptoe past.

Friday. The pub door heavy and warm. The glass cold in the hand. The laugh going up from the corner table where your lot always sit. Through the glass doors you can see the yard where the lights are strung, where the little crowd stands close under the heaters.

This is the scene you have played in dread. Drinks, laughter, the move to the yard, the offered pack, the fear of standing alone, the whisper that one won't hurt.

So let us live it whole, not in dread but in daylight, minute by minute, and see what is actually there.

You drink. No one asks you not to drink. I am not going to tell you to stay home or to nurse orange while others laugh or to leave early like a man under sentence. You came out to be with your friends on a Friday, and you drink what you drink at the pace you drink it. Change nothing else in the night. The night is yours. The company is yours. The laugh is yours.

The table warms. Someone does the impression of the foreman and the beer goes the wrong way with laughing. Your shoulders drop. This is belonging, plain and warm.

Then the move comes. Two stand, three stand, coats lifted. Going out for a smoke. Coming. The chairs scrape. The yard doors breathe cold in when they open. The little crowd gathers under the heaters with collars up, lighters cupped, first pulls going up into the strung lights.

Here is where the old story tightens around the throat. If I do not go, I stand alone inside like the lone guy outside in reverse, the odd one at the table with empty hands while the real party happens in the yard. If I go and stand empty-handed, all eyes will find my empty hands. If I take the one held out with love and laughter, one won't hurt, just this night, just to belong, just to seal the laugh the way the meal was sealed.

Walk it with me as it truly is, not as fear paints it.

You have two honest choices and both are whole. You can go with them into the yard with your glass in your hand, because the yard is only a yard, cold air and heaters and talk continued standing up. Or you can stay at the table with those who stayed, nursing the drink, minding the coats, holding the story until the others come back. Either is belonging. Belonging does not live in the yard. Belonging lives at the table and walks with the people wherever they stand.

Choose the yard, because fear says the yard is the test. Take the glass and push through the doors into the cold. Hear the talk reshape itself around heaters. See the ends glow, the smoke rise into the lights and tear away on the wind.

Someone you love holds the pack out. Go on. One won't hurt. Don't be the only one not.

Look at that hand with open eyes, not with hunger and not with scorn.

Inside that pack there is no belonging. There is shredded leaf wrapped around a dose, rolled to deliver its measure within seconds and then to wake the want for the next measure. The laugh did not come from those sticks. The sticks stood nearby while belonging happened.

Take nothing, and find what happens instead of what fear promised.

Nothing terrible happens. The talk does not stop. No one stares at your hands. The holder shrugs and puts the pack away. Five minutes pass. Ten. The crowd drifts back inside. You walk back in with them, part of the drift, not the odd one, because oddness lived only in fear's picture, never in the yard.

Ask on the way back through the doors, with the warm hitting the face again.

What did the lit ones get out there that you missed. The talk. You had the talk. The air. You had the air cleaner. That little lift at the first pull was the ending of an edginess that had been growing since the last pull an hour before. You stood without the edginess and without the ending, level and warm from drink and laugh.

Stay at the table for the next hour and watch the night prove it.

You laugh as loud. Your glass empties and fills at the same pace. When the night breaks up you walk out with the night whole inside you, not in pieces between lights.

Someone lights a last one under the streetlamp, squinting through the first pull. The old phrase rises, last night on earth, as if this smoke held the night together. He is paying at the door of a pleasure that was already yours free.

You do not envy him. How could you envy a man paying admission to his own Friday. You pity the payment, not the man, and you love the man enough to leave him his night while you keep yours.

That is the strongest case met head on and found hollow. Drinks hold. Laughter holds. Belonging holds. The yard holds with you in it empty-handed and glad. You were never asked to lose friends or to hide from drink to keep your freedom. You were asked only to bring your open eyes to the yard and watch who actually makes the night.

Why The Flicker Still Comes After Coffee

You will ask me an honest question on the second morning, and I want to meet it before doubt dresses it up.

If coffee and meals and breaks are whole without smoke, why does the hand still half-lift after the plate is cleared. Why does the thought still flicker when the coffee is poured. Does that flicker not prove the body still needs it, and this whole seeing is a fine story that the body denies.

No. Listen carefully, because this distinction guards everything we have walked through.

After years of pairing, the mind ties the knot tight. Cup, then reach. Plate cleared, then door. Break bell, then corner. The knot was learned by repetition, the way a path wears through grass because feet always took that turn. When the feet stop taking the turn, the pale line of the old path still shows for a while in the grass. Its showing does not prove the feet must walk it. It proves feet walked it often.

The flicker after coffee is that pale line. A learned cue, not ongoing need. It arrives because the hour arrived, not because the blood calls for its measure. It feels like a small tug toward the yard, a picture of the hand lifting, a ghost of the old seal. It passes in minutes while you sip and talk and clear. It returns thinner. Then it does not return at all.

Do you see how this protects you instead of threatening you.

If every flicker meant need, you would have to heed every flicker forever. If the flicker means habit of hours, you can greet it with a small inner nod and let the coffee do the answering. The picture has no authority. It is memory, not order.

Ask the questions that keep this plain.

If the flicker proved need, why does it come at the old hours and not at strange hours. Because need would knock whenever the blood ran low. A cue knocks when the scene repeats.

And if the flicker proved pleasure missed, why does it thin so fast when unfed. Watch yours fade. The second morning thinner than the first.

So do not change your hours to dodge the flicker. Drink coffee at the same table. Take the break at the same door. Close the meal at the same cloth. Go to the yard when the talk goes.

The flicker is not an order. It is an echo losing its voice.

The Reward Was Never In The Stick

There is one more knot to untie in daylight, because it tied itself around almost everything you finished.

You told me once, in the words smokers use when they are honest past midnight, that sticks were the reward for well, almost everything. Teaching the class. Finishing the story. Finishing the paragraph. Driving five hundred miles. Driving to the shop for milk. Each finished act paid with a light, each small closure sealed with smoke, until the day looked like a string of little parties with fire at the centre of each.

I do not mock that string. I wore it myself. Reward is a deep human hunger, and you fed it the only coin the trap taught you to carry.

Ask what a reward is supposed to do, and then ask what the stick ever did.

A reward should mark the work finished and let the body rest in the finishing. It should taste of the work well done. It should leave the doer fuller than before, readier for the next work, gladder of the hands that did it.

Did the stick ever do that for any finished thing.

You finished the paragraph and you left the desk to stand in the cold. You finished the class and you hurried to the corner. You drove the five hundred miles and you paid each hundred with a dryness coffee had to wash. In each case the work was the pleasure. The stick arrived after the rise and claimed the rise as its own.

That pull was never a real relief or reward. It was the ending of a small edginess that the previous pull planted, dressed up as a prize because it always arrived at prize hour.

Think of the shop run plain. The drive short, the errand done, the bag on the seat smelling of bread. What did the light add to bread and to done. Nothing. It added ten seconds of quiet as the edginess eased, then planted the next edginess for an hour on.

Think of the paragraph plain. The sentence found at last. What sealed the sentence. The sentence sealed itself the moment it rang true.

Once you see the party was never in the stick, the whole string of parties comes back to you undivided.

Let the finished paragraph be its own party. Let the finished class be its own party. Let the finished drive be its own party. The work, the close, the rest, all yours, end to end.

You feared life would feel flat without treats. Find it studded thicker with treats than before, because every finished thing now pays its full pleasure instead of half its pleasure with a fee taken off.

It did plenty TO you. It did nothing FOR you. Read those two sentences against any finished act you choose, and watch them hold.

Complete And Favoured

Stand back now and look at the day you just lived in your mind, because the mind believes what the feet have walked, even in picture.

Morning coffee drunk hot to the grounds, fuller without the veil. Mid-morning break taken outside with nothing lit, rest undivided. Evening meal closed in peace, with nothing ajar. Night out laughed through to the goodnights, yard included, with belonging intact.

No hour avoided. No friend left. No drink refused from fear. No table fled. No yard skirted. You went everywhere you ever went and you did everything you ever did, and each hour held, and most hours shone brighter for being undivided.

That is what I promised when you laid the drawer out. Not a life narrowed to keep freedom, but a life widened because freedom walks inside it. The lightness you felt held down through the long clearing now rises under your feet as ordinary hours prove themselves one by one.

You came to this chapter with empty hands and open eyes, asking whether mornings still work. Leave it with proof in the body. Mornings work. Breaks rest. Meals close. Fridays laugh. Rewards live in the work and the finishing, not in the stick that charged admission. The flicker after coffee is memory, not need, and memory fades while you sip.

Let the day you just inhabited do its quiet work in the chest.

Sleep with the kitchen clean and the coat clean and the mouth clean. Wake to steam and light in bars across the cloth. Drink the cup whole. The moments are intact. They were always yours. They are favoured now because nothing stands between you and them.

SUMMARY

- Morning coffee tastes fuller with nothing lit because the pleasure was always the coffee and the quiet, not the companion beside the cup.
- The work break rests deeper when taken whole, because rest is legs and eyes and talk, not attendance at a timed hunger.
- The meal closes in peace on its own, because fullness and coffee already write the full stop the old seal only scribbled over.
- After-dinner coffee stays rich without the paired companion, and the hand soon forgets the path it once took without thought.
- Drinks and laughter hold without smoke, and the yard holds with you in it empty-handed, so no friend and no evening needs to be lost.
- The offered pack on a night out holds no belonging, only a dose that mistakes its own ending for Friday joy.
- The flicker after coffee or food is a learned picture tied to the hour, not proof the body calls for its measure, and it thins when unfed.
- The stick never paid for finished work, because the lift at prize hour was only edginess ending, never a prize given.
- Every finished act returns its full pleasure when no fee is taken, so days feel studded thicker with treats than before.
- Ordinary hours prove themselves complete and favoured when inhabited, and inhabiting them rises toward freedom without losing anything real.
