Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

*Inside BAD SUGAR one kept sliver keeps the whole loop alive.*

### THE FRIDAY DRAWER

You have watched the cinema row hold its laugh with a clear mouth. You have tasted plain food hungry and found it holds for hours. Now the old voice stops arguing for love and starts arguing for limits.

It says, very reasonably, very kindly:

“Once a week is fine. I will allow Fridays. Just one. Just a treat.”

We all ran that bargain. I ran it. Monday to Thursday plain and virtuous, eyes on Friday, a named bar in a named shop, eaten slow on the sofa as pay for being good. It sounded like control. It sounded like maturity. It sounded like the middle path between slavery and sense.

Look at what that week does in practice, hour by hour, because practice convicts what theory excuses.

Monday starts with a vow and a little pride. The cupboard is bare. The mouth is quiet. By Tuesday afternoon the picture of Friday is already brighter than any Friday ever was. You find yourself turning talk at work to what kind you will pick, which shop, milk or dark, large or small. By Wednesday the picture has sound and smell. By Thursday bread sounds dull at three o’clock and fruit sounds thin and water sounds like nothing. Thursday is no longer plain. Thursday is waiting. Friday morning the hands move faster. The shop door chimes louder. The wrapper crackles louder. The first bite floods harder because four dry days stand behind it. The flood fades fast because floods always fade fast. The hand that promised one counts the week as wages and takes two.

Then Saturday arrives with the bell still ringing.

Thursday taught the mouth to wait. Friday taught it that waiting pays. Saturday asks why waiting should restart so soon. A small voice says Thursday counts, Saturday is close enough, Monday will be clean again. You take a little to close the week. Sunday follows the same logic, a little to open the week. By Monday the drawer is empty and the vow is rewritten and the week starts again with a little less pride and a little more hunger.

Did the Friday dose calm the week or sharpen the hunt till Friday? It sharpened it.

Did Thursday stay plain or turn into rehearsal? It turned into rehearsal.

Did Saturday stay single or bleed to Sunday? It bled.

There is only one honest answer. Cut-down did not shrink the dose. Cut-down lit it up.

That lighting-up is not character. That lighting-up is timetable. Weeks of no-no-no followed by go-go-go teach the mouth that scarcity ends on Friday and scarcity must be answered with hurry. The longer the gap, the louder the bell, the faster the hand. I tell you this as settled fact because your own drawer proved it before any book named it. You watched a permitted sliver turn to hunt in your own kitchen. You watched “I’ve managed to be good this week” turn to “I feel like i cant stop” by Saturday night. No white coat is needed. Your diary is the witness.

The whisper that one kept dose proves control is the Sweet Con at work. It sells you a cage with visiting hours and calls the visiting hours power. This weekly bargain is the Sugar Trap talking. It keeps the belief fed while the hand waits. The belief is the master. The hand only obeys.

Ask what one Friday would have to prove to earn its place. It would have to show that six days plain plus one dose equals calm. It never does. Six days plain plus one dose equals six days of waiting plus one night of flood plus two days of bleed. Six days plain with no dose held in mind equals six days that grow plainer and calmer while taste wakes and mornings clear. Which week would you sign for the next ten years? Which week leaves you thinking about chocolate less?

We kept Fridays and thought about chocolate daily. That sentence convicts the bargain.

### THE CLIFF WITH A MEASURED FALL

Come with me to a cliff, because moderation needs to be seen in daylight where its physics shows.

A friend takes you to the coast on a bright morning. Gulls crying. Salt on the lips. Grass to the very lip. Then a sheer drop to black water far below, waves beating white on rock. He spreads his arms like a showman and says, “Jump. It is quite safe provided you fall only a few metres. I have measured it. Good jumpers stop mid-air. Just do Fridays. Just do birthdays. Just do one.”

You would laugh in his face. You would ask how a falling body stops mid-air because a friend measured it. You would ask who issues the permit for a safe plunge. You would ask what good jumpers have to do with gravity. You would walk back from the edge and call him mad, and you would be right.

Yet we accept the same offer when it wears a wrapper. Jump off the sweet cliff provided you fall only to Friday. Plunge provided you stop at one. Drop provided you land on birthdays only. The physics is identical. A falling body falls. A fed loop loops.

“Just one” is not a dose. “Just one” is a direction. One re-teaches the mouth that evenings close with coating. One re-tunes the bell for next week. One tells the small creature that waits in the gut that waiting pays. Each Friday you feed the Nibbler a little longer. Each Friday the grumble that would have died in days is nursed till next Friday. The creature does not die on rations. It lives on rations.

We told ourselves weaning softens the landing. We cut from daily to Fridays to fortnightly, smaller bars, darker bars, thinner slices, longer gaps. Each cut raised the glow of the kept one. Each cut taught the mouth to meet the smaller hit with larger want. The hit shrank. The want grew. That is not a landing. That is a tightening.

Cutting down makes the thing precious. Forbidden fruit tastes brighter because it is forbidden, not because it is fruit. Dieting taught us this years ago. The week we forbid, we fantasize. The day we permit, we gorge. Then we blame a weak self for a certain result of a timed loop. No wonder the box emptied. The schedule built the binge.

So meet the cliff offer with plain sight. There is no measured fall inside BAD SUGAR. A dose is a shove off the edge with instructions to stop mid-air. Stand back from the edge and there is no fall to measure.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Hold that beside the Friday drawer. Plenty TO the week — waiting, glow, hurry, flood, bleed, Monday shame. Nothing FOR the week — no calm, no pay, no prize that lasts past ten o’clock. The week gave the colour. The dose took the pay.

### JUST ONE KEEPS THE BELL RINGING

Let me take your three last guards in your own words, because each sounds like sense and each keeps the same belief breathing.

“I will have just one and stop. One cannot hurt.”

One already hurt in your own diary. One named biscuit opened to that wrapper rustle to an empty box before the last swallow ends. That hour tells the whole law. The craving once lit carries the binge. Not because you are weak. Not because you are condemned to hunt for life. Because one lit dose wakes the dip its last dose dug and the dip calls for its next spike. “I feel like i cant stop” is not confession of character. It is description of a lit loop from inside. Unlit, the loop has no voice. Lit, it shouts. Why light it to prove you can ignore the shout?

Ask two plain questions. When did one stay one in the last year without a fight that spoiled the hour? And what did the fight cost — counting, bargaining, chewing slow to make it last, hunting the cupboard after with the taste still up? If one were harmless, it would pass like bread and be forgotten. It never passes. It lingers and calls. That lingering is the verdict.

Broad hunger welcomes bread and soup and fruit and waits. Narrow tug names sweet only and cannot wait. Which one behaves like need? Need rests when met for hours. The narrow tug quiets for minutes and returns louder. The urgent voice sharpens where packs cluster and thins in a plain kitchen with bread on the board. Voices that change with wallpaper are not organs. They are lessons.

You never needed willpower to leave bread unfinished. You left it because hunger closed. You never needed a rule to stop soup mid-bowl when satisfied. Satisfaction closed the bowl. Sweet alone demanded a rule, a count, a lock, a sermon, because sweet alone was never answering hunger. It was answering its own last fall. See that and the “just one” plea loses its innocence. It is not a taste request. It is a lighting request.

Ask a third question and let your evenings answer. If one proved control, would the mind go quiet after one? It never went quiet. It counted, pictured, planned the next one while chewing the first. Control that needs counting is not control. It is tenancy. A mind that sees BAD SUGAR plain needs no count. Bread needs no count. Soup needs no count. Only a lit bell needs counting, and the count itself proves the lighting.

Keep no lighting and no count is needed.

### WEANED AND TOMORROW

“I will wean off slowly. Stopping outright is extreme.”

Weaning is Friday logic stretched thin. Smaller hits at longer gaps with brighter pictures between. Between hits the mouth rehearses. Rehearsal is feeding. The bell rings louder when food is near but withheld. Farmers know this. Guards know this. Dieters know this. The slow taper keeps the picture lit for weeks while taste never wakes and mornings never clear. The last ordinary dose taken with attention and a vow ends the rehearsal in one evening. The taper extends it for a month. Which is kinder? Which keeps the mind on chocolate longer?

We cut to half bars and told ourselves half the harm. The mind heard double the prize. We cut to dark bars and told ourselves wiser chemistry. The mouth heard the same sweet flood with a new label. We cut to weekdays plain and told ourselves we were in control. The gut heard a timetable and learned to gorge on time. Each cut fed the Sweet Con the same meal: the belief that the hit pays. The powder changed. The pay-slip stayed.

“I will quit tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays.”

Tomorrow is a drawer with no date. We kept that drawer for years. After the birthday means the birthday dose re-lights the bell and tomorrow starts lit. After the holidays means January starts with a month of lit evenings behind it. The Con does not need your weekdays. It needs your exception. One kept occasion keeps the whole peal tuned.

Ask three plain questions and let your diary answer. If tomorrow is safer, what changes in the body by morning that makes tonight’s dose wiser? Nothing changes but another flood and another dip. If the birthday needs sugar to count, did the candles need frosting to glow when you replayed the table clear? They did not. If holidays excuse dosing, why do holidays dosed leave you coated and hunting at ten while holidays lived clear leave you remembering faces? The excuse pays nothing. The delay charges daily.

Waiting is the Trap. Delay does not prepare. Delay rehearses.

“I want to get off the roller coaster” is the truest sentence you ever spoke in community speech. You spoke of short term lift then fast drop because you lived short term lift then fast drop. You spoke of sugary somethings to get me through because afternoons taught you the phrase. The roller coaster has only one stable exit. Not a slower car. Not a weekly ride. Not a tomorrow ride. The gate out is the belief gate. See that the ride never lifted above the platform, it only dropped below and charged for the climb back, and the wish to ride thins. Keep one ride booked and the queue stays in the mind all week.

### I DON’T KEEP IT IN THE HOUSE

“I don’t keep it in the house. That is my control.”

We said that too. We cleared cupboards on Mondays with a clean sweep and a sermon. No bars. No biscuits. No cola. House clean. Mind made up. Then Tuesday at the till the tired hand reached. Wednesday at work the drawer opened. Thursday at the station the machine glowed. Friday at a friend’s the plate passed and the hand that was strong at home was helpless abroad.

Who was in charge? The house or the belief?

A clean cupboard never touched the belief. It only moved the hunt outdoors. The mouth still pictured Friday. The nose still lifted at wrapper smell. The feet still slowed by the bright aisle. The evening still closed with want. We called absence control while the mind stayed tenanted. Tenants do not leave because you lock one room. They use another door.

Watch what house-rule does to value. Home becomes virtue. Outside becomes prize. The bar outside tastes brighter because home forbids it. The office biscuit glows because the kitchen lacks it. Scarcity at home raises price abroad. The loop does not shrink. It travels.

I am not asking you to stock the house to prove a point. I am showing you that place is not the master. Belief is the master. A mind that sees BAD SUGAR plain walks past bright aisles with no argument and sits at a birthday table with a clear plate and no sermon. A mind that still holds one precious exception hunts whether the cupboard is full or bare.

Ask your own feet. When the house was clean last time, did the thought of sweets visit less or more? It visited more, dressed as planning, as picturing, as counting days to Friday. When the house held a full box after you saw the con, did the full box pull? An unlit bell does not pull. A full cupboard in a house that sees makes no noise. The noise was never in the wood. The noise was in the kept picture.

Keep no picture and no place needs policing.

We were conned here by the oldest house trick in the Sugar Trap. Make home holy, make away a holiday, and the holiday glows. The glow is not in the bar. The glow is in the permission. Permission is belief wearing a key. Take the key back and both rooms quiet together. The kitchen stays plain without virtue. The aisle stays bright without pull. The friend’s plate passes without commentary in the head. No lock is oiled. No door is watched. The tenant left, so doors need no guard.

Ask what house-rule would have to prove to earn its keep. It would have to show that locked wood quiets the mind. It never does. Locked wood loudens the mind. Only a seen con quiets the mind. Which guardian would you hire for the next ten years, a lock that sharpens want or sight that drops want? Locks need watching. Sight needs no watch. That difference is the whole method in one kitchen.

### SWEETENERS AND SWAPS KEEP YOU A USER

Now the last clever door. If the product is the poison, swap the product.

Diet cola. Sweetener in tea. Honey on porridge. Juice in place of cola. Date bars. Protein bars. Low-sugar biscuits that still hit sweet and still come in wrappers and still get eaten standing at the cupboard at ten.

“At least it is not sugar.”

At least it keeps everything that matters. The sweet flood on the tongue. The quick blood hurry. The dip and the tug for more. The hand-to-mouth shuttle while eyes stay on screens. The ten o’clock hunt. The belief that a sweet hit pays for a hard hour. Swap the powder and you keep the pay-slip.

We swapped and watched. Cola swapped to diet still called at three o’clock. Bars swapped to dark still emptied by the box. Sugar swapped to honey still spooned for the flood rather than for hunger. The mouth did not ask for chemistry. The mouth asked for hit. The hit answered with minutes of blanking followed by want. Different label. Same timetable.

Sweeteners keep the ritual lit. Same hour. Same mug. Same rustle. Same standing in the kitchen with the fridge light on the face. Ritual teaches faster than substance. Wrapper, hour, smell, tired hands — bells rung so often the mouth waters at the bell. Ring the bell with a new powder and the mouth still waters. Watering mouths hunt. Hunting hands find old doors.

Ask what a swap would have to prove to earn its keep. It would have to show that sweet taste without sucrose ends the hunt. It never does. It schools the hunt. It tells the small tenant that sweet flood still answers evenings. Each diet hit you feed the Nibbler under a new name. Each kept ritual tells the Sweet Con the belief stands: evenings need a sweet close.

Ask two more. When the swap was in hand, did thoughts of sweets thin across the month or hold steady, dressed in new labels? They held. When the swap ran out, did the hand accept water and bread calmly or hurry to the old bar? It hurried. That hurry names the master. Not sucrose. The sweet-hit belief.

There is no lighter fall from this cliff. Light ropes still hang from the same edge.

We were brainwashed to read the packet, not the pattern. Packet says low. Pattern says hunt. Packet says natural. Pattern says nightly. Packet says protein. ### THE SEATBELT IN A STEADY CAR

Drive with me a short mile and the margin falls into place without fear and without licence.

You drive steady on a dry road, hands on the wheel, eyes ahead. You wear a seatbelt. The belt sits quiet across the chest. It asks nothing. It punishes nothing. It exists for an accidental jolt — a sudden stop, a careless driver cutting in — not for steering into walls. No sane driver says, “I wear a belt, so I can drive erratically.” No sane driver panics at a pothole as proof the road owns him.

Your body rides the same way with food.

A crumb in sauce at a friend’s table. A spoon of sugar in bread you did not bake. A misread label on a dull Tuesday. The body absorbs the jolt and drives on. The steady driver does not swerve into the ditch to test the belt. The steady eater does not spoon a deliberate dose to test the body. One accidental taste revives nothing unless the old belief is let back in to drive.

Hear the line clean because panic and licence both lie here. Your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip, but your mind cannot afford a deliberate one. If a slip happens, it is a rumble lesson, not a crash. Note the bell, see what lit it — late night, bright aisle, old hour — and drive on with eyes ahead. Do not mourn the crumb. Do not chase it. Do not turn one jolt into a steering policy. Guard the belief and the hand stays quiet.

If you ever feel faint, ill, or distressed around food, put this book aside and get care promptly and follow your clinician first.

That single guard stands outside the argument. Inside the argument the road is simple. No deliberate dose, however small, however dated, however sweetened under another name, earns a place. Not Fridays. Not birthdays. Not holidays. Not house-rule abroad. Not weaned slivers. Not swapped powders. The line holds because the line describes truth: inside BAD SUGAR there is no dose that pays.

You came to this chapter negotiating carve-outs. Leave it knowing carve-out is the Con. The drawer, the cliff permit, the tomorrow promise, the clean cupboard that still hunts outdoors, the new label on the old hit — five doors to one room. Close the belief and all five close together.

### NO STRICT DIET, PLAIN SIGHT

Other voices will press at this point with kind, confident ruin.

“Totality is just another strict diet. You preach no rules while issuing a rule.”

Answer it straight from your own replay, because the smear sounds clever and collapses on sight.

No wonder the hand wins by Thursday. No wonder Monday restarts. The door needs strain because the prize is still believed to be behind it.

Ask what a diet counts. It counts calories, days, sins, points. Ask what we count. We count nothing. A diet makes the thing precious by forbidding. Totality makes the thing worthless by seeing. Forbidding raises glow. Seeing drops glow. The Friday glow came from waiting, not from cocoa.
