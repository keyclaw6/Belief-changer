Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

*Waiting till tomorrow keeps you hooked; this ordinary treat closes it tonight.*

### TOMORROW NEVER COMES CLEAN

You have closed every carve-out. No Friday drawer. No birthday pass. No clean cupboard that hunts outdoors. No swapped powder under a new label. The mind is clear on what BAD SUGAR is and what it never was.

And still a soft voice says:

“I will start after the holidays. After the birthday. When work calms. When I feel ready someday.”

We all said it. I said it. It sounds like prudence. It sounds like kindness to yourself. It sounds like a careful eater choosing a calm date.

The fact is delay is not prudence. Delay is the loop keeping its own picture lit.

Ask what tomorrow would have to prove to earn its place. It would have to show that one more week of doses makes the next week easier to close. Did any waited week ever do that in your life? Did Christmas waiting make January plain? Did birthday waiting make the week after quiet? Did “after the holidays” ever arrive with a quiet mouth, or did it arrive with a louder hunt and a heavier jaw and a fresh reason to wait again?

Ask a second question and let your own evenings answer. If tomorrow is safer, what changes in your body by morning that makes tonight’s dose wiser? Nothing changes but another flood and another dip. The mouth wakes coated. The afternoon calls earlier. The evening closes later. The bell rings on time.

Ask a third question. If waiting prepares, why did every period of waiting leave you thinking about chocolate more, not less? Because waiting is rehearsal. Between doses the mouth pictures. Picturing feeds. The picture held till tomorrow is the belief held till tomorrow.

Waiting does not prepare. Waiting rehearses.

See whose voice tomorrow belongs to. It is not your caution. It is the old con keeping one door ajar. It tells you caution means pause while it uses the pause to re-light the dip and re-sharpen the want. We were brainwashed to read pause as wisdom. Pause inside a timed loop is schooling. Each “just after this one occasion” teaches the mouth that occasions close sweet. Each kept occasion tunes the bell for the next.

Look closely at how the delay sells itself, because it never sells itself as fear. It sells itself as manners. Not now, the table is full. Not now, the host went to trouble. Not now, work is loud. Who could argue with manners? We did, for years, and manners always billed us. The full table dosed still emptied to hurry and thirst. The kind host forgotten by ten o’clock while the coating stayed. Work loud dosed stayed loud plus coated. Manners paid nothing. Delay charged daily.

And what does delay promise in return for the charge? A cleaner start. A quieter mind. A stronger self. Has a dosed week ever delivered a stronger self? It delivered a tired self with a fresh excuse. The mouth that pictures all week does not rest. The hands that count days to a date do not calm. The “careful” wait is the busiest week of all for sweet thought, because thought held for later is thought fed daily.

At this edge, you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by seeing delay plain.

You lose no pleasure, because the pleasure was never in the dose. You lose no help for hard days, because the dose never helped a hard day. You lose no love at the table, because love stayed when the mouth was clear. What you lose is the tug you mistook for caution. That pull you call prudence is only the tug-of-war of craving and fear talking, both ropes pulled by the same hands.

One rope says you will fail without it. The other says you will fail without a perfect date. Both ropes say wait. Both ropes keep the picture lit. Cut both by seeing both. There is no failed date and no perfect date. There is only a seen loop and an unseen loop.

I tell you as one who waited years for a clean January: January never comes clean while December is dosed. The road clears the night the dosing stops being booked. Your escape tonight is not early. It is on time.

### ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS

Do not step forward from duty. Step forward from sight.

You should be champing to cross by now, not screwing yourself to a trial. If you are, read on. If some picture still glows, if some “but the cinema” still whispers, put this chapter down and go back through the plain weeks in your mind — the lit drawer, the cliff permit, the clean cupboard that hunted outdoors, the swapped bar that kept the hour — until the glow thins. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

This is not a test of character. This is a gate of sight. I will not ask you to summon strain. I ask you to check your own seeing.

Can you name one dose in the last year that paid beyond ten minutes without calling for its brother? You cannot.

Can you name one waited tomorrow that arrived quieter than tonight? You cannot.

Can you name one plain meal eaten hungry that left you hunting the way a bar leaves you hunting? You cannot.

Those three honest answers are the gate. They mean the belief has thinned and the body is ready to be left to itself.

Let me hold you at the gate a moment longer, because champing matters. Champing is not hype. Champing is recognition. The dog champs at the gate when it sees the field beyond, not when it is whipped toward it. You have seen the field. You have tasted plain food hungry and found it holds for hours. You have watched a clear evening close calm without a sweet seal. You have felt mornings come up clean when the night stayed plain. Why would a mind that saw that want to book another week of flood and bleed to earn what it already owns?

If the heart still flutters, ask what the flutter protects. Does it protect joy? Joy stayed clear. Does it protect help? Help never came coated. Does it protect love? Love never needed frosting to count. The flutter protects nothing but the old picture, and pictures thin when looked at straight.

I know the wobble that comes at a gate like this. It can feel like standing at an open cell door and wanting to stay inside because outside looks wide. That wobble had a shape we know well, like a lion pacing past an open cage from habit, not need. The feet move old steps. The nose follows old smell. The habit paces though hunger is gone and the door stands wide.

Let the wobble be habit, not warning. Habit paces. Warning protects. This pacing protects nothing. It only repeats steps learned when the bell rang nightly. Steps learned can be left un-walked. An open door does not need courage to walk through when you see the cell was never home.

You do not need a perfect mood. You need a clear seeing and a willing mouth. Take the step tonight in your ordinary kitchen, in your ordinary clothes, with your ordinary bar. No ceremony borrowed from elsewhere. No last supper glow. The relief you will know by morning is not bought by strain; it is released by sight.

Do not wait to feel fearless. Wait only to feel honest. Honest is enough. Honest says the dose never paid. Honest says the wait never cleaned. Honest says the plain meal satisfied longer than the bar ever did. With those three honests in hand, the feet move by themselves.

### THE ORDINARY MOUTHFUL

Take your ordinary treat now. Not a laboratory dose. Not an experiment bought to test yourself. Your usual bar, your usual biscuit, your usual handful, taken as you always take it, at your usual hour.

Only this time pay attention all the way through.

Unwrap it as always. Smell it as always. Put the first bite in the mouth and do not look away in the mind. Notice the coating that spreads and sticks. Notice the flood that blanks for seconds and then thins. Notice the chew that hurries though hunger never called. Notice the swallow that leaves thirst and a filmed tongue and a call for another bite before the first has settled.

We ate for years with eyes half closed — television on, phone in hand, talk covering the hurry. Tonight eat with eyes open. Taste the cloy that sits after the third chew. Taste the salt under the sweet put there to sharpen want. Taste how narrow the hit is: sweet only, loud only, gone only to return. Bread rests for hours. Soup rests for hours. Fruit eaten hungry rests and clears. This hit rests for minutes and returns louder. Which one behaves like food? Which one behaves like a bell?

Keep going to the end of the ordinary amount. Do not stop early to prove a point. Do not gorge to punish yourself. Finish as you always finish, then set the wrapper down and look at what is left: a coated mouth, a stirred gut, a faint thirst, a mind already picturing the next close. That picture is not enjoyment. That picture is the dip digging its next call.

Stay with the wrapper a minute longer than comfort allows, because comfort always looked away at this point. Look at the crumbs. Smell the empty foil. Feel the film on the teeth that water does not lift clean. Feel the hurry in the hands that want to tidy it away fast so the mind can keep the glow. Hurry to tidy is part of the loop. The loop hates being watched to the end. Watch it. Let the last chew sit. Let the sweetness turn. What starts as flood ends as paste. What starts as promise ends as press. Where is the treat in paste? Where is the fuel in press?

I did this on my own kitchen floor with crumbs on my shirt, and the ugliness I had glossed for years stood plain for the first time. No pleasure to mourn. No friend to bury. A sticky mouth and a hurried hand and a bell asking for overtime. Once seen it could not be unseen.

Ask what that mouthful would have to show to earn a place for life. It would have to show calm, help, love — something the plain meal did not give better. It showed coating, hurry, thirst, want. The plain meal gave hours of quiet. The bar gave minutes of blanking and an evening of noise. The verdict writes itself.

This ends easily, immediately and permanently when the vow is spoken, because the vow does not fight the mouthful. The vow names it.

Speak it aloud now, steady and plain, hand on the table:

“I see it now. That was not a treat. That was the loop. I close BAD SUGAR tonight.”

Say it as a fact witnessed, not as a wish. You have witnessed. The mouth is witness. The wrapper is witness. The thirst is witness. Then sit a minute and let the room stay ordinary. Kettle ticking. Street noise. Wrapper still. Nothing to mourn. A small, slightly silly joy starts to rise when you feel the picture thin. Let it rise and celebrate the quiet kitchen with a clear mouth for the first time.

Stay seated till the joy steadies. It will not arrive as fireworks. It arrives as quiet. The shoulders drop. The jaw unclenches. The eyes clear. The thought comes — no more booking, no more waiting, no more counting — and with it a laugh you did not plan. That laugh is freedom conferring itself. Do not chase it bigger. Do not test it. Let the ordinary room hold it. Kettle, street, table, breath. You have not lost an evening pleasure. You have got back the evenings.

### THE NEXT FEW DAYS

Hear plain what the next few days hold, so no grumble can dress itself as warning.

For a few days you may notice a faint hollow tug in the early evening, a brief flat hour at three o’clock, a passing picture of wrapper and smell when tired hands open a cupboard. It peaks in two to five days for most, then thins and passes. It is small-scale, days-long, a dying echo, not a lifelong fight. If you ever feel faint, ill, or distressed around food, put this book aside and get care promptly and follow your clinician first.

That guard stands outside the argument. Inside the argument the shape is simple. The tug is not hunger. Broad hunger welcomes bread and soup and fruit and waits. This tug names sweet only and cannot wait. Voices that change with wallpaper are not organs. They are lessons unlearning themselves.

Do not feed the picture to test it. Do not argue with it. Note it the way you note rain on glass — there, passing, not orders — and get on with the hour: market bag unpacked, bread cut, water poured, work resumed. Each un-fed evening the bell rings a little duller. Each plain meal the tongue wakes a little more. Mornings come up clear and the mouth feels clean in a wonderful ordinary way that needs no sermon.

I promise you this from my own first week and from thousands of witnessed weeks: the echo frightens only before it is named. Named, it shrinks. Smile at it when it comes. “There you are. Passing.” Then turn to the faces, the work, the walk, the meal ahead. The body knows how to eat, sleep, wake, move. It did before the loop and it does after. Your job is not to manage the echo. Your job is to keep the belief seen and let the echo starve for lack of picture.

No counting. No watching the clock. No measuring days on a chart. The hump is brief and mild and it ends. What remains is taste waking, hunger speaking plain, satisfaction closing the bowl without arithmetic.

Understand the mildness and you will not dread it. This is not a cliff to climb. It is a hollow to walk through while the view clears. A hollow has walls for a day or two and then opens. Walk steady, eyes ahead, meals plain and full when hungry, water near, bed early. The feet already know the road. They walked it as a child before the loop, hungry then satisfied then running off to play. They walk it again now.

### THE TWO DOORS THAT STAY SHUT

Two thoughts will knock in the coming weeks. Both arrive in your own voice. Both are old scripts, not reasoning. Hear them now so they arrive pre-answered.

The first knocks on a hard day.

“I’ve had a foul day. One will get me through. Just this once to close it.”

We spoke that line for years. “Sugary somethings to get me through” was our dialect for dusk. Ask whose help that one ever gave. Did the bar shorten the hard day or coat it? Did the evening close calmer or louder? Did morning thank you? You know the answers because you lived them: short term lift then fast drop, the roller coaster you said you wanted to leave. A hard day needs sleep, food, water, a kind word, an early bed — not a coated mouth and a second hard hour. When the knock comes, answer with what the day taught: the dose never closed a day; it billed it.

The second knocks on a good day.

“I’m free now. One can’t hurt. I can prove control by having one and stopping.”

That proof is the oldest trap-door in the book. One lit dose wakes the dip its last dose dug. One re-tunes the bell for next week. One teaches the mouth again that evenings close sweet. When did one stay one last year without a fight that spoiled the hour? What did the fight cost — bargaining, chewing slow to make it last, hunting after with taste still up? Bread needs no count. Soup needs no count. Only a lit bell needs counting, and the count itself proves the lighting. Keep no lighting and no count is needed.

If a slip happens — a spoon in sauce unnoticed, a bite pressed on you before thought caught up — do not turn a jolt into a steering policy. Note what lit the bell — late night, bright aisle, old hour — and walk on with eyes ahead. A stumble is a rumble lesson about the bell, never proof the road owns the driver. Guard the picture and the hand stays quiet without commentary in the head.

From tonight, whenever the thought of BAD SUGAR crosses your mind, answer at once with the thought you will keep for life:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it as joy, not as effort. Think it at the till when packs glow. Think it on the sofa when the old hour rings. Think it at the birthday table with a clear plate. Each thought of sweet becomes a hit of gladness that you no longer feed the loop. The intruder becomes the reminder. The reminder becomes the prize. That is how a passing thought stays passing and a mind stays clear without policing places or hours.

You stand now where every free eater stood: ordinary kitchen, ordinary bar finished, ordinary night ahead — and everything changed because seeing changed.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

**SUMMARY**

- Tomorrow never arrives quieter because delay adds another flood and another dip.
- Waiting is rehearsal, not preparation; picturing keeps the bell tuned.
- The gate is sight, not mood; honest answers about past doses open it.
- Wobble at the door is old pacing, not warning about outside.
- The ordinary mouthful watched closely shows coating, hurry, thirst and want.
- Plain meals rest for hours while the hit quiets for minutes and returns louder.
- The vow spoken aloud names the witnessed loop and closes booked dosing.
- The next few days may bring a brief hollow tug that peaks and passes.
- Hunger welcomes many foods and waits; the narrow tug names sweet only.
- A hard-day knock never closed a day; it billed it till morning.
- One to prove control re-lights the bell and brings back counting.
- A missed crumb is a lesson about the bell, never a steering policy.
