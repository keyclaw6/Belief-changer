Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

IN THIS CHAPTER — Morning kettle and breakfast bowl, bright aisle and checkout till, plain meal and clear plate, evening sofa and passing thought.

*There is nothing to watch for and nothing to miss; the plain day holds without a sweet seal.*

### THE KETTLE BOILS AS USUAL

You wake to the same kettle, the same street noise, the same light at the curtain edge.

Pour water. Cut bread. Warm oats. Sit down hungry.

That is the whole morning. No ceremony. No guard posted at the cupboard. Hunger calls, food answers, the bowl closes. Hands wash the spoon. Feet move to coat and door.

We once read mornings as a test. Would the mouth hold till eleven? Would the drawer call at ten? Would the afternoon already cast its shadow over toast? That reading belonged to dosed years, when every plain hour was measured against the next hit and every quiet stomach was heard as lack.

Ask what this morning has to prove to earn its place. Does bread eaten hungry hold you for hours? It does. Does fruit eaten hungry clear the mouth and leave water tasting clean? It does. Does tea without a biscuit beside it still warm the hands and start the day? You are holding the answer.

I stood at my own sink on the first such morning and waited for the tug to order me about. It did not order. A faint picture passed — wrapper, smell, hurry — and passed on like rain on glass. No argument in the head. No bargaining. Porridge, berries, toast, out the door.

If a hollow flicker comes at half past ten, note it the way you note a bus going past. There. Passing. Not orders. Voices that change with wallpaper are not organs. Broad hunger welcomes bread and soup and fruit and waits. The narrow picture names sweet only and cannot wait. That narrowness tells you whose voice it is.

Do not test the picture by feeding it. Do not argue with it to prove strength. Turn to the hour: bag packed, keys found, work begun, walk taken. Each plain morning the tongue wakes a little more. Each plain breakfast the mid-morning stops barking earlier.

What starts as flood ends as paste. What starts as a plain bowl ends as hours of quiet. Which one behaves like food?

The kettle boils as usual. So do you.

### THE AISLE AND THE TILL

Go to the same shop at the same hour down the same aisle.

Nothing moves except your seeing. Packs still glow. Mascots still grin. The checkout still stacks small bars where hands wait idle. The bell over the door still rings.

We walked this aisle for years with eyes half-closed and hands half-open, telling ourselves we chose each bar while the hand moved before the choosing. Now walk it with eyes open and hands quiet.

Push the trolley past the bright wall. Notice how narrow the hit is: sweet only, loud only, gone only to return. Bread rests for hours. Soup rests for hours. A dosed mouth rests for minutes and returns louder. The price ticket tells one cost. The mouth tells the other: coating, hurry, thirst, want.

You need no detour and no armor. Take the same route. Queue at the same till. Stand beside the same glow without commentary in the head. If you ever feel faint, ill, or distressed around food, set this book aside and get care promptly and follow your clinician first.

That guard stands outside the argument. Inside the argument the shape is simple. You came for dinner, not for a dose. Dinner sits in vegetables, bread, fish, oil, fruit. The bar beside the till is not dinner and never was.

Look once at the trolley ahead piled with family packs. We were that trolley. Pity rises, not envy. Does that mountain look like enjoyment? A mother bargaining with herself till Friday, a father counting squares to make it last, a child already taught that love closes sweet. We know the evening that follows: wrapper rustle, box light, regret before the swallow ends. We know the morning that follows: coated tongue, flat hour, fresh vow.

You left the Sugar Trap the night you saw the loop plain. They stand where we stood, still reading the glow as kindness. Let your quiet do what preaching never does. Say nothing unless asked. When asked, speak plain and short and turn back to your basket.

Pay at the till with a clear mouth. Paper bags. Coins counted. Bell ringing for the next. Walk out with food that holds.

### A MEAL THAT FINISHES CLEAN

Eat lunch hungry at the ordinary table.

Market vegetables, fresh bread torn, soup poured, olive oil, salt, an apple cut slow. Notice, savour, stop when satisfied. No counting. No measuring on a chart. The body leads and the plate tells you when.

Like a small child leaving a peach half-eaten to run off and play, the satisfied mouth stops mid-bite without grief.

We once called dessert love and feared eating would turn grey without it. Ask whose love that was. Was it the bar that made the table warm, or faces and hunger met and talk running long? Did the sweet seal close the meal, or coat it and hurry it? When did a lit bowl ever close calm? It closed to hurry and thirst and picturing.

Isolate the moment and the answer writes itself. The meal gave hours of quiet. The hit gave minutes of blanking and an evening of noise. Bread needs no count. Soup needs no count. Only a lit bell needs counting, and the count itself proves the lighting.

I ate such a meal on day two — lentils, carrots, bread, water, pears — and waited for the grey we were promised. No grey came. Colour came up stronger. Salt tasted saltier. Water tasted clean. The jaw unclenched. Eyes cleared. The thought came — no more booking, no more waiting — and with it a laugh I did not plan.

If tired hands open a cupboard from old steps at dusk, let the steps go un-walked. Steps learned when the bell rang nightly can be left un-walked. Pour water. Cut cheese. Warm leftovers. Eat till satisfied and put the plate down. Satisfaction closes the bowl without arithmetic.

Which mouth would you trust to tell you when enough is enough: the one that stops mid-apple and runs off, or the one that hunts the box after the box is empty?

Trust hunger. It spoke before the loop and speaks after.

### WHEN A THOUGHT WALKS PAST

You cannot stop thoughts arriving. Nor should you try.

On the third afternoon, at the old hour, a picture will walk past: wrapper and smell, bright aisle, old chair. On the fifth evening, tired after work, it will knock again. That is not failure. That is a mind clearing old post.

We spent years believing a thought was an order. It is not. A thought of sweet is paper through the door. You read it, smile, answer, get on with the hour.

Do not push it away. Do not hold it close. Pushing feeds it. Holding feeds it. See it for what it is — the Nibbler starving, grumbling as it thins — and answer at once with the thought you will keep for good:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it as joy, not as effort. Think it at the till when packs glow. Think it on the sofa when the old hour rings. Think it with a clear plate at a birthday table. Each thought of sweet becomes a hit of gladness that you no longer feed the loop. The intruder becomes the reminder. The reminder becomes the prize.

Ask two questions and let your own evenings answer. If the picture helped, when did picturing ever close a day calmer? It billed the day till morning. If the picture proved need, why does broad hunger welcome many foods while this picture names one powder only? Need feeds on dinner. Lessons feed on pictures.

I tell you as one who heard that knock for a week: named, it shrinks. “There you are. Passing.” Then turn to faces, work, walk, meal ahead. The bell rings a little duller each un-fed evening. Mornings come up clean and the mouth feels clean in a quiet ordinary way that needs no sermon.

A thought is not a dose. A picture is not a mouthful. Keep the picture seen and the hand stays quiet.

### THE EVENING SOFA

Evening keeps its chair, its lamp, its talk, its programme. Only the seal changes.

Sit where you always sit. Cup of tea. Blanket. Street noise low. The old hour rings — half past eight, lights low — and for a second the hand pictures the dim cinema glow where chocolate once sat beside laughter.

See whose glow that was. Was it the bar that made the room warm, or the story and the company and the hour to rest? Replay the same warmth without a dose and sweetness stays unchanged, because sweetness never came coated. Laughter stayed when the mouth was clear. Rest stayed when the night stayed plain. The bar only sneaked a ride on an evening that already shone.

Look across the street at lit windows where others still close the day sweet. We know that close: hurry, crumbs, filmed teeth, mind already picturing the next. Does that look like enjoyment? You are not deprived. They are billed.

Do not preach to them. Preaching breeds defence and drags you back into arguments that reopen your own doubt. If a friend presses a plate on you, smile and speak short: “No thanks — I closed that.” No lecture. No story unless asked. Visible quiet recruits better than words.

If a slip happens — a spoon in sauce unnoticed, a bite pressed on you before thought caught up — do not turn a jolt into a steering policy. Note what lit the bell — late night, bright counter, old hour — and walk on with eyes ahead. A stumble is a rumble lesson about the bell, never proof the road owns the driver. A seatbelt exists for an accidental jolt, not for steering toward the ditch. Guard the picture and the hand stays quiet.

We dosed for years to close evenings. Evenings close better plain: tea drunk slow, mouth clear, sleep coming sooner, morning starting sooner.

Sit till the lamp clicks off. Kettle, street, breath, clear plate. Nothing to mourn. A small, slightly silly joy starts to rise when you feel the picture thin. Let it rise.

### NOTHING ELSE MOVES

Change nothing else in your life.

Same café. Same walk. Same friends. Same work break at three. Same birthday table. The body returns to its own timing on its own; elaborate scaffolding only tells the mind something was lost.

We once rearranged days to dodge hours, as if hours held power. Hours hold no power. Beliefs hold power, and yours has changed. Go where you always went. Order what you always ordered, minus the sweet hit. Drink coffee as coffee. Eat cake-hour as fruit and bread and talk.

Ask what avoidance would have to prove to earn its place. It would have to show that places pour doses down throats. They do not. Hands lift. Pictures order. Keep pictures seen and places stay neutral.

I kept my route, my bakery, my cinema seat. The bakery smells the same. The seat holds the same. Only the hand stays still, because the mind no longer reads smell as need.

If wobble comes at an open door, let it be habit, not warning. Habit paces old steps. Warning protects. This pacing protects nothing. Steps learned can be left un-walked.

You walked out of the Sugar Trap on an ordinary night in ordinary clothes with an ordinary wrapper set down. Walk on in ordinary days with ordinary baskets and ordinary plates. The grumble thins. Taste wakes. Hunger speaks plain. Satisfaction closes.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

**SUMMARY**

- Mornings start clear on bread and fruit eaten hungry, with no sweet seal needed.
- The same aisle and till stay neutral when the glow is seen as coating and hurry.
- Plain meals eaten hungry hold for hours and close without counting.
- Satisfaction stops mid-bite like a child called to play, while a lit mouth hunts an empty box.
- A passing picture is paper through the door, noted and answered, not obeyed.
- The evening sofa keeps its warmth because laughter and rest owned the glow.
- Other trolleys piled high call for pity, not envy, and no preaching.
- A missed crumb teaches about the bell and never steers the week.
- Same café, same route, same table prove places never poured.
