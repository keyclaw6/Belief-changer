Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The back page, the handed-back peach, the shut book and the meal ahead.

*You need no more teaching; the sweetness is yours again and life is waiting.*

### IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

Put this page down and begin at the beginning.

I say that with a smile and with total certainty. These pages are saved reminders, not the method that frees. The method was the slow unpicking of a con you cannot hurry from the back. Read in order, the seeing builds drop by drop till the last ordinary treat shows itself plain. Read from here, the lines below sound like orders.

We all tried to use the back page as a shortcut once. We hunted for the trick, the rule, the line to hold. That hunt belonged to dosed years, when every answer was another bargain and every bargain kept the bell ringing. This book holds no bargain. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

So if your eye fell here first, close the book now and open the first page hungry and open-minded. Carry on exactly as normal while you read. Let each room do its job. When you arrive here again, the list below will not read as effort. It will read as memory.

If you have read through, stay with me for a few minutes. There is nothing new to learn and nothing to guard with commentary in the head. There is a list to photograph, a door to walk through, and a truth about sweetness you have not yet been told.

### HANDED BACK

Look at a small child with a peach.

She eats hungry, juice to the chin, stops mid-bite and runs off to play. No counting. No grief. No picture of the next peach already forming. Hunger spoke, food answered, satisfaction closed the bowl. That was you before the first sweet hit taught the mouth to hunt.

We were conned out of that eater. The bright packs, the cartoon grins, the checkout ambush, the kindly voice that said a dose rewards, soothes, fuels — all of it trained a plain appetite to read a narrow powder as love. The Trap did not add sweetness to your life. It stole sweetness from plain food and sold it back coated, filmed, hurried, billed for hours after.

Ask your own table to answer. When did BAD SUGAR ever sweeten an hour that was not already sweet? The cinema laughed, the birthday glowed, the evening rested — and the dose rode along claiming the warmth. When did plain food eaten hungry ever leave film, thirst and hunting? Bread holds for hours. Soup holds for hours. Fruit eaten hungry tastes brighter now than any coated mouth ever allowed.

You have not lost anything. The coating, the hurry, the ten-minute blanking and the loud call after — that was never sweetness. Sweetness was the peach, the bread, the berries, the clear mouth tasting cold air on the walk to work. That sweetness was taken, and now it is handed back.

Taste how it returns. An apple cut slow after soup, juice sharp and clean to the back teeth. Berries with porridge, each one distinct, none needing a flood to be noticed. Bread crust torn and chewed, salt and wheat standing up plain. Water drunk after and tasting of water. The mouth uncoated notices more, not less. The tongue woken week by week finds sweetness where the flood had drowned it — in fruit, in warm bread, in milk, in the clean air itself.

Let that noticing run on through the week. Soup saltier on Tuesday than it was on Monday. Bread crust louder. The skin of an apple snapping clean. A glass of water after cheese tasting cold and complete. Nothing in those hours asks to be sealed or chased. Hunger comes, food answers, satisfaction puts the spoon down. The mouth stays clear between meals and the nose wakes with it, finding bakery air, lemon skin, hot coffee as themselves, not as calls to hunt.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing back into the eater you were.

Hold that growing plain. One road kept you dosing for the rest of your life — morning filmed, afternoon fogged, evening rustled, wallet lighter, teeth duller, mind already counting squares for tomorrow. The other lets you eat when hungry and stop when satisfied and live clear for the rest of your life — mornings clean, meals holding for hours, mouth clear, mind on faces and work and walk ahead. Which rest of life do you want? You have already chosen. The choosing was the seeing.

I stood at my own sink on that first clear morning and waited for orders. No orders came. Porridge, toast, apple, out the door. The legs walked without sums. The tongue woke week by week. Small notices, piled hour by hour, rebuilt what years of flood washed out. Yours will do the same, because yours is the same body, the same hunger, the same satisfaction. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

If food ever makes you faint or ill or distressed, put this book aside and follow your clinician first.

That guard stands outside the argument. Inside the argument there is no void to fill and no hour to dodge. Same café. Same shop. Same birthday table. The body returns to its own timing on its own. You do not need ongoing teaching to stay clear. You need only to keep the picture seen.

### WALK OUT

Live outward from this minute.

You came for dinner, not for a dose, and dinner sits waiting. Potatoes to lift for weight. Lemons to smell. Bread to squeeze for freshness. A soup poured hot, oil, salt, an apple cut slow. Sit hungry, notice, savour, stop at enough. Let the stomach report. Let talk run on. Let the window light move across the cloth. That is a meal that closes itself.

Go to the same shop at the same hour down the same aisle. The packs still glow. The bell still rings. Your hands stay quiet because different seeing gives the orders now. Queue at the usual till with your basket of dinner. Pay with a clear mouth. Walk out with bags that hold you till tomorrow. The shop has not changed. The buyer has.

Look once at trolleys piled high with family packs. We were that trolley. Let pity rise, not envy. Say nothing unless asked. If asked, speak plain and short and turn back to your basket. No lecture. No warning. A quiet buyer with steady hands teaches more than a sermon at the till.

Keep your seat through the whole programme in the evening. Tea drunk slow. Blanket clean. Side table clear. Notice the lamp click off without rustle. Notice sleep coming sooner. Notice the teeth brushed clean without film and the morning starting sooner because the night stayed plain. The sofa holds you without a seal.

Morning will show the same plain proof. Feet to floor, kettle on, oats in the pan, fruit cut while the pan ticks. Sit hungry and eat till enough, wash the bowl, step out with a clear mouth into cold air. Midday finds you hungry again at the right hour, not at the taught hour. Evening finds the table set, plates filled, talk unhurried. Day after day the same simple circuit — hunger, food, satisfaction — holds without sums and without commentary in the head.

A picture will walk past at the old hour. Foil and smell. Bright shelf. Old chair. That is old post being cleared. See it, smile, answer with the thought you will keep for good. Each unanswered evening the bell rings duller. Each plain meal the tongue wakes more. Paper comes. You smile. You answer. You live on.

Do not preach to friends and do not argue the method at tables. Preaching breeds defence and drags you back into debate that reopens your own doubt. If a friend presses a plate, smile and speak short. Then turn back to talk, programme, tea.

There is no staircase here. You are clear as of the vow night with hand on table. Keep no lighting and no count is needed. Guard the picture and the hand stays quiet. A crumb met with clear seeing revives nothing. A crumb met with mourning relights the picture.

You need no more chapters. Photograph the list below, close the book, and get on with enjoying your life.

**SUMMARY**

Question what you think you know about sweetness.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

4. NEVER BLAME YOURSELF AGAIN
Every failure was the method, never you.

5. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD
You are escaping, not losing.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

7. SEE THE GRUMBLE AS DYING, NOT DEMANDING
That feeling is the Nibbler starving.

9. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Other voices rebuild the Con.

10. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the wrong method talking.

11. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.
