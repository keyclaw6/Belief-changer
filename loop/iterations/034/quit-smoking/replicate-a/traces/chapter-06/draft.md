Chapter 6
THE TRAP THAT CALLED YOU WEAK

IN THIS CHAPTER — the bedside drawer of half-used patches and gum, the kitchen calendar thick with crossed dates, the bar shelf with whisky glasses and brandy glasses, the bragger on the office stairs

*Your past attempts never proved you were weak; they proved you were handed a method that needs struggle to live.*

## THE DRAWER THAT ACCUSES YOU

We have looked together at the classroom door and the paragraph desk and the grocery car park.

We saw the pellet after teaching add nothing to teaching. We saw the pellet after writing type nothing. We saw the taste after food dry the mouth it claimed to seal, and the holed filter teach harder puffing while calling it mild. That ground is settled between us. The work was yours. The flavour was food's. The mild was theatre.

What still accuses you is not pleasure. It is history.

Open the drawer we all kept. Half a box of patches curled at the edge. Gum in foil gone bitter. A plastic tube that was meant to replace the paper tube. Leaflets folded and refolded. Dates written in hope. Behind it, the calendar on the kitchen wall with crosses and circles, good Mondays that held till Thursday, bad Fridays, the morning the count started again at one.

And with the drawer comes the voice that has hurt you more than any cough.

"I lack willpower." "I need something to hold on to or I cannot do it." "I had probably attempted at least twenty times before and used most of the replacements and meds on the market with no success."

I know that sentence because I lived it and because I hear that drawer described again and again by smokers who think their case is the weak one. It is not weak. It is typical. Years of white-knuckle Mondays and props from the chemist, ending the same way, is the ordinary story of this trap, not proof you are uniquely broken.

Do you keep that drawer because you are feeble? Or because every attempt asked you to endure a loss while still believing the dose gave you something?

Look at what those attempts demanded. Hold out while still believing the back-step dose calms you. Hold out while still believing the after-food dose seals the meal. Hold out while still believing the mild dose is lighter. Hold out while mourning a friend. Then blame yourself when holding out hurts.

No wonder the calendar filled. No wonder the next try frightens you. You were never shown the con. You were only told to endure it harder.

I am warm to you and I am harsh to what did this to you. You were conned, not foolish. The world handed you phoney information before you could question it, the dose taught your body a small tug between takings, and then the method told you the tug was a mountain you must climb by character.

## WILFUL, NOT WEAK-WILLED

Let me turn the insult around, because the insult is the other way around.

It takes no weak will to keep smoking. Think what you overruled to keep doing it. The morning cough. The stairs that left you short. The money counted and counted again. The hiding from partner and kids. The winter step outside the restaurant while others stayed warm inside. The voice inside that said, I do not want this, answered day after day with another light.

Does that sound like a weak-willed person to you? A weak will does not persist for years against its own instincts, its own purse, its own breath, its own shame. A wilful one does. You are not weak-willed. You are wilful — wilful enough to keep paying for a dose that never paid you back, and to call your persistence failure.

Ask yourself plainly, and answer only to yourself.

If the trouble were your character, why did your character hold everywhere else — the job kept, the bills paid, the family carried — and collapse only where the dose was concerned? If the trouble were lack of grit, why did the hardest, grittiest weeks end exactly like the easy ones? If the prop were the answer, why did the drawer fill while the smoking continued?

There is only one honest answer, and it returns your name to you.

You did not fail to escape the 7 a.m. dose on the back step because you lacked backbone. You were held because the method holds everyone who uses it, strong and soft alike.

It was not you who failed the method. It was the method that failed you.

The Willpower Method manufactures struggle and then sells you the struggle as proof you need it. It tells you stopping is deprivation, orders you to suffer the deprivation bravely, and when you cannot bear a deprivation that was never necessary, it points at you. That pointing is the con inside the con. Willpower never worked because it was never meant to.

## WHISKY IN A BRANDY GLASS

Now look at the shelf where the props sit, because the props kept the same lie in a cleaner glass.

Picture a bar shelf at night. A man decides whisky has him chained, so he pours brandy into a different glass and calls it freedom from whisky. Same spirit. Same burn. Same bill in the morning. Only the glass changed, and the changing let him say he was doing something while staying exactly where he was.

That is what the swap did for us. Paper dose in the morning, gum dose at noon, patch dose on the arm all day, vapour dose in a shiny tube at night. Different papers around the same taking. Different glass, same drink.

I am not speaking of medicine for illness. Keep your clinician's care for any illness or prescription — this is about belief, not medicine. I am speaking of what the swap teaches the mind.

It teaches that you need a dose to meet a dose. It teaches that the tug between lights is a pit you must fill with another form of fill. It teaches you to watch the clock, count the milligrams, chew on time, suck on time, fear the hour the patch comes off. It keeps the brainwashing intact — that there is something here you cannot meet with empty hands — while moving the taking from pocket to drawer.

Watch what happens in life when the glass changes and the belief does not. The hand still moves at the old minute. The mind still says, this minute needs something. The body still waits for its squirt. You have removed nothing, because you still believe something was given. You have only taught yourself a new way to feed the same need and to call the feeding progress.

Would a genuine pleasure need to be tapered with another form of itself? Would a genuine friend need to be mourned through a substitute friend? You do not wean yourself off something that never gave. You see it gives nothing, and the wanting falls away with the seeing.

Someone near you will say, perhaps you are already saying it, "Isn't this book just brainwashing me the other way?" I welcome the question. If naming the installer and showing the taking is brainwashing, then every con exposed is brainwashing. The old voices put the lie in. I am taking it out in daylight where you can check each piece against your own day.

Smoke as normal while we finish this seeing. I ask for eyes, not effort.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

## TWO NUMBERS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT YOU

Two numbers will be thrown at you on the stairs and in print, and both are used to frighten you back into the old method.

The first says that in rooms where volunteers sign up and are watched, a licensed nicotine product can lift the odds against a dummy. The second says that out on the street, where most tries happen with no props and no watching, only a handful are still not smoking months later.

Hear them straight, because they are answers to two different questions about two different groups. The watched room is not the street. The street is not the watched room. The first tells you what happens to motivated trial quitters under trial rules. The second tells you what happens to ordinary attempts made while still believing the dose gives something and must be endured. Neither tells you what happens to a smoker who no longer believes the dose gives anything, and neither is a verdict on you.

That last clause matters. A number about groups can sound like a sentence on this reader, and it is not.

If numbers decided escape, no slave would ever have walked out of a trap that statistics called hopeless. Numbers describe what the old method produces in bulk. They do not decide what clear seeing produces in you. The drawer proved the old method produces struggle in bulk. It proved nothing about your worth.

So when the bragger on the stairs holds court — the man who suffered for months and calls his suffering proof, the woman who counts days like medals and warns you will fail without her path — hear the method talking, not truth talking. Their struggle was the method. Their counting is the method. Their warning is the method defending itself.

You do not need their struggle and you do not need their glass. You need your own eyes on your own day: the finish that pleased before the light, the food that tasted before the ash, the mask that taught harder puffing while promising mild. That seeing is yours. No tally can take it from you.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the method, not you.

SUMMARY
- The drawer of props and the crossed calendar did not prove weak character; they proved the method needs struggle to survive.
- A wilful will kept me smoking through cough and cost; the fault lay in the method, not in the will.
- A different glass does not change the drink; swapping the form keeps the belief that a dose is needed.
- Watched-room odds and street odds answer different questions about different groups; neither fixes my case.
- The bragger's hard path describes his method at work; I keep my own judgment and my own eyes.
