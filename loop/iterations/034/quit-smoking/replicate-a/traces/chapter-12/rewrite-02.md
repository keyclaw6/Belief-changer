Chapter 12
LIGHT IT, SEE IT, LEAVE IT

IN THIS CHAPTER — the plain kitchen table, the yellow filter and grey ash, the crushed butt in the tray, the hard-day desk and the bright-night patio

*Smoke this last one with your eyes wide open and you become a non-smoker in this minute, not in a week.*

## ARE YOU READY TO WALK OUT?

You have lived the diary that creeps. You have lived the date that keeps. You have lived tomorrow that bills today for the privilege of waiting.

We kept those smaller cages because we feared what stood past them. We told ourselves the dose steadied the hand. We told ourselves the tube softened the day and gave the pause its meaning and gave the night its edge. We told ourselves seven counted neat was mastery and Christmas kept was kindness and one more pack to finish the pack was only tidy.

I told myself the same while I counted and dated and signed midnight promises that daylight broke before the kettle boiled.

Did those smaller cages ever make the day larger? Did seven timed prizes feel lighter than twenty mindless ones, or did they feel heavier, hotter, longer, waited for with clock-watching that ate the morning? Did a kept date ever stay single for three years running with no creep, no extra, no midnight bargaining for just one more because the night is perfect? Did tomorrow ever arrive as a morning with no hand moving before thought? You know from your own pocket and your own morning mouth. The answer is no, and that no is total.

That is why you stand here with nothing left to bargain for. The excuses have been tried in your own skin and found empty. Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

A small voice may still whisper:

“What if a bad day comes and I need the old quiet?”

Hear it for what it is — the last echo of a story that sold you lack as help. On a bad day the problem sits on the desk after the ash falls. Papers sit. People wait. The edgy minute quiets for seconds because lack is fed, then returns on time asking for the next. It was the pause that rested you, the chair that held you, the work finished that satisfied you, the company that warmed you. The dose only rode beside them and sent the bill to you.

It never gave. It only took.

So ask yourself plainly, man to man: are you champing at the bit to walk out? You stand at the door with your hand on the handle and the proof already in your pocket. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. If the bit is not yet between your teeth, put these pages down and read again from the start until it is. Do not force a vow the eye has not yet made true. A vow forced before seeing is another midnight promise. A vow spoken after seeing is a door closed once.

Ask the questions that test readiness and answer from your diary, not from hope.

When you cut down, did the mind grow larger or smaller? When you kept a special date, did the year grow larger or smaller, with more life in it or more paperwork for life? When you waited for tomorrow, did tomorrow smoke less or did tomorrow smoke the same plus rent? If smaller, smaller, and same-plus-rent is your honest record, what is left to keep? A record like that needs no more testing. It needs closing.

I know the fear that sits beside readiness because it sat beside mine. It says: suppose I am the one it worked for. Suppose my nerves are different, my work is different, my nights are different. Look at your own mornings. Alarm, feet not yet on floor, hand finding pack before thought finds feet. Look at your own breaks. Work done well, then a pellet paid to paper as if paper did the work. Look at your own stairs, your own curtains, your own coat that needed spraying before you could hug your own kids. Was that a gift behaving like a gift? Did a friend ever make you hide in a garage to meet him?

We were conned, not weak. We smoked half-blind for years and called the blindfold normal. Now the blindfold is off. The table is plain. The dose is plain. The seeing is yours.

Do you want to escape? Of course you do — that is why you have read this far. Then let this be the hour you do it with open eyes and a glad heart, not with dread and not with a last fling to honour a liar.

## THE TABLE WHERE IT ENDS

No stage is needed. No packet bought for the occasion, no extra doses to make it grand, no last fling to honour a liar. Take the pack you hold now to a plain table, sit down in ordinary light, and smoke one last ordinary dose with your eyes fully open.

We always smoked half-blind. We lit on the move, in talk, in wind, in rain under a doorway, tasting little past the first flare while the mind ran elsewhere. This time taste everything on purpose and watch what you have been paying for.

Light it. Look at the yellow stain creeping on the filter as you pull. That stain lives on fingers, on teeth, on curtains, on the headlining of the car. Hold the paper between fingers and watch the ash grow grey and ragged, flaking on your sleeve and on the table. Draw and notice the heat bite the throat, the chest pull a fraction tighter to hold the hit, the nose fill with dry paper while food smell and night air sit untouched beside you. Two minutes in, the tongue films. Three minutes in, the mouth turns stale and the end stinks when you lift it away from the face.

Ask while it burns: what did this add to the table, the chair, the light? Did talk grow richer because the hand was busy tapping? Did the pause deepen because grey fell in it? Did the room grow kinder because the air grew thinner? You are walking out of the nicotine trap for good when you let the answers come from the senses, not from the story.

Starve the Nipper and its timed complaint dies unfed.

Keep watching to the short end, where we usually look away. The paper browns. The filter softens with wet. The taste turns truly foul, chemical and bitter, nothing like the first blink we remember from lighting. That foulness is not an accident of this one tube. It is the truth of every tube once the blink passes. We nursed the first three seconds and forgot the next three minutes. This time do not forget. Let the three minutes teach.

Your nose was made for clean air, not for paper smoke.

I sat where you sit. I chose a kitchen table with a chipped mug beside the tray, daylight through thin curtains, no music, no company, no ceremony to hide behind. I watched the stain spread and the ash lean and the end stink, and I asked what it had ever added to coffee, to talk, to work, to night. Nothing came back but the bill. The coffee was coffee. The talk was talk. The work was mine. The night was night. Paper had only ridden beside them and charged by the minute.

Do the same in your own light, with your own mug, your own tray. Notice the fingers, the nails, the faint yellow that washing never quite lifts. Notice the throat after, the faint tightening that we once read as calm. Notice the mouth after, stale where food had left it clean. This is not punishment. This is seeing. Seeing is kind because it ends paying for nothing.

## WATCH IT TO THE BITTER END

Do not stub it early to keep a sweet memory. Smoke it to the short end the way you never let yourself see.

We always timed the stub to protect the story. We crushed it halfway and kept the memory of the flare. We threw the butt before the stink and told ourselves the flare was the whole. This once, do the other way around. Let the flare go and keep the stink. Let the first three seconds shrink beside the next three minutes until proportion returns.

Watch the ember creep closer to the fingers. The draw grows hotter, harsher, more chemical with every pull. The tongue that filmed now coats. The lips that touched paper now taste wet filter. The fingers that held it now smell and will smell after washing. Lift the short end away and sniff it plain, without lungs to soften it. Is that the perfume you crossed streets to buy? Is that the friend you hid in garages to meet?

Ask your stomach what it thinks while the end burns short. Ask your chest what it thinks while it holds heat to keep the hit. The body answers without flattery. It coughs, it tightens, it films, it stinks. The body has always answered this way. We only learned to call its protest proof we needed more.

Look at the tray beside you. Old ash, old ends, grey smear, dead smell rising even before you add this one. Did you ever choose that smell for your coat, your car, your curtains, your kiss? Did you ever walk into a room and think, I wish my air smelled like that tray? You paid to carry that tray in your mouth, in your chest, in your pocket, every day, for years. Why? Because lack whispered and story translated.

When the end shortens and the taste turns truly foul, do not light another to wash it. Crush it out slowly, look at the crushed butt, the yellowed filter, the grey smear in the tray. That ugly remainder is the truth of every dose you ever called a friend. We nursed it because story told us to. You see it now without story. Leave the tray there a minute. Let the smell rise. That is not pleasure remembered. That is paper, chemical, and lack. Let the eyes stay open until the hand has no argument left.

Did this last tube steady anything? Did it mend anything, finish anything, warm anyone? Or did it borrow three seconds of quiet from your own lack and bill you in stain, ash, stink, money, time, and breath? There is only one honest reading. It took. It never lent.

## WHAT THE LAST DOSE REALLY DOES

The fact is the last dose never lifted you past normal. It quieted lack for minutes and billed you for hours.

Watch the clock on any day you tracked. Smoke enters, drug reaches the brain in seconds, shoulders drop for a blink. That blink is real and brief. Within minutes the acute quiet thins and the edgy vacancy returns, exactly on time, asking for another. No problem moved while the quiet blinked. The argument still sat on the desk. The page still sat unread. The music played whether paper burned or not. The people in the room went on talking while you stepped out to tap ash.

I know because I timed it with my own hand for years. I lit for calm and found the row unsolved after. I lit for focus and found the paragraph still unwritten. I lit for joy and found the joy in sun, leisure, company, none of which paper had lent. The soft quiet for a minute is lack fed, not help with the bill on the table.

What follows a final dose is not long torture. For most, the vacancy rises sharp in the first few days and thins within weeks; your hours may run a little earlier or later, not every body keeps the same clock. If you feel ill or worried, see your clinician. The tug is small, brief, already dying when unfed — a faint empty edginess, not proof you need paper to be yourself. It is proof lack is complaining because it is not fed. Leave it unfed and it thins.

Ask your own body what it does between doses every night. You sleep six, seven, eight hours with no puff and wake still you. You sit through films, meetings, flights, meals, with no puff and remain you. If paper were the self, those hours would be impossible. They are not impossible. They are ordinary. The daytime gnaw is the same night-time quiet interrupted by waking habit, nothing more.

And one after months does not test cure. One re-teaches hunger. Five months with no puff can be undone in three seconds because the wire sleeps but does not vanish in a season. Feed it and it wakes calling for the next on time. That is why the end you make tonight is total. Not one, not a drag, not a shared light on a patio when drink flows and a hand offers and the old line pops up. The line lies.

Did a single shared light ever stay single in your diary? Did the morning after ever feel prouder, clearer, calmer than the morning before? Did three seconds of flare ever pay for the week of counting that followed? You have the receipts in drawers of cellophane and in calendars with pencil counts. One keeps hunger alive. None lets it die.

You never bought movement by paying all day to stand still. You only bought the right to pay again. Stop paying and the demand to pay dies with the habit that made it.

## THE VOW THAT MAKES YOU A NON-SMOKER

You have looked at the stain and the ash with open eyes. You have seen the clock and the brief blink. Now the seeing must be sealed with a spoken vow, made once and held by seeing.

We sealed every trap promise at midnight and broke it by morning because belief still held. We promised to bear a loss and woke to mourn it. This vow is different because belief has turned. You do not promise to bear a loss. You declare a fact already proved: paper never gave, only took.

Say it aloud at the table, with the crushed butt before you:

“I will never light another dose. Not one, not a drag, not in joy, not in sorrow, not on any date the calendar can invent.”

Say it like a man closing a door he has already walked through, not like a boy hoping the door holds. Mean it because you see it. The seeing holds the door, not the straining.

From this stub on, you are a non-smoker. Not tomorrow, not after a test of days. Now. You leave it easily, immediately and permanently.

Congratulations. I mean that as a man who sat where you sit and crushed his own last ugly butt and felt the weight drop in the same minute. I watched hundreds do the same and walk taller out of the room with empty hands and a clear tray behind them.

From now, every time the old thought pops — “a dose with coffee,” “a hit on the patio, one won't hurt,” “just to get through this hour” — do not argue with it and do not push it into shadow. Answer it with the truth you own and let the thought turn to joy in your mouth:

YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Let that be your instant answer, your whole script, whenever paper crosses your mind. Say it under your breath on the stairs, say it laughing with the tray before you, say it when the night air smells of other lights. The thought will come fewer times and with less voice each week because unfed wires go quiet, and each coming will only remind you how clear the table looks without ash on it.

Do not count days to earn the name. The name is earned by seeing, and seeing is now. A man does not become a non-smoker by serving time. He becomes one by no longer believing the lie, and you no longer believe it. The crushed butt proves the senses have voted. The vow proves the mind has signed. The rest is ordinary days with the signing kept.

If doubt knocks in the first hours, welcome it as proof the old story is losing. Say: “I know this feels strange, and strange is not need.” Then speak your script and get on with the next thing — kettle, keys, coat, street. The strangeness thins because it was habit, not help. Habit thins when left alone. Help would be missed. Nothing here will be missed.

## TWO DOORS THAT STAY SHUT

Two thoughts will knock. Both wear your voice. Both lie.

The first knocks on a hard day. Phone down after a row, bills spread, head loud. The hand twitches toward the old pocket and the whisper says:

“Just this once. It always took care of stress.”

We believed that whisper for years because the edgy minute went quiet for seconds after a pull. Look again with your table eyes. The row sits unsolved after ash falls. The bills sit uncounted. The head resumes its noise on time, now with a second noise — where is the next? A dose on a hard day does not lift the day. It adds ash to it and teaches the hand to beg on every hard day after.

Ask: did paper ever file a paper, mend a quarrel, pay a bill? Did the calm ever arrive before the flare or only in the seconds after lack was fed? Did the shoulders drop because the problem lifted or because the timed complaint paused? You know because you lived the desk a thousand times. The whisper is hunger talking in a suit. Take the pause without paper — stand, window, water, walk to the next room — and watch the shoulders drop the same without the bill. The pause did the work. Paper only charged.

The second knocks on a bright night. Heaters glow, glasses clink, a friend holds out a lit light with a grin and the old line pops: one won't hurt.

That light never stayed single in any diary you kept. One flare, heaven for three seconds, ash on the tongue, then the wire awake and calling by morning. I took that bright-night test more times than I can count and bought the same pack by Friday each time. You have your own Fridays to consult. Five months clear can be traded for three seconds of flare and a week of noise. That is a poor trade and you know it without my telling.

Ask: did the bright night need paper to be bright? Was it the sun going down slow, the leisure earned, the company laughing, the drink cold in the hand, that gave, while paper only rode beside them? You watched that ride to the end at your table. The stain, the grey, the stale end — was that brightness? Hold your seeing and you hold your leaving. Let the friend smoke his own rental if he must. Pity the rental, do not envy it. You do not want it. You had it for years and paid daily.

When either knock comes, answer with your script and turn away. Do not debate, do not count days, do not measure yourself by longing. A knock is not a need. It is a dying echo asking to be fed. Leave it unfed and it thins. Each thinning makes the next knock quieter until the street with heaters and the desk with bills are only street and desk again, with no paper beside them.

If you ever take a puff after you stop, do not call yourself lost and do not take another to punish yourself. Take it as a rumble strip, a loud warning to hold your seeing tighter, and leave the pack where it is. A rumble strip does not drive the car into the ditch. It shakes the wheel and says look up. Look up. Hold the picture of the tray — plenty taken, nothing given in plain day — and do not let one blink rewrite months of clear mornings, full taste, steady breath, money kept, time kept. One warns. Two rebuilds. Leave it at warning.

You have closed the diary, the date, the tomorrow. You have smoked the last one with open eyes and spoken the vow that seals it. Walk the ordinary street with empty hands. The table behind you holds the proof. The days ahead hold the living.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

**SUMMARY**
- The last dose adds nothing; seen straight with stain and ash, it ends the paper identity on the spot.
- The edgy vacancy after is lack fading on a short clock, not proof of need and not the same in every body.
- One dose re-teaches hunger, so the end holds total with no date kept and no diary restarted.
- The vow spoken once at the table seals what the eye already knows, and the script answers every later knock.
