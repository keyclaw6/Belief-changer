Chapter 12
LIGHT IT, SEE IT, LEAVE IT

IN THIS CHAPTER — the small table by the ashtray, yellow fingers and grey ash, the readiness question

*The last one gives nothing, and stubbing it with open eyes makes you a non-smoker on the spot.*

## ARE YOU READY TO WALK OUT?

You have seen the diary creep. You have seen the date keep you. You have seen tomorrow bill you for today. No cut, no special, no later date holds water now.

We kept those doors because we feared life past them would be flat. We told ourselves the tube steadied the hand and softened the day. We told ourselves a smaller cage was kindness.

I told myself the same while I counted and dated and promised midnight vows that daylight broke.

Did those smaller cages ever make the day larger? Did seven timed prizes feel lighter than twenty mindless ones? Did a kept date ever stay single for three years with no creep and no bargaining? You know from your own pocket and your own morning mouth. The answer is no, and that no is total.

That is why you stand here with nothing to bargain for and nothing to fear losing. Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

A small voice may still whisper:

"What if a bad day comes and I need the old quiet?"

Hear it for what it is — the last echo of a story that sold you lack as help. The problem on a bad day sits on the desk after the ash falls. The edgy minute quiets for seconds because lack is fed, then returns on time asking for more. It was the pause that rested you, the room that held you, the work that satisfied you. The dose only rode beside them and billed you.

It never gave. It only took.

So ask yourself plainly: are you champing at the bit to walk out? You stand at the door of escape with the key already cut. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. If the bit is not yet between your teeth, put these pages down and read again from the start until it is. Do not force a vow the eye has not yet made true.

## THE TABLE WHERE IT ENDS

No stage is needed. No packet bought for the occasion, no extra doses to make it grand. Take the pack you hold now to a plain table, sit down in ordinary light, and smoke one last ordinary dose with your eyes fully open.

We always smoked half-blind. We lit on the move, in talk, in wind, in rain under a doorway, tasting little past the first flare. This time taste everything on purpose and watch what you have been paying for.

Light it. Look at the yellow stain creeping on the filter as you pull. That stain lives on fingers, on teeth, on curtains. Hold the paper and see the ash grow grey and ragged, flaking on your sleeve. Draw and notice the heat bite the throat, the chest pull a fraction tighter to hold the hit, the nose fill with dry paper while food smell and night air sit untouched beside you. Two minutes in, the tongue films. Three minutes in, the mouth turns stale and the end stinks when you lift it away.

Ask while it burns: what did this add to the table, the chair, the light? Did talk grow richer because the hand was busy? Did the pause deepen because ash fell in it? You are walking out of the nicotine trap for good when you let it go.

Starve the Nipper and the timed tug fades to nothing.

Your nose was made for clean air, not for paper smoke.

When the end shortens and the taste turns truly foul, do not light another to wash it. Crush it out slowly, look at the crushed butt, the yellowed filter, the grey smear in the tray. That ugly remainder is the truth of every dose you ever called a friend. We nursed it because story told us to. You see it now without story.

Leave the tray there a minute. Let the smell rise. That is not pleasure remembered. That is paper, chemical, and lack.

## WHAT THE LAST DOSE REALLY DOES

The fact is the last dose never lifted you past normal. It quieted lack for minutes and billed you for hours.

Watch the clock on any day you tracked. Smoke enters, drug reaches the brain in seconds, shoulders drop for a blink. That blink is real and brief. Within minutes the acute quiet thins and the edgy vacancy returns, exactly on time, asking for another coin. No problem moved while the coin dropped. Papers sat. People waited. The gnaw only paused its complaint.

I know because I timed it with my own hand for years. I lit for calm and found the argument still on the desk. I lit for focus and found the page still unread. I lit for joy and found the music playing whether paper burned or not.

The soft quiet for a minute is relief from lack, not help with the bill on the table.

What follows a final dose is not torture for all. For most, the vacancy peaks in the first few days and thins within weeks; your hours may run a little earlier or later, not every body keeps the same clock. If you feel ill or worried, see your clinician. The tug is small, brief, already dying when unfed — not proof you need paper to be yourself.

And one after months does not test cure. One re-teaches hunger. Five months with no puff can be undone in three seconds because the wire sleeps but does not vanish in a season. Feed it and it wakes calling for the next on time. That is why the end you make tonight is total. Not one, not a drag, not a shared light on a patio when drink flows and a hand offers and the old line pops up. The line lies.

Did a single shared light ever stay single in your diary? Did the morning after ever feel prouder, clearer, calmer than the morning before? You have the receipts. One keeps hunger alive.

## THE VOW THAT MAKES YOU A NON-SMOKER

You have looked at the stain and the ash with open eyes. You have seen the clock and the coin. Now the seeing must be sealed with a spoken vow, made once and held by seeing.

We sealed every trap promise at midnight and broke it by morning because belief still held. This vow is different because belief has turned. You do not promise to bear a loss. You declare a fact already proved: paper never gave, only took.

Say it aloud at the table, with the crushed butt before you:

"I will never light another dose. Not one, not a drag, not in joy, not in sorrow, not on any date the calendar can invent."

Say it like a man closing a door he has already walked through, not like a boy hoping the door holds. Mean it because you see it.

From this stub on, you are a non-smoker. Not tomorrow, not after a test of days. Now. You leave it easily, immediately and permanently.

Congratulations. I mean that as a man who sat where you sit and crushed his own last ugly butt and felt the weight drop in the same minute. I watched hundreds do the same and walk taller out of the room.

From now, every time the old thought pops — "a dose with coffee," "a hit on the patio, one won't hurt," "just to get through this hour" — do not argue with it and do not push it into shadow. Answer it with the truth you own and let the thought turn to joy in your mouth:

YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Let that be your instant answer, your whole script, whenever paper crosses your mind. Say it under your breath on the stairs, say it laughing with the tray before you, say it when the night air smells of other lights. The thought will come fewer times and with less voice each week because unfed wires go quiet, and each coming will only remind you how clear the table looks without ash on it.

## TWO DOORS THAT STAY SHUT

Two thoughts will knock. Both wear your voice. Both lie.

The first knocks on a hard day. Phone down after a row, bills spread, head loud. The hand twitches toward the old pocket and the whisper says:

"Just this once. It always took care of stress."

We believed that whisper for years because the edgy minute went quiet for seconds after a pull. Look again with your table eyes. The row sits unsolved after ash falls. The bills sit uncounted. The head resumes its noise on time, now with a second noise — where is the next? A dose on a hard day does not lift the day. It adds ash to it and teaches the hand to beg on every hard day after.

Ask: did paper ever file a paper, mend a quarrel, pay a bill? Did the calm ever arrive before the flare or only in the seconds after lack was fed? You know because you lived the desk a thousand times. The whisper is hunger talking in a suit.

The second knocks on a bright night. Heaters glow, glasses clink, a friend holds out a lit light with a grin and the old line pops: one won't hurt.

That light never stayed single in any diary you kept. One flare, heaven for three seconds, ash on the tongue, then the wire awake and calling by morning. I took that bright-night test more times than I can count and bought the same pack by Friday each time. You have your own Fridays to consult.

Ask: did the bright night need paper to be bright? Was it the sun, the leisure, the company that gave, while paper only rode beside them? You watched that ride to the end. Hold your seeing and you hold your freedom.

When either knock comes, answer with your script and turn away. Do not debate, do not count days, do not measure yourself by longing. A knock is not a need. It is a dying echo asking to be fed. Leave it unfed and it thins.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

**SUMMARY**
- The last dose adds nothing; seen straight, it ends the paper identity.
- The edgy vacancy after is lack fading, not proof of need.
- One dose re-teaches hunger, so the end holds total with no date kept.
- The vow spoken once at the table seals what the eye already knows.
