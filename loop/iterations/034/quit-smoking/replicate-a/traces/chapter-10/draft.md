Chapter 10
THE LAUGH, THE DRINK AND THE LIT END

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The late patio with heaters and string lights, the tray of cold drinks, the extended hand with a lit end, the cold step outside the laugh, my own winter night that fooled me for years

*The laugh and the drink and the night did the giving — paper only rode along and sent the bill.*

## THE ONE YOU STILL KEEP

You have watched the kettle boil without crediting ash. You have watched taste linger after the plate. You have watched the kerb restore without lighting. And still you keep one in the pocket for later.

Not for mornings. Not for work. For them. For the night.

I hear it because I said it for years in your exact words:

"I get the rest. But nights out are different. You often get offered a cigarette and in that kind of environment it's hard to turn down. You don't want to be the only one not smoking."

And underneath, quieter, with drink warm in the chest:

"Because you've been drinking, you don't think about the risks, you think one won't hurt."

I am not asking you to give that night up. I am asking you to look at it straight while you still smoke as normal. Keep the friends. Keep the drink if you drink. Keep the late laugh. I want the favourite to prove itself with everything going for it, not with excuses removed.

We all kept this one last because it looked like proof. Work smokes looked like habit. Morning smokes looked like need. But the patio smoke looked like pleasure. Lights low, glasses cold, talk loose, someone laughing with head back, a hand reaching across with a lit end and a grin. Who would refuse belonging served warm?

Ask what your own nights prove, not what the grin promises.

Did you ever light that patio dose sober and alone on a wet Tuesday and find heaven in it? Or did heaven only arrive when drink and company arrived with it? Did the stick taste sweet, or did the night taste sweet while the stick burned beside it?

You know the answer because you lived it. The night made the dose look kind. The dose never made the night kind.

## THE PATIO AT ELEVEN

Come with me and stand it again with eyes open.

Eleven o'clock. Heaters ticking. String lights swaying a little in warm wind. Table crowded with glasses sweating, lime sunk, beer cold on the tongue, music low enough to talk over. Friends shoulder to shoulder, faces lit amber, someone telling the story wrong on purpose to get laughed at again. You laugh with both hands free around a warm glass.

Then the scrape of chairs. Two stand. Three follow. A voice calls, coming? A hand shakes the pack, offers it open, paper tips out white and clean. The lighter clicks. First pull hisses.

"You coming out for one? Go on. One won't hurt."

We went because we feared standing apart more than we feared cold. We told ourselves we chose. Choice does not pat pockets and count and follow chairs to stand in a doorway. We followed because lack had learned the hour and drink had loosened the doubt, and belonging seemed to sit out there past the doors.

Stand out there and watch what actually happens while you still smoke as normal.

Notice the first three seconds. The light flares. The throat takes the hit. For a blink the shoulders drop and the head says heaven. I know that blink. The moment of lighting can feel like arrival.

Then watch the next three minutes, not the first three seconds.

The talk you came for thins because mouths are busy pulling. The drink you came for sits inside getting warm while you stand outside getting cold. The night air is on the skin whether paper burns or not, cool at the neck, soft after the heated room, carrying food smell and rain somewhere far off. You do not taste it because paper fills the mouth with dry heat. Ash films the tongue that had beer clean on it a minute before. Chest tightens a fraction to hold the dose. Fingers smell. The laugh you followed goes on, but you hear half of it through the business of keeping the end lit.

Ask and answer plain, with the dose still in the fingers.

Did the lights need ash to glow? Did the story need paper to be funny? Did the cold drink need smoke to taste cold? There is only one honest reading. The lights glowed. The story carried. The drink bit cold. Paper added heat and haste and stood between you and all three.

Go back inside after and check the mouth. Beer should still sit clean. It sits stale under ash. Talk should still sit warm. It sits broken by the standing and the missing. You came out to join and spent the joining feeding a timed lack that returned within the hour to bill again.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

## MY OWN COLD STEP

I want to tell you my night because I lived your exception for ten years.

Mine was a basement bar off a side street, winter, brick sweating inside, patio nothing but a yard with two heaters that never quite reached. I was thirty-something, teaching all day, tired in the bones, telling myself I smoked for the little parties between work. Nights out were my proof I was not chained. I could go days counting less, I said, but Friday I chose.

One Friday five of us squeezed round a table scarred with rings. My mate Tom kept time with his glass, tapping when the story peaked. A girl I liked laughed with her whole face and spilled a little wine and did not care. Someone put coins in the jukebox and chose wrong on purpose. I felt rich in company in a way work never gave.

Then chairs scraped. Tom stood, shook his pack at me without even looking, the way you offer crisps. Three others stood. The girl I liked stood. I stood. We filed past the toilets into the yard where breath showed.

I remember the lighter failing twice in wind. I remember cupping it like something precious. I remember that first pull after two pints — shoulders dropping, head saying heaven, Tom grinning, me grinning back with paper between teeth as if we shared a secret instead of a doorway.

Heaven lasted the length of lighting. That is the truth I hid for years.

By halfway the secret thinned. Tom talked with his hands and ash fell on his shoe. The girl shivered and hugged herself and I could not lend warmth because both hands were busy. My pint inside was going flat. The story we had left unfinished went on without its punchline. My tongue, that had tasted cold bitter clean, now tasted like a tray emptied. My chest, that had laughed easy at the table, now pulled shallow against the yard cold.

I finished because money burned if I did not. I always finished because money burned if I did not. I stubbed it underfoot and breathed out grey and smelled my own coat and felt shitty in the mouth and low in the chest, and walked back in telling myself I loved smoking while hating being the man who had left the table to stand in cold.

Inside, the table was warm without me. The jukebox had moved on. Tom was already lighting another man's joke, not mine. The girl had turned her chair a fraction toward someone who had stayed. Nothing cruel. Nothing said. Only the small physics of absence: I had paid to miss the minutes I claimed paper seasoned.

I did that fifty Fridays. Same yard. Same flare of heaven at lighting. Same film after. Same flat pint. Same missing the punchline. I called those nights my reward. They were my rent.

One Friday I stayed inside while they filed out. Not from strength. From curiosity, because this book's question had got into me: what gives here, the night or the paper? I sat with my pint cold in the hand, listened to the punchline land, laughed till eyes watered, felt the night air when the door swung as they came back stamping and smelling, and knew in the body what argument could not lend. I had missed nothing by staying. I had missed everything by going. I felt a small relief that I could sit and sip and hear it whole.

That was not will. That was seeing. Paper had been sneaking a ride on Tom and wine and lights and calling the ride proof.

## WHY DRINK MAKES IT LOOK TRUE

You will say drink changes it. Of course drink changes how it looks. That is the whole trick of this exception, and I want you to see it without fearing drink or doubting yourself.

Drink loosens. It loosens shoulders and talk and doubt together. In the day you count and argue and remember stairs and money. At night with two in you, counting softens, arguing softens, and the hand that offers looks like friendship itself. The rule you held at noon — I will not — collapses by midnight into maybe just this once.

That collapse does not prove the dose gives at night what it denies at day. It proves doubt was gagged while lack stayed awake. The timed gnaw still woke on time. The drink only turned down the voice that questions it.

Watch it in your own hand while you still smoke as normal.

Smoke one sober at the kitchen table with nothing on. Time how long the quiet lasts before the edgy minute returns. Then smoke one with drink on the patio with friends. Time that quiet. It returns on time as always within the hour, whether talk laughed or not, whether glass emptied or not. Drink did not lengthen the gift. Drink shortened the memory of the price.

Did drink ever make ash taste sweet? Did drink ever make morning mouth clean? Did drink ever make stairs cost less the next day? You know from your own mornings after that it made all three worse, while dressing the lighting minute as heaven.

I am not telling you to avoid drink or to avoid friends to stay quit. That would be the old method talking, white-knuckling past triggers as if paper still held power. I am telling you to go out and drink as you would and stand where you would and watch with clear eyes. The night does not need avoiding. The night is yours. Paper is the intruder that taught you to pay to step outside it.

See drink for what it is here: a dimmer switch on doubt, not a maker of pleasure. With doubt dimmed, the old credit error shines brighter — shoulders drop for seconds because lack quiets for seconds, and the mind, loosened, calls seconds a night. Sober or drunk, the mechanism is identical: brief quiet of a need paper created, charged to friendship.

## THE TASTE TEST WITH NOTHING ADDED

Here is the test I want you to run, not tomorrow, not by effort, but tonight in the head while the dose is still in the fingers.

Take the best patio minute you ever had. Hold every element still. Same friends. Same lights. Same cold glass. Same warm wind. Same looseness after two drinks. Now lift paper out of the picture and nothing else. Leave talk, taste, warmth, laugh exactly where they were.

What is lost?

The flare at lighting that felt like heaven for three seconds and filmed the tongue after. The standing in cold while the table went on inside. The half-heard story. The warm pint. The smell in hair on the taxi home. The morning mouth. That is the full inventory of loss. Nothing of night is lost, because night never came from paper.

Now put paper back in and lift friendship out. Same dose, same drink, alone in rain with no laugh, no lights, no hand offering. What is left? A tube burning down fast, ash on shoe, chest pulling, gnaw quieting minutes then returning on time. Would you cross the street for that? Would you pay and shiver for that? Of course you would not — that is why you never do, except when friendship is there to be robbed.

The night gave. Paper billed.

That is the inversion this exception hides. We thought paper seasoned friendship the way salt seasons food. Salt stays on food and food tastes better after. Paper does not stay on friendship. Paper removes you from friendship for five minutes, films the mouth that should taste, shortens the breath that should laugh, and charges the hour for the removal. It never fixed the flat minute. It made the flat minute by stepping you outside, then sold you re-entry as pleasure.

Look at the smoker beside you on that step with pity, never envy, because you know what his shoulders know. He is not indulging. He is pausing his own gnaw at the price of missing the laugh. He stands with drink going warm inside, talk going on inside, warmth going on inside, while he feeds a dose that will call again before the taxi. You step back inside stale, breathless and chained, while the laugh goes on without you — that is not belonging. That is exile with a lighter.

Keep the patio. Keep the friends. Keep the drink if you drink. Let paper prove, with everything going for it, that it adds one drop no other element supplies. It cannot. Sun and leisure and company did the giving on every good night you ever had. Paper only ever sneaked a ride and left ash on the seat.

10. MEET THE BEST CIGARETTE HEAD-ON
Let the favourite prove it gives nothing.

**SUMMARY**
- The patio minute looked like pleasure because lights, drink and company were doing the giving while paper burned beside them.
- Drink plus an offered light collapses the daytime rule into one won't hurt by dimming doubt, not by adding any gift.
- The heaven at lighting lasts seconds and films the tongue after, then the gnaw returns on time as always.
- Lift paper out of the best night and nothing of night is lost; lift friendship out and nothing worth crossing the street for remains.
