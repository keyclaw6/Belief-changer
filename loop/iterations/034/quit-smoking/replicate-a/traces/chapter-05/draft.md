Chapter 5
THE LITTLE PARTIES AND THE HOLED MASK

IN THIS CHAPTER
The classroom door still warm from teaching, the finished paragraph on the screen, the grocery car park with the engine off, the two-minute pause by the window, the white filter with its ring of tiny vents between lips and fingers.

*The party was never inside the paper tube — the work pleased you, the pause rested you, and the mild only made you puff harder.*

## THE PELLETS AFTER EVERYTHING

We paid ourselves in smoke.

Finished the class, stepped to the corridor, lit. Finished a paragraph, pushed the chair back, lit. Drove the long road, drove the short road to the shops, switched the engine off, lit. "Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store."

Do you hear your own list in that list? I do. Mine was coffee done, page done, call done, lawn done, argument survived, dinner eaten, keys in the door. A pellet after every little trick, as regular as a bell in a laboratory. We did not call it that. We called it little parties. A ciggie to finish the job properly. The stick to mark the moment. A habit smoke that felt earned.

I am not going to tell you the pleasure of finishing was not real. It was real. I want you to press where it lived.

Take one ordinary pellet you remember well. Not an idea, a picture. The classroom door. Forty faces gone. Boards wiped. Your throat dry from talking. The hour held together by you. You step out with the packet already in the hand. Strike. Draw. Stand in the cold and watch the street.

What pleased you in that ten minutes? The teaching, or the tube?

Isolate it cleanly, as an investigator and not as a judge. Take away the lighting and leave everything else exactly as it was — the class taught, the hour carried, the room that listened, the weight off your shoulders — and what would be missing? Would the class be less taught? Would the relief of being done be less relief? Would the street be less bright?

Now take away the teaching and leave the lighting exactly as it was — stand in the same cold and draw the same dose with no work behind it, no paragraph, no drive, nothing finished — and would it be a party? You know that minute. The dose between doses. The one with nothing behind it. It tastes thin and stale and leaves you watching the clock.

Both pictures point one way. With work and without smoke, the pleasure stands. With smoke and without work, it falls flat.

We were conned into giving the work's wages to the tube. The fact is, the finished thing pleases. The taught class pleases. The written paragraph pleases. The stocked kitchen pleases. The body likes done. That liking is yours. It was there before the first cough behind the sheds, and it never left you except in the story that pasted a light over every ending.

Ask yourself, and answer only to yourself:

Did the paragraph need the pellet to be finished? Did the drive need the pellet to be driven? Did you type worse on the days you could not step out, or did you type and then miss the bell?

There is only one honest answer, and it returns your own work to you. You taught. You wrote. You drove. The dose sat in the packet while you did it, contributed no sentence, no patience, no mile, and then stepped forward at the end to be thanked.

It is the other way around. The pellet never paid for the work. The work paid for the pellet, and the pellet took the applause.

I know this is hard to accept. Your mind answers: but mine is different. I enjoy the break after. I think better with it. It rounds the moment off.

I do not doubt you felt something round. I doubt only what rounded it. What rounded it was the quieting for a minute of the edginess left by the last dose, laid exactly over the minute when relief from finishing was already rising. Two liftings at once, one from life and one from the tube going quiet, misnamed as one gift from paper.

Watch a non-smoker finish the same work. She teaches the same hour, closes the same door, stands at the same window with empty hands. She stretches, breathes, looks out, smiles at nothing in particular. No equipment. No clock. Her minute is shorter and fresher, because nothing is fed inside it. She does not feel the ending is missing a tube, because her endings never contained one.

Your endings never needed one either. The tube inserted itself into your endings, taxed them, then claimed them.

So look again at the chain you wore so proudly — class, paragraph, errand, light, class, paragraph, errand, light — and see who did the work and who took the party. The work was yours from first to last.

## WHAT THE PAUSE REALLY RESTED

There is a smaller theft inside the pellet that keeps the pellet alive.

The pause.

Phone down. Kettle on. Two minutes with nothing to do. Hands empty. Eyes tired. The non-smoker at the next desk stands, looks out of the window, lets his shoulders drop, breathes, returns. Two minutes. Nothing taken. Nothing burned. The mind settles because pausing settles it.

We spent the two minutes feeding a need. Check the packet. Find the lighter. Step out or lean out. Watch the ash. Half the mind in the cold, half on the next thing waiting inside. Return with a drier mouth and a tighter chest and call that a break.

Did the pause freshen you, or did the lighting end for a minute the edginess the last dose had left, and call that ending freshening?

Press rest for detail. A true rest leaves you clearer than before. This rest leaves you a little staler than before, a little tighter, a little more in need of the next rest. That is not rest. That is service dressed as rest.

And because the false pause was the only pause we allowed ourselves, we came to fear a day without smoke as a day without pauses. What would I do with my hands. Where would I go when the phone goes down. How would I stop the circling thoughts. As if pausing were made of paper. As if breathing at a window needed a license. As if two minutes with empty hands were unbearable without heat.

Test it only by watching others, not by testing yourself in the cold. Watch a non-smoker pause. He does not perform a ritual. He does not need equipment. He stands, he breathes, he looks, he returns. His pause is shorter and fresher because nothing is fed inside it.

Your pause was his pause before the trap taught you otherwise. Return the pause to its owner. It was yours before the first dose, and it never stopped being yours except in the story.

This matters for the pellets because most of what we called reward at work was not celebration but pile — small efforts stacked with no gap between them to breathe. The smoker's gaps are not gaps but feedings, so the pile never gets a true gap and the chest never gets true rest. Each false pause leaves the chest tighter for the next effort, and the tighter chest is read as more need for reward. Round and round. Not because work grew harder, but because the pause was stolen while the work was blamed.

See the theft and the chain changes shape. The work is still there — teaching, typing, driving — but it is now one pleasure, not two, and it can be closed in true pauses that leave you clearer, not staler. You have paused well all your life whenever smoke was impossible. You can pause well now whenever smoke is irrelevant, which is always, once the credit is returned.

## DID YOU EVER LIKE THE TASTE

Say it as we said it.

"I love the taste after food." "Nothing finishes a meal like a ciggie." "I like a mild one with coffee."

Do you hear the tenderness in those lines? I spoke them. I defended my brand the way a man defends his team. Strong. Mild. Menthol. The one with the gold band. We all had a favourite, and we all told ourselves the favourite proved enjoyment.

I want you to look at what liking consisted of, in the mouth and not in the slogan.

Remember the first one. The cough. The burn. The eyes watering. The throat closing. The head turning light in a sick way. Did you like the taste then? No child likes it. No first body likes it. We learned it. We practised through the cough until the cough stopped warning us, and called the stopping pleasure.

Remember the morning one. Feet not yet on the floor, hand already finding the packet, first draw on empty lungs. Stale mouth from the night. Stale end on the tongue. Ash on the lip. Did that taste good, or did it end the night's edginess and borrow the word good?

Remember the meal one we praise most. The mouth full of food's taste — rich, warm, complete — then paper and heat laid over it, drying it, souring it, replacing it with ash. Which taste did the dose improve? Isolate the variable. Let the meal stand with no lighting after it, taste lingering, tongue clean, breath full. Then light ten minutes later on a clean mouth with nothing to cover. Which minute honours food? We told ourselves smoke seals a meal. Seals it from what? From its own taste?

Watch what we do when we are not defending. We mask the taste all day. Coffee with it. Drink with it. Mint after it. Spray in the car. Windows down in January. Febreeze the hell out of myself before going back inside. Does a genuine pleasure need to be washed off the clothes, scraped off the tongue, blown out of the house before the partner and the kids come in? Have you ever seen a non-smoker finish something delicious and rush to stain his fingers yellow to keep the taste?

Ask yourself, and answer only to yourself:

If the taste is the reward, why do you taste less with every year and need more to chase it? If the taste seals the meal, why does the sealed meal leave you staler than the unsealed one?

There is only one honest answer. You never liked smoke the way you like food. You liked the minute the second hunger went quiet, laid over food's pleasure and misnamed as flavour. The food pleased. The company pleased. The sitting down pleased. The tube only dried the mouth and called the drying rounding off.

Think of the ashtray the morning after a party at someone else's house. Cold ends. Grey ash. Stale smell that turns the stomach. That is the taste without the hunger to hide it. That is what the mouth meets when belief is not there to dress it. We lived with that smell on our fingers and called it pleasure because the alternative — that we paid good work and good food to be made staler — was too insulting to face while we still believed.

Face it now, gently, without insult to yourself. You were conned, not foolish. The world handed you the words taste and treat before you had reason to doubt them, then the tube taught your body a small tug between doses, and you fitted the old words to the new tug and called the fit proof.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Once that is seen, the brand loyalty falls apart. Strong or mild, plain or menthol, gold band or white — different papers around the same squirt, different words around the same gap. No favourite ever gave. Each only took in its own costume.

## THE HOLE IN THE MASK

Now the milds. The lights. The low-tar promise that let so many of us keep the pellets with a cleaner conscience.

I smoked them. Many of us switched when the cough grew heavy or the chest grew tight, telling ourselves at least this one is lighter, at least this one takes less, at least I have cut something while keeping my little parties. It felt sensible. It felt like care.

Look at the white filter with clear eyes.

Around the paper, in a neat ring, a row of tiny vent holes. On the machine that tests them, those holes stay open to clean air. The machine draws, air slips in through the vents, the reading comes out low. Mild on paper. Light by number.

Now put a human mouth where the machine mouth was.

Lips close over the very ring they are meant to leave open. Fingers press where the holder never pressed. The draw comes harder and longer without thinking, because the body is not reading numbers. It is asking for its usual squirt. The smoke runs hotter, deeper, longer to pull it through the narrowed path. What the vents dilute on the bench, the lips and fingers close in life, and the lungs make up the rest without asking your permission.

I am not saying every smoker covers them in exactly the same way on every dose — we are human, not machines, and hands move differently. I am saying the design invites the covering, and the covering defeats the number. This is about how the tube is built, not a sentence on you. The fact is, a ventilated dose as smoked does not mean a smaller dose as taken. The lower machine reading does not mean you took less.

Picture it as it is, not as the packet paints it. A mask with holes punched for fresh air, handed to you in smoke, and your own lips sealing the holes to get the hit through. You puff harder through a holed mask and call the harder puff milder. The harder puff is the proof. If the mild truly gave less, why would the body work more to take it? If the light truly satisfied lighter, why would the day still count twenty?

Ask yourself, and answer only to yourself:

When you switched to lights, did the wanting fall? Did the day need fewer? Did the dawn call quieter?

You know the answer because you lived it. The count stayed. The dawn stayed. The throat learned a deeper draw and the fingers learned a new grip, and the packet took credit for care while the body did extra work to keep the level where the trap needed it.

That is the mild fraud in one picture. Not a kinder tube. A holed tube that teaches harder puffing and calls the teaching lightness.

And see what the fraud protected. It protected the pellets. It let the classroom door, the paragraph, the grocery run keep their bell. It whispered, at least this party is safer, at least this pause is cleaner, so the question of what the party ever gave never had to be asked. Once the whisper is heard for what it is — paper theatre around the same squirt — the bell has nowhere to hide. The party was never mild or strong. It was never in the tube at all.

Do not mistake this for advice to smoke the strong ones. Strong papers are not a remedy and I am not pointing you back to them. There is no safer paper, only the same taking in different dress. The point is not to choose a costume. The point is to see there never was a gift inside any of them to choose.

## THE WORK WAS YOURS ALL ALONG

Come back to the three pellets we opened with and close them one by one, with credit returned.

The class. Who held the room? Who found the words when the plan slipped? Who stayed kind to the slow question at the back? You did. The dose waited outside and did no teaching. Then it stepped forward to be thanked for the relief that teaching done already brings.

The paragraph. Who stayed with the sentence until it turned clean? Who cut the weak line and kept the true one? You did. The dose typed nothing. Then it stepped forward to be thanked for the settling that finishing already brings.

The grocery run. Who drove both ways, remembered the list, carried the bags, stocked the shelves for people you love? You did. The dose carried nothing. Then it stepped forward in the car park to be thanked for the quiet that done already brings.

Isolate the worker from the watcher in every pellet you ever paid, and the watcher always arrives late to applaud work it never touched. That late applause is the whole reward story. It is like a man who stands beside you while you dig a garden, hands you nothing, then at the end puts his arm around you and says, we did well, and charges you for the hug.

We all paid the charge. We paid in money, in breath, in mornings that started stale, in pauses that left us tighter, in food whose taste we dried out to seal it. We paid good work to be made to feel like need. That is not your shame. That is the con, and it worked because it flattered the minute you were proudest of and stole the wages while you smiled.

You do not have to prove this to yourself by white-knuckling the next pause to see if I am right. That would be the old method talking — the method that turns everything into a test of grit and then blames you when grit runs out. This is not a test. This is seeing.

Smoke as you normally do while we look. Only watch the order with the eyes of an investigator: finish first, relief already rising second, lighting third, brief quiet of the second hunger fourth, same finished work fifth, a little staler. Watch it three times, four times, in your ordinary day. You will see the quiet arrive too fast to be flavour and fade too fast to be rest, and the work will tell you the rest. It was yours.

And while you watch, keep one boundary clean. There is noise everywhere about what you should do — cut down, switch, chew, suck, puff something else, listen to the man who suffered for months and calls his suffering proof. Let that noise wait outside this room. An argument half-heard is an argument half-kept, and you owe yourself the whole of this one before you borrow another.

SUMMARY
- The pellet after teaching, writing, or driving paid nothing to the work.
- The relief of finishing belongs to finishing, and stands without smoke.
- A pause with smoke leaves the chest tighter, not fresher.
- The taste never improved food, it dried it and borrowed the word pleasure.
- Vented filters read low on machines and do not mean less is taken when lips and fingers close the vents.
- Harder puffing on a mild proves the body seeks its usual squirt, not a smaller one.
- The party, the pause, and the flavour were your own moments with a tax laid over them.
