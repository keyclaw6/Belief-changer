MOVE E14-M1: PARTIAL — Carr names the quit difficulty as fear of deprivation and states plainly that you give up nothing real, only that fear; ours demolishes the second pan and the false FOR thoroughly but never consolidates the surrender frame so the reader sees stopping as losing only fear, not pleasure. Evidence: "The fact is, the second pan is empty." and "The FOR is empty." land the nothing-real half; the fear-of-going-without half is gestured in "what would life be without it? Flat. Grey. No breaks. No reward. No help when the day turns." and answered indirectly through non-smoker comparison, but never turned into the explicit recognition that fear itself is the sole sacrifice.

MOVE E14-M2: PRESENT — Carr strips cigarettes of credit for meals and social life, showing the occasion was already pleasant; ours performs the same decoupling across food, finished work, pause, and company, each time isolating the pleasure from the paper. Evidence: "The fact is, the meal was already finished. The pleasure was the food, the cooking, the hunger met, the company at the table, the sitting still. The dose only sneaked a ride on a pleasure that was complete without it, then claimed the credit and charged its fee in aftertaste." and "It is the other way around. You do not enjoy these moments because you smoke. You smoke during these moments and believe afterwards that smoking made them."

MOVE E14-M3: PRESENT — Carr restores the pre-smoking baseline by showing non-smokers never miss what smokers fear losing; ours does the same by returning the reader to the first dose and to the non-smoker who already has concentration, reward, pause, and company without smoke. Evidence: "Did you fear life without concentration when you lit your first dose? Did you fear missing taste? Did you fear having no reward? You feared none of those things. You had concentration, taste, reward, company already, without smoke, and you never thought to doubt them until the trap gave you new words for old hungers." and "A non-smoker feels tense, finishes work, thinks hard, meets friends, waits for a bus, feels bored, feels flat — and never once feels a gap where a paper tube should be."
