Chapter 5
PARTIES, PAUSES AND MILDS

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The classroom door still warm, the finished paragraph, the short drive to the shops, the little dish of pellets, the mask with pinholes round the edge

*The pleasure was the work done and the pause itself — the dose only charged you for tasting them.*

### THE DISH AFTER EVERYTHING

You saw it at the desk. The trouble sat untouched while the tug went quiet for minutes.

Now look where you still believe it gives.

For me it was after everything. Finished the hard call — light. Finished the page — light. Drove the children somewhere, drove home again — light. I told myself they were little parties. I had earned them.

You have your own list. Say it plainly and you will hear yourself:

“Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store.”

I am not mocking that. I lived it. There is something warm and loyal in wanting to mark work done. A good worker loves a full stop. Of course you put a party after it.

I want you to watch one of those parties with clear eyes, while you still smoke as normal.

Take the teaching. The room was restless at first. You found the thread. You held them. The bell went and your chest was high with it — you did that. Hands still warm from chalk, voice still warm from talking, heart still up. Then the dose.

Take the paragraph. You wrestled the sentence for an hour. Then it came clean. You read it back and it sang. Shoulders back. Smile without deciding to smile. Then the dose.

Take the grocery run. List done. Bags in the boot. Little triumph of ordinary life managed. Sun on the car park. Then the dose.

We did the work, felt the lift, and signed the lift over to the tube.

Did the stick teach the class?
Did the stick find the sentence?
Did the stick drive to the shops and remember everything on the list?

You know. We both do. No.

The joy was already there before the lighter caught. The relief of finished work, the loosening in the chest, the pride that needs no witness — that was yours. The dose arrived after your pleasure began and took the bow.

It is you that achieves, not the dose. It is you that enjoys finishing, not the dose.

See how widely the reward can stick itself. A long drive and a short drive. A class and a paragraph. A story and an errand. If it were a true reward for effort, the big effort would earn it and the tiny errand would not. Yet the tiny errand earns it just the same. What kind of payment pays the same for 500 miles and for milk?

It pays because the clock pays. An hour or so since the last feed, the empty tug stirs, and whatever you have just finished gets blamed for the hunger and credited for the quiet. Finish anything, feed the want, feel a dip from two hungers to one, call the dip a party.

Try it with your own day. Notice the same finished paragraph feels flat if you have just lit, and feels worthy of a party if you have not lit for an hour. The paragraph did not change. The interval changed. Notice the boring errand that demands no party when doses are coming freely on holiday, and demands a party at work when doses are forbidden. The errand did not change. The tug did.

That is not reward. That is rent.

A true reward leaves you richer. This leaves you staler, a little more edgy for the next hour, a little more certain you cannot enjoy finishing without lighting. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

### TAKE THE DOSE OUT OF THE PAUSE

“But I enjoy it. Surely the taste, the pause itself, is mine?”

I hear you. I said it for years. “The moment I light up I’m in heaven.” And I meant the first three puffs. Warm, familiar, hands doing what hands know. Then the ashtray taste, the hot end, the hurry to finish. Heaven for seconds, then stale. Love the lighting, hate being the smoker who must light.

Hold that honestly. If the taste were the gift, why would you hurry the middle and hardly taste the end? If the pause were the gift, why would the pause need smoke to be a pause?

Take the pause without the tube and watch what remains.

Morning kettle. Work break. Meal done. You step out, air on the face, hands free, nothing burning. The shoulders still drop. The head still clears. The food still sits warm and the taste lingers cleaner without the next dose trampling it. Non-smokers take that pause all day and call it nothing special — because it is nothing special, it is just air and time and a minute to breathe.

You stood at the same window and breathed the same air and felt the same drop before the lighter even caught — and then you signed that drop over to the stick. The pause did the work. The dose sneaked a ride.

Isolate it and the credit falls apart. Same chair, same sun on the step, same three slow breaths. With a fresh dose in you, you can sit and enjoy it. With an old dose in you, you cannot sit without reaching. Did the step change? No. The tug changed. You are measuring the age of the last feed and calling it the beauty of the pause.

I found my breaks grew longer and sweeter when they stopped being feeds. No clock counting down to the next light. No standing in rain to finish a hot end. No hurrying back with stale mouth. Just the break itself, clean and complete. The work tasted of work. The rest tasted of rest.

We were conned into paying for what was free.

### THE HOLES ROUND THE EDGE

And then there is the mild.

You were told, as we all were, that the light one, the mild one, the one with the pale pack and the neat gold ring, is softer, cleaner, a little less. You drew it and it felt airy. You told yourself, at least this one does less to me.

Look with me at the filter in your hand.

Round the paper, in a neat row, are tiny vent holes. On the machine they work beautifully. The machine takes its neat little puff, the air rushes in through the holes, the numbers come out low. Low tar. Low nicotine. Mild.

You are not a machine.

Put that filter between living lips, hold it between living fingers, and the holes close without you knowing. Lips cover them. Fingers cover them. And because the dose comes thin, you do what any hungry smoker does without thinking — you pull harder, you draw deeper, you keep it longer, you smoke it a fraction further down to get what the Nipper is asking for.

Not every smoker in exactly the same way, but as a rule the hands and mouth do the compensating the works were designed to invite.

Picture a mask meant to protect you, with pinholes punched round the edge to let clean air in. Put it on in smoke and breathe. Your mouth seals the edge. Your hands press the sides. You suck harder to get air through, and you pull more smoke through the middle. Would you call that protection? Or would you call it a trick that makes you work harder for the same breath?

That is the mild.

It never gave you less. It taught you to take it harder. Bigger puff. Deeper pull. Fingers unknowingly over the holes. The machine smoked air. You smoked the dose.

Ask your own mouth and answer true. Do you smoke a mild the same dainty way, or do you drag it harder and hold it longer? Do you smoke fewer, or do you find the same chain at the same hours? If the mild truly did less to you, why would the timetable not change? Why would the hand still move on its old hour?

You know. The timetable follows the want, not the pack colour.

The factory did not soften the trap. It painted it pale and punched holes in the paper and let your own lips do the rest. And we, warm loyal smokers, thanked it for being kinder while we puffed harder to feed the same tug.

Is that kindness? Or is that a con with air holes?

The mild never cut the dose. It cut your suspicion while you did the cutting yourself.

Feel how this clears you. You did not enjoy smoking more because one tasted smoother. You enjoyed finishing, pausing, belonging to the minute — and the tube charged you for its own fading. You did not smoke safer because one was called light. You smoked the same hunger through a leaky mask.

Once seen, the exceptions cannot stand. The party was the work. The pause was the air. The mild was harder puffing through hidden holes.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

**SUMMARY**
- The reward feeling after teaching, writing, driving or shopping was the lift of work finished, not a gift from the dose.
- The pause pleases because time and air please, and the dose only sneaks a ride on them.
- Taste is seconds of familiar lighting, then stale hurry, not lasting enjoyment.
- Ventilated milds do not mean you took less, because lips and fingers cover the holes and you pull harder to get the dose.
- Seen clearly, parties, pauses and milds were stolen credit and compensated puffing, not benefits to keep.
