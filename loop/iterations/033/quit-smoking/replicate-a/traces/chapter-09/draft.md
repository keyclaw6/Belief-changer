Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

IN THIS CHAPTER
The pack on the kitchen table, the filter theatre and the mild promise, the two fears pulling in opposite directions

*That want was never your nature. It was built for you, and sold back to you as you.*

### THE BOX ON THE TABLE

You have breathed morning clean. You have tasted food clearing on its own. You have stood outside with free hands and felt the break do its work without paper. Keep smoking as you read. I ask you now to look at what is in your hand with clear eyes.

Pick up your pack. Hold it a moment, not as a smoker reaching, but as an investigator weighing something.

What is it? Twenty paper tubes in a coloured box. A day's supply box. Each tube the same length, the same filter, the same burn. Each puff the same small squirt. Factory counted. Factory sealed.

Who counted it?

We did not count it. We never sat down and said, I need twenty hits today spaced forty minutes apart, fading in minutes so I must feed again. We never designed the fade. We only live it. Someone else designed it.

The fact is this: in their own planning rooms, their own scientists described the cigarette not as a flavour or a pleasure or a companion, but as a package. The product is nicotine. The paper, the filter, the taste, the brand — the wrapping. The dose inside — the business.

Ask yourself plainly, with the box in your palm.

Did you choose to need a drug that peaks in seconds and fades in minutes so you must dose all day?

Did you choose the stale mouth and the hourly tug as your nature?

If this was your nature, why did your first ones taste foul and make you cough?

It was the other way around. You did not build a want and find a product to meet it. They built a product that creates want and taught you to call that want you.

I smoked for years believing the hand moving to the pack was my hand. I speak with warmth for you because I know that feeling from inside. It feels like me. It feels private. That is the cleverness of the con. We all fell for it. The hand moves before decision because the timetable was installed in the body long ago, and the mind kindly signs our name underneath.

But whose timetable is it? Yours breathes and eats and sleeps. Theirs lights every hour to keep the fade topped up. That is the nicotine trap. 

See it as it is and the shame falls away. You were not weak. You were conned by men in suits who count doses for a living while you pay per puff to stay nearly normal.

### THE WHITE THEATRE

“But mine are mild. Mine are light. I chose the softer ones.”

I hear you. I said it myself when the cough grew heavy. Let us look at that softness with the filter between finger and thumb.

Hold one up to the light. That white tip looks clean. Scientific. Safe-ish. The word light on the pack sounds kind. Lower numbers on the side sound kind. Kind is the theatre.

The machine smokes differently from a human. The machine takes small neat puffs and reads low. The human wants the dose. So the human — we — puff bigger, draw deeper, hold longer, cover the tiny vent holes with lips and fingers without knowing, and take what the body was taught to seek. As a rule the human defeats the machine.

Tell me from your own smoking, not from their label.

Have you ever smoked a light and felt satisfied with less, or did you smoke just as many and inhale just as hard?

If light meant little, why does the hand move just as often, and why does the tug return just as fast?

Have you ever known a light smoker who smoked only two a day and never thought about the rest?

You know the answer because you have lived it. The theatre does not cut the dose. It cuts the alarm while you take the dose. That whisper that says this one is softer is the Smokescreen.

The filter never filtered the need. It filtered your worry so you would keep feeding. The holes never made it mild. They made it look mild while you worked harder for the same hit. No safer, no cleaner — only better dressed. I say it hard against the maker, never against you. You chose kindness to your chest and they sold you paint.

Strip the paint and what is left? The same tube. The same squirt. The same fade. The same box counting down the day. A con with a white collar is still a con.

### AFRAID TO FAIL, AFRAID TO BE FREE

This is where the second part of the build shows. They did not only build the dose. They built the fear around it, both sides, so whichever way you lean you stay.

Listen to the two voices that pull at night.

One says: what if I try and fail again, like the twenty times before, patches in the drawer, calendar crossed out. Better not to promise. Better to stay where I am than prove I cannot do it.

The other says, quieter: what if I do stop — who will I be on the patio with a drink, on the break laughing, after food leaning back. Will life be flat? Will I be someone else, boring, missing my little friend?

“I can’t quit now. I would fail. And I’m not sure I want the life after.”

I know both voices. I lived pulled between them for years. Let us test them against your own life, not against dread.

If failure proved you were weak, why did strong, stubborn, successful people fail the same way, again and again, on the same method?

If success meant losing yourself, when did you become the dose — before your first one, when you laughed and ate and paused clean, or after?

Which voice ever gave you a free evening? The first keeps you smoking to avoid shame. The second keeps you smoking to avoid loss. Both keep you smoking. Both are paid for by the same hands.

Do you see the geometry? A rope in each hand, pulling opposite ways, and the same trap holding both ends. Fail and you smoke. Succeed-and-lose and you smoke. Fear to stay, fear to leave — and the box empties either way, and is bought again either way.

That is the tug-of-war of fear.

Once seen it cannot be unseen. The fear of failure is not your cowardice. It is the memory of the wrong method that manufactured struggle and called it you. The fear of success is not your truth. It is brainwashing that dressed absence as emptiness after you had already proved morning and meal and break are fuller clean.

Let both ropes drop. There is nothing to hold. You are not choosing between failure and loss. You are seeing that both fears belong to the trap, not to you.

### STALE, BREATHLESS AND CHAINED

Look now, without flinching, at what the bargain really cost while the ropes held you. I will tell it flat because you deserve truth, and then I will tell you why truth is not what frees you.

Morning mouth like an ashtray licked clean. Clothes, hair, car, curtains holding yesterday's smoke no mint can bury. Throat clearing in meetings. Cough in the cold that was not a cold. Stairs taken in stages while a non-smoker friend breathes easy at the top and waits, kindly pretending not to notice. Money burned to ash every single day forever for something you never needed. Evenings cut into segments by the clock. Rainy doorways stood in alone while talk and warmth went on inside. Hiding in the garage, spraying, chewing, lying with a smile about where you were.

Tell me honestly, smoker to escaped smoker.

Was any of that pleasure? Was any of that you at your best?

If it was a friend, why did it leave you like this?

It leaves every long smoker in the same permanent state, not sick in bed, not dying today — but never clean, never clear, never unhooked. Stale, breathless and chained.

If you feel ill or you worry about stopping, speak to your doctor for your own care. I speak only of belief. I do not want you to stop because you are frightened. Fear never freed anyone; it only makes the next dose feel like rescue. I want you to stop because you see there was never rescue, only rent. See the cost, then let joy do the freeing, not fright.

For the cost was never the price of pleasure. It was the price of the tug being briefly quieted. They kept the profit. You kept the ash. That is not a moral sentence on you. It is a business sentence on them. And a business you see clearly is a business you can walk out of rejoicing.

You do not need to become someone else to walk out. You need to come back to who you were before the first con — the body that breathes without teaching, the tongue that tastes without teaching, the person who laughed without timing it by a pack. Nothing missing. Everything returned.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

**SUMMARY**
- The want was not born in you; a manufactured package creates a fast-fading need and teaches you to call the topping-up your nature.
- Mild and filtered do not mean little; the machine number is theatre while the human hand seeks the dose, and no white tip changes the bargain.
- Fear of failing and fear of being free are not two truths; both ropes are held by the same trap to keep the box emptying either way.
- The long price is a stale mouth, short breath and an hourly chain, not pleasure, and that price was profit to the seller, never gain to you.
- Seeing the seller ends blame on yourself and leaves escape as joy, not fright, with nothing missing where the dose was.
