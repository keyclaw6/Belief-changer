Chapter 13
CLEAN AIR ON ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the kettle and the open window, the queue at the till, ten minutes in clean air, evening laughter that runs whole, the wisp that floats past

*Ordinary hours were never made by smoking — they are yours again now, fuller and easier because you no longer pay for them.*

### THE KETTLE BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

You wake and the room is exactly as it was yesterday, and entirely different.

The alarm does its small rude buzz. The light is thin at the curtains. The house makes its morning ticks, pipes, distant traffic, someone else's radio through a wall. For years we woke inside that same small film and reached before we were awake. Feet had not touched the floor before the hand found the pack. That is how morning went inside the con — not chosen, not enjoyed, only topped up before the day could start.

Notice this morning.

Feet touch the floor. Cold lino, warm rug, whatever your floor gives you. You stand. You stretch without thinking about where you will stand to get away with it. You put the kettle on and hear it build from whisper to roar. You open the window an inch and the air comes in as it is — cold, damp, faint petrol from the road, faint bread from somewhere down the street — and it goes straight down to the bottom of your lungs without a toll.

That is the first proof, and it needs no argument.

Do you remember how a morning dose used to feel? Hot at the back of the throat on empty lungs. A faint lift that passed for waking up, then the sour mouth and the tight chest and the need to clear the throat before speaking. We called that starting the day. We were only quieting the hunger the night had left to grow. Eight hours without a dose and the body whispered, and we answered with smoke and called the quiet energy.

This is energy without a fee.

Make tea. Make coffee. Drink cold water straight from the tap if that is what you like. Taste it all the way through. The tongue is not wearing yesterday's coat this morning. Food will taste kinder at breakfast because last night's stale end is not still sitting on your taste. Breathe through the nose while the toast browns. Smell toast as toast. Smell morning as morning. No free lungs ever asked for that papery veil over the first hour.

I woke for thirty years and lit before I spoke. I know the little panic of the empty pack, the dressing-gown search in the cold kitchen, the calculation of shops and opening times before the day had even opened its eyes. I know the lighting at the back door while the children slept inside, telling myself it was peace. It was tenancy. The day did not begin until the fee was paid.

You have no fee to pay today.

Take your time. There is nowhere to step out to. No end to hide, no ash to chase in the wind, no humming arithmetic under the kettle noise. Stand at the window with both hands warm around the mug. Watch the street wake. Hear talk whole. Feel the chest move easy. This is what mornings were before the first conned puff, and what they are again now.

Wash slow. Look in the bathroom mirror with clear eyes and clean mouth. Shower hot and breathe the steam deep without that first shallow cough. Dress without the old pat of every pocket. No checking the box, no counting for the day, no tucking a spare where it will not be seen. The coat smells of cupboard and night air. The fingers smell of soap. The hair smells of nothing but you.

If the old hand twitches toward the pocket where the pack used to live, smile. That twitch is not you wanting. That is a hand that moved a hundred thousand times before the mind decided. Let it find the mug. Let it find the curtain, the sill, the warm radiator. Free hands learn fast.

When did smoke ever make a morning kinder? Did it put light in the window, or taste in the tea, or strength in the legs for the stairs? It took the first clean breath and sold it back to you grey.

It took. Now you keep.

That is morning one. There will be a morning two, and a morning twenty, and they will not get harder. They will get more ordinary, which is to say more wonderful. The extraordinary thing about freedom is how quickly it becomes normal to breathe easy. You will find yourself halfway to work before you remember you used to stop at the corner to finish, and the remembering will make you laugh out loud on an ordinary pavement.

Take the stairs as you find them. Take the bus as you find it. Sit beside a smoker with a coat that smells of last night and feel nothing but warm gladness that the smell is not on you. Your chest goes up a flight without bargaining. Your nose finds coffee, rain, diesel, someone's orange peeled two seats away. The world has more detail when it is not filtered through paper and heat.

### THE QUEUE AT THE TILL

Go to the shop today exactly as you always did.

Same street, same bell over the door, same queue shuffling forward with baskets and boredom. Buy milk. Buy bread. Buy whatever you went in for. Change nothing else in your life to prove you are free. Freedom does not need a new route to the shops.

Only one thing is different, and you will feel it in the pocket and in the chest.

For years we stood in that queue doing secret maths. Money for the box. Which brand, how many days will this one last, can I make it stretch to payday. We told ourselves it was just shopping. It was tribute. Every pack was a day's supply box of doses lined up to be fed, and we carried it home like it was food.

Stand there now without the tribute.

Watch the money stay where it belongs. It is not the pounds that matter most, though the pounds will mount into holidays and coats and fine dinners if you care to count them. It is the lift of not paying to feel nearly normal. It is walking past the lit gantry behind the till without that small clench — shall I, shan't I, just in case — and feeling nothing but relief that the question is gone.

Someone ahead of you will buy ciggies. Someone will step outside as you leave and light with that hurried huddle we all knew. Do not look away in fear and do not stare with envy. Look with clear eyes and warm pity for the con, never contempt for the person.

Does that really look like pleasure? The coat pulled tight, the hand cupped against wind, the eyes on the clock because the bus is coming and there is only time for half. The first drag taken too deep, the little cough covered with a laugh, the sour mouth before the working day has begun. You were there. You know the timetable and the edginess and the secret adding up. You are not being deprived of something precious. They are still paying for pauses in their own hunger.

You will feel a small clean lift as you push the door open into the street. Clean air on the face. Bags in both hands, both hands free in the true sense — free to carry, to hold, to wave, to put in pockets for warmth rather than to pat for a box. Breathe easy all the way home. Taste the cold. Hear the town.

Walk past the old corner where you always finished before work, the shelter with stubs black in the gutter, the doorway where you huddled in rain to get one in before the train. Notice you do not slow. Notice there is no pull to step aside, no glance at the clock, no quickening to finish in time. The pavement is only pavement now. The shelter is only shelter. You walk through the place where the fee was paid and pay nothing, and the legs keep their rhythm and the breath keeps its ease.

I remember counting coins for a meter that never stayed fed. Each dose dropped a coin and the needle lifted for minutes, then sank again by the hour. Shop to shop, day to day, year to year, the meter ate and never filled. Now the coins stay. Now the day stays.

Look at it plainly. When did buying that box ever make the shop kinder, or the walk home sweeter, or the evening safer? When did that paper tube ever add to the bag it rode home in? It rode home on your money and signed your money over to itself.

Now you walk home with your money and your breath and your morning intact. That is not thrift. That is escape.

Rejoice in the ordinary errand done without tax. Marvellous things do not all arrive with trumpets. Some arrive as a till receipt without poison on it, and a pocket without a box in it, and lungs that do not have to prepare for the next hit before lunch. You will get home and put the milk away and realize you have not once done the old pat-and-check, and you will stand in your own kitchen grinning like a fool at nothing at all.

### TEN MINUTES WITH NOTHING TO PAY

Mid-morning will come with its small restlessness, as mid-mornings do.

The screen blurs. The back aches. The children squabble or the boss emails or the house asks to be tidied for the tenth time. In the old hours we read that stir as an order. Time for a break. Time for a light. Time to step out.

Take the break. Leave the light.

Step out if you like stepping out. Stay in if you like staying in. Keep the same chair, the same step, the same yard wall. Freedom is not a new choreography. It is the same dance without chains.

Feel air on the face with free hands. Roll the shoulders. Drink water cold. Stretch the neck until it clicks. Watch pigeons argue on the wire. Watch clouds do their slow nothing. Let the mind wander where it wanders without timing it to an ember.

We used to call those ten minutes little parties. Teaching a class, finished. Light. A paragraph, finished. Light. Driving to the shops, finished. Light. Driving five hundred miles, finished, finished, finished. Light, light, light. The party was never the work done, though the work was real and good. The party was the dose, and the dose stole the credit.

Taste the break without the dose now.

The pause is pleasant because pausing is pleasant. The lungs love air. The eyes love distance after close work. The legs love standing after sitting. The mind loves a small empty field to cross before the next task. None of that ever needed smoke to be sweet. Smoke only ever sneaked a ride on the sweetness and signed it over to itself.

Drive the short run if the day asks for driving. Same keys, same seat, same road past the same shops. Only now the window stays as you like it and the ashtray stays shut and the hand stays on the wheel. No flicking ends into wind. No timing the lights to finish. No sour mouth when you step out at the other end. You arrive tasting of nothing but the drive, clear-headed, present, and the errand gets you whole.

Will the break feel strange the first time? Perhaps faintly bare, like a finger without a ring you never liked. That bareness is not loss. That is the shape of a hand that no longer carries ash. Give it an hour. Give it a day. The hand finds the mug, the rail, the pocket for warmth. The mouth finds water, apple, laughter. The lungs find the thing they were built for.

If a faint edginess brushes past — a thin, quickly passing whisper under the ribs — do not mistake it for yourself. It is not grief and not need and not proof mornings cannot work. It is a dying echo, already fading because the mind no longer feeds it. Breathe easy through it. Move. Get on with enjoying your life while it thins to nothing. Free people live in that quiet all day without topping, because no new hunger is being made.

Think of what a break truly gives. You stepped out and missed the talk. You stood apart and missed the laugh. You came back with mouth sour and fingers stale and called yourself refreshed. Who was refreshed? The break was. You only paid for it.

Now you take the whole break. Talk heard entire. Air tasted entire. Ten minutes that belong to you from first second to last. That is refreshment without a middleman.

Celebrate that. Wonderful is not too big a word for a free break in clean air. Sit a moment longer than you need to, just because you can. There is no ember to beat you. There is no clock except the one you choose. When you go back in, you go back clear-headed, clean-mouthed, present, and the work you return to gets the best of you, not the leftovers after the car park.

I took my breaks for years on the fire escape behind the office, rain in the collar, inbox piling while I paid to pause it. I told myself I thought best out there. I thought best when I came back in and the air moved again. The thinking was mine. The pausing was mine. The sky over the bins was even, in its way, mine. Only the fee was theirs.

Yours now is all yours.

### EVENING LAUGHTER THAT RUNS WHOLE

Evening will test nothing, but evening will show you everything.

Supper done. Dishes warm in the sink. Drink in hand if you drink. Friends round, or family loud, or telly low, or street alive outside the pub. Night air soft. Laughter coming in waves. This is the hour the con loved to claim as its own — the tray of lights on the patio with laughter round it, a kind hand extending and the old whisper that one won't hurt.

Take the whole night without paying for it.

Eat and let taste linger. Meal-end taste does not need drowning. It deepens if you let it — gravy, lemon, coffee, toothpaste-mint, night air — each arriving clean because no stale end is laid over it. Talk and let talk run long. Stay where the laugh is. No clock ticking to the next step outside. No coat fetched. No sentence cut half-finished while you go to miss it.

I spent years leaving rooms to keep a con alive. Stepping out of weddings to stand with strangers in the rain. Missing the toast to feed a tug. Missing the story's end to quiet a whisper. Coming back smelling of it, chewing, spraying, hoping no one noticed, while everyone noticed and no one said. We told ourselves we were sociable smokers. We were absent smokers, present only between doses.

Be present now from first laugh to last.

If someone lights near you, let them. Smoke drifting past is not a command. It smells now as it is — acrid, papery, a little sour — not as memory dressed it. Watch the huddle outside while laughter goes on inside and feel relief rise that you do not have to choose between warmth and want. You have both warmth and freedom exactly where you sit.

If drink is in your hand, drink is in your hand. Company is company. Night air is night air. None of them ever contained nicotine. The pleasure of that late scene was sun on skin long after it set, leisure after work, friends who love your laugh. The stick only ever sneaked a ride on all three.

Sit in the pub garden till the air cools and the lights come on. No counting how long since the last, no slipping out between rounds, no missing the round because you were outside. Hear the whole story with its wrong turns and its punchline intact. Tell yours to the end without watching the door. The glass empties because you drank it, not because you timed it to ash.

Look at it straight. When did ash ever make food taste kinder, or talk sound sweeter, or music go deeper? When did stepping out ever make a night longer, except in the sense of making you miss more of it? When did a borrowed light ever make belonging stronger, except by making you fear you would not belong without it?

It never did. The night was good. The dose charged admission to your own evening.

Now there is no admission. Laugh whole nights through with clean breath and remember every word at dawn. Clothes smelling of nothing. Mouth tasting of supper and sleep, not of ashtray. Chest easy on the stairs to bed. That is belonging without a fee.

This is the proof the con most feared you would find — that the best hours need nothing added to be best. You will come home and hang up a coat that smells of night air and food and laughter, and you will press your face into it like a child, just to check, and you will grin in the dark hallway because the check proves what you already know.

Stay late if you want to stay late. Leave early if you want to leave early. No part of the decision is about getting out for a moment alone with paper. You are never torn between the table and the door. You are wholly at the table, and then you are wholly in your bed, and both places are better for being whole in them.

### WHEN THE OLD THOUGHT FLOATS PAST

It will happen. Not as a storm. As a wisp.

A smell on another's coat in a lift. A break bell at work. A meal-end pause. A bad hour when the phone brings news you did not want. The thought will drift across, thin as smoke from another garden: a dose would fit here.

Do not fight it. Do not mourn it. You do not get thoughts by forbidding them, and you do not lose freedom by noticing one.

You have owned the answer since the second you stubbed the last one. Catch the wisp with a lift in the chest and let the answer fire by itself. You smile and think, YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

That is all. No debate. No tally. No weighing whether you are brave enough to miss out. There is nothing to miss, so there is nothing to be brave about. The dead enemy gets no tears. He gets rejoicing.

See how the script arrives pre-labelled now. Before, that whisper sounded like your voice. Now you hear whose voice it is. It is the old brainwashing talking, the lie that taught you to read hunger as help. It says, just this once to take care of stress. It says, prove you are free by handling one. It says, life feels flat without the little party.

Answer from your own lived days, not from thirst.

Did smoke ever take care of anything except its own tug? The papers sat untouched while you stood outside. The row waited inside while you paid to pause it. The flat feeling lifted for minutes because hunger paused, then sank lower because fresh hunger was guaranteed. The rescuer was the perpetrator all along. Seeing that once is enough to see it forever.

Take the bad hour as you will now meet it. Phone down. Chest tight. Old hand half lifting. You stay. You breathe clean to the bottom. You drink cold water. You handle the next small thing — the note, the dish, the child needing shoes — and let the rest wait till it can be handled. Minutes pass and the tightness softens because life softens, not because hunger paused. Then comes the quiet lift no dose ever gave: I met that present, without stepping out, without paying. Keep that lift. It is yours and it grows each time you feel it.

A confused puff, if it ever happened, would not prove you are weak and would not make you chained by magic. It would warn. It would show how fast fresh water primes the old pump. Note it, rejoice you see it, and take no second to argue with it. What keeps you free is clear seeing, not counting. Keep the seeing and the seeing will keep you.

Pity the smoker still outside in the rain while laughter goes on inside. Warmth for the person, always. Contempt only for the trick that holds them. You were there. You know the sour mouth and the yellow and the adding up no free person ever keeps. Rejoice you do not live there now.

Do not preach. Do not chase friends with arguments. Let visible ease do the recruiting. Hold your head high among smokers and non-smokers alike, breathe easy beside them, taste fully beside them, stay whole beside them. Freedom noticed is more persuasive than freedom announced.

The thought that once meant hunger now means relief. Every old cue becomes a bell that rings escape. Break, meal, laugh, pang, stress, celebration — each one arrives, each one rings, each one reminds you the cage is empty and the air is clean.

That is protection without effort. Not guarding a behaviour with fear, but guarding a belief with joy. You will be crossing a road a month from now and catch a whiff from a doorway and feel the lift before you have time to think, and you will walk on lighter for half a street because the reminder came and went and left you freer.

### GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

There is no second curriculum. That is the beauty of it.

You do not need to learn to be a non-smoker. You were one before the first conned puff, and the body remembers. It returns to breathing and tasting on its own. It knows freedom without being taught. Eat as you eat. Drink as you drink. Work as you work. Sleep as you sleep. Take the same mornings, the same shops, the same breaks, the same nights — and live them without paying.

Think how marvellous to want nothing you have to time. Think how wonderful to miss nothing you have to step out for. Think how exciting to meet bad days present and good days whole, breathing easy through both. The weight off the shoulders is not a future prize. It is now, this ordinary Tuesday with washing in the machine and rain at the window and supper to make.

A bad day will come, because life comes. A bill, a row, a phone call that shakes you. You will meet it present, breathing deep, handling the next small thing, letting the rest pass. Then you will feel the lift that free people feel: I met that without paying. That lift is real calm. That lift is yours. No dose ever gave you that. It only gave you a pause in its own weather and called the pause calm.

A good day will come too, and you will meet it whole. You will eat slow. You will talk long. You will stay to the end of the story. You will laugh without listening for the clock. In the morning your clothes will smell of nothing and your mouth will taste of sleep and coffee, and you will take the stairs without thought and the bus without dread and the shop without maths.

Weeks will pass and the days will stack into seasons. Spring windows open wide. Summer nights long in the garden. Autumn smoke on bonfires that smells only of wood. Winter air sharp in the chest that loves it. Each season will find you a little further from the old arithmetic, a little more at home in clean air, until the whole con feels like a coat borrowed long ago and handed back.

I promise you this as one escapee to one free reader, with total certainty and warm complicity. You have walked out of a con that timed your mornings and priced your clothes and chose where you stood at weddings. You closed it with eyes open on ash and yellow and stale heat, and you will never need another dose because there was never anything to need.

So get on with enjoying your life. Take mornings deep. Take food slow. Take breaks wide. Take nights long. Let money stay. Let breath stay. Let laughter run unbroken because you never leave to miss it.

Whenever the wisp drifts past tomorrow, next month, next year — on a patio, in a queue, at a desk after a row — you know what to do. Catch it, lift, rejoice. You smile and think, YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Breathe deep. Taste the hour. The ordinary days are yours, and they taste better free.

**SUMMARY**
- Mornings are complete without smoke, and breath and taste come back in ordinary hours without a fee.
- The shop queue no longer taxes you, and money and time stay where they belong.
- Breaks refresh because pausing refreshes, and clean air needs no topping to be sweet.
- Nights and laughter were good all along, and staying inside them whole proves the dose added nothing.
- An old smoking thought is only a wisp from another garden, answered with relief and lived freedom going forward.
