Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

IN THIS CHAPTER
Alarm, cold floor and kettle steam. The empty plate and the taste that lingers. Mid-morning air on the face and hands with nothing in them.

*Morning, food and a pause were yours before smoke ever touched them, and they are still yours without it.*

### FEET BEFORE THE PACK

Keep smoking as you read. I ask nothing of your hands yet. I ask only that you watch one morning honestly, as if you were standing beside yourself.

The alarm goes. Your feet have not touched the floor before your hand finds the pack. Before water, before air, before a thought. On an empty mouth and empty lungs, the first drag feels enormous. Huge. We say, there, I needed that. I cannot start without it.

I lived that start for years, so I speak without a speck of blame for you. Let us look at what really started.

All night you fed nothing. For six, seven, eight hours the little hunger slid down with no coin going in. You wake in deficit — stale mouth, thick head, faint gnaw behind the ribs. A non-smoker wakes at zero. He stretches, coughs once clear, puts the kettle on. His body asks for water and movement and air. Yours asks for a dose before the day has asked anything of you.

Tell me plainly, from your own bedroom, not from what you were told.

Did that first dose lift you above him? Or did it drag you from your low back toward where he woke?

Have you ever seen a non-smoker scramble for anything before his feet touch the floor?

If the dose gave morning energy, why did it never carry past the kettle?

You know because you have lived it ten thousand times. The dose did not add morning. It ended the night-without that smoking itself created, then took credit for morning. The light through the blind, the cold floor waking the feet, the kettle beginning to sing, the first clear breath of the house — those were there all along. The dose only quietened its own complaint long enough for you to notice them for a minute, then claimed it had made them.

Try it tomorrow with clear eyes while you still smoke. Feet to floor. Kettle on. Window open a crack. Breathe before you light. Taste water before you light. Notice what your body already does without teaching. It breathes. It swallows. It wakes. It does not need a paper tube to prove the day has begun.

I am not asking you to change the morning. I am asking you to see who owns it. The morning never needed smoke to be morning. Smoke needed morning to look needed.

Once that is seen, the fear of flat mornings without it begins to thin. Not by force. By seeing there was never a gift, only a debt briefly repaid.

### THE PLATE IS ALREADY FINISHED

“But a meal is not finished without one. It seals it.”

I hear you. I said those words myself for years, hand moving by itself when the plate went empty. Let us test that sealing against your own tongue.

Eat tonight as normal and pay full attention to the food itself. The heat, the salt, the full belly, the pause when the fork goes down. Where in that did smoke improve taste? Strip the dose out in memory and run the film again. The warmth came from the cooking. The fullness came from the eating. The pause came from stopping. The dose dried the mouth it claimed to satisfy and laid ash over the taste it claimed to seal.

The fact is a dose seals nothing but the hour since the last feed. Lunch runs long, the meter slides, the hand moves. Tasty feast or dull sandwich, it moves just the same. Hungry or full, it moves just the same. If it sealed flavour, a dull meal would need no seal. But the hand moves for dull and fine alike. It is not the plate calling. It is the clock.

Ask yourself two honest questions and answer from your own table.

If it sealed taste, why does food taste clearer twenty minutes later when the mouth is clean?
If it completed the meal, why does the pleasure of the meal live in memory as warmth and company, never as ash?

You have already proved the answer without knowing it. Think of the best meals of your life — birthdays, holidays, a table full of laughter. What do you remember? Faces. Light. Flavour. Ease. You do not remember the seventh drag after the pudding as the crown of the evening. No one ever says, that was a wonderful night because of the smoke. The night was wonderful because it was a wonderful night. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on it.

And that old pellet after the finished job — the paragraph done, the errand run, the little party you paid yourself — that party was the work done, not the paper lit. Teaching a class well is joyful because you taught well. Finishing the drive is good because you arrived. The dose attached itself to almost any finished act and signed its name where your name belonged. Take your name back. The credit was always yours.

Watch the next meal-end closely. Let the taste linger instead of burying it. Drink water. Breathe. Notice the mouth cleaning itself, the tongue waking up again. Your body knows how to finish a meal. It has known since before you ever smoked. Food ends in fullness and rest. It never needed a dry throat and a sour aftertaste to be complete.

There is nothing to lose here and everything to taste.

### AIR, LEGS AND LAUGHTER

The break is the one you fear most. I know. I feared it too. What will I do with my hands? Where will I stand? Will the pause be empty?

We built our whole working day around going out to feed. Legs want moving, lungs want air, mind wants pause. We go out with the others and light. For minutes the chatter feels warmer, the sky looks wider. We credit the stick.

Take the stick out of the picture and look again at the same five minutes.

Did your legs need smoke to stretch? Did your eyes need it to look at sky? Did your ears need it to hear a friend laugh?

No. The break worked because you stopped, breathed, stepped away. Air did the work. Movement did the work. Company did the work. The dose only silenced the tug it had planted before you arrived, then claimed it had made the rest.

I want you to feel the next break as your body feels it, not as the trap taught you to read it. Step out. Feel cold or warm air on the face. Roll the shoulders. Fill the chest all the way down and let it go slow. Hear what people actually say instead of half-listening through the haze of relief. Notice your hands. Free hands can hold a warm mug, can go in pockets, can gesture when you laugh. They are not empty. They are free.

“But I will feel edgy and left out.”

That edginess when the hour calls is real and I do not mock it. What it is, is small. It is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug, the complaint of the Nipper as its feed fades. It is not you. It is not proof the break needs smoke. It is withdrawal with a short typical peak, already dying between every dose. If you feel ill or you worry, speak to your doctor for your own care. I speak only of belief: that stir is not your nature calling for its missing half. It is a tenant complaining while it is being starved out.

Remember those stairs that left you winded while a friend breathed easy. That was not lack of breaks. That was the cost of the breaks as we took them — stale, hurried, dosed. A true break leaves you clearer than it found you. Ours left us needing another within the hour, plus a thinner edge. What kind of rest needs to be repeated twenty times a day to do the same tiny job? A real pause restores. Ours rented quiet for minutes and guaranteed another hunger.

Ask what part of the pause you actually loved. Was it the throat sting? The ash? The counting down to going back in? Or was it standing still for once, breathing, being a body in air with other people? Keep what you loved. It was never smoke. Smoke only stood in front of it and took the bow.

Your body already knows how to pause. Children pause without instruction. Non-smokers pause without instruction. You paused clean for years before you were conned. Step out, breathe, stretch, laugh — the break is complete because you took it, not because you fed anything during it.

### FULLER, NOT EMPTIER

Here is the turn this whole chapter stands on.

We fear these ordinary moments will be flat without doses because we only ever met them dosed. We lit through every morning, every meal-end, every pause for years. Of course memory says smoke was part of them. Smoke was present. Present is not the same as maker.

Think of sunglasses you wore indoors for a decade. You would swear the house was dim. Take them off and the light feels harsh for a minute — then marvellous. The house was never dim. The glass was dim. So with morning and food and air. The dose never brightened them. It dimmed them and sold you the second of normal as brightness.

Do you want proof from your own life, not my words?

When did food taste best — in the first clean bite, or through the haze after?
When did air feel sweetest — on a morning you breathed deep before lighting, or in the thin exhale after?
When did a laugh feel freest — when you were lost in it, or when half your mind was checking the pack?

It is the other way around. You do not enjoy these moments because you smoke during them. You enjoy them in spite of smoking during them, in the gaps where the body still manages to taste and breathe.

That is why non-smoker pleasure is not less. It is more. The non-smoker meets the kettle with a clean mouth. He meets the plate with a tongue that still tastes the lingering flavour instead of burying it. He meets the break with lungs that fill to the bottom. He does not climb from low to nearly normal and fall again. He starts at normal and rises with the moment itself — sun, leisure, company, rest. All the things we credited to the stick were the scene all along.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

See that once and the hesitant picture changes. Mornings are not empty without it. They are unrobbed. Meals are not unfinished without it. They are allowed to finish. Breaks are not pointless without it. They are finally allowed to do what breaks are for — air, movement, a clear head.

I smoked on balconies in rain while life went on inside without me. I told myself I was enjoying a pause. I was standing apart from the warmth to feed a hunger I had paid to install. You have done the same, and I say it with warmth for you and contempt only for the con that taught us to call exile pleasure. Come back inside the moment. It was waiting all along.

### BREATHE, TASTE, LIVE

You do not need to learn a new way to wake, to eat, to rest. You need to let the old way — the first way, before the trap — do what it still knows how to do.

Your lungs know how to breathe without being taught. Your tongue knows how to taste without being taught. Your legs know how to stretch, your eyes know how to look at distance, your ears know how to hear laughter. The nicotine trap borrowed these joys and charged you per use. The Smokescreen told you the charge was the joy.

Refuse that reading now.

Tomorrow morning, notice the kettle before the pack. Tomorrow at table, let flavour linger instead of ashing it. Tomorrow at break time, take air all the way down and feel your shoulders drop because you paused, not because you fed. You will find no hole where the dose was. You will find the moment itself, fuller and cleaner, with free hands to hold it.

You are not facing deprivation in these small hours. You are facing escape in them — the first sweet proof that life without doses is not less life, but more.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

**SUMMARY**
- Morning never needed a dose to begin; the first drag only repaid the night-without and borrowed credit for waking.
- Food finishes in fullness and flavour; smoke dried the mouth and buried the taste it claimed to seal.
- A break restores because you stop, breathe, move and laugh; the dose only quietened its own call and signed its name on air.
- The edgy stir between doses is small and fading, not the voice of the real self.
- Met clean, ordinary moments are fuller without smoke, not emptier.
