Chapter 7
THE ITCH IT PRETENDS TO SCRATCH

IN THIS CHAPTER
The ticking meter on a grey street, a day of doses rising and fading, the bite scratched raw behind the ribs, smokers laughing on the step

*The lift you feel is not a lift above life — it is the brief quiet of a hunger the last dose left behind.*

### THE METER THAT ALWAYS RUNS OUT

The drawer is closed. The war of force is over. What stands now is cleaner and smaller than you feared. Let us watch one ordinary day together, smoke as normal while we look, and see what the dose actually does inside the hour.

I want you to picture a parking meter on a grey street.

You drop a coin in. The needle jumps. You feel paid up, safe, taken care of. You walk away. Within minutes the needle is sliding. Ten minutes, twenty, forty — not to the same second for each of us, but always the same direction. Down. By the time the hour turns you are digging for another coin, not because the street changed, but because the meter only knows how to run out.

That is your day on doses.

The fact is the hit reaches the brain within seconds and the sharp edge of it fades within minutes, so the mind learns to call for another to keep the feeling topped up. We lit at seven and felt settled. By eight the edge was back. We lit at break and felt steady. By the next break the steadiness had thinned. Lunch, afternoon, phone call, kettle, doorstep, night. Coin after coin. Needle up, needle down, needle up, needle down.

Tell me honestly and answer from your own pocket.

Did any single dose lift you above a non-smoker, or did it only bring you back toward normal for minutes?
Did it solve the morning, or did it only quiet the unease the night without had left?
If it truly gave calm, why did the calm always need another coin within the hour?

You know the answer because you have lived the timetable. The dose never paid for the street. It only rented the meter for minutes and guaranteed you would have to pay again. That is the nicotine trap working exactly as built — a hunger manufactured on the hour, then sold back to you as relief.

Once that timetable is seen, you can walk out of it easily, immediately and permanently. Not by force. By seeing there was never a lift, only a fade briefly interrupted.

### THE BITE YOU KEEP SCRATCHING

We all know the bite that itches worse because we scratch it.

You are out in summer. Something nips your ankle. You scratch and for a second it feels marvellous. Done, you think. Fixed. An hour later it itches harder. You scratch harder. By night it is raw and you are still telling yourself scratching helps, because you only remember the second of ease and forget the day of itch that followed.

Smoking is that bite with smoke on it.

The first doses of your life created a faint restlessness where none lived before. Then each new dose quietened that restlessness for minutes while deepening it for hours. We learned to call the second of quiet pleasure. We forgot to count the day of itch that made the quiet feel needed.

Listen to your own voice when the hour calls:

“But my body needs it. You can talk about mind all you like — I feel it in my chest.”

I do not mock that feeling. I felt it for years. I am telling you what it is. What you feel is not hunger or need. It is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug behind the ribs, the complaint of a tiny physical creature as its feed fades. That little stir is the Nipper complaining as it starts to starve. It is small. It is already dying between doses. What makes it roar is not its size. It is the Smokescreen taught you to read that stir as proof you need a dose to cope, to pause, to belong.

Remove the reading and the stir shrinks to what it is. Keep the reading and the stir feels like life itself.

Here is the settled shape of it, seen from your own day. The scratch never healed the bite. The scratch fed the bite and sold you the second of ease as cure.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

### SMALL, BRIEF AND NOT YOU

“But withdrawal will be awful. I dread the hole it leaves.”

Let us give that dread daylight, because dread grows in the dark.

Typically that restless edge begins within hours when the hourly feed stops, sharpens for the first few days, sharpest around day two or three, then eases over the next few weeks until the body forgets the timetable. If you feel ill or you worry, speak to your doctor for your own care. I speak only of belief: that stir is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical peak, not proof the dose was your real self.

Think of your real self for a moment.

Is your real self edgy every forty minutes for life? Were you born checking pockets, counting sticks, stepping out into rain to feed a paper tube? No. The non-smoker in you breathes without thinking, tastes without hurry, sits through a phone call without a hand reaching. The tug is not you. It is a tenant complaining while it is being starved out.

I smoked through colds that rattled my chest and still lit. I smoked through nights I promised my own children I would not. Not because my body demanded torture, but because my mind believed the tug meant I cannot cope, cannot pause, cannot be me without a fix. When the belief went, the tug stayed small and brief and then went. It had no army behind it.

Ask yourself the question that ends it.

If the tug proves the dose gave you something real, why does a non-smoker never feel that tug and never miss what you miss?
If the dose truly calmed you, why were your most “relaxed” days the days you fed most often?

It is the other way around. You do not crave because it gave. You crave because it took. The craving is the taking felt from inside.

### WATCH THE STEP WITHOUT WANTING

Now comes the test your mind is already picturing.

You see them on the step. Coats half on, laughter spilling, smoke curling, someone holding out a lit stick as if holding out friendship itself. Your old mind says they have something you will not have. They look free. You feel left out.

Look again with clear eyes.

Are they relaxed, or are they feeding on the hour because the meter ran down again? Is that laughter coming from the tube, or from the break, the company, the night air, the pause from work — the scene the dose is only sneaking a ride on? Watch the hand, not the smile. Watch it check the pack, tap, borrow, count, step away early, come back tasting of ash. Does that look like pleasure? Or does it look like rent being paid?

We used to stand there ourselves. We remember thinking one would seal the moment. We remember lighting and feeling the tug quiet for minutes while the talk went on without us for a second. The talk was the joy. The break was the joy. The night was the joy. The dose only interrupted the joy to quiet the itch it had planted before we arrived.

You do not need to envy them. You do not need to avoid them. You need to see them.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

**SUMMARY**
- Each dose rises within seconds and thins within minutes, so the day becomes a chain of brief quiet followed by renewed call.
- The quiet never took you above normal; it only returned you toward normal for a short while and guaranteed the next low.
- The restless stir is small and brief, typically sharpest in the first days then easing, not the voice of your real self.
- Belief makes the stir feel like need; when the credit is removed the body sensation loses its meaning.
- Other smokers on the step are paying hourly rent, not enjoying a gift, and their pleasure belongs to the scene, not the stick.
