Chapter 2
ARE YOU REALLY CHOOSING THIS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The half-open garage door, the Febreeze can and mints, the hand that lights before you decide.

*You did not freely choose this life; you were conned into a trap, and two quiet partners have kept you lighting up ever since.*

### THE SMOKER WHO HIDES

You gave me a fair hearing in those first pages. Keep it. Read as a smoker, comfortable and curious, and look with me at one ordinary evening.

Winter. Cold on the step. Your partner is inside with the kids. You tell them you are just taking the rubbish out. You slip into the garage and leave the door half-open for air. You light up fast, puff hard, check over your shoulder. Then you stub it, bury the end deep in the bin, spray Febreeze the hell out of myself — your words — chew two mints, wash your hands cold, and walk back in smiling as if nothing happened.

We have all lived that small pantomime. The closet smoker with the careful routine. The garage door. The window cracked in the car in January. The lie about numbers. “Only five today.”

Now let me ask you as one smoker to another, with warmth and without a flicker of judgment. Does a free person do that?

If cigarettes relax you and please you and help you cope, why hide the pleasure? You do not hide a good meal. You do not Febreeze a cup of tea. You do not lie to your children about a walk in clean air. Choice does not need an alibi.

“I choose to smoke. I enjoy it. Nobody forces me.”

Is that what you are saying to yourself right now? Good. Say it out loud. Hold it up to the garage door and see if it fits.

If you choose it freely, why do you apologize for it with spray and mints? If you enjoy it, why do you hate the hiding? And if you are in charge, why did you need to hide from the very people you love most in order to enjoy it?

There is only one honest answer. A free choice would not make you divide yourself in two — the smoker outside and the actor inside. Something else is moving you.

The fact is you are caught in the nicotine trap. Not weak. Not foolish. Caught. The trap removed your choice long ago and left you the feeling of choice to keep you quiet.

### WHOSE HAND IS THAT

Watch yourself today. Not tomorrow. Today, while you are still smoking exactly as normal.

You finish a call. The phone goes down. Your hand is already reaching. Pack, stick, lighter — one smooth chain before you have decided anything. You watch your own fingers do it as if they belong to someone else.

Or the alarm rings. Your feet have not touched the floor and your mind has already counted: pack, lighter, window. Or the meal ends. Plate back. Chair back. Hand out. No debate. No vote. Movement first, reason after.

If this were choice, would it look like that? Choice pauses. Choice weighs. Choice can wait until evening. What you live with is different. It is a tug that arrives uninvited and a hand that obeys before you speak.

That tug is small. It is not agony. It is not illness. Between doses you feel it as an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug — a faint emptiness, a slight edge, as if something needs to be done and you cannot name what.

You know that feeling well. We all do. We misread it for years as hunger, as nerves, as boredom, as “I want one.” It is none of those things. It is the tiny complaint of a tiny creature.

I call it the Nipper. It lives on nicotine and complains when it is not fed. Feed the Nipper and the tug quiets for a few minutes. Starve it and it whines for a little while and then dies. It is trivial, already dying, already beaten. It has no power over a clear mind.

So why does so small a tug run so large a life? Because behind it stands something bigger.

I call it the Smokescreen. It is the fog of brainwashing we all breathed in since childhood — that smoking relaxes, steadies, rewards, belongs with drink and friends and breaks — that tells you the tug means “you need a cigarette to cope.” The Nipper creates the tug. The Smokescreen tells you what the tug means. Together they keep you lighting doses you never freely chose.

Notice I name them only in passing. You do not need to fight them. You do not need to study them. You only need to see that choice did not put them there. A con put them there, and seeing the con is the beginning of walking out.

### IF YOU LOVE IT, WHY DO YOU HATE IT

“I love smoking. I hate being a smoker.”

Do you recognize yourself in that sentence? The moment I light up I’m in heaven, but feel shitty afterwards. So whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker. Those are not my words. They are the words of smokers like you and me, spoken honestly about the same ten minutes.

Hold that honesty gently, because it proves you are not lying and you are not blind. You feel a flash of heaven at the light. Of course you do. After an hour without, the tug has grown, the hand shakes a little, the mind nags — and the first puff quiets the Nipper. Relief floods in and you call it pleasure. Then minutes later comes the aftertaste, the stale mouth, the faint shame, the knowledge you will need to do it again in an hour. Heaven, then shitty. Love the light, hate the life.

Now ask the trap question. If the cigarette gave you a real pleasure, would the pleasure last only the lighting and then turn to shame? Would a genuine friend lift you for three minutes and chain you for the other twenty-three hours? And would you need to tell yourself “one won’t hurt” about a true friend?

You know the answer. The heaven is not a gift added to your day. It is the brief quiet of the tug the last dose created. It never fixed the discomfort. It only fed it for a moment so it could return.

You will say, “But when anything stressful happens, a cigarette takes care of it.” I believe you feel that. I felt it a thousand times. Phone down, hand out, puff, and a sense of being taken care of washes over you. I do not mock that feeling. It is real as feeling. What is false is the credit. The problem on the desk sits untouched while you puff. The bill, the argument, the waiting — nothing was taken care of except the tug. We will look that calm straight in the eye very soon, in your ordinary day, puff by puff. For now see only this: a free choice would not need to take credit for calming a storm it helped create.

It is the other way around. The cigarette does not solve the unease. The unease follows the cigarette, and the next cigarette briefly mutes it.

That is why will and scolding never touched it. You were not choosing badly. You were conned completely — first by phoney glamour before your first puff, then by your own body misreading its tug as proof of benefit. Who could blame you for that? I was conned in exactly the same way. We all were.

Picture it clearly and the shame falls away. You are not a weak chooser. You are a decent person caught by a clever trap that removed choice and called it habit. The moment you see the bars you stop polishing them.

### YOU ARE NOT THE TUG

Here is the reframe that changes everything, stated flat because it is true.

You never decided to need doses to get through stress and meals and breaks and nights out. A child does not dream of Febreeze and garage doors. You were led in by curiosity and belonging, kept in by a tiny tug and a thick fog of stories, and now you smoke to quiet the very emptiness smoking keeps alive.

Does a chooser smoke in the rain and call it freedom? Does a chooser count the pack at midnight and feel panic? Does a chooser promise the mirror “never again” and light within the hour? You have lived those questions. You already know what they prove.

You are not your tug. You are the person watching the tug. The tug is small and foreign and dying. The fog is old and borrowed and thin. You are the clear mind underneath, already able to breathe and taste and cope without a dose to pay for every finished act.

Feel the relief in that. There is nothing wrong with you to fix. There is only a trap to see and an escape to enjoy.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

**SUMMARY**
- Hiding to smoke proves this stopped being free choice long ago.
- Your hand moves before you decide because a small tug prompts and old stories interpret it.
- The heaven at lighting is a brief quiet of that tug, followed by the stale cost that brings the next tug.
- Feeling taken care of after a puff is real as feeling, but the problem itself sits untouched.
- You were conned, not weak, and seeing the trap is the first step to walking out of it.
