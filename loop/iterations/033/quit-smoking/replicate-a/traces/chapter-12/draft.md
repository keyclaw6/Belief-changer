Chapter 12
YOUR LAST CIGARETTE

IN THIS CHAPTER — The quiet table, the stained fingers, the bitter ash and the stubbed end.

*Your last cigarette takes nothing from you — it closes the con and leaves you free from the second you stub it out.*

### ARE YOU READY TO WALK OUT?

You have seen through the bargain for one and the lie of tomorrow. There is no careful dose and no kind delay left to defend.

So I ask you plainly, as one escapee to one reader about to escape. Are you champing at the bit to be done? Do you feel the excitement rising under the nerves, the sense that you want this finished now?

If you do, wonderful. We will finish it together in the next few minutes.

If you do not yet feel that lift, do not summon force to cover doubt. Put this chapter down for an hour, start this book again from the beginning and read it with clear eyes. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

I say that with warmth for you and with contempt only for the trick that taught you to doubt clear seeing. We were all conned, you and I, into calling paper and smoke a friend. No weak character brought you here. A clever trap brought you here. A clear mind walks out of it.

And see where you stand. You smoke with the window cracked and the smell in your hair. You count money turned to ash. You step outside to miss the laugh inside. You light the next fix to quiet the edginess the last fix left behind.

“What if I miss it terribly? What if life feels flat without the stick in my hand?”

I hear that voice because it once spoke in my head too. Let me answer it with your own life, not with lecture.

If the dose gave you pleasure, why did every day end with throat raw and chest heavy and the wish you had smoked less? If it gave you calm, why were you edgy again an hour later? If it gave you company, why did you so often smoke it alone, hidden, hurried, a little ashamed of the hiding?

There is only one honest answer. It gave nothing. It took and called the brief quiet of its own taking relief.

The fact is you have nothing to lose and everything to gain by finishing this now.

You stop like this: easily, immediately and permanently.

### THE LAST DOSE WITH CLEAR EYES

Bring one last cigarette to a quiet table. Not a party. Not a celebration smoke with drink in hand. An ordinary dose in ordinary light, the kind you have smoked ten thousand times without looking.

This time look.

Light it and watch it as a free mind watches a con, not as a lover saying goodbye. Feel the paper. See the yellow stain blooming on the filter where your fingers hold it. Taste the first drag all the way through — hot, papery, bitter at the back of the throat. Smell the smoke on your fingers as it burns. Watch the ash lengthen, grey and dead, and the ember creeping toward your lips. Smoke it to the end you always hated, the stale, scorched end that burns hot and tastes foul and makes you cough a little.

Keep your eyes open. This is not enjoyment. This was never enjoyment.

Did that dose add anything to the room? Did it make the table kinder, the air sweeter, your hands more capable, your mind clearer? Or did it do what it always did — feed the Nipper for a few minutes so the faint edginess dipped, while breath shortened and mouth soured and fingers stained?

You know the answer because you have just lived it with clear eyes.

Now stub it out. Grind it hard. Watch the smoke curl and die in the tray.

That small crushed tube in the glass is what held your mornings hostage and timed your breaks and priced your clothes with smell. Look how little it is when you are no longer bowing to it. A paper tube. A chemical whisper. Ash.

This is the moment the nicotine trap closes behind you for good.

No sadness here. No farewell to a friend. Rejoice. You have just smoked the last dose you will ever need, and you smoked it with sight. You will never need another because you see there was never anything to need.

### WHAT YOUR BODY WILL DO AFTER

Let me tell you plainly what happens in the body after the last dose, so no faint tug can frighten you back inside.

The body learned to call for the next dose when the last one faded. For most smokers that call sharpens for a day or two or three without topping, then softens over the next couple of weeks until it is forgotten. It can begin within hours as a faint restlessness under the skin. Not everyone feels it the same way. If you live with illness or feel seriously unwell, speak to your own clinician for care.

Do not mistake that faint call for yourself. It is not grief. It is not proof you lost something real. It is a small creature starving, already dying from the second you stop feeding the Nipper.

We all knew that tug for years and misread it as hunger for smoke. It was hunger made by smoke. You topped it a hundred times a day and called the topping pleasure. Now you let it die once, and dying is quick when the mind no longer feeds it with belief.

When you feel it, you will not mourn. You will smile and feel relief rise, clean and physical, that you do not have to step outside to answer it. You breathe easy through it. You drink water. You move. You get on with enjoying your life while it fades.

The fact is the tug is trivial and brief when the belief is gone. The belief was the engine. The body whisper was only the rattle.

That is why I do not want you changing from fear of what smoke does to breath and heart and years. Fright never freed anyone. Fright makes the next dose feel like rescue. See the taking clearly and move because escape is marvellous. Celebrate the clean air returning to your chest. Celebrate taste coming back to food. Celebrate mornings that smell of nothing and money that stays in your pocket. Freedom is immediate gain, not a future prize.

### THE TWO DOORS THAT ARE NOT DOORS

After freedom begins, the trap will try two doors. Both are painted on the wall. Know them now so they never open.

The first door comes on a bad day. A row. A bill. A phone call that shakes you. The hand moves before thought and the old voice says, “Just this once, to take care of it.”

Hear whose voice that is. The dose never touched the papers on the desk. It touched only its own tug for minutes and left the problem exactly where it was. A bad day does not need a dose. It needs you present, breathing deep, handling what can be handled, letting the rest pass. Pity the smoker outside the door trying to smoke the row away while the row waits inside. You are not being deprived of rescue. He is.

The second door comes on a good day. A wedding. A drink. Laughter and night air and an outstretched pack with kindness in it. The voice says, “One won’t hurt. Prove you are free by handling one.”

That proof is the trap’s favourite lie. One after freedom does not test whether you are cured. It re-teaches the call you let die. The throat remembers. The head lightens the wrong way. By morning the faint edginess hums again and the hand moves before the mind has decided. One keeps the chain alive.

Free people do not prove freedom by refilling the ashtray. They prove it by enjoying the night without paying for it — food tasting clean, breath running deep, talk heard whole, clothes smelling of nothing in the morning.

And hear the kind part. A confused puff does not make you chained again by magic and does not prove you are weak. Note what happened, rejoice that you see the pump primes fast, and take no second to argue with it. Guard the seeing and the seeing guards you.

Ask the trap questions and answer from life, not from thirst.

If one could satisfy, why has one never satisfied? If rescue lived in smoke, why did every rescued hour need rescuing again within the hour?

It is back to front. It always was. Seeing that is freedom.

### THE VOW THAT MAKES YOU FREE NOW

You are ready. You have looked at the last dose with clear eyes. You know the body edge is short and fading. You know both doors.

So make the vow now, quietly and solemnly, with joy under it. Say it aloud if you can.

I am no longer a smoker. I will never smoke another dose. I am free.

Do not wait to feel free tomorrow or after some count of days. Freedom is not earned by waiting. You are free from the second the last dose dies, because there is nothing left to want. The slavery ends when the belief ends, and the belief has ended.

From this second, whenever the thought of a cigarette drifts across your mind — a break, a meal-end, a laugh, a pang — you will not fight it and you will not mourn it. You will catch it with a lift in the chest and answer it with the thought you now own for life.

Think, mean, feel: YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Say it silently on the bus. Say it laughing with friends. Say it on a hard afternoon and on a wonderful night. Let every old cue become a bell that rings relief. The thought that once meant hunger now means escape. The dead enemy does not get tears. He gets rejoicing.

Congratulations. You have done the most exciting thing a trapped smoker ever does. You walked out into clean air with eyes open. Breathe deep. Taste the hour. Hold your head high among smokers and non-smokers alike, not with pride that puffs itself, but with the quiet certainty of someone who was conned and will never be conned again.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

**SUMMARY**
- The last cigarette is smoked with eyes open on stain and ash, then stubbed, and freedom starts at that second.
- The faint restlessness after stopping is a short fading body call for most, not proof smoke gave anything.
- A bad day never needs rescue smoke because smoke never touched the problem it claimed to calm.
- One dose after freedom re-teaches the call and rebuilds the chain, so final means final.
- Any future thought of smoking gets answered with joy, not struggle, and life goes on free from now.
