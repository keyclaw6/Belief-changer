Chapter 14
FREE FOR GOOD

IN THIS CHAPTER — the clean pocket, the morning window, twelve lines on one page, the empty ashtray on the table, the open street in clean air

*You lost nothing to get free, and there is nothing to miss — what lies ahead is clean, easy and wonderful.*

### WHAT THIS WEEK HAS ALREADY PROVED

You know now because you have lived it.

Mornings without the fee. The kettle loud, the window open an inch, breath going straight to the bottom without a toll. The shop queue without the maths. The break without the ember beating you to it. The night run whole from first laugh to last, with no coat fetched and no sentence left half-finished at the table.

That is not theory. That is your own chest, your own tongue, your own hands proving it day after day.

We spent years lighting inside a con and calling it choice. We paid to feel nearly normal and called the pause pleasure. We stood out in rain to miss the wedding and called ourselves sociable. It was never choice. It was a trap fed by hunger and dressed as help.

Did smoke ever put light in the window? Did it put taste in the tea? Did it settle the row, finish the work, deepen the laugh? You know the honest answer because you have checked it without smoke now. The work was good. The pause was sweet. The friends were warm. The dose only ever sneaked a ride and signed the credit to itself.

You came to this book braced for misery. Remember you have nothing to lose and everything to gain. That was my promise on the first page, and you have watched it come true in ordinary hours. No doom arrived. Only clean air arrived.

You escaped easily, immediately and permanently. Not after a sentence served. Not after proving yourself. The second the seeing turned clear you were free, and each free day since has made the seeing clearer.

I smoked for thirty years and lit before I spoke. I know the dressing-gown search, the pocket pat, the huddle in wind with eyes on the clock. I also know the morning I stubbed the last one with eyes open and walked out laughing at how little there was to leave. I tell you with total certainty as one escapee to another: you have stepped out of the nicotine trap for good.

Rejoice in that. Marvellous is not too big a word for breathing easy.

### WHAT TO KEEP IN YOUR POCKET

You do not need to carry this book. Carry what it taught you to say.

Whenever the old whisper floats past — thin as smoke from another garden — you already own the answer. You do not debate it. You do not weigh whether you are brave enough to miss out. You smile because you see whose voice it is, and you let freedom answer.

If the thought says one would take care of stress, answer plain: you will never feed the Nipper again. It is dead because it is starved, small and already dying, and no fresh dose will be thrown to it.

If the thought says you miss the little friend, see clear through the Smokescreen now. There was never a friend there. There was brainwashing that taught you to read hunger as help, and a seller who packed that hunger in paper and sold it back to you.

If a faint stir brushes the ribs — an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug — do not mistake it for you. It is an echo thinning to nothing because the mind no longer feeds it. Breathe easy through it and get on with the hour. Free people live in that quiet all day.

And now the tug-of-war of fear has no rope left to pull. Fear of failing without it? Fear of succeeding without yourself? Both ropes belonged to the same trick. You are not failing. You are not losing yourself. You are breathing, tasting, present, whole.

Hold these as plain settled facts, not as hopes:

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

You know the cost it charged because you lived it — money signed over, mornings veiled, nights cut in half, a body left stale, breathless and chained. That is past now. That is a coat handed back, not a limb lost.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You have done it. Ease was the contract and ease was kept. When doubt knocks, do not reopen the book to argue again. Speak the line that closes it and walk on lighter.

Catch the wisp, lift, rejoice. You smile and think, YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

That is protection without effort. Not guarding a behaviour with fear, but guarding a belief with joy.

### THE PAGE TO PHOTOGRAPH

Keep this page where you can see it. These are not rules to serve. They are the twelve things that freed you, in the words you first heard them.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Give this book a fair hearing and it will free you.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

3. BEGIN BY FEELING GREAT TO BE ESCAPING
There is no doom here, only freedom ahead.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
All you have to do is what this book asks.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the method, not you.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

10. MEET THE BEST CIGARETTE HEAD-ON
Let the favourite prove it gives nothing.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

Photograph them. Fold them into the pocket that no longer holds a box. If a hard hour comes, read them with a grin and remember: you followed them once and they freed you — they will hold you free for the rest of your life.

### THE ASHTRAY WAS ALREADY EMPTY

I want to leave you with one last picture, and it is the truest in this book.

Think of the edge of the table where you stubbed that last one with eyes open on the ash and the stain and the stale end. No ceremony of sorrow. Only disgust seen clear and joy coming up underneath. You crushed it out and the room did not go dark. The room got brighter.

Look now at the ashtray you left behind.

It sits empty. It was always empty. The ash, the ends, the sour smell, the money burnt, the mornings priced — none of that was in the glass. The glass held nothing. You were the one who kept refilling it.

Do you see? The pleasure was never in the thing. The hunger was made by the refilling, and the refilling was taught by a lie. Stop the refilling and there is nothing left to miss. The ashtray was already empty — you were the one who kept refilling it, and now you have put the habit down like a borrowed coat that was never yours.

That coat never fitted you. It smelled of other people's money. It weighed the shoulders and yellowed the fingers and made you step out of your own evenings to wear it. You wore it because you were told you needed it. Take it off and you are not bare. You are yourself, clean-shirted, breathing deep, hands free.

Pity the smoker still outside paying for pauses in his own hunger. Warmth for the person, always. Contempt only for the trick that holds him. You were there. You know the timetable. You are not deprived of something precious. He is still refilling an empty dish.

Do not preach. Do not chase friends with arguments. Hold your head high among smokers and non-smokers alike, breathe easy beside them, taste fully beside them, stay whole beside them. Freedom noticed does more than freedom announced.

### GO LIVE FREE AND CELEBRATE

There is no second curriculum. That is the beauty of it.

Change nothing else in your life to prove you are free. Eat as you eat. Drink as you drink. Work as you work. Sleep as you sleep. Take the same street, the same shop, the same chair, the same laugh — and live them without paying. Your body returns to breathing and tasting on its own. It knows freedom without being taught.

Think how wonderful to want nothing you have to time. Think how marvellous to miss nothing you have to step out for. Think how exciting to meet bad days present and good days whole. The weight off the shoulders is not a future prize. It is now, this ordinary day with washing in the machine and rain at the window and supper to make.

A bad day will come, because life comes. Meet it present. Drink cold water. Handle the next small thing. Let the rest wait till it can be handled. Then feel the lift no dose ever gave: I met that without paying. Keep that lift. It grows each time you feel it.

A good day will come too. Eat slow. Talk long. Stay to the end of the story. Laugh without listening for the clock. In the morning your clothes will smell of nothing and your mouth will taste of sleep and coffee, and the stairs will take you without bargaining.

Weeks will stack into seasons. Spring windows wide. Summer nights long in the garden. Autumn bonfires smelling only of wood. Winter air sharp in a chest that loves it. Each season will find you further from the old arithmetic, more at home in clean air, until the whole con feels like a coat borrowed long ago and handed back for good.

So get on with enjoying your life. Take mornings deep. Take food slow. Take breaks wide. Take nights long. Let money stay. Let breath stay. Let laughter run unbroken because you never leave to miss it.

Whenever the wisp drifts past tomorrow, next month, next year, you know what to do. Catch it, lift, rejoice. There is nothing to debate and nothing to mourn. The dead enemy gets no tears.

You smile and think, YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

**SUMMARY**
- Freedom was not earned over time; it arrived the moment the false credit collapsed, and ordinary days prove it holds.
- Every old pleasure was the scene itself — morning, pause, meal, laugh — and smoke only charged admission to what was already yours.
- The physical whisper is small and dying, and the belief that fed it is cleared, so no stir can turn you back.
- The twelve lines on one page hold the whole escape in pocket form for any future doubt.
- The ashtray sits empty because it always was empty; the coat is handed back because it was never yours.
- Life ahead is lived the same and felt whole — breathing easy, tasting fully, present for bad days and whole for good ones.
- Whenever a cigarette thought floats past, answer with relief and keep walking: YIPPEE! I'M FREE!
