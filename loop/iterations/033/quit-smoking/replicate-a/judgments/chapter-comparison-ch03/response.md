MOVE E14-M1: PARTIAL — Carr opens on surrender: quitting costs nothing real, and the only thing you “give up” is the fear of going without; ours threads relief-not-loss and “nothing to lose” through the benefit audit but never lands that fear-of-deprivation inversion as its own enacted turn, so the reader gets the conclusion without Carr’s named fear frame. Evidence: “the marvellous relief of finding that what you feared losing never gave you anything to lose” and “I had feared I must surrender a friend. I saw there was no friend to surrender, only arms that had held me under while I thanked them.”

MOVE E14-M2: PRESENT — Carr separates the pleasant occasion from the burning paper, showing meals, company, and social ease were already there and the cigarette only interrupted or took credit; ours performs the same credit-stripping across taste, belonging, boredom, finished work, supper, pause, and friends until the situation is named as giver and the dose as thief. Evidence: “The situation gives. The dose takes the credit. Food, rest, friendship, finished work — all real gifts, all yours already — and the Smokescreen points at the light and says, that good moment, that was the cigarette.”

MOVE E14-M3: PRESENT — Carr restores the pre-smoking baseline: non-smokers do not suffer the void the drug creates, and coping without it is what you already had; ours repeatedly places the reader back in that baseline before the trap taught the reach. Evidence: “You did not need to either, before the trap taught your hands to reach whenever nothing was asked of you” and “There is only normal, which is what non-smokers live all day without lighting anything.”
