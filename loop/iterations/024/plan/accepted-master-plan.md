# Master Plan — Quit Sugar
`production-books/quit-sugar/master-plan.md`

## 1. Book Core

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of refined / added sugar and junk carbs — the craving-snacking loop, not nutrition pedantry.

**Reader:** An adult who grazes, tops up and binges on sweet fixes every day, feels foggy and ashamed about loss of control, has failed diets and moderation rules, and suspects the whole approach is wrong but still believes life would be duller without something sweet.

**Load-bearing false belief:** Bad sugar is a genuine pleasure, treat and lift that makes life sweeter and without which I would be deprived.

**Through-line:** You were conned into wanting something that never gave you anything; see what it never did for you, starve the tiny dip and the lie that feeds it, and walk out excited.

**Format:** Full-length Carr book, ~61,200 words, 16 chapters. Front invitation, middle demolitions on installed ground, vow as last ordinary sweet-eating occasion, one ordinary-life chapter, short photographable recap.

**Fork decisions:**
- Fork 1 — Full Carr personification. The trivial physical loop is THE LITTLE SUGAR MITE. The belief-system that feeds it is THE BIG SWEET CON. Both starved, never battled.
- Fork 2 — Total cessation inside the redefined line. No moderation of BAD SUGAR. Commanded with cheerful certainty.
- Fork 3 — Hard facts at full Carr force, flat and frightening where true, then relief. Never quitting out of fear. No literature-review texture. Limits honoured by not overclaiming.
- Fork 4 — Two villains hit hard: the engineered sugar trap and its profiteers, and THE WILLPOWER METHOD that kept the reader in it. Warm to the person always.
- Fork 5 — Change nothing else. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own via body, instinct, real food. No replacement system, no void-filling programme.

**Redefinition:**
When this book says sugar it means BAD SUGAR = added / refined sugars plus junk-processed carbs and starchy snacks engineered to spike and crash you, in any sweetened drink, packet, bar, biscuit, cake, cereal or dessert-form dose. Natural sweetness inside real food as nature packed it is not the target and is not counted. The CAPS name carries the definition thereafter.

**Margin-for-error doctrine:** Your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip, but your mind cannot cope with a deliberate exception. An accident revives nothing unless the Big Sweet Con is allowed back to reinterpret it as proof you need it. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. Seatbelt logic: there for accidents, never for driving erratically.

**Clinical / eating-disorder safety perimeter:** This book does not diagnose, treat diabetes, adjust medication, prescribe weight-loss, or treat eating disorders. Anyone with diabetes, on glucose / insulin / appetite / mood medication, pregnant, with a history of eating disorder, or with active binge-purge / severe restriction, reads only with their clinician in charge of medical decisions and uses crisis support if distressed. No fasting, purging, compensatory restriction or medication change is instructed. All medical specifics route to CLIN-SAFE and to evidence-ledger safety limits, never into instruction wording.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene:** A sunny café garden, unhurried morning, people you love, a beautiful cake shared without rush — the proof that sugar must be pleasure.

**Destination state:** Whenever the thought of bad sugar arises the reader feels relief and joy that they no longer do it, certain there is nothing to miss, pitying users still caught, free from day one.

**Saved ending reframe (fresh, appears only in final movement):** Do not mourn the death of a friend; rejoice at the death of an enemy who never once did anything for you.

**EV-01 — lived fuel misread**
- finding: Recovered writers describe the afternoon sweet top-up as a short lift inevitably followed by a fast drop.
- research units: LEU-001
- source: B-003
- grade: lived, P-04 loop testimony
- scope: mid-afternoon dip, yo-yoers reaching for quick sweet
- reader line: "I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop."
- permitted inference: the lift–crash cycle is self-described by recovered writers; the "fuel" misreads a spike-then-drop as genuine energy.
- prohibited inference: that every reader has clinically low blood sugar, or that sugar is ever real "fuel."
- empirical limit: lived self-report, no glucose measurement
- safety limit: no medical diagnosis claims from these lived-experience quotes. See CLIN-SAFE.
- cited by: C-04

**EV-05 — withdrawal dip**

- prohibited inference: human physiologic sugar withdrawal syndrome

- safety limit: describe dip as tiny and passing, never as medical withdrawal.

**EV-12 — crash mechanism without medicalizing slumps**

- safety limit: use to keep honesty, not to re-open decision.
- cited by: C-11

**EV-16 — cue-driven lingering**
