Chapter 10
WHO BUILT THIS HUNGER

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Bright supermarket aisle under hard white light.
Walls of packets shouting for a tired shopper.
A body flashing warning lights in plain sight.

*You never built this hunger — it was built for you, and your body has been telling the truth all along.*

THE AISLE THAT HUNGERED FIRST

I have stood where you stand, basket in hand, telling myself I came in for bread.

We walk in hungry, tired, ordinary. The light is hard and white. The shelves rise on both sides, shouting in red and gold. Shots of sweet at a child's eye height. Doses by the till for the queue. Fixes built to be seen, grabbed, torn open before the car door shuts. You had no plan to want them. You wanted them the moment you saw them.

That is not accident. That is design.

Armies are paid to make that happen. Flavour men in white coats mixing sweet against fat against crunch until the bite sings and the hand goes back for more. Packet men painting joy on the wrapper. Shelf men paying for the exact centimetre where your eye falls. Money men counting that a craving you never chose becomes a purchase you cannot quite resist explaining.

Ask yourself, plainly:

Did you invent that craving in the aisle?
Did you ask for a body that begs at three o'clock?
Did you choose to hear, I need something RIGHT NOW, in your own voice?

There is only one honest answer. We were conned long before we lifted the packet. That shining aisle is the Sugar Trap working exactly as designed.

You are not weak for hearing the shout. You are a normal shopper hearing a shout built for you. I feel anger at the shout, never at you. Pity the crowd shuffling past with baskets full of bright promises. They are not indulging. They are being harvested.

And the worst trick? They taught us to call the harvest ours. "I just like sweet things." "just a treat." Do you like them, or were you taught to like them by men who profit when you do? It was never yours. It was installed.

THE WARNING LIGHTS YOU CALLED LIFE

Now look at what your body has been saying while the packets kept shouting.

We know this day. Heavy eyes at three. A quick dose for fuel. A lift, then a slide. You end the afternoon tired, run-down and foggy and call it life.

Do you really believe that fog is age, or work, or character? How many afternoons must the same pattern repeat before we admit who ordered it?

This is where I must speak flatly, because warmth without truth is no help to you.

Across large groups, those who take a lot of added sweet carry more heart and stroke trouble later — through an overloaded liver, raised pressure, simmering irritation inside, appetite signals drowned out. I am not diagnosing you — and if you live with diabetes, take medicines, or carry a condition, keep your clinician in charge of those decisions.

In large groups of men followed over years, those who take more sweets are the ones who more often feel low and flat later. Not lifted. Lowered. The daily treat predicts a darker mood, not relief.

Free sugars feed the plaque acid that rots teeth. Not every single sweet means a hole — brushing, fluoride, care all matter — but the acid is real, the decay is real, children and grown-ups sit in dentist chairs because of it.

Teeth. Heart. Mood. Afternoon fog. These are not separate curses of modern life. They are warning lights on the same dashboard.

I do not tell you this to make you stop from fear. I want the opposite. See the load for what it is, then feel the relief flood in: you do not have to carry it to be happy. The fright belongs to the trap that built it. The freedom belongs to you the moment you see through it. You did not choose it. It chose you.

YOU WERE NEVER THE ONLY ONE

Here is the objection I hear, in your tired shopper voice:

"But everyone eats this way. If it were a con, would so many fall for it? That must mean my cravings are just mine."

I love that question because it frees you.

Counted in large groups, about fourteen in a hundred adults and twelve in a hundred children report this same caught feeling around rich food — numbers that sit alongside drink and cigarettes. Not freaks. Not the weak. Neighbours, drivers, nurses, teachers. All saying the same quiet thing: once I start, I feel I cannot easily stop.

Does that sound like private liking? Or like a common snare?

If the craving were truly yours, why does it speak in the same words in every mouth? Why the same drawer, the same evening packet on the lap, the same morning promise to be good? Private tastes differ. Traps repeat.

We did not all invent the same hunger. The hunger was manufactured once, then sold to us all as personality.

Once you see the manufacture, the blame lifts off you and lands where it belongs. You were never a weak shopper in a free market. You were a decent person walked through a shouting aisle built by professionals, with a body flashing honest lights you were taught to ignore.

Let the anger go upward to the builders. Keep the warmth for the shoppers — yourself included. Look at them with clear eyes and you will not envy them. You will pity the queue and rejoice that you are stepping out of it.

So hear my instruction now, while the packets are still shouting and your sight is still fresh:

11. AVOID THE INFLUENCE OF OTHER SUGAR USERS WHILE YOU READ.
Their brainwashing is catching until yours is gone.

Eat as normal. Meet no one for sweet counsel. Lend your ears to no bragging, no sighing, no kind offer of just one. Not because people are bad — they are as conned as we were — but because their talk replays the old lie while you are still washing it out. Guard your eyes for these few chapters and you will walk the aisle next week and hear the shout as noise, not as orders.

**SUMMARY**
- The craving in the aisle was designed by professionals for profit, not born in you.
- Walls of bright packets, prices, positions and recipes work together to trigger want on sight.
- Afternoon fog, low mood later, bodily load and tooth decay are warning lights, not personal failings.
- Millions report the same caught pattern, which proves a common snare rather than private weakness.
- Seeing the manufacture lifts blame off you and places anger where it belongs, leaving relief.
