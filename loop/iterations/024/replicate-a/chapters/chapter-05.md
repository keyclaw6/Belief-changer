Chapter 5
EATING AS IT WAS MEANT TO BE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A hungry morning kitchen before anything is eaten.
A plate of real food, eaten slowly to satisfaction.
Hunger returning as a friend.
What the body does quietly without a sweet fix.

*Hunger answered by real food is pleasure itself — you never needed BAD SUGAR for it.*

The Forgotten Signal

You have watched the whole day slide downhill on sweet fixes. Now let me show you what was underneath it all along.

When did you last feel truly hungry?

Not flat. Not headachy and irritable at three o'clock. Not that gnawing restlessness that sends your hand to a packet in the evening. I mean clean, honest hunger. An empty stomach in the morning. A mouth that waters. A body that says plainly: food now, please.

Most of us in this trap cannot remember. We graze. We top up. We pick. We keep something sweet moving through us from mid-morning to midnight, and we call that staying fuelled. The result is that we never arrive at a meal hungry and we never leave it satisfied. We live in between.

And then we say:

“Without something sweet, eating would be bland. I would just be hungry all the time.”

I understand that fear. Of course you feel it. You were taught that hunger is suffering and sweet is what makes food worth eating.

It is the other way around.

The grazing did not remove hunger. It spoiled it. It kept you from ever feeling the good hunger that makes eating a joy, and from ever feeling the deep satisfaction that ends a meal by itself.

Ask yourself honestly:

When you eat sweet fixes all day, do you ever sit down truly hungry and rise truly satisfied?

When you top up every hour, does food taste better across the week, or duller?

And did a child waiting hungry for dinner ever need a packet to enjoy his food?

There is only one honest answer. The trap took the pleasure out of eating and then sold it back to you in a wrapper.

A Morning In The Kitchen

Let me take you into an ordinary morning, lived as it was meant to be.

You wake hungry. Really hungry. Your stomach is empty. You have not eaten since last night. No fix in the night. No packet by the bed.

You go into the kitchen. Light at the window. Cold floor under your feet. You put on eggs. Perhaps some fruit. Bread. Cheese. Plain, real food of the kind you have always liked — nothing clever, nothing measured, nothing from a rule book.

You sit down and eat slowly.

The first bite is marvellous. Not because you are being good. Because you are hungry. The hunger makes it marvellous. You taste it. You chew it. You feel it land. You go on eating until something quiet and certain happens in you — you have had enough. Not stuffed. Not regretful. Satisfied. The plate is left with pleasure, not policed. You get up and get on with enjoying your life.

No counting. No bargaining. No voice saying:

“I’ve managed to be good this week, so I deserve a little extra.”

No voice saying:

“I need something RIGHT NOW.”

Just appetite, met and ended.

We have all known that feeling and forgotten it. We were born knowing it. Watch any hungry child eat real food he likes. He eats with his whole face. He stops when he stops. He does not negotiate with himself. He does not need a sweet finish to make the meal complete. His body tells him, and he hears it.

That hearing is still in you. Grazing drowned it out. It did not remove it.

Your Body Already Knows

Here is the settled truth, and I want you to hear it with relief, not fear.

You were conned into believing that without BAD SUGAR you would be empty, deprived of something you must import to keep going. Your body already keeps itself fuelled from real food without any sweet dose coming in.

If you live with diabetes or take medicines, keep your clinician in charge of that side while we talk appetite.

I am not asking you to follow a diet. I am not asking you to weigh, measure, forbid, or earn your food. There is no plan here. There is no list of good and bad meals beyond the one line this book drew at the start. This is not control. This is the end of control.

Think of how faithfully your body has signalled through all of this. It told you when the fixes left you flat. It told you when you were not hungry and not satisfied at once. It told you in the evening fog and the morning heaviness. It was never broken. It was overruled, hour after hour, by doses that shouted louder than hunger.

Remove the shouting and what returns is not emptiness. What returns is guidance.

Hunger tells you when to begin. Satisfaction tells you when to end. Taste tells you what you like. That is the whole system. It needed no instruction book before the trap arrived, and it needs none now.

Do you see what that means? You lose nothing by letting BAD SUGAR leave the picture. The fuel, the flavour, the joy of eating — they were never in the packet. They were in you.

Trust your body, your instinct, real food.

That is not a slogan. That is what you did that morning in the kitchen without thinking. You were hungry, you ate what you liked, you stopped when you were satisfied. No method. No battle. Just an animal pleasure doing what it was built to do.

Eat What You Want

“But will I still enjoy food?”

You will enjoy it more than you have in years.

BAD SUGAR never improved your taste. It dulled it. Sweeten every hour and everything begins to taste the same — sweet, then flat. The tongue gets bored. The stomach never rests. The appetite never clears to make room for desire.

Eat hungry and the dullest plain food sings. Eat to satisfaction and you walk away free until hunger calls again. That rise and fall — empty, then full, then empty again in its own time — is the rhythm pleasure lives on. The trap flattened it into a permanent neither-hungry-nor-full where nothing truly pleases.

Try it as an investigator today, while you still use exactly as normal otherwise. Eat one real meal hungry. No topping up just before. Sit down. Eat slowly what you truly like. Notice when the satisfaction arrives. Notice how certain it is. No packet ever gave you that certainty. Packets give you the opposite — the open, unfinished feeling that asks for one more.

You are not learning a new way to eat. You are remembering the old one.

Eat what you want and never crave BAD SUGAR again.

That is not a boast. That is what happens when hunger does its job. Real food becomes your favourite because it answers a real need and then lets you go. The dose never let you go. It answered nothing and kept you coming back.

This is wonderful news. You do not have to fix your appetite. You do not have to carry it. You have only to hear it again.

4. EAT WHEN YOU ARE HUNGRY AND STOP WHEN YOU ARE SATISFIED.
Trust hunger to do its job.

**SUMMARY**
- Clean hunger is not suffering; it is what makes eating pleasurable.
- Grazing on sweet fixes kept you from ever being truly hungry or truly satisfied.
- A real meal eaten hungry and finished at satisfaction satisfies by itself.
- Your body keeps itself fuelled from real food; letting BAD SUGAR leave removes nothing you need.
- Appetite returns on its own when the hourly topping-up stops shouting over it.
