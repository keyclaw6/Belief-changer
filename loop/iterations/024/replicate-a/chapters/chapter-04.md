Chapter 4
THE LIFT THAT DROPS YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A fluorescent afternoon and heavy eyes at the desk.
A drawer, a packet of biscuits and a quick fix.
Hour by hour to a foggy evening on the sofa.
A clear look at what the lift really was.

*That sweet lift never gave you energy at all — it started the slide that took it away.*

Three O'Clock

You know this hour too well.

It is three in the afternoon. The light is flat and fluorescent. Your eyes feel heavy. Your shoulders feel heavy. The work in front of you has gone blurry at the edges. And your hand is already moving toward the drawer where the biscuits live.

We have all lived that moment. We sit there telling ourselves we are being sensible. We need fuel. We need sugary somethings to get me through. We need, as you say to yourself in those words:

“I need something RIGHT NOW or I will not get through this afternoon.”

I do not blame you for that thought. Of course you think it. You were taught to think it. Every packet, every advert, every tired colleague reaching at the same hour taught you the same lesson: sweet means energy, and tired means you are empty.

But look at what actually happens.

You unwrap the dose. You eat it quickly, hardly tasting the first one, then tasting the second one too much. For five, perhaps ten minutes, there is a brightening. The heaviness lifts a fraction. You sit up a little straighter. You think: there, that helped. That was needed.

Watch what happens next.

By four o'clock there are wrappers on the desk and a faint restlessness in you. The brightness has gone. The heaviness is back, only a little thicker now. You feel flat. You feel slightly irritable with the work, slightly foggy in the head. You tell yourself the afternoon is just long.

By half past five you are picking again. A few more biscuits. A sweet drink. Another little hit to get you home. You are not hungry and you are not satisfied. You are topping up a feeling you cannot quite name.

By the evening you are on the sofa, dull and tired and vaguely annoyed with yourself. No real appetite for the evening. No real brightness for the people you love. Just that heavy, cloudy feeling that makes the television blur and the day feel wasted.

Was that energy?

You were told that packet was a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, and at three o'clock you believe it because you want rescue to live in the drawer.

It never did.

Do Not Judge By The Snapshot

Let me ask you to do one honest thing with me, as an investigator, not as a judge.

Do not judge the dose by those five bright minutes. Judge it by the whole day.

Did you ever get through an afternoon on sweet fixes and arrive in the evening clear and lively?

Did you ever have a week of topping up every afternoon and feel more energetic at the end of it than at the start?

And if those fixes truly gave you energy, why do the most topped-up people you know feel the most tired?

There is only one honest answer, and you already know it from your own life.

Those who are out of this now say the same thing in their own plain words. They would take something quick and sweet and get a short lift, and that lift would be followed by a fast drop. No measurement. No lecture. Just lived experience, hour after hour: up a fraction, down a long way.

The reality is simple and settled: BAD SUGAR never puts energy in. It borrows the feeling of energy for minutes and pays you back in hours of flatness.

Think of your own curve. Morning starts passable. Mid-morning dips, so you top up. Midday lifts a fraction, then dips harder, so you top up again. Three o'clock crashes, so you reach for the drawer. Evening ends foggy. And we call that cycle “needing fuel.”

It is not fuel. It is the trap feeding on itself.

If blood sugar is a medical question for you, keep your clinician in charge of that side while we talk belief.

I am not talking about a diagnosis in you. I am talking about what we all lived: the daily yo-yo that no packet ever fixed.

The Lift Is The Drop

Here is the part that changes everything, and I want you to hear it plainly.

The lift and the drop are not two separate events. They are one event.

The same surge that brightens you for those few minutes sets up the flatness that follows. The lift is working in you, and as it fades it leaves you lower than before it started. That lower feeling then reads as “I need another fix.” So you have another. And the next drop goes a little deeper.

It is the other way around.

You do not feel tired and then take BAD SUGAR because you are tired. You feel tired because you took BAD SUGAR last time, and the time before, and the time before that. The dose creates the very dip it pretends to lift.

It is like pinching your own foot all day for the second you let go.

Ask yourself: when did a non-user child need a biscuit to play at three in the afternoon? When did you, long before the daily grazing began, need a packet to be bright? You did not. Your energy was yours. Clear, natural, up and down in a gentle wave, returning by itself.

What changed was not your body losing its power. What changed was the hourly topping-up that broke the wave into a roller coaster.

That is why one fix never settles you. It cannot settle you. Its job is to revive the want for the next one. We were conned into reading that revived want as proof that the last one helped. A prisoner does not rattle the door because the cell is pleasant. He rattles it because the last lock taught him to rattle.

See it now for what it is, with warmth toward yourself and contempt only for the con that taught you to misread it.

Tired, Run-Down And Foggy Is Not You

Let me speak to how this really feels to live inside, because I lived it too.

You wake not quite refreshed. Not ill. Just tired. You wash, you drink something sweet, you move. Mid-morning you are already running on fumes. Not dramatic. Just run-down. The sort of run-down where everything takes a little more effort than it should.

Afternoon comes and the head clouds over. You read the same line twice. You forget why you opened the drawer, then remember with a little rush of relief when you see the packet. Evening comes and you are foggy. Not sleepy in a good way. Foggy in a flat way, where food has no real taste and rest has no real rest in it.

We tell ourselves this is age. Work. Life. Character.

It is none of those things.

That dull morning-to-night heaviness — tired in the morning, run-down in the middle of the day, foggy by night — is what daily dosing does to a bright person. Live in that cycle long enough and you begin to believe that tired, run-down and foggy is just who you are.

It is not who you are. It is where the trap keeps you.

Take the packet out of the picture and watch what returns. Not in a week. Not after some hard test. Your own natural wave was there all along underneath the yo-yo. Children do not run on fixes. Animals do not need a drawer. Your body knew how to be hungry and satisfied, awake and sleepy, long before the first sweet fix taught it to crash.

BAD SUGAR did nothing FOR you in those afternoons. It did a great deal TO you, and then sold you another dose as the cure.

Once you see the whole day, you cannot unsee it. The five bright minutes stop lying to you. You stop asking “did that last fix help?” and you start asking “where did the whole day go?” And the answer is always the same: down.

That is freedom starting, not as a promise for later but as clear sight now.

5. NEVER JUDGE BY THE LAST FIX.
The lift lies; watch the whole day.

**SUMMARY**
- The afternoon fix feels bright for minutes and flat for hours.
- The lift and the later dip belong to the same dose.
- Daily topping-up built the tiredness it claimed to cure.
- Morning tiredness, daytime flatness and evening fogginess come from the cycle, not from you.
- Clear energy was yours all along underneath the yo-yo.
