Chapter 14
YOUR LAST ORDINARY OCCASION

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
An ordinary kitchen table in evening light.
A half-finished packet seen clearly at last.
A seatbelt clicked quietly before the drive.
A pink elephant wandering past the window.
A solemn promise spoken aloud, and joy.

*There is nothing here to mourn, only a tired con to leave behind with joy.*

The Field Is Clear

You have dragged every what-about into daylight and watched it fall. The small portion that never stayed small. The apple that was never the enemy. The dull crackers calling for sweet company. The birthday that belonged to people, not to cake. The clever swap in new clothes. The locked cupboard that never freed anyone.

Nothing is left to negotiate. That is why you feel different now — solemn, perhaps, a little strange to be eager. I know that feeling well. We all stood here, who lived years grazing and topping up, hardly daring to believe the exit could feel bright.

Let me ask you plainly, and answer honestly. Did any sweet during this book give you anything you could name. Did the last fix warm you for more than minutes before the flatness returned. Did you ever once enjoy being foggy and hunted. You know the honest answer. The fact is there was never a genuine gain in it, only a low relieved and remade.

The tug-of-war of fear is over. The rope on one side said you will fail. The rope on the other side said life will be duller if you succeed. Both ropes were held by the same hands — the Sugar Trap itself. See that, and the pulling stops.

Take It With Your Eyes Open

Do not make this last one a ceremony. Do not buy a special box or plan a farewell feast. Take your ordinary occasion, the sort you would have had tonight anyway, and have it with attention.

I did this myself on my last evening. I sat where I always sat, reached as I always reached, and for the first time I did not hurry. I tasted the fierce hit, the dull coating on the tongue, the chew that quickly turned to work. I noticed the brief lift, then within the hour the heaviness, the urge to pick again. It was ugly when watched. It had always been ugly. I had only called it kind because the Big Sweet Con told me to.

Do the same. Have your usual dose with eyes wide open. Notice how little is there. Notice the restlessness underneath that calls for another fix before the packet is closed. That faint dip is only the Little Sugar Mite stirring, an empty, passing unease already losing its food. It cannot compel you. The only thing that ever compelled was the belief that the fix helped.

Is this pleasure. Is this comfort. Is this love. No. It is feeding a loop that borrows brightness and pays back fog.

When you finish, do not linger. You are ready. You should be champing at the bit to be done. If you are not, read again. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

The Solemn Vow

This is the threshold. Cross it joyfully, from user to free non-user, with no loss to mourn.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY BAD-SUGAR OCCASION AND MAKE THE VOW.
Leave as a free non-user, not a mourner.

Say it aloud, slowly, as a promise, not a wish: I will never feed the Mite again. I am free. The Con is dead.

And mean the never in the right way. Not as a threat stretching for the rest of your life, but as a gift opening for the rest of your life — clear mornings, bright afternoons, food enjoyed hungry to satisfaction. You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

You can leave it easily, immediately and permanently, because there is nothing to lose. Eat what you want and never crave BAD SUGAR again. Your body, your hunger, real food — they know their job and will do it beautifully while you get on with enjoying your life.

I congratulate you with all my heart. You have just done the most exciting thing: you walked out of a prison you were conned into, and the door was open all along. Feel the relief flood in. Let pride rise. You were never weak. You were trapped, and now you are free.

What Comes To Mind After

Thoughts will still wander past. Let them. You cannot stop a thought by ordering it away, any more than you can stop picturing a pink elephant by commanding yourself not to. Try it now — do not picture a pink elephant — and there it lumbers, large and pink. The way out is never to forbid the picture. Let it come, see it for what it is, and change how it feels.

So when the old whisper says just one sweet thought, do not argue and do not push. Smile. Label it silently: that is the Big Sweet Con talking, not me. Remember in a flash what it never did FOR you. Then answer with joy:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it brightly every time, whether in a shop, at a table, on a tired afternoon. It is your replacement thought for life. It turns a dying echo into a hit of relief.

And if a crumb crosses you by accident — a biscuit pressed on you before you can speak, a sauce sweetened without your knowing — do not tremble. Think of a seatbelt. It sits quietly across you on every drive, there to soften a jolt you never planned. It is never leave to steer wild on purpose. Your body copes with a blip. Your freedom stands unless you invite the old lie back to say this proves you need it. Guard the belief and the blip revives nothing.

Never envy users you see still grazing. Pity them warmly, as we once deserved pity. Watch the shopper negotiating slivers under bright lights, the guest watching the clock for permission, the friend calling a bar a reward after a hard day. They are not enjoying. They are serving. You are not deprived. They are.

Here is a small prediction I make for you with total confidence. In a few weeks, on an ordinary tired afternoon when you once would have dived for the drawer, you will notice the slump never came. You will be clear, a little hungry perhaps, thinking of real food with pleasure — and you will realise with delight that no fix was wanted. That moment will prove what I tell you now: the trap fed the hunger it pretended to cure.

Walk out now and celebrate. Tell no mournful story. Preach to no one. Live brightly and visibly at ease, and let ease do the talking.

**SUMMARY**
- The last ordinary sweet, watched closely, shows ugliness where kindness was imagined.
- Readiness feels eager and solemn together, and the crossing is joy, not loss.
- A slip by accident revives nothing unless the old belief is allowed back.
- Passing sweet thoughts are reframed with relief, never pushed away.
- Other users deserve pity, never envy, because they remain inside the loop.
- A coming tired afternoon without the old slump will confirm freedom as lived fact.
