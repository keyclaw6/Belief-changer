Chapter 15
MORNINGS, SHOPS AND FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A morning kitchen with kettle steam and real hunger.
A supermarket aisle under bright lights.
A family table and a tired afternoon.
A passing sweet thought, smiled at and gone.

*Ordinary life without BAD SUGAR is not empty — it is brighter and easier.*

Breakfast Hungry

Your first free morning tells you more than any argument could.

You wake, you feel that clean hollow in your stomach, you potter to the kitchen and eat. Eggs, fruit, bread, cheese, whatever real food you love — eaten slowly, hungry, to satisfaction. The kettle sings. Light comes through the window. You taste properly because your tongue is not coated and your head is not foggy.

We all forgot what that felt like. We topped up from mid-morning on, grazed through meetings, picked through evenings, and called it appetite. It was never appetite. It was the loop borrowing brightness and paying back flatness.

The fact is no morning ever needed a dose to be good. Ask yourself honestly and answer honestly. Did the eggs need sweetening to satisfy you. Did the daylight need a fix to be pleasant. Was there one crumb of pleasure in the old drawer biscuit that this calm kitchen does not give you better. You know the answer.

Trust your body, your instinct, real food. Eat when you are hungry and stop when you are satisfied, and let hunger do its friendly job. I ate my first free breakfast standing up, half expecting to feel deprived. I finished the plate, laughed, and realised I had not thought of sweet once — because there was nothing to think of. Hunger met with real food is pleasure itself.

“What about my sweet coffee,” you whisper, “just a treat with my cup.” Look closely. Was it the powder in the cup you loved, or the pause, the warmth in your hands, the ten quiet minutes before the day began. The pause was always yours. The warmth was always yours. BAD SUGAR only ever sneaked a ride on them and charged you all day for the lift.

That is the whole morning in one sentence. It never gave you anything.

The Aisle At Ease

Go into shops exactly as you always did. Buy the food for the week, walk every aisle you need, carry no fear andrun no detour.

You will see it clearly now, perhaps for the first time. The supermarket aisle under bright lights, walls of packets shouting in reds and golds, promises of lift and love and reward stacked to the ceiling. We once stood there negotiating like trapped shoppers — which bar is smaller, which packet can I allow, shall I be good this week. It felt like choice. It was a con talking.

You see it differently now. That is not pleasure on the shelves. That is manufacture. Anger belongs with the design that built it, never with the people still reaching for it.

Watch them warmly and walk on. The mother counting slivers for the children. The tired man hunting a quick hit before the till. The girl telling herself once a week is fine. They are not enjoying. They are serving a loop they never chose. You are not deprived while they indulge. They are caught while you are free.

Do you need to hurry past. Do you need to look away, grip the trolley, preach to anyone. No. You walk at ease because you have nothing to fight and nothing to miss. Pick up what you want to eat, leave the sweet-wrapped doses where they sit, and get on with enjoying your life. Freedom that needs hiding is not freedom.

I love shopping now for the dullest reason — it is dull. No tug, no bargain, no little voice doing sums. Just food, prices, people, weather outside. Marvellous in its ordinariness.

When A Sweet Thought Drifts Past

It will happen, and it means nothing.

A smell from a bakery as the door swings. A film with chocolate on laps. A birthday cake carried singing to a table. A low tired hour when you once dived for the drawer. A sight, a smell, a place, an old hour of day — and a sweet thought drifts past like weather.

Let it come. You cannot order a thought away any more than you can order yourself not to picture a pink elephant. Try not to picture one and there it lumbers, large and pink and absurd. The way out was never to forbid the picture. See it, smile, and change how it feels.

So when the whisper says just a treat, do not argue and do not push. It is not a command. It is not a force pulling your hands. It is only a faint echo from an old interpretation, already dying for lack of food. Remember in a flash what BAD SUGAR never did FOR you — no energy, no comfort, no love — only TO you, the lift that dropped you and the flatness that called for another hit.

Then answer it with joy, brightly, whether you are in a shop, at a table, on a tired afternoon:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it every time and mean it. That glad shout turns an intruder into a reminder. Each passing thought becomes a small hit of relief — proof you no longer serve it, pity for those who still do, delight that the question is closed.

Tell no mournful story about it. Preach to no one at the table. If someone offers you a dose, a simple no thank you, warm and easy, is enough. Live visibly at ease and let ease do the talking. You do not need clever swaps in new clothes or new rules to carry. Real food when hungry, clear days, bright company — that is the whole menu, and it is plenty.

The Tired Afternoon That Proves It

Here is the moment I promised you, and it will come on the most ordinary day.

A few weeks from now you will have a tired afternoon. Work running long, children loud, sleep short the night before. The old hour arrives — the one that used to mean heavy eyes, a drawer yanked open, something quick and sweet to get me through.

You will wait for the slump. And you will notice it never came.

Not the old heaviness. Not the fog hunting for a hit. Just a little honest tiredness, a little honest hunger, thinking with pleasure of dinner. You will look up from your desk, surprised, and realise with delight that no fix was wanted, no battle was fought, no cloud descended. Clear when you expected fog.

That is your lived proof. The dip belonged to the loop, not to you. Without the dose there is no crash to cure. Without the crash there is no cry for rescue.

Was it willpower that carried you through. Was it effort. Was it luck. No. It was understanding. The benefit was never there, so there is nothing to miss and nothing to fight.

From that afternoon on you know how mornings and shops will feel — ordinary and bright, yours to enjoy clear for the rest of your life. Family food is just food and company again. Celebrations belong to people again. Mornings belong to hunger and light again. You meet every old cue as the free person you now are, reframe the thought in a breath, feel the relief flood in, and walk on.

Rejoice in it. Celebrate it quietly as you chop, as you shop, as you sit down tired and clear. You were conned into wanting something that never gave you anything. Now you are out, and out is wonderful.

**SUMMARY**
- Free mornings satisfy fully when real hunger meets real food.
- Shops lose their pull when seen as design, not pleasure.
- Passing sweet thoughts are only echoes, reframed with joy, never obeyed.
- Other users deserve warm pity, never envy, because they remain inside the loop.
- A coming tired afternoon without the old slump proves freedom as lived fact.
- Ordinary days feel normal and bright, with nothing missed and nothing fought.
