The Same Empty Chair

Picture the same small scene in two different houses.

In the first house, an empty chair stands pushed back from the table. The person who sat there is gone. The room feels cold. Someone loved sat there, someone kind, someone who brought warmth and laughter — and now there will never be laughter from that chair again. The emptiness hurts. Of course it hurts. That is grief.

In the second house, the very same empty chair stands pushed back from the very same table. The person who sat there is also gone. But the person who sat there stole from everyone in the house. He lied every day, took money from purses, broke sleep, made the children cry, left the whole house tired, run-down and foggy. For years you wished he would leave and feared he never would. Now the door has closed behind him for good. The emptiness sings. That is relief.

Same chair. Same emptiness. Two opposite feelings. No one has to teach you which is which.

We all know which house we lived in with BAD SUGAR.

It sat at our table morning, noon and night. It promised lift and comfort and reward and love. It never once did anything FOR us. It only did TO us — the lift that dropped us, the flatness that called for another dose, the drawer opened, the box emptied on our lap, the fog that followed us around. It was never a friend. It was a con that fed a tiny dip and called the dip desire.

Now its chair is empty. Do not stand looking at it like a mourner. There is no pleasure to bury, no kindness to miss, no loss to carry for the rest of your life.

Rejoice at a death, not a parting. An enemy who never gave you anything has left your house, easily, immediately and permanently. Celebrate the space. Breathe the clear air where he sat. I stood in my own kitchen on my first free evening and laughed out loud at how light the room felt — not solemn, not brave, just glad he was gone.

Whenever the thought of him drifts past — a smell, a packet, an old hour — answer it the way a free person answers:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it brightly and walk on. That empty chair is proof, not temptation.

Go Live It

You do not need me any more. You do not need rules, swaps, watches or warnings.

Eat when you are hungry and enjoy real food to satisfaction. Walk the bright aisles, sit at family tables, meet tired afternoons clear-headed. Pity the users still caught, never envy them. They are not enjoying what you are missing. They are serving what you escaped.

Get on with enjoying your life — marvellous, wonderful, free days, ordinary and bright. That was always the point. Not to fight something, but to be happy you no longer do it.

I promise you this as one who walked out the same door: there is nothing ahead to dread and everything ahead to gain. Live it, love it, and leave that chair empty.
