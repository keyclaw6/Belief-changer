Chapter 9
STRONG WILLED AND STILL TRAPPED

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A January kitchen, a ruled notebook, a biscuit tin with its lid on tight
- Two old acquaintances at a birthday party, one bragging, one sighing
- A cliff edge with a chalked bargain that makes everyone laugh

*You did not break your promise because you are weak; the promise was built to break you.*

### THE WEEK OF BEING GOOD

You know this week by heart because we have all lived it.

Monday morning. Bright resolve. The notebook comes out. No sweet drinks, no biscuits, no cake, no evening picking. We tell ourselves this time it will be different because this time we mean it.

By Tuesday we are being good. By Wednesday we are being very good. We walk past the drawer and feel virtuous. We tell a friend, “I've managed to be good this week.” Our voice is proud and a little tired already.

By Thursday the day has a hum underneath it. “I need something RIGHT NOW.” We do not want a feast. We want just a treat. One small dose to take the edge off, to prove we can handle it.

Friday we negotiate. “Once a week is fine.” “I don't keep it in the house, so one outside does not count.” We permit the sliver. The sliver wakes the hum into a roar. The packet follows the sliver. The box is empty by late evening, eaten half-standing in bright kitchen light.

Saturday morning comes with fog behind the eyes and the old sinking. What happened to all that resolve?

Let me tell you what happened, as an escaped fellow sufferer who kept that same notebook for years.

Nothing happened to your resolve. Your resolve worked perfectly. You forced yourself through four days of wanting and not having, thinking about sweet all the more because it was forbidden, dreaming about it, timing it, earning it. Then the forbidden thing did what forbidden things always do. It became precious. It became enormous. It took you.

I have done that week more times than I can count. I know how strong you have to be to do it.

Ask yourself, honestly, as an investigator.

If you were weak-willed, how did you manage to starve yourself of something you believed was pleasure for days on end?
If lack of character explains you, why did each hard week of denial end in a bigger binge than the week before?
Does a weak person persist for years in a private fight that leaves them tired and ashamed, and still get up on Monday to fight again?

There is only one honest answer. Persistence like that is not weakness. The fact is, you have shown remarkable will. You turned it against yourself in service of a method that cannot work.

### WHY FORBIDDEN MEANS PRECIOUS

Here is the trick inside every diet and rule.

We take BAD SUGAR, a fix that never gave us anything but a brief lift and a long drop, and we make it the prize for being good. We lock the tin, we count the days, we tell ourselves we cannot have it. And the moment we tell ourselves we cannot have it, we start to want it with a special intensity.

It is not your hunger doing that. It is the rule doing that.

Cut it out by force and every sight and smell sharpens. Allow it back as a reward and every dose feels more enormous for the wait. The tighter the restriction, the louder the hum, the bigger the creep back. We have all watched it: the good week that creates the bad weekend that creates the stricter Monday that creates the worse weekend.

That is not a flaw in you. That is the design of denial.

The fact is, a pleasure you truly loved would not need to be locked up to be enjoyed. And a crutch you truly needed would not punish you more the longer you went without it. What grows under lock and key is not love. It is the illusion of value created by the lock itself.

You were never proving you could live without it. You were proving you still believed you could not live without it, while white-knuckling the days.

YOU DID NOT FAIL THE METHOD. THE METHOD FAILED YOU.

### THE BRAGGERS AND THE WHINGERS

If that method worked, the people who used it would be free and happy. Look at them.

At every party you will meet the bragger. Chest out, thin smile. “I haven't touched sweet for two years. It was hell at first. I still want it every day. But I hold the line.” He wants applause. He wants you to envy his strength. Listen to what he is really telling you. He still wants a fix daily after two years. He still feels deprived. He still calls a confection that gave him fog and regret a pleasure he is nobly refusing. Is that freedom? Does that look like escape to you?

Then there is his twin, the whinger. Soft voice, tired eyes. “I was so good till Thursday, then the kids brought cake in and I caved. I have no willpower. I will start again Monday.” She wants sympathy. She wants you to agree she is broken. Listen to what she is really telling you. She fought herself for days, suffered, then suffered more for stopping suffering. Is that weakness? Or is that a strong person exhausting herself in a fight with no finish line?

Both of them believe the same lie. Both believe BAD SUGAR is a genuine delight and they are the problem. The bragger believes he is strong enough to suffer forever. The whinger believes she is too weak to suffer forever. Neither has questioned the suffering.

I pity both of them, and I do not blame either of them. We were both of them. That grinding contest with no last round was the Willpower Method, not you.

It takes a strong will to persist for years in something that goes against all your instincts, that leaves you foggy and flat, that you have to hide and excuse. You are not weak-willed. You are wilful, and you were misled.

Once you see that, you can stop attacking yourself and start dismissing the method. You can walk out easily, immediately and permanently, because there is no waiting and no wound to heal, only a lie to drop.

### THE CLIFF BARGAIN

“Oh,” says the negotiator in your head, “but surely I can keep the special ones? Just a treat. Once a week is fine. I just won't keep it in the house.”

Let us play that out in daylight.

Imagine we stand together on bright grass at the edge of a cliff. Not a terrible drop, just enough to break an ankle. I draw a line in chalk a few steps back and say, with great seriousness:

“You may jump off this cliff, provided you fall only a few metres.”

You would laugh. You would say, “What a stupid bargain. If I jump, I fall. The fall is the point of the cliff.”

That is exactly what moderation asks of you. Have the dose that creates the dip, provided the dip does not come. Take the hit that makes the next hit feel necessary, provided you do not feel it as necessary. Keep alive the belief that sweet is reward, provided the belief never acts.

If a small weekly fix truly gave you something marvellous, why would you limit so precious a thing to once a week? And if it gives you nothing, why bother with the weekly fall?

The “I don't keep it in the house” version is the same chalk line moved outdoors. We congratulate ourselves for not buying packets, then buy the slice outside, then think about the slice all week, then eat the box on Friday because we were so good not to keep it. Keeping or not keeping changes the shop. It does not change the wanting. And the wanting was never about the house. It was about the belief that one small dose holds comfort.

There is no safe number of jumps. Not because you are fragile, but because the cliff is pointless. Why negotiate how to fall?

Ask the negotiator one more question and watch him go quiet.

If moderation worked, why has every period of cutting down ended with you wanting more, not less?

You already know the honest answer. Cutting down makes each fix feel like a rescue, makes sweet feel rare and kind, guarantees the creep back. Total freedom is not harder than moderation. It is the only thing that is easy, because it removes the preciousness entirely.

### LEAVE THE METHOD, KEEP YOUR STRENGTH

So let us settle this now, with warmth.

You are not a person who cannot stick to things. You stuck to a miserable bargain for years. You counted, you hid, you started again, you endured the hum. That is endurance. That endurance is yours. It was simply hired by the wrong boss.

The Willpower Method needs your belief to survive. It needs you to believe you lost a pleasure, to feel torn every day, to count days and mourn the tin, to admire braggers and pity whingers, to call your natural hunger a failure of character. Take away that belief and it has nothing to hire.

I am not asking you to try harder. I am telling you there is nothing to fight. The tiny dip fades on its own. The loud lie fades when seen. What remains is you, with your strength finally working for you, free to enjoy mornings, shops, food, company, without negotiation.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

So hear this one at the climax, spoken plain and cheerful, and let it close the door on every smug warning and every gloomy confession you will ever hear again:

9. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY THE WILLPOWER METHOD.
They still believe they sacrificed.

Pity them. Do not follow them. You are escaping, not suffering, and you will know it by the relief.

Get on with enjoying your life, clear and bright and free of that endless contest.

**SUMMARY**
- Past diets failed because denial makes each dose feel precious and bigger.
- Restriction followed by binge is the pattern of the method, not proof of weak character.
- Braggers who still suffer and whingers who still blame themselves both believe the same false benefit.
- Keeping strength while dropping the method leaves will intact and struggle gone.
- One small permitted exception keeps the whole illusion alive and guarantees creep back.
