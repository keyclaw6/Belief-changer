Chapter 13
WHAT ABOUT THIS, WHAT ABOUT THAT?

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A drawer-bargain whispered at midnight
- A house party that gets gate-crashed
- A cliff edge and a careful little bargain
- A fruit bowl in clear morning light
- A noisy birthday table, candles still burning

*Every last what-about you can think of is the same empty promise knocking in a different hat.*

### THE SHOPPER FOR LOOPHOLES

You have come far with me, and I am proud of you.

You have seen the lift for what it is. You have seen the treat for what it is. You have just met someone exactly like you who walked out and found ordinary days brighter. And now, right on cue, comes the practical voice.

It does not argue any more. It shops.

"Yes, but what about this one? What about parties? What about fruit? What about just a tiny one?" I know that voice well. We all know it. We lived in it for years, turning the packet over in our hands and looking for the corner that might still be honest.

The fact is there is no corner that is honest.

Not one of these leftovers was ever a genuine pleasure, treat or lift. Each one arrives wearing fresh clothes, but underneath it is the same old story asking to be fed again. Let me show you, quickly, one by one, and then you can be done with shopping.

### "JUST A TREAT. SURELY ONE SMALL ONE CAN'T HURT?"

"Just a treat."

Who decides what is just? You or the packet? Did you ever meet a sweet fix that stayed small once the craving woke? You open the door a crack to be polite, and the whole evening walks in.

A small dose does not give you a small pleasure. It gives you the same dip, the same restlessness, the same clock started again, only with a tidier name.

### "ONCE A WEEK IS FINE. I'LL BE GOOD THE REST OF THE TIME."

"Once a week is fine. I've managed to be good this week."

Good? Do you hear what we are saying to ourselves? As if plain days were punishment and the fix were wages.

Ask yourself honestly. Did the weekly treat make the week brighter, or did the week feel duller because you were waiting for Saturday? Would a free person count days toward a dose? It is the other way around. The special day keeps the other six feeling empty.

### "I DON'T KEEP IT IN THE HOUSE, SO I'M IN CONTROL."

"I don't keep it in the house."

I used to say that too, with great pride, while I stopped at the garage on the way home. We all did. We moved the packet from the cupboard to the car to the desk drawer and called moving it control.

If you have to arrange your house, your route, your will around something, who is in charge? It is like agreeing to jump off a cliff provided you fall only a few metres. A playful bargain that hides a simple truth. The free person needs no house rules.

That dreary bargain belongs to the Willpower Method, not to freedom.

### "WHAT ABOUT FRUIT? AM I SUPPOSED TO FEAR AN APPLE NOW?"

This one frightens kind people, so hear me clearly.

When I say BAD SUGAR I mean the sweetened drinks, packets, bars, biscuits, cakes, cereals and dessert-form doses, with their starchy junk friends, made to spike and crash you. Natural sweetness inside real food as nature packed it is not counted and was never the target.

Would you fear an apple that leaves you satisfied? Does an apple send you back to the kitchen in your socks at night? Of course not. Eat when you are truly hungry and stop when you are truly satisfied, and if you feel unwell or live with anything that needs a clinician, keep them in charge of that part. The apple was never the trap.

### THE GATE-CRASHER AT THE PARTY

"But crisps and chips and white bread aren't even sweet. Surely those are different?"

Let me show you a little house party, because this is where many good people get caught.

Picture plain white carbs on their own. White bread, plain crackers, chips, puffed snacks. On their own they sit rather dull and lonely in the mouth, starchy and bland, no music, no lights. So what do they do? They send for sweet company to make a party.

Butter and salt and then a sweet drink after. A handful of crisps that leaves you reaching for chocolate. A pale roll that leaves you hunting the biscuit tin an hour later. The dull ones open the door, the sweet ones rush in, and soon the whole noisy crowd is dancing in your head and calling it hunger.

The fact is they belong to the same party. I am not asking you to weigh or measure or talk like a laboratory. I am asking you to see the trick. Where the food is made to spike you and drop you and call you back, that is BAD SUGAR, sweet-tasting or not.

### "WHAT ABOUT BIRTHDAYS AND PARTIES? I CAN'T BE THE ODD ONE OUT."

"I need something for parties. Everyone will be having some."

Will they? Look closely next time. Who is laughing — the people or the icing? Did the candles, the singing, the children with sticky faces, need your dose to be joyful?

Ask yourself. Would the day have been dull if you had felt clear and bright through it? Would you remember the company more or less if you left without the fog? You know the honest answers. The joy travels with life, never with the packet.

### "WHAT ABOUT SWEETENERS AND DIET DRINKS AND HONEY AND DARK CHOCOLATE?"

"Can't I switch to the pale impostors? The diet drink? The sweetener? The honey? The dark one?"

What would you be switching? The packet changes its coat, but the meaning stays the same. A diet hit keeps the sweet-craving conversation alive in your head, keeps you paying for a lift that never comes, keeps the old thought rehearsed.

If honey or dark chocolate or juice truly gave you anything real, why would you need to ask permission to keep one? Why would one lead to watching the clock for the next? What loves you about a substitute — the taste, or the hope you can keep the trap with cleaner walls?

### "AREN'T YOU JUST BRAINWASHING ME THE OTHER WAY?"

I have been waiting for this one, and I welcome it.

"This all sounds too neat. You tell me it is easy, you tell me there is nothing to miss, you answer everything. Aren't you just conning me back?"

A fair question from a person who has been conned enough times. So test me the way you would test anyone who wanted your trust.

Did I ask you to fear, to count days, to clench your jaw, to avoid life? Did I ask you to believe me blind, or did I ask you to look at your own afternoons, your own evenings, your own kitchen at night and check? Which voice profits if you stay — mine or the one that sells you the next fix? You have the evidence, not me. Believe your own eyes and walk out because you see.

### NO CORNER LEFT

So where does that leave you?

It leaves you with no loophole still breathing. Portions, schedules, house rules, fruit fears, savoury cousins, party tables, pale substitutes — each one knocked and each one empty. If a little BAD SUGAR slips in by complete accident one day, your body will handle the stumble. What you will never do is plan the stumble and call it wisdom.

You came to this chapter shopping for the one exception that would let the old system live. You leave it with a clearer eye and an empty basket. There is nothing cunning left to answer, nothing brave left to do. Only the seeing left, and the seeing is already yours.

**SUMMARY**
- A small portion starts the same loop as a large one, only with a tidier name.
- A weekly date keeps six days feeling dull and makes the fix feel like wages.
- Moving the packet is not control, and needing house rules proves who is in charge.
- Fruit as nature packed it was never the target and leaves you satisfied, not called back.
- Bland starchy snacks open the door for sweet company and belong to the same party.
- Parties are joyful because of people, light and leisure, never because of the dose.
- Sweeteners and darker and paler versions keep the same craving alive in new coats.
- Checking your own afternoons and evenings is seeing, not blind belief.
