Chapter 10
SOMEONE BUILT THIS HUNGER

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A bright supermarket aisle late on an ordinary weekday
- Walls of shining packets and bottles, all shouting at once
- A dashboard at night, with red lights blinking

*You never invented this craving, and the heaviness you blame on life belongs to the fix.*

### THE AISLE THAT NEVER SLEEPS

Walk with me down that aisle, because we have all walked it.

It is late. The lights are too bright. The shelves rise on both sides, higher than your head, stacked with biscuits, bars, cereals, fizzy bottles, cakes in plastic, sweets at the till for the children to grab. Every packet is shouting. Every colour has been chosen. Every shape fits your hand. Every word promises love, energy, comfort, reward.

And there in the middle stands an ordinary decent shopper. You. Me. All of us.

We came in for food. We stand negotiating with ourselves. "I have been good. Just a treat. I do not keep it in the house, so this one does not count." We pick up a packet, put it back, pick it up again. We feel a little tug and call it our sweet tooth, our personality, our lack of control.

Let me tell you as an escaped fellow sufferer who did that dance a thousand times, I know that tug well.

That tug was put there.

The fact is, armies of clever, well-paid people design that tug. Food engineers, advertisers, packagers. They test the crunch, the melt, the salt against the sweet, the exact dose that lights up your mouth and leaves you wanting another mouthful before you have swallowed the first. They test the colour that makes children reach. They pay for the shelf at your eye level. They buy the cartoon, the song, the happy family on the box.

Be angry at them, not at yourself. I am warm to you. I am harsh to them.

Ask yourself, honestly, as an investigator.

Did you ask as a child for a hunger that wakes you at nine at night and drives you to the kitchen?
If this craving is simply human nature, why does it need ten thousand packets, jingles and offers to keep it alive?
Who gets rich if you stay a little hungry for the next fix for the rest of your life?

There is only one honest answer. You were conned into wanting, and that is the Sugar Trap in daylight.

You are not weak. Across large population surveys, about fourteen in a hundred adults and twelve in a hundred children answer that they lose control around highly tasty food in this same way, about the same share as struggle with drink or cigarettes — I say that so you know you are not strange or broken, just caught with millions of others.

Does that look like free choice to you? IT LOOKS LIKE DESIGN.

### THE TIREDNESS YOU CALL LIFE

Here is the second half of the con, and it is crueller.

We all know the roller coaster. Bright lift, fast drop. Sweet top-up at three, fog by four. Evening packet on the lap, heavy head in the morning. Eyes gritty. Mood thin. Brain like cotton wool.

And what do we say? We say, "That is age. That is work. That is kids. That is me."

"No," says the voice in your head, "everyone feels like this. Sugary somethings to get me through are normal. I need something RIGHT NOW just to function."

I believed that too. I called my afternoons my low energy. I called my mornings my slow start. I thought I was a tired person who needed propping up.

The fact is, we end up tired, run-down and foggy and call it life, when it is the bill for the fix.

Think. Children who have not yet learned the all-day topping up run and shout and concentrate without any sweet fuel. Animals do not queue for a lift to get through the afternoon. Your own body, left to real hunger and real satisfaction, knows how to carry you clear and bright from meal to meal. The heaviness arrives after the dose, not before it. It lifts briefly with the next dose, then returns a little deeper.

It is the other way around. BAD SUGAR does not relieve your tiredness. It creates the tiredness it pretends to relieve.

Why else would the lift always need repeating? Why else would the week of topping up leave you more tired than the week you never started?

You are not a tired person. You are a bright person being tired by a trap.

### WARNING LIGHTS, NOT CHARACTER FLAWS

Your body has been warning you faithfully all along. We have just been taught to misread the lights.

Picture your car dashboard at night. A red light blinks. You would not say, "That light is my personality." You would not cover it with tape and drive on. You would say, "Thank goodness, something needs attention."

Your body blinks lights the same way.

Take mood. In large follow-ups that tracked men over years, those who ate more sweets had higher later odds of troubled mood — I am not telling you what will happen to you, only that across groups the daily treat points toward low mood, not away from it. Does the fix that promises comfort deliver comfort, if the eaters end up gloomier? What comfort ever made its lovers sadder the more they took it?

Take the deeper load. Across big institutional summaries of populations, high added-sugar intake travels with higher heart and vessel trouble through liver overload, raised pressure, simmering inflammation, appetite signals bypassed — again, I am not giving you a personal sentence, and if you live with anything medical keep your clinician in charge of that side, I am only showing the direction of travel for the crowd. The lift is never free. It leaves its luggage inside.

Take teeth. Free sugars feed plaque acid that eats at teeth — not every single sweet in every mouth regardless of brushing and care, but the mechanism is real and ordinary. Ask any dentist what he sees all day. Rot in sweet wrapping. Pain that could have been avoided. Money paid to repair what a confection caused.

I lay this out flat because you deserve the truth, and I lay it without flinching because softening it would keep you trapped.

And now hear the relief in the same breath, because I do not want you to walk out from fear.

Fear never freed anyone. You do not escape because you are frightened of lights on a dashboard. You escape because you see there was never anything to miss. The lights go out on their own when you stop feeding the engine that lit them. Your body wants to return to clear. It knows how. It has been trying to tell you for years because it is on your side.

That heaviness, that low mood after the packet, that ache in the teeth — they were never proof you are failing at life. They were proof the trap was working, and proof your body stayed loyal through it.

### DO NOT CATCH IT BACK

"But everyone eats this way," says the voice. "My friends, my family, the office. Are you saying they are all wrong?"

Yes. Lovingly, yes.

We were all taught the same lesson at the same tables, in front of the same adverts, down the same bright aisles. Brainwashing is catching. When someone near you sighs, "I have been so good, I deserve a fix," and unwraps it with ceremony, your old belief stirs and whispers that you are missing out. You are not missing out. You are watching someone feed a tiny dip and call it love.

Pity them. Do not follow them. Do not argue with them. Do not let their ceremony rewrite what you now know.

You would not stand in a wind tunnel to test a new roof. Keep clear while the old paint dries. In a few days your eyes will be your own again, and other people's fixes will look what they are — not pleasures you envy, but doses they still believe they need.

So hear this one at the climax, spoken plain and cheerful, and let it guard you while you finish this book:

11. AVOID THE INFLUENCE OF OTHER SUGAR USERS WHILE YOU READ.
Their brainwashing is catching until yours is gone.

Stay with me, clear and bright, until the job is done. Then get on with enjoying your life, free in shops, free at tables, free in your own skin.

- The body has been flashing faithful warnings while staying on your side.
