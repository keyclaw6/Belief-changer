Chapter 8
THE OPEN DOOR

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A prison corridor, daylight through an open door, a pair of hesitating feet
- Two ropes pulling opposite ways in the dark
- A lifetime laid flat on the table, first heavy, then bright

*You are not torn because you still want it. You are pulled because the trap holds both ends.*

### THE TWO FEARS THAT FEEL LIKE YOU

It makes complete sense that you feel shaky right here.

We have all stood at this exact spot. We see clearly now that the warmth was never in the sweet and the panic was never power. The tiny dip fades on its own. The loud voice loses its logic. And still the feet do not move.

There are two voices, and they seem to come from inside you.

The first whispers:

"What if I fail again? I have failed every diet, every January rule. What if I walk out and get dragged back? Better not to promise."

The second whispers from the other side:

"But what if I succeed? What if mornings are grey without something sweet? What if parties, cinemas, tired evenings lose their kindness for the rest of your life?"

Failure on one side. Dullness on the other. No wonder you hover.

I want to honour that hovering before we dissolve it. You are not silly to feel it. You are not weak to feel it. Anyone who has been conned for years into linking comfort to a fix would feel torn when the cell door swings open. The fear is real as a feeling. It is simply not yours as information.

Let me ask you as an investigator, with warmth.

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you joy, why would the thought of living without it frighten you? Would a true friend need to threaten you with misery to keep you loyal?

And if you truly could not live without it, why would the thought of staying with it frighten you too? Would a true pleasure make you dread decades of topping up?

If both fears prove you need it, why do they point in opposite directions?

The only honest answer concedes it. A genuine pleasure does not need to scare you to keep you. What scares you on both sides is not love for sweet. It is the pull of ropes.

That tearing is the tug-of-war of fear.

### FEET BY AN OPEN DOOR

Picture it plainly.

A long corridor. Stone walls you know by heart. A small cell where you have lived for years — the drawer, the packet, the late-night kitchen light, the 4pm fog. Miserable in there, but familiar. You know where the shadows fall.

This morning the heavy door stands ajar. Just ajar. A bar of daylight falls across the floor. Air moves. From outside comes ordinary life — voices, traffic, someone laughing, a kettle, a morning.

You walk to the threshold. You put your hand on the cold edge of the door. And your feet hesitate.

Behind you, one rope tugs: you will fail, you always fail, step back inside where no one sees you fail.

In front of you, the other rope tugs: you will succeed and regret it, life outside will be bare, step back inside where at least you have your treat.

You stand there, one foot lifted, heart thumping, mistaking habit for desire. We have all mistaken that thump for proof we still want the cell.

Look down.

Both ropes run back through the dark into the same hands.

The same con that told you the fix lifts you now tells you that you cannot leave it. The same con that told you one sliver soothes you now tells you a whole lifetime without slivers will be empty. It frightened you to stay. It frightens you to go. It wins either way, as long as you keep calling the pulling choosing.

That is the Big Sweet Con, not you.

I stood with my hand on that door for years. I was not held by鎖 by pleasure. I was held by two opposite scares sewn by the same story. Once I saw the hands holding both ropes, my feet moved by themselves.

There is no lock on the outside. There never was.

YOU WERE NEVER CHOOSING. YOU WERE PULLED.

### A LIFETIME, THEN MORNING

Let us lay the lifetime flat for a moment, because the second rope feeds on vagueness.

Stay as you are, topping up for the rest of your life. Not one bad evening, but thousands. Thousands of 3pm fixes that borrow ten minutes and hand back hours of fog. Thousands of evening packets eaten half-asleep, followed by the flat sinking behind the eyes. Thousands of mornings starting already tired, already behind, already promising to be good. Money dripping away. Teeth, skin, energy, mood quietly taxed. The same private bargain in the same bright kitchen, over and over, until the bargain feels like character.

I lay that out flat because you deserve to see where the cell leads when the door stays shut.

I do not want you to walk out because that picture frightens you. Fear never freed anyone. I want you to see it only so the other rope loses its lie that the cell was kind.

Now lift it.

Walk out and live clear and bright and free for the rest of your life. Not one heroic day, but thousands of ordinary ones. Mornings hungry for real food and satisfied by it. Afternoons that dip a little with tiredness and rise again without a fix. Evenings with film and company enjoyed to the end, no box on the lap, no regret after. Shops walked through without negotiation. A mind that no longer argues with itself. No hovering. No counting. No bargain.

Which picture is heavier to carry? Which one asks more courage?

You already know. Continuing is the hard, frightening life sentence. Escaping is relief.

So let the daylight do its work. You do not need to fight the ropes. You only need to see who holds them. A rope seen is a rope dropped.

When doubt slides in later — and it will slide in as a reasonable whisper — do not argue with it, do not weigh it, do not reopen what is settled. Smile at it. It is only the old voice trying its old pull. You are not torn. You are free and walking.

8. NEVER DOUBT THE DECISION.
Doubt is the Trap talking, not you.

Step through while your heart is bright. Get on with enjoying your life, clear and marvellous and free.

**SUMMARY**
- The fear of failing and the fear of a duller life feel like two opposite truths.
- Both fears are pulls from the same trap, not reports from your heart.
- Staying means thousands of fixes and foggy evenings stretching ahead.
- Leaving means thousands of ordinary bright days with nothing missed.
- Doubt after this point is only the old pull trying again, not a reason to turn back.
