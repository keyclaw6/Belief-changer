### I KNOW THAT TIRED CUPBOARD LOOK

I know you.

You graze through the day and top up in the afternoon and pick in the evening, and you tell yourself it is just a little something sweet to get you through. You have hidden wrappers. You have made rules on Monday and broken them by Wednesday. You have felt foggy and flat and angry with yourself, and then you have done it again.

I know because I lived it.

For years I was the one with sugary somethings to get me through. Mid-morning biscuits. A sweet drink at my desk. A bar from the machine because I need something RIGHT NOW. A proper dinner and then the kitchen calling me back. I was not a bad person. I was not weak. I was caught.

And like you, I believed life without something sweet would be duller. I believed stopping would mean misery and deprivation. I believed some people could do it and I could not.

I walked out. Not by gritting my teeth. Not by counting days. I walked out clear and bright, and I have never missed it for a moment. I have helped others do the same. I tell you this at the start for one reason only: so you can feel what I felt when someone first told me — perhaps this does not have to hurt at all.

You are still using while you read this. Good. I ask nothing of your eating today. Read me as you are, sweet day going on around you, no change demanded. I am not here to lecture you.

Warm to you, always. Harsh to the trap that caught us both.

### TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE?

“This sounds too good to be true.”

That was my reaction too. Of course it was.

You have heard it all before. Treat when you feel you deserve one. I’ve managed to be good this week. Just a treat. Once a week is fine. I don’t keep it in the house. And then the evening comes and the rule melts. You know that roller coaster.

So when someone says you can stop without misery, a voice in you says: “Prove it. Everyone says that. Why should you be different?”

Let me answer that voice plainly.

Have all your past attempts left you freer, or tighter in the loop? If the diets and the rules and the Monday promises worked, would you be holding this book? If sugar truly lifted you, why do you crash an hour later and blame yourself?

And if a lifelong grazer like me walked out light and happy, without a fight, what makes you so sure you are the exception who cannot?

You do not need to believe me yet. You only need to keep reading.

There is no catch here. There is no fee for listening. There is no pain waiting on the next page. You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Read that again and let it sit. It is simple arithmetic. If I am wrong, you have lost an hour of reading. If I am right, you walk out easily, immediately and permanently, into a life where food is joy and no packet owns you.

Tell me honestly — what would you lose by hearing this out?

### WHAT I MEAN BY SUGAR

Let me be clear, so we never misunderstand each other.

When I say sugar in this book, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. BAD SUGAR is added and refined sugars plus junk-processed carbs and starchy snacks engineered to spike and crash you, in any sweetened drink, packet, bar, biscuit, cake, cereal or dessert-form dose.

Natural sweetness inside real food as nature packed it — fruit in its skin, plain food you eat hungry and enjoy — is not the target and is not counted. I am not asking you to fear food. I am not giving you a diet.

The CAPS name will carry the definition from now on. When you see BAD SUGAR, you know exactly what I mean.

I give no medical or diet advice here, so if you live with diabetes or take anything for blood sugar, appetite or mood, are pregnant, or carry distress around eating, keep your clinician in charge of those decisions while you read.

That is the whole line. Nothing pedantic. Nothing to weigh or measure.

And here is the promise that sits inside that line:

Eat what you want and never crave BAD SUGAR again.

You read that right. This is not about hunger. This is not about bland plates. You will eat when you are hungry, enjoy what you eat, and live your normal days. The only thing that leaves is the craving-snacking loop that never fed you.

“Impossible,” says the voice. “I like it too much.”

Do you? We will look together. Not today. For now, hold the thought lightly: what if you never liked it at all?

### THE SAFE THAT NEEDS EVERY NUMBER

Picture a small safe in a hotel room.

You spin the dial. First number clicks. Second number clicks. Third number — nothing. You pull the handle. It stays shut. Not almost open. Shut.

You try again, faster, cleverer. Same result. The safe does not reward effort. It rewards the full sequence. One missing number keeps it shut.

That is how this works.

Getting free of BAD SUGAR is not a matter of trying harder. It is a matter of seeing the whole picture. Miss one piece and you stay locked in doubt. Get the full sequence and the door swings.

This book is that sequence. Each short chapter turns one number. None of them asks you to strain or suffer. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

I say that with a smile, and with complete certainty. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have tried the other way. You know its name. This has nothing to do with the Willpower Method that left you exhausted and blaming yourself. That method told you stopping would be hard and then made it hard. We will not do that here.

Here, you read. You think. You notice. The wanting falls away by itself, and the door opens.

You do not need to force any number. You only need to turn them all, in order, with an open mind.

### THE HANDSHAKE

So here is my handshake before we begin.

I will not shame you. I will not frighten you into change. I will not ask you to be miserable for a month in hope of feeling better later. I am on your side against the loop that has dulled your afternoons and stolen your evenings.

You will feel relief from the very start, not at some far finish line. You will feel understood, not judged. You will feel excitement growing as you see what BAD SUGAR never did for you.

It is escape, not loss. It is freedom, not denial. It is marvellous to wake clear, wonderful to eat and be satisfied, exciting to pity the packets rather than want them.

You are not losing sweetness. You are finding yourself.

Take my hand on this one small agreement, and everything else follows:

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND.
You cannot lose by hearing this out.
