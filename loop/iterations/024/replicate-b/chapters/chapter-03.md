Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD IS THERE AT ALL

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- An audit table with two columns
- A bright aisle full of shouting packets
- What a dose takes and what it never gives

*There is nothing to weigh up — BAD SUGAR never did anything for you at all.*

### THE WRONG WEIGHING

You are being very fair with me, and I love you for it.

You say, “All right. I see the trap. I know it costs me. But surely it gives me something too? Surely life would be duller without something sweet?”

I hear that every time, because I said it myself. We all try to be reasonable about it. Harm on one side, pleasure on the other, and we tell ourselves we have weighed it like grown adults.

I am not going to argue about harm with you. Not because harm is not real, but because harm is the wrong question.

Harm-against-pleasure is a rigged scale. Put anything on the pleasure side — one small joy, one little lift, one deserved moment — and you can excuse any cost on the other side. That is how the conned mind keeps itself conned.

So let us kick that table over. I am not asking what it costs you against what it gives you.

I am asking a simpler, colder question: what does it actually give you?

Name it. Not what it takes away for ten minutes. What it gives.

Is it pleasure? Is it comfort? Is it energy? Pick one real gain, one that stands up in daylight and belongs to BAD SUGAR itself, not to the moment around it.

You will reach for an answer, and you will find your hand closes on air.

### WHAT DID YOU EVER GET?

“But I must get something from it. I would not do it otherwise.”

Would you not? We did not start because we had examined the gain. We started because sweetness was put in our hands as love and prize and normal life, and then because the dip came back and we mistook another dose for an answer. Nobody weighed anything. We were brainwashed first and told ourselves reasons afterward.

Look honestly at your own day.

When did a fix ever leave you brighter than a free non-user feels all the time? When did that drawer biscuit or evening packet ever add anything you could point to the next morning and say, there, that was worth it? If it truly fed you, why were you emptier an hour later? If it truly cheered you, why the fog and the flatness and the quiet promise to be good tomorrow?

The fact is... no dose ever rose you above how you would have felt if you had never taken it. It only lifted you a fraction out of the hole the last dose had dug, then let you sink again.

Walk down that bright aisle where the packets shout at you from every shelf. Everyone negotiating, everyone trapped, everyone telling themselves one small sweet is a harmless joy. Does that look like pleasure being freely chosen? Or does it look like hunger being manufactured and sold back to you?

Call the claim by its full name, because it is only one claim wearing three coats. You have been told BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure, treat or lift. Pleasure for dull hours. Treat for good behaviour. Lift for tired hours. Three words for the same promise: that something outside you makes life sweeter.

I am asking you to test that promise, not to fear it. Where is it? Show me one hour made sweeter by BAD SUGAR that was not sweeter because of rest, food, company, laughter, morning light, the film, the break from work — life itself — while the sweet was only sneaking a ride.

There is nothing there. And once you see there is nothing there, there is nothing to weigh, nothing to mourn, nothing to be brave about.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

### START HAPPY

Do you see why this is wonderful news?

If there were a real benefit, you would face a tug between loss and gain. There is no tug. There never was a benefit. You are not working out how to live without a crutch. You are finding out you never had a crutch, only a con that made you limp.

I freed myself from exactly your position, and I tell you flatly: you lose nothing by seeing this. The dread you feel is not yours. It is the Big Sweet Con whispering that investigation means deprivation. It does not. Investigation means freedom.

So meet the rest of this book the way you would meet good news. Bright, curious, almost cheeky. You are not preparing for misery. You are checking an empty box and discovering you were rich all along.

3. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD.
You are finding out, not giving up.

**SUMMARY**
- The harm debate is the wrong question and settles nothing.
- No dose ever gave a real gain that belonged to itself.
- The good moments were good because of life around them.
- Seeing emptiness where a benefit was supposed to be brings relief.
- Curiosity and elation are the right mood for what comes next.
