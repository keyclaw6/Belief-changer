### Voice Gap 2: Conditional method promise

**Our passage:** "It is simple arithmetic. If I am wrong, you have lost an hour of reading. If I am right, you walk out easily, immediately and permanently, into a life where food is joy and no packet owns you."

**Real passage:** "No doubt you find that hard to believe, but, read on, you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain."

**What Carr's voice does here:** Uses risk arithmetic only on the reader's side (nothing to lose). The positive outcome — freedom, ease, permanence — is stated as what the method delivers, not as conditional on the author being correct.

**Where our voice fails:** "If I am right" probabilistically wraps the assigned method promise (easy, immediate, permanent freedom). The reader is invited to weigh odds rather than hear a flat promise backed by earned authority. Effect: distance and unresolved method confidence at the freedom-promise peak.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** writer-prompt — conditional framing may be imported as polite rhetoric where the rubric and Carr both require an unconditional method promise once the nothing-to-lose frame is set.
