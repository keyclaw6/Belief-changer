### WHEN THIS BOOK SAYS SUGAR, IT MEANS BAD SUGAR

Before we use the compass, we fix the map. One line. Clear forever. Box it in your mind.

> **When this book says SUGAR, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.**

BAD SUGAR is all refined and added free sugars — sucrose, glucose, high-fructose corn syrup, honey and syrups added as free sugars — plus junk-processed carbs where fibre and structure have been stripped: white bread, pastries, sweets, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, sugary drinks, confectionery, ultra-processed snacks.

GOOD SUGAR is not SUGAR in this book. Intrinsic sugars inside whole fruit, vegetables and unrefined whole foods eaten as whole foods, and plain natural foods generally, are not restricted. They are food. They are part of Nature's Guide's menu. Sweet taste itself is not the enemy. The engineered hit is.

Do you see why the line matters? You are not quitting food. You are not quitting sweetness as taste. You are not quitting eating itself. You are quitting BAD SUGAR wholly. For BAD SUGAR there is no healthy level other than zero. If there is no benefit to doing it often, there is no point doing it occasionally either.

Does that mean every trace is poison? No. The fact is, population guidance gives a sense of scale without prescribing your plate. The World Health Organization suggests populations keep free sugars below 10% of energy, with additional benefit below about 5% — roughly 50 grams and 25 grams on a typical intake. Most of us have lived far above that without calling it high. That is not a personal prescription. It is a backdrop that shows how far "normal" drifted, and why zero BAD SUGAR is coherent, not extreme. Your clinician guides any personal target; this book guides your belief.

And what about teeth? The fact is, free sugars feed plaque acid and that acid drives decay. Brushing, fluoride and dental care mediate it, but the mechanism is real. Your body warns for a reason. Nature's Guide is simply eating in line with that warning, not against it.

Hold the decree tight. BAD SUGAR is quit. GOOD SUGAR and fresh food are free. Keep that distinction and no confusion can trick you later when someone says, "But fruit is sugar!"

Is fruit BAD SUGAR? No. Is your favourite fresh apple BAD SUGAR? No. Is a biscuit that needs sugar to be palatable BAD SUGAR? Yes.

You have the line. The compass can work.

### THE HUNGER GAUGE — LISTENING WITHOUT COUNTING

The second instrument is even gentler. Not a rule. A listening aid. Use it if it helps; ignore it if your body already tells you clearly. Never track it day to day.

**The Hunger Gauge 0–20**

Think of 0 as stuffed and uncomfortable. 5 as not hungry. 10 as neutral and comfortable. 12–15 as clearly hungry and food would be satisfying. 20 as ravenous and shaky.

Use it like this: eat when you reach about 12–15. Stop when you return to about 8–10 — satisfied, light, energetic. Not stuffed. Not straining. Light.

That is it. No numbers to log. No app. No prescription. Just a dial to help you hear what Nature's Guide is already saying. Some meals you will be at 13 and eat heartily. Other times you will notice you are at 9 and do not need to eat — the BAD SUGAR thought was just the old 3pm cue, not your stomach.

Does this replace medical guidance? Never. Read this box carefully, because your safety matters more than any metaphor.

> **Practical safety advisory:** If you have diabetes, an eating disorder, a history of disordered eating, hypoglycemia, pregnancy, or a clinician-prescribed therapeutic diet, follow your clinician's plan — this compass never overrides medical guidance. The Raw Test and Hunger Gauge are listening aids, not medical prescriptions. If you have any concerns about low blood sugar, medication, or eating patterns, speak to a qualified professional. Crisis and eating-disorder support pointers are in the front and back matter of this book.

Do you have a condition that requires you to eat on a schedule or to treat lows with fast sugar? Then you follow your clinician. This book does not diagnose or prescribe. It changes belief about BAD SUGAR as a treat or lift. Your care team guides medical action.

For everyone else, notice how kind the gauge is. It does not tell you what to eat. Nature's Guide already does. It simply helps you notice when to eat and when you have had enough — without external rules keeping the Sweet Con alive.
