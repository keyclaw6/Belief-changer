Chapter 13
THE LAST SWEET

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the kitchen table and the last packet, the safe door swinging open, the solemn vow spoken aloud, the pink elephant that arrives when you tell yourself not to think

*You are not giving up anything — you are making a solemn promise never to feed the con again, and you are free the moment you make it.*

### ARE YOU READY?

Do you feel ready?

I mean it as a straight question to one reader, not as a speech. If you feel a quiet, almost laughable clarity — as if a knot you carried for years has loosened while you were not pulling at it — you are ready. If you feel excited, a little impatient, as if you want to get on with it and be done — you are ready. You are champing at the bit.

If you do not feel ready — if there is still a tug that says, "But the celebration, but the cinema, but maybe I am different" — then you are not ready, and I am glad you told yourself the truth. Do not force it. Put this chapter down and re-read the book from the start, with an open mind, exactly as you read it the first time. The safe we have been opening number by number is still there. We followed all the instructions in order for exactly this reason. When the last number clicks, the door swings. There is no push.

I will not ask you to summon effort. I will not ask you to be strong.

I ask only that you be honest and thorough. Have you seen that BAD SUGAR never was a genuine pleasure, treat or lift? Have you felt, in your own 3pm body and your own cinema row, that the credit belonged to the moment, not the dose? Have you understood that the trap is the Big Sweet Lie feeding the Sugar Worm, not a flaw in you? If the answer is yes — warmly, flatly yes — then stay with me. The rest is joyful and short.

There is nothing to give up. That is not a slogan to make you feel brave. It is the fact that remains when every justification has been measured and found to borrow its beauty from somewhere else. Keep it with you as we take the last ordinary step.

### THE LAST ORDINARY SWEET

We are not going to do a strange experiment. I am not going to ask you to suck a sweet in a laboratory way, to stare at it, to prove you are disciplined. That would be the Willpower Method wearing a different hat — and we have left that method behind with pity, not with practice.

I am going to ask you to do what you have always done, one last time, as yourself.

At your kitchen table, take the last ordinary sweet or starchy BAD SUGAR you would normally have — the biscuit from the tin, the chocolate square, the white bread roll, the packet you keep for "just a treat." Not the biggest, not the most exotic. The ordinary one. The one the Worm knows by name.

Eat it. Not hurried, not guilty, not wept over. Eat it with attention. Taste it truly.

Notice what it actually is, now that the Lie is quieter. The first second is a blast of weaponized sweetness, too sweet, almost chemical. Then the cloy. The faintly sticky film on your teeth. The quick, wired lift that is already tipping. The hand that wants to go again before the mouth is empty. Ask yourself, calmly, as you chew: where is the love? Where is the reward? Where is the energy that was promised?

The fact is, there is none. There is a dose doing what a dose does — pinching the nerves it will pretend to soothe, borrowing warmth from a moment you supplied. You are watching the con do its work while you know how it is done. Let that be enough. You do not need to hate the taste. You need only see it. The pleasure you thought was in the packet was always in you — in your own palate, your own company, your own life — and the packet was sneaking a ride.

When you have finished that last ordinary mouthful, stand up. You are not mourning. You are finishing a job. We have named the trap together, we have seen the Big Sweet Lie that feeds the Sugar Worm, we have measured the beautiful lie and watched it shrink under our hands. The combination lock has clicked through every number. The door is open. Now you step through.

### THE VOW

Make the vow out loud. Not in your head. In the kitchen, with your own voice in your own ears.

Say this, mean it, and hear how steady you sound:

"I will never feed the Sugar Worm again. I will never again take BAD SUGAR as a treat, a lift, or a reward. The Big Sweet Lie is dead. I am free."

Say it again if you want to. Not to convince yourself — to hear yourself choose. This is not a sentence to be served. It is an escape claimed.

And hear my reply, warm and absolute and without a second's delay:

Don't wait to be free — you already are. You are free as of now.

Not tomorrow morning. Not after three good days to prove it. Not when the Worm has starved. Now. The belief is dead, so the trap is dead. The body will catch up. You have stepped out of the cage and the door will not swing shut behind you unless you invite it to. You have stopped easily, immediately and permanently — not because you forced yourself, but because there is nothing left to want.

That feeling of lightness you have right now — the slight wonder that it can be this simple — that is freedom, not fantasy. Hold it. Let it widen. For the rest of your life it will widen, because you will live it, not count it.

The freedom will not feel like triumph every hour. For a few days, if a little hump comes at all, it is a small, mild, slightly restless flicker. We know from those who have walked this same few days that any such hump, if it comes, tends to peak in two to five days and then pass on its own as the Sugar Worm starves. That is the whole physical story — mild but well-defined, already dying, not mighty, not dangerous — and we do not need to fight it.

You may also notice the old afternoon dip still visits once or twice as your body steadies after its years on the spike-then-drop yo-yo. The dip is real as an after-effect of the spike — and, just to put your mind completely at rest, this is not low blood sugar as doctors define it. It is the echo of the trick, not an emergency, and ordinary real food brings you level again without a fix.

If that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling flickers in the evening when the packet used to rustle, do not test it. Do not argue with it. Do not white-knuckle past it. Rejoice. Name it for what it is and smile that it is dying.

I told you earlier that you cannot win by telling yourself "don't think of cake" — tell a person not to think of cake and the mind serves cake on a platter, warm icing and all. That is the pink elephant that proves suppression fails. We do not suppress here. We reframe. Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — the smell from a bakery, the bright packet at child-eye height, the polite offer at a birthday — we do not say "I must not." We think, instantly, joyfully, with full meaning:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

That is not a chant to drown out desire. That is the truth spoken back to the thought. The thought used to mean "I am deprived." Now it means "I have escaped." The same cue that once fired craving now fires celebration. The Worm whines. You celebrate. You do not have to believe the sentence hard. You only have to think it whenever the old idea appears, and the feeling follows the thought, easily, the way your eyes followed your hands on the tables.

This is your script for any future sugar thought, and it is the only one you will ever need.

To make it unmissable, here are your three instructions for freedom. Follow all the instructions — not because I say so, but because you have seen why each one matters and you want what follows:

1. **NEVER THINK "I CAN'T HAVE IT" — THINK "FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!"** — Swap deprivation for celebration whenever sugar crosses your mind.

2. **NEVER USE A SUBSTITUTE THAT FEEDS THE SAME LIE** — Don't swap one BAD SUGAR for another.

3. **REJOICE AT THE WORM'S WHINE** — When you feel that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling, be glad — it is dying.

Keep them where you can see them tonight. Say them aloud once more before you sleep. They are not rules to strain after. They are the new way you talk to yourself now that the Big Sweet Lie is dead.

### THE TWO DOORS THAT WOULD PULL YOU BACK

There are only two ways the Lie ever gets back in after today. Both arrive as quiet, reasonable voices that sound like you. You will recognise them because I will lend you the words now, before they come.

The first is the bad-day rescue: "Give yourself a break. You have had a horrible day. Just this once, you deserve a little comfort. You can be perfect tomorrow."

The second is the single-dose permission: "One can't hurt. Just one. You have proved you are free — so one to celebrate won't count. You can keep the special ones."

Hear how tender they sound. That is why they work. And hear how flatly false they are once understood.

The bad day does not need a rescue from the thing that made bad days worse. BAD SUGAR never comforted a bad day — it added tired, wired and crashing to whatever was already hard, then charged you shame for the help. The comfort you craved was rest, kindness, company, warmth — all of which arrive faster and stay longer without the hit. Will you ask the person who pushed you in the river to save you again?

And the single dose is not single. We have lived that film. You remember the lived truth: "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop." One permitted sliver becomes the box because the dose feeds the Worm and revives the Big Sweet Lie in one bite — not because you are weak, but because you have just fed the very lie you killed. There is no healthy level other than zero inside the BAD SUGAR line, not because nature is mean, but because one deliberate exception reopens the question you have just closed. An accidental crumb, a mistaken ingredient — your body can cope with a blip, but your mind will not cope with a deliberate "just one." Guard the belief, not the bite.

So when either voice comes — and it may, softly — answer it with the same joyful script. Do not debate. Do not weigh tomorrow. Think: FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and get on with enjoying your life. The thought will pass the way a wave passes. You do not chase it and you do not fear it. You watch it go and feel glad you did not board it.

### ALREADY FREE — WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

Do not look for substitutes. Do not go hunting for a "healthy" BAD SUGAR, a sweetener that hits the same note, a better packet to feed the same lie. That is just the trap repainted. Eat your real food — fruit, vegetables, plain dairy as part of real meals, bread that is food not confection, the house that was always the house before the gatecrasher arrived — and let your palate come back. Nothing extra is required. Change nothing else in your life to prove anything. Live your life exactly where it is, with one difference: you no longer feed that con.

When you see others taking BAD SUGAR — the queue at the counter, the office tin doing its rounds, the friend who says "I've been good this week, so just a treat" — do not envy them. Pity them, gently, as you would have wanted to be pitied, not shamed, when you were inside. Does that really look like freedom? The hand going back to the packet, the negotiation, the crash afterwards? You are not being deprived. They are. Your thought again is FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! — not with smugness, but with relief that you are no longer on that roller coaster.

And if you slip? If one evening you find yourself with a wrapper in your hand and a strange, sad aftertaste? Do not make a tragedy of it. Do not say "I have failed" and hurl yourself back in. A slip is a rumble strip, not a crash — a loud, kind warning to slow down and close the Lie again immediately. It revives nothing unless you let the belief back in. Spit it out if you can, say your vow again out loud, think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and go on free. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief and the behaviour follows.

You may feel a flicker tomorrow morning when the kettle goes on and the biscuit does not. That flicker is not proof you need BAD SUGAR. It is cage-habit, the feet remembering the old pacing after the door is open. Let it flicker. Smile. The Worm whined, you rejoiced — it is starving.

You are free as of now. Not as of next week. Now. The Worm will starve. The Lie will stay dead as long as you never reopen the decision. The thoughts you now own already know how to answer every future whisper, so you need not learn another system.

I am proud of you. We were all conned together, and you have just walked out while the con was still talking. Keep walking. Live your ordinary days with clear taste and steady energy, and watch how quickly ordinary becomes wonderful when nothing is stealing from it.

### SUMMARY

- The last ordinary sweet was seen for what it is — a sickly hit that briefly relieves the low it created — and the solemn vow never to feed it again confers freedom instantly, not at a future milestone.
- The physical aftermath, if it comes at all, is mild and brief, peaking in days and passing as the Worm starves, and any dip after sweetness is the trap's echo, not low blood sugar as doctors define it.
- A single deliberate dose revives the whole trap by feeding the belief that keeps craving alive, while the bad-day rescue is the perpetrator posing as comfort; both dissolve the moment they are named and answered with celebration.
- The pink-elephant mind cannot be ordered not to think — it is re-framed, so every passing thought of BAD SUGAR becomes a cue to think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and to rejoice when the whine is felt dying.
