Chapter 6
EATING LIKE WE WERE BUILT TO

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the kitchen at noon, true hunger arriving on its own, an apple that snaps, roast vegetables, bread that is food not confection, the clean finish of enough, the steady hum that follows

*Your body already knows how to want, enjoy and finish eating — without a single dose of BAD SUGAR.*

### THE QUIET WORRY

You have seen the treat take. You have seen the lift steal.

And underneath, a quieter worry has been sitting there, hasn't it.

"If I let go of BAD SUGAR, what is left? Will eating become a chore? Something bland and measured and disciplined forever?"

I understand that worry. We all felt it. We were taught that eating is control and BAD SUGAR is the fun part — that without the packet, the biscuit, the sweet drink, food would be grey and we would be left managing ourselves for good.

It is the other way around.

BAD SUGAR did not make eating fun. It interrupted eating. It borrowed the pleasure that was already there, left you tired and restless, and then took the credit for any relief you felt when you briefly stopped chewing it.

I want to show you something you have not felt properly for a long while — not by arguing, but by living it with you for a few minutes. No rules. No measuring. Just attention.

### HUNGER — THE SIGNAL RETURNS

We have forgotten what hunger actually feels like.

Not that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling that flickers an hour after a sweet. That is the little whine of the trap closing again. Real hunger does not nag and scratch. It arrives.

Think of the last time you were properly busy — moving, working, outside — and you suddenly noticed: I am hungry. Not panicked. Not shaky. Just clear, open, hollow in the best way. Your mouth waters a little. Colours look a bit brighter. You can picture an apple.

That is your body speaking in its own language. It is not a flaw to be fixed with a fix. It is an instruction from a system that has managed you marvellously since the day you were born.

We — all of us caught in the Sugar Trap — learned to answer every bodily signal with the same packet. Bored? Packet. Tired? Packet. Flat? Packet. Real hunger got lumped in with the trap's whine until we could not tell them apart. So we never let true hunger finish its sentence.

Let it finish now.

Sit with me at noon. You have not snacked. The morning's BAD SUGAR is long gone. There is a clean, light emptiness low in the belly. Not painful. Not urgent. Just unmistakable: the body saying, now. Now is good.

Notice how calm that signal is. It does not shout "RIGHT NOW OR ELSE." It waits for you. It asks. And your body, when it asks like that, already knows what it wants — not a dose, not a hit. Food.

Does real hunger feel like panic, or does it feel like readiness?

Does it feel like punishment, or like an invitation you can trust?

You know. You have just felt it.

### THE MEAL ITSELF

Now let us eat.

Not a plan. Not a prescription. An ordinary meal on a real plate.

Take the apple first. It snaps. Juice runs down the chin if you let it. The sweetness is bright and cold, and behind it is crunch and water and skin. You chew — really chew — and after three or four bites your mouth is alive in a way no engineered sweetness ever made it. No coating on the teeth. No sour after-film. Just clean.

Then the roast vegetables. A tray from the oven — carrots, peppers, onions browned at the edges, salt and olive oil doing their honest work. They are sweet too, but it is a rooted sweetness, deep and warm. You taste earth and fire, not a number on a packet. You keep chewing because chewing feels good. Your jaw knows this work. Your saliva knows this food.

And bread — bread that is food, not confection. Not white fluff that dissolves into sugar before you have swallowed it. Real bread, torn with your hands, with a crust that resists a little and a crumb that is soft and sour. You put it next to the vegetables, perhaps with cheese that has been made properly, or beans, or fish, whatever real food you love. You eat it together and the house makes sense. Real food was the house all along. BAD SUGAR only ever gatecrashed it, ate the host, and told you it *was* the party.

Stay in that meal with me for another minute.

You are not counting. You are not controlling. You are chewing, tasting, noticing. Flavour gets brighter as you go on, not duller. The second half of the plate is as good as the first — better, because hunger is still there making everything delicious.

And then — and this is the marvellous part we have been robbed of — you feel it change.

Not a crash. Not a jolt. A gentle finish. The hollow fills and rounds out. The flavours are still lovely, but the *need* for the next bite softens. You put your fork down, not because you should, but because you want to. There is a clean satisfaction that sits in the middle of you, warm and quiet and complete. You are done. And you are pleased to be done.

I have sat at that table a thousand times since I became free, and I promise you this: there is no flatness in it. There is no discipline in it. There is pleasure, steady and generous, from the first bite to the putting-down of the fork, and then an easy energy that simply continues.

Where was the deprivation? Where was the blandness you feared?

You did not miss BAD SUGAR in that meal. BAD SUGAR would have taken that steady pleasure away.

This is not a technique I am asking you to learn. This is what eating feels like when you let it happen. Your body makes the glucose it needs without a single dose of BAD SUGAR, so this is not removing nourishment. It is removing the con that pretended to be nourishment. The awe is in how little you have to do. You get hungry. You eat real food you like. You stop when you are satisfied. You feel steady afterwards. Your body does the rest, and it does it well.

Once you feel it — really feel it, not as an idea but as a mouthful — you understand that freedom is not a life of managing food. It is a life of enjoying food without interference. And you find you eat well easily, immediately and permanently, because there is nothing to force and nothing to miss.

### ENOUGH IS A FEELING

We were sold a lie that "enough" does not exist. That appetite is endless and only sugar makes life sweet enough to bear.

Enough exists. You just felt it. It arrived without a calculation, without willpower, without a rule. Your tongue got quiet. Your belly felt easy. Your mind moved on — not to the next packet, but to the afternoon, to work, to the people in the room.

Isn't that marvellous? The oldest system in your body — older than any packet, any slogan, any lobby that profits while you pay — still knows exactly how to signal, enjoy and complete. It never left you. It was only drowned out.

You do not need to learn to eat. You need to stop letting BAD SUGAR interrupt eating and then sell the interruption back to you as the fun part.

Think, for a second, of the steady hum after a real meal like that. No wiring. No jitters. No sudden need to go again. Just clear, calm energy that lets you get on with enjoying your life. That hum is not a trick. That is your natural baseline returning — the baseline you never rose above with a fix, only fell below and briefly clawed back toward.

You have not been living without pleasure. You have been living without this pleasure — the clean one. The one that finishes.

### SUMMARY

- Eating without BAD SUGAR is not bland control — it is felt pleasure from hunger through to satisfaction, with real food as favourite.
- True hunger is a calm, clear invitation, not a needy whine, and real satisfaction is a warm, clean finish, not a crash.
- Your body already knows how to want, enjoy and finish eating, and steady energy after a real meal is the return of your natural baseline.
