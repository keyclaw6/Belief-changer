Chapter 9
THE BODY KEEPS THE SCORE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the dentist's small mirror and the sour taste after sweets, the cuff that tightens around an arm at three in the afternoon, the scale in the bathroom that never lies, the bowl of real fruit in morning light

*BAD SUGAR is doing plenty TO you and nothing FOR you — and seeing it clearly is relief, not fear.*

### THE MIRROR

You are on your own side now. Good. You saw who built the canyon and why the wrappers glow. Indignation is the right feeling. Now let us keep it on your side by looking at what your own body has been telling you while the heat rose.

I am not here to scare you. I am here to show you what is already true, so you can feel the relief of walking out. The body keeps the score whether we read it or not.

Come with me to the dentist's chair. Not as a punishment — as a witness.

You open your mouth and the mirror goes in. Free sugars sit on the teeth and the plaque turns them to acid. Acid softens enamel. Softened enamel decays. That is the whole mechanism, plain and direct, and fluoride and brushing only mediate it. BAD SUGAR feeds the acid, the acid eats the tooth.

You felt it before anyone told you. The furry film after sweets. The sting of cold water on a molar that used to be fine. You called it sensitive teeth. It was information.

The fact is… that sweet was never free. The mouth paid first.

And we were taught to describe that payment as clumsiness — "I have bad teeth" — instead of what it is: a bodily stake that BAD SUGAR creates and then pretends to comfort you about. Your teeth are not a moral report card. They are proof the con has a cost.

If you have diabetes, an eating-disorder history, take medicines that affect blood sugar, or are pregnant, speak to your clinician before you change what you eat — that one ordinary clause honoured, and now back to the belief that frees you.

### THE WEIGHT IN THE CHEST

Step out of the dental chair and stand on the scale, then sit for the cuff.

At population level, high added-sugar intake is linked with higher heart and stroke risk — not because one hit causes a heart attack, not because avoiding sugar guarantees you will not have one, but because the load is deferred and cumulative. The liver is overloaded, blood pressure is raised, chronic inflammation is stoked, and the normal appetite control that tells you "enough" is bypassed. That is the pattern the institutional summaries describe at population level. It is not a sentence on you. It is a load on many bodies, year after year.

You felt that load before you named it. We all did. The `tired, wired and crashing` state that became our normal. Tired because the spike spent you. Wired because the sweet left you edgy and reaching. Crashing because the low after a high is the bill arriving, not a call for more. We thought that triple was personality, or age, or "low energy." It was the trap reporting its own working.

And the scale is not about vanity. It is about physics. When access is little and often, then forbidden, then binged, the body carries what the schedule presses onto it. Societies eating as the canyon invites them to eat blow past the world body's population ceilings without even trying — below ten percent of energy from free sugars, about fifty grams, with extra benefit below five — not as a personal prescription, not as "any dose is poison," but as a ceiling the ordinary trolley was built to fly over. You did not fail that ceiling. The norm was built past it and called normal.

Ask yourself, plainly, with the cuff still warm on your arm:

When you felt the three o'clock sag, did BAD SUGAR lift you above the steady state of a person free of it — or did it borrow from the next hour and leave you lower?

When your heart thumped after the sweets-and-cola afternoon, was that energy — or was that effort?

You know. Of course you do. You never rose above baseline. You paid to be returned toward it for twenty minutes, then you paid again.

There is a famous absurdity about moderation that fits here like a glove. "You can jump off the cliff as long as you don't fall more than a few metres." That is the safe-limit story BAD SUGAR sells you. There is no safe metres. The safe limit on a trap is to stop feeding it.

Like the frog where the heat rises by degrees, we thought feeling fine between hits meant we were fine. The water warmed slowly — a little more sugar in the bread, a little more sweetness in the yoghurt, a little more "treat yourself" on every corner — until `tired, wired and crashing` felt like character. That slow warming is not proof you were fine. It is proof the harm was gradual enough to be called normal.

### THE COMFORT THAT PREDICTED THE LOW

Now the tender one. The one we use when we are not hungry at all.

"Just a treat. I deserve it. It comforts me."

I hear you. I said it for years after hard days. If sugar comforted, the comfort should show later. It does not.

In long studies that followed thousands of men year after year, higher intake of sugar from sweets was linked with higher odds of later common mental disorder — a sweets-sugar exposure, men only for that incident finding, observational, with reverse causation checked and not found. The direction was the opposite of the promise. The treat for comfort predicted worse later mood, not relief.

Listen to what that means and what it does not mean. It does not mean a biscuit causes depression in everyone. It does not mean avoiding BAD SUGAR cures a mental condition, and it was not about women in that study at all. It means, in that cohort, the supposed relief left people lower later — the big sweet promise running backwards. You were sold love and delivered a dip.

You felt that dip too, before anyone measured it.

You ate the sweet for the stress. An hour later you felt lower, more irritable, less kind to the people you love — and you told yourself you needed another sweet to fix the irritability the sweet had created. The rescuer and the perpetrator wore the same wrapper.

Ask yourself again, gently, as your own witness:

Did that chocolate after the cinema, after the hard day, leave you calmer the next morning — or did you wake staler, thirstier, quicker to snap?

If it truly comforted, would you have needed more of it to feel less comforted than before?

The honest answer is one sentence long, and it ends the argument. The comfort crowed loudest before the bite and was smallest after it.

This is not pathology and it is not a character flaw. It is cue and blunting acting together: the wrapper and smell shout "this will be wonderful" before you touch it, while the satisfaction after dulls so you want the family size next time to chase the same flicker. That fits what the pictures show, it is not proof you are broken, and it is not a sentence that you are permanently damaged. It is a trick of loud cue and blunted reward — and a trick seen is a trick weakened.

### AND NOW THE RELIEF

So let us put it plainly, with no ledger and no lecture.

BAD SUGAR is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

It is doing plaque acid TO your teeth. It is doing deferred load TO your heart and vessels at population level — not one hit equals one heart attack, but high intake linked with higher risk, year after year. It is doing a predicted low TO your mood the day after the treat, in that sweets cohort, instead of the relief it promised. And it is doing that `tired, wired and crashing` state TO your afternoons, which you then blamed on yourself.

What is it doing FOR you? Stand the question up and make it answer.

Does it give you energy you would not have without it? No. You borrow energy from the next hour and repay it with interest.

Does it give you comfort that lasts? No. In the only long follow-up we can honestly lean on, the sweets-comfort predicted worse mood, not better.

Does it give you taste? Real sweetness is still here. GOOD SUGAR inside whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy as part of real food is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit. The con was never sweetness itself. It was the engineered, added, refined hit that weaponized sweetness and then sold itself as sweetness.

I want you to feel the inversion in your own mouth right now, not as an idea but as a bodily fact. Remember the last time you ate a packet sweet quickly and washed it down. Remember the sting, the thirst, the film, the edgy need to drink or eat again. That was not pleasure. That was the body scoring. Now remember biting a cold apple, or warm roasted carrots, or plain yoghurt with berries — the clean finish, the steady quiet after. That was not deprivation. That was the body saying thank you.

Do you want to keep quitting out of fear? I do not. Fear makes you feel deprived, and deprivation revives the Big Sweet Lie. I want you to stop out of relief. The same facts that could frighten you are the very facts that make relief enormous: you are not about to lose a pleasure and then pay a price — you are about to stop paying a price for something that never was a genuine pleasure, treat or lift.

See it from above for one last second and the two horizons click into place.

Stay in `the Sugar Trap`, feeding the Worm that complains and obeying the Lie that translates the whine as need, and you carry that low-grade harm with you `for the rest of your life` — a little more acid, a little more load, a little more mood dip after every "treat." Walk out, and you are free `for the rest of your life` — the same span, the same days, but without the weight on them.

It is not that you must be vigilant forever. It is that freedom lasts as long as the trap would have.

You were never choosing between health and joy. You were choosing between a con that is doing plenty TO you and nothing FOR you, and the steady, clear freedom that was yours before the con learned your name.

Do not quit from dread. Quit from delight that you do not have to do this any more.

You are not sweet-toothed. You are not weak. You were targeted. And a targeted body that keeps the score is a body that will keep the relief — steadily, kindly, day after day once you stop feeding it what hurts it.

### SUMMARY

- The body keeps a tangible score: free sugars turn to plaque acid that decays teeth, so mouth health is a real stake, not an abstract warning.
- High added-sugar intake is linked at population level with higher heart and stroke risk through deferred load on liver, pressure and inflammation, not through one hit causing disease.
- In long observational follow-up of men, higher sweets-sugar intake predicted higher odds of later low mood, the opposite of the promised comfort, without proving individual causation and without applying to everyone.
- The ordinary norm as built blows past population ceilings of below ten percent energy from free sugars and extra benefit below five, while still not being a personal prescription or "any dose is poison."
- Seeing these stakes clearly turns quitting from sacrifice into relief, and relief — not fear — is the feeling that keeps you free.
