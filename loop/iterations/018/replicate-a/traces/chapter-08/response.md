Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THIS CAGE?

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the supermarket canyon at child-eye height, the bright packets that call your name, the quiet laboratory that found the bliss point, the warning light you were taught to call a flaw

*You never had a sweet tooth — you were sold one, and the craving you call weakness is the body warning you the con is working.*

### THE CANYON

Walk it with me again, awake this time.

You push the trolley into the canyon and the walls rise on both sides — colour, high and bright, at your eye line and your child's eye line. That is not accident. At three feet high the packets shout loudest: cartoon tigers, smiling biscuits, a mountain of chocolate winking — "treat yourself," "you deserve it," "reward." At your height the same promise, only in a sleeker font. The air is sweet before you taste anything.

We all stood there. We all felt the same small tug, the same saliva before we had decided to want anything. We thought that tug was appetite. We thought it was us.

I want you to see it from above for one minute. This canyon was drawn by other hands.

Every packet facing you was placed there by a plan that spent months deciding the exact shade of red that makes a child reach, the exact rustle that makes a hand hurry, the exact height that makes a parent say "go on then, just one." The con is not that sugar exists. The con is that you meet it forty times before you meet a carrot, and each meeting is dressed as love.

You did not build `the Sugar Trap`. You walked into it because every road was paved straight into it. There is nothing defective about your appetite for noticing colour and sweetness — that is a healthy body doing its job. What is engineered is the world that exploits it.

It is the other way around.

### THE LABORATORY THAT NEVER SLEEPS

I have been in food factories. I have spoken to people who worked in them. They are not bakers, not really. They are teams — often literal armies of scientists — whose job is to weaponize abundance.

Their task is precise: find the bliss point. Not too sweet to sicken on the first bite, not so bland you stop. The exact crunch that disappears too fast so you reach again. The melt that coats the mouth and vanishes, leaving the brain asking where it went. The sugar plus fat plus salt that lights the circuit just enough to say "more," and the cue — the colour, the crinkle, the jingle — that fires before the mouth opens.

This is not a kitchen. It is a laboratory for wanting.

And it built a schedule into your day that you then blamed on yourself. Think of your own week. You restrict — "I will be good, I won't keep it in the house, just a little at the weekend." Then the window opens and the hit hits harder and the box empties. You swear it off. You restrict again. The next window, you want the family size. You call that lack of discipline. 

The pattern is the trap, not the person. When access is intermittent — restrict, crave, binge — the amount escalates and the craving shouts louder. When the same substance is simply there, freely, that same escalation does not appear. The schedule — not your character — drives the trap's tightening. That is how the canyon works: fasting in the morning, vending machine at eleven, "just a treat" at three, the whole packet at nine. Each forbidden fruit makes the next hit shine brighter.

On top of that, the packets do a second piece of engineering. They make the wrappers and smells glow before the bite. That glow is not pleasure. It is cue. Your circuits learn to fire at the sight long before the sugar lands, so you feel you want it desperately — and then, after, the satisfaction is smaller than the promise. You need more to chase the same flicker. We call that needing the family size now, and it fits with what the pictures show — the reward dulls while the cue shouts. It is an interpretation that fits the images, not proof that you are broken or that your brain is permanently damaged, and it is not a sentence that you cannot stop. It is a trick of loud cues and a blunted aftertaste.

And the scale is not personal. At population level the world body suggests staying below ten percent of energy from free sugars — about fifty grams — with extra benefit below five — and the ordinary trolley as it is designed blows past both without you even trying. You did not fail a limit. The norm was built past the limit and then called normal.

I say this harshly about the industry because it deserves harshness, and warmly to you because you deserve warmth. You were not weak-willed. You were outgunned. A single appetite, against an industry that profits every time that appetite is turned into a need — of course it learned to feel like needing.

Once you see `the Big Sweet Lie` as a product that was sold to you on purpose, something tender happens. The shame drains. Dignity returns.

### THE LIGHT ON THE DASHBOARD

So let us recode the two things you were taught to call flaws.

That crash at three, that heavy sag after the sweet, that `tired, wired and crashing` slump — you called it evidence you need sugar. It is the opposite. It is your body doing its faithful job, throwing the warning light on the dashboard: this was not fuel, this was a spike. The lift was the sugar spending you, not feeding you. The low after is not a call for more. It is the bill arriving.

And that empty, edgy flicker at seven when the packet rustles — you called it proof you love BAD SUGAR. It is the opposite, too. It is the warning light that says a cue has fired and the little loop is whining for its feed. It is not love speaking. It is the trap reporting that it is hungry.

I want you to feel the difference in your own body the next time it comes. Not as "I am craving, I am weak," but as "there is the light." A light is not a command. A light is information. You do not obey a petrol light by pouring more poison in the tank. You note it and you drive steady.

We all misread the lights because we were taught to. Like the frog that sits while the water heats by degrees, we thought feeling fine between hits meant no harm was accumulating. The heat rose slowly — a little more sugar in the bread, a little more sweetness in the yoghurt that should have been sour, a little more "treat yourself" on every corner — until steady felt like normal, though steady had been moving down for years. That slow boil is why you felt normal while the trap tightened. It is not evidence you were fine. It is evidence the warming was gradual.

See it, and the lights change sides. They stop being proof you need the cage and become proof the cage was built around you.

### WHO MADE YOU SAY "IT'S JUST MY SWEET TOOTH"?

I hear your objection coming before you say it, because it was my last shield too.

You say, "But I chose this. I've always had a sweet tooth. It's who I am."

I love you for saying it plainly. Now answer me plainly, just to yourself.

Did you choose the canyon, or did you find yourself in it after the canyon was built around every school run, every till, every petrol station?

Did your "sweet tooth" appear before the sweets, or did you learn to call that whine a tooth after the sweets had trained you to hear it that way?

And when the wrapper glows, do you feel pleasure, or do you feel the promise of pleasure that never quite arrives the way the advert promised?

You know the answers. Of course you do. You never chose to need the thing that makes you feel less steady. You were taught to describe a cue-driven whine as identity, so you would defend the cage as if it were your personality.

That is the final inversion, and it is the one that frees you. They sold you a con, then they sold you the line that you are the con. The form of the words is flattering — "sweet tooth," as if it were charming — but the function is brutal: keep the blame inside you so the profit stays outside you.

You are not sweet-toothed. You are not weak. You were targeted, from childhood, by a manufactured desire that now pays rent in your trolley and takes its wage from your calm.

There is nothing to defend here. There is a trap to see.

We fell into `the Sugar Trap` together because we were conned together, and `the Big Sweet Lie` gave our whine the voice of love until we believed it. The fact is, every bright packet that pretends to comfort you was engineered to make you feel you cannot be comforted without it.

It is not your appetite. It is their cage.

And a cage you did not build you can walk out of — indignantly, and on your own side at last.

### SUMMARY

- The craving you called appetite was engineered to feel like appetite — bright packets, bliss-point foods and "treat yourself" slogans are built to make you reach.
- The restrict-binge schedule itself drives escalation and the need for more — the trap tightens on intermittence, not on character.
- The glow of wrappers and the need for a bigger size fit a blunted reward and louder cues — a learned hyper-reactivity, not permanent damage or an excuse you cannot stop.
- Societal intake as designed blows past the world body's population ceilings — the norm was built high, you did not fail it.
- The crash and the craving are not flaws — they are warning lights that the con is working, and once seen they turn into reasons to leave it.
