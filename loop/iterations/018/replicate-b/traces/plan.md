# Master Plan — Quit Sugar

## 1. Book Core

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of BAD SUGAR — refined and added sugar plus junk carbs and sugary drinks consumed in the craving-snacking loop, not nutrition pedantry or whole-food eating.

**One reader (planner-facing state):** An adult who grazes, binges, or negotiates daily — hides wrappers, bargains for "just a treat," crashes at 3pm, has tried diets, moderation rules, and white-knuckle willpower and watched each collapse — who half-believes they are weak and half-suspects the whole approach was rigged. Warm, intelligent, already exhausted by self-blame, ready to be shown the trap rather than scolded.

**Load-bearing false belief:** *BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure, treat, and energy lift that makes life sweeter, steadier, and kinder — and I would be deprived, flat, or joyless without it.*

**Through-line:** The treat that takes — how a con that robs you of taste, energy, and calm sold itself as the very thing that gives them, and how seeing that inversion leaves nothing to miss.

**Format:** Full-length Easyway, ~15 chapters, ~61,000 words. One continuous belief-change arc with short, single-purpose chapters, built on the three-part spine (world / inhabit-eating / last ordinary instance + ordinary life + short recap). Front matter carries authority dossier and entry contract before any argument.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene (to be met head-on, once):** The cherished celebration — cinema with chocolate, birthday cake with family, the "I deserve this" reward after a hard day when sugar feels like love itself.

**Destination state:** Whenever the reader thinks about BAD SUGAR, they feel relief and freedom that they no longer feed it — *happy to be free*, not counting days. Stopping is felt as escape from a trap, not sacrifice of a pleasure. The last ordinary instance is behind them; ordinary days prove it.

**One fresh ending reframe (saved for the final movement only):** *You were never giving up sweetness — you were being robbed of it. Real sweetness returns now that you have stopped poisoning your own palate.* No chapter before the close may stage this reframe.

### Fork Decisions (style guide §4 — defaults are Carr)

**Fork 1 — Inner monster:** **DEFAULT CARR — Full personification.** Two proprietary mechanism characters, original and behavior-fitted, frozen in the mantra sheet:
- **The Sugar Worm** = the trivial, mild physical loop — the little creature that complains with an empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling and starves within days when unfed.
- **The Big Sweet Lie** = the dominant belief system — the lifelong brainwashing that misreads the Worm's whine as proof BAD SUGAR gives pleasure, energy, comfort, or reward.
The craving is never a mighty enemy demanding willpower; it is a small, already-dying parasite whose "death throes" become reasons to rejoice. No chapter may blend this with a no-monster frame.

**Fork 2 — Total abstinence vs autonomy:** **DEFAULT CARR — Total cessation within the line.** The book commands total abstinence from BAD SUGAR. Moderation is foreclosed with the pincer and the cliff-jump image. Autonomy is preserved only outside the line: GOOD SUGAR (see Redefinition) remains free.

**Fork 3 — Neuroscience / hard fact:** **DEFAULT CARR — Hard facts at full Carr force, then relief.** Vivid, flat, unhedged consequences (weight, teeth, metabolic load, mood) are delivered as settled fact wherever the argument owns them, then the chapter re-lands on escape-joy and states explicitly that we do not want the reader quitting *out of* fear. No hedging of core reframes; no literature-review texture.

**Fork 4 — Villain:** **Two villains, both hit hard.** (a) The sugar and ultra-processed food industry that engineered and marketed the trap. (b) The named anti-method — **the Willpower Method** — that kept the reader in the trap by selling strain as the solution and licensing failure as proof of personal weakness.

**Fork 5 — Void:** **DEFAULT CARR — Change nothing else in your life.** The body returns to its natural baseline on its own; the reader inhabits old contexts confidently to prove freedom. No elaborate replacement system. Burgeon's root + positive-direction extension is not used for this behavior.

**Method fidelity (founder 2026-07-12):** Escape not sacrifice; warm to the person / vicious to the trap (never contempt for the reader); no willpower; fear raised at full force then disowned by the escape wherever assigned; immediate freedom after belief change; autonomy inside the frame; original prose; Fork-1 line executed exactly as above.

### Redefinition and Margin-for-Error Doctrine

**Precise BAD SUGAR line (boxed definition, decreed in Ch.1 and carried by the CAPS name thereafter):**

> **BAD SUGAR = any added or refined sugar + any product engineered around it + any starchy junk carb that behaves like sugar in the trap:** sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, pastries, puddings, ice cream, sugary drinks, sweetened yoghurts/cereals, white bread, white rice, chips, and other ultra-processed carbs eaten as snacks, lifts, or treats. When this book says *sugar*, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. GOOD SUGAR — the natural sweetness already inside whole fruit, vegetables, and plain dairy as part of real food — is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit.

Full trap logic ("there is no healthy level other than zero") runs **inside** the BAD SUGAR line — one BAD SUGAR keeps the Big Sweet Lie alive.

**Margin for error (seatbelt doctrine):** Your body can cope with an occasional blip — a stray ingredient, a mistaken bite — but your mind will not cope with a deliberate exception. An accidental blip revives nothing if the belief stays dead; a deliberate "just one" feeds the Worm and revives the Lie. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the behavior.

**Conditional bonus:** You are not reading this book to quit plain GOOD SUGAR foods, dairy, or alcohol — only BAD SUGAR. If you find freedom so good you want the upgrade, the door is open and the book points you to help — never as a condition of success.

### Clinical / Eating-Disorder Safety Perimeter

Scope is the everyday craving-snacking trap for a general adult reader. Non-goals: medical nutrition therapy, diabetes management, eating-disorder treatment, weight-loss-program mechanics, or prescribing any diet.

Practical-safety guardrail is defined once in the Instruction Spine as **CA-SAFE** (boxed advisory). It routes medical specifics to a professional and is never fused into instruction wording. Chapter prose honours it silently or in one short spoken clause; chapter cards cite CA-SAFE in guardrails only.

- Source ID: bank-02 B-003
- Grade: LIVED — recurrent self-report
- Scope/context: Adults describing the afternoon yo-yo across recovered writers; P-04 weighted, general adult everyday trap.

- Prohibited inference: That every reader has clinically low blood sugar, or that sugar is ever real "fuel."
- Empirical limit: Lived-experience self-report; not a glucose measurement; no diagnosis.
- Safety limit: No medical diagnosis claims from this quote; do not present everyday slumps as clinical hypoglycemia. See CA-SAFE.

**E-04 — Intermittent-binge model (animals)**
- Finding: Intermittent 12-h sugar access produces bingeing, withdrawal signs, craving, and cross-sensitization behaviorally with sugar as reinforcer.
- Research IDs: SEU-001
- Source ID: S-1
- Grade: SUPPORTED

- Permitted inference: A binge–restrict schedule can generate addiction-like behavioral patterns where sugar is the reinforcer.
- Prohibited inference: That this proves human sugar addiction as a DSM diagnosis or that any dose is toxic.
- Empirical limit: Rat model, intermittent access; translates cautiously to humans.
- Safety limit: Do not use as personal medical prediction.

**E-05 — Each sugar binge releases dopamine in nucleus accumbens**
- Finding: Each sugar binge releases dopamine in the nucleus accumbens, re-triggering the reward circuit (microdialysis, animals).
- Research IDs: SEU-002
- Source ID: S-2, S-8
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope/context: Animal reward-circuit measurement; magnitude smaller than drugs of abuse.
- Permitted inference: Sugar can repeatedly trigger the reward circuit with each binge.
- Prohibited inference: That dopamine equals pleasure or happiness, or that this magnitude equals drug addiction.
- Empirical limit: Animal microdialysis; wanting/liking distinction applies; not human in-vivo.
- Safety limit: None beyond not overstating magnitude.

**E-06 — Withdrawal dip after sugar bingeing (animals)**
- Finding: Withdrawal after sugar bingeing involves low accumbens dopamine with rise in opposing transmitter — a measured dip after the sugar.
- Research IDs: SEU-003
- Source ID: S-3
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope/context: Naloxone-precipitated, opiate-like model; animals.
- Permitted inference: A real measured dip can follow repeated sugar binges in this model, giving a neural analogue for the low that follows the hit.
- Prohibited inference: That human sugar withdrawal is a proven diagnosis or that everyday cravings equal clinical withdrawal.
- Empirical limit: Naloxone-precipitated; human sugar withdrawal not a diagnosis.
- Safety limit: Defuse fear; present as mild, not catastrophic.

**E-07 — Mild but well-defined (lab characterization)**

**E-09 — Opioid system + cue reactivity**

**E-10 — Contested: sugar addiction is open question**
- Finding: Leading Cambridge neuroscientists argue animal bingeing reflects access pattern, not sugar neurochemistry, and human evidence is thin; "sugar is addictive" is open question, not consensus. Both camps grant binge behaviors occur under intermittent access; dopamine ≠ pleasure.

- Empirical limit: Perspective synthesis; wanting/liking distinction central.
- Safety limit: Use to protect credibility; do not weaponize to dismiss help-seeking.

**E-11 — Prevalence: addiction-like eating on validated scales**

**E-12 — Mood: sweets predict later common mental disorder (men)**

### Guardrail Summary for Writers

- Every argument-bearing chapter lands one enacted transition that makes prior valuation less credible now through its owned evidence or logic; trust/definition/safety/recap/bridge/hand-off cards advance, protect, or hand over without becoming a second thesis.
- Freedom is conferred at CH-13 vow — never by streak or milestone. Day-counting and "one day at a time" never appear as method.
- Hard facts in CH-09, then immediate relief landing on escape-joy.
- Mechanism characters are vocabulary from CH-02 onward, never a standalone lesson after.
- Prevalence claim appears once (CH-02); CH-11 invokes it only as settled token without re-presenting the figure. Long testimony lives in main flow (CH-11, E-21), not appendix. Myths Q&A in CH-11. Margin doctrine in CH-01 and echoed at vow.
