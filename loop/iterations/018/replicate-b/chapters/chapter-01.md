Chapter 1
THE PROMISE — HOW TO READ THIS BOOK

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The kitchen counter after the packet is empty. A hidden wrapper in the bin. The safe with ten numbers that will not open until every number is entered in order.

*You do not need willpower to stop wanting BAD SUGAR — you need to see what it has really been doing to you, and then escape feels wonderful.*

### This Is Not What You Think It Is

Let me guess where you are.

You are holding this book at the kitchen counter. Maybe you just finished something sweet and told yourself that was the last one. Maybe you hid the wrapper. Maybe you are telling yourself you will be good tomorrow, or that you were good this week so you deserve this.

You are not here because you love reading about nutrition. You are here because a part of you is tired. Tired of bargaining. Tired of grazing and crashing and starting again on Monday. Tired of promising yourself you will be more careful this time and watching the same loop happen again by Wednesday.

And you are expecting me to lecture you. To tell you to be stricter. To give you rules and meal plans and a pep talk about discipline.

I will do none of that.

I am not a scold and this is not a diet book. We have had enough of being scolded. We have had enough of blaming ourselves for a trap that was built to catch us.

I write this as someone who lived inside the same loop you are in now. For years I grazed and snacked and told myself I had a sweet tooth, that it was just who I was. I hid what I ate. I made little deals: just one biscuit, just after lunch, just this packet and then I will stop. I knew the 3pm slump was coming as surely as the clock. I knew the sofa at 9pm would call for something sweet. I tried to cut down. I tried to be moderate. I tried to be strong. Each attempt ended the same way — with me feeling weaker and more ashamed, and with BAD SUGAR back in my hand.

Then I saw something so simple it changed everything. Not a trick. Not a superpower. I saw the trick itself. The whole thing flipped in a moment. I stopped — not with a grim face and a clenched jaw, but with relief. As if I had been carrying a heavy bag I did not know I could put down.

That is what this book is for. To show you the trick, plainly and kindly, so you feel what I felt: the relief of escape. Not sacrifice. Escape.

### The Promise I Make To You

I am going to make you a promise that sounds outrageous. I make it with total certainty, because I have seen it happen too many times to doubt it.

You can stop eating BAD SUGAR — not by suffering, not by white-knuckling, but with a clear head and a light heart — easily, immediately and permanently.

Read that again slowly. I mean each word literally.

Easily, because there will be no struggle once you see what BAD SUGAR really is. Immediately, because you do not need months of preparation or a perfect moment; freedom begins the moment the belief changes. And permanently, because you will have no nagging feeling of missing out, no sense that you are depriving yourself of something good.

Does that sound impossible? Of course it does. If someone had told me before I saw it, I would have rolled my eyes. I would have thought, easy for you to say. I have tried. Nothing is easy about this.

That reaction is completely normal. I expect your doubt. I welcome it. I only ask one thing of it: keep it with you as curiosity, not as a closed door.

And here is why you can afford to be curious.

For the next few chapters you risk nothing at all. You do not have to decide today whether I am right. You do not have to throw anything away or swear anything off. You just have to read with an open mind and let me show you what I found. If I am wrong, you will know quickly. If I am right, you will feel the ground move under the old belief.

So in that sense, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Think what that really means. You have nothing to lose because I am not asking you to stop yet, to suffer, to give anything up before you understand it. And you have everything to gain because if you see the trap as I did, you walk out of a prison that has been taking your money, your energy, your calm, and your peace with food — and you do not look back with longing. That is not a brave gamble. It is a free investigation.

### What I Mean When I Say BAD SUGAR

Before we go another page, let us agree on language. The food industry loves blurry language. It lets them sell poison in pretty paper and call it a treat. I am going to give you a sharp name that keeps the definition in every sentence.

From now on when I say sugar, I mean BAD SUGAR.

> **BAD SUGAR = any added or refined sugar + any product engineered around it + any starchy junk carb that behaves like sugar in the trap:** sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, pastries, puddings, ice cream, sugary drinks, sweetened yoghurts/cereals, white bread, white rice, chips, and other ultra-processed carbs eaten as snacks, lifts, or treats. When this book says *sugar*, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. GOOD SUGAR — the natural sweetness already inside whole fruit, vegetables, and plain dairy as part of real food — is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit.

That line is important, so let me put it plainly.

BAD SUGAR is the confection, not the peach. It is the engineered sweetness that hits you fast, lifts you for a moment, then drops you and calls you back. It is the packet that rustles, the bottle that hisses, the white bread that behaves like sugar as soon as you eat it.

GOOD SUGAR is not the trap. The sweetness that lives inside real food — an apple, a carrot, a bowl of plain yoghurt — comes wrapped in water, fibre, and nourishment, eaten as food, not as a fix. Your body knows what to do with it. We are not quitting that. This book is not about becoming afraid of fruit or turning every meal into a maths test. It is about escaping the refined, manufactured loop that has been stealing your taste for real food.

Inside the BAD SUGAR line, the trap logic is total: there is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than not feeding the trap. One deliberate dose keeps the belief alive. That sounds strict until you see why — and you will, clearly, in the next chapters.

And to keep you safe while we do this honest work: your body can cope with an occasional blip — a stray ingredient in a sauce, a mistaken bite at a friend's table. Your mind will not cope with a deliberate exception. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. If you have diabetes, an eating-disorder history, any medical condition, take medicines that affect blood sugar, or are pregnant, speak to your clinician about any big change in what you eat. If eating feels distressing, reach out now to a trusted person or your clinician. This book is a belief change about BAD SUGAR, not a substitute for medical care.

### The Combination Lock

Why must you read this book in order and follow every instruction I give you?

Imagine a safe with ten numbers in its combination. You turn the dial to 34, then 7, then 51, then 88 — each number clicks cleanly. You feel clever. You decide the ninth number is boring and skip it. You turn to the tenth and pull the handle.

The door does not open.

It does not open a little. It does not reward you for getting nine out of ten right. A safe is not a school test. Miss one number and it stays locked. Get the numbers out of order and it stays locked. The engineers designed it that way.

We are opening a safe together.

This book is not a collection of tips where you can take what you like and leave the rest. It is a sequence that dismantles a belief system piece by piece. Each chapter turns one number of the lock. If you skip a chapter, or read the end first, or decide you already know the bit about treats and energy, you will rob yourself of the click that makes the next chapter work. You will stay trapped not because you are weak, but because the lock has not yet been fully turned.

I say this not to be bossy. I say it because I want you free. I have watched too many intelligent, determined people fail with the very same knowledge in their heads but in the wrong order. They understood every sentence and missed the door.

So read in order. Follow each number as it comes. Let each chapter do its one job before you ask the next one to do its work. When the last number clicks, the door swings easily. You will feel it.

### How To Read This Book — The Two Agreements

There are only two things I ask you to agree to while you read. They are simple, and they make everything else work.

I ask them with warmth and with complete authority, because I know what happens when they are honoured and what happens when they are not.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND — Question everything you think you know about BAD SUGAR.**

**2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL THE VOW — Carry on exactly as normal while you read.**

That is all. Two agreements.

KEEP AN OPEN MIND means you bring your scepticism with you, but you bring your curiosity too. When I say something that jars — that BAD SUGAR gives you no genuine pleasure, no real energy, no true comfort — do not argue with me from the old belief. Instead, ask, is it possible that what I have always believed about this was installed in me? Could a whole industry have profited from that belief? Could my own low feeling be evidence not of need but of the trap itself? You do not have to believe me yet. Just stay open long enough to test each claim against your own life.

DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL THE VOW means exactly what it says. Do not try to be good while you read. Do not throw the biscuits away on page three to prove you are serious. Do not start a cutting-down plan alongside this book. Why? Because every time we have tried to stop through strain, we have made BAD SUGAR more precious and more powerful. Cutting down makes each hit feel more important. White-knuckling confirms the lie that you are depriving yourself of something wonderful. I do not want you white-knuckling. I want you seeing. If you try to stop before you see, you will turn this book into another willpower test — and willpower is the method that has failed you every time, not you.

So carry on exactly as normal. Eat as you would have eaten. Let BAD SUGAR be there, unthreatened, while we look at it calmly together. That takes away the panic that usually slams the mind shut. There is nothing to defend. No one is taking anything from you. We are just investigating.

When the moment comes, I will ask you to make a solemn vow. Not before. That vow will be joyful, not grim. You will know when you are ready because you will be champing at the bit — actually looking forward to leaving this behind. If you do not feel that yet when we get there, it simply means one of the numbers has not clicked; you re-read the chapter that should have turned it, and the click comes. There is no rush and no failure in that.

Hold these two agreements and the rest is easy. Break them and even the most beautiful argument cannot land. Keep them and you have nothing to lose and everything to gain, easily, immediately and permanently — not as a slogan, but as the lived feeling you will have when the final number turns.

### SUMMARY

- This book does not scold you or put you on a diet; it shows you a trap so you can walk out of it with relief.
- You do not need to stop or cut down while you read; you only need to stay open and keep reading in order.
- When I say sugar I mean BAD SUGAR, the engineered loop of refined sugar and junk carbs eaten as fixes and treats; the sweetness inside real whole food is not what you are escaping.
- The method works like a combination lock — miss one number or turn them out of order and the door stays locked; follow all of them in order and freedom opens easily.
