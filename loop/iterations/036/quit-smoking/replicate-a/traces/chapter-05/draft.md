Chapter 5
PARTIES, PAUSES AND MILDS

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The classroom doorway, the finished paragraph, the grocery bags on the step, the mild filter held to the light

*Your work threw the party, and the mild only made you puff harder for the same dose.*

The Pellet After Everything

You have watched the hand move after pressure and seen the papers sit still. Now we take the excuse that feels kindest, the one we keep because it sounds like kindness to ourselves.

"They are my little parties. I work hard. Surely I have earned this."

I spoke that way for years. I paid myself the same way. We all did.

Picture the chain as we lived it. The classroom door clicks shut. Forty faces gone. Chalk dust in the air. Voice tired in a good way. Before the next thought, the hand is in the pocket. Light in the corridor. That dose is called the reward for teaching the class.

An hour later at the desk. A paragraph fought for and found. The last sentence lands. Shoulders drop. Lean back. Light. That dose is called the reward for finishing the paragraph.

Later still. The drive to the shops and back. Bags on the step. Engine ticking as it cools. Light before the bags are carried in. That dose is called the reward for driving the miles and getting it done.

Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving the miles. Driving to the shops. Almost everything finished got a pellet lit on top of it.

I know how true the feeling is. The chest loosens a notch. The day gets a full stop. We say the dose crowns the work.

Put three plain questions to your own memory and answer from what you lived.

Who taught that class — you with your voice and your years, or the white stick burning in the corridor after?

Who found that sentence — you with your patience, or the ash lengthening above the page?

Who drove those miles and carried those bags — you with your hands and eyes on the road, or the dose lit on the step?

There is only one honest answer. You taught. You wrote. You drove. The dose arrived after and took the bow.

See how widely we spread that bow. Finished means light. Waiting means light. Good news means light. A hard hour got through means light. What kind of reward fits opposite moments so neatly? A true reward has a shape. This one has no shape except timing. It arrives when the tug calls and takes the name of whatever you were doing.

Who Threw That Party

Stay with the paragraph minute, because the detail gives the con away.

You push the chair back. Neck stiff. Eyes dry. The last line reads right. A small lift rises in the chest — done, for now. In that lift the hand moves. Match flares. First draw bites. We call the bite pleasure and the lift reward and fuse the two.

Take the dose out in your head and leave everything else. Chair still there. Stretch still there. Coffee still warm. The lift of done work still there. What is missing? Only lighting, sucking, ashing, the faint sour on the tongue. The satisfaction was already in the room before the match struck.

It is the other way around. The work made the minute good. The dose signed its name on work it never did.

Think of taste, which we guard almost as closely. We say we love the taste after food, with coffee, in the pause. Yet watch what taste does in real life. The first draw of the day bites harsh on empty lungs. The midday one dries the mouth and dulls the next bite of food. The evening one leaves a stale film that sends us for mints and water. If a food tasted that way we would send it back.

What we call taste is the hush of the tug for a breath, borrowed by the moment. After food we call it taste. After work we call it reward. After strain we called it calm. The name changes. The feed stays the same.

Look at it straight. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

The Mild With Holes In It

I hear the next voice, because it spoke loudly in me.

"All right about reward. But I smoke milds. Lights. Golds. I chose the softer kind. That must count for something."

I chose them too. The pack looked cleaner. The smoke felt thinner. The throat felt less hit. We took thinness for kindness.

Hold one of those filters up to the light and look close. Around the paper, in a neat ring, sit tiny vent holes. You never put them there. They were put there for the machine that tests the dose.

Picture a mask meant to keep out bad air, with holes pricked round the edge. Through the holes, outside air rushes in and thins what comes through. On the bench it measures lower. On a human face, lips and fingers close over the holes without knowing, the chest pulls harder to get the feel it seeks, the draw runs longer, the puff runs deeper.

That is what we did with milds. We covered the holes with lip and finger, drew harder without meaning to, held a fraction longer, and took what the machine missed. Not in exactly the same way for every smoker, but the direction is the same — the human takes the dose the bench says he missed.

This does not make the unvented dose a friend or a clever choice. It makes the vent a theatre trick. Same nicotine trap. Softer curtain.

What The Machine Smoked And What You Smoked

The machine sips. You seek.

The machine takes a fixed puff through open vents and writes down a low number. You put your mouth where the vents are, close them without noticing, and puff like a smoker — because you are one — seeking the level the Nipper asks for. You get the hit. The number on the packet stays low. The Smokescreen translates low number into safe permission, and permission keeps us lighting.

Ask it of your own throat. If mild meant mild in the body, why did the day hold the same count? Why did the tug call at the same forty minutes? Why did the hand move just as fast after the class, the paragraph, the shops? If the dose were truly lighter where it matters, the clock would have stretched. The clock never stretched.

That is the tell. The packet changed its clothes. The body kept its schedule.

We were conned twice on the same stick. First the stick stole the name of the work and called itself reward. Then the filter stole the name of care and called itself mild. Both names belong elsewhere. The reward belonged to you. The care never existed.

Keep Your Ears Clear

This is the point where other voices get loud. A friend means well. An article means well. An old idea in your own head means well. Switch to milds. Keep the after-dinner one. Pay yourself with one after hard work. They speak as if they have watched your desk and your corridor. They have not. They speak from inside the same Smokescreen we are clearing.

You do not need a second argument laid over this one while this one is still working. Let one piece of clear seeing finish before you borrow another.

**SUMMARY**
- The lift after teaching, writing, or errands belonged to finished work, not to the dose lit after it.
- The dose added lighting, sucking, ash, and a sour mouth while the satisfaction was already in the room.
- Taste after food or coffee was the tug going quiet for a breath, named after the moment it rode on.
- The mild filter measures lower on the bench because vents thin the sip, while lips and fingers close the vents and the smoker draws harder.
- Machine numbers do not mean the body took less, and the unchanged daily clock proves the schedule held.
