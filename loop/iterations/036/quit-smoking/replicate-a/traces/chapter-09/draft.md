Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

IN THIS CHAPTER
The pack weighed in the palm, the filter theatre and the mild promise, the fear of failing and the fear of succeeding

*You never grew this hunger in yourself; it was built for you, and both fears that pull at you were fitted with it.*

THE MORNING YOU HAVE JUST LIVED

You have stood at the sill with the mug in both hands and found the morning whole.

Feet to the cold boards. Water to the face. Kettle to song. Taste lingering after the plate. Legs moving to the doorway and back with nothing to tend. You have lived those hours as the body meant them, and you have seen the thin pull underneath for what it is, a faint edginess asking for its feed on the clock, not a message about what mornings and meals and pauses need.

And still something holds at the edges.

“I see it does nothing for the morning,” the voice says, low. “But what if I cannot be the one who leaves it? What if I fail again? And what if I do leave it — who am I at the table, at the bar, at work, without the thing that marked those places?”

I know that double pull because I lived it with the pack still in my pocket after I had stopped defending pleasure. Pleasure was gone as an argument. Fear took its place. Not fear of a tug under the ribs. Fear of me. Fear of being the type who always slides back. Fear of being flat and strange in a life that had a prop in every picture.

Look at that fear with clear eyes, because fear is the last glue.

We fell into this as children or as young workers wanting to look grown, to belong, to have something in the hand when hands felt awkward. None of us examined the first dose. Eyes watering. Throat burning. Head swimming. Coughing over the sink while the older smokers laughed. We learned to call that learning. We learned to inhale past the protest till protest went quiet. That was not nature ripening. That was training.

Ask it of your own start and answer from what you lived.

Did you choose the dose because you had weighed what it does for you and against you? Did you wake one morning with a born need for paper and ash? Did your lungs ask for bite before they had ever been bitten?

There is only one honest answer. The want arrived after the doses, not before them. We were conned into the first ones on phoney information — grown-up, calm, company, pause — and then the clockwork took over and we called the clockwork me.

You can look at that history now as a free observer, not as the accused.

The box did not find a need in you. It made one and then charged you to quiet it.

HOLD THE BOX IN YOUR HAND

Take the pack as you hold it today and look at it as an object, not as part of you.

Twenty tubes in a flip box. Cellophane crackle. Foil tongue. Filter tips all facing the same way like cartridges. Health print shouting from the side while the colours whisper from the front — gold, silver, blue, red. Take one out. White paper. Brown filter. Tiny cork print to look like something natural. Weight almost nothing. Smell faintly sweet and chemical before lighting, foul after.

Hold the whole pack in the palm. Feel its weight as a day’s supply box.

That is what it is. Not a friend. Not a pleasure kit. A dispenser for the day’s feeds, counted out by a factory. Morning feed. Mug feed. Work feed. Plate feed. Talk feed. Doorway feed. Night feed. Twenty measured squirts of a drug through paper tubes, spaced by the fading of the last squirt.

Ask it plain while the box sits in the palm.

Who counted the twenty? You did not count twenty needs before you woke. The manufacturer counted twenty gaps. Who spaced the gaps through your day? You did not space hungers around meals. The fading dose spaced them and meals took the blame. Who decided the tube should look clean and the filter should look kind? You did not ask for kindness in a tube. The designer did.

We talk about our brand as if it were a taste we chose. Mild. Lights. Menthol. Regular. King size. Slim. We defend the packet as if it held our character. The packet holds no character. It holds tubes that deliver the same job through different theatre.

That box is the nicotine trap in your palm.

See the theatre. The filter is not a kindness. It is a stage prop that lets the mouth accept the bite long enough to get the dose. The mild name is not a description. It is a costume that lets the mind accept the same bite as gentler while the hand does the real adjusting underneath. The slim is not elegance. It is the same dispenser in a different dress. The menthol is not freshness. It is flavour laid over bite so bite can pass.

We paid for theatre and called it choice.

I speak as a man who carried his brand like a flag and argued for its taste in pubs. Taste? Paper burning tastes of nothing a tongue would ask for twice. What I defended was the familiarity of my own feeds arriving on time in a familiar dress. Take the dress away and the job stands bare.

The job is to keep the faint edginess returning and to sell you its brief quiet, over and over, till you believe the quiet is pleasure and the return is nature.

Once seen, the box cannot go back to being part of the hand. It stands as what it always was, a day’s supply box made elsewhere and placed in your palm for profit.

THE PRODUCT IS NICOTINE

One of their own scientists wrote it down in an internal planning memo, not in a public brochure, not for you to read.

The cigarette should be conceived not as a product but as a package. the product is nicotine.

Read that again in plain words. The paper is wrapping. The tobacco is stuffing. The filter is theatre. The brand is paint. What is being sold through all of it is the dose that keeps the clock ticking in the body so the next tube will be wanted within the hour.

That is not my phrase. That is the maker’s view of his own goods, written when he thought no smoker was listening.

Ask what that sentence does to every line we told ourselves.

“I smoke for taste.” Would a taste need to hide behind menthol and filters and mild names to be borne? Would a taste need twenty repeats a day to be enjoyed? Food tastes. Drink tastes. Music holds. No true taste behaves like this, returning thinner within minutes and demanding again.

“I smoke for pause.” Would a pause need a measured squirt to be a pause? Legs pause. Eyes pause. Lungs pause in cold air. The pause you lived at the doorway stood whole without paper. What needed the tube was not the pause. It was the clock underneath the pause.

“I smoke because I choose it.” Would a choice need a dispenser counted to twenty and carried everywhere, with panic when the count runs low at midnight and the shops are shut? Choice does not panic. Need manufactured elsewhere panics.

The fact is the pleasure never sat in the tube. The tube rode on the pleasure of the moment and signed its name on it.

See the proportion. A vast industry, chemists and designers and packet artists, farms and factories and lorries, all bent to one narrow end — that the faint edginess returns on time and that you read its return as your own desire. Not your hunger. Their schedule, installed in your body by repetition, then read by you as character.

That story is the Smokescreen doing its work.

It works by making the manufactured feel personal. Your brand. Your break. Your moment. Your type. Take the personal pronouns away and what remains? A box. Tubes. A drug that fades fast and must be fed fast. A mind taught to lend that cycle its most intimate words.

Give the words back to their owners. The morning belongs to waking. The pause belongs to legs and air. The talk belongs to people. The box belongs to the seller. When each thing is given its owner, the box stands alone, small and mean, with no part of your life left inside it.

THE HOLE YOU SMOKE THROUGH

Look closer at the mild promise, because the mild promise proves the con better than any speech.

The machine says light. The packet says light. The numbers say low tar, low nicotine. The mouth says smoother. So the mind says kinder, safer, a step toward sense.

Now watch the human smoker, not the machine.

The machine takes a fixed puff through a holder. The vent holes in the filter stay open to the air and dilute the smoke. The numbers come out low.

We do not smoke like holders.

Lips close over the holes without knowing. Fingers close over the holes without knowing. The chest pulls deeper because the draw feels thin and the body asks for the accustomed press. The puff runs longer. The next tube comes sooner because the quiet does not hold. What the vents diluted on the bench, the human undid at the mouth.

That is not accusation. That is design meeting body. A dose-seeking hand will always close the gap that stands between it and the accustomed quiet. We all did it. I did it for years while telling myself I had been sensible to switch to silver.

Ask it of your own silver packet and stay exact.

Did the day’s count fall when you moved to light? Did the thin pull wait longer between tubes? Did the hand lift less often, or did it lift as often and pull a fraction harder while the mind rested on the word mild?

There is only one honest answer in most mouths. The count stayed. The pull hardened. The mind rested while the hand worked harder. We bought the word and paid with the lungs.

The vents are a hole in a gas mask sold as fresh air. Cover the hole without meaning to and breathe harder through the mask, then thank the mask for being mild. That is the size of the fraud, done not in a back room but at your own lips, by your own fingers, without your knowledge, while the packet kept its clean colours.

And mark this, because the seller counts on you missing it. The answer is not to hunt for a stronger tube that lets you puff less. Strong or mild, filtered or plain, slim or king, the job is the same — to deliver the fading dose and bring you back within the hour. To argue which costume is kinder is to stay inside the fitting room. The point is not the costume. The point is the fitting.

Does that make you angry? Good. Be angry at the fitting, never at the fitted. You were not foolish to cover the holes. You were a normal body seeking its accustomed quiet through whatever tube was offered, while the offer called itself choice. The anger belongs on the bench that measured with open vents and printed low numbers, not on the mouth that closed them in life.

See the mild theatre for what it is and the last sensible exception falls. Not milder. Only harder puffed. Not kindness. Only costume.

STALE, BREATHLESS AND CHAINED

Now name what the box charges for its theatre, not in money but in the state it leaves.

Money first, because money is the smallest part and still shames the arithmetic. Count the packs through a year. Count through ten years. Count what else that money could have held — coats that last, food that feeds, days that do not need counting. We did not count because counting would have forced the question, and the question would have forced the look. Small daily sums hide large lifetime theft. That is how dispensers like to be paid, coin by coin, never in a lump that would startle.

Then name the body.

Stale first. Mouth stale on waking. Nose dulled to tea and toast and rain. Fingers yellowed at the tips. Hair holding last night’s doorway. Clothes aired on the line and still carrying it. Partner turning a fraction in sleep from the breath. Child stepping back from the hug while we told ourselves they did not notice. We noticed. That is why the mints, the wash, the spray, the gum, the garage door, the window open in winter. A pleasure that needs hiding after it is not pleasure. It is mess rented as ritual.

Breathless next. Stairs that leave the chest heaving while the non-smoker beside you talks on steady. Hill that shortens the stride. Laugh that turns to cough. Morning cough over the sink that we called clearing, as if lungs were meant to clear ash every morning for life. The body came fitted to climb and laugh and run for buses without commentary. We laid bite across that fitting daily and called the narrowing normal. Normal does not wheeze on two flights. Narrowed does.

Chained last, and chained is the worst because it touches every hour. Cannot sit through the film without glancing at the watch. Cannot take the long flight without counting the gaps. Cannot stand the talk without stepping out to tend. Cannot face the row, the wait, the delay, without the hand moving. Twenty managements a day — light, shield, draw, tap, stub, wash — laid across tasks that others do unbroken. We called that habit. It was a leash measured to about an hour, tugged on schedule.

Put the three together till they stand as one state, not three complaints. Mouth coated, chest tight, day broken into feeds. That is the permanent address the box rents you while it calls itself pause and friend.

Stale, breathless and chained.

Speak that triple as fact, not as insult. I lived inside it and told myself outsiders exaggerated. They did not exaggerate. The ashtray smell in the curtains was real. The stairs were real. The glance at the watch in the cinema was real. What was unreal was the line that said this state was the price of pleasure. It was not the price of pleasure. It was the product doing its work while pleasure stood elsewhere, untouched.

And here is the hard part stated flat, because you must hear it once at full force before we put fear back in its place.

This state does not stay still. Coating thickens. Tightness deepens. The leash shortens as years lay dose on dose. The doctors have their names for where that road runs — cells that forget their manners, vessels that narrow, lungs that lose their spring — and if you feel ill or worried, speak to a clinician for your own care. I do not say that to sentence you. I say it because the seller knew the road and kept counting the twenty while you walked it, and you have a right to be told plain what the twenty buys.

Do not change because you are frightened. Fear never freed anyone; it only made the hand shake harder for the next tube. Change because the box never gave you a single thing worth the rent, and you can see it now with the paint off. The fright belongs to the facts. The motive belongs elsewhere, in the clear morning you have already lived and liked.

THE TWO ROPES

With pleasure gone and cost seen, fear steps forward as the last guard at the door. It comes as two voices pulling opposite ways.

First rope: “You will fail.”

It speaks in your own history. Twenty attempts. Drawer of patches and gum. Calendar marks. Promises to the partner. Monday starts. The voice says you are the type who slides, that the fifth day will find you at the shop, that the night out will undo you, that wanting proves you cannot. It points to every past effort that ended in the doorway and says that ending is you.

Second rope: “You will succeed and hate it.”

It speaks in pictures of the future without. Breaks with nothing in the hand. Meals that do not close. Stress with no off-switch. Nights out watching others lift while you sit empty. Mornings flat. Work flat. Talk flat. A smaller life lived in grey, holding out forever against a want that never fully dies. It says freedom is possible but not worth having, that you will be a stranger to yourself, that the prop held the person up and the person will sag without it.

Pull the first rope and you guarantee the failure you dread, because a mind told it will fail stops looking and starts waiting to be proved right. Pull the second rope and you guarantee the grey you dread, because a mind told life will be flat reads every normal hour as deprivation and begs for the paint back. Either pull tightens the knot. That is the use of both.

See the geometry. Two ropes, one neck, both ends held by the same hands. The box taught the first fear by beating you with the wrong method till you blamed your nature. The box taught the second fear by lending its name to every pleasure till you believed pleasure would go with it. Fail and you smoke. Succeed in gloom and you smoke to cure the gloom. Both roads lead back to the till.

That double pull is the tug-of-war of fear.

Name it when it speaks and it loses its cover. “I will fail” is not your character speaking. It is the memory of efforts made while the belief was still intact, while every hour was framed as loss and every tug was read as proof you needed the tube. Of course those efforts ended at the shop. They were built to end there, because no mind holds out forever against a felt loss. That failure proved the method, not you.

“I will be empty without it” is not prophecy. It is the paint talking. You have already stood at the sill and at the table and at the doorway and found each hour whole without paper. Empty of what? Bite and sourness and the six managements. The kettle still sings. The sauce still lingers. Legs still rest on standing. Which of those did the tube supply? None. The grey you fear is life with the covering on. Take the covering away and colour thickens.

Ask the two questions that close the trap on fear.

If the first rope were true and you were unable, why are you able to go hours without thought when attention is elsewhere — film running, work gripping, child ill in the night — with the body steady and the mind clear? Unable bodies do not go quiet for hours. Clockwork briefly unfed goes quiet. That quiet proves ability, not inability.

If the second rope were true and life without were flat, why did the morning you lived without feel thicker, not thinner — more taste, more air, more continuity, fewer breaks in the run? Flat lives do not thicken when the supposed pleasure is removed. Robbed lives do not feel more continuous when the robber steps out. Yours thickened because nothing was removed but interference.

It is the other way around. Not that you fear because quitting is hard and life will be grey. You were taught to fear so you would not look, and not looking kept the box in the palm.

I speak as a man who pulled both ropes till my hands burned. Dreading the attempt and dreading the success in the same hour, smoking to steady the dread that smoking had built. The dread was not a verdict on me. It was the last service of the paint, holding the box to the hand after taste and pause had let go.

Let both ropes fall. There is nothing to hold on either end but paper.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

Stand with that sentence and the rest steadies. The want has an address. It was packed in foil, vented for numbers, named mild for comfort, counted to twenty for the day, and taught as yours by repetition. When the thought rises later — at the table, at the bar, at the desk — you will know whose voice it borrows. You will see the seller behind the smoke and the thought will arrive already labelled, with nowhere to land.

SUMMARY
- The want was not born in me; it was trained in by first doses and kept ticking by feeds through the day.
- The pack is a day’s supply box, and the tube is wrapping around a dose, not a taste or a pause.
- Mild and filter are theatre; lips and fingers close the vents in life and the hand pulls harder while the packet prints low numbers.
- The daily rent is a coated mouth, a tightened chest and a day broken into feeds.
- Fear of failing and fear of succeeding pull opposite ways but both ends are held by the same box.
