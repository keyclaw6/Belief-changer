Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

IN THIS CHAPTER
The cold bedroom floor and the kettle, the cleared plate and the lingering taste, the work doorway and empty hands

*The morning, the meal and the pause were good before smoke joined them, and they stand whole when smoke no longer cuts across them.*

The Hand Before the Feet

We know that first minute too well to need it explained.

Alarm at six-fourteen. Eyes half open. The room still dark at the edges. Feet have not touched the floor and the hand has already found the pack on the bedside table. Lighter cold in the palm. Swing of legs, sit on the edge of the bed, first pull on empty lungs before water, before washing, before a word spoken. Bite at the throat. Rush behind the eyes. Sourness on the tongue that we called waking up.

I lived that minute for years. I told myself it started the day. I told myself I could not face the kettle without it. I told myself the day had two possible versions and the lighter decided which one I got.

Look at the minute with clear eyes.

What woke you? The bell woke you. The cold boards under the soles woke you. The stretch in the back woke you. The light at the curtain woke you. The bladder woke you. The body came up out of sleep as bodies have done every morning long before paper and ash existed. The dose joined the waking. It did not make the waking.

Ask it of your own bedroom and answer from what you lived.

Did the feet need the lighter to find the floor? Did the eyes need the paper to open? Did the blood need the bite to move to the legs?

There is only one honest answer. The morning began without the dose, and the dose laid its sour mouth across the beginning.

Watch the order. Sleep ends. Breath deepens on its own. Heart steps up its pace on its own. Muscles ask for stretch on their own. All of that was running before the hand moved. The hand moved on a different clock. Not the waking clock. The feeding clock. Eight hours without, thin pull built in the night, hand moving to quiet its own echo before the day has asked anything of you.

We called that first pull the best of the day. Smokers say it in doorways with a kind of pride. That first one is the best. Is it best because morning smoke is finer? Take the same dose at noon after four hours sealed where you could not smoke and it would feel the same press and settle. It feels large in the morning because the night has let the thin feeling build longest. A loud call quieted briefly feels large. Large quiet is still only quiet.

I speak as a man who coughed over the sink and called the cough waking up. The cough was not waking up. The stretch was waking up. The water on the face was waking up. The cough was the body objecting to being met at its cleanest hour with bite.

See what the minute cost. Before the kettle, mouth already coated. Before the first word to the person beside you, breath already soured. Before the street has shown itself, nose already dulled to tea and toast and rain. We did not start the day. We smudged the start and called the smudge a start.

The dose added nothing. It interrupted everything.

The Kettle Sings First

Now live the same minute as it is.

Feet to the cold boards. Toes curl, then settle. Spine lengthening, arms overhead, ribs opening. Water to the face, cold and plain. Kettle filling, click, low roar building to song. Steam on the window. Mug warm in both palms. Stand at the sill while the street comes awake. Throat clear. Mouth clear. Nose open.

That is the morning as the body meant it. Not a gap to be filled. A sequence already complete.

“But mornings will be empty,” the old voice says. “Food will not satisfy. Breaks will drag with nothing in the hand.”

I know that voice because I spoke it over the sink with the mug in one hand and the dose in the other, certain the mug needed the company.

Empty of what? The morning you just lived has feet, water, heat, steam, weight, light, sound. Which of those did the dose supply? It supplied bite and sourness and a thin quiet of its own want laid across them. Take that covering away and the morning does not thin. It thickens.

Follow the kettle minute by minute and give each thing its true owner.

The cold floor belongs to the house and the feet. The stretch belongs to the back that lay still all night. The water belongs to the pipes and the skin. The roar belongs to the element and the tin. The steam belongs to heat meeting glass. The warmth in the palms belongs to the mug and the hands that hold it. The first clear breath at the sill belongs to the lungs and the street. Where in that list is there a place where paper and ash did work? There is none. The dose stood beside the list and signed its name on it.

The fact is the pleasure was the kettle, the stretch, the first clear breath. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on them.

Ask it of the tea. Tomorrow, take it without commentary. No test. No count. Simple noticing. First sip hot, then cooling. Bitter where it should be bitter, sweet where milk softens it. Tongue reporting plainly because no film sits on it. Nose catching toast from the toaster, soap from the hands, rain from the sill crack. Does the tea need the bite to be tea? Did the tea ever ask for ash? The tea was whole before the hand learned to lift, and it stands whole when the hand no longer lifts on schedule.

Ask it of the light. That thin line under the curtain that widens while you stand. Eyes adjusting on their own. No paper taught them. They did it when you were five and they do it now. The body kept its instructions while we stopped listening.

“But I enjoy the pair,” the voice says. “Coffee and the morning dose go together.”

Did they go together from the start? Remember the first morning doses. Eyes watering. Throat burning. Head swimming. Mug pushed aside while you coughed. No one enjoys that pairing on first meeting. We trained the pairing by repetition till the mug seemed to call for the hand. The call was not in the mug. The call was in the gap between feeds landing at mug time. Move the feed and the call moves. Remove the feed and the call has nowhere to land. The mug stays. The morning stays. Only the arithmetic goes.

Stay with the sill longer, because the sill proves the size of what we feared.

Three breaths at the open crack. First breath shallow, chest still tight from sleep. Second breath deeper, down to the belly. Third breath steady, shoulders dropping without being told. No instruction needed. No method needed. The ribs know the shape. That is the body doing its ordinary work while attention rests on buses and bins and pigeons. The thin pull between doses never taught the ribs. It only laid a hum underneath while they worked.

That hum is what we mistook for need in the morning. A faint edginess behind the eyes, a lightness under the ribs, as if something needs checking. Hungry, tired, pressed men who never smoked pass through that same faint hum and think nothing of it. We were taught to read it as light me now. Read it plain and it is only the echo of the last feed fading.

Live five mornings running as noticing and the pairing loosens on its own. Feet, water, kettle, mug, sill. Same order. Same hands. No covering. You will find the morning does not ask for anything to complete it. It asks only to be stood inside.

The Plate That Finishes Itself

We paid for our food twice. Once with money. Once with taste.

Meal done. Plate warm and empty. Mouth full of the last bite — salt, oil, bread, the last of the sauce. Then the hand moving before thought has finished. Paper lit. First pull dragging straight across the tongue. The fine edge of flavour wiped to ash and sourness. We called that the end to the meal.

Ask it plain while the plate still sits before you.

Did the food need the ash to be finished? Did the sauce need the paper to linger? Did your tongue need the bite to know what it just ate?

Watch what the dose does to taste, not what the old lines say about it. The tongue is built to hold flavour after eating. That holding is part of eating. The dose cuts the holding short. It coats the tongue, dulls the nose, leaves its own stale film where butter and coffee stood. We mistook the film for a full stop. It was a smudge.

I speak as a man who once lit over every plate and told himself the meal was not closed without it. The meal was closed. I smudged the close and called the smudge ceremony.

Think of the kitchen you know. Pot on the hob. Onion softening. Bread torn, not sliced. The person across the table talking with a fork still in the air. The last bite taken slow because you do not want the talk to end. That is the meal doing its work. Hunger met. Flavour given. Company held. No part of that work asks for paper. The hand that lights does not finish the work. It stops the tongue reporting the work.

“After eating I need something,” the voice says. “The taste hangs and wants a finish.”

The taste hangs because tasting is what tongues do. The want underneath is not the meal asking for its end. It was only an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug asking for its feed on the meal’s time.

See the timing. Morning feed, then mug. Midday feed, then plate. Evening feed, then plate again. The gaps between feeds are about the length of a morning’s work or an afternoon’s work. Meals happen to land near the end of those gaps. The tug that would have risen at that hour anyway rises at the table and borrows the meal’s name. We say the meal calls for the dose. The clock calls for the dose and the meal takes the blame.

Try the plain test at the next plate while you still smoke as part of reading. Eat as usual. When the hand starts to move, wait three minutes and keep sitting. Keep the talk going. Keep the hands still at the table. Let the tongue do what it does. First minute the sauce stands. Second minute bread stands. Third minute coffee coming from the kitchen reaches the nose clean because no paper stands between. Where is the unfinished feeling now? It thins while flavour stays. What thins was never the meal. What stays was always the meal.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

Let the Taste Stand

Live the meal-end as the mouth meant it.

Last bite taken slow. Fork down. Sit back. Let the taste stand. Bread still on the tongue. Warmth spreading low and steady. Nose clear enough to catch the coffee coming, the peel in the air, the rain still on coats by the door. Talk going on without a pause for the doorway. Hands still at the table. Time unhurried because no clock is ticking underneath it.

That lingering is not missing something. That lingering is the something.

We attached that little pellet after the finished paragraph and called it reward. Teaching a class, finished. Light. Driving to the grocery store and back, finished. Light. Finishing a story, finishing a paragraph, driving five hundred miles, driving round the block — each paid with a light till the day ran as a chain of small pays. Look at the chain with clear eyes and keep the work joy intact.

Who taught the class? You did. Who found the sentence? You did. Who drove the miles steady through rain? You did. The pellet did none of that and claimed all of that. The party was the work done, the hunger met, the road held. The dose joined the table after and claimed the table.

Ask three questions of the pellet and stay exact.

Did the pellet add flavour to the food, or did it coat the tongue that was tasting the food? Coated.

Did the pellet add rest to the pause after work, or did it lay its own ticking underneath the rest? Laid its ticking.

Did the pellet add company to the table, or did it take one chair away to the doorway? Took.

Three honest answers. One verdict. You were the flavour. It was the ash.

Stay with the table after the plates are cleared, because the table proves the point longer than the plate.

Coffee poured. Spoon ringing. Talk loosening because bellies are full. This is the hour smokers leave. Chair pushed back. Apology. Doorway. Cold. Two minutes standing with paper while the talk goes on without them. Return to ask what was missed. What was missed was talk while feeding. Who missed it? The feeder. Who stayed in it? The sitter. Which seat holds the pleasure? The sitter’s. The doorway never added a line to the story. It only cut the story for the feeder and made the feeder glad to sit again for minutes till the next cut.

“But I like the ritual,” the voice says. “Hands, lighter, first pull while the plate is cleared.”

What part of the ritual is kind? The hands moving? Hands move to clear, to pour, to rest. The lighter clicking? The stove clicks. The pull? The pull bites. The kindness you remember is the shoulders dropping when the thin pull quiets, plus the table holding you. Both stand without paper. Keep the table. Keep the sit-back. Keep the hands still. Let the clearing wait. The ritual you love is sitting fed among people with nowhere to be for twenty minutes. That ritual never needed ash. Ash only taxed it.

Taste your tea after the meal without commentary. Let it be tea. Eat your meal and sit. Let the taste linger till it goes on its own. No test. No count. Simple noticing. You will find the noticing does not need teaching. The tongue reports. The nose reports. That is the body doing its fitted work.

The Eleven O’Clock Doorway

We called it a break. Look at what broke.

Ten forty. Eyes hot from the screen. Shoulders up near the ears. Legs stiff from sitting. The body asks for what bodies always ask for — stand, walk, look far, draw breath, let the mind go quiet for minutes. We stood, we walked to the doorway, and the hand lit on schedule. Cold biting the cheeks. Sound of the street. Talk with mates. Shoulders dropping as we stood.

We credited the paper with the drop.

Separate what stood there.

The legs wanted standing. The eyes wanted distance. The lungs wanted cold outdoor air. The mind wanted the task to stop for minutes. The shoulders wanted movement. Every one of those wants is answered by standing, walking, looking, breathing, stopping. No paper in that list. The dose answered none of them and claimed all of them.

Ask it of your own break and stay exact.

Did the pavement need the lighter to hold you? Did the cold need the ash to touch your face? Did the talk need the hand to move in order to be talk?

There is only one honest answer. The pause was the work stopping and the body moving and the air meeting the skin. The dose walked beside the pause and signed its name on it.

Live the break as legs and lungs meant it.

Push back the chair. Stand. Feel the blood come back to the calves. Walk to the door. Push it open. Cold straight on the face. Deeper breath taken down to the belly because no bite stops it short. Look to the end of the street. Hear the traffic and the gulls and the kettle still hissing inside. Hands in pockets, arms folded, nothing to tend, nothing ticking down to a stub. Talk if talk is there. Quiet if quiet is there. Return when the legs are ready, mouth clear, head clear, no arithmetic running underneath about how long till the next.

That is a pause that rests. The other pause did not rest. It fed and returned thinner.

I know the picture that rises here because it rose in me. Two mates already out, lighter clicking, shoulders shaking with laugh. The watcher inside feels left out. They have something in their hands. I will have nothing.

You will have hands. Hands that open doors, hold mugs, button coats, rest still. Hands that do not have to leave the table in rain or step from the story to tend. Look at their hands, not their laugh. Mid-sentence lift. Mid-laugh lift. Lift while waiting for the kettle. We called that habit. It was a thread tugging a hand on a clock.

Ask it of the laugh. Did the laugh start with the lighter? The laugh started with the story, the relief of standing, the cold on hot cheeks. The hand interrupted the laugh to serve its schedule and the laugh resumed between. The laugh goes on between feeds. The feed never fed the laugh.

Ask it of the shoulders. Did the paper lower them? Standing lowers shoulders. Walking lowers shoulders. Looking far lowers shoulders. Cold air lowers shoulders. The quieting of the thin pull lowered them one notch more for a breath, and we gave the whole drop to the paper. Give each drop its owner and the paper holds almost nothing.

Stay with the doorway through a wet Tuesday, because wet proves it.

Rain sideways. Bin area slick. Two smokers huddled under a lip of roof, collars up, paper shielding. A third stands inside the dry, mug in hand, watching, coat still buttoned, mouth still tasting coffee. Who is enjoying the break? The huddle tends. The watcher rests. The huddle will return with wet cuffs and sour mouth and a thin look already returning within minutes. The watcher will return with dry cuffs and clear mouth and no clock. Which picture would you choose if no old line spoke?

You are not watching pleasure you will miss. You are watching the same clock working in other bodies while the pause goes on between feeds.

Hands With Nothing to Hold

“What will I do with my hands?”

I hear it because I asked it of the steering wheel, the phone, the pint, the bus stop. The hands felt naked the first hour I stood without. Naked is the right word. Not pained. Naked. As if a ring had been taken off after years.

Look at the naked feeling with clear eyes.

Hands have their own work. They held the wheel steady before the first dose and they hold it now. They lifted the child, turned the pages, stirred the pot, typed the paragraph that you paid yourself a pellet for. The pellet never taught the hands. It only occupied them for three minutes at a time and left them emptier between.

Track one ordinary hour and count what the hands do without.

Kettle lifted, poured. Mug carried, set down. Coat buttoned, unbuttoned. Door pushed, held for the next man. Phone answered, notes taken. Steering held through the bend. Fork lifted, set down. All of it done with hands free, in one unbroken run, no pause to tend, no stub to manage, no ash to chase in wind.

That unbroken run is what we lost. Not rest. Continuity. Every feed broke the run. Light, shield, draw, tap, stub, wash. Six small managements laid across every task. Remove the managements and the task runs whole. The hands do not need something to hold. They need the run unbroken.

“But the fidget,” the voice says. “Leave the hands empty and the edginess climbs.”

What climbs is not in the fingers. What you feel between moments is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug under the ribs, not a message from the fingers. The fingers borrow its name. Hold the mug. Put the hands in pockets. Fold the arms. The tug hums on low while life goes on over it. It does not stop you hearing the kettle. It does not stop you reading this sentence. That hum is the whole physical story when meaning is elsewhere.

Think of the stairs where you were left short of breath while the man beside you walked on steady. That stairs minute told the truth about the hands too. The hands that reached for the pack at the top were not reaching for joy. They were reaching on schedule after the climb exposed the cost. The man beside you reached for the rail, breathed on, kept talking. His hands had nothing to hold and his breath had nothing to prove. Which body would you choose for the next flight?

See how often the hand moved without the mind deciding. Mid-sentence lift. Mid-laugh lift. Lift while looking for keys. Lift while waiting for the kettle. We called that habit. Habit is too kind a word. Habit suggests a chosen groove. This was a thread tugging a hand.

Cut the thread’s meaning and the hand steadies on its own. Not by holding something else. By having nothing to tend.

Your Mouth, Your Nose, Your Lungs

We were taught not to trust them. Trust the packet. Trust mild. Trust the filter theatre. Trust the hand.

The fact is the mouth knows flavour without ash. The nose knows smoke as intrusion. The lungs know how to draw without bite. The body came with its instructions fitted.

Think of the child’s bedside day you already lived, hours passing without a thought of a dose because attention was elsewhere. Think of the long flight, film running, tray down, hours passing with only a glance at the watch. The body did not collapse. It ate, dozed, watched, waited. The thread hummed low and life went on over it. That is the size of the physical part when meaning is elsewhere.

For most smokers that restless edge builds within hours without a dose, is sharpest around day two or three, then eases over days and into weeks as the body stops expecting the feed, and if you feel ill or worried, speak to a clinician for your own care. That is the typical course, not a stopwatch that runs the same to the second for every smoker, and not weeks of torment for all.

See the proportion plain. A small pull with a short typical peak, fed on schedule till we believed the voice was our own. The loud voice that says you cannot cope, you cannot enjoy, you cannot stand this is not the pull. It is the old lines feeding the pull with meaning. Cut the lines and the thread stands bare. Bare, it cannot hold you.

Which kept you at the doorway? The thread kept the schedule. The old lines kept the schedule sacred. Cut the sacred and the schedule falls.

Taste your tea tomorrow without commentary. Let it be tea. Stand your break without paper. Let it be legs and air and talk. Eat your meal and sit. Let the taste linger till it goes on its own. No test. No count. Simple noticing while you smoke as you are. You will find the noticing does not need teaching. The tongue reports. The nose reports. The legs report.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

You were the flavour. It was the ash.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

SUMMARY
- The morning started with waking, water, light and heat, and smoke only laid sourness across it.
- Food finished itself with lingering taste, and smoke cut the lingering short.
- The break rested because work stopped and legs moved and air met the skin, not because a dose was lit.
- Hands were complete with nothing to hold and nothing to tend.
- The thin pull between moments was a passing tug, not a message about what mornings, meals and breaks need.
