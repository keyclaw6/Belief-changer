Chapter 12
YOUR LAST CIGARETTE

IN THIS CHAPTER
The quiet room after the bargaining, the ordinary table, the stained filter and the cold stub

*Smoke this last dose with your eyes open and you will stub out a smoker and stand up something else.*

ARE YOU CHAMPING AT THE BIT

You have closed every side window. No cutting down. No kept birthday dose. No Monday.

We all stood where you stand now, with the pack half empty and the mind suddenly quiet. We had argued for years that paper helped us meet stress and company and mornings. We watched each claim fall. The bright night gave nothing. The diary crept back. The lamp rebuilt the bottle. There is nowhere left for the voice to hide, and that is why it now whispers delay.

“I understand it all, but I am not quite ready.”

Are you waiting to feel braver? Are you waiting to feel sadder? What would another twenty doses teach that ten thousand have not?

I stood with my last dose in my fingers and felt my heart thump. Not because I was losing something. Because I was about to prove I had never had anything to lose. If you feel that thump, that is right. That is the moment before the door shuts.

Ask yourself plain while the pack is in your hand.

If no dose ever added to food or talk or work, what will one more add? If the number was never the master, what will waiting master? If the tube never owned the night, why pay rent for one more night in its lodging?

That is the nicotine trap speaking last in its softest voice. It no longer says paper gives. It says wait. It says finish the pack neatly. It says choose a calmer week. It lets you despise twenty and keep one more evening, despise the doorway and keep the farewell, as if despising most of a prison made one more night in it a home.

You do not need a calmer week or a stronger mood. You need only to see what is in your fingers and finish it with your eyes open.

Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

You are standing at the edge with the door open. You do not need to be sad to leave and you do not need to be brave to leave. You need to be eager. Do you feel that lift, that impatience to be done? You should be champing at the bit. If you do not feel it yet, put this chapter down and read this book once more from the start. Do not push yourself through gritted teeth. Come back when you are eager to finish it.

There is no merit in forcing a farewell you still half believe in. There is every merit in waiting an hour until the seeing is hot and then shutting the door for good. The door is not on Monday. It is here, while the proof is still warm in your hands.

THE FAREWELL TABLE

Put this book down for ten minutes. Take your chair at the ordinary table. No drink to blur it. No mates to perform for. No music to lend it glow.

Smoke your last dose exactly as you always smoke it. Do not puff harder to punish yourself. Do not puff softly to remember it kindly. Smoke it as normal and watch it as you have never watched it.

We smoked for years without looking. We lit while talking. We lit while working. We lit while walking to the wall while the story ran on without us. We never watched the thing itself because the mind was busy cashing the credit — calm, pause, breath, company — and the thing was busy taking it.

Now look.

Look at the filter after two puffs. See the yellow stain spreading where your mouth sits. That stain was on your fingers every morning. It was on your teeth. It was in the hair you washed and still smelled at night. Touch the ash. It is dry and grey and dead. It powders your fingers and smells sour on the skin. It does not fall like snow. It falls like dust off a shelf no one cleans.

Draw again and notice how thin the effect is. The first scratch quiets the tug for minutes, then the mouth turns stale and the chest feels narrower, not wider. The second drag does not taste sweet. It tastes hot and papery and faintly chemical, with a bitterness at the back of the tongue that water will not lift. The third drag tastes of nothing you would choose if it came in a glass. You would send the glass back.

Did that puff taste sweet after the second drag? Did the ash smell kind on your fingers? Did your chest feel wider after than before you lit?

We paid for that stain and that sour mouth for years and called it a friend. We called the pause ours and paid paper for standing beside it. We called the breath ours and paid paper for watching it.

The pause was always yours. The breath was always yours. Paper only stood beside both and charged you for the company.

Keep watching while it shortens. Watch the paper brown and curl. Watch the ember creep toward your fingers. Watch the smoke thin and sting the eyes. There is no ceremony in it except the one you bring — the decision to look at last. Every other dose you smoked half blind, half thinking of the next gap, half listening to talk while part of you counted puffs. This one you smoke with both eyes on it, and both eyes report the same.

That ember is not pleasure burning. That is money and breath burning to quiet a hunger that dose created.

We thought the dose marked finished acts — class taught, paragraph done, drive ended — and made them shine. Look now and see who shone. The work shone. The talk shone. The meal shone. The dose rode. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

Stub it out halfway if you have seen enough. Stub it when the stale end tells you what a thousand told you and you at last heard. Push the grey end into the glass and watch it die. That stub is your proof. It gave nothing.

Do not keep the stub to sniff. Do not keep the pack for guests. Do not keep one in the drawer to prove you can live beside it. A man who has stepped out does not carry a brick from the cell to prove he remembers the address.

Sit a moment with the sour mouth and the powder on your fingers. Wash your hands. Drink cold water. Notice the sour lift and then go. That sour is not loss. That is evidence leaving the mouth.

WHAT THE TUG WILL DO

“What will I feel tomorrow morning?”

You will feel a hollow tug calling for its feed. That is all.

For most smokers that tug sharpens in the first few days and then fades within a few weeks, and it can start within hours of the last dose. If you feel ill or worried, speak to your clinician. This is not a sentence. It is a brief hunger already dying.

We mistook that tug for ourselves for years. We said, “I need this to cope, to think, to wake.” We said the morning could not start without the first drag on empty lungs, feet not yet on the floor before the hand found the pack. We said the meal could not close without ash laid over taste. We said the break could not rest without managing paper in the wind.

The fact is the tug is not you. It is a small physical echo of dosing, and the belief that fed it is now gone.

Let the Nipper whimper unfed.

Do not argue with it. Do not measure it. When the hand moves half an inch toward where the pack sat, smile. That movement is not command. It is habit reaching for a door that is shut. Drink your tea. Do your work. Walk to the window. The tug quiets whether you feed it or not, but only one way kills it.

Will feeding it teach it to leave you alone? Will one more quiet that lasts minutes buy a morning without calling? If the quiet was only the hunger pausing, why re-teach hunger?

We fed that hunger all day without seeing the feeding. Each puff dropped a coin in a meter that faded in minutes and had to be fed again. Morning coin. Break coin. Meal coin. Stress coin. Night coin. The meter never stayed full because the feeding was the emptying. You were not topping up pleasure. You were topping up want.

Now the coins stop. The meter fades once and then stops asking. That fading is not danger. That is dying.

You leave it easily, immediately and permanently.

That is not a boast. That is what happens when the belief dies and the feed stops. The body rights itself. Taste lingers after meals instead of ash laid over it. Mornings smell of kettle and cold air instead of stale fingers. You will know one moment of quiet in the chest on an ordinary morning and understand the machine has stopped asking.

Do not watch the clock for gaps. There are no gaps now because there is nothing to gap between. There is only the day running straight through — kettle, work, food, talk, sleep — without the small managements that cut it into pieces. Tap, shield, relight, spray the hair, wash the hands, step to the wall while the story runs on without you. All that managing is over.

If the tug comes as an empty, slightly restless tug in the afternoon, hear it for what it is. It is not proof paper helped. It is proof paper kept you hungry. It passes in minutes if you let it pass, and each passing weakens it. Within days it thins. Within weeks most smokers hardly hear it. You do not have to be brave through it. You have only to see it.

Would you scratch a healing scratch until it bled to prove it itches? Then why feed a dying hunger to prove it calls?

THE TWO DOORS THAT STAY SHUT

Two thoughts will come dressed as your own. Both are the trap speaking in your voice. Hear them labelled now so they arrive pre-labelled later.

“I can have just one to prove I am over it.”

One dose after months without does not test anything. I watched a man five months clear take one on a warm night because drink loosened the no and a kind hand held out paper and said one will not hurt. He told himself he could control it and have one. Within the hour the sour mouth was back. Within days he was checking gaps while talk ran on without him. Within weeks his hand moved before decision again, reaching while still talking, lighting while still thinking of something else. Within a month he was buying for himself what he had bought for guests.

Not because he was weak. Because one feed wakes hunger and re-teaches credit.

We all tried that test because we all believed freedom needed proof. The one to test freedom becomes the one that ends it. The mind re-learns in an evening what took months to unlearn — ah, that quiet, that pause, that breath — though the quiet was only the tug pausing for minutes, the pause was yours all along, the breath was yours all along.

Will this throat stay sweet after when none ever did? Will this tube taste different from the thousand that thinned within the hour? If five months of breathing and sleeping taught the body its own rhythm, what can one dose teach except to forget?

A stumble warns you to shut the belief tighter. It does not erase you unless you let the belief back in. But a deliberate one is never small. Small is the lie the bottle tells to get rubbed.

Keep that door shut. One keeps the trap alive. Final means final because hunger means hunger.

“I need one because the day broke.”

The phone will ring. The argument will come. The bill will sit on the mat. The voice will say paper steadies.

Did paper ever lift the papers off the desk? Did it ever answer the phone or mend the quarrel? The problem sat untouched while the tug quieted for minutes and then called again. You know this because you lived it at the desk with the papers still there after the puff, the phone still down, the knot still in the stomach with a new sour laid over it.

Ask while the bad hour shouts.

If the dose truly steadied, would users meet hard days easier than non-users? Watch with clear eyes. They meet them worse — stepping out, checking gaps, tasting ash over drink, missing the help while at the wall. The non-user meets the same bill and the same voice with both hands free and a clear mouth.

There is no rescue in that doorway, only rent. You do not need to test the door to know thieves still walk through it when opened.

That is the whole of keeping in two shut doors. One never opens for proof. One never opens for pain.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have followed them to here — open mind, no cutting down before time, beginning with eagerness not doom, letting this argument finish, refusing the struggle stories, watching users with clear eyes, trusting the body, seeing the seller, meeting the best dose head-on, allowing no kept one. One instruction is left, and it is not a hill. It is a step.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

Smoke it now. Watch the stain. Taste the stale end. Stub it. Push the ashtray away.

You are not losing a friend. You never had one. You were conned out of money and breath by paper that posed as comfort and company, and by a method that told you leaving must hurt. Both had their hands in your pocket. Both are out now.

From this moment every thought of a dose gets one answer. Not debate. Not mourning. When the picture of the patio or the desk floats by, you will feel the lift in your chest and answer with what you know.

I want you to have a thought you can keep for life, a short shout that replaces the old reach. When the hand half moves, when the laugh lifts round the table and someone holds out paper, when the phone goes down hard and the old voice says just one, do not argue. Do not weigh. Say the truth as a shout inside and, if you can, aloud.

YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Say it aloud now. Mean it. Feel how different it sits from “one won't hurt.” One is a chain rattled to sound like music. The other is the door shutting on a room you never need to rent again.

Congratulations. You have smoked your last dose.

You did not smoke it to remember paper kindly. You smoked it to see it plain — yellow, sour, narrowing — and you saw. You do not need to mourn what you saw. You need only to keep seeing it whenever the old picture tries to polish itself.

From this moment you are not someone trying. You are someone out. The identity changed while the ember died. The tug may whimper for a few days and then thin to nothing, but the smoker is already gone because the belief is gone. The body will do the rest without lessons.

When tomorrow morning comes with kettle steam and cold floor under bare feet, you will drink and notice taste lingering where ash used to lie. When the meal closes, you will taste food to its end. When the break comes, you will feel air on the face with hands that have somewhere to be besides managing. Each plain hour will prove what the farewell proved — there was never anything to keep.

If doubt knocks in your voice next week, hear it labelled. “Just one to prove” — trap script. “Only at Christmas” — the same tired record. “Tomorrow when calm” — trap script. You know the voice now and you know its landlord. Answer each with the shout you own.

YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

SUMMARY
- The last dose watched closely gave stain and sour ash and nothing else.
- The hollow tug after is brief hunger fading, not proof the dose helped.
- One deliberate dose wakes hunger and teaches credit again, so final means final.
- A hard day leaves its problem untouched by smoke, so no bad hour calls for a dose.
- Stubbing the dose with open eyes closes the identity of smoker in this moment.
