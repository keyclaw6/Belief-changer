### Reader journey comparison

**The real chapter's movement:** Carr names smoking as a subtle, sinister trap with no real lure — the awful first taste is what springs it — and shows how social example, brainwashing, and the stress paradox keep people inside. The reader ends oriented toward a puzzle to be solved: why smoking feels necessary, why escape feels impossible at the wrong times, and why facts must be established before stopping can become easy. End-state is trap recognition and readiness to learn the mechanism, not yet a full personal demolition of “I choose this.”

**Our chapter's movement:** The reader is carried from defended choice (“I like it, I pay for it myself”) through lived hiding scenes, hand-before-thought automation, and the brief “heaven” second into the verdict that choice is a blanket over a trap. The chapter then names the con (“conned, not weak”), introduces the two hooks — physical tug and story-cloud — and closes with trap vocabulary and continued smoking while watching. End-state matches the card: smoking is not free choice; a trap removed it; two mechanisms (Nipper + Smokescreen / tug + translation) keep the reader lighting.

## Final verdict

**Assigned transition verdict:** A cooperative reader who entered believing smoking is their choice and an enjoyed habit should leave believing a trap removed choice and two mechanisms keep them.
