MOVE E2-M1: PARTIAL — Carr opens by replacing doom, gloom, and Everest-scale dread with elation and anticipation as the frame for the whole project; ours names the brace honestly (“You are braced, if I may put it plainly, for another lecture”) and promises ease later (“It is possible to stop smoking easily, immediately and permanently without carrying a sense of loss into the rest of your life”), but the opening invitation to begin already glad rather than braced for misery lands only after extended skepticism work, not as the opening turn.

MOVE E2-M2: PRESENT — Carr’s easy contract is keep smoking through the book, nothing to lose, and freedom without the willpower fight; ours installs the same contract with explicit permission to keep smoking while reading (“All I ask is that you read with an open mind and keep smoking exactly as you do now while we talk”), the nothing-to-lose arithmetic (“you have nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading on to the end”), and the no-fight promise (“you do not need to fight yourself day and night” / “Change the belief and there is nothing to wrestle”).

MOVE E2-M3: PRESENT — Carr positions the EASYWAY as the working path opposite scare-and-willpower quitting; ours performs that contrast by rejecting the scare-lecture book upfront (“It is not that book” / “You expect graphs of damaged lungs”), refusing threat framing in the stairs scene (“Not as a threat. Not as a diagnosis”), and distinguishing this method from props and deprivation (“This book does not sell you a prop. It removes the want by showing you the want was based on a misunderstanding”).
