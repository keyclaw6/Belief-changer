Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Dusk kitchen, biscuit tin, coated tongue, spoken vow, still morning

*Waiting does not make it safer; one ordinary mouthful seen clearly closes the wait.*

WHY TOMORROW LOOKS KINDER

We all know the voice that says tomorrow.

"I'll start after the holidays. After the birthday. After this box. After the trip. When work quietens. When I am ready."

It sounds kind. It sounds careful. It sounds like wisdom. It is the last clever turn of the con that has run your sweet years, and I say that with warmth for you and steel for the lie.

Look at what tomorrow actually does. You promise a date and today lights up with a glow it never owned. This box must be enjoyed properly because it is the last. Tasting strains. Hurry grows. Pictures brighten. You eat slower to memorise and faster from hurry at once. You buy a little extra to see you through, then a little extra to finish the extra. You tell yourself you will miss this, and the telling teaches missing before anything has left. By the time the kind date arrives the mouth has been taught for seven more days that paper is precious, and tomorrow needs another tomorrow to recover from the farewell tour.

If tomorrow were safer, why has every past tomorrow arrived heavier than the day it was promised? If the box must be finished first, why does finishing always order another box? If waiting prepared you, why do you feel less prepared after a week of watching and weighing than you did on the plain day you first saw through the trick?

There is only one honest reading. Delay polishes. Rehearsal gilds. Waiting lends gold the tongue never found.

We lived that loop together, you and I. We told ourselves a clean Monday would hold us, a quiet January would hold us, a holiday passed would hold us. Monday filled with pictures. January filled with bargaining. The holiday filled with farewell eating that taught the hand to circle faster. The date did not cool the wire. The date kept the wire warm by keeping the eye on paper.

Ask your memory straight. Which days held more thought about sweetness, plain Tuesdays with soup and bread eaten hungry and left behind, or last weeks before a promised quit with calendars marked and drawers counted? Which nights circled longer, ordinary suppers that met hunger and closed kindly, or farewell nights with flood watched too closely? Your record convicts without a lecture.

I know this is hard to accept when the whole world tells you big changes need big build-up. I felt that too. I thought I needed a clear run, a strong week, a settled mind. The fact is the trap needs your waiting. You do not need it.

Consider what you risk by reading on with an open mind, for you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. You are not being asked to summon grit or to white-knuckle through nights. You are being asked to see one ordinary bite for what it is, speak a plain vow, and walk on with clear hands on an ordinary morning.

Waiting is the trap.

ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS

Let me check where you stand, because this next step is gated on genuine readiness and I will not hurry you past it.

You have seen totality. No Friday bargain. No just one. No empty house that moves the search across town. No weaning that brightens pictures. No swap that keeps old humming with new paper. You have seen the fuel story run backward, the reward story hand its gold back to room and people and hunger met, the manufacture laid bare. You stand where seeing turns.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit. Not nervous. Not grim. Eager to close this and have your mind back for work and people and weather.

If a small voice still whispers that you might need BAD SUGAR for hard afternoons or kind evenings, do not push it down and do not obey it. Read again the rooms that spoke to it. Let the doubt show itself fully, then answer it with what you lived. Doubt welcomed and answered leaves no root. Doubt buried returns as a knock at half past three.

Hear the two ropes that have pulled you for years. On one side the narrow call that sneers at soup and points to paper. On the other side the dread of never tasting again, of grey tables, of birthdays dulled. That pull has a name you already know in your bones, the tug-of-war of craving and fear. See it plain now. Both ropes are held by the same hands. The call was lit by last floods. The dread was painted by years of talk that called poison brightness. No rope belongs to you. No rope belongs to wisdom. Cut belief and both slacken at once.

Ask yourself three questions whose only honest answers concede the point.

Has any kept exception ever stayed small? Has any waited-for date ever arrived lighter? Is there one real benefit left that room and people and hunger met did not already own?

You know. No. No. None.

That knowing is readiness. Readiness is not a perfect mood. It is clear sight plus willingness to speak. When you can look at paper and say flat that it films and hurries and reorders and gives nothing the night did not already own, you are ready.

What follows is done easily, immediately and permanently, not by effort but by sight. Sight does the work. The hand follows sight the way morning follows night.

ONE ORDINARY BITE WITH EYES OPEN

Take no laboratory dose. Take no strange test. Take your last ordinary treat the way you always took it, at your ordinary hour, in your ordinary chair.

Open the tin or the drawer you know too well. Choose the biscuit or bar or spoon you would have chosen on any tired evening. Sit down. Do not rush to finish and do not pose to dislike. Pay attention the way an investigator pays attention, kindly toward yourself, cold toward the trick.

Note the paper. Note the crackle that your mind learned to hear as promise. Note the smell, loud for a second, dulling by the third breath. Put the first piece in the mouth and keep your eyes open.

Watch the flood. Loud. Sweet. Rushing. Then watch how fast loud thins. Second bite less loud. Third bite hurrying. Chewing quickens. Talk thins if talk is there. The palm weighs. The eye counts what is left before the mouth has cleared. Film coats. Thirst rises under coating. The mind points to drink, then points to more before swallowing ends.

Stay with the mouth. Coating. Stick. Edges that cling. Tongue searching teeth. Throat asking for water to wash film that water only spreads. Belly receiving noise without closing. Head thinning a little, then fogging a little. Hand circling while the programme plays on unwatched.

I did this too, on my last evening with paper. I had told myself for years that moment was love. With eyes open it showed as clutter. Sticky. Loud for seconds. Dulling by thirds. Hurry where hunger used to be patient. True hunger grows slowly, open and patient, welcomes bread and soup and fruit, waits kindly for the table, latches quiet when met. This was not that. This was narrow haste that sneers at soup and calls for brightness to seal the hour.

Ask as you chew. What is marvellous here that bread breaking loud after hunger does not own tenfold? What love is here that the room, the talk, the blanket, the laugh did not already bring? What strength is here that morning mouth fresh till noon does not carry better? The benefit column stays empty however long you stare.

Let the ugliness register without scolding yourself. No shame. We were conned, not foolish. The fraud was in the teaching, not in you. You are watching years of paint wash off one small square. Let it wash.

When the piece is done, put the packet down. Do not finish the row to prove anything. Do not hide rustle. Do not tidy bins in a hurry. Sit still a minute with coated mouth and clear eyes and let the verdict land.

It films. It hurries. It thins talk. It points to more.

That is all it ever did.

Now speak. Out loud if you can, low and solemn, the way you would speak a true thing with a friend in the room.

This was the last. I have seen it. I take no more BAD SUGAR.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. No extra rules. No counting. No patrol. Plain meals when hungry, stopped at satisfied, paper left unopened, mind left undivided.

WHAT THE BODY DOES AFTER

You may wonder what the body will say in the days after the vow. Let me tell you flat so no vague dread can sell you another delay.

A small grumble may come at the old hours. Half past three. Half past eight. The cinema hour. The tired hour. A hollow twitch, a nag for brightness, a hand half lifting before thought catches it.

That feeling is small. It is a matter of days, peaking in two to five days for most, then thinning to silence. It is not illness. It is not a mighty enemy demanding battle. It is a dying echo kept alive till now by belief, and belief has just been pulled. Starved of feeds, it starves.

If your clinician guides what you eat, follow them first.

Do not weigh the grumble. Do not time it. Do not debate it. Note it the way you would note rain on glass, there and passing, and turn attention back to work and people and weather. Bread and soup and fruit will meet true hunger when hunger comes. Water will wash film. Sleep will come cleaner without midnight coating. Morning mouth will wake clearer than you remember.

You may feel a wobble of habit on the second morning, the strange pull toward paper when paper is not wanted, like a lion pacing past an open cage from old rounds rather than need.

That pacing is not proof you still need the wire. It is proof the feet learned rounds. Let the door stand open. Walk the straight road of the day. Breakfast eaten hungry. Shop walked without patrol. Till passed without bargaining. Evening sofa with talk running late. The feet learn new rounds within days when the mind no longer rings the bell.

Now hear the two lying doors before they knock, so they arrive pre-answered.

First door: the bad hour. A hard day. Sharp words. Rain. Bills. The mind says just this once to soften the edge, a kind rescue, no one will know. Know this now. The edge was never softened by flood. Flood added film to trouble and lent tomorrow a new ache. The kind voice is not kindness. It is the old reading offering brightness as salve. Let the day be hard and let supper be plain and let sleep close it. Hard days pass cleaner without coating.

Second door: the cool test. A party. A bright box passed hand to hand. The mind says one cannot matter, you have proved control, you can take a polite bite to belong. Know this now. One carries the binge because the craving once triggered carries the binge. That carrying is the trap signature, not your nature. A polite bite rehangs the bell and lends gold to foil again. Belong with talk and laugh and plate in hand, not with flood. Empty is quiet. Kept is loud.

If a jolt touches the tongue by accident, sauce thickened without asking, recipe changed without warning, do not scold and do not test again. The body steadies past a jolt. The road stays yours when you did not steer toward paper. Guard belief, not behaviour with panic. Deliberate paper is steering. Accidental crumb is pothole.

THE THOUGHT YOU KEEP

From this bite on, sweet memory will still cross the mind. Paper will flash on shelves. Smell will rise from a neighbour table. An old hour will ring. You cannot not think about something by ordering thought away. You can choose what the thought means the instant it lands.

Till now the thought meant loss. A friend gone. A glow missed. A test of holding out. That meaning was painted by years of talk. With sight, the meaning changes to its true shape. The thought is a dead enemy knocking, not an order, not a friend, not a verdict on your evening.

So give the mind its new work, short and bright, to speak whenever paper crosses thought. Not a debate. Not a count. Not a lecture to yourself. One clear sentence that turns the knock into a reminder of ground under feet.

Picture it now as lived. Morning shop. Bright row flashing. Old tug rising half a second. You see film and hurry and thinning talk for what they are. You see bread and soup and fruit waiting to meet hunger and close kindly. You see tables where talk runs late with mouth fresh. A smile lifts before argument starts. Chest lightens. Feet walk on without patrol.

Speak it there, in that picture, and feel how it lands. Not grim. Not loud with effort. Glad from the ground up.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it again in the evening picture. Programme murmuring. Blanket warm. Old hour ringing. Hand still. Mouth fresh. Mind clear for rest. The knock comes and goes like rain on glass.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Let that be the thought you keep for good. Whenever sugar crosses mind, think this. It takes seconds. It never needs weighing. It turns each old trigger into a small hit of lightness that the wire never gave.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

**SUMMARY**
- Tomorrow never readies the mouth; waiting polishes paper and teaches missing before anything has left.
- One ordinary bite watched with eyes open shows film, hurry and thinning talk where love was supposed to be.
- A small grumble at old hours is a days-long echo that thins when unfed, not a sentence to be served.
- A bad hour and a polite bite arrive as rescue and test; both rehang the bell while giving nothing the day did not already own.
- A passing sweet thought is a dead knock, answered with one kept sentence that turns the knock into lightness.
