### The Maths You've Been Doing

We have all done the same maths.

We say, "I know BAD SUGAR is not great for me, but it's worth it." We say, "A little harm, a lot of pleasure." We put harm on one side of the scales and pleasure on the other and we decide the pleasure is heavier. Does that sound familiar?

That is the maths that keeps the trap alive.

I know that maths well. I did it for years. I would read that sugar rots teeth and think, yes, but it makes life sweeter. I would read that it strains the body and think, yes, but I need the lift. I would hear the warnings and answer them with a single phrase.

It is a genuine pleasure or treat.

Have you said that? Even quietly? Have you thought, "I know, but I enjoy it — it's my treat"? That phrase is not yours. It was given to you. It is the name the Sugar Trap gave to its own effect so you would keep paying.

Let me ask you plainly. Do you really weigh harm against benefit? Or do you assume the benefit and then argue about the harm?

We assume the benefit. We never prove it. We say, "Of course BAD SUGAR gives me something — pleasure, energy, comfort — so the only question is whether the harm is too high." That question already concedes the con. It lets BAD SUGAR keep its claimed benefit without ever showing it.

Why did we never check the other column? Why did we never ask, what does it actually do FOR me?

You are about to. And you will feel the floor shift.

Think of the reader I meet every week. She is P-02 in our shorthand — the in-denial moderate. She does not binge the box. She grazes. She says, "I don't keep it in the house," and means the biscuits, not the bread and the sweetened yoghurt and the juice. She says, "Just one won't hurt," and means the small chocolate after lunch. She says, "Once a week is fine," and means the cake that appears three times. She says, "I've managed to be good this week." Do you recognise her? Have you been her?

And beside her sits P-04 — the energy-crash yo-yoer. He reaches for sugary somethings to get me through. Three o'clock arrives. His head clouds. He thinks, "I need something right now." He takes a sweet drink or a biscuit. He feels a brief lift. An hour later he needs another. He calls that fuel. Is it fuel? Or is it the next payment?

Both of them do the same maths: harm versus a genuine pleasure or treat. Both are sure the treat is real. Both are wrong.

The fact is, we have been doing the wrong sum. We asked, does the harm outweigh the benefit? The honest question is different.

What good is it at all?

Ask it now. What does BAD SUGAR actually do FOR you? Not what you fear losing. What does it give? Name one thing it does FOR you that you could not have without it. One irreplaceable good. Can you?

Hold that question. Do not answer with habit. Answer with evidence.

We will put the ledger on the table and you will see.

### The Ledger On The Table

Picture a bank statement.

Left column: money out. Right column: money in. Every adult knows how to read it. Money out is what the purchase costs you. Money in is what you get back. If the right column is empty, you are not making a purchase. You are being robbed.

That is the image I want you to keep for BAD SUGAR. A simple two-column ledger.

Left column: what BAD SUGAR is doing TO you. Right column: what it is doing FOR you.

We will fill the left column first. Plainly. No lecture. No scare to make you stop. Just the facts as institutions that have no reason to flatter the sugar industry describe them. Then we will look at the right column. You will do that part. Your eyes will do it. Your honesty will do it.

Keep the statement in front of you. Feel the paper. See the two headings. We will write together.

Left column first. Doing TO you.

The fact is, free sugars — that is BAD SUGAR in drinks, sweets, biscuits, chocolate, pastries, white bread and the rest — feed the bacteria in plaque. Those bacteria make acid. Acid can dissolve enamel. Dissolved enamel is decay. Cavities are holes where there were none. That is not a moral tale. It is a mechanism. Brushing with fluoride and good dental care can reduce the risk, but they do not erase the mechanism. The sugars still drive the acid. Does that belong in the left column? Yes. It is doing TO you.

The fact is, at population level, patterns of high added sugar are linked with higher rates of heart and stroke trouble. The paths named are not mysterious: extra load on the liver, a nudge to blood pressure, ongoing low-grade inflammation, and a way of eating that bypasses the body's normal appetite brakes. So the quick lift you feel is not free energy. It is a deferred load. Your body pays later for what your mouth borrowed now. Does one sweet cause heart disease? No. That would be nonsense and I will not insult you with it. Patterns across many people, over time, carry the load. That load belongs in the left column. It is doing TO you.

The fact is, respected health bodies suggest keeping free sugars below about ten percent of daily energy — for many adults about fifty grams — and note there is extra benefit below about five percent. Those are population benchmarks, not a personal prescription from me and not a call to count every gram of fruit. Whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy — those carry intrinsic sugars with fibre and water and are not the target. This book means BAD SUGAR when it says sugar. How does the benchmark help the ledger? Most of us who think we are moderate sit well above those benchmarks without ever counting. Normal, in a sugared world, is not healthy. Normal is high. That belongs in the left column. It is doing TO you, quietly, while you call it normal.

You see the column filling. Acid on teeth. Load on heart and vessels. A daily total that silently exceeds guidance. Tiredness dressed as need. Money out. Money out. Money out.

Do you notice the feeling as you read that? Is it fear? Is it heaviness?

I want to be explicit with you, warmly and firmly. I am not laying this out to frighten you into stopping. I do not want you to stop because you are scared. Fear is a rope that pulls you back into the trap. It makes you feel deprived and vigilant, and deprivation drives the next dose. Understand the left column, then put fear behind you. The left column is not your motivator. It is your clarity. It shows what BAD SUGAR is doing TO you so you stop crediting it with doing something FOR you.

So we leave fear here. We do not carry it forward. We carry clarity.

Now the right column.

This is the column the trap begs you not to examine. This is the column we have left blank for years while we did the wrong maths.

It is headed doing FOR you.

### Doing TO You vs Doing FOR You

We need to name the switch.

For years we asked, does the harm outweigh the good? That question keeps the good in place as if it were proven. It asks you to trade. It makes you a shopper weighing price against prize. The trap loves shoppers. Shoppers always find a reason to buy.

The honest switch is different. It does not weigh. It asks, what good at all?

The phrase that makes the switch visible is doing TO you vs doing FOR you.

Say it slowly. Doing TO you vs doing FOR you. Feel the two directions. TO you is cost, load, harm happening on your body and your day. FOR you is genuine help, real pleasure, true energy that could not be had otherwise. One is what it takes. One is what it gives.

BAD SUGAR is heavy in the first and empty in the second.

Do you see why this matters? If BAD SUGAR did nothing TO you, the FOR question would still stand. If it were harmless, you would still be entitled to ask, what does it actually do FOR me? And if the answer were nothing, you would have no reason to do it. Harm is not the point. Benefit is the point. The trap survives by never letting you look at the FOR column without the TO column beside it to distract you.

So we look. Now. Together.

I want you to lend me your honesty for one minute.

Think of your most believed benefit. For P-04 it is energy. "It gets me through." For P-02 it is moderation and pleasure. "Just a treat, just one, I enjoy it, it harms no one." For many of us it is both, on different days. Pick yours.

Now isolate the variable. Keep the moment. Remove the BAD SUGAR.

Was the three o'clock lift the sugar? Or was it the pause, the stretch, the glass of water, the breath, the step away from the screen? Have you ever felt the same lift from a short walk and a drink of water? Have you ever powered through the dip without sweets and found the dip passed anyway? What does that tell you?

Was the treat the chocolate? Or was it the sofa, the film, the laughter, the feeling of deserving kindness after a hard day? If I gave you the same evening with the same kindness, but the sweet was not there, would the kindness disappear? Would love vanish? No. The trap was sneaking a ride.

You know this inversion already from Chapter 2. The rescuer is the perpetrator. But here the inversion is surgical. It does not say sweets are disgusting. It says they are irrelevant. They do plenty TO you and nothing FOR you.

Let me ventriloquise the strongest objection you still hold, because I respect you enough to say it out loud.

"Fine, there is harm. But it is still a genuine pleasure or treat. Life is duller without it. I would feel deprived."

Do you hear that thought? Is it yours right now? Good. Keep it in the light.

Now answer three questions with flat honesty. There is only one honest answer to each.

First: can you name a pleasure BAD SUGAR gives you that you cannot have without it — not the scene around it, not the rest, not the company, but the BAD SUGAR itself — that exceeds the baseline of a person who never started? Can you describe it without borrowing the scene?

Second: if BAD SUGAR were truly doing something FOR you, would you need to keep re-dosing to feel normal again an hour later? Does a genuine help behave like that?

Third: if the benefit were real, why does the ledger's right column stay empty the moment you remove the story you were told as a child that sweet equals love?

You feel the concession forming. It is not defeat. It is relief.

The benefit you were promised is not there. The phrase a genuine pleasure or treat names an illusion. It looks solid from a distance. Up close, it is paint on air.

We were conned, not foolish. We all were. I was. The con was elegant. It taught us to call relief pleasure and to call a debt a gift. We carried that lesson for years. We built days around it. We said, "I don't keep it in the house," and believed that proved control. Control of what? Of an empty column? We said, "I've been good, so I deserve it," and thought we were being kind. Kind with what? With a cost that does nothing FOR us?

The warm truth is sharper and kinder than shame. You did nothing wrong. You simply did the wrong maths. You weighed harm against a benefit that was never entered into the ledger. Now you correct the maths.

The maths is simple. The left column is full. The right column is empty.

Doing TO you vs doing FOR you. That decision ends the debate.

### The Empty Column

Look at the statement again.

Left: acid on teeth from free sugars turning to acid in plaque. Load on heart and vessels from high added-sugar patterns over time. A daily total that drifts above guidance while you call it normal. Energy that borrows and dips. Money and time and attention spent chasing a fix that never fixes.

Right: nothing. Not one irreplaceable good that survives isolation.

Not taste. Not fuel. Not reward. Not calm. Those are the next chapters, and each will get its full demolition. But you do not need them to see the empty column now. You need only ask the right question. What good is it at all?

We need to land this with the bluntness it deserves. Short. Clean.

The FOR column is empty.

BAD SUGAR does nothing FOR you.

It does plenty TO you.

The trap is not that you love a pleasure too much. The trap is that you believe in a pleasure that is not there.

Do you see it? Can you feel the weight move? Is the loss on the other side now? For years losing BAD SUGAR felt like loss. Now losing it feels like putting down a bill you never owed.

That is the switch. You came in thinking sugar may be harmful but the pleasure outweighs it. You leave seeing the ledger is not balanced and never was. Harm is real. Benefit is not. The negotiation collapses because there is nothing to negotiate for.

We do not need to add a second thesis. We do not need to demolish treat, taste, energy and cope here. Those rooms will be emptied one by one. This chapter does one job. It changes the axis. It moves you from "is harm bigger than good?" to "there is no good." Once that move is made, the rest follows easily.

So hold the image. Carry the statement.

When BAD SUGAR crosses your mind, do not count harms. Look at the FOR column.

Ask, what is it doing FOR me?

Watch the column stay empty.

Watch the desire lose its footing.

You have not lost a treat. You have spotted that a genuine pleasure or treat was never there. You have not been deprived. You have been overcharged. And overcharges are for correcting, not mourning.

Doing TO you vs doing FOR you. Keep that ledger in your hand. It will keep you honest when the old story tries to speak.

You are not giving something up. You are seeing that there was nothing to give up. That is not sacrifice. That is escape.
