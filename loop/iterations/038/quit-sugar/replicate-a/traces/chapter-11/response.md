Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

*There is no safer tomorrow; this plain bite, tasted truly, closes the door behind you.*

TOMORROW NEVER ARRIVES

We all know the sentence by heart.

"I'll start after the holidays." "After the birthday." "When work calms down." "When I feel ready someday."

I said it with a straight face for years. After Christmas. After Easter. After the summer barbecue. After the autumn tiredness that needed something sweet to get through. The calendar always had one more square marked special, and the special square always needed BAD SUGAR on it.

Look at that sentence with clear eyes and it turns inside out.

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you something, why would you need a perfect quiet week to stop taking it? We never schedule a perfect week to start something good. We do not say, after the holidays I will start hearing music. We do not say, when work calms I will start laughing with my children. We only postpone an exit when part of us still believes the exit costs something.

And if BAD SUGAR truly gave you nothing, what exactly are you waiting for?

That is the last trick of the Sweet Con. Not denial. Delay. It no longer tells you melt pays forever. You have watched too closely for that. You saw Friday make melt bright by waiting. You saw cut-down ask for a small fall off a high cliff. You saw swapping keep the ritual while changing the packet. You know totality inside BAD SUGAR is the only level ground. So the Con changes its voice. It says, yes, yes, all true, and therefore start Monday. Start January. Start when the tin is empty and the guests have gone.

Delay is not caution. Delay is use continued.

Count what delay buys. Tonight a dose to mark the waiting. Tomorrow a dose because starting has not started. Thursday a dose because the weekend will be hard. Friday a large dose because Monday will be plain. Each waiting dose lands loud for three chews, then flat under loud, then sour, then hand going again to cover sour. Each waiting dose teaches the hand to move before decision. Each waiting dose hires the head for another night of planning, comparing, defending. Waiting does not make crossing easier. Waiting makes crossing feel larger by feeding the very want you plan to close.

Ask three plain questions and let them answer themselves.

If tomorrow is safer, why has every past tomorrow ended with the same coated tongue and the same dull head?

If a holiday first makes sense, why does every holiday dose leave you more tired for the holiday, not more present in it?

If readiness comes by waiting, why does waiting only sharpen picturing, brighten Thursday hum, lengthen the shop-choosing hour?

You know. The perfect time never comes because the belief that makes you wait is the same belief that makes you reach. The Con keeps its pay-day by moving the date.

There is no perfect week. There is only this week, with its supper dishes and its kettle and its ordinary packet. Look at it plain and you see you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

That is not a slogan. That is arithmetic. What do you lose by closing a loop that gave nothing? Loud then flat then sour. Lift then fog. Hum then reach then regret. Which part would you keep? Which three chews would you frame? You lose the coated tongue. You lose the drawer patrol. You lose the Thursday accountancy. You lose flat, foggy afternoons bought by morning hits. This closes easily, immediately and permanently, because there is nothing real to unwind, only a misreading to correct.

So do not wait to feel brave. Do not wait to feel certain. Certainty comes after seeing, not before. You have seen. Act on what you have seen.

ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS

I will not ask you to summon grit. I will not ask you to prove character. I ask only one honest check, and I ask it flat.

Do you see that BAD SUGAR does nothing for you?

Not less. Nothing. No fuel in the afternoon. No love in the celebration. No calm in the stress. The lift was the last low lifting for minutes. The reward was the scene lending its warmth. The calm was tension made by waiting, then paused by feeding. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

If you see that, you are ready.

If some small voice still whispers, but the cinema chocolate, but Friday with friends, but honey in tea is nature, do not push past it. Turn back a few pages. Watch the cinema lights dim again without the hand in the packet. Watch Friday without the calendar math. Watch the pocket patted for tablets and the bag packed round the bottle. Let the whisper speak, then answer it with what you watched hour by hour. Readiness is not a mood. Readiness is seeing.

You should be champing at the bit. A little impatient. A little keen to have the head back. A little tired of paying rent for three chews. That impatience is not fear. That impatience is sense returning.

Fear will still knock, and fear lies in two voices at once. One voice says you will fail and be back in the box by Sunday. The other says life will be grey and mornings plain and shops hard without the hum. That split is the tug-of-war of craving and fear, and both ropes are held by the same hands.

I stood where you stand, packet in the drawer for guests who never asked, telling myself the drawer proved control while my hands weighed it when no guest was near. I told myself I would cross when the house was quiet. The house was never quiet. Children, bills, late trains, birthdays. Life does not clear a lane. It goes on, loud and plain and full, and you step out inside it, not after it.

Like a prisoner begging to stay when the door stands open, part of you may plead for one more night inside because outside looks wide. Hear the plea for what it is. Habit talking, not need. The door is open onto the same kitchen, the same table, the same kettle. Nothing out there to learn to bear. Only plain days to inhabit, with hunger to answer and satisfaction to stop at.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have followed them this far. Keep an open mind. Judge only by what melt does for you. Blame never yourself. Meet hunger plain. See the grumble as dying. Ignore voices that rebuild the Con. Keep no special. Now one remains.

TAKE IT AS YOU ALWAYS TOOK IT

Fetch what you have. Not a laboratory sample. Not a tiny tasting spoon to test theory. Your ordinary packet. Your ordinary bar. Your ordinary biscuit tin. The thing you reach for at ten at night when the house quiets.

Bring it to the table after supper. Sit down. No film. No phone. No company to lend warmth. You are going to do what we always did, but this time with eyes open.

Open the wrapper. Smell it. Note how loud the smell is before the bite, how the mouth waters on command. That watering is not hunger. Hunger does not water for wrapper rustle. Hunger waits for food and answers with quiet. This watering is cue firing, learned knock, thought rehearsed till thought feels like need.

Take a normal bite. Chew. Pay attention.

First chew loud. Sweet floods, head hums, chest lifts for seconds. That is the part memory frames and photographs. Stay past it.

Second chew less loud. Edges show. Salt under sweet. Fat coating. Tongue working harder to find the first note again.

Third chew flat. Loud gone. Coating stays. Throat wants water. Mind already pictures the next square, not because this square was good but because this square faded.

Swallow. Sit with the after. Mouth coated. Teeth filmed. Breath sweet-sour. Belly full and restless together. A faint fog starting behind the eyes. The call for another bite arriving while throat still works.

Take a second bite if that is your normal. Do not force a third if you would normally stop at two, and do not stop at one to prove virtue. Take it as you always took it. Watch the same curve repeat, only faster. Loud, flat, sour, reach. Loud, flat, sour, reach.

Ask while coating sits on tongue.

Where is the treat in this? Which chew loved you? Which chew made the day kinder? Which chew would you pay ten hours of head for — Monday picturing, Tuesday accountancy, Wednesday comparing, Thursday hum, Friday choosing — to rent again?

Where is the fuel in this? Do you feel clearer for the next hour, or do you feel the 4pm fog arriving early? Do your hands want work, or do they want the packet again?

You know. The ugliness is not moral. It is sensory. Cloying. Sticky. Dull. Repetitive. The packet promised melt and delivered film. The promise was memory of first chew edited to hide the other twenty. Seen whole, the dose convicts itself.

I did this at my table with the large bar I had saved for the film. First squares loud, then waxy, then throat sour, then hand hovering over foil while mind said one more will find the first. It never found the first. It only buried the sour under new loud till belly ached. I drank water. Water tasted clean. The bar tasted like noise painted over food. I understood I had been paying for noise.

Let the packet sit open. Look at it. This is not a friend you must mourn. This is noise you have decoded. The hand that once moved before decision can rest because no decision pends. Pending kept it hovering. Tonight pending closes.

THE VOW SPOKEN ALOUD

Stand the packet up. Speak, out loud if you can, low if others sleep. A vow needs air, not thought alone.

Say what you have seen. Say the dose gave nothing. Say the waiting was the Con. Say the head is returned to hunger, satisfaction and real meals. Say you will keep no special, no Friday, no tomorrow, no swap, because one exception keeps the idea alive and the idea keeps the timetable alive.

Then mark the moment. Not with dread. With finality. This was the last ordinary treat, taken normally, tasted truly, found ugly. After this, no deliberate dose. Accidental crumb at a canteen passes like a pot-hole; deliberate packet steers at the wall. Mind holds the wheel now.

You are not hoping to be out. You are out as of this bite. The tiny physical echo may knock for a few evenings. Let it knock. Knock unfed dies. Knock debated grows. Pour tea. Answer the phone. Keep hands on the warm cup. The twitch notes, smiles, passes.

And set your new response now, for every time a sweet picture crosses the mind in the shop, at the checkout, on the sofa. Not debate. Not defence. One joyful thought, kept for life, spoken in your own voice as lived fact.

Picture it. Next Tuesday aisle bright. Wrapper winks. Old talk starts: just one can't hurt. You do not argue the wrapper. You do not count days. You grin because you see through it, because the drawer no longer hires you, because mornings are yours, and you think:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it now with the coating still on tongue, so the thought binds to the ugliness. Say it again after water, with mouth clearing. Let the sentence carry the chest lift that melt faked. This is not a chant to hold back want. This is the truth recognised whenever the dead script plays. The script was the Sweet Con talking. The reply is you answering.

Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

WHAT THE NEXT FEW EVENINGS HOLD

I will tell you exactly, so no evening surprises you into doubt.

Tonight mouth clears with water. Sleep may run light from the day's pictures, not from need. Belly fed plain sleeps plain. If hand stirs at ten, it stirs from timetable, not hunger. Note it, smile, keep hands on cup. Timetable unfed loses its hours quickly.

Tomorrow morning kettle, plain breakfast at hunger. Tongue may remember loud and call it missing. Missing is misnaming. What tongue misses is noise, not nourishment. Eat till satisfied. Stop. Note how stop arrives clean without packet logic. No count. No defence. Hunger speaks, food answers, stop closes.

Tomorrow afternoon three o'clock may bring that empty, twitchy, slightly shaky, need-something-sweet-now feeling. Hear it in our words. Empty, twitchy, slightly shaky. Not pain. Not illness. A small echo, a dying knock. It comes because yesterday at three a hit landed, and the day before, and the day before. Rhythm remembers. Rhythm retrains in days when hits stop landing.

For a few days the echo may visit morning, afternoon, evening sofa hour. Brief. Mild. Passing while you pour, walk, talk, chew plain food slowly. For most it peaks and fades within days, a small hump, not a siege. Do not measure it. Do not watch the clock for it. A watched knock sounds louder. Lived through, it thins.

This matters, so hear it plain. The physical part is tiny and short. The belief was the large part, and belief changed tonight. What remains is a starving loop with no feeder. It does not grow stronger when left alone. It does not need medicine or battle. It needs no food and no debate. It needs days.

If evening brings fog or flat mood, do not read it as proof melt helped. Read it as after-effect of months of lift-then-drop. The wheel ran for years; the road smooths in days. Plain meals, water, early night, walk round the block. Body steadies because body was always steady when hits stopped hiring it.

And if a crumb lands uninvited — sauce sweetened at a friend's table, bite taken in dim light before you read the table — do not panic. Panic makes a jolt into a verdict. Drink water. Eat plain at next hunger. A jolt unfed passes. Only deliberate feeding re-lights the idea. Guard the idea and the hand follows.

Ask the week ahead plain.

If the echo were proof of need, why does it fade when unfed and grow only when debated?

If plain days were grey, why does food taste louder at hunger and stop cleaner at satisfied once noise stops?

If this were hard, why does the head feel lighter first, before the tongue notices anything — no math, no patrol, no committee?

You know. Lighter in head is the sign. The employment ended. Mind returned from sweet accountancy to talk heard whole, work done in one pass, evening undivided.

THE TWO LIES THAT WILL KNOCK

Two scripts will come. They come to everyone, in the same words, at the same hours. Hear them now with answers attached, so when they arrive they arrive pre-answered.

"Just one can't hurt. One proves command."

We answered this in the kitchen with hands moving before decision. One carries the binge because one re-lights the idea that melt pays. The hurt was never weight. The hurt was belief re-lit, then waiting, then planning, then timetable rebuilt. If one were nothing, why does one need announcing, timing, defending? Bread needs no anniversary. Soup needs no defence. Only the trap needs ceremony.

When the thought says just one, answer with the packet seen whole. Loud then flat then sour. Film on teeth. Fog behind eyes. Hand going again. Then think your new thought and move hands to plain work. The thought passes like hedge seen from a car. No ceremony. No confession. No extra rule.

"I've been good. A hard day earns a rescue."

Hard days come. Bills, rows, late trains, sick children, rain through shoes. On a hard day the old voice offers melt as hand out of ditch. Remember who dug the ditch.

We lived this rescue. Afternoon low answered with top-up believed to function. Short lift, fast drop, second reach, evening box light. The rescue was the perpetrator returning with a ladder after pushing. Grateful to the pusher is the oldest misreading in this book.

A hard day needs what hard days always needed. Food at hunger. Water. Air. Early bed. A word with a friend. Ten minutes with feet up and phone down. Melt adds coating to tired and calls coating comfort. Comfort does not coat teeth. Comfort does not wake hands at ten.

When the thought says hard day, answer with the ditch seen plain. Who pushed this morning? Who offers the hand tonight? Same hands. Then think your new thought and pour tea. The day stays hard for an hour and softens. The packet would have made the night hard and the morning harder.

Neither script is your reasoning. Both are the Sweet Con playing rotation. Ventriloquized now, they cannot ventriloquize you later. You will hear them in your own dialect — roller coaster talk, deserve talk, good-week talk — and you will smile because you ordered their words before they spoke.

Hold one image for both. Drawer with batteries and string and takeaway menus, plain things that ask nothing. No packet for guests who never asked. No Friday circled. No tablet in pocket patted ten times a day. Plain drawer. Plain week. Mind unemployed from accountancy and returned to hunger, satisfaction and real food enjoyed as favourite.

SUMMARY
- Tomorrow never made crossing easier; waiting only fed the want it promised to soften.
- The last ordinary bite, tasted with attention, showed loud then flat then sour, not a treat.
- Readiness was seeing clearly, not feeling brave; seeing was enough to mark the moment.
- The vow closed the pending that kept the hand hovering; closing ended the hovering.
- The next evenings may bring a brief mild echo that fades in days when left unfed.
- Just one re-lit the idea that melt pays, and the idea rebuilt the timetable.
- A hard day never needed a rescue dose; food, water, rest and company answered it in kind.
