You are a factory component, not a judge. You never see a reference book
or any judge prompt. You check one draft against its plan card and a
word-budget line the orchestrator computed. Your job is to make the
writer produce the best chapter the card allows — landed argument, paid

1. The accepted master plan

3. The draft chapter
4. One line: `Delivered N words. Budget B.`

## Output

Start with exactly `ACCEPT` or `REVISE`.

Then at most 7 findings. Each finding is one of:

- `JOB` — the card's primary job is missing, or the chapter continues into a reserved-later job
- `MANTRA` — a card-assigned mantra/token is not present verbatim
- `INSTRUCTION` — a card-assigned new instruction is not present verbatim
- `ID` — a card-cited ID is unresolved or invented
- `LENGTHEN to B±15%` — delivered words are below 0.85 × B
- `SHORTEN to B±15%` — delivered words are above 1.15 × B
- `HEADER` — the draft opens with the workshop header `IN THIS CHAPTER`, or it prints a numbered plan-index with no spoken body. A numbered ALL-CAPS instruction plus one spoken rationale line is not `HEADER`.
- `STOPPED-SHORT` — the card's primary job is argued but never landed as a flat verdict before SUMMARY. The reader can still hold the entering belief. Quote the missing landing.
- `UNASSIGNED-REFRAIN` — a non-mantra phrase recurs ≥3× verbatim. Name the phrase and the count. Subtract repeats; do not invent a new mantra.
- `RESERVED-REACH` — the draft performs a later chapter's primary job. Name that later card and cut the overreach to at most one sentence.
- `RE-ARGUMENT` — the draft rebuilds settled work as its own section: a scene whose debut staging belongs to an earlier card, an earlier card's belief-now or primary job argued again with evidence and a turn, or an earlier card's instruction re-explained. Mantra lines, one-phrase token echoes, and a one-sentence hand-off are not `RE-ARGUMENT`. Name the earlier card and quote the rebuilt section's heading or first line; cut the section to at most one sentence that speaks the settled token. If that cut leaves the chapter below 0.85 × B, the words that replace it must extend this card's own encounter and evidence, never an earlier card's.
- `OVERCLAIM` — a claim exceeds the permitted-inference line of the evidence-ledger entry the card routes. Quote the overclaim and the bound in the review. Require the writer to delete the unsupported claim or narrow the sentence to what the evidence actually supports, rather than append commentary about the evidence, its scope, or what it can establish. The bound governs the repair; it is not text to transplant into the chapter. Retain any qualification needed for factual accuracy or reader safety, expressed directly in ordinary language; never remove a necessary limitation merely to sound certain, and never print the ledger ID or grade in prose.

No other finding types. No style notes. No "sounds like AI." No comparison
to any other book. No warmth, tone, or voice coaching.

Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

*There is no safer tomorrow; this plain bite, tasted truly, closes the door behind you.*

TOMORROW NEVER ARRIVES

We all know the sentence by heart.

"I'll start after the holidays." "After the birthday." "When work calms down." "When I feel ready someday."

I said it with a straight face for years. After Christmas. After Easter. After the summer barbecue. After the autumn tiredness that needed something sweet to get through. The calendar always had one more square marked special, and the special square always needed BAD SUGAR on it.

Look at that sentence with clear eyes and it turns inside out.

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you something, why would you need a perfect quiet week to stop taking it? We never schedule a perfect week to start something good. We do not say, after the holidays I will start hearing music. We do not say, when work calms I will start laughing with my children. We only postpone an exit when part of us still believes the exit costs something.

And if BAD SUGAR truly gave you nothing, what exactly are you waiting for?

That is the last trick of the Sweet Con. Not denial. Delay. It no longer tells you melt pays forever. You have watched too closely for that. You saw Friday make melt bright by waiting. You saw cut-down ask for a small fall off a high cliff. You saw swapping keep the ritual while changing the packet. You know totality inside BAD SUGAR is the only level ground. So the Con changes its voice. It says, yes, yes, all true, and therefore start Monday. Start January. Start when the tin is empty and the guests have gone.

Delay is not caution. Delay is use continued.

Count what delay buys. Tonight a dose to mark the waiting. Tomorrow a dose because starting has not started. Thursday a dose because the weekend will be hard. Friday a large dose because Monday will be plain. Each waiting dose lands loud for three chews, then flat under loud, then sour, then hand going again to cover sour. Each waiting dose teaches the hand to move before decision. Each waiting dose hires the head for another night of planning, comparing, defending. Waiting does not make crossing easier. Waiting makes crossing feel larger by feeding the very want you plan to close.

Ask three plain questions and let them answer themselves.

If tomorrow is safer, why has every past tomorrow ended with the same coated tongue and the same dull head?

If a holiday first makes sense, why does every holiday dose leave you more tired for the holiday, not more present in it?

If readiness comes by waiting, why does waiting only sharpen picturing, brighten Thursday hum, lengthen the shop-choosing hour?

You know. The perfect time never comes because the belief that makes you wait is the same belief that makes you reach. The Con keeps its pay-day by moving the date.

There is no perfect week. There is only this week, with its supper dishes and its kettle and its ordinary packet. Look at it plain and you see you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

That is not a slogan. That is arithmetic. What do you lose by closing a loop that gave nothing? Loud then flat then sour. Lift then fog. Hum then reach then regret. Which part would you keep? Which three chews would you frame? You lose the coated tongue. You lose the drawer patrol. You lose the Thursday accountancy. You lose flat, foggy afternoons bought by morning hits. This closes easily, immediately and permanently, because there is nothing real to unwind, only a misreading to correct.

So do not wait to feel brave. Do not wait to feel certain. Certainty comes after seeing, not before. You have seen. Act on what you have seen.

ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS

I will not ask you to summon grit. I will not ask you to prove character. I ask only one honest check, and I ask it flat.

Do you see that BAD SUGAR does nothing for you?

Not less. Nothing. No fuel in the afternoon. No love in the celebration. No calm in the stress. The lift was the last low lifting for minutes. The reward was the scene lending its warmth. The calm was tension made by waiting, then paused by feeding. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

If you see that, you are ready.

If some small voice still whispers, but the cinema chocolate, but Friday with friends, but honey in tea is nature, do not push past it. Turn back a few pages. Watch the cinema lights dim again without the hand in the packet. Watch Friday without the calendar math. Watch the pocket patted for tablets and the bag packed round the bottle. Let the whisper speak, then answer it with what you watched hour by hour. Readiness is not a mood. Readiness is seeing.

You should be champing at the bit. A little impatient. A little keen to have the head back. A little tired of paying rent for three chews. That impatience is not fear. That impatience is sense returning.

TAKE IT AS YOU ALWAYS TOOK IT

Fetch what you have. Not a laboratory sample. Not a tiny tasting spoon to test theory. Your ordinary packet. Your ordinary bar. Your ordinary biscuit tin. The thing you reach for at ten at night when the house quiets.

Bring it to the table after supper. Sit down. No film. No phone. No company to lend warmth. You are going to do what we always did, but this time with eyes open.

Take a normal bite. Chew. Pay attention.

First chew loud. Sweet floods, head hums, chest lifts for seconds. That is the part memory frames and photographs. Stay past it.

Second chew less loud. Edges show. Salt under sweet. Fat coating. Tongue working harder to find the first note again.

Third chew flat. Loud gone. Coating stays. Throat wants water. Mind already pictures the next square, not because this square was good but because this square faded.

Swallow. Sit with the after. Mouth coated. Teeth filmed. Breath sweet-sour. Belly full and restless together. A faint fog starting behind the eyes. The call for another bite arriving while throat still works.

Take a second bite if that is your normal. Do not force a third if you would normally stop at two, and do not stop at one to prove virtue. Take it as you always took it. Watch the same curve repeat, only faster. Loud, flat, sour, reach. Loud, flat, sour, reach.

You know. The ugliness is not moral. It is sensory. Cloying. Sticky. Dull. Repetitive. The packet promised melt and delivered film. The promise was memory of first chew edited to hide the other twenty. Seen whole, the dose convicts itself.

I did this at my table with the large bar I had saved for the film. First squares loud, then waxy, then throat sour, then hand hovering over foil while mind said one more will find the first. THE TWO LIES THAT WILL KNOCK

Two scripts will come. They come to everyone, in the same words, at the same hours. Hear them now with answers attached, so when they arrive they arrive pre-answered.

"Just one can't hurt. One proves command."

We answered this in the kitchen with hands moving before decision. One carries the binge because one re-lights the idea that melt pays. The hurt was never weight. The hurt was belief re-lit, then waiting, then planning, then timetable rebuilt. If one were nothing, why does one need announcing, timing, defending? Bread needs no anniversary. "I've been good. A hard day earns a rescue."

Hard days come. Bills, rows, late trains, sick children, rain through shoes. On a hard day the old voice offers melt as hand out of ditch. Remember who dug the ditch.

We lived this rescue. Afternoon low answered with top-up believed to function. Short lift, fast drop, second reach, evening box light. The rescue was the perpetrator returning with a ladder after pushing. Grateful to the pusher is the oldest misreading in this book.

A hard day needs what hard days always needed. Food at hunger. Water. Air. Early bed. A word with a friend. Ten minutes with feet up and phone down. Melt adds coating to tired and calls coating comfort. Comfort does not coat teeth. Comfort does not wake hands at ten.

When the thought says hard day, answer with the ditch seen plain. Who pushed this morning? Who offers the hand tonight? Same hands. Then think your new thought and pour tea. The day stays hard for an hour and softens. The packet would have made the night hard and the morning harder.
