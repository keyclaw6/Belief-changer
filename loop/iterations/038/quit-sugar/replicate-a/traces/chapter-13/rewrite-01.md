IF YOU STARTED HERE

If you opened the book here, at the back, put this page down for a moment.

This page will not free you. This page is reminders for someone already free. The method is not in these lines. The method is the whole book read in order, with an open head, letting each picture do its work.

I say that with warmth, not as a scold. We all hunt for the last page first. We want the answer without the walk. With this trap that cannot work, because the walk is what takes the belief apart. A list cannot do it. A summary cannot do it. Only seeing it, room by room, does it.

So close this now and start at the beginning. Read it all, plainly, in order.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Read with food in the cupboard and no panic in the chest. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

You have absolutely everything to gain from reading it straight through, and nothing to lose by doing so. Come back here when you have finished. This page will keep.

NOTHING MORE TO LEARN

You may feel a tug now to keep studying.

We felt it too. Years of diets and rules taught us that freedom must be guarded by study, that without a plan we would slide, that we needed one more tip, one more rule, one more voice to keep us straight. The hand wants a new packet of paper to hold where the old packet of sugar sat.

Hear this flat: you do not need ongoing teaching to stay free.

The belief has changed. That was the whole work. You saw that BAD SUGAR gave nothing, that the lift made the low, that hunger and satisfaction guide and the Sugar Trap only hired noise over them. Once seen, that cannot be unseen. You do not have to rehearse it daily. You do not have to polish it. You live it.

Ask the tug plain.

If staying free needed study, why did study never free you before, through all the weeks of leaflets and plans?

If one more rule kept people free, why did every rule end with the box light before swallow ended?

If plain days needed guarding, what exactly would you guard — hunger, which guides, or a packet, which no longer orders?

You know. Rules kept the question open. Seeing closed it.

Think of the two lives now, whole, for the rest of your life.

On one side, the old life hooked for the rest of your life: morning launch, 3pm reach, evening drawer, fog, coating, second reach, money, time, teeth, tired blood, head hired for sweet accountancy from first cup to last light.

On the other side, the plain life clear for the rest of your life: hunger met, stop clean, food tasting louder, mornings yours, shop yours, sofa yours, head returned to talk heard whole and work done in one pass.

Which rest-of-life do you want? You have chosen. No daily vote is needed. A closed question is not homework.

Do not turn this book into a new timetable. Do not reread to reassure yourself each week. Keep it on the shelf for a friend. Live the days. The days prove it faster than pages can.

GROWN BACK

Here is the last seeing, saved for now because you could not have held it at the start.

You have not given up sweetness.

We feared that at the start. We believed life would be greyer without sweet treats, that some glow would leave eating, that we would watch others hold joy while we held plain. That fear was the Con talking.

The fact is the other way around. Sweetness was stolen from you by the Trap and has now been handed back.

Think when sweetness was loudest. A small child with a peach, juice down the chin, stops mid-bite and runs off. No counting. No guilt. Hunger, taste, enough. That was sweetness before packets taught it. We all lived there once. We were not born picturing bars on Thursdays. We were born eaters.

Then the bright packets came, the bliss-point doses, the cartoon promise, the checkout ambush, the cinema hand, the birthday table, the office tin. Dose by dose, the tongue filmed, the nose dulled, the belly shouted over, the head hired. Real food went quiet under coating. Fruit went bland beside melt. Hunger went unheard beside timetable. What we called a sweet tooth was a hired tooth.

This was never about quitting. It was about growing back into the eater you were.

Look at supper tonight to prove it. Sit hungry at your own table. Eat what you like. Taste it. Bread warm, stew deep, water clean, an apple after that cracks and floods. Stop when satisfied.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Ask that meal plain.

If sweetness had left you, why does plain food taste louder now, and stop arrive cleaner?

If melt had lent joy to meals, why did melt-closed evenings end with fog and hands going again, not with settled pleasure?

If you had lost something real, why does the head feel lighter first, before the tongue notices anything?

You know. Coating muted. Uncoated, tasting returns.

I ate that way the first plain week and waited for the grey I had been promised. What came was not grey. Talk warmer. Sleep plainer. Mouth clean with water at ten. Hands quiet in the shop. No math under the blanket. The eater I was at five sat down at my table again, hungry, pleased, done when done.

That is growing, not losing. A child grown tall does not mourn the small shoes. A freed eater does not mourn the loud dose. You have not stepped down from something. You have stepped back into something.

So shut the book. Pour supper. Answer hunger. Close the book and get on with enjoying your life.

Take your phone and photograph the list below. Lay the page flat, take it straight, check you can read it. Slip the phone back in your pocket. That is your pocket memory. No studying each night. No revising. It sits there if a whisper ever needs answering, and answering takes seconds.

Now close the cover. Feel it shut under your palm. Put the book on the shelf, spine out, for a friend one day if they ask. You are not storing a plan you must obey. You are storing reminders for a seeing you already own.

Step to what is next. Supper waiting, a call to return, the street outside with its buses and rain. Tomorrow holds work, errands, talk, bills, weather, people to meet. Meet them direct, with head unhired and hours undivided. If a sweet thought crosses at a till or on a dull afternoon, you know what it is and you know your reply, and then you turn back to the day in front of you.

That is the whole outward push. No more lessons. No weekly check. No holding yourself in. Live forward and let the days fill with ordinary things done whole. The list stays in your pocket. You stay in your life.
