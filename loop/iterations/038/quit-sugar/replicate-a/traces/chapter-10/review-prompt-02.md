Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

*There is no small, timed, polite dose of BAD SUGAR that leaves the trap shut.*

THE FRIDAY BARGAIN

We all struck it. The bright bargain that looks like common sense.

"I'll allow Fridays." "Just one on the weekend." "I don't keep it in the house during the week, so Friday is controlled."

I struck it for years. Monday to Thursday plain meals, plenty of water, head clear by Wednesday, telling my wife how well the week went. Friday at four the mood turned. Shop on the way home. The large bar, the packet for the film, the dessert promised to the children as if promise needed melt. I ate Friday night with a hum in the chest I called pleasure. Saturday morning mouth sour, head thick, hand back in the packet before lunch because Friday had woken the hand. Sunday night vow to be good again. Monday restart with jaw set.

Watch that week hour by hour and the bargain shows its working.

Monday, rule fresh, mind busy with virtue. Tuesday, rule holds, eyes linger a second longer in the aisle. Wednesday, talk starts: only two days to go. Thursday, glow brightens round the imagined bar. We picture squares breaking, melt running, evening softened. Tongue waters on command. By Thursday night sleep thins a little with planning. Friday morning focus splits between work and evening load. Friday afternoon shop run takes longer, choosing among packets with care that feels like taste and is only hunger wearing a mask.

Friday night dose lands on a tongue made extra hungry by four days of waiting. Loud for three chews. Then flat under loud. Then sour edge. Then hand going again to cover sour. The box emptied before the swallow ended, and morning remembers only the first three chews and calls the night controlled.

That is the Sugar Trap working by calendar.

If the Friday dose were calm control, answer plain.

Why does Thursday hum louder than Monday, if waiting weakens want?

Why does Friday night take more than planned, if the rule holds the hand?

Why does Saturday call for a second round, if Friday closed the account?

You know. Waiting brightens, brightened dose wakes, woken hand asks again. Restriction feeds binge. Binge feeds rule. Rule feeds restriction. The wheel turns because the rule oils it.

The house-rule drawer tells the same tale in another room. "I don't keep it in the house." We say it with pride on Tuesday. Cupboard bare, drawer locked, pride high. Wednesday office biscuit appears and we take two because house is pure. Thursday petrol station chocolate because house is pure. Friday we buy small to keep house pure and eat large in the car so house stays pure. Purity of cupboard proves nothing about mouth. The trap does not live in the drawer. It lives in the belief that a timed dose gives something worth waiting for.

Creep proves it. Friday becomes Friday and Saturday. Saturday becomes Thursday night starter because week was hard. Thursday starter becomes Wednesday treat because you have been good. The line moves because a line that leaves belief alive must move. Belief says BAD SUGAR still pays. Belief will find a reason for pay-day to come early.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Read that Friday night with coating still on tongue. Plenty TO — teeth bathed, attention split, hunger signals drowned, purse taxed, sleep thinned, Saturday hand taught to go again. Nothing FOR — no joke made funnier, no week made kinder, no love made truer. Laughter did the lifting. Company did the warming. Story did the carrying. Dose only rode along and charged for the ride.

You see it plain on Friday night with mouth coated and head dull: totality leaves you free to hear laughter whole.

Friday keeps Monday hungry. That is the verdict. Keep the Friday and you keep the hunger that makes Friday shine.

THE CLIFF YOU ARE ASKED TO FALL A LITTLE

Stand with me at the cliff in morning light.

Sea below, gulls, wind tugging coats. A man in a bright jacket waves you to the edge. Grass short, rock pale, drop straight and long. He smiles like a friend.

"Jump," he says. "Only fall a few metres. Catch that ledge. Everyone does it on Fridays. Just the small fall. Controlled fall. You can handle a small fall."

You stare at him. You stare at the drop. You ask if he is mad.

He laughs. "You don't have to throw yourself off every day. Only Friday. Only a little. Only the special ledge. You don't keep cliff in the house, so one small Friday fall can't hurt."

No sane walker argues about technique. No one asks for a softer rock or a slower wind. The question is not how to fall a little. The question is why jump at all when the ground behind you is solid and wide.

That is what "just one" asks. "Cut down." "Wean a little." "Keep the special ones." Jump, but fall only a little.

I stood at that edge with a packet in my pocket, telling myself a small Friday fall proved I was in charge. Small fall still lands on rock. Small dose still lands on a tongue trained to call loud sweet a signal to go again. The hand that learned to go again does not ask permission from the calendar. It moves before decision, while mind still discusses ledges.

We learned the physics in our own kitchens. Permitted sliver, evening quiet, wrapper rustle, hand moving on rhythm, packet light, belly full and restless together, regret arriving while throat still works. Once triggered, the craving carries the binge. Not because you are weak. Not because you are broken for life. Because a triggered loop runs till sour stops it, and sour never stops it for long. It only pauses it till morning.

That loop is small and starving. That feeling is the Nibbler knocking for a feed.

Hear the knock for what it is. Twitch at seven. Picture of squares at eight. Rational talk at nine: one won't hurt, Friday proves control, tomorrow will be plain. Knock grows louder when fed with debate. Knock dies when left unfed while you pour tea and answer the phone and keep hands on warm cup.

Ask the cliff man three plain questions and watch him fold.

If a little fall is safe, why must the ledge be caught exactly, every time, with no wind allowed?

If cut-down proves command, why does each small dose need louder planning than any plain meal ever needed?

If the special one is precious, what makes it precious but waiting, and what makes waiting but belief that melt still pays?

You know. Precious is made by denial. Denial is made by belief. Belief is kept by the special. Break the circle by keeping no special.

Moderation makes BAD SUGAR important. Importance makes each hit louder. Louder hit makes next low deeper. Deeper low makes next rule stricter. Stricter rule makes next break bigger. The pincer closes from both sides, rule and binge squeezing the week till the week is all thought about dose.

Totality is not harder. Totality is the only level ground. No ledge to catch. No fall to measure. You walk back from the edge onto grass that holds, and the walk back is the escape you were promised from page one.

One line holds the cliff. No safe fall off a trap.

JUST ONE, TOMORROW, WEANING, SWAPPING

Now the rapid answers, because the Sweet Con keeps four scripts for this week and plays them in rotation. Hear each in your own voice, then hear it answered flat.

"Just one won't hurt."

One carries the binge. We saw it in our own hands. Single biscuit permitted, packet opened to prove control, hand moving on rhythm, mouth busy while mind counts, count lost, packet light. The hurt is not in sugar weight. The hurt is in belief re-lit. One says melt still pays. Belief re-lit says wait, crave, plan, fall. If one were nothing, why does one need announcing, defending, timing for Friday? Plain meals need no defence. Bread needs no anniversary. Only the trap needs ceremony.

"Tomorrow. After birthday. After holidays."

Tomorrow never holds a dose. Tomorrow holds only today repeated. I said after Christmas for three years. Christmas became January blues needing lift. January became Easter with children. Easter became summer party. Summer became autumn tiredness. The calendar has no empty square. Each delay says melt still matters enough to schedule life round. Life scheduled round dose is slavery wearing a diary. The day you pick is always today, because belief changes today or not at all. Waiting is the Con talking to keep its pay-day.

"I'll wean off slowly."

Weaning feeds fantasy between hits. Small Monday sliver, smaller Tuesday sliver, mind full of melt all week, tongue measuring, eyes watching packet like a clock. Each tapered hit arrives on a tongue sharpened by hunger and lands louder than a full packet eaten blind. Loud hit teaches louder want. Taper teaches timetable, not quiet. The tiny physical echo dies in days when left unfed. It thrives for months when fed a little on schedule.

"I'll switch. Diet drinks. Sweetener tablets. Honey. Agave. Dark. Fruit juice."

Swap the packet and you keep the user. I swapped to cola with no sugar, to tablets in tea, to thick honey on toast, to dark bars called grown-up. Same hour, same hand, same hum. Morning spoon, mid-morning packet, afternoon can, evening box. Sweet on tongue, sour after, hand going again to cover sour. Name changed. Timetable stayed. The body learned the same lesson: sweet hit means brief loud then call for more. The mind learned the same lie: life needs loud to be kind.

Sweeteners keep the ritual alive. Bottle in bag. Tablet in pocket. Packet in drawer for guests that guests never asked for. Ritual kept means belief kept. Belief kept means the Nibbler fed by thought even when tongue gets no sugar. Thought fed is dose enough to keep the wheel turning.

Ask the swap plain.

If the new sweet were the answer, why does the hour still run to the same timetable — lift, sour, reach, regret?

If honey were nature and tablets were science, why do both leave the same coated tongue and the same evening split between screen and cupboard?

If the product were the problem, why did changing product leave the hand unchanged?

You know. Hand follows belief. Belief says sweet hit pays. Change hit and belief stays. Change belief and hand rests.

That belief is the Sweet Con dressed as advice.

The fact is totality inside BAD SUGAR is the only stable ground. Not because your body cannot handle a crumb. Your body handles crumbs daily from plain meals without a blink. Stable because your mind cannot hold a deliberate exception without re-lighting the idea that melt pays. One exception keeps the idea alive. Idea alive keeps want alive. Want alive keeps timetable alive.

You stand at the drawer with packet for guests and calendar with Friday circled: totality shows you a plain drawer and a plain week, and that plainness is freedom remembered as your own.

THE WEEK THE EXCEPTION DOES YOUR THINKING

Stay in that week a little longer, because the second cost hides here and this is where negotiators get caught. Not the binge. The thinking.

Monday morning. Tongue has had nothing since Sunday night. Yet mind is already at work. You open the drawer to find batteries and see the guest packet. You tell yourself you are checking stock for visitors. You close the drawer. A minute later you open it again to straighten the corner. You close it again. You walk to the kettle and picture Friday squares breaking. You tell yourself picturing is harmless because mouth is plain. Picturing is the work. The exception has hired your attention for the week.

Monday lunch. Colleague offers biscuits. You say no with pride. Pride lasts ten seconds. Then the small voice starts its accountancy. Well done, that counts toward Friday. You have been good, Friday will taste better for this no. You rehearse what you will buy Friday, which shop, which size, whether large bar or small bars, whether to eat in car or save for sofa. No sweet has touched tongue today. Sweet has occupied head for an hour.

Tuesday. You do not keep it in the house during the week, so you eat out of the house to keep the house pure. Office cake at three. Petrol station roll at five because the day ran long. Each time the same sentence pays the toll: house is pure, so this does not count, Friday still stands. You defend the rule while breaking the body of it. By evening you feel oddly tired from arithmetic no plain eater ever does. Bread never needed a ledger. Soup never needed a defence. Only the timed dose needs lawyers.

Wednesday. Planning sharpens. I know this day well. I stood in the aisle for eight minutes comparing two packets I had compared the week before. Cocoa numbers. Weight. Price per hundred grams. Wrapper colour. I told my wife I was being a careful shopper. Careful shopping for plain meals takes two minutes and ends with food in the basket. Careful shopping for the exception takes ten minutes and ends with glow in the chest. The glow is not taste. The glow is preciousness being polished.

Wednesday night. Sleep thins. Not from hunger. Belly is fed. Sleep thins from scheduling. What time Friday? Before dinner or after? With film or with friends? Will the children want some? Should you buy extra for guests so your portion looks moderate? Should you hide your portion so no one comments? Questions breed questions. Each question feels like control. Each question is the Sweet Con keeping its seat at your table while your plate sits empty.

Thursday. The hum you already know, now hear what drives it. Tongue waters at noon without smell or sight. Picture arrives uncalled at two. Irritation rises at four when work drags, and mind names the irritation as need for Friday rather than need for walk and water and early night. Partner asks what is wrong and you say tired, week long. Week was not long. Waiting was long. Waiting makes four plain days feel like corridor to a door. Corridor feels narrow because door is marked special. Mark no door and corridor widens back into days.

Thursday night drawer check. Packet for guests still there. You weigh it in the palm. You tell yourself you are checking freshness. Freshness does not need weighing. Belief needs touching. Hand wants rehearsal. You put it back, take one square to test, tell yourself test does not count because Friday is the real one. Test counts in the only place that matters. Test tells mind the rule bends. Bending tells mind to spend Friday planning the bend.

Friday morning. Focus splits. Emails take twice as long because each gap fills with evening pictures. You tell yourself anticipation is pleasure. Anticipation is labour. Pleasure does not need four days of rehearsal. An apple at hunger needs no rehearsal. Soup at hunger needs no committee. Only the exception needs a week of unpaid thought to make three chews feel large.

Friday shop. The choosing hour. Aisle bright, hands cool, heart quick. You lift, compare, replace, lift again. Six minutes. Eight minutes. Strangers pass with baskets. No one else holds chocolate like a contract. You pay and walk out lighter, as if evening were now safe. Safety that must be bought weekly is not safety. It is rent.

Now count the hours. Not the grams. The hours. Monday picturing. Tuesday accountancy. Wednesday comparing. Wednesday night scheduling. Thursday hum. Thursday night testing. Friday choosing. Add the defending — explaining to friends why weekdays none and Friday all, explaining to yourself why office cake did not count, explaining to partner why this Friday must be large because last Friday was small. Ten hours of head for one hour of mouth. Mouth gets loud then flat then sour. Head gets the bill.

Swap-keepers pay the same bill in different coin. Tablet in pocket means pocket checked ten times a day. Bottle in bag means bag packed round bottle. Honey jar for tea means tea never drunk plain to test if plain could stand. Drawer for guests means guests used as reason to keep packet within reach. Each swap says tongue is safe while head stays hired. Head hired is the trap. The Nibbler does not live on sugar alone. It lives on sweet thought rehearsed till thought feels like need.

I kept the guest packet for a month after I first saw through weekdays. Tin closed, drawer shut, guests twice in that month, neither asked for sweet. I opened that drawer eleven times for no errand. Straighten. Check. Weigh. Close. Each open took twenty seconds. Each open cost an hour of hum after. One night I stood with drawer open and understood I was paying a guard to watch an empty room. Packet sat still. Mind paced round it.

Ask the drawer week plain.

If Friday is calm, why does Monday already rehearse it with mouth empty?

If the guest packet is for others, why do your hands weigh it when no guest is in the house?

If swapping solved it, why does the pocket still get patted, the bottle still get packed, the jar still get watched like a clock?

You know. Exception hires thought even on days tongue gets nothing. Thought hired keeps precious alive. Precious alive keeps timetable alive.

Now see what removal removes. Not only the binge. The employment.

No calendar math. Monday stays Monday, not minus-four-to-Friday. No drawer patrol. Drawer holds batteries and string and takeaway menus, plain things that ask nothing. No flavour committee. Shop takes minutes because basket holds meals, not contracts. No defence speech. No one needs to know weekdays and weekends because weekdays and weekends eat alike. No Thursday hum to interpret. Tired means tired, thirst means thirst, hunger means hunger, each answered in kind without translation into sweet.

The quiet is not empty. Quiet is mind back for other uses. Talk heard whole. Work done in one pass. Evening undivided. Sleep without scheduling. The hand that once moved before decision rests because no decision is pending. Pending is what kept it hovering. Close the pending and hovering stops.

That is why totality feels lighter before tongue notices anything. Lighter in head first. One question closed for good. No small fall to measure. No ledge to catch. No special to guard and polish and defend. Plain drawer. Plain week. Mind unemployed from sweet accountancy and returned to hunger, satisfaction and real food enjoyed as favourite.

No cut-down. No special. No tomorrow. No swap. That is the verdict. All cords out or the lamp stays on.

THE SEATBELT, NOT THE STEERING WHEEL

Drive with me a quiet road at dusk.

Hedgerows, shop lights just on, children on pavements with bags, radio low. You belt up. Click. Shoulders held. You do not plan to crash. You do not drive into posts to test the belt. Belt sits for the jolt no driver invites — sudden brake, pot-hole, dog running out. Belt never licenses wild driving. No driver says, belt on, so I can steer at walls.

Your body works that way with packet sweet.

Plain meals, market vegetables, bread with crust, soup, meat, rice, fruit, cheese, plain yoghurt, water and tea eaten at hunger to satisfied — that is the road. An accidental crumb at a canteen, a spoon of sauce thickened with packet sugar at a friend's table, a bite of cake taken in dim light before you read the table — those are pot-holes. Body absorbs the jolt, tongue clears with water, hunger speaks again at next meal, no panic needed. Mind does not steer into the post because belt exists.

Deliberate exception is steering at the wall. Friday bar bought with care. House-rule packet saved for guests and eaten at midnight. Just one to prove control. Sweetener kept in pocket to soften plain. Each says mind chose the jolt. Belt cannot save a driver who aims at posts.

I learned the difference at my mother's table. She served stew with a spoon of packet sweet stirred through, the way she learned from packets that show smiling cooks. I ate, tasted faint loud under salt, drank water, ate on. No hum. No timetable. No hand waking at ten. Belief stayed shut, so body stayed quiet. Six weeks earlier the same spoon would have started a night of cupboard trips, because belief stood open and every sweet taste said go. Same spoon. Different mind. Mind holds the wheel.

If you live with diabetes, take medicines that move sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with food illness past or present, or carry risk in changing meals, talk to your clinician first and follow them first.

Hold the line without panic and without preaching. Accidental taste never breaks a belief. Panic breaks more than taste ever could. Panic says one crumb proves you failed. Panic invites the full packet to finish a failure already declared. Belief says crumb is jolt, belt held, drive on. Drive on means next plain meal at hunger, water, sleep on one load, morning mouth plain.

Watch the two drivers hour by hour.

Driver one hits pot-hole, grips wheel, breathes, keeps road, eats plain at next hunger, forgets jolt by night. Thought passes like hedge. No ceremony. No confession. No extra rule.

Driver two hits pot-hole, cries failed, buys packet to punish failure, eats to sour, wakes hand, spends week in rule and binge, tells friends method needs will and struggle. Thought fed with debate grows to timetable.

Same pot-hole. Different steering. Steering is belief.

That is why a triggered packet matters only when belief lets it matter. Fed once by accident, echo dies. Fed nightly by design, echo learns to knock louder. Knock is dying when left alone. Knock is demanding when answered with packet.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you. Belt or no belt, wall still breaks glass. Do not aim at walls.

Totality is the road, not the belt. Belt for accidents. Mind for direction. Direction set once, held without debate, brings a quiet kind of relief that sits in the chest on the drive home when shops glow and hands stay on the wheel.

One line holds the road. Guard the belief and the hand follows.
