Chapter 7
THE ITCH IT PRETENDS TO SCRATCH

*That quiet you get from a cigarette is not a lift above normal, it is the last dose wearing off and asking for the next.*

### THE METER ON THE CORNER

Leave that drawer shut. You have seen the orders were false. What is left now is the fear in the body, the thought that says, all right, the method was wrong, but I still need it, my nerves still need it.

So let us watch one ordinary day with clear eyes, you and I, dose by dose.

We wake and light. We teach, we write, we drive, we queue, we argue, we laugh, we light again. Twenty times a day the hand moves. Twenty times a day something settles for a little while and then stirs again. We call that life with cigarettes. Look closer and it is a timetable.

Picture the parking meter on the corner outside my old flat. Iron post, glass eye, slot for coins. You drop a coin and the needle jumps. You walk away. Within minutes the needle is sinking. An hour later it is flat and flashing. Drop another coin or get a ticket. All day the same post, the same slot, the same sinking.

That post is what we carry in the chest.

When smoke enters the lungs, nicotine reaches the brain within seconds. The acute buzz fades within minutes for most of us, so the hand looks for the next dose to push the needle up again. What you call relief is the needle swinging back toward where a non-smoker stood all morning.

Ask what that means, plainly.

If each dose truly gave calm, why does the calm never hold till the next hour? If each dose truly took you above normal, why do you need twenty a day to feel normal at all? A non-smoker does not spend the day topping anything up. We do. Who is ahead?

This is the nicotine trap ticking in daylight.

We were conned twice, you and I. Once into believing the coin buys something. Once into forgetting that the meter was installed by the coins themselves. Before we smoked, there was no hourly sink. After we started, the sink appeared, and we were sold the coins as the answer to the sink.

Watch your own afternoon answer it. Light at two. By half past two the edge is back. Not the row from lunch. Not the bill on the desk. A small physical nag, empty, nagging, asking. Light at three. Same brief settle. Same return. The problems on the desk sat untouched through both. The only thing that rose and fell was the level the last dose left.

It does plenty TO the body. It mends nothing underneath.

That is the first half of the day to price. The second half is the half no smoker ever watches, because we always drop the coin before we see what happens without it.

### THE TUG IS SMALL

“But I feel it. It is real. I get edgy, I get empty, I cannot settle.”

I know. I fed it for years. I am not telling you that you imagine it. I am telling you what it is, and how small it is once named.

What is left is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

Not pain. Not collapse. Not a seizure of the nerves. A tug. Like a collar a size too tight. Like hunger an hour before lunch. Like an itch you notice when the room goes quiet. We who smoked for years learned to read that tug as emergency. It was never emergency. It was the fading of a dose.

That is the Nipper stirring, nothing more.

The Nipper is a trivial physical creature. Feed it and it quiets for minutes. Starve it and it whines for a little while and then dies. It has no strength of its own. All its strength is borrowed from what we believe about it.

Think of the tight shoe. Wear a shoe two sizes too small all morning and the moment you kick it off in the afternoon feels marvellous — no, it feels like bliss. Did the kicking-off give you something? Or did the shoe create the pain the kicking-off stopped? We wore the shoe, you and I. Every dose was kicking the shoe off for minutes while keeping the shoe on for life.

“I love smoking but I hate being a smoker.” I hear that often. The heaven at lighting, the flat shame after. That heaven is not a gift. It is the second the shoe comes off. The shame after is the shoe still on your foot.

Do you see how the credit was stolen? You taught the class. You finished the paragraph. You drove the miles. You held your temper on the phone. Then you lit and told the stick it did it. The work was yours. The hands were yours. The patience was yours. The dose only quieted its own tug while you did it, then claimed the afternoon as its own.

It takes. It does not give.

Now keep your eyes on that tug a moment longer, because fear has made it bigger in memory than it ever was in the body. When we smoked, the tug never got a chance to finish. We fed it every forty minutes. We never saw its whole shape. We saw only its first whine and called the whine unbearable. A man who always scratches at the first prickle never learns that the prickle passes on its own. He believes scratching saved him.

You have never watched the meter without a coin. That is why you fear it.

### THE DAY IT FADES AND RETURNS

Follow the tug a little longer, because this is where fear lies to us most.

That restless empty feeling begins within hours of the last dose, typically sharpens over the first couple of days without, then eases over the coming weeks. That is the usual pattern, not a sentence on you. Some feel it more, some less, some barely at all once they see it. If you live with a medical condition or take prescribed medicine, speak to your clinician for that care.

Mark what that pattern proves.

It proves the tug is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical peak, not proof the cigarette was your nature. It proves the body is returning toward its own baseline, not falling away from it. A wound itches as it closes. The itch does not prove you need the nail again.

All the big reasons on top are the Smokescreen.

The Smokescreen says, this tug means you cannot cope, you will always be edgy now, you have damaged yourself for good. Listen with clear eyes. The Smokescreen is brainwashing talking, installed by years of ads and pack talk and our own repeated misreading. It takes a small physical fade and dresses it as identity.

Ask the questions that strip it, and answer honest.

If the dose cured edginess, why did every day on doses contain edginess between doses? If the dose made you you, why did you exist for years before the first dose without this hourly nag? If the tug proves need, why do non-smokers live whole days, hard days, bright days, without it and never miss it?

You know. The edginess between doses was the product, not the cure. The you before the first dose was the normal you. The tug proves a fading level, nothing more.

We never rose above normal. Each hit only dragged us briefly back toward the calm a non-smoker keeps for nothing, then guaranteed the next dip. A lift that guarantees a dip is not a lift. It is a loan with the interest taken in advance.

### THE METER WITH NO COIN

Now do the watching we never did. Leave the coin in the pocket and watch the needle.

It is eight in the evening. You have had your last dose of the day. Normally you would light at nine, at ten, once more before bed. Tonight no coin drops. What happens in the chest?

For the first hour, little. The needle is still high from the last coin. You drink tea. You talk. The hand makes its old half-move toward the pocket and finds nothing, and the mind notes it like a missed step on stairs. Not pain. A blank.

By the second hour the glass eye starts to sink. Here comes the familiar prickle. Empty. Slightly restless. The shoulders want to roll. Attention snags. On a smoking day this is the second you would already have lit and called the lighting relief. Tonight you sit and let the needle move.

Watch what it does. It does not shoot to zero. It sinks a notch and holds. Then sinks another notch. The tug gets a fraction louder, then stays. Like a tap dripping in the next room. Annoying if you listen for it. Easy to live beside if you get on with supper.

By the third and fourth hour the drip is steady. This is the point where the old brainwashing speaks loudest. It says, you see, it is getting worse, it will get worse all night, you will not sleep, you will climb the walls by morning. Do not argue with the voice. Watch the meter instead.

You go to bed. The meter goes with you, flashing quietly. You sleep. The body sleeps with a sinking needle as it has slept a thousand times between doses. No coin is dropped all night on any smoking night either. Six, seven hours pass without a dose every night of your smoking life, and you do not wake screaming. The Nipper whines in its sleep and is ignored. Remember that in the morning.

Morning comes. The needle is low. The tug is there, unmistakable, that collar-tight feeling. On a smoking day you would read this as proof you need it. Read it true today. This low needle is not damage. This is the same low needle you woke with every smoking morning before the first light. The only difference is you are now seeing it without instantly covering it with a coin.

Let the morning run without the coin. Wash. Dress. Kettle. Keys. The hand reaches three, four times out of habit. Each reach is followed by a small hollow surprise. That surprise is not suffering. That is the habit-reach meeting clear eyes.

By mid-morning the tug has a shape you can draw. It rose last night, it held, it is still there, a low background hum. It has not doubled. It has not trebled. It has not become pain. It is the same tug you felt between every dose, only you are now letting it stay for an hour instead of burying it at minute thirty. A coin buried it for minutes. Time without a coin lets it show how small it is.

Ask the meter plainly as the hours pass.

If no coin for four hours meant collapse, how did you sleep six hours every night without one? If the tug were growing without limit, why is the ten o'clock tug much the same size as the eight o'clock tug, only more familiar? If this were need, why can you still pour, answer, laugh, carry, while it hums?

You can. That is the answer the fed day never let you hear.

Afternoon of the first coinless day, the hum changes key. It gets duller. Less sharp. More like tiredness than edginess. Smokers misread this badly. They say, I am flat, I am dull, this proves I need it. Look at the post again. A meter with no coin does not stay flashing forever. The spring inside loosens. The glass eye dims. The post is still there on the corner, but the demand is less urgent than at hour two. The Nipper fed every hour screams for its hour. The Nipper unfed for ten hours starts to lose its voice.

Evening comes. You have gone a whole working day with a sinking needle and no ticket. No disaster arrived. Papers were answered. Buses caught. Food eaten. Temper kept. The tug visited, sat, visited again. Each visit a little less convincing. This is not theory. This is what the usual pattern means in hours and hands: the fade rises, holds, then begins to lose edge because there is no fresh level to fall from.

That is the starved meter. Fed, it sinks fast and demands. Starved, it sinks, flashes, then slowly goes quiet.

### THE PEAK THAT FOOLS

I will not lie to you about the second day. I am on your side, and you need true seeing, not surprise.

For most of us the second and third day without coins hold the loudest whine. The needle, left low, sends up its sharpest nag. The collar feels tighter. Concentration slips. The small hum becomes a tap on the shoulder every twenty minutes. This is the short typical peak the body runs when a daily level stops being topped up.

It feels, for a day, like the opposite of what I just told you. It feels like it is growing, not easing. That feeling fools every smoker who ever tested it with torn orders.

So watch it with the meter in mind, not the fear in mind.

On a smoking day the needle jumped twenty times and sank twenty times. Up, down, up, down. Sharp relief, sharp sink. That jagged line felt normal because it was frequent. On a coinless second day the needle stays low and sends one dull, steady signal. No jumps. No sinks. Just the low whine of a spring unwinding. Because it does not jump, the mind calls it endless. Because it does not sink further, the mind calls it proof.

It is neither. It is the Nipper at its thinnest, making its most noise before it thins out.

Picture the post on that second day. No coin since yesterday evening. The glass eye is dim. The mechanism inside, used to coins every hour, keeps clicking the shutter as if a coin should have come. Click. Click. Empty. Click. That clicking is the tug. It is not power. It is habit in metal. A meter cannot keep clicking forever without coins. The spring runs down.

Walk through the hours of it so fear has nowhere to hide.

Morning of day two, the tug wakes before you do. Mouth dry. Chest hollow. Hands a little too quick. You would have lit before feet touched floor on any smoking morning. Now you stand, wash, move. The quickness stays for an hour, then thins while you are busy with coat and keys. Notice that thinning. A true need does not thin while you button a coat. A fading signal does.

Midday, the taps come. Ten o'clock, half past, eleven. Each tap says now, now, now. On smoking days you obeyed each tap and the next tap still came. Today you do not obey, and watch: the tap, disobeyed, does not grow fangs. It taps, waits, passes. Ten minutes later you have forgotten it until the next one. A master does not tap and leave. A dying click does.

Afternoon, the old stories queue. The Smokescreen brings its costumes. One says you are deprived. One says you are brave to suffer. One says one coin would prove you are still in charge. All three agree the tug is big and must be answered. All three are the same voice with different hats. The tug underneath the hats is still the same small hum, a little louder today, still not pain, still not collapse, still the fading of a level, not the loss of a prop.

Evening of day two, tiredness arrives. Smokers call tiredness withdrawal and reach. Call it true. You are tired because you have carried attention all day, because sleep was lighter, because the mind watched itself. Tiredness is not danger. Rest. Eat. Sleep early. The meter clicks in the dark and you sleep through most of it, as you always did between evening and morning.

Day three runs much the same, for most a fraction softer. The peak does not climb a mountain. It rounds a low hill. Up a little on day two, flat, down a little on day three. By day four and five the hours between taps stretch. An hour without thinking of it. Then two. Then a morning where the hand does not make the half-move. The glass eye is dusty. The post is still on the corner of memory, but you walk past it to the shop without stopping.

Ask the questions that close the fear, and let your own hours answer.

If the tug without coins grew without end, why do the taps get farther apart after the second day instead of closer? If day two proved you need coins, why did day two, with no coins, still let you work, eat, talk and sleep? If the low needle were your true state, why does the low needle get quieter the longer no coin drops, instead of louder?

You know, because you have now watched it. The loudest day is still small. The small, left alone, gets smaller.

That is what the fed day hid. Fed, the Nipper looked endless because we never let it finish a single whine. Starved, it finishes. It peaks briefly, then eases. Only belief can keep it alive past that easing.

### ONLY BELIEF CAN RE-FEED IT

Here is the turn where the whole trap shows its hinge.

After the spring loosens, the body has done its part. The level is gone. The clicks thin. What is left to put a coin back in the slot is not the chest. It is the thought.

Watch how the thought arrives, because it never arrives as physics. It arrives as a sentence.

“You have done well. One to prove you can take it or leave it.”

“Today was hard. You earned a little party.”

“Everyone is out there. One will not hurt.”

Each sentence sounds like you. None of them is the tug. The tug by then is a faint hum you can barely find at noon. The sentences are the Smokescreen, keeping its seat after its servant has starved. Feed the sentence and you feed the Nipper back to life. Refuse the sentence and the Nipper stays starved.

This is why two smokers can pass the same second day and live two different weeks. One watches the clicks thin and thinks, the fade passes, I will let it. The other watches the same thinning clicks and thinks, I have proved I can suffer, now I deserve the coin. Same chest. Different belief. One walks past the post. One drops a coin and rewinds the whole timetable to hour zero, needle jumping, sinking by half past, tug back by three.

Do you see where the power sits? Not in the metal. In the meaning.

The Nipper cannot buy its own coins. It has no hands, no money, no vote. It can only whine and hope you translate the whine as need, pleasure, reward, calm. The Smokescreen does the translating. It says whine means you cannot cope. It says whine means life will be flat. It says whine means you are deprived. Correct the translation and the whine is just a whine, brief, typical, already easing. Accept the translation and the hand moves to the pocket on its own.

I learned this watching my own meter go quiet. The third evening without, I sat where I always lit. The tug came, thin as thread. Then came the sentence, loud as a friend: go on, one to close the day. For the first time I heard the gap between them. The thread in the chest. The voice on top. The thread could not have lifted a feather. The voice could have rebuilt the whole cage. I did not argue with the voice. I named it. That is not me. That is the old tutoring talking. The thread passed in minutes. The voice, unheard, passed with it.

You can step out of this easily, immediately and permanently.

That sounds too large only while the voice is speaking. Look at the mechanics you have now watched. A small fade that peaks briefly and eases. A belief that dresses the fade as self. Remove the dress and there is nothing left to fight. You do not need to overpower the post. You need to stop feeding it and stop believing its shutter-click is orders.

Ask the last questions on this hinge, and answer from your own coinless hours, not from borrowed fear.

If the physical need were dominant, why does the same faint hum lead one man to freedom and another back to twenty a day, with no difference in the hum? If the coin fixed anything, why does the re-fed meter demand again within the hour, proving the fix was the restart? If belief were nothing, why is the sentence always there at the exact moment the hum gets quiet — “just one” arriving precisely when one is least needed?

You know. The hum does not decide. The sentence decides. Guard the belief and the hands stay free. Let the sentence back in and the hands re-enlist. Belief is dominant. The body follows.

That is the whole mechanism without paint. A trivial creature starved in days. A large tutoring cleared in the mind. Starve the small. Clear the large. There is nothing else to fight.

### THE SMOKER AT THE NEXT TABLE

Now watch the others, because they will try to pull your eyes back.

Sit in a cafe. The man at the next table laughs, talks, lights. For ninety seconds his shoulders drop. He looks, for ninety seconds, like a man helped. Then the talk goes on. Ten minutes later his fingers tap. Twenty minutes later his hand is in the pocket. Half an hour later he is out in the cold feeding the meter again while his coffee cools.

Did you witness pleasure? Or did you witness a coin dropping?

“Just one with coffee. Just one after the row. Just one because we are out.” I know those sentences. We all spoke them. They sound like choice. Watch the timetable inside them. The coffee smoker lights after coffee every day. The row smoker lights after calm has already returned. The night-out smoker lights because others light. Same slot. Same sinking. Different story on top.

Keep your own counsel when they offer their story. The bragger with his hard weeks, the mourner who still sighs after years, the joker who taps the pack and says one will not hurt. Warm to the person, always. We were that person. But do not borrow their timetable. You have priced your own day. You have watched the needle sink in your own chest. Their coins do not change your meter.

Who is in charge of your pocket now, you or the post?

Watch a whole evening of them without envy, and let the meter do the pity work for you. First light, brief settle. Second light, brief settle. Third search for a place to light, irritation, going without, tapping, leaving early to feed. Between coins the same edginess you once called yours. Inside coins the same brief quiet you once called help. From outside, with clear eyes, it is not a party. It is maintenance of a sink. Would you envy a man who must leave his friends every forty minutes to feed iron?

You know the answer, because you have lived both sides of the table now — the fed side where the needle always sank, and the first coinless hours where the needle sank, flashed, then began to quiet. Their ninety seconds do not disprove your whole day. Their tapping proves it.

### IT CAUSED IT

Bring it down to the skin, because the skin tells truth.

Every smoker knows an itch that seems to ask for the nail. Mosquito summer. You scratch. Ah. For seconds the place quiets. By night the place is red and raised and asking louder. You scratch again. Same seconds. Same louder return. After a week you have a sore you swear proves you must scratch.

Did scratching fix anything?

Smoking is scratching that guarantees more itching. The first doses installed the hourly sink where none stood. Every later dose quieted the sink for minutes while deepening the sink for the day. The quiet felt real, and it was real, as the seconds after scratching are real. Real and back-to-front.

Feel it in your own hours, not in theory. Morning dose. Quiet. Tug by mid-morning. Mid-morning dose. Quiet. Tug by lunch. Lunch dose. Quiet. Tug by two. All day the same wave, up for minutes, down for the hour. The wave was never the sea. The wave was the coin.

Now feel the coinless hours beside it, not as idea but as lived contrast. Evening tug. No coin. Hum through supper. Sleep. Morning low. No coin. Hum through coat and keys. Midday taps. No coin. Tap, wait, pass. Second-day loudness. No coin. Loudness rounding the hill, then thinning. No new sink installed because no new level was poured. The itch, unscratched, losing its redness. The skin, left alone, closing.

Which proved the cure — the scratch that asked again in an hour, or the leaving-alone that asked less each day?

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

That is the whole inversion in one line to keep. A small physical fade, misread by a large implanted belief, topped up twenty times a day until topping up looked like living. Starve the small fade and it passes. Clear the large belief and there is nothing left to feed.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

**SUMMARY**
- Each dose lifts a sinking level for minutes and guarantees the next sink, so relief is a brief return toward where non-smokers stand, not a rise above.
- The tug is a small physical signal with a short typical peak that begins within hours, sharpens for a day or two, then eases, not proof of need and not proof of self.
- Left without a coin the meter sinks, flashes loudest on the second day, then quiets, and only a belief-sentence can put a coin back in.
- Other smokers show the same fed timetable, a ninety-second settle followed by tapping and leaving to feed, not freedom to envy.
- The itch was installed by scratching, and leaving it unscratched lets it close, so stopping is escape from the sink, not loss of help.
