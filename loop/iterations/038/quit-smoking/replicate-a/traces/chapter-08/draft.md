Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The bedroom floor, the kettle and the first breath; the plate after the last bite and the taste that stays; the work doorway, the cold air and empty hands

*Your morning, your meal and your break were never lit by a cigarette — they were complete before the match was struck.*

### THE HAND BEFORE THE FEET

We know that morning.

The alarm cuts. The eyes are not open. The feet have not touched the floor and the hand is already moving across the bedside table, finding the pack by feel, finding the lighter by feel. We sit on the edge of the bed and put smoke on empty lungs before we have swallowed, before we have stood, before we have thought a single thought of our own. Then we cough and call the cough waking up.

"I need it to start me. Without it I cannot face the day."

I hear you. I lived that half-asleep fumble for years. I am not mocking the feeling. I am asking you to look at what it was.

If that first dose woke you, how were you awake enough to find it in the dark? If it gave you energy, why did you still move like a man underwater until the shower, the water, the kettle did their work? A non-smoker wakes with the same thick head, the same dry mouth, the same slow limbs. He stands, he washes, he drinks something warm, and the thickness clears. You did the same — stand, wash, drink — only you put a dose in the middle and gave the dose the credit.

Watch it without the dose for a morning in your mind.

Feet on cold floor. Stand. The weight goes through the legs. Walk to the bathroom. Water on the face. Cold stings, then clears. The mouth is sour — that is the stale end of last night, not a lack. Brush, rinse. Kettle on. The click, the hum, the steam on the window. First breath at the open back door while it boils. The chest moves. The air is sharp. Cough, yes, a little. Then a second breath, deeper, taken all the way down.

Where in that chain did smoke do anything but interrupt?

What is left over in those first minutes is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

Not need. Not self. That is the usual shape, not a sentence on you. A collar-tight hum from the last dose fading overnight. We read it for years as proof we could not start. It was proof we had finished the night low, as every smoker does between evening and morning, and covered the low instantly instead of letting the kettle and the water and the moving legs do the starting.

You start. The dose never started you.

### THE PLATE THAT DOES NOT NEED A FULL STOP

Then the meal.

We finish the last bite. The plate is empty. The taste is still in the mouth — salt, butter, coffee edge — full and warm. And before the taste has faded the hand is already out. Click. Light. Draw. The taste is gone under grey. We lean back and say that finished it. That rounded it. That made it perfect.

"Food is nothing without that cigarette after."

Is that true? Answer from your own mouth, not from the pack talk.

If the after-meal dose made food taste better, why did it always come after the tasting was done? If it completed the meal, why did it wipe the meal's taste off the tongue and replace it with ash? Did you ever light before the first bite to make the food better? Of course not. You waited until pleasure was already there, then stood a dose in front of it and told the dose it brought it.

Think of your own evenings. The Sunday roast eaten slow. The curry with friends, laughing, second helping. The sandwich at the desk eaten in four bites because work pressed. In each one the pleasure was heat, hunger met, company, rest from work. The dose added no flavour. What smoker ever said, this needs no salt, it has smoke? We put good things in our mouths and then burned the aftertaste on purpose.

Try the end of a meal in your mind without it.

Last bite chewed slow. Fork down. Sit. Do not get up. Let the taste stay where it belongs. Breathe through the nose. The mouth is alive for minutes after eating — that is the body tasting, as it is built to do. Sip water. Sip coffee. Talk. The full belly settles. The shoulders drop because work is paused and hunger is gone, not because a tube burned.

You will feel the old half-move. Hand to pocket. That old pellet after a finished paragraph, that little payment after teaching, driving, finishing — the same hand, the same second. That tug visits, thin, and passes if you leave the taste alone. Ten minutes later the taste has faded on its own, clean, and you are still satisfied. With a dose, ten minutes later the taste was ash and the tug was already rebuilding for the next hour.

Which honoured the food — the leaving of the taste to linger, or the burning of it?

It was you that tasted. Not the stick. The work of eating, the hunger, the cooking, the company — yours. The dose only quieted its own claim while you did it, then stole the meal's name.

### THE BREAK THAT WAS NEVER A BREAK

And the break. This is the one we guard hardest.

The screen blurs. The back aches. Voices press. We push the chair back, fish the pack, step out to the doorway. Cold hits the face. We light, draw, stare at nothing. Ten minutes. Then back in. We say we rested. We say we can think again. We say non-smokers do not understand what a break needs.

We lived that doorway, you and I. Let us look at what rested us.

Was it the paper and the powder? Or was it pushing the chair back, standing, walking, cold air on hot cheeks, eyes looking far instead of near, hands empty for ten minutes, nobody asking for anything? Strip the dose out and every one of those stays. A man who steps out with nothing in his hands still stands, still feels the air, still looks at the sky, still lets the shoulders fall. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on the pause.

Do it now in your mind, as you will do it tomorrow.

Chair back. Stand. Legs straighten. Walk. Door opens. The outside hits — traffic hum, rain smell, that stairs-breath pull halfway up when you climb for air. Let the chest take it. Roll the neck. Unclench the jaw. Watch people pass. Think nothing on purpose. Hands in pockets, nothing to hold, nothing to tap, nothing to time.

The tug comes, of course. Empty, nagging, a little edgy at the edges. Let it sit beside you while you stand there. It does not stop you standing. It does not stop you seeing. It does not stop the back easing because you stood. Two minutes in, the mind clears — because you stepped away, because blood moved, because noise dropped. That clearing was always the break. We credited smoke for what distance did.

Ask the doorway plainly.

If the break dose rested you, why did you always need another one an hour later? If it cleared the head, why did the head always cloud again before the next doorway? If non-smokers cannot rest without it, why do they take the same ten minutes, drink the same tea, stare at the same car park, and go back in steady without once thinking of a pack?

You know. The rest was stopping work. The clearness was air and distance. The dose only fed the Nipper for minutes while you rested, then made the next hour's itch certain.

The Nipper is small. The Smokescreen around the break is large. It says pause means puff, pause has always meant puff, pause will be empty without puff. That tutoring was installed, not born. Before the first cigarette you paused a hundred times a day — kicking a ball, looking out a classroom window, sitting on a wall — and every pause worked without a light. The body knows how to pause. It never forgot.

### YOUR BODY ALREADY KNOWS

So bring the three together — floor, plate, doorway — and feel the shape that is left.

Morning without covering the low: feet, water, kettle, breath. Meal without burning the taste: last bite, linger, sip, talk. Break without feeding in the doorway: stand, walk, air, far eyes, empty hands. None of them asks for anything but what is already there. None of them leaves you less.

"I will miss the favourite. The morning one. The after-food one. The one with tea on the break."

That sentence sounds like three favourites. It is one sentence with three hats. The favourite was never morning, meal or break. The favourite was the brief quiet of the tug while morning, meal or break did the real giving. Take the same morning, the same meal, the same break, and let the tug quiet on its own — which it does, minute by minute, once no fresh level is poured — and the giving stands out cleaner than before. Food tastes more because ash does not take it. Air feels more because the chest is not full of grey. Mornings start more because you feel the starting happen in the legs and lungs, not in the lighter.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

That is not a slogan to learn. That is what your own floor and plate and doorway just told you. Each dose put the itch back where the last dose left it. Each pause without a dose lets the skin close a little more. A smoker never rises above the normal a non-smoker keeps for nothing. He only climbs briefly back toward it, then sinks. Living those hours without topping up is not going without. It is staying where non-smokers stand all day.

I stood in that doorway myself the first week without, tea going cold, hands doing that stupid half-search. The tug tapped. Then the air came cold over the yard and the back of the neck loosened anyway. The tea tasted of tea all the way down. I laughed out loud because the break had worked without the thing I swore made it work. No courage in it. No white knuckles. Just eyes open.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

**SUMMARY**
- The morning start comes from standing, washing, moving and warm drink, not from smoke on empty lungs.
- The meal is finished by hunger met and taste lingering, not by ash laid over the tongue.
- The break rests because work stops, legs move and air and distance clear the head, not because a dose burns in the middle of it.
- The small tug that visits in each moment is a fading level, not a need and not a self, and left alone it passes while the moment still gives.
- Morning, meal and break lived without a dose are fuller because nothing stands between the body and its own tasting and breathing.
