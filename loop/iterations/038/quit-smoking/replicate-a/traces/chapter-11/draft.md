Chapter 11
NO SPECIAL ONES, NO TOMORROW

IN THIS CHAPTER
The five-months morning, the diary that creeps back up, the brass lamp, the cliff edge, the word tomorrow, the myths table

*There is no careful one and no safe tomorrow — one dose rebuilds the bottle and delay only keeps the bill running.*

### THE BARGAIN WE ALL MAKE

You stood with me on that late patio with the heaters ticking and watched the best one prove nothing.

Now the voice changes its bid. It stops arguing that ciggies are pleasure. It sounds reasonable. It says:

“All right. No more excuses. Just don’t be extreme. Cut down. Keep one for after dinner. Keep one for nights out. Quit properly tomorrow.”

We have all made that bargain. I made it for years. It feels modest. It feels adult.

Look at it straight.

If one is harmless, why did a hundred never satisfy? If cutting down controls it, why did every cut-down diary you ever kept creep back up? If tomorrow settles it, why has tomorrow moved a hundred times and never arrived?

That is how the nicotine trap stays fed — not by joy, but by the bargain that leaves one string attached.

You are not bargaining with a friend who will keep half a promise. You are bargaining with a con that lives on the half kept. You saw the night give and the stick take, and any deal that leaves the stick at the table leaves the taking in place, dressed up now to look free of risk.

ONE DOSE DOES NOT CLOSE THE DEAL. IT REOPENS IT.

### FIVE MONTHS AND ONE MATCH

Let me tell you about a morning I will never forget because it taught me what one means.

I had gone five months without a puff. Not five hours. Five months. Mornings to the kettle without lighting. Meals finished and taste lingering. Breaks with hands full of other things. The empty, slightly edgy tug had gone quiet long before. I thought of myself as clear.

Then at a wedding a mate held out a pack and said what mates say. “Go on. One won’t hurt. You’ve proved you can control it.”

And I thought the same thought you have thought: after this long, surely one to be sociable cannot drag me back.

I lit it. First pull, head swimming, throat burning after months of nothing. Second pull, coughing. I put it out half-burned and laughed. See? No pleasure. No danger.

By night I was thinking about the second half. By next morning I was buying a pack. By Friday I was back to a pack a day, standing where I had stood half a year before, only now with the added sting of having to climb again.

“I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way.” I have heard that sentence from smokers who lived my wedding morning to the letter. I do not mock it. I lived it.

Hear what that morning proves and what it does not prove.

It proves one lit match can re-light the whole chain because the chain was never about that night’s pleasure. It does not prove you are doomed if you ever slip. A slip is a rumble strip, loud and sour in the mouth, there to steer you straight back to sight. It only turns into a crash if you let the old belief back in and keep feeding doses after it.

Ask from your own mouth:

If one after five months proved control, why did control need a second one the same night? If that one was pleasure, why did it taste of burn and bring cough, not joy? If you were free for months and one made you caged again, what did the one give?

Nothing. It took. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

### THE LAMP AND THE CLIFF

Picture a brass lamp on a shelf.

While you do not rub it, it sits dull and harmless. Rub it once — just one little rub to remember old times — and the genie boils out, big and grinning, and rebuilds the whole bottle around you. Walls. Bars. Pocket-checks. Doorway dates. The timetable. You did not summon a memory. You summoned the tenant.

That is what a special one is. Not a taste. Not a test. A rubbing of the lamp.

We tell ourselves some are outside the trap. The morning one. The after-dinner one. The night-out one. The holiday one. The crisis one. Give them pretty names and they sound like exceptions. They are the same dose in prettier clothes.

And cutting down is the cliff edge of the same trick.

Picture a man at the cliff who says, “I won’t jump. I’ll just dangle and fall a little.” Would you call that safe? Would you call that control? Falling less is still falling, and the drop teaches the feet to step back to the edge faster next time.

That is what the cut-down diary does. Three a day instead of twenty. Each dose now arrives after hours of white-knuckle waiting, so each hit feels bigger. The tug grows louder between doses. The mind spends all day counting, watching the clock, dreaming of the next fix. You smoke less paper and think about smoking more. Then stress comes, or drink comes, or the wedding comes, and three becomes five becomes ten becomes twenty, because the belief that one helps was never cut at all.

I kept such diaries. Monday: four. Tuesday: three and proud. Wednesday: six and ashamed. Thursday: lie to the diary. The paper got fewer numbers. The slavery got louder.

Did cutting down make each cigarette less precious or more precious? Did waiting longer make the tug smaller or bigger? Did the cliff get safer because you promised to fall only a short way?

You know the honest answers because your own hand wrote them. No special paper is mild. No small number is safe. Both keep the Nipper fed on a shorter leash, and a fed Nipper always pulls for more, leaving you glad to be free of counting at last.

ONE KEEPS THE BOTTLE BUILT. NONE LEAVES IT EMPTY.

### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP'S FAVOURITE WORD

“Not today. Tomorrow. After Christmas. After the exams. After this rough patch.”

We say it to sound kind to ourselves. The Smokescreen loves that kindness because it costs the trap nothing and buys it everything.

Tomorrow does two jobs for the trap. It admits smoking is doing you harm — otherwise why quit tomorrow? — and it lets you keep dosing today. Fear one way, comfort the other. That pull in both directions is the tug-of-war of fear, and both ropes belong to the trap.

Count what tomorrow charges.

Twenty today. Twenty tomorrow. Four hundred a month. Mouth sour each morning. Chest lined a little tighter each stair. Money burned. Hours cut into doorway slices. Another month photographing the match-strike and calling it the evening.

If tomorrow is certain, why not now? If now feels too soon, what will tomorrow add but more doses between? If the crisis makes today impossible, did the dose ever solve a crisis or only interrupt it while the papers sat untouched on the desk?

The fact is delay does not soften the change. It hardens the chain. The body does not need a Monday to return to breathing and tasting. The mind does not need a new year to see a con. What needs time is the trap, because time is doses, and doses are wages.

I am not asking you to hurry from panic. Panic never freed anyone. I am asking you to see that tomorrow is not a date. It is a dose-keeper, and you can put it down today and walk lighter for having done so, free from the bleak calendar of one more pack first.

THERE IS NO SAFE TOMORROW. THERE IS ONLY TODAY KEPT OR GIVEN AWAY.

### STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Put the leftovers on the table and answer them fast, in your own voice first, then in truth.

“I’ll just cut down to three a day.” Then you will live for three hits, wait harder between them, make each one precious, and creep back up when life presses. Cutting down polishes the cage. It does not open it.

“I’ll keep just the night-out one. Just the morning one.” Which one kept you? The morning dose started the day’s chain. The night dose taxed the night you loved. If there is no benefit in twenty, what benefit hides in one? One keeps the belief alive, and the belief rebuilds the twenty.

“I’ll quit tomorrow, after the wedding, after stress.” Tomorrow keeps today’s wages flowing to the seller. The wedding will have drink and talk with or without paper. Stress will sit untouched while you step out. Why pay another week for nothing?

“But the numbers prove most attempts fail and I need a prop.” Listen carefully because two true facts get twisted here. In motivated trials a licensed product can lift odds against control. Out on the street most smokers try with nothing and few succeed in any single try. Different people, different questions. Neither number is your sentence and neither tells you what to buy. What decides the outcome is whether the belief that a dose helps stays alive. If you live with illness or take prescribed medicine, speak to your clinician for your own care. This book changes what you believe a dose gives you. It does not prescribe.

“But I blew five months with one. I’ve blown it for good.” No. You got a loud warning, not a life sentence. The sour mouth, the tight chest, the quick climb back — that is evidence against the one, not against you. Guard the belief now and the slip stays a lesson. Let the belief back in and repeat the dosing and the chain rebuilds. Learn, do not mourn.

“But everyone around me still lights. How can I never again?” Watch them with clear eyes. Pocket-checks. Doorway dashes. Half-heard stories. Are they enjoying something you will miss, or feeding a tug you no longer feed? You do not need to preach or avoid. You need to see.

Every one of these says the same thing in different clothes: leave a string attached. Every answer cuts the same string. There is no careful dose and no careful date. There is only the bottle rebuilt or left unrubbed, and your days run cleaner with it left alone, free of the timetable in the pocket.

### IS THIS BRAINWASHING?

“I know what this is. This is just brainwashing. You’re telling me I never liked it to trick me out of it.”

Good. Say it out loud. I said it myself when a mate first pressed this argument on me.

We were brainwashed into the trap. Bright packs. Laughing posters. Mates offering. Films teaching that paper means adult, calm, sociable, sexy. Thousands of doses teaching the tug to call itself pleasure. That is real brainwashing — installed without your permission, paid for by the seller.

This book does the opposite. It does not ask you to believe. It asks you to check. Check the patio hour with and without. Check the morning mouth with and without. Check the five-months morning with and the morning after. I bring no white coat, no chart, no threat. I bring your own evenings back to you and ask which mouth told the truth.

If I am wrong, the questions will expose me. If the dose truly calmed, truly gave, truly belonged to sun and company, your own answers will save it. But your answers have buried it, piece by piece, from desk to meal to patio.

So call it counter-brainwashing if you like the blunt word. I call it washing out a dye the seller put in. Either way the test is not my authority. It is your life, weighed in your palm like a pack that has grown light because you see what is in it, and you choose with eyes open what no con can survive — the plain sight of what a dose does.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

**SUMMARY**
- Cutting down makes each dose more precious and the diary always creeps back because the belief stays intact.
- One special dose after months without rebuilds the whole chain, proved by the wedding morning that turned to a pack by Friday.
- The lamp shows one rub rebuilds the bottle, the cliff shows falling less is still falling.
- Tomorrow admits harm while licensing today, charging more money, more sour mornings and more doorway hours for nothing.
- Trial numbers and street attempts answer different questions and neither decides your outcome or prescribes a prop.
- A slip is a loud warning to guard belief, never proof of doom and never license to keep dosing.
