Chapter 5
THE LITTLE PARTIES THAT NEVER WERE

IN THIS CHAPTER — The classroom door after the bell, the warm paragraph on the screen, the grocery bags on the step, the filter holes held to the light

*The party was your work finished, not the puff that followed it — and the mild never made it lighter, only made you puff harder.*

### THE PELLETS AFTER EVERYTHING

We all said it in the same words.

"Cigarettes were my reward for… well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store."

I know that chain from the inside. I lived pellet to pellet. The bell goes. The door shuts. The chalk still on the fingers. Out to the corridor, tap and light, as if the lesson could not be called done until the stick said so. The paragraph lands, the last sentence clicks, shoulders drop — and before the satisfaction has time to settle, the hand is already paying for it with a light. The car rolls into the drive, bags in the boot, engine off — and the errand is not closed until the puff closes it.

I am not mocking that minute. The pause after a finished thing feels warm and earned. I felt it a thousand times, and I signed the warmth over to the tube exactly as you do.

Watch what actually finished the thing.

Did the class teach itself while you stood outside? Did the paragraph write itself while the ash grew? Did the 500 miles drive themselves while you held the wheel with two fingers and worried the ash out of the window? You taught. You wrote. You drove. The hands shook a little less after because a job done settles any human chest — boy, clerk, mother, driver, long before any packet.

We reversed the order. The work gave the glow. The dose arrived a minute later and took the bow.

Take the teaching afternoon you know best. The room empties. Chairs scrape. One boy stays to say thanks and then he is gone. There is that bright tiredness teachers know — voice hoarse, board wiped, plan done. You step out. Light. First drag. You stare at the yard and tell yourself the smoke is rounding it off.

Now separate the threads, as a witness.

Thread one is finishing. Lungs let go because effort let go. Jaw unclenches because talking stopped. Eyes rest because watching thirty faces stopped. That loosening belongs to done. A non-smoker leans on the same doorframe with folded arms and feels the same bright tiredness without a flare.

Thread two is the dose laid over it. Tap out. Light. Hot scratch. Nose sting. Eyes watering a fraction. One hand busy holding and tapping. Mind leaving the bright tiredness to count draws, watch ash, think about smell before the next class, think about how many left for the evening. Tongue dulling. Mouth drying.

Which part glowed? The done. Which part interrupted the glow to run small errands? The tube.

Ask yourself with kind eyes, as I asked myself after my own finished pages.

If the stick made the paragraph good, why did the good sentence arrive before the match, never during the puffing? If the stick closed the drive, why do you remember the road and forget the puffs except for the burn in the throat? If the stick was the party, why did the party feeling fade the instant the stub went out and leave a stale mouth where the glow had been?

You know the honest answers. The party was work. The puff only rented the room the party was in.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

### TASTE IT WITHOUT

"But I like the taste. The pause. The little break."

I hear you. We all said it. Let us test it the way we test anything we claim to like — by looking at what we do with it.

Do you light the first inch for flavour and then lay it down to admire? No. You puff it down to the filter, chase the last third even when it turns hot and sorry, stub it halfway only because the end tastes of paper and ash. Is that how we treat a taste we love? We sip good soup slowly. We keep it. We do not hurry it before it burns down and then cough and then want another identical taste forty minutes later.

Watch the grocery run. Bags on the step. Door kicked shut. Kettle on. You step out with the stick and tell yourself this is the taste of a job well done. First puff, sharp and hot. Second, duller. Third, paper. By the stub the tongue is coated and the tea you made to go with the break tastes of ash. Did the tube sharpen taste or borrow it?

Taste tomorrow's finished thing without the overlay, while you still smoke as you are. Only notice. Finish the paragraph. Hands off the keys. Sit back. Let the eyes rest on what you made. Breathe through the nose. Drink water. Feel the bright tiredness settle without a flare laid over it. That settled minute is the party you thought the tube gave. It was always yours. The tube only stood in front of it and charged admission.

It never added flavour. It dulled the tongue and then took credit for the hunger returning.

### THE HOLE IN THE MASK

And the milds. The lights. The smooth ones with the pale pack and the promise in the name.

We believed them because we wanted to believe there was a softer way to stay inside. I believed them too. I held that pale stick like a sensible compromise.

Hold one to the light tomorrow. Look close at the filter. See the ring of tiny vent holes punched around the paper, neat as pinholes. On the factory machine, air rushes in through those holes and the machine reads lower numbers. Clean. Neat. Lower.

You are not a machine.

The fact is plain, and I state it as fact. Those ventilated sticks do not mean the smoker took less. Lips close over the holes. Fingers close over the holes without knowing. The chest pulls harder to find the hit it was taught to expect. Not puff-for-puff identical for every smoker — we all hold and pull our own way — but human mouths do what brass mouths never do. They cover and compensate.

Picture what that is. A mask with holes drilled to let clean air in, sold as easier breathing. Only the wearer, without meaning to, presses his lips and fingers over the holes and sucks harder through the cloth to get the same breath. Would you call that easier breathing? Would you thank the maker for the holes?

We did. We paid the same, puffed harder, and called harder lighter.

Did the mild ever leave you wanting fewer, or did the day hold the same count with deeper pulls and more checking? Did the throat feel less scratched, or only scratched in a smoother place while the evening packet thinned at the same hour? Did one mild ever close the want, or did the next want arrive on the same clock?

You have lived the answers. The want arrived on the same clock because the want was the same need. The pale pack never touched the need. It only taught the hands a new grip and the chest a bigger pull, and then took credit for gentleness.

That is not a kinder dose. It is the same dose with a mask that leaks the moment a human holds it. The industry that punched the holes counts the doses by the day-box, not by the flavour word. If you feel ill or take prescribed medicine, see your clinician for that care — this book settles what the puff gives, nothing else.

Once seen, the mild cannot be believed again in the same way. The hand still covers. The chest still pulls. But you know the pull is covering, not tasting, and the covering proves the need was never about taste at all.

### NO OTHER VOICE GETS A VOTE NOW

I know this is hard to accept at first. The little parties were the last kindness we allowed the habit. To see them go feels, for a minute, as if the day will be flat.

It will not be flat. The day was flattened by having to pay every finished thing with a light. Teaching, sentence, drive, bags on the step — each bright minute had a toll booth put across it. Remove the booth and the road does not vanish. The road runs clear.

You will hear other voices as we walk. A mate with a tip. A packet with a new name. A memory that says a certain break needs a certain stick. A headline that says cut down, switch, soften. Hear them and do not borrow them. You are inside an argument that has not finished yet, and a half-heard tip now only rebuilds what we have just pulled down.

Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

**SUMMARY**
- The glow after teaching, writing, driving and errands belonged to finishing, not to lighting.
- The taste you credited was dulled by the puff that claimed it, and the break was interrupted by holding, tapping and counting.
- The mild with vent holes did not mean less was taken, because lips and fingers cover the holes and the chest pulls harder.
- A finished thing settles any human chest on its own, and the settling is fuller without ash laid over it.
