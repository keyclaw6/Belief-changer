Chapter 12
YOUR LAST CIGARETTE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The readiness gate, the farewell table with stain and ash, the stubbed end, the two shut doors.

*Smoke this last one with your eyes open and it gives you nothing to keep — you close the door as a non-smoker and it stays shut.*

### ARE YOU CHAMPING AT THE BIT

You have cut every string we tied in this book.

You watched the desk hour prove the calm never touched the papers. You watched the little parties turn out to be work and company with paper laid over them. You watched the patio hour give everything while the stick took. You watched the lamp show one rub rebuilds the bottle and the cliff show falling less is still falling.

Now there is no careful dose left to defend and no kind tomorrow left to wait for.

So I ask you plainly, as I ask every smoker at this gate: do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit to be done with it. Not braced. Not gloomy. Eager to put the last dose out and walk on without it.

If you do not feel that eagerness, do not force it. Put this chapter down. Go back and re-read the patio and the lamp until your answers come clean again. A vow made from dread does not hold because dread still believes something is being lost.

If you do feel it — a clear, solemn lightness, a wish to finish — then stay. This is the moment of escape you prepared for from the first page.

I have stood at this table myself after twenty failed tries with drawers full of gum and patches. I know the flutter in the chest that says what if this is different. Listen to me: Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Ask yourself in your own voice:

If the last dose truly helped, why did the thousand before it leave you still reaching? If lighting truly belonged to you, why did your hand move before you decided? If stopping truly left a hole, what hole did the non-smoker you were as a child ever feel?

You know the honest answers. Nothing was given. The hand was conned. No hole waits. You leave it behind easily, immediately and permanently.

### THE FAREWELL TABLE

Do not make this last one special. Make it ordinary and make it awake.

Sit where you usually sit. No drink bought to christen it. No last supper lighting. No chain laid out to test yourself. One ordinary dose from the pack, smoked as normal to its end, with full attention on what is actually there.

We have always smoked half-asleep, eyes on talk or work while the hand did the labour. This time keep your eyes on the stick.

Look at your fingers as you hold it. See the faint yellow at the nail edge that soap never quite lifts. Smell the paper warming. Pull, and notice the first heat rasp the throat you have taught yourself not to notice. Taste the grey lay itself over the tongue. Watch the ash lengthen, pale and dead, and tap it off. Watch the smoke curl and thin and leave nothing in the hand but smell.

Halfway down, pause. Let the mouth speak. Sour at the back. Dry on the lips. Chest a fraction tighter. Is there pleasure here, or only the quieting of edginess that the previous dose planted? Is the talk brighter, or only interrupted? Does the body say thank you, or does it cough to clear an insult?

Smoke to the stale end, where we always used to stub or pinch and light another to cover the taste. That stale end is the truth of every dose you ever called heaven at lighting. The lighting flash lasted seconds. The tray taste lasts hours.

Now stub it. Grind it until no ember lives. Look at the crushed butt, the grey smear, the smell rising off the tray. This is how you shut the nicotine trap with your eyes open.

You are a smoker with lit paper in the tray a minute ago and a non-smoker with cold paper in the tray now, and you are free of the timetable in the pocket from this minute.

I do not ask you to hate the smoker who lit it. I was him. Warm to him. He was conned by bright packs and by thousands of tugs that called themselves pleasure. Harsh to the con that fed him. The stick did plenty TO mouth and throat and chest and pocket. It did nothing FOR talk or work or calm.

Leave the Nipper unfed and it dies within days.

### THE VOW

Stand up from the table and make the vow out loud, even if only in a whisper.

“I am not smoking any more. Not one puff. Not ever.”

Say it as fact, not as wish. Not as hope. As done.

You do not wait to become a non-smoker after some trial of days. You are one now because there is no belief left that a dose gives. The body will do what bodies do — breathe, taste, clear — without lessons from you. If you live with illness or take prescribed medicine, speak to your clinician for your own care. This book changes what you believe a dose gives. It does not prescribe.

When the thought floats up next week — a doorway, a laugh, a hand reaching where the pack used to be — you do not argue with it and you do not mourn it. You answer it from sight with the thought you will keep for life, said with joy in the chest because an enemy is dead and no friend is lost:

YIPPEE! I'M FREE!

Say it now once, to feel how it sits in the mouth compared with ash. Sour was the stick. Clear is you.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

### THE TWO DOORS THAT STAY SHUT

Two thoughts will knock. Both sound like you. Both are the old script arriving pre-labelled.

The first knock comes on a bad day.

Phone down after harsh words. Papers still on the desk. Shoulders high. The hand starts its old reach and the voice says, “If anything stressful happens in my life, a cigarette takes care of it.”

You have checked that lie at the desk already. The dose never moved a paper. It only paused you in a doorway while the problem sat untouched, then sent you back with drier mouth and less time. The tug asks to be fed and calls that relief, but it is only the tug talking.

Ask from your mouth before the hand finishes its reach: did the puff ever settle the argument or only interrupt it? Did shoulders drop because trouble lifted or because a fading dose was topped up? If paper truly took care of trouble, why did trouble wait on the desk through every dose you ever took?

The fact is trouble stays and the tug passes. Let the tug pass unfed. Drink water. Keep hands in the work. In three minutes the empty, edgy tug thins because no new dose replanted it. In days that edginess, which typically sharpens for two or three days and then eases within weeks, though not on the same clock for every smoker, loses its voice because belief no longer answers it.

The second knock comes wearing a smile.

Night out. Drink bright. A mate holds out a pack. “You often get offered a cigarette and in that kind of environment it's hard to turn down. You don't want to be the only one not smoking and because you've been drinking, you don't think about the risks, you think one won't hurt.”

You have checked that lie on the patio and at the wedding morning already. One after months does not prove control. It reopens the chain. I lit that wedding one and coughed and laughed and put it half out, and by night I was after the second half and by Friday I was back to a pack. I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way.

Ask from your mouth with the pack under your nose: if one is pleasure, why did that wedding pull burn and bring cough, not joy? If one is safe, why did it want a second the same night? If months clear proved nothing, what does a lit match prove but that paper still burns?

Nothing. It took. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

A slip heard as warning leaves sight intact. Grind the half out, rinse the mouth, go back inside to talk uncut by doorways. A slip fed with belief rebuilds the bottle. Do not feed it. Never allow the smile its rub.

ONE STUBBED END CLOSES IT. ONE LIT MATCH REOPENS IT.

**SUMMARY**
- The last ordinary dose smoked awake showed stain, ash and stale end with no gift inside.
- The vow made at the table marked the crossing to non-smoker as done now, not after days counted.
- The replacement thought answers any future doorway or laugh with joy that paper is no longer used.
- The bad-day reach leaves papers untouched while the tug thins unfed within minutes and eases across days.
- One offered light after months clear does not prove control, it rebuilds the chain, so no puff is allowed.
- A warning heard and heeded stays a lesson steered straight from, never mourned and never repeated.
