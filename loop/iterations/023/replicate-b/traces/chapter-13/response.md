Chapter 13
THE LAST SWEET MEAL

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- An ordinary pudding on an ordinary evening table
- A quiet kitchen after the plate is pushed away
- A late cinema box remembered without a pang
- A clear morning with no dread waiting in it

*You are not losing anything sweet tonight. You are walking out of the con for good.*

### ARE YOU READY TO MARK IT?

You feel it, do you not?

The big noise has gone. The questions about fruit and honey and dark squares and low days have been answered and have stayed answered. There is no tug left to argue with, only a readiness humming underneath.

That is exactly how it should feel before freedom.

I am not asking whether you feel brave enough. Brave has nothing to do with it. I am asking whether you see clearly that there was never anything there to miss. If you do, you are champing at the bit. If you do not quite, read again with an open mind and let the sight settle. Freedom cannot be forced. It can only be seen.

Look at yourself honestly for a moment.

We spent years paying for a lift that dropped us, paying for love in a wrapper that never loved us back, paying for taste-joy that belonged to the moment, not to the dose. We were conned, not weak. Now the con is empty.

Do you still believe BAD SUGAR does anything FOR you?
Can you name one genuine treat or lift it ever gave that was not relief from the low it made?
If it gave so much, why are you so glad to imagine life without it?

You have answered yourself. Again.

You have nothing sweet to lose and everything to gain.

That is why tonight is not sad. It is exciting, marvellous, wonderful. The door is open. You have only to step through and close it behind you.

### AN ORDINARY DOSE, WITH OPEN EYES

I want you to take your last ordinary sweet meal.

Not a laboratory test. Not a strange ritual with foods you never eat. Take what you would ordinarily take on an ordinary evening — pudding, biscuits from the packet, ice cream on the sofa, whatever your evening dose has been. Eat at your own table, at your normal hour, in your normal way.

With one difference. Pay attention.

In the past we never paid attention. We ate half in hunger and half in hurry, chasing the first bright hit while the television murmured and the hand went back by itself. Tonight you will notice everything.

Notice the first spoonful, loud and too sweet, almost chemical after real food. Notice how quickly the loudness turns cloying. Notice the chewing without hunger, the mouth coating, the throat tiring. Notice the hand wanting a second spoon before the first is finished — not from appetite, but from the loop calling. Notice how the pleasure, if you can call it that, is gone by the third bite and only the stuffing continues.

Keep going to your normal finish. Do not stop early to be good. Do not force yourself to feel sick. Just watch.

Then push the plate away and sit.

Feel the fullness that is not satisfaction. Feel the flatness spreading behind the eyes. Feel the faint restlessness already stirring underneath, the old whisper that in an hour it will want more. That whisper is not you. It is the echo of the last dose, already dying.

This is what every dose has always done TO you. It has never done anything FOR you.

If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, or carry another fragile condition, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while you read.

You do not need to remember that late cinema box you once opened while the lights went low as proof of joy. The film made the night, the company made the laugh, the rest from the week made the relief. The chocolate only sneaked a ride and then called you back the next evening.

See it now, with open eyes, for what it is: ugly, noisy, tiring stuff that leaves you tired, flat and wanting more.

Are you going to mourn that?
Or rejoice that you never have to pay for it again?

### THE VOW AND THE MOMENT YOU ARE FREE

When the plate is empty, make your vow.

Not whispered to the future. Not tried. Decided.

Say it solemnly, out loud if you can, as a free person speaks:

I will never feed myself BAD SUGAR again. Not as reward, not as fuel, not as comfort, not as exception. I am free.

Then feel what happens.

A lightness rises in the shoulders. The kitchen looks the same and utterly different. The packet on the side is no longer a temptation. It is a dead noise. You are not waiting to become free after days of strain. You leave it easily, at once and for good.

I have seen this moment thousands of times and I tell you with total certainty: it is instant. Do not wait to be free. You already are.

And in that lightness a thought shouts by itself, before you choose it, joyful and certain:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE FROM BAD SUGAR!

Let it shout. That is your new thought. Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — tomorrow morning, next week in a shop, next month at a birthday — that same shout will come. Not as effort. As relief. As identity.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Eat as much real food as you love and never count sugar again.

No counting. No policing cupboards. No white-knuckling through evenings. You eat like a hungry person, you stop like a satisfied person, and you get on with enjoying your life.

This is escape, not loss. Marvellous escape. Celebrate it tonight, not tomorrow.

### TWO OLD LINES, ANSWERED FOR GOOD

I will not leave you unguarded. Two old lines may knock in the coming days. They will sound like your own reasoning. They are not. They are the last echoes of the con, arriving pre-answered.

The first comes on a bad day.

“You've had a terrible day. One little dose will rescue you.”

Hear it with warmth and answer it flatly. When did a dose ever rescue a bad day? It added fog to tiredness, crash to pressure, shame to strain, and called you back for more an hour later. The day was hard. The dose made hard feel heavier. What rescues a bad day is rest, real food eaten slowly, kindness, sleep — life itself. Will you thank the hand that pushed you in for throwing you a rope made of water?

You know the answer. Rejoice you do not need rescuing from freedom.

The second comes on a good day.

“You're free now. Just one can't hurt.”

Ah, the polite liar. Ask it plainly: if one gives nothing, why take it? And if one gives something, why would you stop at one? There is no special dose that gives and does not hook, because the hook is the dose. One reopens the question you have just closed forever. Your body can cope with an accident, but your mind must never reopen the door on purpose.

You do not test freedom by stepping back into the cell to see if you still like it.

When either line knocks, do not argue and do not fear. Just change the feeling attached to the thought. Think:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE FROM BAD SUGAR!

And get on with your evening. The thought will pass whether you feed it or not. Feed it and you renew it. Smile at it and it starves.

13. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY SWEET MEAL AND DECIDE YOU ARE FREE
Notice the ugliness, vow solemnly, and rejoice at once.

**SUMMARY**
- Freedom starts tonight with an ordinary dose seen clearly, not with strain tomorrow.
- The last meal tastes loud, cloying and tiring when eaten with open eyes.
- The vow makes you free at once, not after days of waiting.
- A bad day is never rescued by a dose, only made heavier.
- A single deliberate taste reopens the trap because no dose gives anything real.
- A stray sweet thought becomes instant relief and joy that you no longer feed it.
