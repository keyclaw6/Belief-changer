Chapter 14
MORNINGS, SHOPS AND ORDINARY FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A bright kitchen on the first free morning
- Real breakfast eaten slowly to clean fullness
- The sweet aisle walked past to the till
- A quiet sofa evening with an empty drawer
- A rumble on the road that warns without shame

*Your ordinary life tastes brighter now because there is nothing missing from it.*

### The First Morning

You pushed the plate away last night. You spoke and you were free.

Now morning comes, the same morning that used to call you.

The kettle sings. Light comes through the kitchen window onto the same cups, the same table, the same hunger growling low in your belly. Nothing has moved. Nothing needs to move.

In the old days we answered that growl with a quick shot of sweet and called it breakfast. We told ourselves we needed a lift to function. We ate standing up, half in hurry, and by mid-morning we were empty again, hunting the drawer.

Watch what happens today.

Sit. Eat real food slowly. Bread, eggs, porridge, fruit, cheese, whatever your kitchen holds that truly feeds you. Chew. Taste. Let clean fullness spread the way it did that day at the market stall lunch eaten slowly in the noise and sun, when you noticed your body knew enough.

If you live with diabetes, use medicine that touches blood sugar, are pregnant, or feel medically fragile, let your clinician stay in charge of food and medicine while you live these days.

Do you wake heavy? Do you drag yourself to the kettle for a fix before you can speak? Can you feel that old 3pm dread already waiting at 8am?

You know the answer. You wake clear, no longer tired, flat and foggy.

That is your body returning to its natural rhythm, not your new discipline holding you up. I have seen it thousands of times. Hunger comes, food satisfies, energy steadies. No counting. No policing. No white-knuckling through the hours.

You are not coping with mornings. You are enjoying them.

### The Aisle You Walk Past

You will still go to the same shops. That is the joy of it.

Change nothing else in your life. Go at the same hour, down the same aisles, with the same basket. Freedom that needs a new route, a new shop, a new set of rules around the house would not be freedom at all. It would be fear dressed up.

So walk in confidently. Let the bright packs shout. Let the till queue coil past the little bars and bottles. You have seen who makes that noise and why. White coats in a far kitchen dialling crunch and melt to keep hands returning. It was never about your hunger. It was about their clicks.

Now you walk past the sweet aisle.

A thought may rise, light as habit: my evening drawer, my Friday box, my little reward.

Do not push it away. Do not argue with it. Smile at it and change how it feels. Let it become relief by itself:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE FROM BAD SUGAR!

And get on with enjoying your shop.

Look around you with clear eyes. Look at the trolley piled with family packs, at the tired parent paying for a fix to quiet the back seat, at the young man buying a shot of sweet to face the afternoon. Does that look like pleasure? Does that look like love in a wrapper? Who is missing something here, you or they?

We were all conned in the same way, and warmth is all any sweet-eater deserves from you. Never preach. Never lecture. Feel what you feel when you see someone still trapped in a tight cage: not envy, pity. Pity that they still believe they need the dose. Joy that you no longer believe it.

You are not being deprived. They are.

That flutter as you pass is not need. There is a circus lion that naps by its open gate the first days out, from habit alone. The door is wide, the plain is there, yet the old hollow calls. It is only cage-habit, not hunger for the cage. Leave the gate open behind you and walk on. The flutter fades when it is not fed.

Do you still believe a pack on a shelf adds anything to your basket? If those sweets gave joy, why does the thought of never paying for them again feel like lightness? If they gave energy, why are your mornings clearer without them?

You have answered yourself again.

### The Quiet Evening

Evening is where freedom proves itself sweetest.

Same sofa. Same programme. Same hour when the hand used to go by itself to the drawer. Sit there. Keep everything the same, on purpose, so you can feel the difference.

In the past the evening dose promised comfort and delivered fog. Loud first bite, cloying second, stuffing after, then flatness behind the eyes and the faint gnaw an hour later calling for more. We called that rest. It was the loop renewing itself.

Tonight there is no drawer to open. There is no gap to fill.

Eat your supper. Eat as much real food as you love. Stop when you are satisfied, the way a free appetite stops, without arithmetic. Feel true fullness, clean and quiet. Notice the programme is funny when it is funny. Notice the company is warm when it is warm. The moment makes its own pleasure now. Nothing needs to sneak a ride on it.

A sweet thought may drift across, thin as smoke: just a picture of chocolate, just a memory of crunch.

Welcome it. It cannot hurt you unless you obey it. You cannot stop thoughts arriving, and you never need to. Let this one arrive and turn it to joy at once. Think the thought you already own and feel the relief rise with it. That is not suppression. That is celebration taking the place of craving.

If a gnaw comes, low and restless under the ribs, know it at once for what it is.

It is only the Nibbler dying.

A tiny creature, starving, already beaten, making its last complaint. We fed it for years without knowing its name. Now we starve it by living. Revel in its grumble. It means the death throes are passing. In two, three, four days it is a whisper, then nothing you would cross the kitchen for.

You do not miss a dose. You miss nothing. Marvellous, wonderful relief spreads where the tug used to live, and you get on with your evening, happy to be free.

### If The Road Rumbles

I forgive you in advance for any stumble, and I tell you plainly what it means.

If, in tiredness or at a birthday or from sheer old habit, BAD SUGAR passes your lips, do not wail and do not preen. You have not blown freedom. You have hit a warning strip.

Drivers know the rumble strip at the edge of the road. It shakes the wheel to wake you, not to punish you, not to invite you to drive on it. Take the shake as it is meant. Notice the loud cloying taste, the flatness after, the old whisper to go back for more. See the con doing its work TO you again, doing nothing FOR you. Then steer gently back to the centre and drive on free.

Your body can cope with a blip, but your mind must never reopen the door.

That is the whole margin. An accident is an accident. A deliberate taste to test yourself, to prove you can take just one, to keep a special occasion alive, is not an accident. It is reopening a question you closed with joy. There is no special dose that gives and does not hook, because the hook is the dose.

So never test. Never mourn. Never keep one sweet alive to prove you are strong. Strength has nothing to do with it. Sight has everything to do with it.

You see now. Keep seeing.

Tomorrow morning the kitchen will be there again. The shops will be there again. Real food will satisfy again. The sweet thought, when it comes, will shout less and your joy will shout more, until one ordinary day you realise you passed the aisle, watched the programme, woke clear, and never once felt deprived. You felt what free people feel: ordinary life, fully tasted.

Celebrate that. Rejoice in it. Tell no one unless they ask, and then let your ease do the talking.

You escaped, easily, at once. Live as an escaped person lives.

**SUMMARY**
- Mornings are clear and steady when hunger is answered with real food.
- Shops stay the same, and walking past the sweet aisle proves nothing is missing.
- Other sweet-eaters deserve pity, never envy, because they still pay for the con.
- An evening thought or gnaw turns to relief at once and then passes.
- A blip warns like a rumble strip and never gives permission for a deliberate taste.
- Ordinary days, lived unchanged, taste brighter because the trap is gone.
