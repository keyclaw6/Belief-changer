Chapter 12
SMALL DOUBTS ANSWERED

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A health-shop shelf with a honey jar beside a fruit bowl
- A dark square melting too sweet on the tongue
- A kitchen three mornings after the last box
- Small voices around the table asking what-if

*Once the big lie is gone, the little what-ifs have nowhere left to hide.*

### YOU ARE WILFUL, SO ASK CLEANLY

You know now you are not weak. We lived the diary week together, you and I, Monday brave and Friday ashamed, and we saw plainly that strain was the cruelty, not you.

So you stand wilful, not weak-willed, and that is exactly why small questions linger.

They are not the old roar for a fix. They are careful, almost polite. What about fruit? What about honey? What about that dark bar they call healthy? What if the low is terrible? What if easy is a trick?

I welcome them. A free mind asks cleanly. Let us answer cleanly, one by one, and be done.

I speak to you as one who has walked out and stayed out, and I promise you this: there is no small door here that leads back to the cell. Look through each one with me and you will see daylight on the other side.

### THE JAR AND THE BOWL

Picture yesterday afternoon. You are in a health shop because you want to do right by your body.

On the left, a handsome jar of honey, golden in the light, with a wooden dipper and a label full of meadows. On the right, a plain bowl of apples and pears, a little dull-skinned, nothing clever about them.

A voice whispers: are they not the same? Sweet is sweet?

No. Your tongue knows the difference, and your life knows it too.

Eat two apples slowly. Notice what happens. Chewing, juice, clean fullness spreading. You stop because you are satisfied. You do not hide the cores. You do not plan the next apple while eating this one. You do not wake at midnight hunting apple.

Now take the honeyed dose, the spoon in tea, the flapjack, the yogurt with swirls. Notice what happens. A quick bright hit, then the hand looking for more. The jar calling from the shelf. The evening hunt beginning.

Which one leaves you reaching for a third and a fourth?
Which one can you leave half-eaten without a backward glance?

You have answered yourself.

BAD SUGAR is refined, freed from its food, engineered for craving. Fruit is whole food with water, fibre and its own stop signal, part of the real eating you love. To call them the same because both taste sweet is like calling rain and a burst pipe the same because both are wet.

And your body? Does it need an import of powder to keep going? It does not. Your body manufactures its own glucose from protein and food you eat, hour by hour, quietly, without your help. Leaving out BAD SUGAR is not leaving out nourishment. If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, or carry another fragile condition, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while you read.

Fruit feeds you. Candy fools you.

### SMALL WHAT-IFS, STRAIGHT ANSWERS

Let me put your questions on the table in your own words, and answer as I would to a friend across that table.

“But doesn’t my body need sugar?”

We were all taught that. We picture fuel going into an engine. But watch your own mornings. That growling belly before lunch, then real food eaten slowly to clean satisfaction — does that feel like a machine missing powder? It feels like a body that knows enough. Your system makes what it needs from ordinary eating. What it never needed was the bright hit that lifts then drops you and calls the drop hunger.

“Isn’t fruit just candy from a tree?”

Taste them side by side and let your behaviour judge. When did an orange ever make you eat the box, hide the skins, and feel foggy and cross at four o’clock? Fruit satisfies and lets you go. BAD SUGAR teases satisfaction and brings you back. Same word sweet, opposite job.

“But dark chocolate — surely that little square is healthy?”

Ah, the respectable dose. Bitter, small, wrapped in talk of minerals and pure pleasure. Put it on your tongue with full attention. Is it food? Or is it the same con in a clever coat? Notice the after-effect, not the story. One square re-lights the evening hunt for most of us. A lump of cocoa with sugar and fat, counted in clicks by white coats dialling melt and crunch to keep the hand returning, does not become nourishment because the packet speaks softly. If there were nourishment there, why would one always call for another?

“Won’t the low be unbearable?”

This is the frightened one, so hear me tenderly and clearly. In animals given the on-off pattern, each binge shows a brief chemical dip afterwards, a low echo with its opposing signal rising. In us that echo arrives as restlessness, an empty tug, a call to repeat. A pro-addiction laboratory, one that believes in the grip, describes the whole dependency as mild but well-defined. Mild. Well-defined. Not mighty. Not endless.

And the afternoon slump? The lift you felt at three was insulin doing its work, moving the rush along, and that same surge can leave a sag behind. That explains why the top-up always seems to help for minutes and then leaves you lower. Your everyday sag is not a clinical collapse. True clinical low is rare in healthy people and is a medical matter, not an afternoon feeling. Do not let ordinary tiredness be renamed as disease.

How long does the tug last? Days of small complaints, not weeks of torment, and each wave passes whether you feed it or not. Feed it and you renew it. Smile at it and get on with your morning, and it starves.

“Isn’t this method too good to be true?”

I love this doubt because it proves you are thinking. You have been sold so many hard cures that easy sounds like a con.

But ask: which story asks you to believe more? That freedom comes from seeing there was never anything to miss, and so you walk out rejoicing? Or that freedom comes from the Willpower Method, forcing yourself daily not to have a wonderful pleasure, counting hours and policing cupboards for life? The first is simple truth. The second is the trick that kept us bleeding.

Easy is not the warning sign. Hard was the warning sign.

### THREE MORNINGS IN MY KITCHEN

Let me tell you plainly what happened to me, because I was you.

For twenty-two years I ran the sweet day. Three sugars in morning tea. A vending bar at eleven to be kind to myself. Two more at the desk at three to get me through. A pudding after supper because the day deserved one, then biscuits straight from the packet while the television murmured, crumbs on my shirt, promising tomorrow would be good.

The morning I saw it, I had eaten an ordinary dessert the night before with full attention — not a test, not a punishment, just noticing. Cloying. Too loud. Tiring to finish. I slept badly, woke with a coated mouth and that familiar flat head, and thought: this did nothing for me last night. Not comfort. Not joy. Only noise and fog.

I ate breakfast like a hungry person. Eggs, bread, an apple eaten over the sink with juice running. Clean fullness. At eleven the old hour struck and a faint restlessness passed through — empty, nagging, familiar. I smiled at it and kept peeling carrots for lunch. By midday it was gone.

Second morning. The shop on the corner. Bright packs shouting by the till. My hand did not move. Not because I was being good. Because I felt nothing to buy. Relief rose so fast it surprised me — marvellous, light-shouldered relief, like putting down a heavy bag I had forgotten I carried.

Third morning. Sunday. My wife cut peaches into a bowl. We ate them slowly at the table while light moved across the wood. Sweetness, real sweetness, exactly where it belongs. I thought: I am not missing anything. I am tasting everything.

That was years ago. No counting. No secret drawer. No Friday collapse. Mornings clearer, shops calmer, food kinder. If a stray thought of BAD SUGAR crosses, it arrives as pity for a dead noise, not as desire.

I tell you this not to boast. I tell you because I restarted my honesty too. Once, months in, tired and rushed, I took a birthday sliver to be polite. It tasted loud and left me flat and a little restless an hour later. I did not scold myself. I saw the warning for what it was — the old echo, not a new need — and rejoiced that I was still free. One blip taught me, it did not own me.

You can have this. Not a harder struggle. A clearer sight.

### BORROW NO ONE ELSE’S HUNGER

Now the last trap for a careful mind: other people.

At work they will say, once a week is fine. At home they will say, I don’t keep it in the house, which only proves how loudly it calls when it is in the house. A friend will wave a bar and say, just a treat, I’ve managed to be good this week. The television will show white teeth and caramel pours and call it love.

Do not borrow doubt from mouths that still feed the loop.

Can the swimmer still in the river tell you about the bank? Can the hand still reaching tell you reaching is joy? They speak from inside the hunger they think is normal. You have stepped outside it. Their appetite cannot report your freedom.

When you hear the old phrases — the roller coaster, sugary somethings to get me through, I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, I want to get off the roller coaster — hear them with warmth, not envy. You know that voice. We all spoke it. It is not wisdom. It is the echo asking to be fed.

You do not need their permission to be free. You do not need their doubt to be careful. You have your own body, your own bowl of real food, your own three mornings waiting.

Are you going to trust a craving mouth about nourishment?
Or your own clear mornings about what satisfies?

You know.

Rejoice. The small doubts are answered. Nothing big remains to fight, because there was never anything good to lose.

12. AVOID BORROWING DOUBT FROM SWEET-EATERS
Other appetites cannot report your freedom.

**SUMMARY**
- Your body makes its own glucose from real food; it needs no import of BAD SUGAR.
- Fruit satisfies and lets you stop; candy teases and calls you back.
- Dark squares and honeyed health wraps still light the same evening hunt.
- The low after a dose is a small brief echo, not an unbearable torment, and everyday slumps are not clinical collapse.
- Easy is honest; the hard struggle belonged to the wrong method.
- Other mouths still in the hunger cannot judge your freedom.
