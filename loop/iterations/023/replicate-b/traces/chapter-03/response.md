Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD AT ALL

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- An office table, a plastic plate, and birthday cake eaten bite by bite
- Borrowed party shoes a size too small, worn till midnight
- The quiet work your body does after real food

*I am not asking whether the harm outweighs the pleasure. I am asking what pleasure was ever there at all.*

### THE BARGAIN YOU THINK YOU ARE MAKING

You carry a memory from yesterday morning into today, and I want you to keep it close.

That growling belly. That simple lunch eaten slow. That clean fullness that arrived by itself and let you get on with your day.

We both know what that proved. Your body knows how to ask, how to enjoy, how to stop.

And yet the old arithmetic starts up again, does it not? I hear it because we have all said it:

“All right, it does me no good. I see that. But surely it does something *for* me? Surely a little sweetness is only fair?”

I love you for asking it so honestly. There is no shame in the question. It is the last sensible question of a conned investigator, and it deserves a straight answer.

You are trying to balance a set of scales. On one side, the heaviness, the fog, the teeth, the money, the empty boxes. On the other side, the pleasure. You tell yourself, if I am careful, if I am moderate, the pleasure might still justify a small place.

Let me change the question entirely, because scales are the wrong instrument here.

I am not asking you to weigh harm against delight. I am asking you to look hard at the delight side and tell me what you actually find there.

What good at all does BAD SUGAR do for you?

### THE CAKE ON THE OFFICE TABLE

Come with me to the office yesterday. It will do more good than a hundred arguments.

Someone’s birthday. A sponge with bright icing on a plastic plate. Candles, singing, paper napkins. You take a slice because everyone takes a slice. You tell yourself, this is the nice part of the day, this is what you call a genuine treat or lift.

Eat it with me bite by bite, with attention, as an investigator would.

First bite. Cold icing, very sweet, noise all round you. Do you taste cake, or do you taste occasion? The laughter, the break from the screen, the kindness of being included — is it the sugar that is warm, or the moment?

Second bite. Already sweeter than sweet. The edge has gone. You drink water. You shift in your chair.

Third bite. The mouth is coated. The pleasure is not rising, it is fading. You are finishing because it is on the plate, because to leave it would look odd, because the Sweet voice in old books would have called it wasteful.

Fourth bite. Relief that it is gone. You wipe your mouth. You feel heavier, a little dulled, already thinking about coffee to clear the cloy.

Now answer me honestly, as you alone know the truth of your own mouth.

Did the joy grow with each bite, or did it shrink?
If that slice lifted you, why did you feel lower and foggier after it than before?
And if it was truly doing something for you, why was the best moment the moment it was finished?

You know the answer. We all know it when we stop performing enjoyment and start watching it.

The company lifted you. The pause lifted you. The cake sneaked a ride on both and then claimed the credit.

### THE SHOES AT MIDNIGHT

Let me lend you a picture you will never forget, because it settles this whole bargain in one evening.

You are invited to a wedding. At the last minute your shoes break, and a friend lends you a dazzling pair for dancing. They are beautiful. They are also a size too small.

You squeeze in. You smile for photographs. You dance. You stand through speeches. All evening they pinch and throb. You think about them constantly. You count the hours.

At midnight you finally kick them off under the table and walk barefoot across the cool floor. Ah. Wonderful. What relief. What pleasure.

Now tell me. Did those shoes give you pleasure?

Of course not. They gave you pain, then lent you the relief of stopping the pain. To call them pleasurable because taking them off felt marvellous would be upside down. The marvellous feeling was not from the shoes. It was from ending what the shoes did to you.

That is exactly what a dose of BAD SUGAR does.

We pinch ourselves with the last dose, we throb for a while, we take the next dose and feel the pinch ease for minutes, and we call the easing a lift. It is not a lift above normal. It is a brief return toward the comfort you had before you ever learned to pinch yourself.

Look again at the office cake. The lift you remember so fondly is the midnight kick-off. The flatness you feel an hour later is the shoes being laced on again for the next wearing.

Once you see it, you cannot unsee it. The benefit side of your scales is empty. There is nothing to balance.

The fact is, you never enjoyed the chemical. You enjoyed the pause, the people, the break in the day — and you blamed the sugar for the joy that belonged to life.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

### WHAT YOUR BODY ALREADY MAKES

“But I need something,” says the old voice. “Surely the body needs sugar?”

I want to set that fear to rest with a simple, settled truth about your own frame, and I say it gently so you do not take it as a sentence on yourself.

Your body already makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food. Plain meals, vegetables, fruit, milk in food, the good sweetness of real living — your system turns what you eat into steady fuel by itself, without any need for refined hits. That market lunch proved it. You ate, you were satisfied, you forgot about food till hunger honestly returned.

This means leaving BAD SUGAR is not leaving sweetness, and it is not leaving energy. The good sweetness stays on your table. Your appetite stays with you. What goes is only the pinching pair of shoes.

Ask yourself the last question of this chapter, and let it close the bargain for good.

If BAD SUGAR truly fed you, why are you hungry again so soon and so restlessly after a fix?
If it truly sweetened life, why does life feel sweeter in the clean hours after real food than in the sticky minutes after a binge?
And would you willingly agree to keep paying, in money and fog and toothache and low evenings, for the rest of your life for something that never once lifted you above where you started?

You would not. No free person would. The only reason we ever paid was because we thought we were buying a genuine treat or lift. We were buying the removal of shoes we should never have put on.

There is wonderful relief in this. You do not have to weigh and measure and be moderate about an empty bargain. You have only to see that one side holds nothing, and the whole anxious arithmetic falls away.

Hold your market memory again. That calm fullness was you without the pinch. That is not a special state to earn. That is your natural state waiting to be heard again.

Start this part happy. There is no doom in seeing through a con. There is only the excitement of an escape beginning.

3. BEGIN WITH ELATION
There is no doom here, only an escape to enjoy.

**SUMMARY**
- The scales are the wrong test. Harm against pleasure assumes a pleasure exists.
- Office cake watched bite by bite shows joy fading as eating continues.
- Pleasure remembered belongs to company and pause, not to the dose.
- Relief after a fix mirrors kicking off pinching shoes worn all day.
- The body makes steady fuel from ordinary food, so no real need goes unmet.
- With no benefit on the table, moderation stops looking sensible.
