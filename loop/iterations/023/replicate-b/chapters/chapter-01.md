Chapter 1
WHY THIS IS NOT ANOTHER DIET BOOK

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A kitchen drawer stuffed with diet rules, shake sachets and broken promises
- A late-night kitchen, an empty wrapper, and an honest look at who is in charge
- A boxed definition left on the table for the whole book

*You are not about to suffer to get free from BAD SUGAR; you are about to see it clearly and walk out happy.*

### YOU HAVE EVERY RIGHT TO BE FED UP

I do not blame you for picking up this book with a sinking feeling.

We have all been there. We start the year full of hope. We buy the books, we tear out the meal plans, we stick the chart on the fridge. For a few days we are saints. Then comes the bad afternoon, the birthday in the office, the long evening, and we find ourselves back at the same drawer, the same packet, the same secret feeling of embarrassment.

If you feel tired and a little embarrassed that you are reading yet another book about sweets, good. That tiredness is honest. It proves you are not lazy and not stupid. It proves you have tried.

Let me ask you something simple. If you loved BAD SUGAR with a free and happy choice, why would you be holding a book with this title at all?

Would you need a book to help you stop eating fresh peaches? Would you need a method to stop eating soup you enjoy? Of course not. The very fact you are here tells a truth you already sense: something has taken hold of you that you did not quite choose.

That does not make you weak. It makes you conned.

We were all conned in exactly the same way. We started as children when sweets meant love, parties, prizes. We went on as adults when a sweet meant a break, a reward, a little comfort after a hard day. Nobody warned us that each dose was laying the wire for the next craving. We thought we were treating ourselves. We were feeding a trap.

I know this is hard to accept at first, but stay with me. I am not going to lecture you. I am not going to weigh you, count you, scold you or hand you another set of rules to white-knuckle. I have been where you are, and I got free, easily, at once and for good, and I have helped a great many others do the same.

### HOW I KNOW EASY IS TRUE

I was not a moderate sweet-eater who dabbled. I was the person who said one square and woke among crumbs. Morning rolls, afternoon biscuits, evening ice cream eaten standing by the freezer so nobody would see the tub. I knew every diet drawer trick. I did the cutting down. I did the no-sugar Mondays. I did the secret promises.

Every failure left the same sting. I thought the failure was me.

It was not me. It was the method I was using. I call it the Willpower Method, and you know it by heart: pick a Monday, grit your teeth, fight your own appetite, mourn the pleasure you think you are missing, and hope you hold out. With the Willpower Method there is no finish line. Each day feels like a test you might fail. No wonder we failed. No wonder you failed.

When I finally saw the trap for what it was, I did not need force. Understanding did what force never could. I ate my normal food, I finished the book in my head, I took my last sweet meal with my eyes open, and I walked out. No fight. No misery. Just relief.

That is why I can promise you ease without blushing. This is not ease as a sales trick. It is ease because there is nothing real to fight once the false belief goes.

Do you want to be free? Of course you do — that is why you are reading this book. And you can be free and happy to be free, not glumly holding out.

Consider what I am asking of you. I am not asking you to stop eating. I am not asking you to go hungry, to count, to measure, to carry a food diary like a guilty conscience. Quite the opposite. Eat as much real food as you love and never count sugar again. Read that again and let it settle: real food, eaten with joy, until you are satisfied. That is part of freedom's menu, not a reward for being good.

And consider what you risk by reading on with an open mind. Nothing. You risk no hunger, no pain, no embarrassment. You stand to gain your appetite, your energy, your evenings, your self-respect. You have nothing sweet to lose and everything to gain.

### WHAT I MEAN WHEN I SAY SUGAR

We need clear terms or we will talk past each other, so let me leave this definition on the table and keep it there for the whole book.

> WHEN I SAY SUGAR IN THIS BOOK, I MEAN BAD SUGAR = refined sugar, added free sugars, sugary drinks, confectionery, pastries, desserts, and junk and heavily processed starchy carbs engineered for craving. GOOD sweetness — whole fruit, vegetables, plain whole foods, milk in food, and glucose your body itself makes — is not the target and belongs in your free life.

Read it once more. BAD SUGAR is the manufactured dose. The bar, the can, the frosted bun, the bag that whispers from the cupboard. GOOD sweetness is the peach, the carrot, the potato at dinner, the milk in your tea. Your body already knows the difference, and soon your mind will too.

Why does this matter now? Because nobody needs to fear food to get free. You will not live on air. You will not live on rules. You will eat — warm bread, soup, meat, fish, rice, vegetables, fruit — and you will learn to trust hunger again. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects glucose, are pregnant, have a history of eating trouble, or feel medically fragile, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine decisions while you read.

Somewhere behind the bright packets there are bright labs where crunch and melt and sweetness are tuned to keep your hand going back. I mention that only in passing now. We will look squarely at that manufacture later. For now it is enough to know you were never facing a simple lack of character. You were facing a product made to create repeat craving, plus a method that told you the answer was more effort.

That combination explains everything. The product creates the craving. The Willpower Method tells you to fight the craving by force while still believing the product gives you something precious. It is a cruel circle. We blame ourselves for not fighting hard enough, and each bout of self-blame sends us back for another hit of comfort.

Shall we step out of that circle together?

### OUR CONTRACT

I will not ask much, but what I ask matters.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. That is the whole contract. Not most of them when you feel like it. All of them, because they work together like a combination. Miss one number and the lock stays shut. Follow them and the door swings wide.

Here is how we begin.

You do not have to stop or cut down now. Carry on exactly as normal while you read. I mean that. Eat your usual sweets, live your usual days. This removes the dread that closes the mind. Belief must change first; behaviour will follow on its own. If you try to force the behaviour now you will only stir up noise and miss the argument that sets you free.

You must read with curiosity, not with dread. Cast aside the gloom you were taught to expect from quitting anything. There is no doom here. There is an escape to enjoy. Bring elation to the start. Think how marvellous it will be to leave the grazing and bingeing behind and get on with enjoying your life.

And you must let the argument finish. Do not dip in, skip to the end, or patch together a version from half-remembered tips. A half-seen truth will not hold you when the lights go low and someone passes the box.

Are you willing? I believe you are. You have already proved your will is strong by persisting through so many failed diets. That strength was never the problem. It was spent in the wrong method. Now we will spend your intelligence instead.

So let us seal the first step, cheerfully and firmly:

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Question what you think you know about sweets and let the argument finish.

**SUMMARY**
- This book is not a diet and not a lecture about food rules.
- Past failures came from a cruel method that asked for force while leaving the false belief intact.
- BAD SUGAR means manufactured sweet doses, not real food and not natural sweetness.
- Freedom comes from seeing clearly first, while eating and living normally.
- Reading on with curiosity costs nothing and opens the door to escape.
