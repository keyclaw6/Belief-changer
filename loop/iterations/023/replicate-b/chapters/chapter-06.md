Chapter 6
HUNGER YOU CAN TRUST

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- The quiet home table at midday, plain real lunch and an empty kitchen
- A growling belly watched without panic to clean fullness
- The remembered market stall lunch, warm beside this ordinary meal

*Hunger is not a warning that you need sweets. It is your body calling you to real food it loves.*

### THE FLAT-FOOD FEAR

Keep that evening with you. You have seen the pudding borrow its glow from your victory. You have seen comfort leave you flatter, not kinder.

And still a colder doubt sits underneath.

"All right, but what will eating be without sweets? Won't food go flat? Won't I be left hungry and counting the hours?"

I know that fear because we have all felt it. We imagined a life of plain chewing, joyless plates, a belly that never feels settled unless a sweet comes after. We imagined hunger itself as an enemy that only BAD SUGAR can quiet.

It is an understandable fear. It is also back to front.

Think of your own days. When does hunger feel frantic and unbearable? It is not in the morning before real food, when the belly growls cleanly. It is in the afternoon after sweets, when the voice screams:

"I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling"

That panic is not hunger. That is the restlessness after a sweet dose, dressed up as hunger to get another dose.

True hunger does not scream. It rumbles. It waits. It is patient and honest. We confused the two because we fed both with the same wrapper.

If sweets truly satisfied hunger, why does the frantic feeling return fastest after sweets?
If real food leaves you flat, why did that market stall lunch leave you full for hours and able to forget food entirely?
And when your belly growls before lunch, does it name a brand and a wrapper, or does it simply ask to be fed?

Answer from your own body, not from my words. You already know the difference.

If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, or eat under a clinician's care, keep them in charge of food and medicine while you read.

### LUNCH AT THE HOME TABLE

Let me ask you to do one ordinary thing, not as a rule and not as a diet, but as a watch.

Tomorrow, or today if you can, sit at your home table at midday with real food you like. Not a plan from me. Your food, from your kitchen — warm soup, bread and cheese, eggs, meat or fish, potatoes, vegetables, fruit, what you already love when no one is watching. Eat slowly. No screen in your face. No counting, no weighing, no clock-racing.

First notice the growl before you begin. Do not fear it. It is not an emergency. It is appetite waking up, the clean signal that eating will be good.

Take a bite. Chew. Put the fork down. Taste it.

You will notice something sweets never let you notice. The food has smell and texture. Warmth spreads. The growl softens bite by bite, not in a flare and a crash, but in a steady settling. Halfway through you breathe easier. Near the end you feel a quiet instruction from inside: enough. Not stuffed. Not drugged. Satisfied.

Stay with that minute. That clean fullness that lets you stand up, clear the plate, and forget food for hours — that is what satisfaction feels like when no dose is shouting over it.

I have sat at that same table. Bread still crisp, soup still steaming, belly moving from empty to easy. No fireworks in the first bite and no emptiness after. Just the pleasure of hunger met and gone.

Compare it honestly with a sweet lunch. A bar at the desk, eaten fast with your eyes elsewhere. Sweet, rush, gone. Ten minutes later the mouth is empty and the belly is confused — full of noise but not fed. An hour later the same frantic voice is back, asking again. That is not deeper pleasure. That is shallower feeding.

You do not need me to tell you which meal loved you back. Your body told you at the market stall, when a simple lunch eaten slowly carried you through the afternoon without a drawer, without bargaining, without fog. This home table is the same lesson in your own kitchen.

Hunger met with real food ends. Hunger met with BAD SUGAR repeats.

### YOUR BODY ALREADY KNOWS ENOUGH

Here is the part the sweet voice never told you.

Your body does not need imported BAD SUGAR to keep your blood and brain going. It makes the glucose it needs itself from the real food you eat. Eat meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, bread, potatoes, dairy in your ordinary meals, and your marvellous body does the converting quietly, hour by hour, without your help.

That is why the market lunch held you. That is why the home lunch holds you. It was never willpower that carried you to evening. It was sufficiency.

We were conned into believing sweetness from a packet was fuel, and food without it was deprivation. It is the other way around. The packet gives a flare and leaves need. Real food gives substance and leaves peace.

So trust the growl. Let it come. Welcome it as proof your compass works. Feed it with food you enjoy until it goes quiet, then get on with your day. You will find appetite is not a bottomless pit to be feared. It rises and falls like thirst — clear, rhythmic, easy to answer.

Does a healthy child need a lesson to know when he is hungry and when he is done?
Did you need a rule to feel the clean fullness at the market stall?
And has a sweet ever, even once, left you settled for hours the way that simple lunch did?

You know it has not. We gave credit to the wrapper for work your body was doing all along.

Eat as much real food as you love and never count sugar again.

That is not a diet. It is permission to step out of counting and back into eating. No points, no forbidden plates, no hungry evenings proving how strong you are. Just hunger, food, satisfaction, repeat — the rhythm you were born with before the con taught you to interrupt it with a fix.

You will not miss the flat chase of sweet on sweet. You will find eating becomes a favourite again, not because I promise it, but because you will feel it at your own table.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND EAT REAL FOOD WITH JOY
Your body already knows enough and satisfaction.

**SUMMARY**
- Hunger is a clean signal to eat, not a panic that needs sweets.
- The frantic RIGHT-NOW feeling follows sweet doses, true hunger rumbles and waits.
- Real lunch eaten slowly settles the belly for hours and lets you forget food.
- Sweet eating flares fast and returns fast, it never leaves lasting satisfaction.
- Your body makes what it needs from real food, no sweet import keeps you going.
- Eating becomes favourite again when appetite is trusted, not interrupted.
