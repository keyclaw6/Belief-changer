Chapter 8
MADE TO CRAVE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- Supermarket aisle, bright walls at eye-height and till
- White kitchen, dials and click-counters
- Dentist chair, bright light and cold mirror

*Those shelves were built to make you crave, and the load lands in your body while you are told it is harmless.*

### THE WALL THAT WAS NEVER AN ACCIDENT

You have seen it now. The low follows the dose. The dose never fixes the low.

Now come with me on an ordinary shop, because we have all done it a thousand times without really seeing it.

You push the trolley round the corner and there it is. A wall. Bright packs at eye-height, at hand-height, at the till, at the door. Chocolate against biscuits against frosted rolls against fizzy bottles, shouting in reds and golds. At home you meant to be sensible. In the aisle your hand moves on its own.

Listen to what we tell ourselves in that aisle:

"They are only giving people what they want. The shelves are full because we love sweet things."

I stood in that same aisle and believed that too. I thought abundance was accident. I thought warnings were for other people, far away.

That is the sugar trap.

Walk it slowly. If this is neutral choice, why is the sweetest stuff placed where a tired hand cannot miss it? If this is accident, why does every shop, every station, every till build the same bright wall? If this is harmless pleasure, why do you leave with more than you meant to buy and eat it faster than you meant to eat it?

You know the honest answers. You were not weak in that aisle. You were aimed at.

### THE WHITE KITCHEN

Behind that wall is a quieter room. I want you to see it, because anger belongs there, never on yourself.

Picture a clean white kitchen. Men and women in white coats, kind faces, serious notebooks. On the bench a biscuit, a bar, a drink. They turn one dial a fraction — a little more crunch. They turn another — a little more melt, a little more sweet-salt, a little more chew that calls for another bite. Then they watch. They count how many hands go back for a second. They count the clicks. They adjust until the hand returns on its own.

No single cook wishes you ill. The system does one job: make the dose impossible to eat once and easy to eat twice. Fat married to sugar, salt kissing sweet, melt that vanishes so the mouth thinks nothing arrived. That marriage seems to have a louder call than either partner alone, and the mouth learns it fast.

The fact is, craving was manufactured before you felt it.

That tired voice that says this is normal food is the Sweet-Talker.

Ask him plainly, from your own trolley:

If the recipe were innocent, why lab-test it for return-buying?
If your stopping were free, why engineer the bite to bypass fullness?
Did you choose the wall, or did the wall choose your hand long before you arrived?

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

### WHAT THE LOAD DOES

Now look at what rides home in the bag, because the Sweet-Talker calls it distant and exaggerated. It is not distant. It lives in your mouth and belly and afternoons.

Sit with me for a moment in another chair you know. The dentist chair. Bright light in your eyes. Cold mirror tapping. That sour smell. The little catch when the probe finds softness where hardness should be. Sweet liquid feeds the film on teeth, the film turns it to acid, the acid eats the surface. Brushing and fluoride stand in the way, yes — but the stake is real and it is in your own mouth. Not a lecture. A memory we all carry.

Lift it wider. Across whole populations, those who drink and eat high added sugar carry more heart and stroke load — liver overworked, pressure raised, low-grade fire in the vessels, the fullness signal jumped over meal after meal. Health bodies set a plain ceiling for free sugars — below a tenth of daily energy, about fifty grams, with extra benefit below half that — because everyday eating now sails far past both. That is population guidance, not poison in a crumb. I am not telling you this will happen to you. If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, are pregnant, or eat under medical orders, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while we talk.

And feel how it lands day to day. Not dramatic collapse. A duller tax. You wake unrefreshed. Mid-morning you need a shot of sweet to start. Afternoon you sink. Evening you are snappy and flat, fog in the head, heaviness in the limbs. Month after month, year after year. Do you want to pay that for the rest of your life, or be clear and free for the rest of your life?

That is what I mean by tired, flat and foggy. Not one bad day. The permanent weather inside a sweet-fed body. Tired in the morning, flat in the afternoon, foggy in the evening — and calling it your nature.

You are not uniquely weak to feel it. When questionnaires count addiction-like eating of highly tasty food, around fourteen in a hundred adults and twelve in a hundred children score that way — close to drink and tobacco figures. That is not a medical label stamped on you. It is proof crowds of decent, ordinary people get caught the same way.

Some honest scientists still argue about the word. Leading brain researchers say the animal bingeing shows the pattern of on-off access more than sugar chemistry alone, and that human proof is thin. The label is an open question, not a settled verdict. Note what both sides grant without quarrel: under that on-off pattern, binge behaviour happens. You have lived that pattern. Diets, rules, good weeks, blown evenings. The trap form is real even while professors debate the name.

So no, the warnings are not exaggerated into distance. They are close, bodily, daily.

And yet — hear me — I do not want you to move from fear.

Fear would make stopping feel like obedience to a scare. I want something cleaner. See the design, feel the load, then choose gain because gain is true. You lose no pleasure by walking out. You reclaim mornings, teeth, lightness, money, hours, self-respect. Freedom is immediate gain and escape, not a future milestone.

8. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT ASKS YOU TO QUIT FROM FEAR
Hear the facts, then choose freedom for gain.

**SUMMARY**
- The bright wall is placed and recipe-tested for return-buying, not neutral plenty.
- Fat married to sugar calls louder than either alone and teaches the hand to return.
- Sweet liquid feeds mouth film to acid that softens teeth, brushing only mediates.
- High added sugar rides with higher heart and liver load across populations.
- Everyday use taxes the body into a dull daily weather, not a distant threat.
- On-off access drives binge behaviour even while scientists debate the label.
- Facts inform, gain decides — fear is not the reason to walk free.
