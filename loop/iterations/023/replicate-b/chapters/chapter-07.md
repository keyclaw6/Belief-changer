Chapter 7
HOW THE LOW IS MADE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- Evening kitchen, biscuit tin and one innocent biscuit
- Ninety minutes later, sofa, low light and the returning gnaw
- Borrowed party shoes worn too tight till midnight

*The emptiness an hour after sweets is not need. It is the last dose finishing its work.*

### THE BISCUIT AT NINE

Keep your home table beside you. You have felt true hunger rumble, true food settle it, true fullness let you forget food for hours.

Now watch the opposite evening with me, because we have all lived it.

Nine o'clock. Kitchen quiet. You are not hungry. You tell yourself you will have just one biscuit from the tin to round the day off. One innocent dose of BAD SUGAR. It is sweet, it melts, it is gone in four bites.

Ninety minutes later you are on the sofa and something has changed. A faint gnaw under the ribs. A restlessness in the mouth. A thought that will not sit still.

You hear it in your own voice:

"Why am I thinking about the tin again? I only had one. What is wrong with me?"

I have stood at that same tin. I know that sour confusion, staring at crumbs as if they proved something broken inside me. Nothing is broken. Something is working exactly as the con built it to work.

That back-to-front hunger is the sugar trap in action.

You did not eat the biscuit because the gnaw came. The gnaw came because you ate the biscuit.

### THE DIP AFTER THE LIFT

Here is what the dose does after it leaves.

A shot of sweet lifts you briefly toward normal, then lets you sink a little lower than before you took it. As measured in animals, each binge is followed by a dip in the reward chemistry below its starting line, with the opposing chemistry rising to meet it. That dip is the inversion made visible. Relief, crash, call for another fix.

Even the laboratories most friendly to the addiction language call this physical side mild but well-defined — a faint complaint, not a mighty seizure. I want you to hold both truths. It is real enough to feel. It is small enough to starve without drama.

This does not mean you carry a medical withdrawal illness. It means you feed a tiny creature and mistake its hunger for yours. If you take medicine for blood sugar or eat under a doctor's orders, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while we do this.

That gnaw is only the Nibbler losing its feed, nothing more.

The panic around it is the Sweet-Talker turning a whimper into an order.

See how the two work together. The Nibbler makes a little emptiness. The Sweet-Talker sells you the meaning: you need energy, you need comfort, you need just one more to get through. And because the wrapper, the smell, the crinkle, the nine o'clock sofa have ridden along so many times, the mouth can start watering before a bite is even taken. It is an echo of cue circuits seen in animals, not proof you are helpless for life.

That is why one becomes the box. Not weakness. Trigger and dip. What you feel is an empty, gnawing, slightly restless craving, not true hunger.

When that restlessness is tracked, it shows as a small hump of a few days, then it fades — not a life sentence, only a dying complaint. The lift you praised was insulin doing its job and overshooting. The low you feared was the bill for the lift.

The fact is, the low follows the dose; the dose never fixes the low.

### THE SHOES THAT LIED

It is like dancing all evening in borrowed party shoes two sizes too tight and praising the shoes for the relief when you kick them off at midnight. No one would call that pleasure from the shoes. The shoes made the pain they then briefly ended.

BAD SUGAR does the same. It pinches, then sells you the kicking-off.

Listen to the Sweet-Talker for a moment, because he sounds so reasonable:

"Craving must mean need. Why else would one bite turn into a box?"

Answer him from your own evenings, not from my words.

If that biscuit truly filled you, why are you emptier ninety minutes later than before you ate it?
If craving proved need, why does a real lunch silence food-thought for hours while a sweet wakes it within the hour?
When your belly growled cleanly before lunch, did it name a wrapper, or did the wrapper teach the growl to scream afterwards?

You know the honest answers. We gave credit to the dose for work your body was doing, and blame to yourself for work the dose was doing.

It never lifted you above normal. It dragged you below normal and lent you a moment back toward it. It creates what it pretends to cure.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

**SUMMARY**
- One biscuit does not prove need, it starts the gnaw ninety minutes later.
- The lift is brief, the dip below baseline follows it and calls for another dose.
- The physical part is faint and days-long, not a mighty or lifelong seizure.
- Wrapper, smell and sofa-time can fire the mouth before a bite, echo not order.
- Tight-shoe relief is not pleasure, ending a pinch the dose made is not a gift.
- Hunger met with real food ends, hunger met with BAD SUGAR repeats.
