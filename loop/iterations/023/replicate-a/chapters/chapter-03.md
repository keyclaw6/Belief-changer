Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD AT ALL

IN THIS CHAPTER
- An office birthday cake cut into small squares
- Borrowed party shoes worn until midnight
- A pair of scales with nothing on one side
- The quiet work your body does without sweets

*If BAD SUGAR gave you anything real, you would not need to argue yourself into it.*

THE BARGAIN WE ALL TRY TO STRIKE

I understand you completely, because we have all stood exactly where you stand now.

You have seen the empty box. You know you were conned into it, not flawed in character. You remember that slow lunch too — plain food, eaten slowly, leaving you clean and full for hours without a fix. That memory matters. Keep it close.

And still a bargainer voice speaks up in you. It says:

“I know too much sugar isn’t ideal, but I do get something from it. A little pleasure, a little lift. Surely the sensible answer is just to weigh the harms against the benefits and keep it moderate?”

It sounds fair. Balanced. Adult.

I am glad you have said it out loud, because that bargain is the whole trap talking. Let us look at it together, you and I, with open eyes and no doom.

We always ask the wrong question. We ask, does the harm outweigh the pleasure? That question already hands the con everything it needs. It assumes there is a pleasure to weigh.

I am asking a different question. Not how heavy the harm is. But what good is there at all?

THE CAKE, BITE BY BITE

Come with me to the office. Three o’clock. Someone’s birthday. A flat white box opened on the copier, pink icing, candles already blown out. Everyone laughing. You take a square to be friendly.

First bite. Sweet. Cold icing. Chewing while people chat.

Second bite. Sweeter. Stickier. You are already reaching for water.

Third bite. Cloying. Your teeth feel coated. Your stomach gives a small lurch. You put the paper plate down and smile and hope no one notices you do not want the rest.

Tell me honestly now.

Did that square add joy to the break? Or did the break — the laugh, the pause from work, the faces you like — carry the whole joy while the square sneaked a ride?

If it was truly delightful, why did the pleasure fade by the third bite?

If it was a kindness to yourself, why did you feel slightly queasy and flat after it, not kinder?

If a little is sensible and stable, why were you already bargaining with yourself about whether to take another piece you did not even want?

We do this weighing bite by bite and we never notice the scale is rigged. We put headaches, fog, bills, tooth-rot, weight creep, secret eating and shame on one side. Then on the other side we put — what? A memory of pleasure that never survives examination.

That is not balance. That is brainwashing doing the weighing for you.

THE BORROWED SHOES

Let me show you what that so-called pleasure really is, because once seen you cannot unsee it.

Picture a Saturday party. You borrow a pair of dazzling celebration shoes a size too small because they go with your clothes. You squeeze in. All evening you smile, dance, perch, wince. They pinch your toes. They rub your heel raw. You cannot wait for midnight.

At midnight you kick them off under the table. Ahh. Wonderful relief flooding up your feet. You sigh. You laugh. You wiggle your toes on the cool floor.

Now tell me. Did those shoes give you pleasure?

Of course not. They gave you hours of pain, then the relief of ending pain they themselves created. No one in their right mind would wear pinching shoes all evening for the joy of taking them off — unless they had been conned into believing the relief was a gift of the shoes.

We did exactly that with BAD SUGAR, you and I.

A dose creates a faint, fretful low. The next dose briefly ends that low. We thank the dose for the lift. We never see that the lift is only the ending of a discomfort the last dose created. It never takes us above the ease of a person who never started. It only returns us for a minute toward the normal we broke.

That is why the office cake faded by bite three. That is why one small square never stays small in the evening. The relief is real, but it is not a benefit. It is the midnight kick-off after a day in borrowed shoes.

WHAT IS THERE ON THE OTHER SIDE?

So let us empty the scales and start honest.

People tell me BAD SUGAR must do something for us — taste, comfort, energy. Leave taste and comfort aside for a moment; we will meet them face to face and you will see who really brings the joy. I am asking about the deeper claim: that you need it, that without it something vital would be missing, that life without it would be deprivation.

The fact is, your body already makes the glucose it needs for living, from ordinary food, without a single spoon of BAD SUGAR.

Read that again slowly, because it removes the fear underneath the bargain. You are not facing a life of missing fuel. The clean fullness you felt after that slow real lunch was not an accident. It was your system doing its quiet work. Hunger noticed. Food enjoyed. Fullness felt. No dose taught it that. No wrapper improved it.

If your body makes what it needs, what exactly are you balancing? What are you moderate about? A pinch that sells its own removal as pleasure?

Do you want to keep paying for borrowed shoes for the rest of your life, just to enjoy taking them off?

Look at the cake again, not with guilt, with clarity. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

That sentence changes everything once you let it stand. TO, not FOR. Plenty TO your teeth, your energy, your mood, your purse, your evenings. Nothing FOR your joy, your strength, your celebration, your life. The benefit side is empty. There is no benefit to balance, only a con to leave.

And what we have called pleasure all these years — that little hit, that sweet moment — what was it ever but what we took to be a genuine treat or lift? Say it plainly and you hear how thin it sounds. A genuine treat would leave you better, not queasy and bargaining. A genuine lift would lift you above normal, not drag you low and then briefly stop dragging.

It never gave. It only took and then paused taking.

You do not need courage to walk away from an empty plate. You need only to see it is empty. And is it not marvellous to see it? No loss. No weighing. The question collapses because there is nothing to weigh.

3. BEGIN WITH ELATION
There is no doom here, only an escape to enjoy.

SUMMARY
- The fair question is not harm versus pleasure but whether any real benefit exists at all
- The office cake pleasure fades bite by bite because the moment brings the joy and sugar sneaks a ride
- The lift after a dose is only relief from a low the last dose created, like kicking off pinching shoes at midnight
- The body makes its own glucose, so living without BAD SUGAR is not deprivation
- With nothing real on the benefit side, moderation is not sensible balance
