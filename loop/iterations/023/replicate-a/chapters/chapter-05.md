Chapter 5
NOT LOVE IN A WRAPPER

IN THIS CHAPTER
- The takeaway tub after a small win
- A spoon alone at the kitchen table
- The Friday late show glimpsed in passing
- The market bench remembered in the belly

*No wrapper ever loved you back — the kindness was yours, and the sweetness only rode along.*

THE DESSERT YOU PAID YOURSELF

You have seen the afternoon clearly now. The drawer never fuelled you. It lent brightness and collected fog.

And yet something softer still holds.

You finish something hard. You sort the bills, you get through the week, you handle the difficult call. You are tired in a decent way. On the way home you pick up a takeaway, and you add the little dessert. Not from hunger. From fairness.

Alone at the table you peel the foil lid. You think, in your own kind voice:

“I’ve earned a 'treat'… when you feel you deserve one.”

I know that moment, because we have all lived it. It feels tender, not greedy. It feels like you are looking after yourself at last.

My friend, I am warm to you and utterly harsh to what tricked you there. You do deserve kindness. You do deserve payment for effort. The question is only whether that tub ever paid you.

Let us watch what actually happened that night, spoon by spoon, the way an investigator would, not a judge.

DESERVE IS REAL, THE PAYMENT IS FALSE

The win was real before the lid came off.

You had done well. Your shoulders knew it. There was relief in your chest, a little pride, a little ease. You were already smiling when you reached for the spoon.

Then the first spoonful: cold, sweet, quick. Very quick. For half a minute there is a flare. Then you chase the flare with a second spoon, a third. The tub empties faster than any hunger would require. The kindness you meant to linger over is gone in minutes.

And after? Not held. Not comforted. A little sickly, a little flat, a touch sorry. The pride of the win dimmed under sugar heaviness. You put the empty tub aside and the evening feels smaller than it did before you opened it.

Ask yourself honestly now.

If that dessert was love, why did it leave you feeling less loved?

If it was a reward, why did the sense of reward shrink as the tub emptied?

If it comforted you, why did you need comfort from the comfort?

There is only one honest answer. The love was never in the wrapper.

You brought the win. You brought the relief. You brought the tired, decent pride of a thing done well. The dessert only sneaked a ride on a good moment that was already yours, then charged you for the ride with heaviness. It never added kindness. It borrowed your kindness and gave you back a sinking feeling.

That spoon never was a genuine treat or lift.

It was your own good feeling, briefly interrupted by a dose.

Those who get free say it plainly in their own words — they were celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema with chocolate, as if the chocolate did the celebrating. It did not. I say this carefully and without lecture: I am not forbidding sweetness, and I am not moralising pleasure. Whole fruit, real meals, the natural sweetness of food — that all stays on your menu. What I am showing you is narrower and kinder. The self-payment frame keeps the loop alive. The wrapper takes credit for the joy you made.

Think of a friend who always arrives after you have cleaned the house, sits on your clean sofa, and says, look what a lovely home I made for you. You would laugh. You would know who did the work.

You cleaned the house. You won the day. The dessert sat on your joy and claimed it.

THE COMFORT THAT POINTS DOWN

Comfort tells the same story on a longer clock.

We tell ourselves the sweet soothes a hard day. And for minutes it seems to. Then watch the direction of travel.

In one long look at men, those who ate sweets day after day were more likely to feel low and flat months and years later. Not soothed. Lower. I am not diagnosing you, and I am not saying one dessert dooms a mood. For men, day after day, the daily sweet predicted later low mood, not relief. I say it only to show the arrow.

What is sold as a lift points down.

Does your own evening agree? After the takeaway dessert, did you feel warmer toward life, or a little more tired, a little more flat, a little more in need of another fix to lift the flatness? You know the roller coaster. Up on the spoon, down in the hour. Up again only by reaching again.

A true comfort holds you after it is gone. A warm bath holds you. A kind word holds you. The market lunch held you — remember? Slow food on a bench, steam rising, belly filling cleanly, hours of quiet ease with no bargaining after. That held.

The tub never held. It flared and left. That is not how love behaves. That is how a lender behaves.

So take the strongest little picture, just in passing. Friday late show, lights low, hard week behind you, shared box opened while the film begins. It feels like the chocolate is part of the love. Leave it out once in your mind and look again. The film is still funny. The friend is still beside you. The relief of the week ending is still in your chest. Who was bringing the joy then? The moment, the company, the rest — never the box.

You do not need to test this by force. You only need to see it clearly. The celebration does not need sugar to prove itself. It proves itself every time you let it stand without the wrapper, and find the joy still there, cleaner and longer.

See that, and the fear changes shape. Without sweets, kindness does not disappear. It stays where it always lived — in you, in the win, in the rest, in the company, in real food eaten with appetite. What disappears is only the heaviness that used to follow the kindness and pretend to be part of it.

You deserve the reward. You never deserved the wrapper.

5. LET CELEBRATION PROVE ITSELF WITHOUT SUGAR
Notice who brings the joy when sweets sit out.

SUMMARY
- The win and the relief were yours before the spoon was lifted
- The dessert flared briefly then left flatness, not comfort
- Day after day the sweet points mood down, not up
- True kindness holds after it is gone, the tub never held
- Celebration keeps its joy when sweets sit out, because the joy was never theirs
