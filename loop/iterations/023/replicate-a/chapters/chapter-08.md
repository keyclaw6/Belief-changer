Chapter 8
MADE TO CRAVE

IN THIS CHAPTER
- The supermarket aisle and walls of bright packs
- The white-coated kitchen where craving is dialled in
- The dentist chair memory
- The afternoon body that wakes tired, flat and foggy

*You were never weak in a neutral world — you were placed where every hand reaches, and the load was kept out of sight.*

YOU SAW THE LOOP. NOW SEE THE ROOM

You come to me as a loop-seer.

Last time we stood together in the evening kitchen and watched it plainly: one neat biscuit, ninety minutes, then the gnaw. You know now the gnaw follows the dose. You know the little complaint is small and dying, and the loud voice translating it is old brainwashing.

So a fair question rises, and I welcome it:

If the loop is so simple, why is it everywhere? Why is BAD SUGAR in the bread and the sauce and the till-tray and the petrol station, smiling at you at every turn? Surely if it loaded the body the way you say, someone would have stopped it?

Be warm to yourself for asking. We all asked it. We mistook ubiquity for innocence. We thought, what is everywhere must be harmless, and what harms must come with a loud warning.

It is the other way around.

Walk with me down one ordinary aisle and I will show you it was never accidental.

THE WALLS BUILT FOR YOUR HAND

Picture yesterday's shop. Not a special trip. Just milk and bread.

You turn into the aisle and the walls close in — bright packs at eye height, small packs at the till for the tired hand, big family packs stacked like bricks at the ends. Colours shout. Cartoon faces smile. Promises lean out: extra, new, share-size, treat-size, lunchbox-size.

You did not go in craving. You came out with a dose you never meant to buy.

Ask yourself, from your own trolley, not from my words:

When did you decide to want what you reached for — before you saw the wall, or after?

If abundance were innocent, why does every packet fight so hard to be touched first?

If choice were free, why do you buy the same bright fix in the same tired hour in the same long queue?

There is only one honest answer. Your hand was led.

We told ourselves the shop simply stocks what people love. We never asked who taught people to love it, shelf by shelf, jingle by jingle, since we were children tall enough to stare into the sweet drawer. We thought we were choosing. We were being chosen.

THE WHITE-COATED KITCHEN

Let me take you where the choosing is done.

Not the shop. Further back. A clean, white room that smells of warm biscuit and vanilla. Men and women in white coats stand around a steel bench. On the bench, trays. Tray after tray of almost-identical bites.

One woman breaks a biscuit and listens for the snap. A man closes his eyes to judge the melt. Another notes the chew, the crunch that turns to silk, the salt that lifts the sweet, the sweet that hides the salt. They dial a little more crunch. A little less chew. A little faster melt so the mouth says, again, before the mind can answer.

Behind them a counter clicks. Not money. Mouthfuls. How many bites before the hand stops. How many seconds before the hand returns.

The fact is, that return is the product.

I blame no tired mother buying the packet, and I blame no child reaching for it. I blame the dialling. An army of clever, decent people paid to find the exact point where your tongue lights up and your fullness stays dark — where fat and sugar together seem to have a pull larger than either alone, where the crunch-sweet-melt keeps your hand moving while your belly never gets the message, I am satisfied.

Does that look like neutral food to you? Does that look like accident?

We were not weak among honest plenty. We were outnumbered in a designed trap. Once you see the kitchen, you cannot unsee the aisle. Every bright wall is that bench made large. That is how the sugar trap was built to work — not by forcing your hand, but by training it while the Sweet-Talker told you it was your own taste.

Be angry, if you feel anger, at the dialling. Never at yourself.

THE LOAD YOU CARRY QUIETLY

And now let us look, without flinching, at what the dialled doses do inside.

I will tell you plainly because you deserve plainness, and then I will tell you why you must not act from fright.

BAD SUGAR does not sit light and vanish. Dose upon dose, day upon day, the liver takes the flood it was never built to clear as a staple. Pressure creeps. Inflammation simmers. The body's own control of appetite is bypassed, so hunger signalling blurs while the load grows. Across whole populations, high added-sugar patterns are linked with higher risk to heart and vessels. This is not energy given. This is load deferred.

And if you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, are pregnant, or carry old food wounds, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while you read — this is about belief, not your prescription.

Look too in the small mirror of the dentist chair. You remember the light too bright, the bib too tight, the probe pausing. Free sugars feed plaque, plaque makes acid, acid pits teeth. Brushing and fluoride mediate it, yes, but they do not erase the cause. No one with a drilled molar calls decay distant.

Step back and hear the sober measure the world itself has set. The world's health guidance says keep free sugars below a tenth of daily energy — about fifty grams — with further benefit below half that, precisely because ordinary eating now sails far past both. Not because a crumb is poison at any dose. Because the daily flood is.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

Now — do you feel the chill? The urge to stop because the picture frightened you?

Do not. I do not want your freedom bought with fear, and fear never freed anyone for the rest of your life as a happy non-feeder.

WHY YOU ARE NOT THE ODD ONE

“I must be uniquely weak,” the voice whispers. “Others manage. Look at them.”

Let me answer that whisper with kindness and with honesty about what is known and not known.

On validated scales for highly palatable foods, around fourteen in every hundred adults and twelve in every hundred children score in the addiction-like range — a share similar to that seen for alcohol and tobacco. That does not diagnose you and it does not doom you. It simply says you are not a freak in an aisle of moderates. You are a normal human caught by a normal trap.

And here is the honest split I will not hide from you, because hiding would make you distrust me later. Serious scientists disagree about the word addictive for sugar itself. Some say the bingeing we see comes from the on-off pattern of access rather than sugar chemistry alone, and that human proof is still thin. Others point to the animal binge, the dip, the cue-fire and say the shape is clear.

The fact is, both camps grant what you have lived: under intermittent sweet access, binge behaviour happens. The wanting outruns the liking. The box empties after the sliver. We need no grand label to see what our own evenings proved.

So tell me:

If fourteen in a hundred struggle the same way, is that proof you are broken — or proof the trap is good at its job?

If experts argue over the chemistry while agreeing on the binge, does your empty box need their permission to be real?

If the wanting was dialled in a white room, counted in clicks, stacked at your eye height, does that sound like a flaw in you?

There is only one honest answer, and it lifts shame clean off. You were conned, not defective.

TIRED, FLAT AND FOGGY

Let me name what the load feels like from the inside, because you have felt it and blamed everything but the cause.

Morning. You wake after a full night and still feel tired. Not the clean ache after honest work. A heavy, sandbag tiredness behind the eyes.

Afternoon. The bright flare fades and you go flat. Limbs dull. Interest thins. The drawer calls not because you are lazy but because flatness begs for lift.

Evening. Head foggy. You re-read the same paragraph. You snap at nothing. You promise an early night and find yourself prowling instead.

Tired, flat and foggy — that is the permanent state of the feeder. Not a character. A condition.

We thought life made us so. Age. Work. Weather. Children. We never saw the dose making the dip it then offered to lift. We fed the Nibbler to cure the fog the last feed left behind, and called the brief clearing proof the fix helped.

Once seen, the triple loses its power to shame you. It becomes evidence against the trap, not against you. You do not need sugar to function through slump. Slump is what constant sugar leaves.

8. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT ASKS YOU TO QUIT FROM FEAR
Hear the facts, then choose freedom for gain.

Let the facts stand where they belong — behind you, as the ugliness you walk away from — not above you as a whip. You do not escape because you are frightened of the load. You escape because you see there was never a genuine treat or lift in it to lose, only a manufactured pull and a quiet load, and stepping out is pure gain. Rejoice that you can leave it easily, at once, and find your mornings clear again.

You came in thinking abundance was innocent and warnings were for others. You leave seeing design.

SUMMARY
- Bright abundance was dialled and stacked to pull your hand, not innocent plenty
- The body carries a real deferred load behind the brief sweet flare
- Teeth, heart load and daily excess are population facts, not personal doom
- Struggle is common and human, not proof of unique weakness
- The daily tiredness and fog belong to the doses, not to your character
- Freedom is chosen for gain, not driven by fright
