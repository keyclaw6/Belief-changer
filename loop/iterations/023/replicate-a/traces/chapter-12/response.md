Chapter 12
SMALL DOUBTS ANSWERED

IN THIS CHAPTER

- Office chair at half past three with shaky hands
- Evening table with one dark square left in the foil
- My own kitchen after thirty years of sweet afternoons

*Look straight at the last what-abouts and they vanish; there was never anything in them to keep you hooked.*

YOU ARE WILFUL, AND ALMOST CLEAR

You have settled the large matter.

You see that no sliver closes it, no cut-down loosens it, no swap or delay holds. You see that past collapses blamed you for a method that had to fail. You stand straight again. Wilful, not weak.

And then, in a smaller voice, the questions come:

“But what about honey? What about fruit? What about the dark square they call healthy? What about the shakes if I stop? And what about this book — can it really be this easy?”

I love those questions. They mean the large lie is dead and only its dust is left. Let us wipe the dust now, kindly and completely, so you walk to your last sweet meal with no backward glance.

Come with me to the health shop first.

THE JAR AND THE BOWL

Saturday morning. Wooden shelves. A honey jar glowing amber beside a bowl of plain apples and oranges. A kind assistant says honey is natural, fruit is sugar too, so what is the difference, and does the body not need a little sweet import to keep going?

We have all stood there, jar in one hand, apple in the other, feeling muddled.

Let the muddle clear.

When this book says sugar it means BAD SUGAR — refined sugar, added free sugars, sweet drinks, confectionery, pastries, desserts and junk carbs built for craving. It does not mean whole fruit, vegetables, plain whole foods, milk in food, and the glucose your own body manufactures. GOOD sweetness is food. BAD SUGAR is a dose.

Your body builds its own glucose from protein and from glycerol in ordinary food. You need no import of white powder to keep blood and brain supplied. Cut BAD SUGAR to zero and you deprive yourself of nothing your body cannot already make. If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medicine, are pregnant, or carry an eating-disorder history, keep your clinician in charge of food and medicine while you read.

Now hold the two objects up.

The apple comes wrapped as nature made it — water, fibre, chew, slow satisfaction. You eat one, you stop, your belly tells you. The honey jar, the chocolate bar, the frosted pack — those were dialled in white coats in a flavour lab to make the hand go back. Crunch, melt, sweet, salt, measured to the click so enough is never enough.

Ask yourself:

Did you ever binge on plain apples until the bowl was empty and hide the pips in shame?

Did you ever tell yourself, “just one apple,” and wake with sticky hands at midnight?

Does the apple call you at three in the afternoon, or does the dose?

There is your answer. Fruit is not candy with a better story. Candy is candy with fruit as its alibi.

BUT THE DARK SQUARE IS HEALTHY

“I have heard the dark kind is different. Antioxidants. Less sugar. The French. Surely one square after dinner does me good?”

I hear the hope in it, because we all wanted one sweet to be wise.

Strip it bare.

A dark square is still BAD SUGAR with a darker coat. Cocoa, butter, sugar, coaxed to melt fast and call for another melt. No vitamin inside it needs chocolate as its carriage. You can love cocoa talk and still see the trick — the industry that taught you milk was childish now teaches you dark is adult, so you keep paying at a higher shelf.

Do the honest test we always do. Isolate the moment.

When did you eat that square for health? Did you weigh it, note the milligram, and walk away nourished? Or did you break it after a hard day, let it soften on the tongue, feel a brief lift, then think of the second square while the first was still melting?

And if it truly nourished you, why did one never close the evening? Why did health need hiding, and company, and “I have been good this week” as permission?

Health food does not need permission. A dose does.

The square never fed your health. The evening fed you, and the square sneaked a ride.

THE SHAKE THAT FRIGHTENS YOU

“But the shakes. The three o’clock fog. The I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. If I stop, will I collapse?”

Let me hold your fear with respect and then remove it, because this is where kindly truth matters most.

The lift you felt after a sweet hit was insulin working — your body rushing to clear the surge. That same surge can leave you dipping after, flat and headachy an hour later. That dip is real.

But that afternoon fog is not clinical low blood sugar. True clinical low is rare, measured, a medical matter, not an office drawer at half past three. Your everyday slump is the loan coming due, not illness arriving.

In animals, a sugar binge is followed by a measured dip in the reward circuit with a rise in the opposing chemical — a small see-saw after the hit. One pro-addiction laboratory, no friend of mine, called the whole chemistry “mild but well-defined.”

Hear those three words. Mild. Not mighty. Well-defined. Not imaginary.

So you will feel something when you stop feeding the doses. A hollow gnaw. A restlessness at the old hour. A call for “sugary somethings… to get me through.” It lasts days, not life. Two to five days at its hump for most, then it thins and passes. It never needs a drug to cure it because it was never disease. It is a small creature starving, and you get to watch it die.

Ask yourself:

If stopping created collapse, how do children run and laugh for hours with no drawer to raid?

If the shake proved need, why does it arrive only after the last dose, never before the first sweet you ever ate?

If the price were unbearable, why do free readers describe those first sweet-free afternoons as clearer, not darker?

The shake was never proof you need it. It was proof it had you.

TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE?

Now the last doubt, aimed not at sugar but at me:

“This sounds too neat. No suffering? No counting? Just see the trick and walk free? That cannot work. That must be brainwashing.”

I welcome it. Doubt well aimed finds truth.

You tried the hard way for years. Rules, red pens, “once a week is fine,” “I don’t keep it in the house.” You suffered and called suffering proof you were serious. Now I tell you freedom needs no suffering, and a voice says, “If it does not hurt, it cannot be true.”

Whose voice is that?

It is the voice of the Willpower Method.

That method taught you that escape must be earned by pain. It made the dose precious by forbidding it, made each hit louder by counting it, left you obedient for now and never free. Of course a painless exit sounds false to a mind trained by pain. The prison teaches that the corridor is safety.

Let me ask you straight:

Did pain ever free you? How many painful Mondays bought you a free Friday?

Did counting ever quiet the call, or did counting keep the call talking all day?

If ease proves falsehood, why do you trust your eyes when you eat a market lunch slowly and feel clean fullness with no effort at all?

Ease does not prove falsehood. Ease proves you stopped fighting the wrong enemy.

I do not ask you to believe me. I ask you to believe the logic you already lived. The struggle was the method, never your measure.

WHAT I SAW AT MY OWN TABLE

Let me tell you plainly, as one escapee to another.

I ate sweets for more than thirty years. Afternoon drawer, evening bowl, cinema box, car sweets for the drive home. Three, four hits a day without counting, because counting would have shamed me. I told myself I loved taste, needed fuel, deserved reward. The same three stories you told.

When I saw the inversion — that each lift made the next low — I ate my last ordinary dessert with attention, not nostalgia. Sticky, cloying, too sweet after the second spoon. I decided I was done that night.

The first three afternoons the old hour called. Half past three, hands a little empty, head saying, “something sweet would be nice.” I did not argue. I ate real lunch slowly, drank water, went back to work. The call thinned by day four. By the second week I walked past the office plate without the old pull and felt a laugh rise — relief, not pride.

I will give you restart honesty too, because you deserve it. Two months free, tired after a funeral, I took a biscuit without thinking. Old habit, not hunger. It tasted dusty and over-sweet. No pleasure, only information. My body coped. My mind did not reopen the door. I did not scold, did not count, did not start again. I saw the warning and rejoiced that freedom still held.

That is ten years ago now. Mornings clear. Shops easy. Evenings mine. No drawer to guard. No red pen. I do not miss a friend. I celebrate an enemy dead.

You can have that completeness too, not by being stronger than I was, but by seeing clearer than we both once did.

12. AVOID BORROWING DOUBT FROM SWEET-EATERS
Other appetites cannot report your freedom.

SUMMARY
- The body makes its own glucose, so zero BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing
- Whole fruit satisfies and closes, while engineered doses are built to call again
- A dark square is still a dose, and health never needed it as carriage
- The afternoon dip follows the last hit, and the hump after stopping is small and brief
- The painless exit is not trickery, it is the end of the painful method
- A flown moment of doubt from others cannot measure the freedom you now see
