Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — Morning market air, paper-wrapped bread still warm, a plain favourite meal eaten hungry, the stopped plate, the fruit bowl in daylight

*Real food eaten when you are hungry and left when you are satisfied tastes like the joy you thought only sweets could give.*

THE FUEL LIE IS DEAD

You walk in here a different eater from the one who walked into the last room.

In the last room you sat at the desk at half past two and watched the whole afternoon tell the truth. The drawer. The bright packet. The ten-minute buzz. The four o’clock fog and the second reach. You saw the tight shoes for what they were. Pain put there by the last dose, brief hush when fed, tighter pinch behind it. You took the credit back from the packet and gave it to yourself where it belonged.

Keep that sight. You will need it here, because we are about to answer the fear that has waited underneath the fuel story all along.

I know this fear because I carried it for years. We all did.

We told ourselves that without sweet hits eating would turn grey. Plain food would be dull. Meals would be duty. Fruit would be a poor cousin. Dessert was love, and without dessert what is left but chewing and swallowing?

Do you feel that still? Good. Bring it close. Bring your favourite plain meal and your emptiest evening. We will eat together once, hungry, with attention, and you will feel what your body knew before the grazing years began.

The fact is, hunger and satisfaction were never the enemy. They are the flavour.

I speak as one who lived the loop. Grazing through mornings that should have been bright. Picking through afternoons. Arriving at dinner already half-full and half-empty at once. Calling the box love and the meal fuel and getting both backwards. Praising the shout and missing the hum. I thought joy lived in the packet because I never let hunger speak loudly enough to be heard.

We all did that. We ate full and felt empty and blamed the meal.

You arrive here forgiven and clear-eyed. You leave here as an eater at home in your own body.

THE GREY PLATE FEAR

Let me voice the thought exactly as it comes at night.

“But I love food because of sweets. Take away the pudding, the chocolate after, the biscuit with tea, and what is left? A grey plate. Chewing. Duty. I will eat like a machine and feel like one.”

“I call dessert love. My evening sweet is the kind part of the day. The meal is just the queue for it.”

I hear you. There is no shame in that thought. It was printed into us bite by bite. Sweet after savoury. Treat when you feel you deserve one. Celebrating with chocolate. The sweet placed at the end like a prize, so the meal learned to bow to it.

Ask your life a cleaner question. Not, can I bear meals without sweets? But, when did meals stop being enough?

Think back. Before the daily grazing, a meal eaten hungry was an event. Steam. Smell. First bite that made the eyes close. Second bite slower. Talk around the table. Bread torn and gone. Hunger quieting bite by bite until the body said, done, and the plate could sit. No clock-watch. No hum underneath. Satisfaction that registered.

What changed was not the meal. What changed was the hum laid over it.

Full stomach, empty hum. Meal eaten, drawer still glowing. Enough never landing as enough. We called that proof the meal was dull. It was proof the loop was loud.

When did a plain meal last taste truly bright to you? On holiday, perhaps. After a long walk, starving, a simple plate put down and eaten with both hands. No box in the cupboard calling. No packet voice. Did that taste grey? Or did it taste marvellous, wonderful, complete?

And when did a box eaten full ever satisfy? Thursday night, the permitted sliver that became the evening, wrapper rustle, hand moving, regret arriving before swallowing ends. Did that taste like love? Or did it taste hurried, coated, half-noticed, gone?

There is only one honest reading. The grey was never in the plain food. The grey was in the eating that could never stop.

It was hunger unheard that made food joyless, not hunger honoured.

Feel what that reversal does. It lifts blame off the meal and off you in one move. You were not dull of palate. You were drowned in hits, so the palate could never clear. Clear the hits and the palate clears with it.

HUNGER SPEAKS PLAINLY

Hunger is not an emergency. Hunger is an invitation.

Real hunger arrives simply. A hollow warmth low down. Attention sharpening toward smell. Food sounding good — not one packet sounding urgent, but food sounding welcome. The mouth watering at bread, at soup, at fruit. Patience still present. Joy still possible.

We learned to misread every signal as orders for BAD SUGAR. The I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. The afternoon thinning. The evening restlessness. We called each one proof the body wanted sweets. The body wanted eating. The loop shouted a brand name over the top.

Listen again with clean ears.

A body that has eaten real meals and slept and moved speaks plainly and stops at satisfied. It asks. It receives. It rests. It asks again hours later. That rhythm is older than any packet. It led you before you could read. It leads still on days the loop loosens — those bright stretches when hours pass without thought of sweets because work or play held you, and then hunger arrives clear as a bell and food tastes vivid.

Who taught you to fear that bell? Who taught you to answer it before it rings, with grazing that keeps it forever half-muffled?

The fact is your body makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food, so life without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. Bread, vegetables, fruit, plain meals — the ordinary menu carries what the machine needs to hum along. If you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar or appetite, keep your clinician in charge and use this book for the belief only.

Hear the relief inside that fact. You are not removing fuel. You are removing noise. Hunger can speak. Satisfaction can register. Energy returns as steady brightness underneath, not shout for ten minutes and bill for sixty.

Do you say, but I never feel true hunger now, only cravings? Of course. We all lost the signal under the grazing. Grazing keeps the stomach forever half-full and the nerves forever half-hungry. No emptiness to answer. No fullness to land in. Only the loop. Let meals be meals and the signal returns within days. Not by rule. Not by clock. By need met and left alone.

A MARKET MORNING AND A PLAIN MEAL

Come with me to prove it, not as theory but as doing.

Morning market. Cold air and shouting stalls. Greens stacked high and wet. Roots earthy in crates. Apples with scent still on the skin. Bread in paper, warm through the paper, crust cracking as you squeeze. Nothing in bright packets shouting. Everything smelling of itself.

You buy simply. Vegetables for the pan. Fresh bread. Fruit for after. No counting. No lecture. No good and bad columns. Only what looks alive and what you like.

Home. Hunger awake because you let it wake. Not starved. Not stuffed. Ready.

You cook plainly. Vegetables in hot oil, salt, colour brightening. Bread cut thick. Water on the table. Fruit washed and set out. Steam rising. Smell filling the kitchen. Hands a little quick with hunger. That quickness is joy arriving, not an emergency.

Sit. First bite hungry.

Notice. Really notice. The crunch that yields. The heat that spreads. The salt that lifts. The bread that tears and soaks. Chew slower than the loop ever let you. Taste arrives in waves where grazing allowed only the first shout. Second bite slower still. Shoulders dropping. Breath deepening. Talk if company is there — company flavours food more than any glaze ever did.

Halfway, pause. Where is attention? Not in the drawer. In the plate. In the warmth moving outward. In hunger quieting from sharp to soft.

Eat until satisfied. Not stuffed. Not cleared. Satisfied — that warm, easy done-ness when hunger goes quiet and pleasure turns from keen to calm. Put the fork down while food still tastes good. Leave some. Push the plate a little away. Feel what follows. Not the coated mouth and the hunt. Warmth. Clarity. Done.

Then fruit, if wanted. Bright, cold, juicy, sweetness that belongs to itself, eaten for itself, not as a prize for virtue. Notice how different this sweetness feels from a hit. It does not shout and bill. It refreshes and leaves. It can be loved without being chased.

This is the sentence that carries the whole room.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read it as permission, not as rule. Want is allowed. Hunger leads. Satisfaction ends. No punishment after, ever. No being good this week. No paying for pleasure with shame. The eater you were before the packets knew this by nature, like the small child with the peach who stops mid-bite to run off and play.

Feel the credit inversion your mouth demands. Who made that meal marvellous? Not a dose. Hunger made it marvellous. The body made it marvellous. The market, the heat, the hands that cooked, the company at the table made it marvellous. BAD SUGAR only ever sneaked a ride on that marvellousness and claimed the wage.

Take the wage back.

BUT FRUIT IS SUGAR TOO

I hear the clever objection, and I welcome it because it pretends to be logic while protecting the loop.

“But fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Bread turns to glucose. It is all the same. Why draw a line at all?”

It sounds fair. It is the loop talking philosophy to keep its packet.

Answer with your mouth, not with argument. Eat an apple hungry after a walk. Slow. Juicy. Done at one, perhaps two, satisfaction registering clean. Now remember the box eaten full on the sofa. Hurried. Coated. Never landing. Hand moving before decision. One wakes hunger and closes it. The other mutes hunger and multiplies it.

Are those the same experience? Does your body treat them the same? Does one leave you warm and finished and the other leave you restless and hunting?

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined and added free sugars and junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, the doses that ride the loop. Natural sweetness inside whole fruit and vegetables and plain meals belongs to the menu that satisfies. It does not shout. It does not bill. It does not keep the hum alive.

“But I love sweet taste. Will I never love sweetness again?”

You will love sweetness more, because you will taste it clean. Fruit in daylight. Bread crust. Roast roots sweetened by heat. Milk. The plain sugars of real food, met hungry, heard at satisfied. What fades is not sweetness. What fades is the hunt that drowned sweetness in noise.

Ask again with clear eyes. If fruit sweetness satisfied, why did the loop never let fruit be enough? If all sugars were one, why did one leave you done and the other leave you hunting?

You know. We all know once hunger speaks plainly. One belongs to eating. The other belongs to the trap.

SATISFACTION IS THE PLEASURE

Satisfaction has a feeling, and you have almost forgotten it, so learn it again the way you learned everything true — by noticing.

It is not fullness bursting. It is hunger going quiet. Keenness turning to calm. Food tasting a little less urgent and life tasting a little more vivid. Eyes lifting from the plate. Attention freeing for talk, for air, for play. Body warm. Head clear. No hum underneath ordering more.

Grazing never let you land there. Each hit promised satisfaction and delivered its opposite — brief hush, louder return. Full stomach, empty signal. Plate cleared, cupboard glowing. We called that proof we were broken eaters. It was proof the signal was jammed.

Let hunger and satisfaction do their old work and the jam clears.

Eat when hungry. Not by the clock. Not by the packet voice. By the hollow warmth and the welcome toward food.

Stop when satisfied. Not when punished. Not when permitted. By the quiet done-ness that says, enough, thank you.

Enjoy real food. Not as duty. Not as virtue. As favourite — noticed, savoured, loved in company, left with pleasure.

Do you fear you will eat too much without rules? Look at who overate. The grazer with rules ate the box after the permitted sliver. The hungry eater without rules stops mid-bite and runs off. Rules made food precious. Hunger makes food enough.

Do you fear meals will bore you without a sweet ending? Watch a meal eaten truly hungry to satisfied. Bright beginning. Slow middle. Calm end. No ending needed because no emptiness remains to fill. Boredom was the fog between hits calling itself appetite. Satisfaction has no boredom in it.

Hunger was the flavour all along.

Feel freedom warming here, not as a shout but as a homecoming. Mornings with appetite. Shops without hunt. Meals that begin and end. Evenings that do not dissolve into crumbs and vows. The body trusted. The plate left. Life getting on with itself while food does its quiet work underneath.

You came in fearing eating would turn grey. You leave knowing grey was the loop and colour was hunger met.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**

- Food joy never lived in sweet hits, it lived in hunger met and satisfaction heard.
- The grey plate was grazing that kept hunger half-muffled and fullness from landing.
- Real hunger arrives as welcome toward food, not urgent orders for one packet.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes bright, slows halfway, and ends in calm done-ness.
- Fruit sweetness refreshes and leaves, while boxed hits hush briefly and hunt again.
- The body makes what it needs from ordinary meals, so meals without BAD SUGAR miss nothing.
- Satisfaction feels like hunger going quiet and attention freeing for life.
- Company, smell, texture and hunger flavour meals more than any glaze.
