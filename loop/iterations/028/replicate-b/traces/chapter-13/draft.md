Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — A photographed list on the fridge door, a closed book on the table, morning light in the market, a shared meal ahead

*Sweetness was never yours to lose; it has been handed back, and you are free to live.*

IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

If you opened the book here, at the end, close these pages now and start at the beginning.

I say that with warmth and with flat authority. This page is not the method. This page is saved reminders for a free eater. The freedom was built room by room, belief by belief, and it cannot be borrowed from a list. The list will keep you free once sight is clear. It cannot clear sight by itself.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Read whole, with an open mind, carrying on exactly as normal while you read, and you will walk out where I stand now — happy to be free, pitying trapped eaters, inhabiting mornings, shops and meals with ease. Then come back here. This short close will be waiting, and it will mean everything.

For you who have walked the whole road, stay with me a little longer. There is one last turn to take, and it is marvellous. Then I release you to life.

YOU NEED NO FURTHER LESSON

There is a whisper that visits every freed eater.

“But I will need to keep studying. I will need to keep topping up. I will need a new rule for birthdays, a new rule for bad days, a new rule for holidays, or I will slide back.”

I know that voice. We all knew it. We lived years believing freedom had to be guarded with study, with vigilance, with fresh teaching piled on old teaching. The Willpower Method taught us that. It told us we were fragile and forgetful, and only endless effort would hold us straight.

Hear it clearly now, because this is the last of its lies to die.

You do not need ongoing teaching to stay free. You need no course, no refresher, no deeper level. What kept you trapped was never lack of knowledge about sugar. What kept you trapped was a false belief about what BAD SUGAR did FOR you. That belief is gone. What remains is sight. And sight, once clear, does not need to be re-taught.

Ask, honestly, with the book nearly closed in your hand.

Did you stay trapped for years because you had not read enough diet books? Did one more tip, one more plan, one more warning ever loosen the hunt for an hour? Or did every new rule make the sweet more precious, the hunt more frantic, the box emptier by ten o’clock?

You know. We all know once the question is weighed.

More teaching never freed anyone. Clear sight frees at once. You have it.

I tell you as one who escaped after years in the hunt: I carry no file of rules in my head. I count nothing. I watch nothing. I weigh nothing. I live. Hunger comes, I eat. Satisfaction comes, I stop. A sweet thought crosses, relief answers. That is the whole of it, lived in ordinary days. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with a history around eating that ever needed care, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice first.

This book changes a belief. It leaves your body, your hunger, your table in your own glad hands.

Do you see how light that leaves you? No second book to carry. No maintenance to dread. No pupil to remain forever.

There is nothing left to learn. There is life to live.

CARRY ONE LIST, THEN LIFT YOUR EYES

We loved lists in the trap. We collected them. Forbidden foods. Allowed hours. Good weeks. Bad weeks. “I’ve managed to be good this week” written proudly on Monday, torn up by Friday. Lists were whips. They measured our slavery and called it progress.

This list is not that.

This is not homework. This is not a staircase to climb day by day. This is a photograph of the door you already walked through — kept in the pocket so that, if mist ever drifts, you can glance down and remember the door is behind you, closed, and the field is before you, wide.

Photograph it. Keep it on the fridge, in the phone, by the kettle. Glance at it when you laugh and when you weep, when you feast and when you hurry. Let each line remind you of sight already won, not work still to do.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is eating. That is all of eating. Hunger, joy, satisfaction, forgetting. No numbers. No atonement. No bargaining with tomorrow. Real food enjoyed hungry as favourite — bread that crackles, fruit heavy with juice, soup warm to the hands, a plain supper laughed over and left clean. The body knows. Trust it and it steadies, meal by meal, morning by morning.

And when any sweet picture glows — foil, glass stand, old hour striking — let the heart answer at once:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Not pushed away. Not lectured. Reframed with joy. Picture, then shout. Glow, then laugh. Memory, then relief. Each time old glow fires new joy, joy strengthens till joy fires by itself faster than glow. That is guarding the belief, not guarding crumbs with fear. Sight held, life follows. You never reopen a settled question. You rejoice that it is settled.

Ask, with the list in your hand and a meal ahead.

Does a free eater need to rehearse freedom to keep it? Does a person with clear eyes need lessons in seeing? Or does seeing keep itself, bright and easy, while living does the rest?

You know. Freedom keeps itself while joy lives it.

THE SWEETNESS HANDED BACK

Now the last reveal, saved for this hour alone.

We feared life without BAD SUGAR would be greyer. Less sweet. Less kind. A plain road with the lights taken out. Even after the hunt died, even after mornings cleared and tables brightened, a small sorrow lingered in some of us: something precious buried.

Hear the truth that buries the sorrow.

Nothing precious was buried. Something precious was stolen, and now it has been handed back.

We were born eaters who loved food. Think of a small child with a peach — juice to the elbows, eyes wide, whole body hungry, then stopping mid-bite to run off playing, forgetting food utterly because life called louder. No counting. No guilt. Hunger clean. Satisfaction soft. Taste bright. That was you. That was all of us before the con found us.

Then the bright packs found us. Bliss-point engineers, cartoon tigers, checkout ambush, cinema warmth, “treat when you feel you deserve one,” “sugary somethings to get me through.” Dose by dose the tongue was coated, the hunger drowned, the satisfaction blurred. Real sweetness — peach, berry, bread, hunger met — was dulled under blast after blast, then sold back to us in wrappers at a cruel price: brief buzz, fast drop, fog, hunt. We paid with money, hours, teeth, mood, midnight shame, and called the payment pleasure.

It was always the other way around.

BAD SUGAR never added sweetness to your life. It stole the sweetness you were born with and sold you echoes. It made mornings flat, afternoons foggy, evenings hunted, meals hurried, love noisy with rustle — then offered another hit as the cure for the flatness it dug. The rescuer was the perpetrator all along.

And now? Tongue clean. Hunger clean. Taste bright again. Bread has crust and music. An apple cracks and floods. Hunger seasons better than any coating ever did. Meals end clean and leave the evening whole. Mornings begin light because evenings close clean. That bright, ordinary sweetness — food, hunger, satisfaction, life tasted whole — is yours again.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing back into the eater you were.

Let that land in the body, not only the mind. You have not lost a treat. You have recovered a palate, a belly, a day. You have grown out of a cage you were lured into as a child and grown back into wide fields. An eagle raised as a chicken does not mourn the coop when wings remember air. It lifts. It soars. It forgets the wire.

I promise you flat, with the total certainty of one who lives it: the months ahead will taste sweeter than any year in the hunt. Not because you try to make them sweet. Because sweetness lives in clear hunger, shared tables, laughed-over suppers, cold water drunk thirsty, hot tea in lamplight, fruit eaten hungry — and you are back where you can taste it.

There is no lost friend to mourn. There is a dead enemy to rejoice over.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

CHOOSE YOUR REST OF LIFE

Stand now and choose, plainly, with eyes open, for the choice is stark and joyful.

Picture two roads.

On one, the hunt continues for the rest of your life. The same 10pm reach. The same bright aisle marching. The same lift then sag at four. The same box emptied with regret before swallowing ended. The same Monday vow, the same Friday glow made precious by forbidding, the same creep to Thursday and Saturday. fog money paid daily for fog. Teeth, mood, wallet, hours quietly taxed. Hunger never clean. Satisfaction never soft. Evenings never whole. All for doses that never rose above the normal of a person who never started. Flat, foggy evenings bought at full price, forever.

On the other, freedom continues for the rest of your life. Mornings light. Shops calm. Meals hungry and satisfied and forgotten. Attention whole. Laughter unbroken by rustle. Pity where envy lived. Pictures that glow met with joy. Crumbs smiled at and forgotten, planned exceptions never invited. Evenings whole. Sleep clean. Hunger bright. Real food favourite. Hours your own. All for nothing paid, nothing carried, nothing counted.

Both cannot be yours. One is gone the moment sight holds.

Ask, and only honest answers will do.

Which rest of life feels like burden, and which feels like relief? Which road pays daily for emptiness, and which road receives daily for nothing? If the sweet truly gave, why did the years of giving leave you flat, foggy and never satisfied? If freedom truly took, why do your mornings, tables, shops already feel marvellous, wonderful, exciting?

You know. We all know once the roads are weighed.

You have chosen. The vow is spoken. The last ordinary treat taken with attention on its cloying ugliness, then rejoicing. The decision never to be reopened. A passing thought a dead enemy, not an order.

Hold it with joy. Sight held, behaviour follows. Change nothing else in life. Live the same day — kitchen, work, table, bed — with clear head and honest belly. Inhabit every old room confidently to prove freedom. That forgetting — hours gone by with no thought of doses — is freedom proving itself.

Get on with enjoying your life.

Go to the market hungry. Cook with steam and colour. Eat with people loved. Walk out at noon with bags swinging. Sit whole through films. Sleep clean. Wake light. Pity trapped eaters warmly. Preach never. Let visible ease do the talking. If asked, speak simply: it gave me nothing, I feel brilliant free, I eat what I love hungry. Then return to joy.

I have sat at birthdays, at weddings, at tired Tuesdays, with clear head while trays circled. I tell you: the evening is brighter without the hunt inside it. Talk remembered. Faces seen. Freedom proves itself by forgetting.

You are not a pupil to be taught forever. You are an eater grown back. You are not fragile. You are free.

Do not wait to be free — you already are.
