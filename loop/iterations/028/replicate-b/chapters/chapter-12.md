Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — Dawn kitchen light and a singing kettle, a market table hungry at noon, the bright shop aisle and ringing checkout, an evening sofa in lamplight

*You do not watch yourself live without sweets; you live, and sweets are forgotten.*

FIRST LIGHT, CLEAR HEAD

Wake gently on the first ordinary morning.

No ceremony. No drum roll. No tightness in the chest. Light comes thin through the curtain. The kettle sings in the kitchen. The house makes its small morning sounds — pipes, birds, a distant door. You lie for a moment and notice what is missing.

No hunt.

We know that old morning well. We woke heavy and foggy, mouth sour from last night's box, head dull, belly uneasy, mind already bargaining. Shall I be good today? Shall I skip breakfast to pay for last night? Shall I allow one sweet thing with coffee to lift the fog? The day began with numbers and promises and quiet shame before feet touched floor. Half the mind on the day ahead, half the mind on wrappers.

That is over.

You stand. You stretch. You breathe morning air deep into clear lungs. How light the body feels when no fog sits behind the eyes. How clean the mouth tastes when no syrup filmed it at midnight. How quiet the mind is when no voice counts and bargains.

Go to the window. Look out. Roofs, sky, washing lines, school bags swinging down the street. This is your life returned, marvellous in its plainness.

You have walked out of the Sugar Trap.

Say it plainly, with joy, as the day begins:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Let that be the first laugh of the day. Not held with effort. Not spoken to convince yourself. Spoken because it is true. The heart shouts what the mind knows. Mornings are not a test. Mornings are yours.

Wash. Dress. Move about your ordinary jobs. Make the bed. Feed the cat. Open post. Answer the early message. Notice how attention holds. We lived years with attention cut in half — half on work, half on craving, half on faces, half on wrappers. Now talk runs whole. Work runs whole. Laughter runs whole from first sip of water to last crumb of breakfast.

If a faint flatness drifts by, smile. That faint grumble is the Nibbler dying.

It is tiny. A brief emptiness. A passing thought of something sweet. A light twitch in the hands. Then gone, like mist off a field when sun rises. You do not argue with it. You do not mourn it. You rejoice at it. A dying echo cannot order a free eater about. It can only fade while you get on with enjoying your life.

You do not guard the morning with rules. You live it. Water. Air. Movement. Light. Hunger will come when hunger comes, and you will meet it gladly. Till then, you are busy with life, and life is busy with you. That forgetting — an hour gone by with no thought of doses — is freedom proving itself.

Ask, standing in dawn light with clear head, and only honest answers will do.

Did a free morning need a hit to begin? Did clarity need coating to feel bright? Or was brightness already whole in sleep closed clean, mouth clean, mind quiet?

You know. We all know once morning is weighed.

HUNGRY AT THE TABLE

Hunger arrives clean now. Learn its voice again.

Real hunger is open and friendly. It grows slowly in the belly. It makes plain food shine. It leaves cleanly when satisfied. It does not shout for one narrow thing at ten at night. It does not twitch and shake and demand. It invites.

Sit to breakfast hungry. Not by clock. Not by rule. By hunger.

A bowl of warm oats with fruit. Eggs and fresh bread with butter melting. Plain yogurt with berries. Market vegetables left from last night warmed through with rice. Eat slowly. Taste. Notice.

We forgot how good real food tastes when the tongue is not coated. BAD SUGAR dulled taste buds with blast after blast, then called dullness need for more blast. Clean tongue wakes. Bread has crust and crumb and salt. An apple cracks and floods. Cheese is deep. Soup is warm right down to the hands. Hunger seasons all of it better than any coating ever did.

Eat till satisfied, then stop — like a child who stops mid-bite with a peach to run off playing.

No counting. No paying. No punishing. No second bargain about lunch to atone for breakfast. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with a history around eating that ever needed care, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice first.

I tell you as one who lived the count and escaped it: the body knows. Hunger rises. Satisfaction falls soft as evening light. Trust it and it steadies day by day, meal by meal.

Ask, with spoon in hand, and only honest answers will do.

Does this meal leave fog or clarity? Does it leave hunt or peace? Does it leave shame or quiet pride? Did joy come from coating or from hunger met, from warmth, from morning light on the table?

You know. We all know once food is weighed.

Lunch comes the same. Hungry, eat. Satisfied, stop. Supper the same. Between meals, live. Work. Walk. Talk. Laugh. Drink water when thirsty. Rest when tired. Real needs met in real ways give real relief. The old narrow want for a fix between meals was never hunger. It was echo. Echo fades when unfed, and hunger shines brighter for its fading.

This is change-nothing-else lived. You do not eat by a new system. You do not carry packets or plans or safe snacks to clutch. You do not rearrange life around absence. You live the same day — kitchen, work, table, bed — with clear head and honest belly. Marvellous relief. Wonderful ease. The day holds together whole.

And if memory glows mid-morning — foil, glass cake stand, drawer that once opened by itself — let the heart answer at once with joy. Do not push the picture away. Do not lecture it. Reframe it.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

The thought becomes the trigger for relief. That is not effort. That is freedom firing.

THE AISLE AT NOON

Walk into the shop at noon as a free eater.

Same doors sigh open. Same cold air. Same bright packs shouting. Same sweet smell from the bakery corner. Nothing outside changed. Everything inside you changed.

We walked this aisle for years as hunters. Eyes flicked to ends and offers. Hands reached before decision. Trolleys filled with bargains that were not bargains. Checkout dread already building while mouth watered. We called that choosing. It was marching. We were conned by bright coats and clever placement, and we blamed our own hands for moving.

Walk it now slowly. Head high. Eyes clear.

Look at the packs. Cartoon tigers. Gold foil. Family size shouting value. Two for one. Limited joy. See them for what they are. Bright coats on a con that made billions from fog and hunt. Harsh to the trap, warm to you: you were never weak for hearing that shout. You were outgunned by armies who studied bliss and placed ambush at child height. Now you hear the shout as noise, not music.

Pause by the biscuits you once emptied. Look straight. Small brown discs. Too sweet a smell. Sticky film remembered on teeth. Fog remembered behind eyes. Hunt remembered till midnight. Does that look like pleasure? Does that look like love? Ask as an investigator with trolley rolling free.

If that packet truly gave joy, why did the box always end in regret before swallowing ended? Why did one always pull a second and a second pull a box? Why did evenings close heavy rather than bright? What did the dose ever add to the room that the room did not already hold?

You know. The room gave. The dose took. It was always the other way around.

Move on lightly. Buy real food you love. Bread that crackles. Fruit heavy with juice. Vegetables bright as paint. Fish, eggs, cheese, rice, beans — plain favourites eaten hungry as favourite. Fill the basket with colour. Feel pride hum quiet. No bargaining at the shelf. No hiding. No second basket in mind for later.

 linger a moment by the drinks chillers, tall bottles glowing like jewels. We once read that glow as refreshment. Now see the film it leaves, the buzz then sag, the thirst that returns louder. Does a free throat envy that? Does clear water, cold fruit, hot tea drunk thirsty, not refresh deeper? You know. Thirst met with drink satisfies. Thirst met with syrup returns.

At the checkout bells hear the old rustle behind you — someone's bar unwrapped in queue, someone's bottle tipped. Do not flinch. Do not preach. Look with clear eyes and let pity rise where envy once lived.

Poor trapped eater, still queuing. Still paying fog money for hunt. Still believing a hit will lift the afternoon it will flatten by four. You are not deprived while they indulge. They are trapped while you are free. Does their rustle sound like joy or like echo? Does their quick chew look like ease or like need?

You know. Envy dies the moment sight lives.

Smile to yourself and think your joyful thought. Walk out into noon light with bags swinging, head clear, belly calm, hours your own. Shops passed calmly. That is freedom proved in the ordinary — not on a mountaintop, but by the till.

THE MEAL SHARED

Come home and cook. Or order plain and good. Or sit at a friend's table. Life feeds you in many rooms now, all of them easy.

Hunger leads. Satisfaction closes. Between them lies one of life's great pleasures — real food enjoyed hungry, with people loved, in light or lamplight.

Set the table. Notice steam. Notice colour. Notice talk rising. Chop, stir, taste for salt, laugh at the pot boiling over. Bring bowls to the table hot. Eat. Laugh. Stop satisfied and stay at the table for talk, not for picking. We once left tables early to hunt, or stayed late to pick at crumbs while talk died. Now talk lives because hands are quiet.

There is talk that runs unbroken for an hour. There is bread passed hand to hand. There is second helpings taken gladly because hunger still calls, and none taken sadly because echo calls. There is pudding of fruit and cheese and coffee, savoured, finished, forgotten. That forgetting is joy. A meal that ends clean leaves the evening whole for what comes next.

If the host brings dessert with love — cake under glass, shared chocolate when the lights went low remembered from old evenings — meet it with ease. No speech. No lecture. No wide eyes. No tale of your escape unless asked warmly and briefly.

Take plain coffee or tea or fruit if hunger still calls, or nothing if satisfied, with thanks and warmth. Love lives in the host, in the laugh, in the evening, not in icing. Will your no dim the candles? Will your clear head sour the joy? Or will your laughter, unbroken by rustle, lift the room?

Ask with heart full, and only honest answers will do.

Did the sweet ever make the company, or did company carry the sweet? Did laughter need frosting to be laughter? Did the friend need syrup to be loved?

You know. It was always the other way around.

Do not evangelize. Preaching breeds defence and drags you back into argument that reopens your own settled sight. Let visible ease do the talking. A free eater laughing whole, eating hungry, stopping satisfied, forgetting packets for hours, teaches more than a sermon. If someone asks, speak simply: I found it gave me nothing, I feel brilliant free, I eat what I love hungry. Then return to joy. If no one asks, live joy and leave them to wonder.

I have sat at birthdays, at weddings, at late films, at tired Tuesdays, with clear head while trays circled. I promise you flat: the evening is brighter without the hunt inside it. Talk remembered. Faces seen. Sleep clean after. Freedom proves itself by forgetting — hours in work and laughter with no thought of doses.

Clear the plates together. Wash up chatting. Walk home under street lamps with belly satisfied and head light. That walk — cool air, full heart, no shop glowing too bright — is the life you were promised and now own.

EVENING SOFA, WHOLE NIGHT

Evening was the hard hour for many of us. Now watch evening turn kind.

Lamplight. Sofa. Book or film or talk or quiet. Belly satisfied from supper. Head clear. Hands still. No rustle. No wrapper arithmetic. No cupboard parade at ten.

We remember the old ten o'clock well. We, all of us, lived it. Full day survived, then the narrow voice: need-something-sweet-now, just one to close the day, earned, deserved, needed to unwind. Hand moving before decision. One becoming box. Box becoming fog. Fog becoming vow to be good tomorrow. Tomorrow becoming today with fresh excuse.

That wheel is broken.

Sit whole through the film. Notice plot. Notice faces. Laugh unbroken. Make tea. Stretch legs. Listen to rain on glass. If the old hour strikes — ten chimes and memory glows — greet it. Ah, there you are. A passing picture. A dead echo. Not an order. Think your thought and smile.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Feel relief flood where dread once pooled. Happy to be free — not merely free, but glad, proud, excited, relieved. That emotion is the goal, and it is yours to keep feeding with sight.

Go to bed light. No heavy belly. No sour mouth. No shame review of the day. Sleep comes clean when no hunt wakes at midnight. Mornings begin light because evenings close clean. Clear evenings make clear mornings make clear afternoons. The wheel now turns the other way — toward life.

And if a faint twitch visits in the early days — brief flatness, brief restlessness — welcome it as proof of dying, not sign of need. Small-scale. Brief. Already fading while you live. You will hardly remember its name beside the joy. Celebrate it. Pity it. Forget it. It has no feeder now, and what has no feeder starves.

WHEN THE PICTURE GLOWS

It will. Pictures glow for a while. That is mind, not body. That is memory replay, not order.

A wrapper rustles on a screen. A smell drifts from a bakery door. An old hour strikes — three in the afternoon, ten at night. A friend laughs with cake. A bad day bruises. A good day shines and offers.

Hear each script for what it is and pre-label it now, so when it sounds later it sounds like the con, never like your wisdom.

“I deserve one. I've been good.” Goodness never needed coating. Goodness shines brighter clear. Was pride ever found in a smear? Or was pride found in a clear day lived whole?

“I need sugary somethings to get me through.” Through what? Through a low the last dose dug? Clear head gets through faster than fog. Did the lift ever carry you past four, or did it drop you by four with a louder want?

“Celebrating with chocolate is love.” Love lives in people, light, talk, hunger met. The dose only ever sneaked a ride. Did the candles burn brighter for icing, or for faces round them?

“Just one cannot hurt, to prove I am free.” Freedom proves itself by forgetting, not by scheduling. One re-lights preciousness while belief is tempted back. Does a free eater need to kiss the cage to prove the door is open?

Ask of each whisper, with eyes open.

Will this dose fix the quarrel or sour the stomach while the quarrel still stands? Will this bar pay the bill or add fog to worry about the bill? Will this slice add love to the room or noise to attention, fog to head, ache to belly, hunt to midnight?

You know. We all know once comfort is weighed.

Do not suppress the picture. You cannot not think of a pink beast by forbidding it. Let the picture come and attach joy to it. Picture, then shout. Glow, then laugh. Memory, then relief. Each time the old glow fires your new joy, the glow weakens and joy strengthens, till joy fires by itself faster than glow. A smell drifts and the heart sings. An old hour strikes and the heart answers with ease.

That is guarding the belief. Not watching crumbs with fear. Not holding tight with white knuckles. Guarding sight with joy. Sight held, behaviour follows. You never reopen a settled question. You rejoice that it is settled.

IF A CRUMB SLIPS

Live wide and easy, not tight and watchful.

Food in the world carries hidden spoons — sauce sweetened, bread brushed, bite mistaken in a hurry. If a crumb slips in unplanned, smile lightly and move on. The body absorbs a bump. No doom. No restart. No failure machinery. A rumble strip warns the driver to steer steady; it does not mean the car crashed. Learn, laugh, walk on.

Only a planned exception matters, because only a planned exception revives the belief. Guard the belief with joy, and crumbs cannot cage you. A deliberate swerve is never licensed by a bump. Got away with it never means do it again.

See how light this makes you? Not a tightrope walker counting steps. A free eater on a wide road, eyes on faces and hills, not on crumbs. Pity the voice that says one slip proves you blew it. That voice is the con selling all-or-nothing shame to turn a bump into a binge. We do not buy it.

You are not fragile. You are free. A free eater does not fall from freedom by accident. Freedom falls only if sight is thrown away on purpose — and why would you throw away marvellous clarity for cloying fog you have tasted true?

Walk on. Eat hungry. Stop satisfied. Enjoy real food. Forget packets for hours. That forgetting is the proof. That ease with morning, shop, table, sofa is the whole method working while you live.

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

Stand in that now. Behind you, years of queue and hunt and fog. Before you, ordinary days — dawn kitchens, noon aisles, shared tables, whole evenings — lived clear. No decision left to make. No debate left to hold. No tomorrow left to wait for.

Rejoice you are free. Celebrate you are free. Get on with enjoying your life.

Happy to be free, pitying trapped eaters, never reopening, inhabiting mornings, shops and meals with ease — whenever sweet memory crosses the mind, relief answers.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

**SUMMARY**

- Mornings begin light and quiet, with hunger clean and attention whole.
- Shops are walked calmly, with bright packs seen as noise and real food chosen with joy.
- Meals shared hungry and stopped satisfied leave clarity and laughter unbroken.
- Other eaters still queuing are pitied with warmth, never envied and never lectured.
- A passing sweet picture triggers joyful relief, not debate or mourning.
- An accidental crumb is smiled at and forgotten, while a planned exception is never invited.
- Evenings close whole and sleep comes clean, with ordinary life unchanged except freedom.
