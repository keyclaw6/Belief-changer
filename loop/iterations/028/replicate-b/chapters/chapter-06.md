Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — Morning market air and wet greens, paper-wrapped bread still warm, the kitchen with steam and salt, a plain favourite meal eaten hungry, the stopped plate, the fruit bowl in daylight

*Hunger met with real food and left at satisfied tastes like all the joy you thought only sweets could give.*

THE FUEL LIE IS DEAD

You walk in here a different eater from the one who walked into the last room.

In the last room you sat at the desk at half past two and watched the whole afternoon tell the truth. The drawer. The bright packet. The ten-minute buzz. The four o'clock fog and the second reach. You saw pain put there by the last dose, brief hush when fed, tighter pinch behind it. You took the credit back from the packet and gave it to yourself where it belonged. You began with elation, not dread.

Keep that sight. You will need it here, because underneath the fuel story there has waited a quieter fear all along.

I know this fear because I carried it for years. We all did.

We told ourselves that without sweet hits eating would turn grey. Plain food would be dull. Meals would be duty. Fruit would be a poor cousin. Dessert was love, and without dessert what is left but chewing and swallowing?

Do you feel that still? Good. Bring it close. Bring your favourite plain meal and your emptiest evening. We will eat together once, hungry, with attention, and you will feel what your body knew before the grazing years began.

The fact is, hunger and satisfaction were never the enemy. They are the flavour.

I speak as one who lived the loop. Grazing through mornings that should have been bright. Picking through afternoons. Arriving at dinner already half-full and half-empty at once. Calling the box love and the meal fuel and getting both backwards. Praising the shout and missing the hum. I thought joy lived in the packet because I never let hunger speak loudly enough to be heard.

We all did that. We ate full and felt empty and blamed the meal.

You arrive here forgiven and clear-eyed. You leave here as an eater at home in your own body.

THE GREY PLATE FEAR

Let me voice the thought exactly as it comes at night.

“But I love food because of sweets. Take away the pudding, the chocolate after, the biscuit with tea, and what is left? A grey plate. Chewing. Duty. I will eat like a machine and feel like one.”

“I call dessert love. My evening sweet is the kind part of the day. The meal is just the queue for it.”

I hear you. There is no shame in that thought. It was printed into us bite by bite. Sweet after savoury. A treat when you feel you deserve one. Celebrating with chocolate. The sweet placed at the end like a prize, so the meal learned to bow to it.

Ask your life a cleaner question. Not, can I bear meals without sweets? But, when did meals stop being enough?

Think back. Before the daily grazing, a meal eaten hungry was an event. Steam. Smell. First bite that made the eyes close. Second bite slower. Talk around the table. Bread torn and gone. Hunger quieting bite by bite until the body said, done, and the plate could sit. No clock-watch. No hum underneath. Satisfaction that registered and freed attention for the evening.

What changed was not the meal. What changed was the hum laid over it.

Full stomach, empty hum. Meal eaten, drawer still glowing. Enough never landing as enough. We called that proof the meal was dull. It was proof the loop was loud.

When did a plain meal last taste truly bright to you? On holiday, perhaps. After a long walk, starving, a simple plate put down and eaten with both hands. No box in the cupboard calling. No packet voice. Did that taste grey? Or did it taste marvellous, wonderful, complete?

And when did a box eaten full ever satisfy? Thursday night, the permitted sliver that became the evening, wrapper rustle, hand moving, regret arriving before swallowing ends. Did that taste like love? Or did it taste hurried, coated, half-noticed, gone?

There is only one honest reading. The grey was never in the plain food. The grey was in the eating that could never stop.

It was hunger unheard that made food joyless, not hunger honoured.

Feel what that reversal does. It lifts blame off the meal and off you in one move. You were not dull of palate. You were drowned in hits, so the palate could never clear. Clear the hits and the palate clears with it.

I want you to hold that clearance as a promise while we go deeper. You have not lost taste. You have not lost love of eating. You have lost quiet, and quiet is returning.

Think of the dearest meals of your life. I will wager they were not the sweetest. They were the hungriest. The soup after skating. The bread after swimming. The late supper after a wedding dance. The picnic eaten cross-legged on grass with salt on the fingers. Hunger made those meals sing. No glaze made them sing. The body made them sing.

Why would the body stop singing now? It would not. It was only shouted over.

HUNGER SPEAKS PLAINLY

Hunger is not an emergency. Hunger is an invitation.

Real hunger arrives simply. A hollow warmth low down. Attention sharpening toward smell. Food sounding good — not one packet sounding urgent, but food sounding welcome. The mouth watering at bread, at soup, at fruit. Patience still present. Joy still possible.

We learned to misread every signal as orders for BAD SUGAR. The I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. The afternoon thinning. The evening restlessness. We called each one proof the body wanted sweets. The body wanted eating. The loop shouted a brand name over the top.

Listen again with clean ears.

A body that has eaten real meals and slept and moved speaks plainly and stops at satisfied. It asks. It receives. It rests. It asks again hours later. That rhythm is older than any packet. It led you before you could read. It leads still on days the loop loosens — those bright stretches when hours pass without thought of sweets because work or play held you, and then hunger arrives clear as a bell and food tastes vivid.

Who taught you to fear that bell? Who taught you to answer it before it rings, with grazing that keeps it forever half-muffled?

The fact is your body makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food, so life without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. Bread, vegetables, fruit, plain meals — the ordinary menu carries what the machine needs to hum along. If you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar or appetite, keep your clinician in charge and use this book for the belief only.

Hear the relief inside that fact. You are not removing fuel. You are removing noise. Hunger can speak. Satisfaction can register. Energy returns as steady brightness underneath, not shout for ten minutes and bill for sixty.

Do you say, but I never feel true hunger now, only cravings? Of course. We all lost the signal under the grazing. Grazing keeps the stomach forever half-full and the nerves forever half-hungry. No emptiness to answer. No fullness to land in. Only the loop. Let meals be meals and the signal returns within days. Not by rule. Not by clock. By need met and left alone.

Learn the language again the way you learned it first — by feeling.

Morning hunger is light and open. It likes bread and fruit and warmth. Midday hunger is broader, readier for weight and salt. Evening hunger is tired and tender, glad of heat and company. Night hunger, true night hunger, is rare when days were fed well. What calls at ten o'clock most nights is not hunger. It is habit wearing hunger's coat. You will know the difference within days once meals satisfy, because true hunger welcomes food and habit welcomes only one packet.

Let me ask you three plain questions, and answer from your own mouth.

First. When you are truly hungry after work or play or good walking, does plain food taste dull or vivid?

Second. When you are already full and the packet calls, does the first bite taste vivid for long, or hurried and coated from the start?

Third. Which of those two states ever left you warm and done, and which ever left you hunting?

You know. We all know. Hunger flavours food. Fullness hunted by hits flattens it.

Hunger was the flavour all along.

A MARKET MORNING AND A PLAIN MEAL

Come with me to prove it, not as theory but as doing.

Morning market. Cold air and shouting stalls. Greens stacked high and wet. Roots earthy in crates. Apples with scent still on the skin. Bread in paper, warm through the paper, crust cracking as you squeeze. Nothing in bright packets shouting. Everything smelling of itself.

You buy simply. Vegetables for the pan. Fresh bread. Fruit for after. No counting. No lecture. No good and bad columns. Only what looks alive and what you like.

Walk home slowly with the bag heavy on the arm. Notice how appetite sharpens in cold air. Notice how smell wakes before taste. Notice how the body leans forward a little toward lunch. That lean is health. That lean is the oldest guidance you own. No packet taught it. No advert installed it. It was there before words.

Home. Hunger awake because you let it wake. Not starved. Not stuffed. Ready.

You cook plainly. Vegetables in hot oil, salt, colour brightening. Bread cut thick. Water on the table. Fruit washed and set out. Steam rising. Smell filling the kitchen. Hands a little quick with hunger. That quickness is joy arriving, not an emergency.

Sit. First bite hungry.

Notice. Really notice. The crunch that yields. The heat that spreads. The salt that lifts. The bread that tears and soaks. Chew slower than the loop ever let you. Taste arrives in waves where grazing allowed only the first shout. Second bite slower still. Shoulders dropping. Breath deepening. Talk if company is there — company flavours food more than any coating ever did.

Halfway, pause. Where is attention? Not in the drawer. In the plate. In the warmth moving outward. In hunger quieting from sharp to soft.

Eat until satisfied. Not stuffed. Not cleared. Satisfied — that warm, easy done-ness when hunger goes quiet and pleasure turns from keen to calm. Put the fork down while food still tastes good. Leave some. Push the plate a little away. Feel what follows. Not the coated mouth and the hunt. Warmth. Clarity. Done.

Then fruit, if wanted. Bright, cold, juicy, sweetness that belongs to itself, eaten for itself, not as a prize for virtue. Notice how different this sweetness feels from a hit. It does not shout and bill. It refreshes and leaves. It can be loved without being chased.

Sit a minute longer with the stopped plate. Feel the novelty. No urge to clear. No urge to follow with a packet. No bargain with tomorrow. Only the quiet pride of an eater who heard and answered and rested. That quiet is the natural state. That quiet is what the grazing years stole and what one hungry meal returns.

This is the sentence that carries the whole room.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read it as permission, not as rule. Want is allowed. Hunger leads. Satisfaction ends. No punishment after, ever. No being good this week. No paying for pleasure with shame. The eater you were before the packets knew this by nature, like the small child with the peach who stops mid-bite to run off and play.

Feel the credit inversion your mouth demands. Who made that meal marvellous? Not a dose. Hunger made it marvellous. The body made it marvellous. The market, the heat, the hands that cooked, the company at the table made it marvellous. BAD SUGAR only ever sneaked a ride on that marvellousness and claimed the wage.

Take the wage back.

Linger in the kitchen after. Wash the pan with hot water. Put the bread away for tomorrow. Set the fruit where daylight hits it. Notice how little time food now occupies in the mind when hunger was met. No drawer glowing. No evening plan forming. Attention free for work, for talk, for air. That freedom of attention is the first gift of satisfied eating, and it arrives at once, not after weeks of earning.

THE TABLE IS LARGER THAN THE PACKET

There is more flavour in a hungry meal than the loop ever let you count, and counting it now will finish the grey plate fear for good.

Smell first. Real hunger sharpens smell the way morning sharpens air. The bread crust. The hot oil. The green bite of vegetables. The skin of an apple. Each smell arrives clean when the nose is not coated. Grazing coats the nose. Hits shout so loudly the nose stops listening for whispers. Let hunger clear the air and whispers return.

Texture next. The crack then chew of crust. The yield then bite of cooked greens. The juice that runs at the chin with fruit. The loop taught you that pleasure lives in melt and rush — the swift dissolve that hurries swallowing. True pleasure lives longer. It lives in chew and pause and second taste. It asks you to stay, not to hurry.

Temperature next. Heat that spreads from the belly outward. Cold that wakes the mouth after heat. The contrast that makes a simple meal feel composed. Hits arrive at one note, loud and brief. Real meals play chords.

Company next, and greatest. Laughter while chewing. Story between bites. Bread passed hand to hand. Eyes lifting from the plate to faces. The loop ate alone even in crowds — hurried, half-hidden, mind on the next packet while others talked. Hunger met at a table eats with others, fully present, fully fed in two hungers at once.

Ask your memory for proof. Which meals do you still carry years later? The hurried bar at the desk? The box on the sofa while television murmured? Or the table where hunger, warmth, and faces met? Which memory warms the chest now? Which memory do you tell as a story?

You know. We all know. Love never lived in the wrapper. Love lived around the table and the loop claimed the wage.

Feel the widening. Eating without BAD SUGAR is not narrower. It is wider. More smell. More texture. More contrast. More company noticed. More hours free from thought of food. The packet narrowed the world to one shout. Hunger widens it to a landscape.

Do you fear plain cooking will bore you? Plain is not dull. Plain is clear. Salt, heat, oil, bread, fruit — these are not punishments for virtue. They are favourites rediscovered. The palate that was drowned in loud hits needs a few meals to rinse clear, then plain tastes bright again, bright as holiday food, bright as childhood tea after play. Boredom was the fog between hits calling itself appetite. Satisfaction has no boredom in it.

BUT FRUIT IS SUGAR TOO

I hear the clever objection, and I welcome it because it pretends to be logic while protecting the loop.

“But fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Bread turns to glucose. It is all the same. Why draw a line at all?”

It sounds fair. It is the loop talking philosophy to keep its packet.

Answer with your mouth, not with argument. Eat an apple hungry after a walk. Slow. Juicy. Done at one, perhaps two, satisfaction registering clean. Now remember the box eaten full on the sofa. Hurried. Coated. Never landing. Hand moving before decision. One wakes hunger and closes it. The other mutes hunger and multiplies it.

Are those the same experience? Does your body treat them the same? Does one leave you warm and finished and the other leave you restless and hunting?

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined and added free sugars and junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, the doses that ride the loop. Natural sweetness inside whole fruit and vegetables and plain meals belongs to the menu that satisfies. It does not shout. It does not bill. It does not keep the hum alive.

“But I love sweet taste. Will I never love sweetness again?”

You will love sweetness more, because you will taste it clean. Fruit in daylight. Bread crust. Roots sweetened by heat. Milk. The plain sugars of real food, met hungry, heard at satisfied. What fades is not sweetness. What fades is the hunt that drowned sweetness in noise.

Ask again with clear eyes. If fruit sweetness satisfied, why did the loop never let fruit be enough? If all sugars were one, why did one leave you done and the other leave you hunting?

You know. We all know once hunger speaks plainly. One belongs to eating. The other belongs to the trap.

Hear the kindness inside the line. Nothing wholesome is forbidden. No meal is a test. No apple needs apology. The target was never joy. The target was never sweetness itself. The target was the engineered hits that turned hunger into hunt. Leave those hits unfed and sweetness returns as friend, not master.

Do you worry the line will blur at parties and bakeries? The line is not a fence you patrol with fear. The line is understanding you carry with ease. Bread at a table that satisfies belongs to eating. A boxed dose that husters hunger belongs to the loop. Your body will teach the difference quickly once hunger speaks, because one ends in done and the other ends in more. Trust the done. Pity the more.

SATISFACTION IS THE PLEASURE

Satisfaction has a feeling, and you have almost forgotten it, so learn it again the way you learned everything true — by noticing.

It is not fullness bursting. It is hunger going quiet. Keenness turning to calm. Food tasting a little less urgent and life tasting a little more vivid. Eyes lifting from the plate. Attention freeing for talk, for air, for play. Body warm. Head clear. No hum underneath ordering more.

Grazing never let you land there. Each hit promised satisfaction and delivered its opposite — brief hush, louder return. Full stomach, empty signal. Plate cleared, cupboard glowing. We called that proof we were broken eaters. It was proof the signal was jammed.

Let hunger and satisfaction do their old work and the jam clears.

Eat when hungry. Not by the clock. Not by the packet voice. By the hollow warmth and the welcome toward food.

Stop when satisfied. Not when punished. Not when permitted. By the quiet done-ness that says, enough, thank you.

Enjoy real food. Not as duty. Not as virtue. As favourite — noticed, savoured, loved in company, left with pleasure.

Do you fear you will eat too much without rules? Look at who overate. The grazer with rules ate the box after the permitted sliver. The hungry eater without rules stops when calm arrives and runs to life. Rules made food precious. Hunger makes food enough.

I have watched eaters live this within days. Breakfast hungry, eaten slowly, left with ease. Lunch hungry, enjoyed fully, closed clean. No mid-afternoon hunt because midday was met. Evening meal savoured, fruit if wanted, evening free for sofa and talk without rustle and regret. Not perfect. Not measured. Human. The scale of eating shrinks on its own because satisfaction shrinks it. No counting does that. Hearing does that.

Feel how different this is from every diet you ever served. Diets taught you to distrust hunger and to fear satisfaction as failure. They gave you clocks and allowances and I've managed to be good this week as a leash. This book gives you the opposite. Hunger is authority. Satisfaction is success. Pleasure is proof you heard right. There is no leash because there is no trap to guard once hits lose their glow.

And feel how different this is from the grey plate you feared. Grey was eating without hunger to a rule, full yet hunted, joy postponed to a packet that never delivered. Colour is eating with hunger to done-ness, warm and clear, joy present in the bite itself. You feared colour lived in the box. Colour lived in the hearing.

EVENINGS PROVE IT

Take this hearing into the evening that once owned you, and watch the evening change shape.

Old evening. Meal hurried because the sweet called louder. Plate cleared while mind planned pudding. Pudding hurried while mind planned packet. Packet opened with the vow of one. Hand moving. Wrapper speaking. Mouth coated. Heart sinking before swallowing ends. Sofa restless. Television unwatched. Tomorrow promised as ransom. Morning stale.

New evening. Hunger welcomed, not feared. Meal cooked simply and eaten slowly. First bite vivid. Middle slow. End calm. Fruit in daylight's memory, bright and finished. Plate stopped while good. Kitchen closed without bargain. Sofa soft. Body warm. Talk easy. Attention whole. Night clean.

Which evening loved food more? Which evening loved life more? Which evening would you choose if both were offered free tonight?

You know. We all know. Love never lived in the rustle. Love lived in the done.

Try the memory test once more with kindness. Recall a night you ate real food hungry and stopped at satisfied — rare, bright nights on holiday, nights lost in company where no box called. Did you feel deprived? Or did you feel warm, clear, done, almost surprised at how little thought food needed? Recall a night of grazing. Did you feel loved? Or coated, thick, already planning the first hit of morning?

Your life already compared the states. The loop never won except in the ten-minute lie.

Carry that comparison as a quiet verdict you can use for life. Whenever the old glow whispers that plain eating will be grey, answer with your evenings, not with my words. Grey ate from the box. Colour ate hungry to done.

THE BODY THANKS YOU AT ONCE

There is a last joy to name before I give you your instruction, and it arrives faster than you expect.

When meals satisfy, the body thanks you in its own language. Mornings lighter. Mouth clean. Eyes clearer at ten. Hands steadier at noon. Afternoons steadier because midday was met. Temper kinder because hunger is no longer hunt. Sleep deeper because evenings close clean. Not miracles. Not rewards for virtue. The natural baseline returning on its own because noise was removed.

We told ourselves dessert was the kind part of the day. Kindness was hunger met all day. We told ourselves sweet was the bright part of the meal. Brightness was satisfaction heard. Reverse those thank-yous and they land where help lives. Thank your appetite for speaking. Thank your palate for rinsing clear. Thank your hands for cooking plainly. Those thanks are not sentiment. They are accuracy.

Do you say, but I still hear the packet voice at dusk? Of course you do. Years of dusk hits leave an echo for days. Hearing an echo is not obeying an order. An echo fades while you eat, while you talk, while you sleep. It has no power to create hunger. It has only power to remind you that hunger, once met, needs no packet to complete it.

Walk the market again in your mind and feel freedom warming, not as a shout but as a homecoming. Mornings with appetite. Shops without hunt. Meals that begin and end. Evenings that do not dissolve into crumbs and vows. The body trusted. The plate left. Life getting on with itself while food does its quiet work underneath.

You came in fearing eating would turn grey. You leave knowing grey was the loop and colour was hunger met.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**

- Food joy lives in hunger welcomed and satisfaction heard, not in sweet hits.
- The grey plate came from grazing that kept hunger half-muffled and done-ness from landing.
- True hunger feels like welcome toward food and sharpens smell and taste.
- A plain favourite meal with market vegetables, warm bread and fruit tastes bright when hungry and ends in calm.
- Fruit sweetness refreshes and closes clean, while boxed doses hush briefly and call again.
- The body makes what it needs from ordinary meals, so plain eating leaves nothing missing.
- Satisfaction feels like hunger going quiet, eyes lifting, attention freeing for life.
- Company, warmth, texture and smell flavour meals, and the packet only claimed the wage.
