Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — An ordinary kitchen plate at dusk, a biscuit held to lamplight, a solemn vow at the table, an open doorway at dawn

*Tomorrow never frees you; this ordinary sweet seen true frees you now.*

TOMORROW IS THE LAST COSTUME

You have closed the side doors.

No Fridays only. No birthdays only. No drawer for guests that opens at ten for the host. No weaning parade. No polite slice that wakes three days of hunting. You have watched each rule polish the dose and widen the hunt, and you know.

And now, right on schedule, the old voice tries one last costume. It sounds kind. It sounds careful. It sounds like wisdom.

It says, “All right. I see it all. So I will start properly after the holidays. After the birthday. After this box. After a less busy week. Tomorrow will be safer.”

We have all heard that voice. Warm to you, harsh to the lie inside it: that is not wisdom. That is the con talking to buy another week of fog.

We lived years inside that tomorrow. We said next Monday with a full heart on Sunday night. We said after Christmas with a hand already moving toward the tin. We said when work calms down, when the children sleep through, when life feels easier. Did life ever feel easier while the doses kept coming? Did tomorrow ever arrive as tomorrow? Or did tomorrow always arrive as today with a fresh excuse pinned to its chest?

Ask as an investigator, because you are one now.

If waiting made quitting easier, would years of waiting have made you free already? How many tomorrows have you paid for with foggy afternoons, with boxes hidden, with vows restarted? What did delay ever give you except another turn of the wheel?

And if this ordinary sweet truly gave joy, why would you need to quit after it — and if it gives nothing, why feed yourself another day to prove it? What does tomorrow buy except another evening of noise to attention, fog to head, ache to belly, hunt to midnight?

You know. We all know once tomorrow is weighed.

Tomorrow is not caution. Tomorrow is the trap holding its breath. The dose stays precious while you wait for it. The mind rehearses the farewell. The mouth waters at the calendar. Waiting does not cool desire. Waiting heats it.

Think how tomorrow works in practice. You promise a clean start on Monday, so Sunday becomes a farewell feast. You promise freedom after the birthday, so the week before becomes a countdown where every shop window glows brighter. You promise after the holidays, so the holidays become a final binge you hurry through with a heavy belly and a heavy heart, unable to enjoy faces for thinking about wrappers. Does that sound like preparation? Or does that sound like worship stretched over time?

That long pull you felt for years was the tug-of-war of craving and fear.

One rope pulled toward the packet. Fear of missing, fear of emptiness, fear of evenings with nothing sweet to close them. The other rope pulled away from the packet. Fear of fog, fear of teeth, fear of weight, fear of never stopping. Both ropes burned the hands. Both ropes belonged to the same hands that tied them.

See it plain. The craving rope and the fear rope are not two masters. They are one con with two ends. Cut the belief and both ropes fall at once.

There is no safer tomorrow. There is only a precious today stretched into a precious week stretched into a precious year. Freedom never lives in tomorrow. Freedom lives in sight, and sight is now.

If tomorrow truly helped, the years would prove it. They prove the opposite. Every delayed start made the object larger. Every farewell tour made the memory louder. Every date on the calendar made ordinary days feel like waiting rooms. The kindest thing you can do for yourself is refuse another waiting room.

ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS

I speak to you now as one escaped eater to one soon-free eater, with total certainty and total warmth.

You do not need more time. You need one clear look and one solemn vow. If you see that BAD SUGAR does nothing for joy and plenty to attention, belly, mood, teeth, wallet, hours — if you see that the lift was relief of a low the last dose made — if you see that one keeps the hunt alive and Fridays make the hunt sacred — then you are ready.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit. A lightness in the chest. An impatience to be done. A quiet eagerness that says, let me finish this and get on with enjoying my life.

Hear your own doubt speak, and let me answer it with love.

“But what if I fail again?” You never failed. The wrong method failed you by leaving the object precious and asking your hands to fight your belief. Hands always lose that fight. Belief always wins. Change the belief and hands have nothing to fight. You were not weak. You were misled, and misled people do not need scolding. They need sight.

“But what if evenings feel empty?” Evenings felt empty because doses broke them in half — buzz then fog, hunt then regret. Plain evenings eaten hungry, laughed through whole, closed clean, are not empty. They are full. Think of talk that runs unbroken. Think of a book that holds attention to the last page. Think of sleep that comes clean because no hunt woke at ten.

“But what if the grumble comes?” The grumble is tiny. I tell you flat, to calm you, never to frighten you: the physical echo is mild and well-defined but small, a days-long hump that passes, not a lifelong battle. A faint twitch, a brief flatness, a passing thought of something sweet, then gone. Small-scale. Brief. Already dying from the moment you stop feeding it. You will hardly notice it beside the joy. In two to five days it fades to nothing, and what remains is not effort but relief.

“But is it really the right moment, with so much going on?” When was life ever quiet while you waited? Work never calmed. Children never stopped needing. Bills never paused. The trap always found a busy week to hide behind. A clear head meets busy weeks better than a foggy head. You do not need calm to get free. You get free to meet noise clearly.

Remember you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

What is there to lose? Fog? Hunt? Bargaining? Boxes hidden? Mornings that start heavy? Afternoons that sag at three and demand sugary somethings to get me through? Nights that end with regret before swallowing ends? What is there to gain? Clear heads. Hunger that arrives cleanly and leaves cleanly after plain food enjoyed hungry. Laughter unbroken by rustle. Shops passed without pull. Pride that hums quietly all day. Mornings that begin light.

If you feel that eagerness, the door is open. If you do not yet feel it — if some picture still glows, if some Friday still whispers, if some fear still grips — do not force yourself with grit. Read this book again slowly, from the start, with an open mind, and let sight ripen. Freedom cannot be forced. It can only be seen. When sight is whole, crossing is joy.

I do not ask you to trust my strong words. I ask you to trust your own kitchens, your own desks at three, your own boxes emptied before films ended. Evidence you already own beats any lecture. You have lived the lift that became the low. You have lived the one that became the box. You have lived the Friday that ate the week.

Are you ready to finish this easily, immediately and permanently?

That triad is not a boast. It is the nature of sight. Easily because there is no loss to mourn. Immediately because freedom is conferred by vow, not earned by time. Permanently because when a con is seen, it cannot un-see itself.

TASTE IT TRUE

Then do this. Not as a test. Not as an experiment. Not as a laboratory tasting with notebook and timer. As the last ordinary instance, taken normally, in your ordinary place, at your ordinary hour.

Fetch your ordinary sweet. The biscuit you always reach for. The bar you keep for evenings. The dessert you call a treat. Take it at the table, in lamplight, without rush, without guests to perform for, without film to hide behind. Just you and the object, face to face at last.

Now pay attention as you never allowed yourself to pay attention while hunting.

Look at it first. Small. Brown. Glossy with coating. Too sweet a smell that once pulled and now, looked at straight, smells cloying. Notice how much of the pull was wrapper, hour, habit, picture — how little was the thing itself. Notice the size of it against the size of the fuss you made over it for years. Notice how ordinary it looks when no music plays and no laughter covers it.

Take the first bite normally. Let it melt. Follow it with your full mind.

Is it marvellous? Or is it sickly, gritty, over-sweet, sticking to teeth, flooding then fading within seconds? Does it lift you? Or does it coat the tongue, dull the taste buds, leave a sour film and a louder want for a second bite to recover the first? Stay with the aftertaste. That sticky film on the molars. That thick sweetness on the tongue that water does not wash clean. That faint quease behind the breastbone.

Take the second bite if you take sweets in twos. Watch the glow thin. The first bite promised paradise. The second chases the first. The third chases the second. Each bite less than the bite before. Is that pleasure? Or is that a mouth chasing a memory that never lived in the object? Watch how fast delight turns to duty — chew, swallow, reach — and how little delight was ever there.

Feel the belly. Feel the head. A faint nausea under the sweetness. A faint fog creeping behind the eyes. A twitch in the hands toward more, not from delight but from emptiness. That empty urging you once read as need — see it now as the echo of the last dose, not the voice of your nature. Real hunger is clean and open and glad of food. This is narrow and twitchy and glad of nothing.

We did this blind for years. We ate fast, eyes on screens, minds on worries, hands moving before decision. We never tasted. We only hunted and swallowed and reached. Tasted true, the object stands revealed. Cloying. Noisy. Small. Doing plenty to mouth, belly, head, mood — doing nothing for joy, love, rest, energy.

Hold it to lamplight after two bites. Look at the smear. Look at the crumbs. This is the great reward? This sticky smear that leaves fog and want? Does a free eater envy this? Does a child eating a peach hungry, stopping mid-bite to run off laughing, miss this? Does a hungry eater with plain bread and ripe fruit at a sunlit table trade places with you for this film on the teeth?

Ask, with mouth still full, and only honest answers will do.

Did this bite add love to the room? Did this bite add rest to your body? Did this bite add clarity to your mind? Or did the room, the rest, the clarity all dim a little the moment the sugar touched the tongue? Did the quiet of the evening deepen or did the noise rise?

You know. The scene gives. The dose takes. It was always the other way around.

Let disgust rise naturally. Not forced. Not whipped up. Just the quiet recognition: I have been bowing to this? I have been paying fog and hunt and money and hours for this cloying smear? I have been letting this small brown smear decide when evenings end?

Good. Keep that sight. That sight is freedom ripening.

Now, while the taste still sits true on the tongue, speak the vow. Solemn. Quiet. Joyful. Not to me. To yourself, for life.

Say: I see it does nothing for me. I am done feeding it. I am free.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have kept an open mind. You carried on normally while you read. You judged only by what it does for you. You never blamed yourself again. You began with elation, not dread. You trusted hunger and satisfaction. You saw the grumble as dying. You ignored voices that rebuild the con. You ignored tales of struggle by the wrong method. You kept no special sweet. Now take your last ordinary treat and close it with joy.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

Push the plate away lightly. Stand. Breathe. Smile with your whole chest.

You are free as of now. Not after three days. Not after the hump passes. Not after some test proves it. Now. The belief died. The hunt has no axle. The tiny echo starving in the background has no feeder.

This ends easily, immediately and permanently.

Feel relief flood in. Marvellous lightness. Wonderful clarity. The weight off shoulders you carried so long you forgot its weight till it lifted. Mornings ahead clear. Shops ahead quiet. Evenings ahead whole. Hunger ahead honest. Meals ahead favourite. Hours ahead your own.

And now I give you the thought you will keep forever. Not as a technique. Not as a shield to hold with effort. As the natural shout of a freed heart whenever memory glows.

For years, when a sweet picture crossed your mind, the old script whispered, oh, that was good, I miss that, just one would be lovely. That script was never your reasoning. It was the con replaying. You need a new script, joyful and true, that fires by itself.

Think of a diver surfacing. Think of hands untied. Think of morning air after a stuffy room. When the picture of foil, of cake under glass, of chocolate at dim lights crosses your mind — in a shop, at a birthday, on a screen — do not argue. Do not mourn. Do not lecture yourself. Rejoice at a dead enemy. Let the heart shout what the mind knows.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it now, out loud if you can, silently if eyes watch. Feel how true it sits. Brilliant because escape is brilliant. Free because you are. No loss inside it. No longing inside it. Only pity for trapped eaters still queuing, and relief you no longer queue.

Whenever sugar crosses your mind for the rest of your days, think that. At first you will think it deliberately, smiling. Soon it will fire by itself, faster than the old glow. A wrapper rustles behind you and the heart laughs. A smell drifts from a bakery and the heart sings. An old hour strikes — three in the afternoon, ten at night — and the heart answers with joy. That is not suppression. That is reframe. The thought becomes the trigger for relief.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Keep it. It is yours. It is the last line of this struggle and the first line of ordinary joy.

THE TWO DOORS THAT NEVER OPEN

I warn you now, as an escaped eater who loves you, about the two doors that will rattle. Not to frighten you. To pre-label them, so when they rattle they sound like the con, never like your own wisdom. Finish in relief: neither door can hold you once seen.

The first door knocks on a bad day.

Bills. Quarrel. Sleepless night. Heavy rain on the windows. The old script slides in soft: you deserve comfort, one sweet will soothe, just this once to get through. You have earned a treat when you feel you deserve one. You need sugary somethings to get me through.

Hear it for what it is. The rescuer offering to push you in again. We all know that comfort. First bite, brief hush. Second bite, already less. Third bite, chasing. Then fog on top of trouble, hunt on top of worry, regret on top of tiredness. Did the dose ever fix the quarrel? Did it ever pay the bill? Did it ever give sleep? Or did it do plenty to the evening while doing nothing for the pain?

Ask on that bad evening, with eyes open. Will a biscuit soften the quarrel or will it sour the stomach while the quarrel still stands? Will a bar pay the bill or will it add fog to the worry about the bill? Will a binge give rest or will it steal sleep and hand you a heavy dawn on top of a hard day?

You know. We all know once comfort is weighed.

On a bad day, eat when hungry. Rest when tired. Speak when hurt. Laugh when you can. Drink water. Step outside for air. Live the trouble straight, with clear head and full heart, and watch trouble pass faster without fog glued to it. Pity the voice that offers sweets for sorrow. It sells ditch-water for thirst. Trouble halves when met sober. It doubles when met sugared.

The second door whispers on a good day.

Sun. Laughter. Birthday candles. Cinema lights low. Someone offers cake with love. The script says, just one cannot hurt, one to celebrate, one to be polite, one to prove I am free. Celebrating with chocolate, the old line runs, is love.

Hear it for what it is. The polite invitation to re-cage. We all know that one. One re-lights preciousness while belief is tempted back. One wakes the hunt for three days. One turns a clear evening into bargaining till midnight. Does love live in the slice? Does the film need the bar? Does politeness require fog? Or does joy live in people, light, talk, hunger met — with the dose only ever sneaking a ride?

Ask on that bright day, with heart full. Did the laughter need icing to be laughter? Did the friend need frosting to be loved? Did the sunset need syrup to be beautiful? Or was sweetness already whole in light, company, hunger satisfied by real food enjoyed as favourite?

On a good day, celebrate with your whole heart. Sing. Laugh. Hug. Eat hungry and stop satisfied and enjoy real food as favourite. Let empty hands prove ease. Freedom proves itself by forgetting — hours in work and laughter with no thought of packets. A clear eater does not prove ease by scheduling doses.

If a crumb slips in unplanned — a hidden spoon in sauce, a mistaken bite — smile lightly and move on. The body absorbs a bump. No doom. No restart. No failure machinery. Only a planned exception matters, because only a planned exception revives the belief. Guard the belief with joy, and behaviour follows.

Do you see how light this makes you? Not a tightrope walker counting steps. A free eater on a wide road, eyes on faces and hills, not on crumbs. A rumble, if it comes, is a lesson to slow down, never proof you have blown it. And a deliberate swerve is never licensed by the bump. Got away with it never means do it again.

Your wobble, if it comes, is pacing by the open cage from habit, nothing more — memory, not hunger, echo, not need. Smile at it. Think your joyful thought. Walk on into ordinary days.

REJOICE AT ONCE

So finish here, at the table, with plate pushed away and heart light.

You waited long enough. You bargained long enough. You counted tomorrows long enough. The wait is over. The sight is whole. The vow is spoken.

Stand in the doorway this book opened. Behind you, the table with the smeared plate, the lamplight, the cloying smell already fading. Before you, dawn air, clear mornings, honest hunger, meals savoured, shops passed calmly, evenings closed clean, laughter unbroken. No ceremony to maintain. No calendar to watch. No drawer to guard.

Rejoice you are free. Celebrate you are free. Get on with enjoying your life with clear mornings, honest hunger, meals savoured, shops passed calmly, evenings closed clean, laughter unbroken.

Happy to be free — not merely free, but glad, proud, excited, relieved. That emotion is the goal, and it is yours now.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

**SUMMARY**

- Waiting never made stopping easier, it kept the dose precious and heated the hunt.
- The last ordinary sweet tasted with full attention shows cloying smallness, not pleasure.
- Freedom arrives by solemn vow in this moment, not after days of proving.
- The tiny physical echo passes in days and needs no dread or battle.
- A bad day never needs a rescue dose and a good day never needs a single exception.
- A passing sweet thought now triggers joyful relief and pity for trapped eaters.
