Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The Saturday market in morning light, the warm loaf torn open at the table, the plain favourite meal eaten hungry, the fruit bowl in late sun, the quiet belly that says enough

*Real hunger met by real food is the feast itself — BAD SUGAR never fed it, it only talked over it.*

### THE GREY PLATE FEAR

You arrive here with clear eyes about the afternoon.

You have watched the bar borrow brightness for minutes and bill fog for hours. You have watched the buzz fade by the second bite and the hand hurry for quiet rather than pleasure. You know the lift makes the low. That seeing stays. I take nothing from it.

And still, underneath that clear seeing, a soft fear waits that has nothing to do with energy.

It speaks in the evening when you picture life without doses.

“Without something sweet, eating will be grey.”

“I love food. Dessert is love. Take the sweet away and joy goes with it.”

“Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters.”

I hear you. We all whispered that. I whispered it for years while I called dessert the best part of the day and pitied in advance the dull plates I imagined waiting for me. Dry chewing. Dutiful vegetables. A life of saying no at the table while others smiled over pudding.

The fact is the reverse. The grey you fear from plain food is the grey BAD SUGAR laid over plain food. Lift the noise and eating brightens. Hunger returns as appetite. Satisfaction returns as pleasure. Real food stands forward as favourite, noticed, savoured, enough.

Do you want to love eating for the rest of your days? Of course you do. That love is not at risk here. That love is what we are coming back to.

This hour we do not fight. We eat. We inhabit what was yours before the loop and what waits to be yours the moment the loop stops taxing it.

Sit with me at a real table, hungry, with real food, and watch what your body already knows how to do without a lesson from me.

I lay no menu on you. I count nothing. I weigh nothing. I moralize no plate. Warm to you always. Harsh only to the talk that narrowed your joy to a wrapper and called the narrowing love.

Ask from your own mouth, not from wrappers, and let your mouth answer.

When did plain food last taste wonderful because you were truly hungry? When did you stop at enough with pleasure and turn gladly to the rest of life? When did a meal shared with people you love leave you eased and bright without a sweet finish laid on top of fullness?

Hold those hours. We are going back to them, not forward into duty. You are not losing the feast. You are walking back into it.

### THE MARKET MORNING

Come with me on a Saturday morning, air cool, baskets high, hunger clean from sleep.

No hum under the ribs. No drawer calling. No clock telling the belly what to feel. The belly speaks plainly, an open hollow, a willingness. Mouth waters at bread smell from the bakery door. Stomach growls once, honest, unhurried. Feet move a little quicker toward colour.

Tomatoes still warm from the van. Dark green leaves with water on them. Peppers glossy as paint. Apples stacked red and gold, skins tight, scent sharp when lifted. A melon heavy as a baby. Fresh bread with a crust that crackles under the thumb, steam rising when torn. Cheese with a rind that smells of cellars. Eggs brown and speckled. Herbs that perfume the hands when bruised.

You lift, you smell, you choose. Hunger chooses with you. Eyes brighten at what hunger wants. Hands want to touch. Nose wants to follow. That brightening is appetite, alive, unforced. No wrapper glows. No slogan speaks. The body speaks.

We were taught to call this dull. Plain shopping. Plain cooking. Plain eating. We repeated that language while our mouths watered at the market. Look at the lie in daylight. Is there anything dull in a hungry person among real food? Cheeks lift. Talk quickens. Steps quicken. Children pull at sleeves and point. Old men argue about melons. The whole hall hums with wanting that needs no lesson.

That wanting is yours by nature. It was buried, not broken.

Think how we shopped and ate in the loop on such mornings. Sweet coffee on the run. Sweet cereal eaten standing. Juice gulped from a carton. A biscuit at eleven to quiet the hollow. Hunger never allowed to arrive clean. Taste never allowed to wake clean. The tongue coated. The belly full yet listening. The market colours seen through glass, admired but not wanted, because want had been narrowed to one narrow sweet note and everything else sounded faint beside it.

Hunger narrowed cannot enjoy. A radio stuck on one station cannot play music.

Let hunger arrive today and the stations return.

Walk the aisles hungry and notice how much you notice. The crack in the loaf where steam escapes. The bloom on grapes. The green bite of an onion freshly cut. The salt crystals on butter. The perfume of a ripe pear through skin. The loop dulled all this by coating the tongue and hurrying the hand. Hunger cleans the tongue. Attention slows the hand. Flavour returns in layers, first bright, then deep, then rich, then enough.

I speak as one who lived both mornings. Looped mornings ran on hurry. Mouth sweet by nine, belly vague by ten, eyes already listening for eleven. Market mornings now run on appetite. Belly hollow, nose open, eyes bright, hands glad to carry weight home. Same market. Same nose. Same belly. Different master.

Which morning feels like love of food? Which morning feels grey?

There is only one honest answer. The grey was never in the vegetables. The grey was in the coating that kept vegetables faint.

Your body makes what it needs for blood and brain from plain meals, so a life without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing in fuel. I speak only of belief here, and if you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar your clinician leads. For the belief the point stands flat and joyful: hunger and satisfaction are a complete system. They need no sweet fix to work. They need only to be heard.

So let hunger come. Let it hollow. Let it sharpen. Let it choose. Appetite is not an enemy to manage. Appetite is the invitation to the feast.

### HUNGER, THE HONEST SIGNAL

Learn hunger again, because the loop taught a false hunger and called it need.

True hunger arrives open. It grows slowly. It waits without panic. It welcomes many foods. Bread will do. Soup will do. Vegetables will do. Fruit will do. The belly feels empty and willing. The mouth waters at savoury as gladly as sweet. The head stays clear. Patience holds. You can chop, you can talk, you can walk home with bags and hunger walks with you, bright, unhurried, growing riper.

False hunger arrives narrow. It comes sudden, urgent, twitchy, clock-bound. It wants one note and one note only. It will not wait. It will not take bread. It will not take soup. It argues, it snaps, it says now or nothing. The belly may be full while this hunger shouts. The mouth may be coated while this hunger begs for more coating. The head feels fogged and the hands feel hurried.

We called both hunger. They are not the same.

One is the body asking for food. The other is the loop asking for a dose. One ends at eating. The other renews at feeding.

Ask from hours you have lived a thousand times.

When you woke hungry after sleep and ate a real breakfast slowly, what happened? Hunger quieted. Satisfaction came. Attention left food gladly. Hands turned to the day. Brightness held steady without spike, without bill to pay. No rustle followed you from the table. No mental drawer hummed.

When you ate sweet on a full belly at three, at nine, at midnight, what happened? Want renewed at feeding. Mouth coated, belly tight, mind still listening. Full yet reaching. Fed yet hunting. Bright for minutes, then dimmer, then louder want. That hunting we misread as love of food. It was noise over food.

Which signal feeds? Which signal taxes?

True hunger honours all food. False hunger honours only the hit that keeps the loop oiled. True hunger ends in ease. False hunger ends in chase.

I want you to feel the difference in your own skin today, not as theory but as lived hours.

Wait till the belly hollows truly. Notice the openness. Notice the patience. Notice how many foods sound good. That breadth is the signature of honesty. Narrowness is the signature of the loop. Breadth says feed me. Narrowness says feed the noise.

You do not need a new skill to tell them apart. You told them apart for years before the loop taught hurry. You tell them apart still on bright days when meals are plain and hunger is allowed to speak first. The body remembers. Feet remember walking after tight shoes are left off. Bellies remember hunger after noise is left off.

Trust what hollows and waits and welcomes. Smile at what snaps and narrows and hurries. One is you. One is the echo of doses.

Hunger enjoyed is feast enough. Hunger obeyed leaves brightness steady into afternoon without ticket or drawer. Let hunger lead and it will lead you straight to pleasure.

### EATEN HUNGRY

Now the table. Midday. Hunger ripe.

A plain favourite — market vegetables roasted till edges crisp, fresh bread, a little cheese, fruit after. Nothing staged. Nothing counted. Nothing earned. Hunger at the elbow, company near or quiet of your own, time enough to notice.

First bite hungry. Oh, the noise hunger makes turn to music. The crunch of crust. The oil on the tongue. The sweet of pepper when truly tasted. The salt lifting everything. Chewing slows you without effort. Shoulders drop. Breath deepens. Talk softens between mouthfuls. Eyes close half a second at a good bite. Hands pause, then return with pleasure, not hurry.

The second bite tastes deeper than the first. The third deeper still. Hunger seasons better than any jar. A plain onion tastes sweet when hunger is clean. A plain tomato tastes rich. Bread tastes wonderful torn and chewed slowly, steam and crumb and crust in one mouthful. No sauce has to persuade. No sugar has to brighten. Brightness is already there because hunger brought it.

Notice how slow you go without trying to go slow. No rules on the wall. No chewing count. No lecture in the head. Hunger sets the pace. The body chews, pauses, breathes, returns. Talk fits between bites without hurry. Laughter fits. Quiet fits. The meal holds the table without shouting for more.

Second helping, perhaps, because hunger still speaks. Taken gladly. Eaten with the same attention. Enjoyed with the same ease. No guilt at the edge. No virtue at the edge. Hunger still open, food still bright, hands still glad.

This is eating as favourite. Not as fuel stop. Not as reward wage. Not as brave choice. As joy practised without effort.

Compare it with the loop meal you know so well. Sweet drink beside the plate. Sweet finish already glowing in the mind before the first bite. First bites hurried to get to the sweet. Vegetables pushed, bread barely tasted, hunger barely consulted, eyes on the pudding. Then sweet on fullness, taste bright for two bites, then hurry, then coating, then fog. Belly full, mind still listening. Table left with rustle in the head.

Which table honoured food? Which table honoured hunger? Which table left you bright?

The plain table leaves you clear-eyed and eased, ready for work or play or rest. The looped table leaves you coated and chased, listening for the next note while the plates are cleared.

You say, “But I love food. I am a food lover. Will a food lover be happy here?”

I answer as a food lover to a food lover. I never loved food in the loop. I loved the first two bites of sweet and hurried the rest. I never tasted meals. I tasted craving with meals beside it. Now bread stops me mid-sentence. Now a peach stops time. Now hunger makes a plain lunch feel like a feast I would cross town for. Love did not shrink when doses left. Love grew, because attention returned and coating lifted.

Eat hungry and you will see. Hunger is the flavour all along. Hunger makes plain wonderful. Hunger makes enough joyful. Hunger makes the table a place you want to linger, not a station you rush through on the way to a hit.

Take your time today. Smell first. First bite slow. Chew. Pause. Let flavour brighten, deepen, then soften. Let hunger tell you when bright has done its work. You will know. The body speaks plainly when noise stops talking over it.

### STOPPED AT SATISFIED

Then a change. Subtle, certain. Stay with it, because your whole fear of grey lives or dies here.

Flavour dims a shade, not from fault in food but from fullness in you. The next bite tastes a touch less bright than the last. Chewing slows further. Stomach says, in its quiet voice, enough. Bread laid down mid-slice without pain. Bowl left with colour in it without grief. Body eased, mind clear, hands still.

That dimming is satisfaction. That laying down is freedom practised at every meal since childhood, like the small child with peach juice on his chin who leaves half the peach and runs to play.

No counting taught that stopping. No guilt enforced it. No strength held the hand back. Hunger met turned to satisfaction obeyed, and joy went with the running, not with the stuffing.

In the loop, that moment rarely arrived. Sweet at the end kept the mouth searching after the belly had spoken. Dessert after fullness. Biscuits after dinner. Grazing after supper though the belly ached. Taste chased past satisfaction till satisfaction itself felt mythical. Then we blamed plain food for failing to satisfy, when satisfaction had been talked over by dose after dose.

Watch the difference clean.

Hunger met by real food satisfies and releases. The hand stops because the body says stop. Attention leaves food gladly for play, work, talk, rest. No rustle follows you from the table. No mental drawer hums. Brightness holds steady, without spike, without fog bill to pay.

A dose never behaves like that. A dose renews want at feeding. It asks louder after the bar than before it. It leaves mouth coated, belly full, mind still listening. Full yet reaching. Fed yet hunting.

Ask three plain questions from your own table, and answer from hours lived.

First. When hunger is clean and food is real, does eating feel grey? Call the market morning. Call the first bite hungry. Call the ease after enough. Was colour missing? Was pleasure missing? Or was pleasure present precisely because no dose talked over it?

Second. When a meal leaves you flat and still listening though full, what came last? Was it hunger met or doses stacked? Was it bread and vegetables noticed, or sweet upon sweet past satisfaction till taste dulled and hands hurried? Which plate taught grey?

Third. Who stopped easily in your memory? The grown eater hungry after honest work who eats, sighs, lays the fork down, and turns gladly to talk. The eater at a long lunch who leaves food shining on the plate and feels only ease. Did either need a lesson in stopping? Did either count, fight, earn? Or did hunger and satisfaction lead, and joy follow?

One honest answer covers all three. Hunger enjoyed ends in satisfaction enjoyed. The grey was never in plain food. The grey was in the chase past enough.

Practise stopping today exactly as satisfaction asks. Notice flavour brighten then soften. Notice the belly ease. Notice the hand glad to rest. Lay food down mid-pleasure, not mid-disgust. Walk away without commentary. Let brightness hold. No punishment follows. No wage is owed. Punishment — skipped meals to earn sweet, guilty hours after sweet, Monday vows, forbidden-must-have rebound — belongs to the old method that kept the loop oiled. Leave it with the loop.

Stopping at enough is not loss. Stopping at enough is the proof the meal worked.

### FRUIT, HUNGER, AND THE HONEST LINE

“But fruit is sugar too,” you say, “so nothing matters. If I must watch every apple, I would rather keep the cake.”

Hear the line drawn clean, because the line frees you.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined sugar with added free sugars with junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals with grazing doses of the same. That is the quit target. That alone.

Natural sweetness inside whole fruit, vegetables, plain meals is not the target. It belongs on your table. It belongs in hunger met. An apple eaten hungry, stopped at satisfied, behaves as food — chewed, noticed, ending. Grapes sour after enough. Melon palls when the belly eases. The body speaks and the hand obeys.

Do you see the difference in your own mouth? Fruit eaten hungry refreshes. Doses eaten full chase. One ends at satisfaction. The other forbids satisfaction. One leaves brightness steady. The other spikes then fogs. To call them the same because both taste sweet is to call rain and flood the same because both are wet.

No counting lives here. No good-food sermon. No bad-food shame. I lay no eating plan on you and I moralize no plate. There is no virtue in an apple and no vice in a cake beyond the trap they do or do not feed. There is only hunger met or hunger talked over, satisfaction obeyed or satisfaction drowned.

The settled permission for this whole book, spoken plainly so you can live it, is this single sentence you already own. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as permission, because it is permission. Hunger leads. Satisfaction ends. Enjoyment lives in between.

You need no new skill to do this. You did it as a small eater before the loop taught hurry. You do it still on bright days when meals are plain and hunger is allowed to speak first. The body remembers.

So practise today exactly as hunger asks. Wait till hungry — truly hollow, truly willing, not clock-bound. Eat what you want with attention — smell first, first bite slow, chew, pause. Notice flavour brighten then soften. Stop when satisfied — flavour dimmed, belly eased, hand glad to rest. Walk away without commentary. Let brightness hold.

If the old thought rises, “I have been good this week, I deserve a treat,” smile at whose voice that is. Goodness needs no payment in fog. Virtue needs no wage that taxes virtue. Hunger paid by food is reward complete. The wage language falls away and joy stays. The room stays. The meal stays. The people stay.

You say, “But dessert is love. The family pudding. The birthday cake. Celebrating with chocolate.”

I ask you gently to separate what the loop glued together.

Was the love in the spoon or in the room? The laughter round the table. The child proud of candles blown. The friend leaned close, shoulder warm. The evening unhurried, faces lit. The hunger met first by the meal, then the sweet laid on top of fullness and praised for the glow the meal and the company and the hunger met had made.

Take the sweet out of that room in memory and replay it. Hunger met. Faces loved. Talk long. Would the room go grey? Would love drain because no sugar coated the end? Or would the meal still satisfy, the company still warm, the night still held?

And take the room out and keep the sweet alone. Late night, alone, standing at the fridge, spoon in a tub, belly already full, eyes a little sore, hurry in the hand, taste gone by the third spoon, shame rising before swallow ends. Is that love? Is that feast? Does the spoon comfort or does it chase?

There is only one honest reading. The meal gave. The company gave. Hunger met gave. The dose rode along and claimed the credit.

It was you that enjoyed, not the hit. It was the hunger, the food, the people. The sweet was only ever sneaking a ride.

That honesty takes nothing from celebration. Celebration stays. Laughter stays. Love stays. Pudding memory stays as room memory, warmer now because no chase talked over it. You will enjoy sweet occasions more without doses, not less, because you will taste hunger, food and people clean.

### THE TABLE THAT WAITS

I promise you as one who lived both tables.

The loop table: hurry, rustle, second reach, taste fading fast, belly full, mind still hunting, evening ending dim behind the eyes, cloud behind tasks, never eased by feeding. That state you know. That state ends when belief ends.

The free table: hunger clean, food noticed, company tasted with food, satisfaction obeyed with pleasure, hands free after, mind free after, brightness steady into afternoon without ticket or drawer.

Nothing is lost moving from one to the other. Everything crowded out returns. Morning appetite. Midday relish. Evening ease. Fruit bright as itself. Bread wonderful as itself. Meals remembered. People met across food rather than chased beside doses.

You feared eating would shrink to duty. It grows back to favourite.

Picture tomorrow lived this way from morning. Wake hollow. Drink water. Move. Let hunger ripen. Eat what you want with attention. Taste the first bite fully. Let the middle bites deepen. Lay food down at the soft dimming and turn gladly to work. Walk past colours at noon and feel hunger choose. Sit at midday with time enough to notice. Eat till eased. Stop. Walk out clear. Feel afternoon hold steady without a drawer. Come home hungry for supper. Eat. Talk. Laugh. Stop. Rest. Sleep without grazing hum.

No vigilance in that day. No ledger. No wage. Hunger and satisfaction carry the whole day as they carried your earliest days, quietly, surely, joyfully.

Do you see how little needs to change? Change nothing else in life. Same shops. Same kitchen. Same people. Same hours. Only the noise lifted. Only hunger heard. Only satisfaction obeyed. Freedom does not ask for a new life. Freedom hands back the life the loop crowded out.

I lived the crowded years. I praised dessert as love while love sat beside me untasted. I praised sweet as feast while feast cooled before me uneaten in attention. I called plain grey while grey was coating. Now plain shines. Now hunger sings. Now enough feels like wealth, not denial.

You will feel it first in small hours. An apple cold and loud. A crust that crackles. A soup that warms to the toes. A belly that says enough and means it. A mind that leaves the table without backward glance. Small proofs, bright, repeated, until the fear of grey sounds strange in your own ears, like fearing daylight will be dark.

Hear that strangeness now and rejoice. The fear was never yours. It was talk laid over appetite. Talk lifts. Appetite stays.

So hear the word for this hour, spoken with cheerful certainty, at the table, hungry, with real food before you:

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Plain food turns bright when hunger arrives clean and attention slows the hand.
- True hunger welcomes many foods and ends gladly at enough, while narrow urgent want renews at feeding.
- A favourite meal eaten hungry satisfies layer by layer, then releases attention to life.
- Satisfaction arrives as flavour softening and belly easing, easy to obey with pleasure.
- Celebration love lived in the room, the people, and hunger met, not in the dose laid on top.
- Fruit and plain meals belong at the free table and close by themselves at enough.
- Waiting for hollow willingness, tasting fully, and laying food down at eased fullness restores steady brightness.
