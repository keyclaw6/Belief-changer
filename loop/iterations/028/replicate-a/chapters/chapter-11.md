Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The quiet kitchen after supper, the ordinary bar on the table, the cloying mouthful seen clear at last, the solemn vow, the first clear morning

*There is no safer tomorrow — this ordinary sweet, tasted with open eyes, ends it now.*

### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP

You have seen the week clear. The Friday glow made the wanting huge. The small permission carried the binge. The drawer never freed the head. The calendar never brought a clean Monday. Totality ended the argument and let hunger lead again.

And now, with all that seen, the old voice puts on its kindest face and says the last reasonable thing it owns.

“I see it. I agree. I will begin after the holidays. After the birthday. After this box is gone. When work calms. When I feel a little more ready. Someday soon, not tonight.”

I know that kindness. We all spoke it while topping up the very low we claimed to be leaving. We said tomorrow because today still looked precious, which proves we still believed there was something precious to lose.

Hear it kindly, because it sounds like wisdom, and meet it flat, because time does not soften a con.

Delay never loosened the wire. Delay tightened it.

Ask from years you have lived, and answer honest.

Did tomorrow ever arrive with less wanting, or did it always arrive with more?

Did waiting ever make the first bite smaller, or did waiting always make it brighter, larger, louder in memory?

Did a farewell box ever stay farewell once you gave it a farewell glow?

There is only one honest answer. The glow was the waiting. The waiting was the engine. Take away the waiting and the glow dies with it.

We fell for that engine together, you and I. I said after Christmas for three Christmases and each Christmas found me deeper in fog, more tired at three in the afternoon, more loud at ten at night. I told friends I was preparing. Preparing meant rehearsing. Rehearsing meant worshipping. Worshipping meant a bigger flood when permission came. I was not weak. I was conned by a voice that calls stalling prudence.

Look close at the excuses, because they all wear different coats and all speak with one mouth.

After the holidays. There will always be holidays. Lights and tables and travel and tiredness. If freedom needs an empty calendar, freedom never comes. The calendar fills as fast as you clear it. You have watched a whole year fill itself with reasons — birthdays, weddings, trips, hard weeks, good weeks — and each reason borrowed the same sentence. The sentence never protected the day. It only mortgaged it.

After the birthday. Whose birthday? Yours, hers, his, the child's, the office cake wheeled out singing. There is always a candle somewhere ahead. Do you want to live by candlelight, timing your mouth by other people's cakes? A birthday needs laughter and people and story. It never needed your fog the next morning. You have seen celebration whole with no wrapper needed. The wrapper added nothing to love and took a little from memory.

When work calms. Work never calms for long. A hard project ends and another begins. A quiet week arrives and fills itself with errands. If you wait for calm to be free, you will wait through every storm and every calm and call both weather proof you are not ready. The low you feel at work is the same low you feel at home — flat by afternoon, twitchy by evening, lifted for minutes by a hit and dropped for hours after. Calm never came from the dose. The dose stole calm and sold it back loudly.

When I feel ready. What does that feeling look like? A bell? A sign? A morning when no thought of sweetness crosses? That morning never comes while you feed the thought. Readiness is not a mood that visits. Readiness is seeing. You are ready the moment the con is seen, trembling or not, excited or not. Waiting for a warmer readiness is like waiting to feel dry while standing in rain.

See the shape clean. If BAD SUGAR gave nothing on a free evening, what will a month of waiting add to it except fantasy, tension, and a deeper crash? If one ordinary dose leaves the mouth coated and the head foggy tonight, what magic will the same dose gain by being eaten in January? None. The date adds nothing. The Con adds everything — dread before, ceremony during, regret after.

Waiting is the trap talking with your mouth.

That flutter before acting is not wisdom warning you to wait. That flutter is simply the tug-of-war of craving and fear, both ropes held by the same con. One rope pulls toward the dose and calls it comfort. The other rope pulls away from freedom and calls it risk. Both ropes belong to the Sugar Trap. Cut either and you will feel the other go slack, because there was only ever one hand holding both.

Do you want to pay rent on fog for another season, bright for minutes and dim for hours, watching a calendar for permission to feel coated? Or do you want to own your mouth from tonight, hunger clean, satisfaction clear, head quiet?

It is the other way around. Tomorrow is not safer. Tonight is safer, because tonight is seen.

### ARE YOU CHAMPING AT THE BIT

So let us settle readiness, because I will not hurry you with force and I will not leave you hovering.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit to be done with dim afternoons and loud evenings, with counting and hiding and starting again on Mondays. If you feel that lift, that excitement at ground under feet, you are ready. If you still feel flat dread, do not summon force. Read again the rooms that still glow for you — the afternoon desk, the cinema lights, the Friday tin — until the glow goes out. Freedom comes by seeing, never by bracing.

Ask and answer clean, with no dodge allowed.

Is there any pleasure left you still believe in that you have not watched dissolve?

Is there any comfort left you still trust that you have not seen billed in fog?

Is there any fuel left you still count on that you have not seen crash by four o'clock?

If the answer is none, you are ready. If the answer is one bright corner still glowing, go look at that corner till it dims. Do not march forward half-convinced. March forward clear.

And see this true as you stand here: you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by ending it tonight. There is no pleasure to mourn, no crutch to miss, no love to lose. Mornings clear. Shops easy. Meals favourite. Evenings owned. What leaves is fog, chase, coated tongue, and the endless head-argument. What stays is you, hungrier for real food, steadier in mood, lighter in body, free to enjoy sweetness where sweetness lives — in fruit cold and loud, in bread warm, in laughter whole.

You leave easily, immediately and permanently, by seeing, not by force. That is not a boast. That is the nature of a con once seen. You do not need months to stop being fooled. You stop the moment you see. The body echo that follows is small and brief, already dying, never a sentence to serve.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. Hunger your guide. Satisfaction your rest. No special sweet kept. No tomorrow bargained. No drawer guarded. No calendar watched. That is the whole contract, plain and kind.

I speak as one who hovered long. I told myself I needed a perfect week, a quiet house, a strong month. The perfect week never came because the Trap ate every perfect week and called the eating proof I was not ready. I was ready the night I saw waiting as the last trick. I paced then like a freed lion pacing by an open cage from habit, not need — close to ground, afraid of ground, free of the bars and still listening for them. The listening passed. The ground held. Yours will hold.

Feel how light that is. No edge to balance on. No rule to polish. No performance to sustain. Only seeing, then walking.

If readiness is in you, even trembling a little beneath excitement, we go now. Not with gloom. With elation. You are escaping, not losing.

Excitement is the correct feeling here. Dread belongs to the old method that told you leaving would hurt. Leaving does not hurt. Staying hurt, daily, hourly, in teeth and mood and money and hours. Excitement tells the truth about tonight. You stand at an open door with the night clear behind you and the morning clear ahead. Walk through rejoicing.

### TASTE IT AT LAST

Take your last ordinary sweet now. Not a laboratory dose. Not a strange ritual. Your ordinary thing, from your ordinary shop, eaten in your ordinary way after supper, at the quiet kitchen table while the house hums.

Supper done. Plates cleared. Warmth still in the kitchen. The lamp on. The street quiet outside. The packet there, ordinary, familiar, a little tired-looking once you look without hunger for it. This is not a feast. This is not a party. This is the truth-telling hour, you and one small packet with no audience to impress.

I ask one thing only: pay attention as you never allowed yourself to pay attention.

Unwrap it normally. Notice the rustle lift the head before the mouth. Notice the mouth water on cue, trained like a bell. That bell was rung a thousand times by bright packets and late nights. Ringing never ordered the legs.

Look at it before you bite. Small. Glossy. Made to glow under shop lights, duller under kitchen light. Smell it. Loud at first, then faintly chemical at the edge when you stay with it. We never stayed with it. We hurried. Hurry was part of the con.

Put the first bite in. Let it sit. Taste fully.

Sweet, loud at first. Then very sweet. Then coated. Then cloying. Then faintly sickly at the edges, the tongue filming, the teeth edged, the throat seeking water. Chew slower than habit wants. Habit wants speed because speed blurs. Slowness tells the truth.

Notice what the mouth does. The cheeks pull. The tongue works to clear itself. The throat asks for drink to wash down what was sold as delight. Notice what the nose does. The first aroma bright, the second fainter, the third gone. Notice what the stomach does. Nothing. No welcome. No hunger met. A bypass, a noise above while hunger waits below untouched.

Take the second bite and watch pleasure leave while eating continues. The hand moves though the mouth has gone quiet. The mind already leans to the next square though this one has not finished. That lean is the loop, not hunger. Hunger asks and is glad to rest. This never rests. This asks louder the more it gets.

Take a third bite if your ordinary way takes a third, and watch the film thicken. Tongue coated. Teeth furred. Breath sweet-sour. The evening a shade less bright than before the wrapper opened. Joy did not rise with dose. Dose rose and joy fell.

Ask while chewing, and answer honest.

Is this love? Is this comfort? Is this fuel?

Does the mouth feel clean, or coated? Does the head feel bright, or dimmed at the edges? Does the belly feel met, or bypassed while something else shouts?

Watch the after-minute. The flood that promised lift settles into flat. The tongue seeks to scrape itself clean. The evening that was clear before the wrapper feels a shade duller after. Short lift, fast drop, and the drop calls for another lift that will drop again.

Feel the ugliness without flinching. I felt it on my last night with a bar I had loved for years. Halfway down I wanted to put it down, not from virtue but from disappointment, the way you put down a story that bores halfway. The second half I ate from momentum, not desire, tasting mostly paper and habit. I thought, is this the treasure I guarded a drawer for? Is this the Friday I worshipped all week? The answer came as relief, not shame.

We were never enjoying. We were feeding the Nibbler and calling its brief quiet pleasure, and obeying the Sweet Con and calling its orders desire. The dose did plenty to the teeth, to the afternoon, to the evening, to the wallet, to the hours. It did nothing for hunger, for mood, for love, for life.

Let that settle as settled fact. No debate left. No benefit hiding. No precious exception glowing in the corner. Only a small, loud packet that borrows brightness from waiting and pays back in fog.

If a low mutter comes in the evening for a few days after tonight, smile when you hear it. It will be small, brief, already fading — that empty, twitchy pull for something sweet, heard once as dying, never as order. It passes in days, not lifetimes. It needs no brace, no vigil, no cure. Hunger will still call clean. Meals will still satisfy. Mornings will still clear. The mutter is echo, not need.

That echo is mild and well-defined, a tiny physical grumble already starving. It complains a little because it is dying, not because you need feeding. In a few days the grumble thins and goes. The head that once translated every grumble as must now hears it as music — proof the little creature is losing its voice.

See it now, whole and ugly and small, and you are ready for the vow that ends it.

### THE TWO MOMENTS THAT WILL COME

Before we speak the vow, let me walk you past the two moments that will visit after, so they arrive already disarmed.

The first will come on a hard day. Rain on the windows. Work heavy. Shoulders tight. The inbox full. The kitchen loud. Feet aching. The old friend-voice will offer rescue.

“You have been so good. You deserve one. Just this once to take the edge off. No one would blame you.”

Hear it for what it is. The edge it offers to take off is the edge it put on. BAD SUGAR never softened a hard day. It borrowed brightness from relief and charged fog by night and a lower morning after. A hard day needs supper, rest, warmth, a kind word, sleep — hunger met, body honoured. A dose on a hard day adds a coated mouth to a heavy heart and calls the adding comfort.

See that evening lived two ways.

In the old way, you come home heavy, tear paper, eat standing, taste little, chase much, sit foggier, sleep lighter, wake coated with the hard day still there plus regret. The problem waited while you fogged it. Nothing solved. Strength less.

In the free way, you come home heavy, eat hunger's supper slowly till satisfied, drink water, sit warm, speak kindly, sleep clear, wake clear with the hard day smaller in morning light. The problem met, body kept, head quiet. Nothing added. Strength kept.

Ask when it comes, and answer honest.

Did a dose ever solve the hard thing, or did it only pause the clock while adding a second problem?

Did comfort ever arrive in wrapper, or did comfort always arrive in the room — the chair, the soup, the voice — while the wrapper rode along and sent the bill?

There is only one honest reading. The rescuer is the perpetrator with a kind face. Pity the offer. It has nothing to sell.

The second will come on a bright day. Sun out. Good news. Laughter in the car. Friends round. Cake lifted high with candles. The voice will wear party clothes.

“Celebrate. Just one to mark it. One cannot hurt when you are free. Be normal.”

Hear it flat. One kept sweet keeps the whole belief breathing. The belief drives the binge. Permission re-fires wanting and calls the firing proof you need it. You have lived the permitted single biscuit that emptied the box before joy ended. You know that whisper true to life. A bright day needs no wrapper to be bright. The dark gave the laughter. The people gave the love. The dose only rode along.

See that brightness lived two ways.

In the old way, you mark it with paper torn, first bite floodlit, second duller, third coated, hand moving after pleasure gone, laughter a little louder then a little flatter, memory filmed. The day remembered through coating. Sweetness claimed, brightness taxed.

In the free way, you mark it whole — eyes bright, mouth clean, laughter full, food enjoyed as favourite till satisfied, memory clear. No chase after. No repair before bed. The day remembered as itself. Sweetness where sweetness lives — in people, in light, in hunger met.

Ask when it comes, and answer honest.

If the dose gave nothing when taken often, what will it give when taken rarely?

If celebration was whole with no wrapper needed, what will wrapper add except fog to memory?

If normal means coated and foggy and never satisfied, do you want to be normal, or do you want to be free?

There is only one honest reading. A kept exception is the engine with a party hat. Totality is the kindness that ends the argument.

Meet both moments as weather, not orders. A thought crossing the mind is a dead enemy walking past, not a master calling. You do not argue with weather. You notice, you smile, you get on with enjoying your life.

When the thought crosses — and it will, faint at first, then fainter — you will not mourn a lost friend. You will rejoice at a dead enemy. You will feel a lift, warm and certain, and you will think the thought that seals freedom with joy. Let me give you that thought now, because it is yours to keep for the rest of your years. Whenever sugar crosses your mind, in a bright aisle, at a birthday table, on a tired evening, you will feel relief flood up and you will think, from the belly, loud with delight:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it now and feel the shape. Not wistful. Not brave. Joyful. The way a debtor laughs at a burned bill. Brilliant, because the con is seen. Free, because the mouth is yours again. Say it till it fires on its own. Whenever the old image glows, think BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! and watch the glow go out like a lamp in morning sun. That is not suppression. That is replacement by truth, and truth needs no effort.

You are not losing sweetness. Sweetness lives in real food, in hunger met, in fruit and meals and mornings. What leaves tonight was never sweet. It was bill in sweet coat.

### FREE AS OF NOW

So here we are, at the table with the wrapper empty and the truth full.

You have seen waiting as the last trick. You feel ready, champing to cross. You have tasted the ordinary thing with open eyes and found it ugly and small. You know the two moments and how to meet them with delight. There is nothing left to argue and nothing left to fear.

Speak the vow now, solemn and cheerful, standing on ground, not on an edge. Say it aloud if you can, or silent and whole if others sleep. Mean it as a free eater means a true thing.

“I see it gives nothing. I am done with it. I am free.”

You do not vow to fight. You vow to be done fighting, because there is nothing to fight. The belief is dead. The body echo starves without it. Hunger stays your guide. Satisfaction stays your rest. Real food stays favourite.

I vowed on a night like this with crumbs on the table and relief in the chest, and I have never reopened the question, not through hard days, not through bright ones. I tell you with total certainty, warm to you and harsh to every voice that would pen you: the moment you vow with seeing, you are free. Not in a week. Not after proving. Now.

Marvellous. Wonderful. The wire cut. The head quiet. The mouth yours. Mornings ahead clear, shops easy, meals favourite, evenings owned. Pity the bright aisle rather than envy it. Rejoice when the grumble mutters and dies. Celebrate when the thought crosses and brings relief.

Take the word that closes every back door, with cheerful certainty, with hands free:

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

**SUMMARY**
- Waiting never made leaving easier, waiting kept the wanting bright and the crash deep.
- Readiness is a clear seeing that tonight holds nothing worth keeping, not a perfect week arriving.
- The ordinary dose, tasted with full attention, shows coated mouth and flat head where love was promised.
- A hard day is met with supper, rest and warmth, a bright day is whole in its own light.
- A passing sweet thought becomes a flash of relief that the mouth belongs to hunger again.
- Freedom starts with the vow tonight and lives in clear mornings, easy shops and satisfied meals.
