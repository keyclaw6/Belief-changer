Chapter 1
THE INVITATION

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Night kitchen and full cupboard, boxed line around BAD SUGAR, escaped eater talking plainly, reading contract

*You are not about to start another diet; you are about to look clearly at one narrow sweet fraud and walk free happy.*

### THIS IS NOT WHAT YOU THINK IT IS

I know what you are holding.

You saw the title and part of you lifted. Another part sank. You have been here before. Another book, another plan, another set of rules about what you may eat and when and how much, another bright Monday morning followed by a grey Wednesday night. You are half-hoping this one might be different and half-scoffing because you have learned to protect yourself from hope. That is sensible. Keep that sharp eye. You will need it, but not as a weapon against yourself.

This is not a diet book. It is not a nutrition lecture. It is not going to scold you about your weight or your teeth or your character, and it is not going to hand you a meal plan or a list of forbidden joys or a calendar to tick off clean days.

If you are waiting for me to tell you to eat less and move more, you can put the book down now. I will not tell you that. If you are waiting for me to tell you that you are weak and must become strong, you can put the book down now. I will not tell you that either. If you are bracing for shame, you can let that breath out. There is no shame in this book. Not for you. Not once.

We have all lived the same tired loop. We buy the biscuits with the firm voice in our head saying just for the weekend, just for the guests, just one after dinner. We eat one standing in the kitchen and tell ourselves that was the one. We eat a second to prove the first did not count. We close the cupboard and open it again without quite deciding to. Later we stare at the empty packet and wonder who ate them all, as if some stranger passed through while we were not looking. That bewildered hand moving before the decision is not proof that you are greedy. It is proof that a fraud has taken place. I name it now only as a fraud, nothing more. You will see its full shape with your own eyes as we go.

Ask yourself a plain question. If you truly loved those sweet hits, if they truly added sweetness to your life, why would you need a book about stopping them? You do not need a book to help you go on doing something you enjoy. No one writes manuals for how to keep enjoying a warm bath or a walk in fine weather.

And ask yourself this. If the diets and the moderation rules and the white-knuckle weeks truly worked, why did each one collapse in the same way, at the same hour, with the same dazed regret? Was it really you that failed each time?

And ask yourself this third question. What if the failure was never yours? What if the method you were given was built to fail? What would that mean about you?

There is only one honest answer to all three, and you already feel it. That is why you are still reading.

I am not asking you to believe me yet. I am asking you to investigate with me. Question what you think you know about sweetness. Let the bright sweet-lover in you who swears life would be grey without treats sit beside the tired evening eater who whispers I want to get off the roller coaster, and let us look at both with kind eyes.

You do not have to change anything about what you eat while you read. Your cupboard can stay full. Your routine can stay exactly as it is. There is no threat in these pages. I promise you this reading asks almost nothing of you, because you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading on with an open mind.

That promise is not sales talk. It is simple arithmetic. If what I say is untrue, you will have lost a few hours of reading and kept your habit exactly as it was. If what I say is true, you will have gained your mornings, your evenings, your appetite, your ease in shops and kitchens, and the quiet pride of no longer being tugged. No other book you tried offered you those odds. They offered you struggle first and hope later. I offer you curiosity first and freedom as you read.

You are wary because you are clutching moderation rules. I understand. Just one after dinner. No sugar on weekdays. Only when guests come. Only dark. Only little. I do not keep it in the house. Those rules felt like protection. They felt like proof you were in charge. We will examine together, gently, whether they protected you or kept you tied. For now you do not need to drop them. You do not need to defend them either. Bring them with you as evidence. We are investigators, you and I, not defendants.

### WHO IS TALKING TO YOU

I was you.

I am not a guru on a mountain who never liked cake. I liked it the way you like it. I loved the lift of the first bite, the ceremony of the wrapper, the private peace of the kitchen late at night when the house was quiet and the packet was all mine. I told myself the same stories. I need sugary somethings to get me through. I have managed to be good this week, I deserve one. Celebrating with chocolate is part of love. Work will stop without a sweet top-up at three.

I tried what you tried. I did the forbidding and the counting and the bargaining. No sugar before six. No sugar on weekdays. Only dark, only little, only when guests come. I do not keep it in the house — until I did. I threw packets away and fished them back out. I ate standing so it would not count. I ate quickly so no one would see. I promised tomorrow, after the birthday, after the holidays. Tomorrow came dressed as today and the promise moved again.

Each collapse left the same sting. I thought it proved I had no character. I thought other people could take it or leave it and I could not. I thought I would be chained to craving for the rest of my days, sneaking and regretting in a private circle.

I was wrong about every part of that. The chain was not my character. The method was wrong, not me. When I saw the mechanism clearly, I stopped, and it was not a battle. It was relief. It was morning without the tug. It was shopping without the negotiation. It was eating with hunger and satisfaction leading again, the way a body is meant to lead.

Since then I have helped many sweet-trapped eaters do the same. Not by making them stronger. Not by frightening them into obedience. By showing them what the thing really does and does not do, until the want fell away by itself. That is why I can speak to you with complete confidence now. I have been where you are, I have come out where you want to be, and I know the road between is short and bright.

You do not need to admire me. You need only to allow the possibility that a lifelong sweet lover who failed every diet could walk free without a fight. If I could, with my history, what does that say about your chances? You are not reading the words of a superior being. You are reading the words of an escaped eater talking to one reader in a quiet room.

There is no magic in me. There is clarity in the method. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That clause is the whole contract in one line. It sounds almost too simple, and I mean it exactly as it sounds. You do not have to summon courage you do not have. You do not have to arrange your life around avoidance. You do not have to count days or carry a score. You follow the reading instructions as they come, in order, and you let understanding do the heavy lifting.

Think how much energy you have already spent on the other way. Think of the mental arithmetic at every shelf, every menu, every party. Can I. Should I. Have I been good enough to earn it. Will I start again Monday. That chatter is exhausting. It never gave you peace, even on the good days, because the good days were only the pause between tugs. Imagine that chatter gone. Not silenced by effort. Gone because there is nothing to argue about. That is what following simple instructions toward clear sight does. It ends the argument by removing its subject.

I know this is hard to accept. You have been told for years that stopping anything sweet must be hard, that desire means value, that craving means need. Of course you doubt a claim that it can be easy. I doubted it too. Doubt is welcome here. Doubt makes a good investigator. Keep your doubt beside you and keep reading. The proof will not be my word. The proof will be your own recognition, page by page, as you catch your own hand, your own thoughts, your own roller coaster in these descriptions and feel the quiet click of truth.

### WHEN I SAY SUGAR, THIS IS WHAT I MEAN

We cannot talk honestly until we draw one clean line. The word sugar has become foggy. It is used for everything from a peach to a petrol-station energy drink, and that fog keeps you confused and guilty about foods that never harmed you.

So let us clear it now, once, in a box you can keep.

> When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. BAD SUGAR means refined sugar plus added free sugars plus junk processed carbs and starchy snack-carbs eaten as sweet hits, including sugary drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and equivalent grazing doses. Natural sugars inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals are not the target. They belong to real food, hunger, satisfaction and the body you will enjoy again.

Read that again slowly. This book is not about food. It is not about eating. It is about one narrow confectionery fraud inside eating: the engineered sweet hit that comes in packets, bottles, wrappers and bakery boxes, designed to be craved and grazed.

An apple is not BAD SUGAR. A carrot is not BAD SUGAR. Potatoes in a plain meal, rice in a plain meal, fresh bread eaten hungry at a table until you are satisfied — none of those are what we are talking about. Fruit is sugar too so nothing matters — you have heard that line, perhaps you have used it yourself when the rules felt too tangled. Let that tangle fall away. We are not tangled here. We are precise.

Inside that precise line, the logic is total. There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR that you need to seek. Your body manufactures the glucose it needs from ordinary nourishment. Taking the sweet hits away is not deprivation. It is removal of an extra load. If a crumb lands in your food by accident in a restaurant, your body will cope with it. That is not the issue. The issue is never the accidental crumb. The issue is the deliberate dose believed to help you. Guard the belief and the behaviour follows. Panic about perfection helps no one.

If you live with diabetes or pre-diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, please talk to your clinician before changing anything and follow their advice first.

This book changes a belief about BAD SUGAR. It does not give medical care. Your clinician leads on your body; I lead on the belief.

With that line drawn, you can breathe. You are not being asked to stop eating. You are being asked to see one impostor clearly. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is not a slogan. That is the eating contract of this book. Read it again and feel how different it is from every diet you have tried. No good-food list and bad-food shame. No points, no sins, no being good this week. Hunger is your signal to begin. Satisfaction is your signal to end. Pleasure is allowed — required, even. Real food enjoyed hungry until satisfied is one of the great daily joys, and BAD SUGAR has been blocking it, not providing it.

You will inhabit that joy fully in the middle of this book, at a table, with a plain favourite meal. Not as theory. As lived appetite. For now, hold the promise: nothing about natural hunger will be taken from you. Something false that spoiled hunger will be lifted away.

Let me be very plain about what this precision gives you. The diet world made the field huge and vague. Eat clean. Cut carbs. Fear fruit. Count every gram. That vagueness kept you anxious and hungry and always a little guilty, which is exactly the state in which a packet looks like comfort. Precision does the opposite. It shrinks the enemy to its true size. We are not at war with eating. We are not at war with pleasure. We are not at war with your body. We are looking at BAD SUGAR doses — drinks, sweets, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and their grazing cousins — and asking one calm question of them: what do you do for me? Keep that small, sharp question in your pocket. You will need it.

### EAT AND READ

Every other approach you tried started with the behaviour and left the belief intact.

It told you sweet hits give you energy, comfort, reward, love, concentration, a break, and then told you to walk past them with clenched hands. It left the treasure glowing on the pedestal and ordered you to show character. Of course you stared. Of course you reached. Of course you blamed yourself when you did. Who would not?

We do the opposite here. We start with the belief and leave the behaviour alone until the belief changes. That is why you are to carry on exactly as normal while you read. Buy as usual. Snack as usual. Do not cut down, do not skip, do not test yourself. If you feel the old I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, answer it as you always have. This book does not need your hunger to prove its point. It needs your attention.

That instruction shocks new readers. Should I not start at once? Should I not show commitment by suffering a little first? No. Suffering first would only feed the old story that freedom must be earned by pain. Freedom here is not earned by pain. It is recognized by clarity. The moment you see that a dose gives you nothing, you will not need to push it away. You cannot miss what you see gives you nothing.

Think of all those Monday restarts. Think of the bright resolve at breakfast, the virtuous afternoon, the slow unravel in the evening, the short term lift then fast drop that you chased again at nine and again at eleven. Did that feel like free choice? Did that feel like a person enjoying a genuine pleasure? Or did it feel like a roller coaster you never bought a ticket for?

You know the answer. We have all ridden it. We told ourselves just a treat when you feel you deserve one, and then watched one become a box. That is not weakness speaking. That is a loop speaking. A loop that creates the emptiness it pretends to fill.

It is vital that you eat properly while we examine it. Do not skip meals to compensate for sweet hits. Do not go hungry to prove you are serious. A hungry reader cannot think clearly, and I need your clearest thought. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again. Let that line govern your plate while your mind does the work of reading.

Notice how kind that line is compared with every rule you have obeyed. Those rules made you eat by the clock, by the allowance, by the verdict of good or bad. This line returns you to the body. Hunger tells you when. Satisfaction tells you when to stop. Taste tells you what delights you among real foods. No punishment follows. There is no moral ledger here. There is only an animal wisdom you were born with and that BAD SUGAR doses have been drowning out with noise.

You may fear that permission to eat will make you eat more sweet hits while you read. Let that fear pass through. You are not being asked to control the amount. You are being asked to observe it with curiosity. Notice the hand. Notice the hour. Notice the story in your head just before the reach — sugary somethings to get me through, treat when you feel you deserve one, celebrating with chocolate. Do not argue with those stories yet. Just hear them. We will meet each one in its own time, in daylight, and see what it is made of.

### WHY EASY SOUNDS IMPOSSIBLE

Let me tell you what is waiting if you do.

You will stop easily, immediately and permanently. I state that outrageous-sounding triad with total confidence because I have watched it happen again and again when the belief changes first. Easily, because there will be nothing to fight once you see there is nothing to miss. Immediately, because freedom starts the moment the con is seen, not after weeks of probation. Permanently, because a seen-through trick cannot fool you again unless you invite the belief back in.

Does that sound impossible? Of course it does. Every trapped eater thinks so at the start. I thought so. The readers who wrote to me after — the afternoon yo-yoers, the evening grazers, the cinema sharers, the birthday-table negotiators — all thought so. Their disbelief did not stop the method working. It only made the relief sweeter when it did.

Consider why easy feels like a lie. You have equated hard with true. If stopping felt terrible before, you assumed terrible is the truth about stopping. But what if terrible was the truth about the method you used? The white-knuckle week, the forbidden-must-have rebound, the Monday restart — those do not reveal the nature of BAD SUGAR. They reveal the nature of trying to hold your breath while still believing you need air. Change the belief about air and holding the breath ends. That is not positive thinking. That is mechanics.

You have nothing to prove and no test to pass. You are not on trial. The fraud is on trial. You are the witness, the investigator, and in the end the judge.

So keep your cupboard full for now. Keep your routine. Keep your doubts. Bring them all with you. Bring the wary sweet-lover who fears life without treats would be grey. Bring the tired eater who longs to get off the roller coaster. Both are welcome. Neither will be shamed. One will be shown to be mistaken, gently and completely, and will be glad to be mistaken.

There is a particular joy in being glad to be mistaken. Remember a time you feared a noise downstairs and found it was only the wind. The relief was not thin. It was marvellous. It flooded the body. That is the texture of the freedom ahead: not grim relief that you survived a loss, but bright relief that there never was a loss, only a misunderstanding. You will feel it in ordinary places — at the sink, at the checkout, at the table — and you will laugh that you once knelt to a packet.

Do those steps feel heavy? They should feel light. They are light. The heaviness you remember belonged to the old method that made you fight yourself. This method lets you befriend yourself and face the con.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. I will repeat that clause whenever the road feels steep, not as a scold but as a handrail. It means: do not pick and choose among the steps, do not skip ahead to the end, do not add private tests of your own. Read in order. Let each page do its work. Eat normally. Keep thinking. When a doubt rises, note it and read on. The combination opens only when every tumbler falls, and every tumbler is small.

### THE CONTRACT

So let me state the contract plainly, as one eater to another.

First, you will keep reading with curiosity, not dread. You will begin with elation, not fear. You are escaping, not losing. The elation is not decoration. A mind closed by dread cannot investigate. A mind lit by curiosity can see through any con.

Second, you will follow each reading instruction when it comes, in order, without picking and choosing. They are small. They are spoken plainly. Together they are the combination that opens the lock. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Third, you will judge everything by your own experience, not by my authority and not by the shouting of the diet world outside. If something sounds too good to be true, welcome that doubt and read on. I was a skeptic too. Skeptics make the best escapees because they test everything and own what they find.

And fourth, you will eat. You will not go hungry to prove anything. You will not skip meals to compensate for sweet hits. You will not punish your body to purify your soul. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again. That line stands for the whole book. Hunger and satisfaction are the authorities we return to. They have never lied to you. The packets have.

The first step of that contract is the smallest and the largest. It asks only for thought, yet thought is where every chain was forged. Open thought dissolves what closed thought built. You have lived long enough inside warnings and rules. Allow, for the length of this book, another posture: bright, skeptical, willing to be surprised.

I ask it with cheerful certainty, as a friend who knows the way and knows you can walk it. There is no scold in my voice. There is excitement. We are at the start of an exciting adventure out of a grey loop into appetite regained. Feel that lift. It is not a sweet hit. It is yours.
