Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THE WANT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The bright table carried from dusk, the supermarket aisle at opening time, glowing packs and cartoon friends, the checkout ambush, the mirror that says personality, the crowded room and the bell before the bite, the precision engine with its warning lights, the cell with its door standing open, the lion pacing by habit past freedom

*You did not invent this want — it was built for you, and the fear that holds you belongs to the builder.*

### YOU BRING DIVIDED SEEING

You bring something precious from dusk.

You bring the divided seeing. The faint echo low in the ribs. The loud story that translated echo as need. You bring evenings watched wave by wave, twitch noted, smile held, brightness holding without renewal. You bring hunger known clean and satisfaction known clear, bread torn with steam rising, hunger seasoning better than any jar, the hand glad to rest at enough.

I take nothing from that. That seeing stands.

And still a last defence waits behind it, quieter than hunger, more respectable than craving.

It does not speak as a twitch. It speaks as character and common sense.

“All right, the evening makes sense. But my want is mine. I have always had a sweet tooth. My family has it. Everyone eats this stuff. If it were that bad, would every shop glow with it? Would every birthday crown it? I am simply the type who loves sweet. And even if I saw through, I fear what comes next. Fear of failing again. Fear of living bare without it.”

“I have a sweet tooth. Everyone eats it so it must be fine. I am the addictive type.”

I hear you. We all spoke those lines. I spoke them for years while my cupboard filled itself and my afternoons ran bright for minutes and dim for hours. I called want personality. I called fear wisdom. I called normal proof.

The fact is want was never personality. Fear was never wisdom. Normal was never proof.

We fell into the loop together, you and I and millions of quiet daily grazers, reaching before decision, blaming nature for what nurture had drilled. This hour we walk the workshop where want was hammered together, look at the mirror that lied about you, and open the door fear kept shut.

Judge only by what you have lived. Keep an open mind while I show you the makers. If you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar, are pregnant, or carry an eating disorder past or present, your clinician leads and this book speaks only to belief.

### THE AISLE THAT WANTS FOR YOU

Come with me into the encounter itself. Morning. Nothing staged. Same shop you have entered a thousand times.

Doors sigh. Air cool. Trolleys clatter. Light white and kind.

You came for bread and milk. You walk the long aisle to find them.

Left and right the packs glow. Red shouts. Gold promises. A cartoon tiger leans from a box at a child’s eye height, grinning, arm out, friend for life. A princess dances on a pouch. A football hero holds a bar as if strength lived in wrapper. Words climb: treat, reward, fuel, love, energy, happy, family, celebration, deserved. Colours tuned to stop the eye. Shapes tuned to fit the hand. Smells pumped from the bakery corner to turn the head. Music soft enough to slow the feet.

You did not ask to feel want. Want arrived before thought. Mouth watering though the belly is full. Hand moving though the list says bread and milk. Memory lighting though hunger is silent.

At the till the ambush waits. Low shelves on both sides. Small bars, bright buttons, sweet gums at a child’s reach and a tired adult’s reach. The queue crawls. The belt stalls. Hands idle. Eyes fall. One more dose for the road. Tired hands add it without decision and call adding choice.

Did you choose that want? Did your body invent those colours? Did your nature tune that crunch to shatter then melt, salt edged to sharpen sweet, fat laid to carry flavour long past swallow? Did your tooth design the pack to tear open with one pull in traffic, in office, in sofa dark?

Ask from hours you have lived a thousand times.

When did you first learn that sweet means love? Before you could read, or after you weighed evidence?

Did you fear low energy when you tasted your first birthday cake at three? Did you need comfort from deadlines at five? Did you decide, after calm inquiry, that celebration requires chocolate? Or did mother’s purse open like love, father’s pocket jingle like reward, teacher’s drawer gleam like prize, television sing treat while cartoon friends danced?

When the packs changed — bigger bars, deeper fillings, louder crunch, brighter bottles — did your hunger order the change? Or did boardrooms order it, armies of flavour men tuning the bliss point till the tongue lit and the hand hurried, till the first bite sang and the tenth still called?

There is only one honest reading. Desire was manufactured.

I speak as one who lived inside that manufacture and called it me. I believed my evening reach proved my nature. I believed the queue’s glow proved harmlessness. I believed the tiger was my friend. I see now the tiger worked for someone else. The glow worked for someone else. The want worked for someone else, and I paid for the work with fog and chase and money and teeth and hours.

That is the Sugar Trap working as designed. That is the Sweet Con installed early and renewed daily at every shelf.

We were not foolish. We were conned. Warmth stays with you — deceived, never defective — and harshness falls where it belongs, on the makers who built want and sold it as your character.

Look closer at the building, because the building proves the want was never yours.

No child is born asking for a wrapper. The hollow newborn asks for milk and sleep and arms. The toddler asks for peach and bread and play. Sweet in dazzling dress arrives later, placed at eye height, sung in jingles, tied to prize and party and brave boy and good girl. Repeat the tie a thousand times and the head lifts at the sight before hunger speaks. Call that lift nature and the con is complete.

The industry knows what it does. It buys the brightest shelf. It buys the end of the aisle. It buys the child’s gaze. It pays for crunch that fades fast so the hand hurries back. It pays for melt that coats then vanishes so the mouth calls again. It pays for colours that shout across a crowded room. It pays for laughter round the product so memory lends glow. It does not sell food. It sells cue and flood and repeat.

And we paid twice. We paid coin at the till. We paid hours in fog after.

Did the street glow because the product was harmless? Did the birthday crown it because the body needed it? Did the office tin sit full because free people chose it? If glow proved good, every successful con would be innocent by its own lights.

The street glowed because glow sells. That is all.

### NOT YOUR NATURE

Stand with me before the second encounter. Not the shelf. The mirror.

The mirror speaks in your own voice, which is why it fooled us.

“Everyone eats it. It must be fine. My whole family eats it. My friends eat it. The office eats it. Look at the street — bins full of wrappers, queues full of cups. Normal cannot be trap.”

“Mine is different. I have no control around sweet. Others can take one. I take the box. I must be the addictive type. Something in me is broken.”

“I feel like i cant stop. I have managed to be good this week and then ruined it on Friday. Sugary somethings to get me through — that is just who I am.”

I hear the shame under the logic. Let me honour the feeling before I disarm it — of course it feels personal when the hand moves in your kitchen and no one else is in the room — and then show you what personal never meant here, because once seen it cannot be unseen.

Everyone eating it never made it free choice. Everyone once breathed smoke in cinemas. Everyone once crowned every table with white loaves and called tired normal. Ubiquity is what a successful con looks like from inside. The con needs the street to glow so the prisoner calls the cell the world.

Your family eating it never proved your genes ordered it. It proved the story entered early through loving hands. Mother gave what her mother gave. Father shared what advertising taught him sharing means. We breathed that talk before we could argue. We repeated till repetition felt like reason. That is brainwashing, not blood.

And the broken-type story? That is the cruellest turn of the con. It takes the signature of the trap — a permitted sliver carries the binge — and sells it as your defect.

Ask from plain evenings you have already lived.

When one biscuit became the box, did the box prove mighty hunger? Or did the first dose re-light the echo and belief shout, “you have started, you may as well finish”?

When others took one in public, did you see their kitchens at ten at night? Did you see the drawer, the car compartment, the office tin, the bin buried under paper? Public neatness never proved private freedom.

When you called yourself weak, were you weak in other rooms? Did you raise children through sleepless years? Did you hold jobs through hard winters? Did you persist for years against your own instincts, white-knuckling days and restarting Mondays? Weak people do not do that. Wilful people do that. Outgunned people do that.

You were not weak. You were conned.

About one in seven adults and about one in twelve children answer the same way on food-craving questionnaires — much the same as for alcohol and tobacco. I lay no label on you in that. I lay comfort there. If millions answer the same, the fault cannot live in your character. The trap is common because the manufacture is common.

It was never you that was defective. It was the loop that was efficient.

Once you see want as installed, pity replaces shame. Anger finds its true address. Did the ditch prove you are broken? Did the empty box prove you lack character? Did the midnight hand in the cupboard prove choice? If you needed a book to stop a hobby you loved, would you call that love? The mere fact you hold this book proves choice had been removed, not that will had failed.

The traits we feared were ours were the result of the loop, not the cause. Flat afternoons made us call ourselves lazy. Evening chase made us call ourselves greedy. Morning fog made us call ourselves dull. Remove the loop and the mirror clears. You were never the type. You were never the tooth. You were a clean eater taught a loud script.

There is only one honest answer. Normal never proved free. Family never proved fate. The box emptied never proved broken.

### THE CROWDING AND THE RUSTLE

Why did so small an echo feel so mighty in a normal life? Because the trap learned to crowd the room and ring the bell before the bite.

Watch the crowding first, because you have felt it without words.

First year two squares lit the evening. Then two squares lit minutes only. Then the bar lit minutes. Then the family bar lit minutes. Dose grew while glow shrank. Tongue wanted louder to hear the same song. Evening wanted more to buy the same pause. Morning woke flat, foggy and never satisfied, listening early for the first sweet note to quiet it.

That is not gluttony. That is crowding. The circuit dulled by flood and asked for flood to feel normal. Needing more for the same effect is the trap’s arithmetic, never proof of a large need in you.

I speak of lived experience here, not brain destiny laid on you. You felt enough stop being enough. You felt the bar grow while the lift shortened. You felt the morning ask earlier each year. That felt history is enough. It needs no scan to prove it and no scan could compel your hand.

Watch the rustle next, because the rustle fired before hunger spoke.

Wrapper from another room and mouth floods though supper filled the belly. Sweet smell from an open bag and hands stir though hands were still. Clock says three, sofa says eight, credits roll — and want arrives on cue though the body asked for nothing. That firing is learned, not compelled. A bell rung long enough lifts the head. Lifting the head never ordered the legs to follow. Cue says remember. Cue never says must.

We obeyed cues because the Sweet Con told us cues were orders. “I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling.” “Sugary somethings to get me through.” “I feel like i cant stop once I start.” We spoke those lines as confession. They were scripts handed to us. The evening box emptied not because mighty hunger overpowered a weak self but because a permitted sliver re-lit the echo and belief shouted, “finish it, you have started, you have blown it.” Loss of control is the signature of the trap, never proof of a broken eater.

In caged animals given sweet on and off, schedule plus sugar drives binge and chase and fretful signs between. I sell you no human sentence in that. I keep the shape for us: on-off plus sweet breeds on-off hunger. Diet weeks and tidy days and forbidden-must-have rebound ran the same schedule in your kitchen. Restriction by day, flood by night, want louder for the yo-yo. The yo-yo never proved you cannot live without doses. It proved the yo-yo manufactures want.

And the low that follows the lift? In one long follow of men, the daily sweet went with low mood years later rather than lifting mood — I am not laying that on you as your future, only refusing the rescue story. The comforter did not comfort onward. The lifter did not lift onward. What promised to carry the afternoon billed the evening, and what billed the evening paled the morning. I make no diagnosis of you in that and promise no mood to you. I only close the lie that the dose fixes feeling.

Take those three together and the mighty need thins to mechanism.

Crowding explains why enough never stayed enough. Cue explains why want arrived before hunger. Schedule explains why forbidden days ended in flood. None explains you as broken. All explain the loop as built.

The story that fed the small echo borrowed even your virtues to do it. “I deserve one” deserved evenings owned, mornings clear, a body unborrowed. “I have been good” needed no payment in fog. “It gets me through” got you through minutes and kept you from hours. It was you that enjoyed the room, the talk, the hunger met — not the dose. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride.

There is only one honest answer. The want was built, stocked, lit, rung and billed by others. You only paid.

### THE ENGINE THAT WARNS

Look now at the body you feared would fail without doses, because awe does the work argument cannot.

Your body is a precision engine, marvellous in signal, wonderful in repair, exact in hunger, clear in satisfaction when the noise lifts. Hunger hollows clean. Food satisfies. Enough softens flavour a shade and bids the hand rest. Tired asks sleep. Thirst asks water. Flat asks movement and air and company. Those feelings are information, not enemies. Warning lights on a dashboard, faithful, early, kind.

BAD SUGAR taught you to unscrew the bulbs.

Head fogged at three — bulb glowing: rest, air, real food, movement needed. Dose unscrewed the bulb. Glow gone for minutes. Engine still thirsty. Fog thicker at four.

Mouth empty at eight though belly full — bulb glowing: echo unfed, story loud, evening asking talk and ease and sleep. Dose unscrewed the bulb. Quiet chewing for minutes. Echo renewed. Want louder at ten.

Teeth aching, gums tender, belly blown, skin tired, sleep shallow, morning coated — bulbs glowing row by row. Doses unscrewed bulb by bulb till the dashboard looked dark and the engine ran hot underneath.

Would you call a driver wise who unscrews each warning light and praises the darkness? Would you call an owner kind who pours syrup in the tank and thanks the sputter for the brief roar?

We did that together, you and I, not from folly but from con. We mistook pause in warning for help. We mistook dark dashboard for ease.

The fact is flat. The body never asked for BAD SUGAR. It asked for food when hollow and rest when tired. It asks still. Hear the bulbs and the engine steadies on its own. Change nothing else in life and hunger returns clean, satisfaction returns clear, brightness holds without ticket.

That seeing takes nothing from you. It hands the machine back.

Consider how faithful the signals were even under flood.

Hunger still hollowed in the morning when the night had been light. Satisfaction still softened flavour at a real meal eaten hungry. Tired still asked sleep, and sleep still came cleaner on plain evenings. Thirst still lifted with water. The engine never stopped speaking. We only stopped listening while the sweet roar covered the dials.

I do not ask you to change from fear of the dials. I ask you to rejoice that the dials still work. Fear never freed a driver. Seeing frees him at once, with relief, with delight at the hum returned. The lights are not threats. They are kindness. Let them glow and guide. Let hunger hollow. Let enough whisper. Let tired lead to bed rather than to drawer.

Once you hear warning as information, the rescuer story falls. The dose never fixed the engine. It only dimmed the lamp that begged for true help. True help was simpler: food when hollow, water when dry, movement when flat, rest when tired, company when lonely. Plain tools for plain signals. No wrapper required.

You do not need to manage the engine. You need to stop unscrewing its bulbs.

### THE DOOR STANDING OPEN

And now the last rope. Fear.

Not craving. Fear of leaving craving. Fear wearing wisdom’s coat.

It pulls two ways at once. That is why it holds so well. That is the tug-of-war of craving and fear.

One rope says fail. “You have failed every diet, every tidy week, every Monday restart. You will fail again. Better to hold the small comfort you know than reach for freedom and prove yourself hopeless.”

The other rope says succeed and lose. “Suppose you do walk free. What will celebrations be? What will evenings be? What will you be without the sweet tooth that made you you? Better to keep the known glow than live bare in unknown rooms.”

Fail and you lose. Succeed and you lose. Pull either rope and the knot tightens. Tremble in the middle and call trembling proof you should stay.

Both ropes belong to the same hands. Both hands belong to the Trap.

Picture the cell to see it plainly, because pictures do what argument cannot.

A prisoner sits long years in a small cell. The door stands open. Light falls through. Air moves. Birds call from the yard. Outside, a table waits, hunger clean, people talking, life unborrowed.

The prisoner looks at the light and begs to stay.

“Outside I will fail. I will starve. I will be lost. Inside at least I know the walls. Inside at least I have my small comforts, my drawer, my ritual, my evening glow. Do not make me go.”

Guards need no lock. Fear locks from inside.

Picture the lion beside him to see the second rope, because habit wears nature’s mask.

A lion raised behind bars grows vast and pacing. One morning the cage opens wide. Grass runs to the horizon. No bars. No keeper. The lion steps to the threshold, sniffs the open air — and paces back inside. Paces the old line. Lies in the old corner. Not from need. From habit. The feet remember the circuit though the door forgot the lock.

Freed creatures hover by open cages out of habit, not hunger. Wobble after opening never proved the cage was home. It proved feet learned a path.

That is fear of failing and fear of succeeding in one image. The prisoner begs from dread of the yard. The lion paces from memory of bars. Neither needs the cell. Both obey the cell because the con taught obedience to feel like wisdom.

Ask from rooms you have already lived.

If the cell were home, why did you pick up this book? If you loved the drawer, why did you dread its call? If freedom were loss, why did the thought of mornings clear lift your chest even while fear lectured?

If fear were wisdom, would it speak with the same voice on both ropes — “stay or you will fail” and “stay or you will lose”? Does wisdom pull both ways to keep you still? Or does a trap?

There is only one honest reading. Fear never protected you. Fear penned you.

I speak as one who paced both lines. I feared failing and proved failure by staying. I feared succeeding and called the cage my taste. When I saw both ropes in one pair of hands, the hands lost power. I stepped through though feet remembered. Feet learned a new path in days. The yard was not bare. The yard was marvellous. Hunger clean. Talk long. Evenings owned.

You will wobble. Wobble is habit, not hunger. Smile at the pacing and keep walking. The door does not close behind you unless belief closes it. Guard the belief and the feet follow.

I do not want you to walk out because I frightened you with the cell. I want you to walk out because you see the cell was never home and the yard is yours. Let fear do its worst in words and then let relief answer in fact. The door stands open. The air moves. The table waits.

Do you want to live hooked for the rest of your life, bright for minutes and dim for hours, drawer-bound, rustle-bound, clock-bound? Or do you want to live free for the rest of your life, hunger your guide, satisfaction your rest, evenings your own? Both rests-of-life wait through the same open door. Only the con tells you the cell is safer than the yard.

Hear the verdict while the door stands open. The cell never kept you. Fear kept you.

### OTHER VOICES

Other voices will rush to close that door. They always do when a prisoner stands.

Family will press a plate and call refusal insult. Friends will laugh and call freedom fuss. Offices will chant normal. Screens will sing treat. Kind advisers will offer cut-down, special ones, tomorrow, weaning, substitutes, rules, ledgers, brave holding out. Ex-users will preach struggle and warn you how hard their weeks were by the wrong method.

Hear them kindly and decline them wholly. Their struggle was the wrong method talking. Their kindness carries the old translation. Each exception keeps the belief alive. Each rule feeds the small echo with attention. Each counsel to manage the cell rebuilds the Sweet Con you are starving.

You carry a seatbelt for the road — the body can meet an accidental crumb without ruin — but a belt for a jolt never licensed steering into the wall. Mind the steering and the belt minds itself.

I say this as escaped guide with total certainty, warm to you and harsh to every voice that would pen you. You investigate. I direct. Freedom is immediate gain and escape, not a future wage to earn by listening round.

Take the word that governs every aisle and mirror and open door ahead, with cheerful certainty, with hands free:
