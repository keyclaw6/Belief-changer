Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Morning table with notebook and cold tea, till receipts and tired wallet, aching molar in the bathroom mirror, evening sofa with wrappers, the quiet question of the ditch and the hand

*Forget what it costs you for a moment — name one true thing it gives you, and watch the page stay blank.*

### STOP ARGUING ABOUT HARM

You already know it harms you.

We all know. Teeth. Weight creeping up around the middle. The flat afternoons. The money sliding through the till. The dentist shaking his head. The headlines about hearts and livers and early graves. You do not need me to frighten you with that list, and I am not going to insult you by pretending you have never read it.

Here is the strange thing. Knowing the harm has never freed anyone.

Have you noticed that? You can recite the harms while your hand opens the packet. You can feel your molar throb while you chew the very thing that feeds the throb. You can step on the scales in the morning and reach at night. Knowledge of harm sits in one room of your mind. Want sits in another. They do not meet.

That is why I will not waste your time proving harm to you. The harm debate is a trap inside the Trap. As long as we argue harm against benefit, you will always answer yourself the same way.

"Yes, it rots teeth, but I enjoy it."
"Yes, it costs money, but I need a little joy."
"Yes, it makes me sluggish, but life would be grey without it."

Do you hear the shape of that answer? Harm conceded, benefit clutched. The scales tip toward harm, and still the hand moves, because the mind whispers that something real would be lost. That whisper is the whole lock. Break the whisper and the scales do not matter. Leave the whisper intact and no scare will hold you for a week.

So I am changing the question completely.

I am not asking what BAD SUGAR does TO you. We know it does plenty there. I am asking what it does FOR you. What gift? What help? What true gain that belongs to the dose and to nothing else?

Bring your honest investigator mind from last night. You saw the hand move before decision. You saw secrecy that free joy never shows. You know you were caught, not weak. Keep that clarity. Now use it on daylight.

If you were conned, the con must have promised you something. What was it?

### A DAY ON PAPER

Let us do something simple together, right where you sit.

Take an ordinary yesterday. Not a bad night. Not a binge you are ashamed of. An ordinary, moderate, respectable day. The kind you would defend.

Morning. Tea with two sugars, or the sweetened cereal eaten standing while the kettle boils. Mid-morning biscuit with the second cup. Afternoon chocolate bar from the machine to get through the files. Evening pudding after dinner because dinner was proper and you were good. Nightcap biscuit while you lock up.

Write it if you like. Time, dose, place. No judgment. Just ink.

Now beside each dose write four plain things.

Mood, an hour later. Teeth and mouth. Wallet and time. And the gift — what that dose did FOR you that nothing else that hour could have done.

Do it faithfully and you will see a pattern you have lived for years without looking at.

Mood, an hour later: thinner. Not tragic. Just thinner. The bright ten minutes gone, attention frayed at the edges, patience shorter with the children, eyes heavier at the screen. You reached for lift and found yourself a little lower, reaching again.

Teeth and mouth: coated. That furry film on the tongue. The sweet sourness at the gum line. The molar that catches cold water and sends a small electric complaint. You brush, of course you brush, and brushing matters, and still the acid from frequent free sugars bathes the enamel day after day. This is not dental advice from me. I am no dentist. If you have tooth trouble, see your dentist first and follow what you are told. I am naming what your own mouth already reports. Frequent sweet doses keep the mouth in acid. Brushing softens the blow. It does not erase the bath.

Wallet and time: small leaks, daily. A pound here, three pounds there. The vending detour, the queue, the hunt for the bar that will hit right. Nothing ruinous in one day. Add the days. Add the years. Add the hours spent thinking about sweets, planning sweets, regretting sweets, promising about sweets. What else in your life takes that much rent and calls it a treat?

Now the last column. The gift.

What did the morning sugars do FOR you that hot tea, food, sleep, daylight could not have done? What did the mid-morning biscuit give that a pause, a stretch, a drink of water, a real bite when hungry could not have given? What did the afternoon bar add to the files? What did the pudding add to the dinner, to the company, to the warmth of the table?

Be strict with yourself here, as an investigator is strict. Not what it did TO your tongue for ninety seconds. What it did FOR your life.

You will want to write "cheered me up" or "helped me through" or "made the moment special." Hold those answers. We will look each in the eye in the chapters ahead. Energy, reward, comfort — each will have its hour in court, and each will leave with nothing. For now I ask a narrower thing. Look at the paper. Point to one entry where life was truly richer because BAD SUGAR was in it, richer in a way that stayed past the swallow.

Can you?

Most honest readers sit with the pen hovering and find the column will not fill. Mood down. Mouth coated. Wallet lighter. Time spent. And under gift, words that sounded solid in the head turn to mist on paper. Nice. Lovely. Needed it. Deserved it. Those are not gifts. Those are labels stuck on after, to dignify a reach that happened before thought.

I know this is hard to accept. You still feel the glow when you picture the bar. I am not asking you to deny the glow. I am asking you to place it. Where did it live? In the dose, or in the pause, the sit-down, the laugh, the break from work, the warmth of the evening that would have been warm anyway?

We will strip each claim cleanly, one by one, in daylight. You have my word on that. Today we do only the audit. And the audit says what your hands said last night. The cost is real. The gain will not write down.

### THE MOUTHS THAT TELL THE TRUTH

Let me stay a moment with the plainest TO, because it sits in your bathroom mirror.

Free sugars feed plaque acid and decay. That is settled. The sticky film on teeth turns frequent sweet doses into acid attacks that soften enamel and open the door to cavities. Guidance everywhere now says limit free sugars, and sets a daily ceiling well below what most of us eat without thinking, with a lower target for extra benefit. I state no toxicity line and no personal sentence on you. If you live with diabetes or take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with any eating trouble past or present, follow your clinician first on all food changes and use this book for the belief only.

Feel how ordinary over-the-line has become. A frosted cereal, a juice, two biscuits, a bun, a sauce from a jar, a chocolate bar to get through — by tea you can drift far past guidance while feeling moderate. Feeling fine misleads here. Harm this gradual does not announce itself. It warms slowly, like water around a frog that never notices the heat until the heat is normal.

Your mouth notices before your mind admits. The edge on the tooth. The bleed when you brush too fast. The breath that turns sweet-sour by evening. The child with the peach leaves juice and runs to play. The adult with the packet leaves film and reaches again. One mouth cleans itself with hunger satisfied. The other calls for more of the thing that coated it.

And teeth are only the visible stake. The wider load lands where you cannot feel it dose by dose — liver, vessels, pressure, appetite signals drowned in noise. I will not dress that as a verdict on you today. Population risk is not a sentence on this reader. I raise it to close one door forever. Do not tell yourself your intake is small because you feel normal. Normal intake now sits far above guidance. Feeling normal inside a sweet-flooded world proves nothing about the flood.

Add money. Add hours. Add the low hum of thought — shall I, shan't I, I have been good, just this once. That hum follows you for the rest of your life if you stay hooked. Hear that time-horizon plainly as threat. Every day, forever, paying in coin and attention for something you do not need.

Now turn the horizon over. Picture mornings, shops, meals with that hum gone for the rest of your life. Hear it as reward. Same years. Different tenant. No daily negotiation. No rustle at ten o'clock. Hunger, food, satisfaction, life resumed. That is the other rest-of-life on offer, and it costs you nothing to take.

Which rest do you want? Do not answer with fear. Answer with arithmetic. One column keeps taking. One column stops taking and claims to take nothing real with it. The only question that decides between them is the gift column. If there is a real gift, perhaps the price is worth debating. If there is none, the debate is over.

### WHAT IS THE GIFT, EXACTLY?

Voice your objection for me, out loud in your head, because I held it word for word.

"Fine, it harms. Everything harms. But it gives me something. It is a little pleasure. A pick-me-up. My treat. My fuel through the day."

Thank you. That is honest. Let us hold that something to the light until it shows its shape.

You call it a genuine treat or fuel.

Say the phrase slowly. Taste each word. Genuine. Treat. Or. Fuel. It sounds solid. It sounded solid to me for years. It is the phrase the whole Sweet Con stands on. If BAD SUGAR truly treats you or truly fuels you, then quitting would be loss, and fear would be wisdom, and this book would be another lecture. If it does neither, then you are paying for an empty box.

So which half do you mean today? Treat or fuel? You cannot spend the same coin twice, but the Con lets you blur them, so the dose feels good coming and going. Tired? It lifts. Sad? It comforts. Bored? It entertains. Celebrating? It rewards. A tool that does everything does nothing. A key that opens every lock is a master story, not a master key.

Ask three plain questions. Answer from yesterday's paper, not from the glow in memory.

First. If it is genuine fuel, why are you emptier an hour later? True fuel fills and lasts. Bread when hungry steadies you for hours. Water when thirsty ends thirst. What fills, then hollows within the hour, then calls for itself again by name? That is not fuel behaviour. That is echo behaviour. A tiny low that lifts briefly when fed and returns whether fed or not. You have mistaken the brief quiet for feeding. Those are very different things.

Second. If it is a genuine treat, why does it behave nothing like joy? Joy lingers. Joy shows itself. Joy leaves satisfaction, not hurry. Recall the night posture — standing, quick, sideways from the door, wrapper buried. Recall the daylight version — bar eaten at the desk without looking, taste gone by square two, wrapper gone by square three, eyes already back on the screen. When did a genuine treat need to be eaten blind to be enjoyed? The child with the peach tasted every bite and left half without pain. The trapped eater tastes one bite and needs the box. Which looks like treating?

Third. If the gift is real, why can you never name it without naming yourself? Listen to the sentences. I need something to get me through. I deserve one. I have been good. I want to get off the roller coaster but I need a lift. Every gift-sentence is about your state, not the dose. Tired, tense, bored, virtuous, tired again. The dose is blank. You supply the meaning. Remove your tiredness and the magic goes. Remove the hour and the treasure dulls. A genuine gift carries its own light into any hour. This light moves with your low. That should tell you who owns the light.

There is only one honest answer, and you feel it even while you argue. No fuel leaves you hungrier for fuel. No treat hides and hurries. No gift borrows all its glow from your need.

What you called help was a small body echo read as a message. A hollowness, a fidget, a dip in mood and focus that arrived after the last dose and eased for minutes when you topped up. The easing felt like giving. It was only quieting. The Con translated quiet into kindness and sold you back your own relief as generosity. That translation is the belief we are dissolving. Name both halves now and leave the deep mechanics for later. There is a tiny echo. And there is a big story that calls the echo proof of a gift.

See them apart for a second and the glow thins. The echo is real and small and passing. The gift is story. Story cannot feed. Story can only bill.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Read that verdict twice and let it stand without cushion. Plenty TO you — mouth, mood, money, hours, the hum that follows you year to year. Nothing FOR you — no fuel that lasts, no treat that satisfies, no help that does not return as need. One sentence closes the harm debate forever, because harm no longer needs to outweigh anything. There is nothing on the other pan.

### THE QUESTION IN THE DITCH

Let me leave you with a picture as a question only. We will walk its ground fully together later.

What if you learned that the hand lifting you from the ditch each day was the same hand that pushed you in that morning, and that your gratitude was part of the trick?

Do not answer yet. Do not change what you eat. Just keep yesterday's paper where you can see it. Look at the empty gift column when the glow calls. Ask who dug the hollow the dose then claims to fill.

You came to me braced for a lecture on harm. I give you a harder and kinder task. Stop weighing harm against glow. Weigh glow against nothing and see what remains.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

**SUMMARY**
- Harm alone never freed anyone, because the mind clings to a supposed benefit while conceding the cost.
- An ordinary moderate day leaves mood thinner, mouth coated, wallet lighter and hours spent, with no lasting gain to show.
- Frequent sweet doses bathe teeth in acid and carry a wider bodily load, while feeling normal proves nothing about intake.
- Small daily costs in money and thought become a lifetime rent when the loop stays alive.
- Fuel that empties within the hour behaves like an echo quieted, not a body fed.
- Joy eaten in hurry and secrecy behaves like a loop completed, not a treat enjoyed.
- The gift borrows all its glow from tiredness and timing, which shows the light belongs to you, not the dose.
- The column that matters is benefit, and on honest paper that column stays empty.
