Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The Friday tin and the Thursday creep, the cliff edge in plain daylight, the midnight box emptied before joy ends, the seatbelt in a family car

*Inside the line there is no careful dose — one kept sweet keeps the whole belief alive.*

### THE FRIDAY DEAL

You have seen the night clear. The dark gave the laughter. The people gave the love. The bar only rode along and sent the bill.

And now the old voice puts on a reasonable suit and offers a deal.

"All right. I see it. So I'll just be sensible. Fridays only. Just one. A small treat when I feel I deserve one. Nothing in the house through the week. I'll start properly after the birthday."

I know that voice. We all knew it by heart. We spoke it in kitchens at 10pm with great dignity while the hand was already moving. It sounds mature. It sounds balanced. It sounds like freedom with manners.

It is the trap talking, with your mouth.

Let us live the Friday rule together for a week, hour by hour, and watch what it does to a free human.

Monday morning you are virtuous. The tin is empty by law. You tell colleagues, "I've managed to be good this week." That goodness feels cold and tight by 3pm, that empty, tugging pull rising, thoughts circling the office tin that is not yours. You watch the clock. You count days to Friday. You rehearse what you will allow.

Tuesday you are still good, but goodness has a price. The forbidden glow grows brighter because it is forbidden. A biscuit you would have chewed without noticing last month now shines in memory like a jewel.

Wednesday the rule starts to chew you. "Sugary somethings to get me through." The afternoon dips lower because anticipation sharpens every dip. You tell yourself the wait proves you are in control. The wait proves the opposite. A free eater never waits for food with trembling hands.

Thursday the creep begins. Shall we admit what always happens on Thursday? The glow is so bright you bring it forward a little. Just a taste to take the edge off. Just a small one so Friday will be cleaner. Or you push Friday back in your head to Saturday because Saturday has a party, and a party would be a pity to waste. The line moves because a line drawn inside a con must move. It has no ground to stand on.

Friday arrives at last with fanfare. Paper torn. First bite huge, floodlit by four days of hunger that was never hunger. Sweet, then cloying, then coated, then the old whisper, "I feel like i cant stop." The permitted sliver carries the binge because the loop was primed for days. Wrapper rustle and the box empty before joy ends. That midnight box you know so well is not a failure of character. It is the signature of the Friday rule working exactly as the trap designed it.

Saturday morning you wake coated and foggy and you pay the Friday bill with a new vow. "Right. Strict from Monday. I want to get off the roller coaster." Monday you are virtuous again. The roller coaster you want to leave is the one the rule keeps powering.

Look at that week clean.

Did Friday taste wonderful because Friday was wonderful, or because Monday to Thursday made it precious?

Did the rule reduce the wanting, or did the rule manufacture the wanting and then sell you relief?

If the dose gave nothing on a free evening, what exactly did five days of waiting add to it except fantasy, tension, and a bigger crash?

There is only one honest answer. Moderation does not tame the wanting. Moderation makes it precious, then calls the preciousness proof you need it.

That is how the Sugar Trap keeps its rent paid. Not by force. By exception.

### THE CLIFF EDGE

Come out into daylight with me, because this needs plain seeing.

A friend takes you to a cliff. White rock. Sea far below, hard and loud. Wind tugging the jacket.

He points to the edge and says, cheerful as you like, "Jump. You can jump as long as you only fall a few metres. Be sensible. Fridays only. Just land soft."

You would stare at him. You would ask if he had lost his reason.

"How do I jump and only fall a little? Falling is falling. Gravity does not do Fridays."

He shrugs. "Willpower. Be good through the week. Count. Earn it."

You would not jump. No sane walker jumps off a cliff to prove balance.

Yet we all agreed to that jump with BAD SUGAR and called it maturity. One bar on Friday, provided you do not slide. One pudding at birthdays, provided you do not crave. One biscuit with tea, provided the box stays full after. A safe fall off a trap.

Ask and answer clean, with no dodge allowed.

Can you fall a little? Can a permitted flood re-fire the wanting a little? Can you feed the loop a little and stay outside it?

Did your just-one ever stay one once belief still said the dose helps? Did your Friday ever stay Friday once Thursday learned to bargain?

It is the other way around. The smallness of the dose is what makes it huge. A tiny permitted hit gets days of fantasy before it and hours of chase after it. The miser hoards crumbs and calls crumbs control.

I speak as one who kept a Friday tin for two years and grew heavier, foggier, more obsessed while eating less often. I spoke of balance at lunch and raided at midnight. I was not weak. I was conned by a rule that physics cannot honour.

The fact is flat. Inside BAD SUGAR there is no careful edge to stand on. There is the cliff, and there is the field behind it.

Step back onto the field and the whole dizzy business ends. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

No safe fall. No safe sweet. Marvellous relief to stop negotiating gravity.

### THE HOUSE RULE AND THE EMPTY DRAWER

"Very well," says the voice, shifting ground. "I don't keep it in the house. If it is not in the drawer, I am free."

Are you? Let us walk that house together.

Monday the drawer is empty and the chest puffs a little. Good. You drive home past the bright tills and feel virtuous because you buy nothing. Tuesday the empty drawer feels less like freedom and more like a test. You open it to prove you do not need it. Wednesday you open it twice. Thursday you buy "for the children" and hide the bag high up and call hiding control. Friday the bag is open and the drawer is full and the law is broken by hands that feel oddly separate from you.

Whose hands were those? Yours, translated by belief. An empty drawer never emptied the head. While the head still reads the dose as reward, the legs will walk to the shop in rain. While the head still reads the dose as comfort, the car will turn into the bright forecourt though petrol is low.

I hear the next whisper before you speak it.

"So this book demands everything, for ever? That sounds extreme. That sounds like the diets that broke me. You promised escape, not tyranny."

Hear me kindly and hear me flat, because this is the method itself on trial and I will not dodge it.

Diets broke you by caging hunger by day and flooding by night, by counting and banning real food and calling fear control. This book cages nothing. Hunger stays your guide. Satisfaction stays your rest. Real meals stay favourite, eaten gladly till enough whispers and the hand is glad to rest. The line is drawn only round BAD SUGAR, the engineered doses that never fed hunger and never kept satisfaction.

And totality is not tyranny. Totality is what makes it easy.

A little keeps the Big voice alive, and the Big voice breeds the little grumble. One exception means the head must still argue, weigh, earn, count, mourn. That is the tug and ache you call craving. No exception means the argument ends. The head stops translating bell as must. That grumble is the Nibbler dying, small and brief, already passing. That voice that begs for Fridays is the Sweet Con talking, loud because it is losing.

Would you call it extreme to draw no safe line inside poison in sweet coat? Would you call it tyranny to refuse a small slavery on Fridays? You would call it sanity.

Do you want to spend life guarding a drawer, polishing a tin, watching a calendar for permission to feel foggy? Or do you want to live in a house where the drawer holds nothing worth guarding, mornings clear, shops easy, evenings owned?

Both houses look the same from the street. Only the Con tells you the guarded one is safer.

### TOMORROW AND THE WEAN

Tomorrow is the kindest liar of all.

"I'll quit tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays. After this box. Let me wean down first, taper gentle, then stop clean."

We all spoke that kindness to ourselves while topping up the very low we claimed to be leaving. We said tomorrow because today felt too precious to lose, which proves we still believed there was something to lose.

Ask from weeks you have lived.

Did tomorrow ever arrive with less wanting or more? Did the last box ever stay last once you gave it a farewell glow? Did tapering ever taper, or did smaller hits spaced wider only sharpen fantasy between them, each hit brighter because rarer, each crash deeper because longer waited for?

Watch the wean in your own mouth. Half a spoon in tea, then longing for the full spoon all morning. One square at night, chewed slow while eyes close, then hours of mouth-memory licking crumbs from paper. Cutting down teaches the tongue to worship crumbs. Quitting whole teaches the tongue to forget them.

And birthdays? Holidays? There will always be a cake ahead glowing in the calendar. If celebration needed wrapper, no day would ever be safe to be free. You have seen celebration give everything with no wrapper needed. The calendar is not a wall. It is paper.

Let me press the pincer gently, because it closes this door for good.

If BAD SUGAR gave nothing when taken often, what exactly would it give when taken rarely? If it never fed hunger, never steadied mood, never made love, what magic would rarity lend it? Rarity lends only ceremony to nothing.

You do not wean off a con. You see it. You step out of it. You rejoice that stepping is immediate.

Do you want to pay rent on fog for the rest of the season waiting for a perfect Monday that never comes, bright for minutes and dim for hours, clock-bound, paper-bound? Or do you want to own your mouth from tonight, hunger clean, satisfaction clear, pitying trapped users in the bright aisle rather than envying them?

There is only one honest reading. Tomorrow keeps the belief alive. Totality lets it die.

### THE SEATBELT, NOT THE STEERING WHEEL

Now hear the margin straight, because frightened minds hear totality and picture panic over crumbs. I will not leave you in panic. Panic belongs to the Willpower Method, never to escape.

Picture a family car on an ordinary road. Morning light. Children in the back singing wrong words. Bread bought, fruit bright in the bag.

You clip the seatbelt. Click. You do not plan to crash. You do not drive erratically to test it. You do not steer toward walls to prove it holds. You wear it so an accidental jolt passes without drama.

So with BAD SUGAR. Your body copes with an accidental crumb in a sauce, a trace in bread, a confused spoon at a kind table. No doom falls. No purity is broken. You notice, you smile, you go on with enjoying your meal.

What the seatbelt never means is steering into the wall on Fridays to prove courage. A deliberate special sweet is not a blip. It is hands back on the wheel toward the cliff, belief let back in through the one door you left open. The body shrugs at accident. The mind must never plan one.

If you live with diabetes, take anything that touches blood sugar, are pregnant, or carry an eating disorder past or present, your clinician leads and this book speaks only to belief.

Keep that kindly and keep walking. Guard the belief, not the behaviour with fear. A slip from accident teaches and passes. A planned exception teaches the Con it still owns you.

That is the whole margin, plain and kind. No counting. No confession. No terror over traces. Cheerful driving on the clear road, belt worn, eyes forward, laughter whole.

Feel how light that is. No drawer to guard. No calendar to watch. No Friday to earn. Hunger your guide. Satisfaction your rest. The grumble, when it mutters in the early evening, heard as echo, never as order. The thought, when it crosses, met with quiet delight that you no longer feed it.

You are not white-knuckling toward a distant prize. You are stepping off the cliff field entirely, with relief, with excitement at ground under feet.
