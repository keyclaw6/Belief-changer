Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The Saturday market in morning light, the warm loaf torn open at the table, the plain favourite meal eaten hungry, the fruit bowl in late sun, the quiet belly that says enough

*Real hunger met by real food is the feast itself — BAD SUGAR never fed it, it only interrupted it.*

### THE GREY PLATE FEAR

You arrive here with clear eyes about the afternoon.

You have seen the bar borrow brightness and bill fog. You have seen the tight shoe praised as comfort. You know the lift makes the low.

And still, underneath, a soft fear waits that has nothing to do with energy.

“Without something sweet, eating will be grey.”

“I love food. Dessert is love. If the sweet goes, joy goes.”

“Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters.”

I hear you. We all whispered that. I whispered it for years while I called dessert the best part of the day and feared a life of dry plates and dutiful chewing.

The fact is the reverse. The grey you fear from plain food is the grey BAD SUGAR laid over plain food. Take the noise away and eating brightens. Hunger returns as appetite. Satisfaction returns as pleasure. Real food stands forward as favourite, noticed, savoured, enough.

This hour we do not fight. We eat. We inhabit what was yours before the loop and what waits to be yours the moment the loop stops taxing it.

Sit with me at a real table, hungry, with real food, and watch what your body already knows how to do.

### THE MARKET MORNING

Come with me on a Saturday morning, air cool, baskets high, hunger clean from sleep.

No hum under the ribs. No twitch behind the eyes. No drawer calling. The belly speaks plainly, an open hollow, a willingness. Mouth waters at bread smell from the bakery door. Stomach growls once, honest, unhurried. Feet move a little quicker toward colour.

Tomatoes still warm from the van. Dark green leaves with water on them. Peppers glossy as paint. Apples stacked red and gold, skins tight, scent sharp when lifted. A melon heavy as a baby. Fresh bread with a crust that crackles under the thumb, steam rising when torn. Cheese with a rind. Eggs brown and speckled. Herbs that perfume the hands.

You lift, you smell, you choose. Hunger chooses with you. Eyes brighten at what hunger wants. Hands want to touch. Nose wants to follow. That brightening is appetite, alive, unforced. No wrapper glows. No slogan speaks. The body speaks.

We were taught to call this dull. Plain shopping. Plain cooking. Plain eating. The Sweet Con taught that language, and we repeated it while our mouths watered at the market. Look at the lie in daylight. Is there anything dull in a hungry person among real food? Cheeks lift. Talk quickens. Steps quicken. Children pull at sleeves and point. Old men argue about melons. The whole hall hums with wanting that needs no lesson.

That wanting is yours by nature. It was buried, not broken.

Think how we ate in the loop on such mornings. Sweet coffee on the run. Sweet cereal eaten standing. Juice gulped. A biscuit at eleven to quiet the hollow. Hunger never allowed to arrive clean. Taste never allowed to wake clean. The tongue coated. The belly full yet listening. The market colours seen through glass, admired but not wanted, because want had been narrowed to one narrow sweet note.

Hunger narrowed cannot enjoy. A radio stuck on one station cannot play music.

Let hunger arrive today and the stations return.

I do not prescribe a menu. I describe a capacity you own. Your body makes what it needs for blood and brain from plain meals, so a life without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing in fuel. If you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar, your clinician leads here and I speak only of belief. For the belief the point stands flat: hunger and satisfaction are a complete system. They need no sweet fix to work.

Ask from your own market hours, not from wrappers.

When did an apple last taste wonderful? Not dutiful. Wonderful. Cold, crisp, juice running. When did warm bread need anything added to be loved? Torn, steaming, chewed slowly. When did a plain meal eaten truly hungry leave you brighter than any dessert eaten full?

You know the answer. Hunger was the flavour all along.

### EATEN HUNGRY, STOPPED AT SATISFIED

Now the table. Midday. Hunger ripe.

A plain favourite — market vegetables roasted till edges crisp, fresh bread, a little cheese, fruit after. Nothing staged. Nothing counted. Nothing earned. Hunger at the elbow, company near or quiet of your own, time enough to notice.

First bite hungry. Oh, the noise hunger makes turn to music. The crunch of crust. The oil on the tongue. The sweet of pepper when truly tasted. The salt lifting everything. Chewing slows you without effort. Shoulders drop. Breath deepens. Talk softens between mouthfuls. Eyes close half a second at a good bite. Hands pause, then return with pleasure, not hurry.

Second helping, perhaps, because hunger still speaks. Then a change. Subtle, certain. Flavour dims a shade, not from fault in food but from fullness in you. Chewing slows further. Stomach says, in its quiet voice, enough. Bread laid down mid-slice without pain. Bowl left with colour in it without grief. Body eased, mind clear, hands still.

That dimming is satisfaction. That laying down is freedom practised at every meal since childhood, like the child with the peach, juice on his chin.

Stay with that moment, because your whole fear of grey lives or dies here.

In the loop, that moment rarely arrived. Sweet at the end kept the mouth searching after the belly had spoken. Dessert after fullness. Biscuits after dinner. Grazing after supper though the belly ached. Taste chased past satisfaction till satisfaction itself felt mythical. Then we blamed plain food for failing to satisfy, when satisfaction had been talked over by dose after dose.

Watch the difference clean.

Hunger met by real food satisfies and releases. The hand stops because the body says stop. Attention leaves food gladly for play, work, talk, rest. No rustle follows you from the table. No mental drawer hums. Brightness holds steady, without spike, without fog bill to pay.

A fix never behaves like that. A fix renews want at feeding. It asks louder after the bar than before it. It leaves mouth coated, belly full, mind still listening. Full yet reaching. Fed yet hunting. That hunting we misread as love of food. It was noise over food.

You say, “But dessert is love. The family pudding. The birthday cake. Celebrating with chocolate.”

I ask you gently to separate what the loop glued together.

Was the love in the spoon or in the room? The laughter round the table. The child proud of candles blown. The friend leaned close, shoulder warm. The evening unhurried, faces lit. The hunger met first by the meal, then the sweet laid on top of fullness and praised for the glow the meal and the company and the hunger met had made.

Take the sweet out of that room in memory and replay it. Hunger met. Faces loved. Talk long. Would the room go grey? Would love drain because no sugar coated the end? Or would the meal still satisfy, the company still warm, the night still held?

And take the room out and keep the sweet alone. Late night, alone, standing at the fridge, spoon in a tub, belly already full, eyes a little sore, hurry in the hand, taste gone by the third spoon, shame rising before swallow ends. Is that love? Is that feast? Does the spoon comfort or does it chase?

There is only one honest reading. The meal gave. The company gave. Hunger met gave. The dose rode along and claimed the credit.

It was you that enjoyed, not the hit. It was the hunger, the food, the people. The sweet was only ever sneaking a ride.

### FRUIT, HUNGER, AND THE HONEST LINE

“But fruit is sugar too,” you say, “so nothing matters. If I must watch every apple, I would rather keep the cake.”

Hear the line drawn clean, because the line frees you.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined sugar with added free sugars with junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals with grazing doses of the same. That is the quit target. That alone.

Natural sweetness inside whole fruit, vegetables, plain meals is not the target. It belongs on your table. It belongs in hunger met. An apple eaten hungry, stopped at satisfied, behaves as food — chewed, noticed, ending. A peach stops mid-bite when play calls. Grapes sour after enough. The body speaks and the hand obeys.

Do you see the difference in your own mouth? Fruit eaten hungry refreshes. Doses eaten full chase. One ends at satisfaction. The other forbids satisfaction. One leaves brightness steady. The other spikes then fogs. To call them the same because both taste sweet is to call rain and flood the same because both are wet.

No counting lives here. No good-food sermon. No bad-food shame. I lay no eating plan on you and I moralize no plate. Warm to you always. Harsh only to the con that narrowed your joy to a wrapper.

The fact is settled: Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as permission, because it is permission. Hunger leads. Satisfaction ends. Enjoyment lives in between. Punishment — skipped meals to earn sweet, guilty hours after sweet, Monday vows, forbidden-must-have rebound — belongs to the old method that kept the loop oiled. Leave it with the loop.

You need no new skill to do this. You did it as a small eater before the loop taught hurry. You do it still on bright days when meals are plain and hunger is allowed to speak first. The body remembers. Feet remember walking after shoes removed. Bellies remember hunger after noise removed.

So practise today exactly as hunger asks. Wait till hungry — truly hollow, truly willing, not twitchy, not shaky, not clock-bound. Eat what you want with attention — smell first, first bite slow, chew, pause. Notice flavour brighten then soften. Stop when satisfied — flavour dimmed, belly eased, hand glad to rest. Walk away without commentary. Let brightness hold.

If the old thought rises, “I have been good this week, I deserve a treat,” smile at whose voice that is. Goodness needs no payment in fog. Virtue needs no wage that taxes virtue. Hunger paid by food is reward complete. Let dessert-as-wage fall away with the rest of the wage language. Joy stays. The room stays. The meal stays. The people stay.

Ask three plain questions from your own table, and answer from hours lived.

First. When hunger is clean and food is real, does eating feel grey? Call the market morning. Call the first bite hungry. Call the ease after enough. Was colour missing? Was pleasure missing? Or was pleasure present precisely because no dose talked over it?

Second. When a meal leaves you flat, foggy, still listening though full, what came last? Was it hunger met or doses stacked? Was it bread and vegetables noticed, or sweet upon sweet past satisfaction till taste dulled and hands hurried? Which plate taught grey?

Third. Who stopped easily in your memory? The small eater with juice running who leaves half without pain and runs to play. The grown eater hungry after honest work who eats, sighs, lays the fork down, and turns gladly to talk. Did either need a lesson in stopping? Did either count, fight, earn? Or did hunger and satisfaction lead, and joy follow?

One honest answer covers all three. Hunger enjoyed is feast enough.

### THE TABLE THAT WAITS

I promise you as one who lived both tables.

The loop table: hurry, rustle, second reach, taste fading fast, belly full, mind still hunting, evening ending flat behind the eyes, fog behind tasks, never eased by feeding. That state you know. That state ends when belief ends.

The free table: hunger clean, food noticed, company tasted with food, satisfaction obeyed with pleasure, hands free after, mind free after, brightness steady into afternoon without ticket or drawer.

Nothing is lost moving from one to the other. Everything crowded out returns. Morning appetite. Midday relish. Evening ease. Fruit bright as itself. Bread wonderful as itself. Meals remembered. People met across food rather than chased beside doses.

You feared eating would shrink to duty. It grows back to favourite.

So hear the word for this hour, spoken with cheerful certainty, at the table, hungry, with real food before you:

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food without BAD SUGAR is not grey, the grey was noise laid over appetite.
- Hunger arriving clean makes plain food bright, noticed, and wanted.
- Satisfaction is a quiet dimming of flavour and an eased belly, easy to obey.
- The love at sweet occasions lived in hunger met, meal shared, and company near.
- Fruit and plain meals belong at the free table and end at enough.
- Waiting till hungry, eating with attention, and laying food down at enough restores steady brightness.
