Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — kitchen table at 7am with cold tea and crumbs, a lined notebook with two columns, bathroom sink and toothbrush at night, till receipts in a coat pocket, the sofa at 9pm with wrappers in the cushion crack

*Forget for a moment what it costs you — tell me what it ever actually gives you.*

The Wrong Courtroom

You have come this far with me, and I thank you for it. You have seen the hand move before thought at 10pm. You have stood beside the peach child in daylight. You know you were conned, not weak.

And still, I hear the thought that holds you. Let me give it voice, because it was mine for years.

“All right. Perhaps I am caught. Perhaps it does me harm. But it still does something for me. It still helps. Life would be greyer without it.”

I do not blame you for that thought. We all spoke it. We spoke of sugary somethings to get me through as if they were tools. We said treat when you feel you deserve one as if it were wisdom. I have said every one of those lines in my own voice.

But look at the courtroom that thought sets up. Harm on one side, benefit on the other, scales in the middle. Harm versus benefit. Cost versus pleasure. If harm grows heavy enough, perhaps you will act. If benefit still glows, perhaps you will wait.

That courtroom will keep you trapped for the rest of your life if you let it.

Why? Because harm can always be argued away. Harm is tomorrow. Harm is somebody else. Harm is a warning you can bargain with. You can tell yourself you feel fine. You can tell yourself others eat more. You can tell yourself you will handle tomorrow when it comes. In the harm courtroom, the con always finds a lawyer.

So I refuse that courtroom. I move you to a different one.

I am not asking whether BAD SUGAR does more harm than good. I am asking a simpler, colder question. What good is there at all?

Put harm to one side completely. Forget teeth for a moment. Forget weight. Forget money. Forget lectures you have heard a hundred times. Keep only one column on the page. Benefit. What does it do FOR you?

If there is a real benefit, it should stand up alone, without needing harm to look the other way. A true friend does not need you to forget his thefts in order to praise him. A real pleasure does not need a harm debate to survive.

Ask yourself plainly, from your own life. When you take a dose, what arrives that was not already yours?

Does a fully free pleasure need defending in this way? You never ask what good a peach does while you eat it hungry in August. You never weigh harm against benefit while you eat fresh bread with appetite. You eat, you enjoy, you stop, you move on. The question of benefit never rises because benefit is present and obvious.

Why does the question rise here? Why does BAD SUGAR need a courtroom at all?

That unease you feel as you read is not guilt. It is honesty waking up.

A Tuesday On Paper

Let us do what investigators do. Let us take one ordinary Tuesday and put it on paper. Not a bad day. Not a binge day. A normal day, the kind you call moderate, the kind where you tell yourself you have it under control.

Morning. Tea with two sugars and a sweetened cereal eaten standing, scrolling. Mid-morning, a biscuit with the second cup, taken from the tin without much thought. Lunch, plain enough, with a fizzy drink because water feels dull. Afternoon, the drawer opens at 3pm. Chocolate bar, ten minutes of buzz, then fog. Evening, pudding after dinner because it is there, because the day was long, because celebrating with chocolate is what tired people do. Night, a small handful of something from the cupboard while the television murmurs.

Nothing dramatic. No box emptied in shame. Just doses spaced through the day like fence posts.

Now take a lined notebook, as I did, and draw two columns. Left column: what it did TO you. Right column: what it did FOR you.

Left column fills fast. Mouth sticky after each hit. Teeth filmed. Energy up then down then up then down. The roller coaster you know by name: short term lift then fast drop, then the I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling arriving an hour later wearing hunger's clothes. Mood thinner by late afternoon. Wallet lighter by pounds you never notice until the month ends. Time gone in small reaches, buys, unwrappings, tidying of traces. Sleep a little lighter. Morning mouth a little sour.

Be careful here. I am speaking of the wide pattern, not pointing at you, and if changing food carries medical risk for you, your clinician leads.

Right column. Benefit. Be strict with me, as a friend would be strict. Write only what BAD SUGAR itself added, nothing borrowed from the moment around it.

Morning cereal. Was the pleasure the sugar, or was it breakfast after night hunger, warm kitchen, first quiet minutes? Mid-morning biscuit. Was it taste, or was it pause, stretch, chat, hands resting? Afternoon bar. Was it fuel, or was it ten minutes away from the screen, a breath, a change of air? Evening pudding. Was it love, or was it meal, company, sofa, laughter, the day closing?

Do you see what happens when you isolate the variable? The warmth stays with the scene when you lift the dose out. The scene gave. The dose rode along.

We never noticed because we never separated them. We credited the powder for the work of the hour.

I know this is hard to accept. I know the mind protests. Stay with the page a little longer. Look at your own Tuesday. Where is the entry that belongs to BAD SUGAR alone?

Teeth, Wallet, Hours

Let us linger on three entries from the left column, because they are photographable. You can see them with your own eyes without taking my word.

First, teeth. The fact is, frequent free-sugar doses feed the acid in plaque that eats at enamel through the day. Brushing and fluoride matter, they do real work, and still the mechanism runs: sugar in, acid up, surface softened, decay given its opening where doses come often. Ask your own mouth. That filmed feeling after a sweet drink. That dull ache after a sticky evening. That visit where the dentist looks, pauses, and you already know what the pause means before a word is spoken. Is that doing something FOR you?

Second, measure. Public guidance for free sugars sets the daily ceiling low, below a tenth of calories, around fifty grams, with added benefit below half that again. The fact is, ordinary eating now sits far above that guidance, not because you are extreme but because sweet hits hide in everything. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low. The water can warm so slowly you never notice. I speak here of the picture across whole streets and shops, not a verdict on your body. But ask your own bin and your own receipts. How many wrappers left the house this week? How many bottles, bars, packs passed through the till uncounted because each one was small? Small, repeated often, is how large is built.

Third, hours and pounds. Add the Tuesday doses across a year. Money first. Two pounds here, three there, the bar with the petrol, the cake with the coffee, the sharing bag that was never shared. It looks like nothing until you multiply. Then time. The walk to the shop in rain to fetch back the very thing you told yourself you would not keep in the house. The minutes staring into the cupboard. The minutes regretting. The minutes promising to be good this week. All paid, daily, for the rest of your life if the loop holds, for something you cannot name a single benefit for when pressed.

Which brings the question home. If a friend charged you daily for nothing, emptied your pockets coin by coin, filmed your teeth, blurred your afternoons, and asked for thanks, would you call him a friend?

And here is the seed question I want left ringing while we work, to be met fully later when the ground is ready. What if the hand that lifts you out each afternoon is the same hand that pushed you in that morning?

Do not answer yet. Keep auditing.

What Do You Call It

We need a name for the thing we are hunting, because a hunt without a name chases fog.

We call it pleasure. We call it comfort. We call it help. Listen to our own dialect: I want to get off the roller coaster. I’ve managed to be good this week. Treat when you feel you deserve one. Those lines sound like my voice. They were the voice of old teaching laid over mine.

Strip the kind words away and look at the object itself. What do you believe BAD SUGAR is?

You believe it is a genuine treat or fuel.

Say that phrase slowly to yourself, because everything turns on it. A genuine treat or fuel. A treat means a real extra joy, something life would be poorer without. Fuel means real energy, something work would stop without. If BAD SUGAR is either of those, your caution makes sense. Who would throw away joy or fuel?

But if it is neither, what is left to defend?

Look at treat first, from your own diary, not from theory.

A genuine treat behaves in a certain way. It delights more when rare. It satisfies when done. It leaves gratitude behind. It does not demand repetition an hour later. It does not hide its traces. It does not leave the thought, I feel like i cant stop, ringing after the wrapper opens.

Did your Tuesday treat behave like that? Did the first biscuit taste bright and the fourth taste brighter? Or did taste fade while reaching continued, joy falling as the hand kept moving? Did you finish with thanks, or with rustle and faint disappointment that it was already gone, as if pleasure left before crumbs did? Which one looks like a treat and which one looks like a pull wearing treat’s clothes?

We were taught to call it a treat when you feel you deserve one. Notice the timing of that teaching. It arrives after effort, after virtue, after being good. It turns goodness into a debt that only a dose can pay. Is that the behavior of joy? Or is that a story laid over a tug to make the tug feel noble?

Now look at fuel, again from your own afternoon, not from slogans.

Real fuel behaves in a certain way. It builds steadily. It lasts for hours. It leaves you clear. It does not drop you lower than before you took it. Bread eaten hungry does that. A plain meal eaten hungry does that. Rest does that.

Did your 3pm hit behave like that? Ten minutes bright, then fog, then the drawer opening again at 4pm. Short term lift then fast drop. You called that energy because the lift was loud and the drop was quiet, and you blamed the drop on the day, the job, the age, the weather, anything but the lift that made it. If fuel leaves you flatter, foggier, and reaching again within the hour, in what sense is it fuel?

We spoke of sugary somethings to get me through. Through what? Through the low that the last something left behind. We never saw the circle because we only looked at half of it.

I speak to you warmly and flatly at once: the object you defend has never behaved as the name you give it. It does not treat. It does not fuel. It borrows the warmth of the moment and the brightness of relief, then sends the bill a little later.

That is why the phrase matters. Once you see that a genuine treat or fuel would satisfy and strengthen, and this neither satisfies nor strengthens, the name falls off. What falls off was never yours. It was paint.

Name One Thing

I will now press you as a friend presses, kindly and without letting go.

Name one thing it does FOR you.

Not TO you. FOR you.

I hear your answers rising. Let me meet each one with a question, and you answer from what you have lived.

“It gives me pleasure.” Does it? Or does it give you relief from the flat, restless pull the last dose left, relief you mistake for pleasure because the pull stops for minutes? When you eat a plain favourite meal truly hungry, pleasure rises, peaks, settles into satisfaction that lasts. When you take a sweet hit between meals, pleasure spikes, vanishes, and calls again. Which one looks like pleasure and which one looks like a bell rung to stop a noise the bell itself started?

“It gets me through stress.” Does it? Or does it pause stress for the length of a chew while adding money worry, tooth worry, mood dip and self-reproach to the pile you must get through tomorrow? Did the strictest weeks bring the calmest evenings, or the loudest collapses? You own the evidence. The thing that claims to carry you leaves you heavier.

“It is my little reward.” Reward for what, decided by whom? Who taught you that being good this week earns a dose that makes next week harder to be good in? Who profits when virtue is paid in wrappers? Look at the arithmetic of that reward across a lifetime. Paid daily, for the rest of your life, for a glow that fades before swallowing ends — or kept in the pocket, with mornings clear and food enjoyed as favourite, for the rest of your life free of the bill. Which rest-of-life do you want when both are laid bare?

You see what those answers do. They do not shame you. They clear you. Each objection, honestly answered, hands credit back where it belongs: to the meal, the pause, the company, the body that was already yours.

We fell into the same hole together. I defended the same object with the same lines. I told myself I loved the taste while masking half the tastes I claimed to love under sugar. I told myself I needed the lift while never noticing the lift never rose above where I had stood before the first dose of the day. I was not lying. I was misreading, in good faith, on false information. A good-faith error corrects the moment better information lands.

So land it now. Look at your Tuesday page again. Left column crowded. Right column empty. Not thin. Empty.

The verdict stands on evidence you already own.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Let that verdict stand. Do not reopen it.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit

**SUMMARY**
- The harm debate keeps the trap alive because harm can be bargained with tomorrow.
- One ordinary day on paper shows doses spaced like fence posts with mood dipping after each lift.
- Frequent sweet doses feed plaque acid and sit far above low public guidance, while brushing still does its own real work.
- Wallet and hours leak coin by coin and minute by minute for no named return.
- Treat would satisfy and fuel would strengthen, and the daily hit does neither.
- The benefit column stays empty when credit for warmth is handed back to the moment around the dose.
