MOVE G15-M1: MISSING — GSBS 15 opens by putting the food industry and every other addict aside and commanding the reader to reverse brainwashing for themselves alone (“DO IT FOR YOURSELF”; “Put everyone else to one side. This is your happiness we’re talking about”); our chapter never turns that corner and stays entirely on warm exception scenes (cinema, birthday, pay-and-comfort lines) without the selfish-for-once frame that freedom is for the reader, not for reforming the world or carrying others.

MOVE G15-M2: PARTIAL — GSBS 15 names a deliberate two-pronged attack: first re-see nature’s foods as wonderful packages, second re-see supposed favourites as bland, advertised junk through plain taste tests; our chapter kills belief hard in the exception rooms and once states that killing belief starves the body echo (“You kill belief and the body echo starves”), but it never lands Carr’s paired prongs — no exploration of real food as gain, no plain-starch taste confrontation — so the turn is gestured, not performed as the two-action reversal GSBS 15 owns.

MOVE G15-M3: PRESENT — GSBS 15 separates happy occasion from the substance (“Usually, if any social occasion is a happy one it’s not because of the food, it’s because of the company”; “Take away the cake and biscuits but keep your favourite people and the occasion will still be a happy one”); our chapter performs that move at full force across cinema and birthday, with the explicit verdict that scene owned the glow and dose only rode along: “The scene gave everything. The dose only rode along in your hand and claimed the applause on the way out”; “The cake did not carry love. The hands carried love. The singing carried love”; and the summary line “The cinema glow came from rest, laughter, nearness and story, never from melt.”
