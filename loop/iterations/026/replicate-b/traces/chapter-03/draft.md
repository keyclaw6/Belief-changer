Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A Tuesday kitchen table, wrappers lined up in daylight
- A desk drawer, coins and receipts and sticky keys
- A bathroom mirror late at night, toothbrush in hand
- An ordinary day logged hour by hour, mood against dose
- A quiet question about a ditch and an outstretched hand

*Forget for a moment what it costs you — tell me plainly what it ever gave you.*

YOU WERE CAUGHT, NOW LOOK CLEARLY

You have seen that you were caught, not weak. You have watched your own hand move before decision at 10pm and understood that a con stood behind it.

Good. Keep that clarity with you, because we need it now.

Up to now you have argued with yourself the wrong way round. You have said, I know sweet hits harm me a little, but surely the pleasure outweighs the harm. You have balanced harm on one side and joy on the other, and told yourself the scales just tip in favour of carrying on.

We believed the same. We all did. We told ourselves we knew the price — weight creeping up, teeth duller, money leaking away, afternoons foggy — and we paid it willingly because we thought we were buying something real in return.

I am going to change the question. I am not asking whether it harms you more than it helps you. I am asking what help there ever was at all.

Hear your own voice as I hear it:

“I know it is bad for me, but I enjoy it. Life would be grey without my treats. I need something sweet to get me through.”

“I have managed to be good this week. I deserve one.”

“Celebrating with chocolate just makes the evening.”

I do not blame you for those thoughts. We all thought them. I thought them for years with my hand in a packet. I am asking you to put them on the table in daylight and test them like an investigator, not defend them like a prisoner.

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you something, why must you always apologize for taking it? Why the hiding, the secret eating, the wrappers pushed deep? Why do you need to earn it by being good first? You never have to earn a peach when you are hungry. You never apologize for fresh bread after a long walk. What kind of gift needs an excuse before it and guilt after it?

And if one small dose truly satisfied, why does one call for six? Why does the evening box end empty? Why does the roller coaster keep rolling — short term lift then fast drop, then that I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling roaring back louder?

Those are not accusations. They are plain questions, and there is only one honest answer that fits them all. But do not take my word for it. Let us audit one ordinary day together, calmly, without scolding, and see what is actually in each column.

AN ORDINARY TUESDAY, LOGGED HONESTLY

Take an ordinary Tuesday. Not Christmas. Not a binge you are ashamed of. Just a normal moderate day, the kind you call harmless.

Morning. Tea with two sugars, a sweetened cereal bar on the way to work because breakfast was rushed. Mid-morning, biscuits with coffee in the office, three without noticing because the plate was there. Lunch, a sandwich and a fizzy drink, then something sweet to finish because lunch does not feel finished without it. Afternoon, chocolate from the machine to get through the flat hours. Evening, dessert because you cooked well and the family was round the table. Night, a few sweets while watching television, hardly counted.

Now log it the way a kind detective logs, not to punish but to see.

Mood. Bright for ten minutes after each hit, then flat again. Foggy by four. Twitchy and snappy by six. Flat, foggy and never satisfied by ten, staring at the television with crumbs on your front. Did any dose leave you clear and grounded for hours the way a proper meal does? Or did each quiet last minutes and hand you back a louder hum?

Teeth. I am not writing your dental chart here, I am describing what happens across millions of mouths. Frequent free-sugar doses feed the plaque acid that eats at enamel and opens the road to decay. Brushing and fluoride matter, dentists matter, other things shape whether a cavity forms. No single mouth is doomed by a single sweet. But bathe teeth in sweet hits all day, every day, and you keep that acid coming. Look in the mirror with the toothbrush in your hand and ask what those daily doses did TO those teeth. Did they ever do anything FOR them?

Wallet. Add the coins. The bar, the biscuits, the fizzy drink, the machine chocolate, the dessert, the night sweets. Small money each time. Then multiply by weeks, by years. What else leaked with the money — time queuing, time hunting, time thinking about sweet hits when you wanted to think about your life? Did any dose ever pay you back in time or money? Or did it take a little of both every single time and leave nothing to show?

Time and attention. Count the minutes spent picturing the cupboard, planning the treat when you feel you deserve one, promising Monday will be different, forgiving Friday because you were good. Sugary somethings to get me through — how much of Tuesday was spent getting through, rather than living?

Put the columns side by side.

TO you: flat mood and fog, tooth acid, money gone, hours spent circling food, evening shame, morning vow.

FOR you: what? Name one thing that stayed. Name one hour that stayed bright because of a dose. Name one tooth helped, one coin saved, one worry lifted and left lifted.

You cannot, and that inability is not your failure. It is evidence.

We ran that audit together for years without seeing it. We wrote harm in small letters and benefit in large ones because we were told benefit was there. Remove what you were told and look at what you lived. The harm column is crowded. The benefit column is empty.

DO NOT BALANCE — ASK FOR THE BENEFIT

This is the turn that frees you.

As long as you ask, does it do more harm than good, you stay trapped. Harm can be argued away. You feel fine, so you must be fine. Someone thinner eats more. Someone older lived long and ate cake daily. Guidance can be dismissed as nagging. Dentists can be answered with a better toothpaste. The trap loves the harm debate because the harm debate never ends.

I am asking a different question, and it ends the debate in a minute.

Forget harm versus benefit. Ask for the benefit alone.

What does BAD SUGAR actually do FOR you that you could not have without it? Not what does it cost. What does it give?

If a friend sold you a machine that took money every day, made noise all night, needed constant attention, stained your teeth, left you foggy, and never once produced anything you could hold, would you keep it because it did not burn the house down? Would you say, well, it has not killed me yet, so the benefit must outweigh the harm? You would laugh. You would ask, what does it do FOR me? And when the answer was nothing, you would have the thing taken away and rejoice at the space left behind.

Why give a dose a privilege you would never give a machine?

The official guidance says keep free sugars below a tenth of daily energy, about fifty grams, with further benefit below half that, because ordinary intakes sit far above both. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low. Most of us live far above that line while calling ourselves moderate. I tell you that not to frighten you into change. I am not asking you to change from fear. I am telling you so you stop trusting feeling fine as proof of benefit. Feeling fine while the acid keeps coming and the money keeps leaking proves nothing except that gradual harm is quiet.

Do you see? Even if harm were zero, the question would stand. Zero harm plus zero benefit still equals no reason to feed a loop that empties boxes and crowds minds.

That is why I press you. Not, look how it hurts you. But, show me where it helped you.

You enter this chapter saying, it may harm but benefits outweigh. You will leave it having to name one real benefit — and finding none. That emptiness is not loss. It is daylight.

A GENUINE TREAT OR FUEL

Let us name the ghost you have been paying.

When we said benefit, we meant two hazy promises tangled together. We called sweet hits a treat, a little love, a reward that made life sweeter. And we called them fuel, a lift that got us through the afternoon and put energy back in. A treat for the heart. Fuel for the body.

Say it plainly with me, as we all said it: we believed BAD SUGAR was a genuine treat or fuel.

Hold that phrase up to Tuesday and watch what happens to it.

A genuine treat should leave you richer than before. Did Tuesday leave you richer? You had seven small moments of smoothness and ended the night flat, foggy and never satisfied, promising to be good tomorrow. A treat that leaves the eater flatter than it found him is not a treat. It is a trick wearing treat clothes.

A genuine fuel should carry you further without demanding more fuel to fix the fuel. Did Tuesday carry you? Each hit gave minutes of buzz and handed back hours of dip and renewed tug. You needed more to get through what more had made harder to get through. Real food eaten hungry does the opposite. It grounds you, warms you, releases you for hours. You think about it with pleasure while you eat, then you stop thinking about food at all. That is what fuel does. It finishes its job and lets you live.

So what did you actually experience? Not treat. Not fuel. A brief quieting of a hum, then the hum returning louder. A brief smoothing of a restlessness, then restlessness returning with interest.

I know this is hard to accept. Your mind objects: but I do taste something sweet, surely that taste is real. Taste is real. No one denies sweet tastes sweet. A peach tastes sweet. Fresh bread tastes wonderful hungry. Warmth, company, rest after work — those are real. The question is never, was there a taste. The question is, what did the dose add that was not already there in mouth, body, moment, company?

Keep that phrase with you now as a test, not as abuse. Whenever the old glow whispers, ask it: are you a genuine treat or fuel, or are you the memory of a hum briefly quieted? You will find the glow has no answer, because there was never anything there except relief from a need the last dose helped create. The glow was the quiet minute mistaken for a gift.

Once you see it as a phrase, you can test it in a second. In the shop. At the table. At 10pm with the cupboard open. Is this a genuine treat or fuel? Or is this another dose asking to be fed? The answer comes back clean every time, because truth is clean.

WHAT WOULD YOU CALL THE HAND THAT HELPS AFTER IT HURTS?

Let me ask you a quiet question, and answer it honestly.

What would you call a man who tripped you into a ditch each morning on your way to work, then hurried down with an outstretched hand to pull you out, and waited to be thanked for his kindness?

Would you call him rescuer? Would you feel grateful? Would you pay him daily for his help getting through?

Or would you see in a flash that the rescue and the push belonged to the same hand, and that gratitude was the final part of the trick?

Hear that question as kindness to you and harshness to the trick. You are not foolish for having thanked the hand. We all thanked it. We mistook the brief pull-out for help, because we never saw the secret push that put us in the ditch an hour before. The push was quiet. The pull felt warm. Who would suspect?

Keep that question with you. You do not need to answer it fully tonight. The full sight of that hand belongs a little further on, and you will see it clearly when we come to it. For now let it sit beside Tuesday's empty column and do its quiet work.

If the thing you thank for lifting you is the same thing that lowered you an hour before, what exactly are you thanking? If the thing you call treat leaves you flatter, and the thing you call fuel leaves you emptier, what exactly is it giving?

There is only one honest answer, and it is not shameful. You were thanking a push you had not yet seen. Once seen, thanks ends. Not with anger at yourself. With clarity about the hand.

FOR THE REST OF YOUR LIFE — WHICH ONE?

Now stretch Tuesday out, because the trap hates a long view.

You are moderate. You are careful. You count. You earn. You tell yourself a few doses a day are your pleasure and your right. Very well. Multiply that moderate Tuesday by weeks, by years, by decades.

Do you want to go on paying with foggy afternoons and acid-washed teeth and leaking money and circling thoughts for the rest of your life for something that never once paid you back?

Look steadily at that picture. Not as a threat from me. As arithmetic from your own hand. The same hum, the same brief quiet, the same fast return, the same empty box at night, the same morning vow, the same hidden wrappers, the same flat, foggy and never satisfied evenings. Every single day forever for something you do not even need. That is what continuing means. Continuing is the painful sacrifice, not stopping.

Now turn the picture over.

Do you want to walk clear of that circling, with hunger clean and satisfaction quiet and evenings your own, for the rest of your life with the whole con behind you?

Same length of life. Two opposite lives inside it. One hooked, paying daily for an empty column. One free, keeping the money and hours and clarity that were always yours. Both last the same years. Only one gives the years back to you.

I am not asking you to earn freedom by suffering. I am asking you to see that freedom is the gain and the trap is the loss. We feared life without sweet hits would be grey. The fact is the grey was Tuesday itself — the fog after the buzz, the tug before the dose, the counting and earning and hiding. Colour was never in the packet. Colour was in hunger met, in meals enjoyed, in evenings undivided.

Choose with open eyes. Which rest of life do you want? The one that keeps taking TO you and never gives FOR you? Or the one where you get on with enjoying your life and leave the empty column empty for good?

IT DOES PLENTY TO YOU — NOW DEMAND WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU

This is where the axis turns and locks.

You came in conceding harm but clinging to benefit. You have logged mood, teeth, wallet, time. You have named the ghost. You have felt the ditch question stir. You have stretched the picture long.

Now land the verdict with me, flat and settled, the way truth lands when argument is done:

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Say it again slowly, because it carries the whole book in one breath. It does plenty TO you — to mood, to teeth, to money, to hours, to peace. It does nothing FOR you — no lasting brightness, no real fuel, no love added, no hour saved. Plenty TO. Nothing FOR.

If that is true, what is there to balance? What is there to weigh? What is there to lose?

There is nothing to lose and everything to be examined further with joy, because a thing that gives nothing cannot be missed. You cannot miss an empty column. You can only rejoice at not paying it any more.

So I give you the rule that holds this chapter in your pocket. Take it as a command from one who escaped by it, not as advice to consider.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

Use it everywhere from tonight. In the aisle, do not ask, will this harm me. Ask, what will this do FOR me. At the desk, do not count harm. Ask for benefit. At 10pm with the rustle familiar as your own name, do not lecture yourself about teeth. Ask calmly, what did the last dose ever do FOR me, and what will this one add? The answer is always the same clean nothing, and nothing has no power to tempt once seen.

You do not need to fight tonight. You do not need to purge or punish. Carry on exactly as normal while you read. All you do is judge by the new axis and let the empty column stare back at you. The looking does the freeing. Behaviour follows belief the way a shadow follows a body.

You were never weak. The belief was false. The column is empty. Rejoice at the sight, because sight is the beginning of escape.

**SUMMARY**
- The harm debate never ends, so the question changes to benefit alone.
- An ordinary Tuesday shows plenty taken in mood, teeth, money and time, with no lasting gain left behind.
- Frequent sweet doses keep tooth acid coming, while brushing and other factors shape the outcome.
- Ordinary intakes sit far above guidance, and feeling fine does not mean intake is low.
- The old benefit shrinks to a brief quiet mistaken for help, not lasting treat or fuel.
- A rescuer who secretly causes the fall deserves clarity, not thanks.
- Stretching Tuesday across a lifetime shows continuing as the costly sacrifice.
- Plenty is done TO you each day, with nothing lasting done FOR you.
