Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- A grey plate feared in advance, dessert called love
- Saturday market stalls, warm bread, ripe fruit in the hand
- A plain table eaten hungry, noticed bite by bite
- Hunger rising clean and satisfaction closing like a door
- An evening table where company laughed and the sweet sneaked a ride
- Apples and berries that never ordered a second reach

*Real hunger met with real food is the feast itself — BAD SUGAR never made it sweeter.*

THE GREY PLATE YOU FEAR

You arrive here with the lift seen for what it is.

You watched the three o'clock buzz thin into wool. You saw the ten bright minutes borrow from the fifty flat ones. You know in your body that the bar never fed you. That seeing stays. I do not ask you to revisit it. I ask you to walk forward with me into the fear that remains.

Hear it plainly, in your own voice:

"Without something sweet, eating will be grey."

"Pudding is love. Dessert is the point. Take that and what is left to enjoy?"

"I've managed to be good this week and food already feels dull. If this is freedom, where is the joy?"

I honour that fear. We all carried it. I carried it for years. I told friends I lived to eat. I meant I lived for the flood at the end of eating. I called myself a food lover while hardly tasting food at all. I was not foolish and you are not foolish. We were conned about where joy lives.

We learned to call the sweet the meal and the meal the waiting. Roast and bread and greens were the corridor. The shining door at the end was sugar. Take the door away and the corridor looks long and grey. Who would want to walk it for the rest of your life?

Look closer with me, as an investigator who has already proved strong. That grey picture was never drawn from eating. It was drawn from eating without hunger and eating without stopping, then blaming plain food for the dullness the loop created.

When you graze all day on doses and hits, hunger never arrives clean. The stomach stays crowded and twitchy. The tongue stays coated. Real food eaten on top of that fog tastes like cardboard because the mouth is coated and the body is not asking. Then the sweet arrives, bright and loud against the dullness, and seems by contrast to be the only thing with flavour. We concluded food is grey. The fact is fog is grey.

Ask yourself from your own table. Did you ever taste bread properly while already full of biscuits? Did you ever meet an apple with true hunger after an afternoon of topping? And when the mouth is sticky and the belly is crowded, what could taste bright except the loudest hit of all?

There is only one honest answer that fits all three. You never tested real food. You tested fogged food.

You fear a life of grey plates because you ate grey while trapped. You have not yet eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied and watched what eating does when the loop is not shouting over it. That is what this hour is for. Not a lesson. A meal.

A MARKET MORNING, HUNGRY

Come with me on an ordinary Saturday, no ceremony, no virtue, no rules pinned to the fridge.

Morning hunger wakes first. Not the hurried, gnawing, must-have-something-now clamour that names one packet only. The other one. Slow. General. Patient. An emptiness that would welcome anything warm and plain. The body asking, not ordering.

I walk hungry among stalls. Market vegetables heaped and damp from the morning wash. Carrots with tops still on. Tomatoes that smell of sun when you lift them. Dark greens, peppers, mushrooms, potatoes with earth still in the creases. Bread stacked warm, crust cracking as it cools, steam rising when a loaf is torn. Fruit piled high — apples, pears, grapes, berries that stain the fingers.

No counting. No guard. No voice saying be good. Only hunger and plenty.

I fill a bag with what pulls the eye. I am not following a plan. I am following appetite. The body knows what it wants when the hum is not shouting over it. I take what looks alive and let the rest stay.

Home. A kitchen with light in it. I cook simply, because hunger is the seasoning and needs little help. Vegetables in a pan with oil and salt. Bread torn, not sliced. Fruit washed and left whole in a bowl. Nothing weighed. Nothing forbidden. Nothing earned.

I sit when hunger calls, not when the clock orders. First bite noticed.

See what happens when hunger meets food, hour by hour in the mouth, not in theory.

First mouthfuls bright. The crunch of pepper. Juice running. Warm crumb with chew in it. Salt and oil and sweet of carrot that needs no help. The tongue wakes. The nose wakes. Pleasure arrives at once, without a wrapper, without a flood, without a promise.

Middle of the plate. Pace slows on its own. Shoulders drop. Conversation, if someone shares the table, grows easy. The food holds attention without demanding it. You taste and talk and taste again. No hurry to finish. No hurry to fetch more. Each bite a little less urgent than the last, a little more savoured.

Then the quiet signal. Not full to bursting. Not stuffed. Satisfied. The edge leaves hunger. The next bite would be less good than this one was. The body closes the door gently. You stop because stopping feels right, not because a rule says stop.

After. No buzz. No fireworks. No ten bright minutes to admire. Only ease. Hands steady. Head clear enough to forget about the head. Hours pass without the drawer lit in the mind. Hunger returns clean when it returns, glad of the next meal, any meal.

That is eating as the body built it. Noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied.

Ask yourself. When did a sweet ever leave you like that? When did a box emptied in the evening ever close gently? And when did a packet ever let you forget about food for hours after?

Only one honest answer fits. Real food satisfies. Doses repeat.

HUNGER KNOWS ITS JOB

"But hunger frightens me," you say. "That empty feeling means I must eat at once or I will fade. Surely I need quick sugar to keep going?"

I hear you, because I feared hunger too. We all learned to read every hollow as emergency.

Let me show you hunger clean, so fear leaves the room.

True hunger is slow and general. It grows like light in the morning. It does not name a brand. It does not point to a cupboard. It welcomes bread and soup and fruit with equal gladness. It sharpens taste rather than dulling it. A hungry mouth finds an apple loud.

The hurried hunger is another creature. Fast. Specific. Twitchy. It arrives by the clock, points at one thing only, and will not be answered for long by anything else. It calls before the stomach speaks and returns an hour after being fed. Does that look like a body asking for dinner? Or does that look like a loop asking for renewal?

The fact is your body makes what it needs from plain meals. Take BAD SUGAR away and nothing is missing. Glucose the blood needs is built from the food you eat when hungry. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

See the kindness in that. You are not built to run on packets. You are built to run on hunger and satisfaction. Hunger tells you when. Satisfaction tells you when to stop. Taste tells you it is good. No weighing. No virtue. No punishment.

We lost trust in those signals because the loop shouted over them for years. We ate by time and by twitch and by rule. Hungry we topped. Full we reached. Empty we feared. The signals never left. We only stopped hearing them under noise.

Watch them return. Let hunger come till it is plain. Eat what you want till satisfaction closes. Then live till hunger returns. That rhythm carried you as a small eater and carries every clear eater now. It needs no teaching. It needs quiet to be heard.

Consider the proof you already own. On days you ate plain meals hungry, did hunger ever fail to return in time? Did the body ever forget to ask? And did satisfaction ever fail to arrive unless the meal was followed by a flood that washed it away?

Only one honest answer fits. The system works when the flood is gone.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is not a diet. That is the body heard at last.

SATISFIED IS A FULL STOP

There is a moment in every clear meal I want you to notice with me, because it changes everything about joy.

It arrives without drama. The plate half full or half empty, no matter. The next mouthful looks less bright than the last one was. The belly feels easy, neither empty nor crowded. The mind drifts toward the day, not toward more. You put the fork down. You breathe. You are done.

No decision. No battle. No voice counting bites. Satisfaction closed. Hunger will open again when ready.

Hold that moment beside the other ending you know too well.

Evening. Permitted single biscuit. Wrapper rustle. Hand moving before decision has finished forming. Sweet on the tongue, then hurry, then another, then the box light. No slowing of pace. No lessening of urge. No gentle close. Only a sudden stop when the box is empty or nausea rises, with regret arriving before swallowing ends.

Mark the difference clean. One ending comes from inside. The other never comes.

Real food has a full stop built in. Taste fades as need fades. Pleasure completes itself. You stop because joy is finished, the way a song finishes.

BAD SUGAR has no full stop. Taste dulls while urge grows. Pleasure never completes because relief renews need. You stop because supply ends or shame interrupts, the way noise stops when the plug is pulled, not the way music ends.

That is why trapped eaters call themselves greedy and clear eaters never think of greed at all. Greed was never in you. Endlessness was in the dose. Put endlessness away and the eater you are stops on time without trying.

We blamed our character for the empty box. We praised our character when we managed a week of guard duty. Both judgments missed the object. Character never made the box empty. The dose made finishing impossible. Character never made the week hard. The method made stopping feel like loss.

See yourself clean at a plain table. You stop mid-plate and rise to the day, like a toddler leaving a peach half-eaten to chase pigeons across the grass. No finishing from duty. No reaching from fog. Hunger led. Satisfaction closed. That is not childish. That is human, before noise taught us to finish what never finishes.

LOVE WAS NEVER IN THE WRAPPER

"But dessert is love," you say. "Celebrating with chocolate. The cinema with sweets. Food is love and you would leave me loveless."

I hear you, because I guarded that one longest. We all kept one shining exception where we swore the glow belonged to sugar.

Bring that table into the room and let us look at it whole.

Evening. People you love around wood. Laughter. Warmth. A meal eaten hungry, plates cleared, bodies eased. Then the shared sweet placed in the middle, candles or lamplight, voices soft. You taste and smile and feel held.

I ask you as a friend who has sat at that table a thousand times. What made the eyes shine? The sugar on the spoon, or the faces around the light?

Isolate the moment. Same faces. Same laughter. Same hunger met. The sweet absent or present, noticed or not. Does love leave when the dose leaves? Does laughter thin? Does the room cool?

Or take the cinema in the dark. Shoulder to shoulder. Story swelling. Hand finding hand in the shared bag. Would the film move you less with water and warm breath and closeness alone? Would the joke fail without melt on the tongue?

Credit where credit is due. It was you that laughed, not the dose. It was company that warmed, not the fix. It was hunger met and ease and occasion that glowed. The sweet only ever sneaked a ride on that glow and claimed the fare.

Think of your own memory honestly. Keep the whole evening and play it twice. Once with the dose remembered bite by bite. Once with faces and talk remembered word by word. Which memory holds the warmth when the taste has faded by morning? Which would you grieve to lose — the packet, or the people?

Only one honest answer fits. Love was meal and company and hunger met. Never the wrapper.

Hear the limit with full warmth, so no fear hides. I am not saying sweet taste may never be enjoyed. Berries stain and please. An apple cracks and floods. A plain meal carries its own sweetness that the clear tongue greets gladly. Enjoyment stays. What leaves is the frame that made a packet the payment for virtue and the proof of love. Celebrating, rewarding myself with chocolate kept the loop alive by calling a dose a prize. Call the evening the prize and the loop has nowhere to live.

That is the inversion that frees eating. You lose no love by leaving the trap. You recover the love the trap claimed as its own.

FRUIT IS NOT THE TRAP

"Fruit is sugar too so nothing matters," you say. "Bread turns to sugar. Everything is sugar. Why draw a line at all?"

I hear you, because the line sounds like trickery until you live it. Let me draw it in food, not in words.

BAD SUGAR is the refined and added flood and the junk processed hits built to hit fast — drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and their grazing kin eaten as sweet hits. That flood arrives without hunger, passes without satisfaction, and orders another within the hour.

An apple is not that. A bowl of vegetables is not that. Bread torn at a hungry table is not that. These are met by hunger, slowed by chew, closed by satisfaction, and forgotten for hours. When did an apple ever send you hunting the packet an hour later? When did bread eaten hungry ever order you back before the plate was cold? When did berries eaten to satisfied ever leave you crowded and twitchy and hunting more?

Only one honest answer fits. One feeds. The other repeats.

The body knows the difference even when the dictionary argues. Hunger welcomes one and quiets after. Hum demands the other and renews after. Taste of one deepens as you notice. Taste of the other dulls as you hurry. After one you forget food. After the other you remember nothing but food.

Do not moralize the line. There is no good eater and bad eater here. No virtue in the market bag. No shame in the past box. There is only what satisfies and what repeats, what closes gently and what never closes. Choose the close because the close is joy, not because a rule says so.

I stood where you stand, bag in hand, fearing I would miss the flood. I missed nothing. Mornings grew bright without buzz. Afternoons carried without topping. Evenings ended with ease rather than regret. Food grew larger, not smaller — colour and crunch and steam and juice where once there was only melt and wrapper. Eating returned as favourite, not as corridor.

It was you that feasted, never the dose.

That is the verdict the table gives. Keep it.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

Carry that into the next hungry hour. Let hunger come till plain. Eat with attention till satisfied closes. Then get on with enjoying your life and let the hours prove it — bright without buzz, quiet without topping, marvellous in its ordinariness. You are not losing sweetness. You are recovering taste.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy never lived in the packet, it lived in hunger met and company kept.
- Hunger arrives slow and general and welcomes plain meals, hum arrives fast and names one hit only.
- A clear meal is noticed, savoured, and closes gently at satisfied for hours of quiet.
- Doses never close gently, they hurry and renew and leave the hand hunting.
- Fruit and bread and market meals belong to eating and satisfy, they never order the loop.
- Love at the table was faces and laughter and ease, the sweet only rode along.
