THE FIRST MORNING

Wake up.

That is all. Wake up as a free eater.

No ceremony. No counting. No heavy chest. The air in the kitchen is the same air. The kettle clicks the same click. Light comes through the same window. You slept, you woke, hunger will come when hunger comes, and you will meet it gladly.

We who lived years inside the Sugar Trap woke to arithmetic. What did I eat last night. How bad was the box. What rule starts today. Will today be good. Will today be bad. The mind toured the cupboards before the feet moved. Shame stood by the kettle. Plans stood by the shame.

You are out. Let the morning prove it gently.

Drink water if you are thirsty. Make tea or coffee as you love it. Sit a moment before food if you have no hunger yet. Hunger is not an emergency. Hunger is a quiet messenger that knocks when real food is wanted. You do not chase it. You do not fear it. You welcome it.

Vegetables with oil and salt. Eat till the edge leaves hunger, till satisfaction settles warm and deep, then stop, the way a child who stops mid-bite and runs off to play stops, without counting, without guilt, body leading.

Notice how clean that is. No buzz chased. No fog paid. No second reach an hour later. Real hunger met by real food leaves no debt. The mouth is calm. The belly is calm. The mind is clear for work and laughter.

Ask yourself in that clear hour.

Did the morning need melt to start?

Did tea need sweetening to comfort?

Did absence leave a hole, or did presence return?

You know the answer because you feel it without theory. The morning never lived in the packet. The morning lived in sleep ending, light returning, hunger knocking, food satisfying. BAD SUGAR only ever borrowed brightness for fog and called the borrowing breakfast.

You walked out of the Sugar Trap with eyes open. Mornings now belong to you.

BREAKFAST WITHOUT BARGAINING

A good week meant stern rules and tiny portions eaten standing up. Free breakfast has no bargaining.

You eat hungry. You enjoy each mouthful with attention. You notice texture. Crunch. Warmth. Salt. Sour of fruit. Chew of bread. You stop when satisfaction says enough — not stuffed, not strict, satisfied. Then you rise and walk into the day with a steady belly and a clear head.

Do you miss the flood? Ask honestly.

Did the flood ever give deep satisfaction, or only loudness for ten seconds then coating on teeth?

Did the flood ever carry you clean to lunch, or did it always call again by mid-morning?

Did plain food ever taste grey once hunger met it fully, or did plain food taste marvellous once the tongue stopped being shouted at?

You know. Loud is not deep. Coating is not comfort. The free tongue tastes more, not less. Burnt sugar blunted taste. Clean taste returns within days, and with it real favourites return — fruit bright, bread deep, cheese full, meals remembered.

If you take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

That is the only caution I lay, lightly and once. For the belief itself needs no caution. Belief is clear: BAD SUGAR gave nothing. Hunger and satisfaction give everything.

AN EVENING ON THE SOFA

Evening was our hardest hour. Now evening becomes our kindest proof.
