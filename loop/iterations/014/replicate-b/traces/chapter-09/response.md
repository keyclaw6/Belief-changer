Chapter 9
THE BODY KEEPS THE SCORE

IN THIS CHAPTER — The dentist's mirror, the cuff at three o'clock, the bathroom scale, the quiet ledger your body has kept while you were told you felt fine

*You are not being told BAD SUGAR might be bad — you are seeing that it does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you, and that seeing it is relief, not fear.*

### THE MIRROR THAT DOES NOT FLATTER

Come with me to three small rooms you already know. No lecture. Just looking.

First, the dentist's chair. The light clicks on, the mirror comes out, cold and honest. You did not go in feeling ill. You went in for a check. The dentist tilts the mirror and says softly, "We have a little decay here." Not because you are dirty or careless. Because every time free sugars sit on your teeth, the plaque makes acid, and acid makes a hole. That is what free sugars do TO teeth. They do not clean them. They do not strengthen them. They feed the acid that takes enamel away. You felt fine while it happened.

Second, the afternoon at your desk. The cuff tightens at 3:17. Your shoulders are up, your head is a little thick, your hand has been back in the drawer twice. We call this normal. We say "I am `tired, wired and crashing`." That phrase is not poetry. It is the state of a person fed on hits. The lift you thought was energy was the spike; what you feel afterwards is the dip your body makes to correct it. Over months and years, that high intake is linked — at the level of whole populations — with more load where you do not want load: the liver made to handle more than it was built for, blood pressure nudged up, a low-grade inflammation that simmers, and an appetite control that keeps getting bypassed so you want more when you have already had enough. Not one biscuit causing a heart attack. Not a sentence on you. A pattern: high, frequent BAD SUGAR, more strain.

Third, the bathroom scale and the mood that follows it. You step on, step off, tell yourself "I will be good tomorrow." In men followed for years, the pattern was strangely steady: more sweets predicted more common mental strain later — low mood, anxiety, that flat "I cannot get going" feeling — and the check was done the other way too, to see if low mood came first and led to sweets. It did not. Again, this is a link seen across many men, not a diagnosis of you, not proof that sugar causes depression in everyone, and sweets were the source counted. I tell you because you deserve the truth plain, not because you need a new fear to carry.

I am not your clinician, and this book is not medical advice — if you have diabetes, an eating-disorder history, take prescription medication, or are pregnant, speak to your clinician before changing what you eat. I say that once, warmly, and then I tell you what your body has been trying to tell you without words.

We were told we would feel harm if it were real. We did not. The water heated slowly. Remember that frog — the heat rising by one degree so it never jumps? Your body kept the score while you were told the score was love, energy, treat. The cuffs and mirrors and scales are not moral. They are mechanical. They add without flattery.

Ask yourself, honestly, as one honest person to another:

When your teeth ached, did BAD SUGAR ever repair a tooth, or did it only ever feed the acid that makes the hole?

When you felt that wired crash at three, did the next sweet make you steady and clear for hours — or did it lift you for minutes and leave you lower, reaching again?

If BAD SUGAR were truly doing something FOR your heart, your mood, your mouth, would the people who use most of it be the steadiest, calmest, clearest among us — or would they be the ones saying "I need something sweet to get through"?

You know the answers. You have lived them. BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

### THE QUIET LOAD YOU WERE TOLD WAS NORMAL

The fact is… BAD SUGAR sells itself as relief and delivers load.

For a while we told ourselves a comforting story: "A little of what you fancy." The shop told us the same, louder. But look at what "a little" has come to mean in the world we walk in. Whole populations are guided to keep free sugars below about ten percent of energy — roughly fifty grams — and to know that below five percent would be better still, because most of us live far above both. That is not a prescription for you. It is a measure of the canyon. The shelves are built to push you past steady, every day, and then to sell you the crash as your need.

I want you to feel the weight of that without fear, because fear is not the reason we stop. We stop because we see. And what we see is a hill of small TOs that add up while FOR stays empty.

TO your teeth: plaque acid, decay, the quiet drilling that never had to happen. TO your vessels and metabolism: more work for the liver, a nudge to blood pressure, a simmer of inflammation, a hunger signal that keeps getting tricked — high intake linked with higher risk of heart and stroke trouble across populations, not the doom of a single bite, but the load of the repeated hit. TO your mood — in those men followed over years — more sweets, more later mental strain, not less comfort, not relief, and not explained by starting low.

And FOR you? What does BAD SUGAR do FOR you?

Does it give you steady energy? No. Real food does that — quietly, without the spike-then-dip. Does it give you calm? No. It makes `that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling` and then rents you twenty minutes without it. Does it give you love? No. The people did. The moment did. The sugar only sneaked a ride and left you `tired, wired and crashing` after the guests had gone.

Hold that plain for a second. It is not a verdict on your character. It is a verdict on the con. We — you and I — believed a hit was a help because the hit removed, briefly, the discomfort the previous hit had left. The rescue was the perpetrator in a different outfit, diving in to save you after pushing you in.

We were told a safe limit would save us. That is the cliff in the mind. "You can jump off the cliff as long as you don't fall more than a few metres." Does that sound like safety, or does it sound like a rule written by someone who profits when you stay near the edge? A trap does not become a treat if you set a limit on how far you fall into it. Inside BAD SUGAR there is no healthy level other than zero — not because your body cannot cope with an accidental blip, but because one deliberate "just one" feeds the belief that keeps the trap alive.

You are not weak for believing the limit. We all believed it. The Big Sweet Lie printed it on the packet and called it balance.

### IF THIS WERE PLEASURE, WHAT WOULD FREEDOM FEEL LIKE?

I know the whisper that comes now, because it came for me.

"But this is frightening. Are you saying I am doomed? That my teeth, my heart, my mood are already decided?"

No. I am saying the opposite, and I want you to feel the relief with me.

The fact is… your body is not your enemy. It is the faithful instrument that kept score while you were misled, and it is also the instrument that returns to steady when you stop misleading it. Plaque stops being fed. The spike-then-dip stops being triggered. The load you added daily stops being added. The low after the hit stops being mistaken for your nature. That is not a promise that no harm ever happened or that every body bounces back the same way — I will not make a personal prediction about your mouth or your heart. It is the simple, wonderful fact that stopping adds no new harm and removes the cause of the ongoing harm, and that freedom is felt quickly as relief, not only later as repair.

We do not quit from fear. Fear makes a tight, white-knuckle vow that snaps. We quit from the indignation and relief of seeing: I was paying for the very thing that left me `tired, wired and crashing`, and I never needed to.

Picture the two roads. On one, you stay in the trap `for the rest of your life` — not in dramatic collapse, but in the familiar, daily tax: the drawer, the dip, the dentist's "little decay," the cuff a little tighter, the evening promise to be good tomorrow, forever. On the other, you are free `for the rest of your life` — same mornings, same shops, same meals, but without the tax, without the dip-making hit, eating real food and feeling steady and clear. Both roads take `for the rest of your life`. Only one is freedom. That daft cliff-rule — a little poison in pretty paper if you keep to metres — falls away the moment you see the road is yours to choose, and you would never choose the taxed one if you could see it plain.

And you can. Right now.

### WHAT YOUR BODY WANTED TO TELL YOU ALL ALONG

Your body never asked for BAD SUGAR. It asked for food.

When you eat real food, hunger arrives honestly, satisfaction arrives cleanly, energy stays steady between meals. That is not discipline. That is nature, doing what it has always done when we do not jam the signal with weaponized sweetness. Your body does not need you to rehearse rules or count days. It needs you to stop feeding the voice that engineered the craving and called it hunger.

We fell into this together, by con, not by character. We normalized the load and called it "a treat when you feel you deserve one." We called the dip "I need sugar to function." We called the glow on the packet appetite. All that time, your body kept the honest score — not to punish you, but to warn you — and you were taught to pour sweetness on the warning light.

See it now and the light changes meaning. The crash after sweets is not proof you need sweets. It is proof you have just had them. The craving at the packet is not proof you love it. It is proof the cue rang and the belief answered. The heaviness after the box is not proof you are weak. It is proof the trap is load-bearing and you have been carrying it.

You do not need to carry it.

There is a small, marvellous relief waiting on the other side of this chapter — not the relief of a fix, but the relief of putting down a weight you did not know you were holding until you noticed your shoulders. You do not have to wait months to feel it. You feel it the moment the belief dies: "Ah. It was doing plenty TO me and nothing FOR me. I am not giving up anything. I am getting away."

Stay with that for a breath. Let the fear sift off. We raised the stakes at full strength because you are an adult and you deserve the truth without hedging — teeth, vessels, mood, the quiet overshoot above any guidance — and we leave fear where it belongs: outside the decision. You are not quitting because you are scared. You are quitting because you are free to see, and seeing makes the desire collapse.

If a flicker of that old thought comes — "maybe just a little, maybe Friday, maybe the special one" — hear it for what it is: the Little creature whining for a feed and the Big Lie translating the whine as love. Hear it, smile, and let it starve. Your body is already on your side. It kept the score honestly. It will keep the steady, too, the moment you stop feeding what hurts it.

You are not on trial. The trap is.

And the verdict is simple. There is nothing here to miss.

### SUMMARY

* BAD SUGAR does plenty TO the body — it feeds plaque acid that makes decay, it adds metabolic load linked across populations with higher heart and stroke risk, and in men followed over time more sweets predicted more later mental strain — and it does nothing FOR steady energy, calm, or comfort.
* Everyday slumps after sweets are not low blood sugar as doctors define it; the spike-then-dip is the trap's after-effect, not proof you need sugar.
* The sense of being fine while harm added up is explained by gradualism and by a normal environment built to push intake far beyond whole-population guidance of below ten percent of energy and ideally below five percent.
* The "safe limit" is the trap's rule: a deliberate exception keeps the belief alive; freedom comes from ending deliberate feeding, not from budgeting the hit.
* Seeing the bodily stakes does not mean quitting from fear; it means choosing relief and escape because there is nothing real to sacrifice and everything steady to gain.
