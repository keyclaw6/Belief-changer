Chapter 1
THE PROMISE — HOW TO READ THIS BOOK

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The kitchen counter after the quiet binge — the crumpled packet at the bottom of the bin — the locked safe that only opens with every number in order — the boxed line that tells you exactly what BAD SUGAR is and is not

*You don't need more willpower — you need to see what BAD SUGAR is really doing, and once you see it you will want to walk away.*

### You're Not Here To Be Scolded

If you are holding this book at the kitchen counter, if you have just told yourself "just one treat" and then found the packet empty, if you have hidden a wrapper, bargained with tomorrow, promised Monday and meant it — you are the reader I wrote this for.

And I know what you are braced for.

"Here comes another lecture. Here comes the chart of what I've done wrong, the meal plan I won't stick to, the person who will tell me I lack discipline and then tell me to try harder."

I don't blame you for thinking that. We have all been taught the same story: that people who eat too much BAD SUGAR do it because they are greedy or weak, and that the answer is to control themselves more tightly. When that story fails, we don't blame the story. We blame ourselves.

So let me say this plainly and warmly, because it is true: you are not weak. You are not greedy. You have been caught in a trap that was designed to catch you, and you have been trying to get out with a method that cannot get anyone out. Of course you are tired of it.

Do you want to stop feeling controlled by sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, pastries, puddings, ice cream, sugary drinks, sweetened yoghurts and cereals, white bread, white rice, chips and all the other ultra-processed carbs that get eaten as snacks and lifts and treats? Of course you do — that is why you are reading this book. Do you want to be told you are broken? No. Do you want to be free without feeling deprived and without spending the rest of your life fighting yourself? Yes. Then we are on the same side from the first line, and we stay there.

This book has no scolding in it. It has no diet, no permitted daily amount, no points, no forbidden-food anxiety, no weighing and measuring, no pep talk about digging deep. It is not there to tell you to be stronger. It is there to show you something you have not been shown before. When you see it, the desire changes. The behaviour then changes on its own.

I will make you a promise at the start, because you deserve to know the terms before we go any further. If you read this book as directed, you can stop eating BAD SUGAR easily, immediately and permanently. Not by gritting your teeth. Not at some distant milestone when you have finally earned it. Easily, immediately and permanently — as a relief, not as a sentence.

I know that sounds outrageous. It sounded outrageous to me before I got free. I thought freedom would be hard, miserable, a life of missing out. It wasn't. What was hard was staying in the trap. What was marvellous was leaving it. And that is the first thing I want you to let in: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain. If I am right, you walk away free. If I am wrong, you have lost an afternoon of reading. You can test it without risk while you read, exactly as you are now.

### How I Got Free

I need to tell you who is speaking to you, because you should not take your freedom on trust.

I was the person who negotiated all day. "I've been good this week." "Just a treat." "I don't keep it in the house" — until I did, and then I told myself it was for the kids. I knew the 3pm drawer by heart. I knew the sofa at 9pm and the hand that went back to the packet before I had decided to have another one. I knew the little lift that came with the first bite and the flat, edgy sag that followed it, and how the sag then sold the next bite as the cure.

I tried what you tried. I cut down. I kept only the special ones. I made rules about weekends, about after dinner, about only when I deserved it. I white-knuckled for days and felt virtuous and exhausted at the same time, and then some bright packet would call from the aisle and I would hear myself think, "Oh, just one can't hurt," and the whole exhausting bargain would collapse. And I thought that collapse proved something about me.

It didn't. It proved something about the method and the trap.

I did not get free by becoming a more disciplined person. I got free by seeing the con clearly. I saw that what I had taken for a genuine pleasure, a treat and a lift was not giving me anything at all. It was taking. It was creating the very emptiness, the tension, the flatness it then briefly relieved, while the real pleasures — the company, the film, the rest, the meal itself — were doing all the work and BAD SUGAR was sneaking a ride. The moment I saw that, I did not need to resist. There was nothing to resist. I wanted to escape. And I did — without strain, without drama, without counting days.

That is why I can speak to you with certainty. I was where you are. I am now where you want to be — happy to be free. I did not do it through willpower and I will not ask you to. I will ask you to see what I saw. The book will do the rest.

### What BAD SUGAR Means In This Book

Before we go further, we need a clean line. The world has made sugar confusing on purpose. There are a hundred names for it, and a hundred foods engineered around it that don't look like sugar at all but behave exactly like it in the trap.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. I will use that name every time so there is never a doubt.

> **BAD SUGAR = any added or refined sugar + any product engineered around it + any starchy junk carb that behaves like sugar in the trap:** sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, pastries, puddings, ice cream, sugary drinks, sweetened yoghurts/cereals, white bread, white rice, chips, and other ultra-processed carbs eaten as snacks, lifts, or treats. When this book says *sugar*, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. GOOD SUGAR — the natural sweetness already inside whole fruit, vegetables, and plain dairy as part of real food — is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit.

That line is not a diet. It is a definition so the trap logic can work cleanly. Inside the line, the logic is total: one BAD SUGAR keeps the lie alive. Outside the line, you are free. GOOD SUGAR inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain dairy as part of real food is not quit, not counted, not negotiated. Real food stays yours. You are not here to stop enjoying eating. You are here to stop feeding a con that steals enjoyment from eating.

Two small doctrines sit beside that line, and you should hear them now so they are settled when we need them later.

The first is the seatbelt. Your body can cope with an occasional blip — a stray ingredient, a mistaken bite, the sugar someone put in a sauce without telling you. Your mind will not cope with a deliberate exception. An accidental blip revives nothing if the belief stays dead. A deliberate "just one" feeds the little creature and revives the big lie. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the behaviour.

The second is the bonus. You are not reading this book to quit plain GOOD SUGAR foods, or dairy, or anything else. You are reading it to quit BAD SUGAR. If you find freedom so good you want the upgrade and want to look at what else you eat or drink, the door is open and I will point you to help. Never as a condition of success. Freedom from BAD SUGAR is complete on its own.

### The Safe That Only Opens One Way

I need you to understand how to read this book, because how you read it is part of the method.

We have all seen a safe with a combination lock. Ten numbers sit on the dial. You can know nine of them perfectly, you can turn the dial beautifully, and the safe stays locked. Miss one number and it stays locked. Hit all ten in order and it clicks open without force.

This book is that safe.

For years you have been given one number at a time — cut down a bit, use more willpower, swap this for that — and then blamed when the door didn't open. It wasn't you. A partial method cannot open this lock. The belief that keeps you in the Sugar Trap has ten numbers to it, and they have to be hit in order. This book lays them in order. Your job is simply to follow the sequence and let each one click before the next turns.

That is why the book asks two things of you at the start. They are not grand demands. They are small agreements that make the whole thing work.

If you have ever tried to fix a problem by tightening control while still believing the thing you were controlling gave you something precious, did you feel free or did you feel torn in two? When you told yourself you were giving up a treat, did the treat become less precious or more precious? When you promised you would only have a little, did you have a little or did you hear yourself think, "I just crave sweets and when I eat them, I feel like I can't stop," and watch the little become the box?

Those are not flaws in you. They are the lock telling you the numbers are out of order. When the book puts them back in order, the craving stops being a fight and becomes information. You will see it. You will feel the click.

### Your Two Promises

So here at the doorway, before any argument, I ask you for two promises. You give them once, and they carry you the whole way.

**KEEP AN OPEN MIND**
Question everything you think you know about BAD SUGAR.

You have lived with brainwashing your whole life — family, friends, advertising, the bright packets at child-eye height that told you BAD SUGAR is love, comfort, energy, celebration, the normal way to be kind to yourself. Of course you believe it. We all did. Keep an open mind does not mean believe me. It means be willing to investigate what you have been told, as a curious, intelligent person would, and to let your own experience answer honestly. If something in this book sounds strange, don't argue with it and don't accept it. Keep reading. Let the next pages test it.

**DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL THE VOW**
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.

This may be the most important sentence in the whole book. Do not change anything yet. Eat your BAD SUGAR exactly as you do now while you read. Do not reduce, do not race ahead, do not try to prove anything to yourself by white-knuckling through a single afternoon. The reason is simple: if you try to stop before you have seen the trap, you will feel deprived, you will make BAD SUGAR more precious, and you will think the discomfort proves you need it. That is the trap defending itself. We will remove the belief first. Then stopping will feel like what it is — an escape, not a loss. When we make the vow together, you will be ready, and you will be glad to make it. Not before.

Those two promises are the whole entry contract. Keep them, and you have done everything this chapter asks.

What you can expect next is straightforward. We will look at the trap you didn't see, we will split what it does TO you from any fantasy of what it does FOR you, we will walk through eating as your body already knows how to do it, we will name the little physical whine and the big lie that feeds it, we will meet the industry that engineered the cage and the willpower method that kept you in it, we will meet the strongest, most beautiful lie — the celebration that feels as if it would be nothing without BAD SUGAR — and show you it was everything despite BAD SUGAR, not because of it. Then we will make the vow, together, on a last ordinary sweet taken with your eyes open, and you will walk into ordinary days — mornings, shops, sofas, kitchens — and find they are wonderful without it.

You are not being asked to become a different person. You are being asked to return to the person you were before the con. That person already knows how to enjoy food, to feel steady energy, to celebrate, to be kind to themselves. We are not adding strain. We are taking a weight off.

Read in order. Keep the two promises we just made. Let the book do its work. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading on with an open mind and letting the belief change in its own time. You can become free easily, immediately and permanently, and get on with enjoying your life.
