Chapter 9
THE BODY KEEPS THE SCORE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The dentist's mirror and the small dark spot — the cuff at the pharmacy — the scale in the bathroom after the holidays — the frog in water that never felt the heat rise

*BAD SUGAR never did anything FOR you — it did plenty TO you, and seeing that clearly feels like relief, not fear.*

### You Feel Fine

You feel fine.

That is how the Sugar Trap holds you the longest. Not with agony. With fine.

We felt fine too while it was busily doing its work. A little tired at three. A little wired after the second biscuit. A little crashy and snappy by six. A little heavier around the middle year by year. But fine. Normal. Everyone lives like this.

You remember the water brought slowly to the boil. The heat rises by degrees and the frog calls the new heat normal. The baseline drifts down so quietly you never hear it move. Your taste dulls by degrees. Your steady energy drifts down by degrees. Your calm gets taxed by degrees. You do not wake up one morning and think "I am being harmed." You just get used to being tired, wired and crashing and you blame age, work, character, willpower.

The fact is… fine was never fine. Fine was taxed.

I want you to feel the ground you are standing on, because it is not your private appetite. Health bodies set a ceiling for free sugars — less than ten percent of energy, roughly fifty grams, with less than five percent for additional benefit — because the current norm sails past both. Supermarket portions, packets made to share that no one shares, "treat culture" on every endcap: the normal you were sold sits far beyond that ceiling. That is not your greed. That is an engineered normal that keeps you thinking over-consumption is moderation.

Ask yourself as a friend:

If that level of sweetness were truly doing something FOR you, why do you feel less steady the more faithfully you live inside it?

If it were harmless in your hands, why does "I feel fine" need so much explaining, so many naps, so many coffees, so many bargains with tomorrow?

There is only one honest answer. You felt fine because the harm was gradual. The body keeps the score whether you feel the heat or not. That is not a sentence. That is a relief to see before we go any further — because what is gradual can be left behind whole.

### What The Body Actually Pays

Let me show you, plainly and kindly, what that slow tax looks like in the body you live in. Not as a scare to chase you. As a settled accounting, so you stop believing BAD SUGAR leaves you untouched. Then we will put fear down where it belongs — out of your reason to escape.

First, the mirror at the dentist.

Free sugars feed plaque. Plaque makes acid. Acid eats enamel. That is not a metaphor. That is the mechanism that makes a small dark spot become a cavity, a filling, a root, a gap you feel with your tongue. Fluoride and brushing mediate it, but they do not erase the feed. Every sweet hit, every sugary drink, every white-bread snack that behaves like sugar in the trap, lays that feed again. BAD SUGAR does not rot one tooth with one bite — it feeds the acid-making day after day.

Do you remember being told sweets were a harmless little pleasure? Does that look like harmless?

Second, the quieter load.

At population level, high intake of added sugar is linked with higher risk to heart and vessels — not because one hit clogs an artery, but because a steady high load burdens the liver, nudges blood pressure, keeps low-grade inflammation smouldering, and slips past the appetite controls that real food obeys. The lift you thought was energy is deferred load. You borrow a bright minute and the body pays the interest later, in steadiness lost.

We are not saying one chocolate bar gives one person heart disease, and we are not saying stopping guarantees you will never get sick. Populations show the link. Bodies show the mechanism. That is enough to know the deal: the lift is not free. It was never FOR you. It was doing plenty TO you while calling itself FOR you.

Third, the mood you thought it mended.

In long cohorts of men, those who ate more sugar from sweets had higher odds later of common mental strain — low mood, worry, the flatness we used to treat with "just a treat." The daily "I deserve this" predicted feeling worse later, not better. Reverse causation was checked and not found — it was not that low mood simply drove sweet eating after the fact.

Listen to what that means and what it does not mean. It does not mean sugar causes depression in everyone. It does not mean that putting BAD SUGAR down cures low mood. It does mean, in those men followed for years, the comfort story ran backwards. The thing sold as relief was linked with more trouble later. If sweet comfort truly comforted, why would the comforted feel worse?

And even if your mood has not felt that link, your mouth and your heart have kept the score. So has your energy. That afternoon slump you blamed on your character was a taxed body flashing a warning you were taught to read as a want.

I know the Lie will speak now in your voice. Let me lend it words so you hear it clearly.

It says: "But I feel okay. Maybe I'm one of the lucky ones. Maybe it's not that bad for me."

It says: "That all sounds like populations and maybes — not me."

So ask the trap questions whose only honest answer concedes the point:

If BAD SUGAR were truly a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, why does it need to feed acid that eats your teeth while you smile?

If it were giving you energy, why does high intake link with the very heaviness and inflammation that steal energy?

If it were kindness, why would the daily kindness predict later flatness instead of lasting comfort?

There is only one honest answer. It was not kindness. It was a con that did plenty TO you and sold it as FOR you. And seeing that is not a sentence. It is the moment the con loses its power.

### The Cliff Is Not Safer When You Loiter Near The Edge

I do not want you to quit because you are frightened.

Fear makes you bargain. Fear makes you negotiate. Fear says "maybe just a little, maybe less often, maybe I'll be careful." Fear keeps you on the cliff, measuring how far you might fall.

You know the absurdity they sold us: you can jump off the cliff as long as you don't fall more than a few metres. A safe limit on a trap. A healthy level of the very thing that keeps the Trap alive. If there were a safe level inside the BAD SUGAR line, the Lie would not need to keep inventing it for you — "just on weekends," "just dark chocolate," "just after I have been good." The line exists precisely because moderation keeps the belief alive.

The body can cope with an occasional blip — a stray ingredient, a mistaken bite — but your mind will not cope with a deliberate exception. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the bite.

So do not bargain with the cliff. Step away from it. That is what freedom is.

You came in stuck with a vague idea — "sugar is probably bad for me, but it gives me something." That halfway idea leaves you defending it forever. You are leaving this chapter with a settled, lived accounting: BAD SUGAR is doing plenty TO me and nothing FOR me. The spot on the tooth, the pressure in the cuff, the weight on the scale, the wired crash at four o'clock — those were never proof you were weak. They were proof you were taxed. And a taxed person does not need fear to walk out. A taxed person needs to see the exit.

Feel the difference in time.

Imagine staying inside this — tired, wired and crashing for the rest of your life — paying the slow interest, bargaining for the next hit, calling the taxed state normal while the heat rises one more degree.

Now feel the other valence of those same words: free for the rest of your life — steady, clear, your own taste back, your own calm back, walking past the same aisles and feeling relief instead of tug.

Both phrases use the same horizon. Only one belongs to you.

This is not doom. This is dignity. You were taxed, not defective. You were sold a normal that sits far beyond any ceiling your body would choose. You were taught to read warning lights as wants. Once you read them correctly — once you see TO not FOR — there is nothing to mourn and nothing to fear. There is a trap to leave.

I do not want you to leave this chapter frightened. I want you to leave it relieved. Relieved that the price was never invisible, relieved that the benefit was never real, relieved that stepping out is not sacrifice but bookkeeping set right. The body kept the score while you were told not to look. Now you have looked, and the score says the same thing every freedom says:

There was never anything here FOR you. There was only a bill TO you. Put it down.

Rejoice that you can.
