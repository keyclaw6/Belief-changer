Chapter 6
EATING LIKE WE WERE BUILT TO

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The kitchen at noon — the empty belly, the apple on the board, the roast vegetables steaming, the quiet plate, the clean finish

*Your body already knows how to want food, enjoy food, and finish food — BAD SUGAR only taught you to forget.*

### The Worry You Bring With You

You have watched the lift for what it was. Not fuel. Not above steady. Just the brief relief from the dip it made, sold back to you as help.

And now a new worry arrives, quite naturally, and it sounds reasonable.

If that lift was not real energy, what will eating be without it? Will food be bland? Careful? Measured forever? A life of chewing joylessly while other people get the fun part?

I hear you. We all heard that whisper. We imagined freedom as a long, disciplined queue of plain meals we would have to endure because the exciting ones were now forbidden.

That is not what freedom is. Freedom is not eating by numbers. Freedom is eating like you were built to.

Look at how we have been eating in the trap. Not from hunger to satisfaction, but from craving to grazing. Hand in packet before you decided to reach. Sweet on sweet to quiet the edgy hum, then slump, then hunt again. We called that eating. It was feeding. We called sugar the fun part. It was the part that spoiled the fun of the rest.

You are not about to lose enjoyment. You are about to get it back.

### Hunger Is Not An Emergency

Before we eat, let us feel hunger itself again — not the needy whine that BAD SUGAR taught you to call hunger, but the real one.

Real hunger arrives quietly. It does not shout. It does not stab. It builds like light coming into a room. First, an emptiness that is not unpleasant — a clean, open space behind the ribs. Then a gentle attention: food sounds like a good idea. Then a simple, bodily wish to eat. No panic. No "I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling." No frantic hunt for a fix.

We had forgotten that feel. In the trap, hunger was never allowed to arrive. We fed the whine long before the body asked. We mistook that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling for hunger, and we answered it with a hit. The hit silenced the whine for twenty minutes and kept us from ever feeling the deeper, calmer signal underneath.

So let hunger come. I do not mean you should wait and suffer. I mean notice it when it does come — perhaps before lunch, perhaps after a morning of honest work. Notice it is not edgy. It is not wired. It is not personal. It is your body saying, plainly: we are ready now.

There is a quiet confidence in that signal that BAD SUGAR can never give you. It is yours. It was there when you were small, before any bright packet told you what taste should be. It is still there, steady, waiting for you to stop jamming it with noise.

You will feel it easily, immediately and permanently once you stop feeding BAD SUGAR — not because you have learned a technique, but because you have stopped interfering. Your body makes the glucose it needs from protein and glycerol already in real food, so there is no deprivation in leaving BAD SUGAR alone. That is simply how you were built to be fueled. The trap taught you that sweetness must be added on top. Your body never needed it added. It was already equipped.

### The Ordinary Meal

Come and eat an ordinary meal with me. Not a project. Not a plan. Just food.

It is midday. The plate is warm. On it is real food — perhaps roast vegetables that have browned at the edges and turned sweet in the heat, a piece of fish or chicken, a scoop of rice or potatoes that are food not confection, a simple piece of fruit on the side, maybe plain yoghurt. Nothing engineered to shout. Nothing in pretty paper designed to hit and crash. Just food that was food before a factory got to it.

Pick it up. Smell it. The smell does not scream the way a packet does. It invites.

Take the first bite slowly, and chew. Really chew. Notice what happens when you let yourself taste.

The roast carrot is sweet — not weaponized sweetness, not a blast that wipes your palate, but a deep, round sweetness that belongs to the carrot. The apple snaps and juices, clean and bright, and the sweetness finishes without cloying. The bread, if you have it, is not a sweet at all — it is earthy, chewy, satisfying, and it asks to be eaten with the rest, not chased with sugar after.

This is not discipline. This is pleasure that does not steal from the next hour.

We were told real food was the dull part and BAD SUGAR was the excitement. Look again. Real food is the house. BAD SUGAR was only the gatecrasher who ate the host and left you thinking the party was his. When the gatecrasher is gone, the house does not become empty. It becomes yours again.

Eat at the pace your mouth wants, not the pace a craving dictates. Your mouth will tell you. The flavours get louder as you chew. The belly sends its small, clear signals — not a crash, not a wired buzz, but a gradual, warm sense of being satisfied. You will notice the point where the food is still delicious but you no longer need the next bite with urgency. That point has a feeling. It is not excitement. It is ease.

We forgot what ease felt like at the table. In the trap we ate until the packet was louder than the body. We called the packet's end the end of eating. Free, you end because you are content. The difference is marvellous.

I remember my own first ordinary meal after I saw this. I had expected to miss something. I had braced. Instead I was surprised by how much there was to taste. I ate slower without trying to eat slower. I left a little on the plate without meaning to be virtuous — I was simply done. And an hour later I was not looking for a lift. I was clear. Steady. Ready to get on with my afternoon without bargaining with a drawer.

That is not a special power. That is the normal baseline returning. You have not added a new pleasure. You have stopped poisoning your palate, and your palate has come back to you.

### Enough Is A Feeling

The trap sold you a lie about enough. It told you enough is a number you must control, a limit you must enforce, a line you must not cross or you will lose control.

Your body never worked that way.

Enough is not a calculation. It is a feeling that arrives on its own when the food is real and the con is out of the way. You feel it as a quiet closure — the hunger that arrived cleanly now settles, the taste that was bright now rests, the body says thank you and moves on. There is no edgy negotiation. No "I can't stop once I start." No need to keep it out of the house because you cannot trust yourself in the house.

You could not trust yourself in the trap because the trap was designed to make one bite become the box. The craving carried the binge; moderation made the dose more precious. Free, you do not need a rule to leave food alone. You leave it alone because you are satisfied. Real food finishes. It does not loop.

Do you see the release in that? You do not need to control eating when eating is not being driven by a con. You were never missing discipline. You were missing the chance to feel done.

We were taught to fear hunger and to chase fullness with fixes. Real hunger does not need to be feared. Real satisfaction does not need to be enforced. Both are already in you, tuned by a far older intelligence than any packet. Your job is not to manage them. Your job is to stop drowning them out.

Eat when you are hungry. Eat real food until you are satisfied. That is not a rule I am giving you. That is the system that returns when BAD SUGAR is gone. It is steady, it is delicious, and it is enough — not because you have made it enough by effort, but because your body knows enough when the noise stops.

### What Steady Feels Like

An hour after that ordinary meal, check in.

In the old afternoons, an hour after a sweet you were sagging, hunting, a little sticky, a little tired, wired and crashing in that familiar grey. An hour after real food you are simply — well. Clear-headed. Calm in the belly. Energy that does not glitter and drop, but holds. You can work. You can walk. You can laugh without a packet in your hand.

This is not a boast. This is how eating was before the con taught you to call pinching normal.

You will notice other small returns, and they are wonderful because they are ordinary. Taste comes back. Fruit tastes sweeter, not because you have sugar-starved yourself into appreciating it, but because your palate is no longer dulled. Sleep settles. The late-night hunt loses its pull. You do not feel virtuous for not grazing. You feel relief that you do not need to.

Ask yourself, gently, as you sit with that finished plate:

Did that meal need BAD SUGAR to be enjoyable — or was it enjoyable because it was real, and you were hungry, and you ate until satisfaction arrived?

When you felt satisfied, did you need a sweet to finish — or did the wish for a sweet only ever arrive after a hit, to patch the dip the hit left?

You know the answer in your mouth and your belly, not in a theory. The ordinary doing tells you.

There is nothing to learn about portions. There is something to remember about pleasure. Real food pleases without borrowing from the next hour. BAD SUGAR borrows and calls the debt a treat.

You are not giving anything away by leaving BAD SUGAR alone. You are restoring the favourite experience itself — to want, to taste, to be satisfied, to be steady. That favourite was never the hit. The favourite was always the meal.

Let that settle without doing anything with it yet. Keep eating exactly as you have been while you read. I do not want you to prove this by effort. I want you to feel it prove itself the next time you are properly hungry and you eat real food to satisfaction and notice the clean finish, the absence of crash, the quiet pride of a body that has been listened to.

That quiet finish is freedom speaking in the body's own voice. It says: we are looked after. We are free to enjoy. We can get on with our day.

**SUMMARY**
- Real hunger is a calm, clean signal, not an edgy emergency, and it returns when the con stops jamming it
- Real food is delicious on its own terms — its sweetness and satisfaction are deep and complete without BAD SUGAR
- Enough is a feeling of steady satisfaction that arrives naturally with real food, not a number you must enforce
- Your body is built to make the steady fuel it needs from real food, so leaving BAD SUGAR alone restores taste and steadiness rather than depriving you
- Eating becomes simple and enjoyable — hunger to satisfaction to clear energy — when the trap is no longer driving it
