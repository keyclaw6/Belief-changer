Chapter 6
EATING IS FAVOURITE AGAIN

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the morning market, warm bread in paper, the hungry table, ripe fruit in a bowl, the clean stop at satisfied

*Real hunger makes plain food marvellous, and a satisfied body asks for nothing more.*

The Grey Fear

You arrive here with afternoons unmasked.

We watched the desk at three together. We saw the brief brightness and the longer fog. We saw borrowing called fuel. That false reading has lost its power. The strength was always yours. The work was always yours. The brightness that broke through for ten minutes was your body breaking through, not the packet helping.

Now a quieter fear rises to meet you, and I want to meet it with kindness, because it sounds like love.

"But food will be grey without something sweet."

"Dessert is love. Pudding is the favourite part. Without that, what is eating?"

"I am a comfort eater. Take the sweet away and you take the joy."

I know that voice. We all carried it. I carried it through family tables and tired evenings when I told myself the day only became kind when the sweet arrived. You carry it through kitchens where you have soothed and celebrated with chocolate and cake, through lunches where the savoury felt like waiting and the sweet felt like arrival.

The thought makes stopping look like loss. If joy lived in the packet, a life without the packet would be smaller, duller, well-behaved and sad. No wonder the heart tightens. Who would choose grey?

The fact is, joy never lived in the packet. Joy lived in hunger, in taste awake, in a body allowed to finish. BAD SUGAR only dulled that joy while taking credit for it. Eating, left to hunger and satisfaction, is favourite on its own.

I ask only that you test this as an investigator, not as a believer. Not with theory. With a hungry table, noticed bite by bite.

The Hungry Table

Picture a morning you know from another part of life, before the daily stitching took over.

Market air cool. Stalls piled high. Green leaves wet, tomatoes warm from the sun, herbs sharp when you bruise them between fingers. Bread still warm in paper, crust cracking as you tear it. A bowl of fruit on the table at home, skins bright, juice ready to run.

Now picture yourself arriving at that table truly hungry.

Not gnawing for a fix. Hungry in the clean sense. Belly empty, mouth fresh, attention clear. The kind of hunger that makes water taste good. The kind children feel at noon when play pauses and the body calls.

You sit. You eat.

First bite of warm bread with butter melting, soup steaming, vegetables sweet from the earth, cheese, eggs, rice, fish, whatever plain favourite your house loves. Taste arrives loud because hunger turned the volume up. You chew slower without deciding to. You notice salt, warmth, crunch, softness. Company laughs. You answer with your mouth full. Second helping because hunger still asks, then a pause.

That pause matters. Watch it.

Hunger softens. Interest narrows. The next bite still tastes good, but the urgency has gone. You leave half a slice. You push the bowl back an inch. Belly says thank you. Mind says done. No arithmetic. No bargaining. Pleasure complete.

Do you recognise that completeness? Like that child with the peach who stops mid-bite and runs off.

That is eating as the body built it. Hungry, eat, thank you, done.

We all lived this before the trap taught us a second way to eat. Not hungry, but empty for a hit. Not eating, but topping. Not satisfied, but dulled and circling. Mouth full, belly full, mind still loud. Sweet, bright, sour, more. That loop never said thank you and done. It said again.

Ask your own tables for evidence, kindly, without attack.

When you sat down starving after swimming, walking, working outdoors, did plain food taste marvellous without any sweet finish?

When you ate a real lunch to satisfied, with no pudding and no sweet drink, did you work and talk with ease, or did you miss anything except the thought of missing?

When you were absorbed at a loved table, laughing, bread passing hand to hand, did joy come from hunger met and people near, or from a packet emptied after?

There is only one honest answer. The joy was hunger met. The love was meal and company. The packet only sneaked a ride on a joy already moving.

This is why free eaters do not live in grey. Not because they settled for less. Because they kept the more. Hunger makes flavour. Satisfaction makes peace. BAD SUGAR muted both and sold the muting as extra pleasure.

What Satisfaction Feels Like

Satisfaction has been slandered.

We were taught to hear it as absence. No dessert. No glow. Restraint. Being good. A thin virtue with a long face.

The reality is the other way around.

Satisfaction is a positive sensation, as warm and physical as hunger itself. Learn its signs and you will trust it as you trust thirst quenched.

Hunger begins as information. Empty ease, then clear call, then strong request. It sharpens smell. It brightens taste. It makes simple food vivid. It never shouts in panic when the stack is gone. It speaks.

Eating answers. Warmth spreads. Attention settles on flavour. Chewing slows on its own. The body leans back a fraction. Breath deepens. The next mouthful pleases, but does not command.

Then the stop. Clean, kind, final. Not stuffed. Not deprived. Satisfied. Belly full enough. Mind quiet. Taste thanked. Hands still without order. You want to talk, to walk, to work, to play. Food thoughts leave because their job is done.

That is the full arc. It needs no sweet seal. It needs no closing ceremony. It closes itself.

BAD SUGAR broke that arc by breaking the stop. Doses bypass appetite signals so more is eaten without more joy. Taste dulls while wanting loudens. You finish the plate and the packet and still circle. Is that the mark of a favourite? A favourite completes. This renews. A favourite thanks. This nags.

The fact is your body makes the glucose it needs from protein and ordinary food, so a clean table without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief change only.

Hear the size of that relief without turning it into a plan. I hand you no menu, no rules, no good-food list and bad-food list. Menus are the old prison with new paint. I hand you back to authority already inside you. Hunger to begin. Satisfaction to end. Taste awake between.

Think of tomorrow lived that way. Waking hungry and glad of it. Eating what you love when hunger calls. Stopping when satisfaction arrives. No counting. No guilt. No drawer arithmetic. Morning clear, afternoon steady, evening kind, mouth clean. That is not fantasy. That is an unstacked body doing what bodies do when left to do it.

Love Was Never In The Packet

Now meet the tenderest objection head on, because tenderness deserves truth.

"But I love dessert. Food is love. Sweet comforted me when days were hard."

I honour the days that were hard. I honour the wish to be comforted. We all reached for warmth in a cold hour. You reached with an open heart. There is nothing in that to condemn. The person was loving. The reading of where love came from was mistaken.

Isolate the moment and watch where the warmth actually sat.

A hard day. You come home tired. Someone you love puts a meal down. Steam rises. You sit at last. Shoulders drop. First bites in good hunger. Talk softens. The room warms. You feel held.

Where was the holding? In white powder from a packet, or in rest, warmth, hunger met, shoulders dropping, a loved voice near?

A birthday. Table crowded. Candles lit. Faces bright. Laughter loud. Cake appears and everyone sings. You eat a slice among hugs.

Where was the brightness? In the icing, or in singing, faces, hugs, belonging, the child proud of blown-out candles?

An evening low. You eat chocolate alone and feel soothed for minutes.

What soothed? Pause, sitting down at last, sweet taste loud on a tired tongue, then sourness and circling within the hour? Or would rest, a real meal to satisfied, a kind word, have soothed deeper and left no bill?

Ask the love its own plain questions, whose only honest answers concede the point.

If comfort lived in the sweet, why does the low return deeper within the hour and call for more, while comfort from a satisfying meal and rest leaves you quiet for hours?

If love lived in dessert, why does love remain on days dessert is absent and hunger is met with care, and why does loneliness remain on days dessert is present and care is absent?

If sweet completed eating, why do sweet finishers circle the cupboard while plain-meal finishers forget the kitchen and get on with enjoying life?

Your tables answer without cruelty. Meal completed. Company warmed. Hunger satisfied. The sweet only rode along and charged fog for the ride.

It was you that loved, not the dose.

Read that as justice returned. Years of thank yous laid at the wrong door come home. The appetite was yours. The tasting was yours. The laughter was yours. The getting through hard days was yours. Take the credit back from the fix and lay it where it belongs, on hunger, on food, on people, on your own brave body that kept showing up.

Fruit And Real Sweetness

A clever whisper will try a last conflation here. You have heard it in shops and kitchens.

"Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Sweet is sweet."

It sounds logical. It is a trick of words, and words matter.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Refined sugar with added free sugars with junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, in drinks, bars, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses. That is the quit target.

Natural sweetness inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals is not the target. It belongs on your table as part of favourite eating. A peach, berries, an apple eaten hungry, stopped at satisfied — that is real food, noticed and enjoyed, not a fix.

Taste the difference in lived body, not in labels.

Whole fruit eaten hungry: chew, juice, fibre, flavour unfolding, hunger easing toward thank you and done. Satisfaction arrives. Mind quiets.

BAD SUGAR eaten as a hit: rush, noise, brightness for minutes, sour mouth, thirst, hunger renewed while taste dulls. Satisfaction never arrives. Mind loudens.

One completes. One loops. Calling them the same because both taste sweet is like calling rain and a burst pipe the same because both are wet.

And hear this limit plainly so sweetness is never moralised into fear. Dessert as self-payment — celebrating with chocolate, rewarding a hard week, treating when you feel you deserve one — is a lived frame many of us used. Seeing the frame does not mean no one may ever enjoy sweet food. It means the reward was scene and rest and pride, not powder. Enjoyment remains. Favourite remains. Real meals remain, with fruit bright among them. Freedom adds flavour. It removes noise.

Look at a bowl of fruit with free eyes. Colour, scent, weight in the hand. Eaten when hungry, it pleases cleanly and lets you stop. No rustle of wrappers. No hurry. No regret before swallow ends. Children know this without teaching. Bodies know this without lessons. You knew this before daily hits taught you to doubt it.

I speak as one who feared grey and found colour. My tables did not empty. They filled. Hunger returned as friend, not emergency. Bread tasted like bread again. Vegetables tasted sweet from the ground. Fruit tasted bright without needing to shout. Meals ended with peace, not with bargaining. Work after lunch steadied because no stack pulled. Evenings softened because no grazing called. Nothing was counted. Nothing was punished. Eating became favourite again, marvellous in its plainness, wonderful in its sufficiency.

You will find the same, not by effort, but by attention. Hunger to open. Taste to enjoy. Satisfaction to close. That rhythm held you as a child. It holds you still beneath the noise. Let it be heard.

So now, at the warmth of this seeing, take the instruction that turns seeing into living. Speak it as your own smart choice, cheerfully, without gloom. Let it set the key for every table after. No dread. No bracing. Rejoicing.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy never lived in BAD SUGAR, it lived in hunger met and satisfaction felt.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes marvellous without any sweet seal.
- Satisfaction is a warm stop, belly thanked and mind quiet, not a thin virtue.
- The body makes what it needs from ordinary food, so clean tables leave nothing missing.
- Meal, company and rest gave the love and comfort, the packet only rode along.
- Whole fruit and plain meals belong to favourite eating and complete cleanly.
- Hunger to begin and satisfaction to end close eating on their own.
