Chapter 6
EATING IS FAVOURITE AGAIN

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the morning market, warm bread in paper, the hungry table, ripe fruit in a bowl, the clean stop at satisfied

*Real hunger makes plain food marvellous, and a satisfied body asks for nothing more.*

The Grey Fear

You arrive here with afternoons unmasked.

We watched the desk at three together. We saw the brief brightness and the longer fog. We saw borrowing called fuel. The strength was always yours. The work was always yours. The brightness that broke through for ten minutes was your body breaking through, not the packet helping.

Now a quieter fear rises to meet you, and I want to meet it with kindness, because it sounds like love.

"But food will be grey without something sweet."

"Dessert is love. Pudding is the favourite part. Without that, what is eating?"

"I am a comfort eater. Take the sweet away and you take the joy."

I know that voice. We all carried it. I carried it through family tables and tired evenings when I told myself the day only became kind when the sweet arrived. You carry it through kitchens where you have soothed and celebrated with chocolate and cake, through lunches where the savoury felt like waiting and the sweet felt like arrival.

The thought makes stopping look like loss. If joy lived in the packet, a life without the packet would be smaller, duller, well-behaved and sad. No wonder the heart tightens. Who would choose grey?

The fact is, joy never lived in the packet. Joy lived in hunger, in taste awake, in a body allowed to finish. BAD SUGAR only dulled that joy while taking credit for it. Eating, left to hunger and satisfaction, is favourite on its own.

I ask only that you test this as an investigator, not as a believer. Not with theory. With a hungry table, noticed bite by bite.

We have been taught to picture a free table as an empty table. A plate with something missing. A meal with the best bit removed. A polite, pale, joyless correctness. That picture is false, and it was painted for you. It was painted by years of doses that muted taste and then sold themselves as taste. It was painted by evenings where the tongue was tired and the mind was loud, and you were told the loudness was love of food. It was not love of food. It was noise around food.

Real eating is not pale. Real eating is loud in the best sense, vivid, warm, funny, full. It is appetite arriving clean, flavour arriving bright, company arriving near, and a body that knows when it has had enough and says so with pleasure.

You have lived that eating. You have not lived it as an idea. You have lived it as mornings after swimming, as lunch after walking, as supper after a day outdoors when plain food tasted marvellous and no one reached for anything after. Those hours were not grey. They were coloured. The colour came from hunger met, not from powder added.

So let dread go for this chapter. We are not here to argue loss. We are here to inhabit gain. Sit with me at a table. Eat hungry. Taste. Stop at satisfied. Feel what you were built to feel. Then decide whether anything grey remains.

The Hungry Table

Picture a morning you know from another part of life, before the daily stitching took over.

Market air cool. Stalls piled high. Green leaves wet, tomatoes warm from the sun, herbs sharp when you bruise them between fingers. Bread still warm in paper, crust cracking as you tear it. A bowl of fruit on the table at home, skins bright, juice ready to run. Eggs in a box, cheese on a board, soup waiting for noon, rice, fish, potatoes, butter, oil, salt, pepper, the plain favourites your house has always loved.

Now picture yourself arriving at that table truly hungry.

Not gnawing for a fix. Hungry in the clean sense. Belly empty, mouth fresh, attention clear. The kind of hunger that makes water taste good. The kind children feel at noon when play pauses and the body calls.

You sit. You eat.

First bite of warm bread with butter melting, soup steaming, vegetables sweet from the earth, cheese, eggs, rice, fish, whatever plain favourite your house loves. Taste arrives loud because hunger turned the volume up. You chew slower without deciding to. You notice salt, warmth, crunch, softness. Company laughs. You answer with your mouth full. Second helping because hunger still asks, then a pause.

That pause matters. Watch it.

Hunger softens. Interest narrows. The next bite still tastes good, but the urgency has gone. You leave half a slice. You push the bowl back an inch. Belly says thank you. Mind says done. No arithmetic. No bargaining. Pleasure complete.

Do you recognise that completeness? Like that child with the peach who stops mid-bite and runs off.

That is eating as the body built it. Hungry, eat, thank you, done.

We all lived this before the trap taught us a second way to eat. Not hungry, but empty for a hit. Not eating, but topping. Not satisfied, but dulled and circling. Mouth full, belly full, mind still loud. Sweet, bright, sour, more. That loop never said thank you and done. It said again.

Ask your own tables for evidence, kindly, without attack.

When you sat down starving after swimming, walking, working outdoors, did plain food taste marvellous without any sweet finish?

When you ate a real lunch to satisfied, with no pudding and no sweet drink, did you work and talk with ease, or did you miss anything except the thought of missing?

When you were absorbed at a loved table, laughing, bread passing hand to hand, did joy come from hunger met and people near, or from a packet emptied after?

There is only one honest answer. The joy was hunger met. The love was meal and company. The packet only sneaked a ride on a joy already moving.

This is why free eaters do not live in grey. Not because they settled for less. Because they kept the more. Hunger makes flavour. Satisfaction makes peace. BAD SUGAR muted both and sold the muting as extra pleasure.

Stay longer at that hungry table, because inhabiting is the whole job here. Notice how hunger seasons.

Hunger is the seasoning no shop sells. It makes a crust sing. It makes butter profound. It makes an apple loud. A tired tongue dosed hourly hears little. A hungry tongue hears everything. The first spoonful of soup after a morning's work lands like news. Salt, heat, depth. The second spoonful lands like comfort. The third lands like sufficiency gathering. By the fourth or fifth, the body begins its kind closing. Flavour still pleases, but call has quieted. That quiet is not loss of pleasure. That quiet is pleasure fulfilled.

We confused loud mind with loud taste. Dosed eating is loud in the head and quiet on the tongue. Hungry eating is quiet in the head and loud on the tongue. Which would you call favourite? The meal that leaves you thinking about the next bite while chewing this one, or the meal that leaves you thinking about the walk, the talk, the day, because the bite has done its work?

Watch hands at a hungry table. They move without hurry. They tear, they pass, they pause. No rustle of wrappers hidden in a palm. No quick pocketing of evidence. No standing at the cupboard in dim light. Hands belong to the table, not to the drawer. That belonging is dignity returned. Eating in the open, at ease, with people, at an hour, to an end.

Watch time at a hungry table. It stretches. A dosed hit takes minutes and steals hours. A hungry meal takes its time and gives hours back. Twenty minutes, thirty minutes, talk between bites, second serving without fear, then done for hours. No circling at dusk. No sour mouth at nine. No bargaining about tomorrow. Time freed is joy freed.

What Satisfaction Feels Like

Satisfaction has been slandered.

We were taught to hear it as absence. No dessert. No glow. Restraint. Being good. A thin virtue with a long face.

The reality is the other way around.

Satisfaction is a positive sensation, as warm and physical as hunger itself. Learn its signs and you will trust it as you trust thirst quenched.

Hunger begins as information. Empty ease, then clear call, then strong request. It sharpens smell. It brightens taste. It makes simple food vivid. It never shouts in panic when the stack is gone. It speaks.

Eating answers. Warmth spreads. Attention settles on flavour. Chewing slows on its own. The body leans back a fraction. Breath deepens. The next mouthful pleases, but does not command.

Then the stop. Clean, kind, final. Not stuffed. Not deprived. Satisfied. Belly full enough. Mind quiet. Taste thanked. Hands still without order. You want to talk, to walk, to work, to play. Food thoughts leave because their job is done.

That is the full arc. It needs no sweet seal. It needs no closing ceremony. It closes itself.

BAD SUGAR broke that arc by breaking the stop. Doses bypass appetite signals so more is eaten without more joy. Taste dulls while wanting loudens. You finish the plate and the packet and still circle. Is that the mark of a favourite? A favourite completes. This renews. A favourite thanks. This nags.

Learn the stop in your own body so you trust it again.

It is not a rule. It is a feeling. A small sigh. A loosening of interest. A glance away from the plate toward the room. Food still looks good, but the pull has gone. You could take another bite, but you do not want it. You want conversation. You want air. You want the next part of the day. That wanting of the next part is proof. Satisfied bodies turn outward. Looping bodies turn inward to the cupboard.

We feared the stop meant missing. The stop means returning. Returning to work with a clear head. Returning to play with light legs. Returning to talk without mouth-full mumbling through a wrapper. The meal ends so life resumes. That resumption is marvellous. That resumption is what eating is for.

I speak as one who lost the stop and found it again. For years I ate to dulled, not to done. Plate, packet, fridge, drawer. Belly full, mouth busy, mind loud. I called that appetite. It was not appetite. It was echo. When echo quieted, real hunger returned, shy at first, then clear. I learned its voice in days. Morning emptiness glad to be awake. Noon call glad to sit. Evening ease glad to close. I learned the stop in the same week. Pause, sigh, push back, done. No counting. No guilt. No triumph. Just thank you. If you think you have forgotten, you have not. The rhythm waits beneath noise. Let noise cease and rhythm sounds.

The fact is your body makes the glucose it needs from protein and ordinary food, so a clean table without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief change only.

Hear the size of that relief without turning it into a plan. I hand you no menu, no rules, no good-food list and bad-food list. Menus are the old prison with new paint. I hand you back to authority already inside you. Hunger to begin. Satisfaction to end. Taste awake between.

Think of tomorrow lived that way. Waking hungry and glad of it. Eating what you love when hunger calls. Stopping when satisfaction arrives. No counting. No guilt. No drawer arithmetic. Morning clear, afternoon steady, evening kind, mouth clean. That is not fantasy. That is an unstacked body doing what bodies do when left to do it.

The Morning Bowl And The Midday Plate

Let us walk a whole day at hungry tables, so inhabiting becomes memory you can step into.

Morning. You wake empty. Not shaky-empty. Clean-empty. Mouth fresh. Belly ready. Light through the window. You drink water and it tastes good, which tells you hunger is true. You make what you love. Porridge with fruit, eggs with bread, yoghurt with berries, rice, soup left from night, whatever your house calls breakfast. You sit, not stand. You eat, not graze. First bites bright. Hunger makes even plain toast vivid. You eat to that kind pause, then stop, leave food without grief, wash the bowl, go out. No mid-morning crash that needs bridging, because no morning spike built it. Attention holds. Hands steady. Mouth clean.

Midday. Hunger returns as friend. Not emergency. Invitation. You shop or chop or warm. Market vegetables, fresh bread, oil, herbs. A plate you love, enough to satisfy, not to stun. You sit with people or with quiet. You taste. Crunch, warmth, salt, sweet from the ground. Carrots sweet. Onions sweet. Tomatoes sweet in their own clean way. You take a second serving because hunger still asks, then feel that softening, that thank you, that done. You push back an inch. You talk a while. You rise. Afternoon holds without drawer arithmetic, because lunch closed. Work resumes. Talk resumes. Life resumes.

Notice what is absent. No sweet seal needed to feel finished. No pudding to mark arrival. Arrival was hunger met. Seal was satisfaction felt. The meal closed itself. That self-closing is freedom you can feel in the belly.

Evening. Hunger again, mild after a lived day. Supper plain and loved. Company near. Bread passing. Laughter. You eat hungry, you stop satisfied, you linger because lingering is pleasure, not because circling holds you. Mouth clean after. Mind quiet after. Evening yours for talk, walk, book, sleep, not for nibbling through fog.

Ask those three tables their plain questions.

If plain meals leave grey, why does the morning bowl eaten hungry taste brighter than the sweetened cereal eaten gnawing, and why does brightness last for hours after the bowl and for minutes after the cereal?

If satisfaction is deprivation, why does the midday plate to satisfied leave you free for the afternoon, while the plated lunch plus sweet finish leaves you circling by three?

If eating needs a sweet close, why do hungry tables close on their own with peace, while dosed tables never close and call that openness love of food?

Your days answer. Hunger opened. Satisfaction closed. Plain food sufficed and delighted. The extra was never extra. It was noise.

Love Was Never In The Packet

Now meet the tenderest objection head on, because tenderness deserves truth.

"But I love dessert. Food is love. Sweet comforted me when days were hard."

I honour the days that were hard. I honour the wish to be comforted. We all reached for warmth in a cold hour. You reached with an open heart. There is nothing in that to condemn. The person was loving. The reading of where love came from was mistaken.

Isolate the moment and watch where the warmth actually sat.

A hard day. You come home tired. Someone you love puts a meal down. Steam rises. You sit at last. Shoulders drop. First bites in good hunger. Talk softens. The room warms. You feel held.

Where was the holding? In white powder from a packet, or in rest, warmth, hunger met, shoulders dropping, a loved voice near?

A birthday. Table crowded. Candles lit. Faces bright. Laughter loud. Cake appears and everyone sings. You eat a slice among hugs.

Where was the brightness? In the icing, or in singing, faces, hugs, belonging, the child proud of blown-out candles?

An evening low. You eat chocolate alone and feel soothed for minutes.

What soothed? Pause, sitting down at last, sweet taste loud on a tired tongue, then sourness and circling within the hour? Or would rest, a real meal to satisfied, a kind word, have soothed deeper and left no bill?

Ask the love its own plain questions, whose only honest answers concede the point.

If comfort lived in the sweet, why does the low return deeper within the hour and call for more, while comfort from a satisfying meal and rest leaves you quiet for hours?

If love lived in dessert, why does love remain on days dessert is absent and hunger is met with care, and why does loneliness remain on days dessert is present and care is absent?

If sweet completed eating, why do sweet finishers circle the cupboard while plain-meal finishers forget the kitchen and get on with enjoying life?

Your tables answer without cruelty. Meal completed. Company warmed. Hunger satisfied. The sweet only rode along and charged fog for the ride.

It was you that loved, not the dose.

Read that as justice returned. Years of thank yous laid at the wrong door come home. The appetite was yours. The tasting was yours. The laughter was yours. The getting through hard days was yours. Take the credit back from the fix and lay it where it belongs, on hunger, on food, on people, on your own brave body that kept showing up.

Hear this limit with free ears, so love is never turned into fear. Many of us used sweet as self-payment. Celebrating with chocolate. Rewarding a hard week. Treating when you feel you deserve one. Naming that frame does not mean no one may ever enjoy sweet food. It means the reward was scene and rest and pride, not powder. The cinema warmth was people and story and laughter. The birthday warmth was belonging and song. The hard-day relief was sitting, pausing, being kind to yourself. Enjoyment remains. Favourite remains. Love remains, larger now that credit sits where credit belongs.

We called dessert love because love was near dessert. Proximity fooled us. A loved table with sweet after got credit for the sweet, not for the table. A hard day eased by sitting got credit for the bar, not for the sitting. Reverse the credit and love grows, not shrinks. You do not lose comfort. You find where comfort lived all along, and you can visit without paying fog.

Fruit And Real Sweetness

A clever whisper will try a last conflation here. You have heard it in shops and kitchens.

"Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Sweet is sweet."

It sounds logical. It is a trick of words, and words matter.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Refined sugar with added free sugars with junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, in drinks, bars, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses. That is the quit target.

Natural sweetness inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals is not the target. It belongs on your table as part of favourite eating. A peach, berries, an apple eaten hungry, stopped at satisfied — that is real food, noticed and enjoyed, not a fix.

Taste the difference in lived body, not in labels.

Whole fruit eaten hungry: chew, juice, fibre, flavour unfolding, hunger easing toward thank you and done. Satisfaction arrives. Mind quiets.

BAD SUGAR eaten as a hit: rush, noise, brightness for minutes, sour mouth, thirst, hunger renewed while taste dulls. Satisfaction never arrives. Mind loudens.

One completes. One loops. Calling them the same because both taste sweet is like calling rain and a burst pipe the same because both are wet.

Look at a bowl of fruit with free eyes. Colour, scent, weight in the hand. Eaten when hungry, it pleases cleanly and lets you stop. No rustle of wrappers. No hurry. No regret before swallow ends. Bodies know this without lessons. You knew this before daily hits taught you to doubt it.

Eat an apple hungry and watch. Skin snaps. Juice runs. Sweetness arrives with crunch and scent and chew, not as blast but as unfolding. Hunger eases with each bite. After one, after two, thank you arrives. You put the core down without grief and turn outward. That is real sweetness doing its true work: pleasing, feeding, closing.

Eat a bar gnawing and watch the other arc. Blast, brightness, hurry. Second bite less tasted, third barely tasted. Hunger not eased but renewed. Mouth sour. Thirst up. Mind already on the next. That is not sweetness. That is hit. Sweetness pleases. Hit loops.

Vegetables tell the same truth quietly. Roasted roots, fresh peas, corn, peppers, tomatoes warmed by sun. Their sweetness is deep, not loud. Eaten hungry with bread and oil and salt, they satisfy profoundly. Dosed tongues miss them, because dosed tongues hear only blast. Hungry tongues hear whisper and find whisper marvellous. Your tongue will re-learn whisper in days. Flavour will widen as noise fades. Bread will taste like bread again. Butter will taste like butter. Water will taste good. That return is not small. That return is eating handed back.

Evenings Without Bargaining

Picture evenings freed, because evenings were where grey fear bit hardest.

Old evening: supper, then sweet, then grazing. Plate, packet, fridge light, drawer. Mouth never clean. Mind never quiet. Television on, taste off. Nibbling through fog while promising firmness tomorrow. Sour mouth at night. Vow at midnight.

New evening: supper hungry and loved. Steam, talk, second helping, pause, done. Mouth clean. Mind quiet. Evening yours. Walk, book, bath, talk, sleep. No cupboard arithmetic. No wrapper evidence. No bargaining about Fridays and holidays and deserving. Bargaining belonged to noise. Quiet needs no bargain.

You will ask: will I not miss the ritual? The bowl on the lap, the spoon, the glow?

Ask what ritual gave. Lap, couch, warmth, rest, story on screen, person near. Those remain. Bowl of fruit if hungry, tea, water, nothing if satisfied. Ritual was rest and company and pause. Hit only rode it. Keep ritual. Drop hit. Evening stays kind and grows kinder, because kindness no longer bills.

I speak as one who feared grey and found colour. My tables did not empty. They filled. Hunger returned as friend, not emergency. Bread tasted like bread again. Vegetables tasted sweet from the ground. Fruit tasted bright without needing to shout. Meals ended with peace, not with bargaining. Work after lunch steadied because no stack pulled. Evenings softened because no grazing called. Nothing was counted. Nothing was punished. Eating became favourite again, marvellous in its plainness, wonderful in its sufficiency.

You will find the same, not by effort, but by attention. Hunger to open. Taste to enjoy. Satisfaction to close. That rhythm held you as a child. It holds you still beneath the noise. Let it be heard.

So now, at the warmth of this seeing, take the instruction that turns seeing into living. Speak it as your own smart choice, cheerfully, without gloom. Let it set the key for every table after. No dread. No bracing. Rejoicing.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy lives in hunger met and satisfaction felt, not in packets added after.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes vivid and closes on its own.
- Satisfaction is a warm stop, belly thanked and mind quiet, that frees the hours after.
- The body makes what it needs from ordinary food, so honest tables leave nothing missing.
- Meal, company and rest gave the love and comfort long credited to sweet.
- Whole fruit and plain meals belong to favourite eating and complete cleanly.
- Hunger to begin and satisfaction to end need no counting, no guilt, no bargaining.
