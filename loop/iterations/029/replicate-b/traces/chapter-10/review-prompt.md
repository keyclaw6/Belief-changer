Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — Friday kitchen with circled calendar and bright packets, cliff edge with a chalked safe line, box with one biscuit gone, car seatbelt clicked in a parked car, quiet shop doorway at dusk

*One kept sweet keeps the whole slavery alive, because the trap lives in the exception.*

The Negotiator

You have killed the glow where it lived brightest.

The cinema warmth stayed with the film. The birthday light stayed with the faces. The sofa softness stayed with rest and quiet. You saw perception lie with two cups from one pot. You are no longer fooled about what joy belongs to.

And now a new voice arrives, very reasonable, very adult, very worried for you.

It says, well then, just keep the sensible ones. Just Fridays. Just birthdays. Just one with coffee. Just keep it out of the house. Just start properly after the holidays. Just wean a little. Just find a cleaner swap.

I know that voice. We all hired it. I hired it for years while my evenings disappeared. You hire it still because it sounds like maturity. It is not maturity. It is the last move of a beaten con trying to keep one cord plugged in.

Hear it plainly in your own dialect.

“Just one on Fridays is fine.”

“I don’t keep it in the house, so I’m in control.”

“I’ll start after the holidays, after the birthday, on Monday.”

Every one of those sentences feels like freedom choosing. Every one is slavery negotiating.

Ask the negotiator its plain questions, whose only honest answers free you.

If Fridays are safe, why do Fridays glow brighter all week, colouring Tuesday and Wednesday with waiting? Does safe behave like that, pulling the mind toward itself for days?

If one is control, why does one always talk about the next while still sweet on the tongue? Does control need to argue for more before the first is swallowed?

There is only one honest answer. The exception does not tame the want. The exception feeds it.

We believed moderation proved we were free. Moderation proved we were hooked, because only hooked eaters need rules about one.

The Friday Bargain

Let us live the Friday bargain hour by hour, because argument never lands until you feel it in your own week.

Monday morning. You declare the rule with relief. No BAD SUGAR till Friday. House cleared. Drawer empty. You feel virtuous, light, almost free. Monday passes with a little edgy circling at three, Tuesday passes with louder talk at eight.

“I’ve managed to be good this week.”

Wednesday the talk sharpens. You picture Friday. You choose the shop. You choose the bar, the cake, the pudding you will allow. The picture grows warmer each time you visit it. Thursday the picture is bright as a film. You can taste Friday on Thursday tongue. You tell friends about your sensible plan and they praise you.

Friday arrives at last. Morning drags. Afternoon drags. At seven you buy the allowed dose with ceremony. Hands a little quick. Breath a little short. First bite, eyes closing. Bright for minutes. Then wrapper rustle.

Then gone. Then flat. Then fog. Then the old familiar circling for more, mouth never clean, belly loud, head dull before the film ends.

And then the creep every negotiator knows.

Thursday night borrows a little Friday, because you were so good. Saturday morning borrows a little Friday, because Friday was so short. Sunday becomes preparation for another good week. Monday restarts the waiting.

That is not a week with one sweet in it. That is a week owned by one sweet.

The fact is restriction makes precious. Forbid a dose till Friday and Friday turns holy. Make a dose rare and rarity paints it gold. We lived that yo-yo for years, tight rules, bright breaks, larger breaks, new rules on Monday. You live it still whenever you set a house rule and watch the rule become the centre of the house.

Does holy behave like that, leaving you flat, foggy, circling within the hour? Does sensible leave you bargaining on Saturday for what Friday promised?

No. Sensible would leave you satisfied and quiet. What leaves you loud and reaching was never sensible.

Inside the Sugar Trap there is no small holy corner. There is only the trap, whole, waiting for the one cord you leave plugged in.

Jump And Fall Only A Little

Picture it as sugar, because pictures tell truth where words negotiate.

A friend walks you to a cliff on a bright morning. Sea far below. Wind fresh. He points to the edge and smiles kindly.

“Jump. You will love the rush. And don’t worry. I have marked a safe line halfway down. Fall only to that line. Fall only a few metres. Everyone does it on Fridays.”

You stare at him. You laugh. You ask if he is mad.

Can you fall only a little once you jump? Does gravity honour chalk marks? Is there a skilled way to drop?

No one would call that offer sane. Yet we accept the same offer weekly with BAD SUGAR.

“Have dessert on Fridays, but don’t slide.”

“Take just one biscuit, but don’t empty the box.”

“Keep the birthday slice, but don’t wake the daily circling.”

Jump, but fall only a little. Dose, but crave only a little. Feed the loop, but keep the loop small.

The fact is the fall is the trap. The dose lights the next because the bright is relief of a low the last dose built, then guarantees the low returns. One Friday dose rebuilds the edgy emptiness for Saturday. Saturday whispers for a small repair. Repair rebuilds Monday want. By Wednesday you are not choosing Friday. Friday is pulling you.

Ask the cliff its questions.

If a safe fall exists, why does no negotiator stay on it, year after year, rule after rule? Where are the lifelong happy Friday-only eaters, bright-eyed, quiet-minded, never creeping?

If skill controls the drop, why did your skilled weeks all end the same way, larger packs, louder cues, shorter bright, longer fog? Did skill fail, or did gravity work?

There is only one honest answer. Gravity works. The trap works. The chalk line is a joke told by the jumper to himself on the way down.

You do not need a safer cliff. You need to walk away from the edge rejoicing that you never have to jump again.

Why One Lights The Next

“But it was only one. How can one small bar own a week?”

I hear you. We all asked it while holding foil. I asked it nightly while promising this packet would be the last sensible one. You ask it still while feeling the second reach rise before the first is gone.

Look at your own hand, not at theory.

You permit a single biscuit after supper. First bite bright. Second bite less bright. Hand moving before decision. Kitchen quiet. Box open. Hand again. Then empty. Then standing in dim light with crumbs and a dull head, thinking a sentence you know by heart.

“I feel like i cant stop.”

That sentence is not weakness. That sentence is not character. That sentence is the trap signature, spoken honestly. The craving, once triggered, carries the binge. One lights the next because the next was already built into the one.

We were never out of control people. We were controlled by a loop that turns one into many. I lived that loop. You live it whenever you test it. Test after test ends the same way, not because you fail the test, but because the test is rigged.

Ask your hand its questions.

If one proves control, why does one so rarely stay one in your own evenings, witnessed a hundred times? Does control behave like that, vanishing at the exact moment it is proved?

If the first bite satisfied, why does satisfaction never arrive, only louder calling? What satisfied leaves the mouth louder than it found it?

There is only one honest answer. One does not satisfy. One reawakens.

And see the second half. Starve the loop wholly and the loop thins fast. Feed the loop rarely and the loop stays bright forever. Rare doses get the full ceremony, the full waiting, the full glow, the full relief of days of low relieved at once. Frequent grazers at least grow dull to the trick. Friday keepers keep the trick shiny.

That is why cut-down never calms. Cut-down polishes. Each kept hit arrives bigger in the mind, longer waited for, harder to question. The Sweet Con loves the Friday rule because the Friday rule keeps belief alive. Belief alive keeps the tiny physical echo alive. Belief dead lets the echo starve.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That verdict holds for daily boxes and for holy Fridays alike. Daily or rare, cheap or costly, weekday or celebration, the dose does the same work. It brightens minutes by muting a low it built, then rebuilds the low. Rare does not mean mild. Rare means worshipped.

I Do Not Keep It In The House

“But I don’t keep it in the house. Surely that proves I am free.”

I honour the tidy cupboard. We all tidied cupboards. I threw packets away on Sundays with solemn pride while my feet knew the corner shop route by heart. You tidy still and call tidy freedom.

Let us look kindly at tidy.

If the house is clear, where do you walk at eight when the edgy emptiness rises? Do you sit easy with tea, or do you find reasons to pass the bright aisle, to stop for petrol, to check the shop for milk you do not need?

If absence is freedom, why does absence fill the mind with planning, routes, allowances, rewards earned elsewhere? Does freedom plan like that, mapping shops by closing time?

If control lives in the cupboard lock, who lives in the mind that counts days till Friday, pictures the bar, rehearses the order? Is the lock on the door, or is the lock on you?

Your feet answer. Tidy cupboards never tidied want. They moved want outside and dressed it as virtue.

We were conned into calling geography freedom. Freedom is not a locked drawer. Freedom is a quiet mind in an open shop. Pity the bright packs as you pass, glad no hand pulls, and you will know the difference between locked and free. Locked watches the clock. Free forgets the clock.

And see how the house rule keeps the belief fed. “I must not have it near me” whispers “it is precious.” Precious whispers “I miss it.” Miss whispers “Friday will be wonderful.” Friday arrives wonderful for minutes, dull for hours, and Monday rebuilds the lock. Round and round on the roller coaster, locked doors rattling all the way.

A free eater keeps no lock because no lock is needed. Bread in the cupboard. Fruit in the bowl. Meals eaten hungry to satisfied. Shops walked through easy. No ceremony. No guard. The mind quiet because nothing precious waits anywhere.

You do not need a better lock. You need no precious thing to lock away.

I Will Start Tomorrow

“But Monday is better. After the birthday. After the holidays. When work calms. Then I will stop properly.”

I know tomorrow. We all polished tomorrow till it shone. I polished a thousand tomorrows while tonight’s foil piled up. You polish still because tomorrow feels kind.

Tomorrow is not kind. Tomorrow is the trap talking.

Ask tomorrow its questions.

If tomorrow is safer, what changes between tonight and tomorrow inside your mouth, your belly, your shops? Will packets grow less bright? Will evenings grow less circling? Will belief die by waiting?

If delay proves wisdom, why does delay always add more doses, more fog, more waiting, never less want? Does wisdom behave like that, charging interest for its own advice?

If tonight is harmless, why do you need tomorrow at all? And if tonight harms, why pay it again tonight?

There is only one honest answer. Delay is permission dressed as planning.

We told ourselves we needed the right moment. The right moment never came because the trap moves the moment. Birthdays breed holidays. Holidays breed Mondays. Mondays breed hard weeks that deserve a bar. The calendar fills while the low rebuilds daily.

And count the cost honestly, without gloom, as information. Every tomorrow week is seven more evenings of bright then fog, seven more three o’clocks of lift then drop, seven more mornings of dull mouth. Not to frighten you. To free you from the tale that waiting is free. Waiting is expensive, and the bill is paid nightly in clarity and ease.

The fact is freedom is not safer tomorrow. Freedom is clearest now, while seeing is bright. Tomorrow seeing dims and bargaining returns. Now you see the cliff, the lock, the Friday glow for what they are. Now is the easy moment, because now belief is thin.

Do not trust tomorrow to do what today already did. Today saw. Let today finish the job by refusing the exception.

The Cleaner Swap

“But what about a smaller swap? A lighter bar. A diet drink. A little honey. Fruit sweets. Just wean with less-bad hits?”

I hear the hope for a soft ladder down. We all built ladders. I built them from larger to smaller packs, from bars to biscuits to sweet drinks, each rung greased. You build still, hoping less will mean free.

Less never meant free. Less meant waiting between hits, brighter hits, harder belief.

Ask the ladder its questions.

If lighter proves free, why does lighter still end with circling, still end with second reach, still end with mouth never clean? Does free circle?

If swapping solved the loop, why do swappers still ride the roller coaster, short term lift then fast drop, good weeks then bad evenings, new rules each Monday? Did the packet change, or did the name change?

If weaning thins want, why does want grow sharpest between small hits, pictures brightest, hands quickest? Does thinning sharpen?

There is only one honest answer. The loop does not read labels. It reads hits.

BAD SUGAR as this book means it means refined and added free sugars with junk processed hits, sweets, bars, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweet drinks, sweetened cereals and grazing doses of starchy snack-carbs eaten as sweet hits. Smaller of the same is still the same. Lighter of the same still lights the next. The body meets hits as spike then call, whatever costume the hit wears. Whole fruit eaten hungry to satisfied closes quietly. Hits open loudly. Different loads, different loops.

We were taught to hunt the perfect mild dose. There is no mild dose inside the loop. There is only fed or starving. Fed glows then calls. Starving for days, unfed, dies to quiet.

You do not need a softer cord. You need every cord out, rejoicing.

The Seatbelt And The Steering Wheel

Now the honest worry rises, and it deserves a plain answer.

“But what if a crumb slips in? A sauce sweetened. A bite offered. A meal cooked by kind hands. Am I doomed if perfection breaks?”

No. Perfection is not the method. Belief is the method.

Picture a car on an ordinary road. You click the seatbelt. Firm across the chest. Quiet click. You drive well, watching the road, glad of the belt, never thinking of crashing.

Does the belt exist so you can drive wildly? Do you steer toward walls because the belt will hold? Do you test the belt nightly?

Of course not. The belt is for the accidental jolt, the sudden stop no driver plans. It absorbs what accident brings and is forgotten again. Safe driving is the steering, not the belt.

So with BAD SUGAR. Your steering is the belief, clear and joyful. Your body easily absorbs an accidental crumb, a hidden spoon in a sauce, a kind cook’s unmarked dish, without waking the old life. The crumb passes. The belly settles. Nothing revives unless belief is let back in.

But the mind cannot afford a deliberate swerve. Choose the Friday bar on purpose and you have not tested the belt. You have steered into the wall to prove the belt works. Deliberate exception rebuilds the Sweet Con, wakes the Nibbler, repaints the dose precious, restarts waiting and creep. Accident is absorbed. Intention is instruction.

I lay no medical sentence on you, and if you live with diabetes or take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry any medical risk, your clinician leads and this book is belief only.

Guard the belief, not the behaviour with panic. If a hidden dose slips in, smile. Notice the dullness if dullness comes. Rejoice that accident proved nothing precious. Drive on, steering true, belt quiet. If the old thought whispers “you blew it, you may as well empty the box,” hear it for what it is, a dead enemy rattling, not an order. One accidental jolt never drove a car into a ditch. Only hands on the wheel drive.

We spent years panicking about crumbs while steering weekly into Fridays. Panic about accident, permission for intention — the trap loves that upside-down care. Turn it right side up. Calm about accident. Firm about intention. Belief guarded joyfully. Then the road stays easy.

Ask the wheel its question.

If safety lives in never tasting a grain again, who could ever be free in a world of sauces and kind cooks? Does freedom behave like that, trembling over spoons?

If safety lives in clear steering, why does one accidental taste leave free eaters calm, clear, pitying the bright packs rather than envying them? What stayed safe, the spoon or the reading?

There is only one honest answer. Reading steers. Spoons do not.

Click the belt and forget it. Steer true and rejoice at the open road.

You Are Extreme

One last voice stands behind all the bargains, speaking about the method itself. It must be met here or it will rebuild belief from outside.

“This all-or-nothing talk is fanatical. Normal people moderate. Mature people allow treats. You are turning a pleasure into poison.”

I honour your wish to be normal and kind. I ask you to see who taught normal.

Normal was manufactured. Bright aisles, cartoon packs, checkout ambush, adverts tying love to foil, bliss-point recipes built to light the next. We did not vote for normal. We were trained into it before we could choose. Calling trained normal proves nothing about freedom. A full cinema laughing proves the film funny. It proves nothing about foil.

Ask mature its questions.

If mature means one Friday bar, why do mature moderators still circle, still bargain, still creep to Thursday and Saturday, still end the night flat? Does maturity circle?

If extreme means refusing the whole trap, is the non-smoker extreme for refusing the whole pack, or free? Is the clear driver extreme for refusing the cliff, or sane?

If totality proves fanaticism, why do free eaters live calmest of all, no rules, no clocks, no locks, meals favourite, mornings bright, shops easy? Does fanaticism behave like that, forgetting the thing it fears?

There is only one honest answer. Totality is not extreme. Totality is the only stable shape.

We called it poison in sweet coat where the coat fooled kind people, never to shame the eater. Warm to you always. Harsh to the con always. The eater was never extreme for wanting quiet. The trap was extreme for selling loud as love.

Other voices will call you rigid. Labels will call moderation balance. Friends will describe hard battles and warn you. Every one of those voices rebuilds the old belief whether the mouth means kindness or profit. You have seen the builders. Do not hand them the keys again. Pity the struggler, never envy the packet. Pity the bright aisle, never mourn it.

The fact is you need no better rule, no softer swap, no cleaner house, no kinder tomorrow. You need no exception. The exception is the trap. Remove the exception and the trap stands with nothing to stand on.

One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.
