Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THE WANT

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — late supermarket aisle under bright lights, bliss-point packs and cartoon laughter, checkout ambush, mirror of old excuses, open cell door and pacing lion, dashboard warning lights

*Your want was not yours to begin with, it was built for profit and your fear was built with it, and once you see the builders you stop blaming yourself.*

The Aisle That Wanted For You

You have seen the split.

You watched the evening twitch and named it truly, a tiny grumble kept loud by a story. You felt how small the creature is when belief no longer feeds it. That seeing is yours. No dose can take it.

Now walk with me where that story was taught, because a story that large was never born in your belly.

Yesterday evening I stood in a supermarket with a basket of plain food and watched the aisle do its work. Go there with me in memory. Bright light, low shelves at child eye height, packs shouting in reds and golds. Bliss-point packs row on row, each one tuned to hit bright then fade fast, each one priced to look like kindness. Cartoon mascots laugh from boxes, bears and tigers promising friendship to your children. Sweet drinks stand cold at the end, sweating, as if they were waiting only for you. Then the last trap before the till, the checkout ambush, bars and buttons at hand height where tired hands wait in a queue with nothing to do but reach.

We stood there tired after work, hungry in a clean way, meaning to buy supper. Within minutes the mouth watered, the hand hovered, the old thought spoke.

"Everyone eats it so it must be fine."

"Treat when you feel you deserve one."

"Sugary somethings to get me through."

Who spoke? Was that your reasoning, or was it the aisle speaking through your mouth?

The fact is, desire on that scale does not grow by accident. Food engineers tune crunch and melt and sweet-salt to a bright peak that fades on purpose, so the glow calls for another dose. Advertisers pour love and laughter over the pack, so packets arrive already dressed as reward and comfort. Ubiquity does the rest, sweet on every corner, sweet at every till, sweet at every birthday photographed, until the abnormal looks normal and the normal looks grey.

We were never foolish to fall for it. We were outgunned from childhood. I was outgunned. You were outgunned. Lone eaters against armies of flavour labs and cartoon friends and jingles that taught us before we could read.

Ask the aisle its plain questions, whose only honest answers change the blame.

If that want were yours by nature, why did it need ten thousand reminders, mascots, adverts, placements, to stay alive? Do natural hungers need cartoons to be felt?

If those packs were food for hunger, why do they leave you flat, foggy and never satisfied within the hour, circling for more? Does food behave like that, renewing hunger by feeding it?

If the choice were free, why does the hand move before the mind decides at ten at night, in queues, at tills, exactly where planners placed it? Whose hand is that?

There is only one honest answer. The want was manufactured. The hand was guided.

That is not a metaphor to soften you. It is the shape of the Sugar Trap in brick and light. A trap that must advertise to live. A con that must dress poison in sweet coat to sell. Once you see the builders, you stop calling the building your home.

You were conned, never weak. The blame belongs on the makers, never on you.

Everyone Eats It So It Must Be Fine

"I know the aisle pushes. But everyone eats it. My family always did. My friends do. Normal people do. It cannot be that bad or nobody would allow it."

I hear you, because we all said it. I said it for years while my evenings disappeared into wrappers. You say it while your trolley fills with what trolleys around you carry. It feels like wisdom. It feels like proportion.

It is brainwashing wearing the mask of common sense.

When did common become proof of good? Whole streets once smoked over breakfast and called it normal. Whole offices once drank at lunch and called it normal. Normal only proves the con was large, never that the dose was kind.

Look kindly at what normal costs. In laboratories where sweet comes in on-off bursts, binges grow, wanting fires, lows follow highs. In life where sweet comes in diet-binge bursts, the same loop walks the streets in daylight, lunchbox, desk drawer, evening box emptied before swallow ends. The roller coaster you ride is not private folly. It is public traffic.

On standard questionnaires about highly palatable food, about 14 in 100 adults and about 12 in 100 children answer in ways that look like addiction patterns, a share much like alcohol and tobacco. I tell you that to lift shame, not to stamp you. That is not a diagnosis on you. It only means you are not singled out, not strange, not broken. Millions hear that eight o'clock rustle louder than it is. Millions were taught the same lines.

If everyone eats it, everyone pays. Look without flinching and with pity, never contempt.

Men who take sweet treats daily are more likely to feel persistently low years later, even when earlier mood is taken into account. I do not say a bar causes darkness in you, and I do not diagnose your mood. I say the daily treat predicts later low rather than fixing low. The rescuer does not rescue.

Needing a larger family pack for the same glow is not greed. Wrapper rustle and bakery smell firing before the bite is not compulsion. It is learned circuitry, crowding that dulls the bright while cues grow loud, lit before eating, recalled where feeding used to happen. That firing does not force your hand. It only recalls old feeding places.

So meet the normal excuse with pity for us all, not fear for yourself.

If normal proved safe, why are normal mouths drilled, normal afternoons fogged, normal evenings circled, normal sleep broken by a clean mouth never found? Does safe behave like that, leaving millions tired by three?

If normal proved free, why does the normal eater need a book on how to stop? Do free choosers need escape manuals for pleasures?

If normal proved kind, why does the kindest normal day still end flat, with regret before swallow ends and a promise of tomorrow?

Your days answer. Normal is not proof. Normal is the proof of scale. The Sweet Con grew so large we mistook its walls for the world.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you. And it does it to millions at once.

I Am The Addictive Type

"All right, the aisle built it and normal spread it. But my case is different. I have always been like this. Sweet-toothed family. Addictive personality. Others can take or leave it. I feel like i cant stop once I start. I must be wired wrong."

That whisper kept me trapped longer than any advert, because it turns a common con into a private doom. It sounds like honesty. It sounds like science. It is the Sweet Con speaking in your voice.

Let me answer with love, and with steel, because you deserve both.

First, look at cause and effect turned the right way up.

You believe your traits caused the bingeing. The fact is, the bingeing caused the traits you blame. Secrecy, evening circling, short term lift then fast drop, I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, eating standing in dim light, hiding wrappers, promising Monday. Those are not personality. Those are the signature of the trap lived from inside. Free eaters do not show them. Trapped eaters all show them, whatever their families, whatever their temperaments. Same trap, same marks.

Ask the mirror its questions.

If you were wired for sweet, why did a peach eaten hungry to satisfied close hunger for hours in childhood, without counting, without guilt, without a second peach chased? Were you wired differently then, or simply unpushed?

If stopping proves wiring, why do the most frantic grazers forget the kitchen all evening once the belief dies, without struggle, without clocks watched? Did their genes change overnight, or only their reading?

If one bite carries you to the box, does that prove you are broken? Or does it prove the trap is built to carry, one dose lighting the low that calls for the next, schedule driving volume?

There is only one honest answer. The traits are the RESULT, not the cause. The loop made the person you blame.

Second, look at history larger than one mirror.

If sweet need were written in a few faulty characters, why do millions across families, streets, countries show the same evening call once sweet is everywhere and dosed in bursts? Did millions share one gene, or one supply? Population spread follows supply, not blood. The want arrived where the packs arrived.

And hear the graceful close that ends the excuse forever. Even if you did carry a hungrier wanting than your neighbour, this method still frees you. We do not ask you to change your nature. We ask you to see a con. A hungrier belly still closes on real food. A louder cue still fades unfed. A deeper history still ends when feeding ends. Nature never needed BAD SUGAR. The body makes its own steady fuel from plain meals and carries you clear.

You are not the addictive type. You are the conned type, which is to say the human type. Warm blood, tired evenings, kind heart, bright packs everywhere. There is nothing in that to condemn.

The Low That Warns

Now I must show you the body you were taught to mistrust, because mistrust keeps you dosing.

You were taught your body is faulty, that dips mean you must top up, that fog means you need a hit, that restlessness means you lack fuel. You learned to unscrew the warning bulb and call darkness light.

Your body is a precision machine. Marvellous in design, wonderful in repair, exact in signal. Hunger rises clean and closes at satisfied. Taste brightens on a clean tongue. Energy returns after rest and food. Heaviness, fog, edgy emptiness after sweet are not commands to dose again. They are information. Warning lights on a dashboard, telling you the last fuel was false.

Think of a car flashing oil. Do you pour sugar in the tank to quiet the light? Do you smash the bulb and drive on, proud the light is gone? You read the light, you change the load. So with eating. The 3 p.m. fog, the evening edginess, the mouth never clean, the sleep never deep, tell you the dose shoved you into the ditch. Thank the light. Do not feed the ditch.

This is why fear talk never frees, and why I give you hard truth only to hand you relief.

Yes, frequent free sugars rot teeth through acid after dose, whatever brushing you do. Yes, heavy added load burdens liver and pressure and appetite signals over years. I do not say one bar dooms you, and I do not lay a sentence on you. I say the lift is deferred load, not free energy. The light is real.

I do not want you to stop from fear. Fear never freed anyone. I want you to stop from seeing, because seeing turns fear into pity and pity into freedom. The frightening part has done its work by showing the ditch. Now let joy do the rest. You are not about to endure pain. You are about to stop being pushed.

Expect the old lights to blink for a while at old hours. Wrapper, smell, three o'clock, sofa evening. Smile at them as dying echoes, not orders. Pour tea. Talk. Walk to the window. Get on with enjoying your life. The ripple passes unfed. Each passing thins the next. Rejoice each time, and rejoicing becomes the new memory tied to the cue.

Your body can cope with an accidental crumb, as a seatbelt holds an accidental jolt. It was never built for deliberate swerving. Guard the belief and the body steadies itself. That seed will grow clear later; hold it lightly now.

The Two Fears With One Owner

"But what if I fail? What if I succeed and life is grey?"

Those two fears hold millions in place, pulling opposite ways, tearing the heart. They feel like wisdom. They feel like prudence. They are the tug-of-war of craving and fear, and both ropes are held by the same hands that sold you the want.

Let me show you the ropes, and the empty hands behind them, in two pictures you will keep.

Picture a prison cell with the door standing open.

Evening light falls across the floor, corridor clear, no guard. The prisoner sits on the bunk, knees up, staring at the open door. He has dreamed of leaving for years. Now leaving is a step away, and he begs to stay.

"What if outside is worse? What if I cannot cope? What if I fail and am dragged back? Safer to stay where I know the walls."

Is he free because the door is open? Is he wise because he fears? Or is he held by habit dressed as caution, fear-of-failure locking from inside a lock the trap left open?

You know that prisoner. We have all been him, hovering at the edge of freedom, calling dread prudence, begging the cell to keep us from the risk of leaving. Succumb to fear-of-failure and you guarantee the thing you fear, dosing for the rest of your life to avoid failing at escape.

Now picture a lion born in a travelling cage.

For years bars were his world. Food arrived through bars. Sleep happened against bars. One morning the keepers lift the whole side away. Grass, open air, wide light. The lion sniffs, steps out, stretches, then paces back and circles the line where bars used to be. Evening finds him sleeping by the ghost of the cage, free in body, caged in habit.

Does he pace because he needs bars? Does he miss prison? Or does old learning walk his feet while his eyes learn the plains? His wobble is cage-habit, not proof of need.

You know that lion. We have all paced like him after a change, hovering near old hours, old tills, old cupboards, calling the hover proof we cannot live free. That is fear-of-success, the identity whisper that says life without sweet will be grey, evenings empty, celebrations flat, you no longer you.

Both fears feel opposite. Both are owned by one keeper.

If fear-of-failure were wisdom, why does it advise staying in the cell that makes you flat, fogged, circling, ashamed? Do wise counsellors advise slavery to avoid the risk of freedom?

If fear-of-success were truth, why do free eaters describe mornings brighter, shops easy, meals favourite, laughter louder without dose? Is their joy grey, or is grey only the cell seen from inside?

If either rope were yours, why do both pull you back to the same hand, the next dose, the next low? Do your own reasonings both sell the same packet?

Your heart answers without cruelty. The ropes belong to the trap. Cut belief and both fall at once.

You do not need courage to leave. You need seeing to walk. The door is open. The plains are wide. The pacing fades in days when you pity the cage rather than envy the cell.
