Chapter 1
THE INVITATION

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the full cupboard beside the reading chair, the shelf of tired diet books, the box drawn around one word, the plain reading contract

*You are not about to be lectured, and you are not about to lose anything you need.*

WHY ANOTHER BOOK ABOUT SWEETS?

So here you are, holding another book about sugar.

I know what that feels like. You half hope it will be different. You half scoff at your own hope. You have read the meal plans. You have done the pink sticker charts on the fridge. You have been good all week, then not good at all on Friday night, then full of firm promises on Monday morning.

And somewhere underneath, a small voice says: please don't tell me life is going to be grey without sweet treats.

Let me answer that voice straight away.

This is not another diet lecture. This is not a scolding about calories. This is not a list of forbidden foods pinned to your fridge to make you feel watched in your own kitchen.

I am not here to tell you to eat celery with a smile. I am not here to count your spoonfuls. I am not here to weigh you, measure you, photograph your plate, or make you feel small for loving the very things every advert, every birthday table, every checkout queue told you to love.

We have all lived inside that loop. We have all told ourselves we will be strict from tomorrow. We have all learned the little rules by heart — a sliver is fine, a square is fine, just at weekends is fine — and we have all watched those clever rules collapse by Tuesday evening with the wrapper still in our hand.

If you feel wary, good. Be wary. Be properly sceptical. I was.

The fact is, you are not holding a diet book. You are holding an invitation to see something clearly, once, and then to walk out of something that never did anything for you in the first place.

You do not have to believe me yet. You only have to read on with an open curiosity and let the picture build.

And let me put your mind at rest about one thing at the very start: eat exactly as you do now while you read this book. Keep your cupboard as it is. Keep your routine as it is. There is nothing to change until you see the whole picture. This reading itself is the work.

HOW I KNOW THIS FEELS EASY TO DOUBT

I know this is hard to accept, but I know this trap from the inside.

I was not a nutrition saint who never understood craving. I was the person who said no at lunch in front of other people and then finished the packet alone in the evening. I was the person who bought biscuits for the children and knew, with a hot стыд-free certainty, that half of them would be gone before the children saw the packet. Not from hunger. From that restless hand-moving pull at ten o'clock at night, when your hand seems to know the way to the shelf before you have made any decision at all.

Do you know that moment? The cupboard is already half empty and you are telling yourself you chose it freely?

There is a fraud in that moment. I say that with warmth toward you and with anger toward the fraud. You were not foolish. You were not weak. You were never shown how the trick works.

I tried what you tried. The white-knuckle weeks. The moderation arithmetic. No sweets till Friday. Only dark. Only a bite. Only when guests come. Every version ended the same way: more thought about sweets, not less. More glow around them, not less. More proof, apparently, that I lacked something other people had.

That was my reaction too when I first heard that quitting could be easy: too good to be true.

Then I saw the mechanism. Not in a laboratory. In my own ordinary day. I saw that what I called a treat behaved nothing like a treat. I saw that what I called fuel behaved nothing like fuel. And once I saw it, I could not unsee it.

I did not need to be brave. I did not need to be miserable. I stopped, and the strangeness was how little there was to miss. Of course you want to stop — that is why you picked up this book. I don't blame you for wanting out, and I don't praise the trap that told you escape would hurt.

Since then I have watched thousands of confirmed sweet lovers do the same. People who swore they lived for dessert. People who baked for a living. People who said, word for word: life would be greyer without it. They read, they saw, they took their last ordinary sweet with their eyes open, and they left happy to be free.

I tell you this not to impress you but to settle you. You are not a special hard case. You are not broken. The method that failed you was broken.

Which brings me to the promise I make to every reader at this point, and I make it with total confidence.

Believe me: you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Read that again slowly and let it do its work. You are not being asked to hand over comfort, joy, energy, celebration, or love with food. You are being invited to investigate whether those things ever came from BAD SUGAR at all. If I am wrong, you will have lost an hour of reading. If I am right, you will walk free easily, immediately and permanently.

Yes. Easily, immediately and permanently. I know how outrageous that sounds after years of yo-yo weeks. I state it plainly because it is true once the belief changes. This is not a climb. It is a seeing.

WHAT WE MEAN WHEN WE SAY SUGAR

Before we go further, we must draw a clean line. Otherwise we will talk past each other for a whole book.

When people say sugar they mean everything from an apple to a fizzy drink to a mountain of frosting. That blur keeps you trapped, because it lets the trap whisper: fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters, so you might as well carry on.

So here is the line for this book, boxed and kept:

> BAD SUGAR means refined sugar plus added free sugars plus junk processed carbs and starchy snack-carbs eaten as sweet hits, including sugary drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and equivalent grazing doses.
>
> When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.
>
> Natural sugars inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals are not the target. They belong to real food, hunger met, meals enjoyed.

Read that box twice. It matters.

We are not talking about an orange. We are not talking about carrots in a stew, rice in a plain meal, a peach eaten hungry in the sun. We are talking about the engineered dose: the sweetened drink that hits like syrup, the biscuit that calls for three more biscuits, the cereal that tastes like pudding for breakfast, the evening grazing that never meets hunger because it was never about hunger.

There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR that you need to seek. Zero sought is the stable place inside this line. That is not a threat. It is wonderful news, as you will see, because zero means no arithmetic, no bargaining, no Friday allowance glowing brighter all week.

And to keep you safe while we talk about eating: this book changes a belief about BAD SUGAR. It is not medical care.

> If you live with diabetes or pre-diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician before changing anything and follow their advice first. This book changes a belief about BAD SUGAR; it does not give medical, diabetes, or eating-disorder care. If you ever feel faint, ill, or distressed around food, seek care promptly and put this book aside until you are cleared to continue.

Keep that where you can see it. If it applies to you, honour it first. The belief work will wait for you, and you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by doing the medical part properly.

THE EAT CONTRACT

Now the part that surprises every dieter.

You have been taught that freedom around food comes from control around food. Count, limit, forbid, earn, repay. Good today means a prize tonight. Bad tonight means punishment tomorrow.

That whole school kept you in the loop. It made BAD SUGAR precious by making it forbidden. It taught you to think about sweets all day in order to have none, then to think about them all evening because you had one.

We do the opposite here.

From the first page to the last, the eating rule of this book is one sentence. Learn it now, because you will live inside it:

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Let me say what that does and does not mean.

It means you eat when your body signals hunger, you enjoy real meals, you stop when the satisfaction tells you enough. No clock worship. No points. No good-food pride and bad-food shame. No repayment runs, no skipped breakfasts to pay for last night.

It does not mean a new diet with a cheerful mask. It does not mean swapping one set of chains for a lighter set. It means trust returns to where it belongs: hunger and satisfaction, the signals you were born with and that every small child still obeys without lessons.

Watch a small child with a peach sometime. Really watch. Hungry, she grabs it with both hands, juice down her chin, total attention. Halfway through, full, she drops it mid-bite and runs off to play. No guilt. No bargain that she was good so she earned it. No vow to be strict tomorrow. Body led, joy followed, end of story.

We were all that child once. We did not lose that guidance because we grew up. We lost it because the engineered doses drowned it out.

You will not be asked to dig a hole and fill it with willpower. You will be asked to let hunger and satisfaction speak again while the false voice gets quieter and quieter.

And that is why I can promise ease without blushing. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That sentence is the whole contract. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. Not most of them. Not the ones you like. All of them, in order, with an open mind. They are small. They are clear. They ask nothing painful. They protect the seeing while it happens.

WHY YOU MUST NOT CHANGE ANYTHING YET

This will sound strange, and I want you to take it exactly as I mean it.

While you read, carry on eating and drinking exactly as you do now. Do not start a strict week to prove yourself. Do not throw the cupboard away in a burst of enthusiasm tonight. Do not cut down to show willing.

Why? Because this book changes wanting before it asks for doing.

If you force a hungry, frightened stop now, you will read every page through the fog of feeling deprived. You will turn investigation into endurance. You will make BAD SUGAR look precious at the very moment we need to look at it coolly.

We need you curious, not deprived. Fed, not faint. Honest, not heroic.

So read after a meal if you like. Read with your usual cup on the desk. Notice your habits as a kind observer, not as a judge. When the ten o'clock hand moves, notice it. Smile at the interest of it. Jot nothing, count nothing, confess nothing.

There will be a moment, later, when you take your last ordinary sweet with full attention and step free. That moment is not now. Now is for seeing. You will know when seeing is complete because you will be champing to cross, not dragging yourself toward it.

This patience is part of how you walk free easily, immediately and permanently. The ease is not a trick of words. It comes from doing the mental work first, so the outer act is trivial.

THE TWO READERS IN FRONT OF ME

I am talking to two parts of you at once, and I want to name them kindly.

One part hopes. That part picked up this book, read this far, and feels a lift at the thought that mornings, shops, birthdays and late evenings could be easy around sweets. That part remembers the child with the peach and wants that simplicity back.

The other part scoffs. That part says: I have heard easy before. I know myself. Just one at the cinema is different. Just one when I deserve it is different. You don't know my afternoons.

I know both voices. I have heard them word for word from sweet lovers who now cannot imagine going back. I welcome the scoffing voice most, because that voice guards you against being conned again. Keep it sharp. Turn it on your old belief, not on your chance of freedom.

Ask yourself, gently, as an investigator:

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you energy, why do your most sluggish hours follow your sweetest hours?

If it truly gave you comfort, why do you need comforting again an hour later?

If you truly choose it freely, why do you need a book about how to stop something you enjoy?

You do not have to answer yet. Just let those questions sit beside you while you read. They are not accusations. They are doors.

And notice what you are already doing that proves you are no helpless case. You have persisted for years against your own instincts telling you something is off. You have started again after every collapse. That is not weakness. That is a strong will aimed at the wrong method. Point that same strong will at seeing clearly, and it will carry you out.

I make no apology for directing you. I am an escaped sweet lover talking to a trapped sweet lover, and I know the way out. My job is to keep your eyes on the fraud and off your own worth. Your job is small and joyful.
