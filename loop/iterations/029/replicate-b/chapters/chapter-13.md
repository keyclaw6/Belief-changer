Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the fridge door with a photographed list, the closed book on the table, the bright morning kitchen, the noon shop in ordinary light, the plain favourite meal eaten hungry, the evening sofa with quiet hands

*Sweetness was never in the packet; it was yours all along, and now it is handed back.*

IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

If you opened this book here at the end, I am glad you are here, and I ask you to close this chapter now and begin at the beginning and read straight through.

This page is saved reminders, not the method. The list you can photograph below keeps freedom clear once freedom is won. It does not win freedom on its own.

I say that with love, not with rules. We all wanted the shortcut for years. We turned to the back page hoping the answer lived in a tip. The answer never lived in a tip. It lives in seeing the con whole, in your own time while you read.

You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading properly. That was my promise on the first pages, and it stands on the last. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

So begin at the beginning. Read easily. Keep a clear mind. Carry on exactly as normal while you read until the vow finds you. Let each page do its small job. Come back here free, and this short chapter will hold you free.

If you have read through and you are free, stay with me a few minutes. I have one last thing to show you, and it is wonderful.

NOTHING LEFT TO STUDY

We believed for years that stopping would leave a hole that needed filling with teaching. Do I need a plan to stay free? Do I need ongoing lessons? What if I forget? What if the noise comes back?

Hear how strange that fear sounds against what you already know.

If someone locked you in a small grey room for years and at last the door swung open, would you ask for lessons in how to stay out of the room? Would you carry a manual for not returning to a cell? Would you wake each morning and rehearse reasons not to go back inside?

There is only one honest answer. You would walk out blinking into light and live.

This is that walk. You walked out. There is nothing to study. There is only living.

I promise you from my own escape and from seeing thousands walk the same road: the mind that sees clearly does not need guarding. Clear seeing guards itself.

We spent years in the tug of sweet doses and diets, bright then dull, good then guilty, promise then repair. We counted days. We bargained for Fridays. We hid wrappers. We woke with furry mouth and called it normal. We told ourselves sugary somethings to get me through the day, and then we wondered why the day needed getting through.

Do you remember what that argument felt like underneath every hour? That low hum of when may I, shall I, should I not, just one, after Monday. That hum is gone. Notice its absence. That absence is not a mood. That absence is truth settling.

You do not need to rehearse freedom to keep it. You do not need to repeat arguments to keep them true. True stays true without effort. The sun needs no defence in the morning. Hunger needs no lecture to return at noon. Freedom needs no maintenance once the belief that fed the prison has died.

Ask the quiet mind its plain question.

If freedom needed effort, why does this morning feel easier than any careful diet morning ever felt, lighter, calmer, with no calculation running underneath? Does effort feel like relief?

There is only one honest answer. There is nothing to hold upright, because nothing precious was left behind.

So close the teaching. There is no second course. There is no advanced level. There is no test. This last chapter is a pocket mirror, not a school. Photograph the list, close the book, and go to your meal hungry.

I know the whisper will try once: but I must do something to stay free. You already did it. You saw. Seeing is doing. Live from seeing and seeing stays alive by living.

Think of a child who learns that a shadow on the wall is not a hand. Does she need nightly lessons not to fear the wall? Does she chant reminders? Does she avoid the room? She laughs once, sleeps easy after, and forgets to remember. That forgetting is not carelessness. That forgetting is healing.

You will forget in the same happy way. First days many bells, each answered with joy. Then fewer. Then you forget to remember till a jingle crosses the street and you laugh because joy answers before want forms. Soon you live hours without a single sweet thought, then days. A free mind does not patrol. A free mind lives.

WHAT WAS STOLEN IS HANDED BACK

Sit a moment before you photograph anything. I want you to feel the size of what happened, because small seeing makes small joy and whole seeing makes whole joy.

We came to this book believing BAD SUGAR made life sweeter. A lift. A comfort. A reward. A love-token at birthdays and cinemas and tired evenings. We believed sweetness lived in bright packs and that a life without them would be greyer, duller, less kind.

Ask the plain question you learned to ask.

If sweetness lived in those packs, why did sweet years taste less and less? Why did the first square promise bright and the box end flat, foggy and never satisfied, with circling mind and loud belly? Why did plain hunger meet bread or berries or a peach with louder joy in a quiet mouth than any frosted dose ever gave in a loud mouth? Does sweetness behave like that, charging for its own loan and leaving the tongue coated?

There is only one honest answer. Sweetness was never in the packet.

Sweetness was stolen from you by the Sugar Trap and has now been handed back.

Think how it worked. We were born eaters. Hungry, satisfied, done. A child eats hungry, stops mid-bite, runs off to play. No counting. No guilt. No bargaining. Taste loud because hunger makes it loud. Enough because satisfaction makes it enough.

Then bright doses taught the tongue to shout and the belly to go unheard. Each hit spiked, then dropped, then called for the next hit to lift the drop it made. Each lift made food quieter and made hunger unreadable. Each year made real flavour fainter. We called the faintness normal. We called the shouting a genuine treat or fuel. We paid for our own dulling and thanked the seller.

Do you see? The Trap did not add sweetness to your life. It borrowed sweetness from your tongue, your hunger, your mornings, your meals, and sold it back to you in small bright loans at cruel interest.

Now the loans stopped. Taste returns. Hunger speaks clearly. Satisfaction arrives on time. Mornings come bright without repair. Afternoons pass without hunting. Meals end quiet. Evenings soften without foil and regret.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing back into the eater you were.

I felt it on my first free week like growing taller inside old clothes. Same kitchen. Same shop. Same table. Same friends. Only the shouting gone. Bread tasted of morning again. Fruit snapped again. Water was wonderful again. Tea drunk hot, not gulped between hunts, was marvellous. I laughed at how little I had understood sweetness while I chased it hardest.

You are growing back. That is why freedom feels familiar rather than strange. You are not learning a new life. You are remembering your own.

The thought comes: but I loved sweet things. Was all that love false?

It was not false loving. It was true loving aimed at the wrong object. You loved celebration, warmth, company, relief after effort, kindness handed across a table. The dose only sneaked a ride on that love. Love was the laughter, the light, the hunger met, the moment held. Now love stays and the rider is gone. Nothing loved is lost. Everything loved sounds louder.

We loved cinema lights dimming and laughter rising and a hand finding ours in the dark. We called that chocolate. It was never chocolate. It was togetherness, story, rest after a long week. We loved birthday candles and singing and a child proud. We called that cake. It was never cake. It was belonging. We loved evening quiet after noise. We called that biscuits. It was never biscuits. It was peace asking to be felt without circling.

Ask the birthday its question.

If the dose made the party, why did parties with doses end with sticky hands, dull heads, and children whirling then weeping, while parties lived for play and people end warm and remembered? Does joy leave fog?

There is only one honest answer. Joy was the people. Fog was the dose.

So do not mourn bright packs. They mourn you. They sit shouting on shelves while free eaters walk past with quiet minds and full baskets. Pity them warmly from the outside. Envy never enters again.

THE ORDINARY PROOF

Freedom proves itself where life happens. Not in ideas. In mornings, shops, meals, evenings. You lived that proof yesterday and you will live it again tomorrow. Let me hold it up once so you recognise it forever.

Morning. Light through the blind. Kettle loud. Belly empty in a clean way, asking for breakfast. We woke for years with coated tongue and called it normal. We reached for something quick before we were awake and called it breakfast. We told ourselves the roller coaster was energy. You woke today without the loan. No dull mouth. No foil. No plan for being good. No dread of being bad by night. Drink water. Make tea or coffee as you always do. Eat when hunger calls. Real food. Bread. Eggs. Fruit. Porridge. What you like. Eat it hungry and notice how loud flavour sounds in a quiet mouth. Stop when hunger quiets. Leave food if satisfaction arrived first. Your head stays clear because nothing spiked it and nothing dropped it. That is baseline returning on its own. That is wonderful in its quiet.

Noon. Same shop. Same aisle. Same bright packs. Same bells. We walked that aisle for years like hunters, eyes snagging on every glow, basket filling before decision, feeling deprived if we walked past and guilty if we did not. Walk it now with clear eyes. Look at the family-size bars. Look at the bottles that promise lift. See them for what they are. Bright coats over the same dull ending. Nothing there ends hunger. Nothing there ends satisfaction. And look at the eaters with kindness. The man piling bars for the office. The woman tearing a wrapper before she pays. The tired parent buying peace at the till. Tight jaws. Quick hands. Eyes elsewhere. Already planning the next dose before this one ends. We were those hands. I was that quick hand at the till. You know that hurry. Say inside, with quiet gladness: poor chap, he is still circling. I am glad I am out. That softness, not tightening, is proof belief changed. Trust that proof. Buy real food for hungry meals. Vegetables with colour. Bread that smells of morning. Cheese. Fish. Fruit that snaps and drips. Pay. Walk out. Notice your step light because no argument walked with you. If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar, are pregnant, or live with any eating disorder past or present, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

Meal. Sit down hungry. Not starved. Not stuffed. Hungry, with the belly asking clearly. Eat your plain favourite slowly. Soup steaming. Potatoes crisp. Greens bright. Rice. Bread torn. Fruit after if hunger still asks. Notice chew. Notice warmth spreading. Notice the moment hunger softens and pleasure peaks, then the quiet moment when enough arrives. Stop there. Not from rules. From satisfaction. I eat now with more pleasure than in all my sweet years, and I eat with less fussing, not more. I never bargain. I never promise to be good this week. I eat what I want, when I am hungry, and leave what I do not want. My afternoons steadied. My mouth woke clean. None of that was a plan. All of that was the body leading again. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again. That is not a diet. That is eating returned to its natural job.

Evening. Same chair. Same soft lamp. Same street noise outside. We lived for this hour and feared it most. One biscuit meant as choice, hand moving before choice, wrapper rustle, box empty, regret before swallow ends. We called it relaxing and felt flat, foggy and never satisfied. Live this evening without the box. Eat dinner satisfied. Drink water or tea. Sit down. Feel hands quiet in your lap. No rustle. No crumb hunt. No second trip to the cupboard. Old habit may stir once. Three days, perhaps five, a soft tug at eight o'clock. Welcome the tug. It is good news wearing old clothes. A tiny echo starving because belief no longer feeds it. Unfed, it fades within days. Why feed a dying grumble to rebuild a prison? Tonight tiredness meets rest truly. Breath slowing. Eyes heavy at a sane hour. Sleep deep without sugar mouth. Morning bright without repair.

Ask the ordinary day its question.

If BAD SUGAR gave anything, why does the day without it hold more flavour, more clarity, more calm, more laughter early, more conversation long, more love close with no shame between you and the room? Does loss behave like gain?

There is only one honest answer. Nothing was taken. Everything was returned.

THE TWO RESTS OF YOUR LIFE

You stand between two lives, and both run for the rest of your life. Choose with open eyes, then never look back.

If you kept feeding bright doses, what would they buy you for the rest of your life? More lift then drop. More hunt then fog. More baskets filled before decision. More evenings wired then weary. More money and hours and toothache for something you do not even need. More of the roller coaster, short term lift then fast drop, I feel like i cant stop circling the cupboard at ten. Does that sound like sweetness or like rent paid to a landlord who never repairs?

There is only one honest answer. It is rent on a cell. It does plenty to the eater and nothing for the eater.

We paid that rent gladly because we were conned, not because we were weak. We were told by bright advertising and by everyone eats it that shouting was food and that hunger could not be trusted. We believed the Sweet Con script as if it were our own reasoning. We fed a tiny grumble and called feeding comfort. We blamed ourselves when moderation collapsed and called collapse character. No wonder we failed on the Willpower Method. The method kept the belief alive while demanding we fight the want the belief created. Every failure was the method, never you.

If you live free, what will you hold for the rest of your life? Clear mornings. Quiet shops. Hungry meals that satisfy for hours. Evenings wide without the box. A mind that forgets food between meals because the body no longer begs. A tongue that tastes louder each month as dulling fades. Bright mouth. Steady head. Quiet belly. Easy sleep. Money kept. Hours returned. Self-respect that needs no pride-talk. The marvellous relief of no argument underneath the day.

There is only one honest answer. It is yours again.

Ask the future its plain question.

Which rest do you want? The rest that pays for dull, or the rest that tastes sweet because the tongue is quiet enough to taste?

You already chose. You chose when you took your last ordinary treat with attention on its cloying ugliness and vowed freedom and rejoiced at once. You chose when mornings came bright without a fight. Do not vote twice. Never reopen the decision. A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order. Joy cuts both ropes that debate kept tight.

I want you to feel how proud you may feel walking past bright shouting with soft eyes. I want you to feel how exciting ordinary meals turn when hunger leads. I want you to feel how wonderful to want what feeds you and to leave what dulls you without effort. You are not merely free. You are happy to be free. That happiness is your guard. No vigilance needed where joy lives.

GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

You are free. Now live as free people live.

Get on with enjoying your life from the first hour tomorrow. Not from next Monday. Not after proof. Now. Freedom needs no waiting room.

Eat when hunger calls. Stop when satisfaction arrives. Enjoy real food as favourite. Drink water gladly. Move gladly. Work clear. Laugh early. Sleep deep. Change nothing else in life to stay free. Same alarm. Same cup. Same street. Same jokes. Same walks. Old life minus con is new enough to feel exciting for years.

I changed nothing else and everything changed. I still drink tea as I always did. I still shop at noon. I still sit on the same sofa. I still go to birthdays and cinemas and suppers. Only the circling stopped. That sameness is the miracle. Freedom proves itself by fitting inside your life without costume.

And when a sweet thought flickers, as it will, let it make you glad.

You cannot ban thoughts and you do not need to. A passing thought is weather, not orders. Let it pass through a clear mind and it dies again. Hold it with debate and you polish it precious. So do not debate. Rejoice.

Think at once, loudly inside, with a grin:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Let each old cue become a bell for relief. Wrapper memory means freedom remembered. Eight o'clock tug means echo fading. Party plate means you take what truly feeds hunger with glad hand. Three o'clock hour means you drink water, eat if hungry, work on. Each thought strengthens quiet rather than threatening it.

Do not preach freedom. Live it visibly and let ease recruit. Friends will notice you calm in aisles, easy at tables, light in evenings. If they ask honestly, share the seeing kindly. If they tease or offer, smile, take real food with pleasure, say you feel marvellous without sweet doses and leave it there. Preaching breeds argument, and argument drags you back into debate. Ease persuades where sermons fail.

If a hidden spoon finds you — sauce too sweet, label misread at speed — smile and drive on steering true. Notice dull if dull comes. Set it down. Eat your next hungry meal hungry. Accident teaches and is forgotten. Only a chosen special would repaint precious, so keep none special and keep mind clear. Belief guards behaviour on its own.

Hear the old dialect try once: celebrating with chocolate, treat when you feel you deserve one, I have been good this week so one will not hurt, I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. Hear it for what it is. A dead script. Deserve has nothing to do with dull. Good has nothing to do with fog.

Answer with joy, not debate. I deserve a quiet mouth and a satisfied belly. I have them. BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Ask the street its last question.

If trapped eaters hold a pleasure you lack, why do their hands hurry, their eyes look elsewhere, their joy need repeat before joy ends? Does possession behave like hunger?

There is only one honest answer. You lack nothing. They lack quiet. Pity them warmly. Walk on light.

The book ends. Life continues. Close these pages, leave them closed, and go to your meal hungry. Go to the morning bright. Go to the shop calm. Go to the table glad. Go to the evening soft. Go to birthdays free, tasting company louder. Go to cinemas free, tasting story deeper. Go to work clear, tasting hours longer.

You have not lost sweetness. Sweetness lost its thief. You have not left life. Life left its cell. You are growing, not quitting. Grow tall. Grow loud in taste. Grow quiet in mind. Grow kind to trapped hurry because you were hurry once.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You followed them. Now follow life.
