Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER
Morning kitchen table and lunchbox, office drawer at three, till receipt and coins, bathroom mirror and toothbrush, evening sofa and empty packet, a quiet ledger with one column blank.

*Once you stop weighing harm against pleasure and ask plainly what pleasure it ever gave, the answer is none.*

THE WRONG FIGHT

You come to me with an open bargain.

You say, I know BAD SUGAR rots teeth. I know it piles on weight. I know the crash comes every afternoon. I know the money leaks away. But surely the lift, the comfort, the little glow in a grey day, surely that counts for something. Surely life without it would be duller, flatter, harder to bear.

I hear you with warmth. We have all said that. I said it for years.

The fact is we are fighting on the wrong ground.

Harm against benefit is a fight you can never win clearly. Harm can always be argued away for one more day. You feel fine this morning. Your teeth look fine in the mirror. Your friend eats more than you and looks trim. The packet says thirty grams, the advert shows laughing people, the voice in your head says, a little of what you fancy, I have managed to be good this week. Harm feels distant. Harm feels like tomorrow. Benefit feels like now.

So I am not going to stand here and pile harm on one side of the scales and beg you to find it heavier. That is the diet lecture. That is the dentist poster on the wall you stop seeing by Friday. That is the fright film that makes you nod on Sunday night and reach again on Monday at three.

Have you noticed how clever the harm debate makes the trapped mind? A sore tooth is blamed on brushing. A heavy month is blamed on age. A foggy afternoon is blamed on bad sleep. A light wallet is blamed on prices in general, never on the daily river of bars and bottles and packets. We become lawyers for our own dose, explaining away every exhibit. And while we argue harm, the benefit is never examined at all. It sits in the corner, unquestioned, glowing softly, pretending to be the reason we put up with the harm.

I ask a cleaner question. Forget for an hour whether it harms a little or a lot. Forget scales. Tell me what it does FOR you.

Not what it does TO your teeth and mood and wallet and time. We know it does plenty there. What does it do FOR you that is real, that is yours, that you could not have without it.

That is the whole of this chapter. One question, answered honestly.

We were conned into believing BAD SUGAR is a genuine treat or fuel. Those five words hold the entire glow. A treat says, I deserve delight. Fuel says, I need energy. Put them together and you have a food that both delights and drives you. No wonder the hand reaches. Who would turn down delight that also drives?

But a name is not a proof. Call a stone bread and it will still break your tooth. We have to open the packet of that pretty phrase and see what is inside.

Keep your mind open and your stomach calm as we look. A calm witness sees. You have absolutely nothing to lose by asking, and everything to gain by hearing your own true answer.

AUDIT OF AN ORDINARY DAY

Bring me one ordinary day. Not Christmas. Not a binge you are ashamed of. A neat, moderate, respectable day, the kind you would defend.

Seven-thirty. Tea with two sugars and a biscuit while the kettle boils. You are half awake. Do you taste delight? Truly? Or do you taste habit, warm liquid and hurry, the biscuit gone in three bites while you check messages? The lunchbox is packed in the same hurry, a sweet bar slipped in for later because the morning already whispers about the afternoon. Notice the time taken, the crumbs wiped, the packet opened before thought.

Ten-thirty. A sweet drink from the machine, a bar from the drawer. The morning has turned edgy. You feel busy and a little hollow. The first sip is loud, the rest is typing. The drawer sticks a little because packets have spilled sugar in the runners. You chew while you answer mail. Ten minutes later the edge returns with friends. The hollowness is wider now, not narrower. You tell yourself you needed sugary somethings to get me through.

One o clock. Proper lunch, then something sweet to finish. You tell yourself you have been good this week. The pudding is pleasant for four mouthfuls, then it is finishing, then it is a film over the tongue and a wish for coffee. The canteen till beeps. Coins and card taps add up without ceremony. No one course felt like a feast, yet the tray carries two sweet items and the receipt carries their price.

Three o clock. The drawer again. Concentration dips, eyelids grow heavy, the room feels stuffy. The chocolate brings a brief flare, a little louder talk, a little faster typing. By four the flare has gone and a fog sits behind the eyes. You reach again without deciding. The wrapper joins the morning wrapper in the bin. The bin tells the truth the mind edits out.

Evening. The film packet. The permitted share that does not stay a share. Eaten fast, half tasted, followed by thirst and a furry mouth and a vow that tomorrow will be tidy. The sofa cushions hold crumbs. The packet lies light and empty on the armrest. The bathroom mirror later shows a tired face brushing hard to clear the sweet film, toothbrush working, mouth still coated.

Now lay that day on the table and rule two columns.

Column one: what did it do TO you. Money out, time out, teeth bathed in sugar again and again, appetite blunted then sharpened, mood lifted for minutes then dropped for hours, evening stained with hurry and regret. Count the small purchases and they make a rent. Count the minutes thinking, buying, unwrapping, regretting, promising, and they make hours every week. Count the moods and they make a roller coaster you never bought a ticket for, yet you ride daily. Brushing and fluoride matter, of course, teeth are not a one-cause tale, and I give no tooth lecture here, but frequent sweet doses feed the acid that hollows them, that is plain body fact. Across whole populations the intake now sits far above the guidance line set to protect hearts and teeth, I speak of the picture across millions here, not a sentence on you, yet feeling fine this morning does not mean the intake was low.

Column two: what did it do FOR you. Name it. Not TO. FOR.

Was there one moment of true, clean delight that belonged to the sugar itself? Was there one hour of clear, steady energy that came from the dose and stayed? Was there one minute of peace that did not need another dose hard on its heels?

Ask with your day, not with theory.

Did the seven-thirty biscuit make the morning bright, or did the morning happen and the biscuit ride along? Did the ten-thirty bar fix the edginess, or did it pause the edginess for minutes and charge you a deeper edginess after? Did the lunch pudding add joy to the meal, or did the meal satisfy and the pudding blur it? Did the three o clock chocolate clear your head, or did it brighten the eyes for ten minutes and fog them for two hours? Did the evening packet make the film funny, or was the film funny and the packet only rustled through it?

Only honest answers survive.

You will find column one crowded. Column two stays empty. Not thin. Empty.

That emptiness is not your failure to enjoy properly. It is the nature of the thing. We kept a treat that never treated, a fuel that never fuelled, and we paid TO all day for FOR that never arrived.

IF IT GIVES, WHY MUST IT KEEP PROVING IT?

“I know it harms,” says the voice in you, “but it helps me. It gets me through. It is my little pleasure. Take everything else, leave me that.”

I love that voice because it is honest. It admits the TO and clings to the FOR. Let us hold it kindly to the light.

If BAD SUGAR truly gives delight, why does delight need such defence? A ripe peach needs no defence. A laugh with a friend needs no defence. A walk in cool air needs no defence. You never hear yourself say, I know walking harms but it is my little pleasure. True pleasures stand quiet.

Why does this one pleasure leave a furry mouth, a fogged head, a lighter wallet, and a vow? What kind of treat punishes the tongue that praised it? What kind of little pleasure must be eaten in secrecy, bargained for in advance, and apologised for after? You do not bargain with a peach. You do not hide apple cores. You do not promise the mirror that tomorrow you will be good about fresh air.

And if it truly gives energy, why must the gift be topped up every two hours, louder each time, with less gift and more need? What kind of fuel empties the tank as you pour? Real help does not behave like that. Real rest leaves you rested. Real food eaten hungry leaves you quiet for hours. This help leaves you hungrier, emptier, more twitchy than before.

Look at your own audit again and answer with your evening, not with theory.

When was the last time one small dose left you bright for hours, calm in body, clear in mind, with the cupboard forgotten?

When was the last time one small dose did not call for a second dose before the sweetness had left the tongue?

When was the last time the pleasure grew with the amount, rather than the amount growing while the pleasure shrank?

Your memory already knows. The first bite flares. The second bites less. The third is barely tasted. By the fourth you are finishing, not feasting. Pleasure falls while urge climbs. That crossing is not enjoyment. That is a loop proving itself empty in your own mouth.

It is the other way around. You do not eat because it gives. You feel lack because you eat, and you read the brief pause of lack as a gift.

What if a friendly hand lifted you out of a ditch each morning, and you learned one day that the same hand had pushed you in during the night so you would be glad to be lifted? Would you call that hand kind? Would you pay it for rescue? Or would you see the rescue was part of the push?

Hold that question lightly. We will look full at the pusher later. For now let the question sit beside your empty column and do its quiet work. Does a rescuer who creates the danger deserve thanks? Does a pause that guarantees a deeper drop deserve the name of help?

A GENUINE TREAT OR FUEL

We have to take that pretty phrase apart, because it rules your hand as long as it stays whole.

A genuine treat. What makes a treat genuine? It delights once and leaves gladness behind. It does not bargain, hide, hurry, or demand repeat. The child with the peach showed us: hunger, delight, enough, play. Done. No rustle after. No shame after. The joy stays after the eating stops.

Does BAD SUGAR do that? Watch. The wrapper opens with promise. The first mouthful is loudest. Then promise thins. Attention moves from taste to getting, from getting to finishing, from finishing to regret. Gladness does not stay. Want stays. A treat that leaves want in place of gladness is not a treat. It wears the coat of a treat while doing the work of a trap.

Think of the difference in your body. A genuine treat is tasted to the end. The last spoonful is as good as the first. You put the spoon down with a sigh of enough. With a trapped dose the last mouthfuls are the worst. They are eaten fastest and tasted least. The hand hurries as if to finish before the mind catches up. No one hurries a genuine delight. Hurry is the footprint of a con.

A genuine fuel. What makes fuel real? It burns steady and carries you far. Bread and soup and fruit eaten hungry carry you for hours, quiet in the body, clear in the head. You forget eating and get on with living.

Does a sweet hit do that? Watch the hour after. Brief buzz, bright talk, fast heart, then dip, fog, yawning, pallor, the drawer opening again. That is not fuelling. That is lighting a paper fire to warm a cold room. Flare, ash, colder room. Real fuel does not need refuelling before the hour is out. Real fuel does not leave you flatter than before you took it.

We believed it because we were told it, brightly and often, and because a tiny body echo seemed to confirm it. There is a faint sinking between doses, a restlessness, a hollow call for something sweet right now, and the dose pauses it for minutes. Pause feels like proof. Ah, you see, it helped. But pausing a discomfort the last dose created is not giving. It is lending back a little of what it took, then taking more.

Name both halves plainly so the con loses its blur. There is the faint echo in the body, small and brief. And there is the big belief that reads the echo as need, as reward, as help, as love. The first is sensation. The second is interpretation. Neither is you. Both arrived after the loop closed. You know the I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, and you know the short term lift then fast drop that follows. Both are entries in the same ledger.

Leave the full split for later seeing. You do not need the whole map tonight. You need only to see that a genuine treat or fuel would not behave this way in your mouth, in your hour, in your evening. Yours behaves exactly like a loan shark: quick cash, warm smile, cold terms, deeper debt.

Once seen, delight without delight is no delight. Fuel that empties is no fuel.

FOR THE REST OF YOUR LIFE

Now stretch your ordinary day into a life.

Take the till receipts of a year and lay them end to end. Take the hours spent thinking about sweets, buying sweets, hiding sweets, regretting sweets, planning to be good, failing to be good, starting Monday again. Take the dentist visits, the afternoon fogs, the evening hurry, the morning vows. Take the tug between want and worry that hums under every celebration. Take the lunchboxes packed, the drawers refilled, the packets hidden from guests and found light.

Do you want to pay that price for the rest of your life for an empty column?

That is the threat inside the phrase. Hooked for the rest of your life to a treat that never treats, a fuel that never fuels. Every single day for something you do not even need. That is not enjoyment. That is rent paid to a landlord who owns nothing.

Picture it year by year, not as drama but as arithmetic. The same drawer. The same three o clock stare. The same furry mouth at night. The same promise on Sunday. The same bright packets changing their colours while the loop stays identical inside. The body a little more tired, the mind a little more occupied, the joy a little thinner, for nothing returned.

Turn the phrase over and feel its other face. What would it be to never pay it again for the rest of your life. To walk past bright packets with clear eyes. To finish a meal and forget food. To watch an evening film and taste the story, not the rustle. To wake with a clean mouth and a steady morning that asks nothing sweet of you. To keep your coins, your hours, your attention for living, not for feeding a loop.

Both futures use the same words. Only one is freedom. The words themselves do not decide. Your seeing decides.

I do not frighten you to make you act. Fear never freed anyone. I lay the hard facts flat because they are true, then I set fear aside. I do not want you to move from dread. I want you to move from clarity into relief. There is nothing to lose by seeing, and a whole quiet life to win.

Count the cost and you will feel the weight lift, not fall. Continuing is the burden. Seeing is the opening.

IT DOES PLENTY TO YOU

Gather the threads. No new argument. Only the verdict your own day wrote.

Morning, noon, night, the dose arrived promising delight and drive. Delight shrank while dose grew. Drive flared while steadiness sank. Teeth, mood, money, time, attention, peace, all paid TO. Nothing real came back FOR. The bright moments of the day, the laugh, the break, the company, the rest, the hunger met by real food, gave what was good. The sugar rode along and claimed the credit.

It claims the film. It was the film.

It claims the break. It was the rest.

It claims the energy. It was your body returning toward normal for minutes before sinking again.

Strip the rider and the horse still runs. Strip the scene and nothing of the sweetness remains worth keeping.

The fact is settled in your own mouth, your own hour, your own bin full of wrappers. A treat that leaves want is no treat. A fuel that leaves flatness is no fuel.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Let that sentence stand clean. Do not argue with it. Do not cushion it. Check it against tomorrow and the next day and watch it hold. The empty column stays empty because there was never anything in it but belief and echo.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

SUMMARY
- Harm debate keeps the loop alive because harm feels distant and benefit feels like now.
- An ordinary neat day shows plenty paid out in mood, teeth, money and time.
- The benefit column stays empty when tested hour by hour in real eating.
- The pretty name falls apart because delight shrinks while want grows.
- A tiny body echo gets misread as proof of help when it is only brief pause.
- Stretched across a life, the price is daily rent for nothing received.
- Seeing the empty benefit clearly makes escape feel like gain.
