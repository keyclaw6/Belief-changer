Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

*You have lost nothing at all — sweetness is yours again, so close the book and live free.*

IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

If you opened the book here, stop here. This page cannot set you free by itself.

What you hold now is a handful of saved reminders, not the method. The method was the slow unwinding that came before — the seeing through of the Sugar Trap, the unmasking of the Sweet Con, the plain audit of what BAD SUGAR ever did for you, the last ordinary treat taken with wide eyes, the mornings and aisles and evenings lived bright. A list cannot do that work for you. A list can only remind a free eater what sight already taught.

So do the kind thing. Close these last pages for an hour. Start at the first page with an open mind and read through as normal. Carry on exactly as normal while you read. Let each seeing land in order. Come back here when you are already free and want something small to keep in your pocket.

I tell you this as one who tried to cheat with endings myself. I loved endings. I flipped to the back for the answer, hoping to carry the answer away without the walk. The fact is freedom never lived in the back. It lived in the walk that made the back true.

You would not frame the last photograph of a holiday and claim you had swum the sea. You swim. Then the photograph means something. Read. Then this list means something.

If you skipped ahead because doubt whispered, hear that doubt kindly and answer it with action, not argument. Doubt says, just give me the lines to hold. Sight says, let me show you there is nothing to hold. Lines without sight are paper. Sight without lines is freedom. Go and get the sight. It is waiting on the first page with warmth and certainty, and you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by going to meet it.

Would a photograph teach a child to taste a peach? Would a recipe teach hunger what satisfaction feels like? Would a summary teach your tongue what a clean morning tastes like? Only honest answers stand here. You learn by seeing, eating, living. Then you keep a note to smile at.

So if you are new to this page, leave it. Read the book. Take your vow when you are champing to take it. Live your ordinary days. Then return here light-handed for your keepsake.

If you have read through and taken your vow, stay with me a little longer. There is one last seeing to place in your hands, and then I will get out of your way for good.

THERE IS NOTHING MORE TO STUDY

“I feel free now,” a voice says in your head, “but how do I stay free? What do I study? What do I watch? What do I practise?”

Hear how old that worry sounds. We lived years as pupils of our own appetite. Rules on the fridge. Clocks for meals. Good foods and bad foods. Small penance salad followed by large secret dose eaten standing with the cupboard door open. We studied ourselves and still woke prowling. Study never freed us because study was never what trapped us. A false belief trapped us. Belief seen through does not need studying.

Let me ask you plainly, and answer honestly.

Did you ever need a lesson to enjoy bread when you were truly hungry? Did a small child need a course to push a peach away mid-bite and run off bright? Do you need a manual to feel relief when a dead echo passes and you smile?

Only honest answers survive here. Hunger needs no teaching. Satisfaction needs no guarding. Relief needs no practice.

We believed freedom would be vigilance. The fact is freedom is sight. You see bright packs for what they are. You see a passing thought for what it is. You see hunger met plainly leave you steady. Sight does not fade because you stop polishing it. Sight stays because it is true.

If you live with diabetes or take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with any medical risk, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief change only.

I am stern with you here because I love you out of the last rope. Do not turn this book into another diet to carry. Do not count days. Do not weigh mornings. Do not make a project of being free. A free eater does not practise freedom. A free eater eats and lives and laughs and forgets the whole affair for hours at a time.

You are not a pupil any more. You are an eater.

Ask the pupil-fear its plain questions and watch it fall.

If study kept eaters free, would the most studious dieters not be the freest people you know? If watching yourself kept your hand light, why did the years of watching end with the box empty before regret could finish its sentence? If freedom needed labour, why does this morning feel light without labour?

You know the honest answers. Labour was the Willpower Method talking. Watching was slavery talking. Freedom talks in relief.

We were conned into believing eating needed management because BAD SUGAR made eating unmanageable. Dosed daily, hunger spoke in sweet words. Satisfaction never arrived. Craving called the hand before thought caught up. Of course we reached for rules. Rules were a sane response to an insane loop. Now the loop is seen and gone. Hunger speaks plainly again. Satisfaction arrives and holds. Rules have nothing left to manage.

Do you hire a guard for an empty prison? Do you polish a window that is already clear? Do you rehearse walking after you have remembered how to walk?

Only honest answers stand here. The guard is dismissed. The window is clear. The legs remember.

So let the book get thin in your memory. Let whole days pass without one food thought. That forgetting is not danger. That forgetting is proof. A trapped eater thinks of doses hourly. A free eater thinks of dinner when hungry and thinks of living the rest of the time. Get on with enjoying your life and let freedom prove itself by how rarely you need to mention it.

THE PHOTOGRAPH ON THE FRIDGE

Keep something small. Not a course. Not a system. A photograph.

Copy these few lines in your own hand or take a picture of them on your phone and pin it where you will see it on a tired evening. When doubt whispers that there is a cost, read them and smile at how little there is to remember.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That was always the whole contract. Not effort. Not hunger. Not white heat. Follow the sight. Guard the sight. Let the body do the rest.

I love how small that contract looks now. We expected a mountain. We were handed a clear pane of glass. Follow what you have seen. When a bright pack glows, see cardboard and clamour. When a sweet thought crosses, see a dead echo and rejoice. When hunger calls, eat till comfort and stop. That is the whole art. Small enough to photograph. True enough to hold for life.

When we dosed daily, life was arithmetic around BAD SUGAR. How much. How often. How hidden. How excused. One small bag promised, three bought. One bite permitted, box emptied before regret could finish its sentence. That arithmetic always lost because craving was belief calling the hand before thought caught up.

That arithmetic is gone. Hunger is your clock now. Satisfaction is your bell. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that line slowly. It is not a rule. It is a description of what the body does when no coating shouts over it. The child with the peach lived it without words. You lived it this morning without effort. You will live it tonight without debate.

Eat what you want means eat real food you truly fancy, warm, savoury, fresh, filling, bright with fruit, till comfort arrives. When you are hungry means when the belly asks cleanly, not when the clock orders or the gloom suggests a payment in coating. Stop when you are satisfied means stop at the quiet that follows comfort, the easy fullness that asks for nothing more. And never punish yourself again means no penance salad, no skipped breakfast to pay for evening, no shame-talk after a crumb, no lecture in the mirror. Kindness, not arithmetic.

“But what if I forget a line?” That is the old fear borrowing wisdom’s voice. Answer it with your own day as witness.

Did forgetting ever place a dose in your mouth? Did remembering misery ever keep one out? What kept your hand light in the bright aisle — memory of a sentence, or clear seeing that bright cardboard held fog and prowl? If seeing kept you light once, will seeing need notes to keep you light twice?

Only honest answers survive here. Seeing keeps you free.

So keep the photograph for comfort, not for labour. Glance when you like. Forget for days. The list does not hold you up. You hold the list lightly because you no longer lean on it.

Look at the fridge door tomorrow evening when you are tired and the house is loud and the old hour calls. There is your own handwriting. There is your own clear sight in small letters. You smile because you remember the walk that made those letters true. You feel pity, warm and brief, for shoppers still doing arithmetic in bright aisles, and marvellous relief that your evening holds dinner and rest and laughter with no second reach. No lecture. No debate. One clear look and you are past into living.

Would a free bird carry its cage in photographs to stay free? Would a clear morning need a note to stay bright? Would relief need rehearsal to stay relief?

Only honest answers stand here. The cage is gone. The morning is bright. Relief rings by itself.

SWEETNESS HANDED BACK

Now the last seeing, saved for this hour. It changes nothing and it changes everything.

You think you have left something sweet behind. You have not.

Think of the mornings dosed and sour, the afternoons buzzed then fogged, the evenings sealed with a fix that left thirst and second reach. Was that sweetness? Did the tongue awake taste more, or less? Did food glow brighter, or duller? Did love at the table grow fuller, or thinner? If those hits were sweetness, why did the sweetest years taste coated and leave you flat, foggy and never satisfied, prowling for more?

You know the honest answers. The coating dulled the tongue it claimed to delight. The fix borrowed joy from rest and company and hunger met, while digging the low that made joy feel thin.

Here is the truth to carry out of these pages. You have lost nothing sweet. Sweetness was stolen from you by the trap and has now been handed back.

Look at a peach now with a clean tongue. It cracks. It juices. It satisfies without starting a clamour. Look at bread with bite, soup with steam, berries bright after a savoury meal. Look at tea tasting like itself. This is sweetness as the body meant it — part of hunger met, part of satisfaction holding, part of flavour awake. The trap shrank all that vast brightness down to one narrow hit that never satisfied, then sold the narrowing back to you as treat.

We all began as that eater. Hungry. Present. Done when done. No counting. No bargaining. No payment in coating for being good this week. Then came bliss-point packs and cartoon grins and checkout ambush, and the slow teaching that joy lived in a wrapper. We were conned, not foolish. We mistook relief from a self-dug low for a gift. Now the con is seen and the eater stands clear again, older, kinder, wider awake.

This was never about quitting. It was about growing back into the eater you were before the narrowing began.

Feel the difference in your mouth as lived proof. A dosed tongue tastes through gauze. Sugar on sugar on sugar, all highs shouting, no nuance left. A free tongue tastes awake. Salt sings. Sour brightens. Warmth spreads. Crisp cracks. Ripe perfumes. Fruit after dinner is not a prelude to a binge. It is a bright close that finishes and leaves quiet. Bread at lunch is not fuel for the wait till the real event. It is the event, chewed slow, stopped at comfort, remembered with pleasure.

Did a real dinner ever leave you prowling the way a sweet hit leaves you prowling? Did soup and bread ever call for more soup and bread an hour later the way one biscuit calls for the box? Did hunger met plainly ever leave you foggy, thirsty and faintly ashamed the way a fix leaves you?

Only honest answers survive here. Real food finishes. A dose starts what it cannot finish.

Think of the celebrations you feared would turn grey. Lights low. Laughter loud. Faces you love around a table. We believed the glow came from the shared chocolate at the funny scene. The fact is the glow came from rest, laughter, company, ease of the day laid down. The dose only borrowed that glow. Now the table is present and remembered. Talk is followed. Sleep comes clean. Morning wakes bright. What was grey? What is grey now?

You know the honest answers. Grey was the fog after the fix. Bright is the evening without it.

Ask which life you want to keep.

Do you want to dose and crash and crave for the rest of your life, paying every single day for something you do not even need, watching evenings end prowling and mornings begin sour?

Or do you want to eat and live clear for the rest of your life, waking bright, working steady through hours that used to dip and dive, sitting at tables with laughter remembered and sleep clean?

Only honest answers stand here. One rest-of-life is slavery. The other is yours by sight.

You are not losing. You are regaining. You are not shrinking your world to avoid a pleasure. You are growing back into a wide world after a narrow hit had shrunk it. The eagle raised as a chicken does not mourn the coop. It stretches and finds the sky was always there.

GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

So go. Get on with enjoying your life.

Eat breakfast hungry. Eat lunch hungry. Eat dinner hungry. Stop at comfort. Notice salt, sour, warm, crisp, ripe returning like old friends. Walk the bright aisle slow with clear eyes. Pity trapped hands warmly, briefly, and walk on light. Sit on the evening sofa with tea and let the day settle. Laugh at the funny scene. Remember the talk. Sleep unloaded. Wake bright.

Change nothing else in your life. Same streets. Same shops. Same friends. Same work. Same birthdays — only present now, remembered now, slept off cleanly now. No new system. No void to fill. No calendar to grip with white knuckles. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own. Hunger comes clean. Satisfaction holds.

A thought will cross at times. A picture of gloss. A smell of bake. A memory of ease. Let it come. It has no hands. Only belief could give it hands, and belief is gone. Answer it at once with joy. Think how marvellous it feels to walk past bright packs wanting nothing. Think how wonderful evenings feel without prowling. Think how exciting mornings feel without sourness. Let pity rise for users still shuffling with bags of fixes, and relief rise for you.

I walked through those first ordinary days myself with wonder at how little there was to do. Breakfast. Work. Shop. Dinner. Sofa. Sleep. All the old rooms, all the old hours, lived bright. The sweet thought crossed thinner each month, and each time it brought the same bell of relief. No vigilance. No labour. No missing.

Do not mourn. Rejoice. Do not preach. Shine. Do not wait to be free. You already are.

Close the book. Leave it shut. Go to your kitchen hungry and eat with pleasure. Go to your people and be fully there. Go to your bed clean and sleep deep. Tomorrow will ask nothing of you but living.

You stand now in the clear place every free eater knows — mornings that satisfy with plain food, aisles that hold no orders, evenings that stay full on rest and company, thoughts that answer themselves with joy, crumbs that teach by their cloy and pass without power, quiet ease that recruits louder than any preaching.

Guard one thing and one thing only: the sight that there is nothing here you want. The body follows the sight. Keep the sight clear and no street, no shelf, no celebration can touch you.

Life ahead is not a watch you keep. Life ahead is a meal you eat hungry, a table you sit at present, a road you walk light. Take it with both hands and rejoice you are free to taste it all.
