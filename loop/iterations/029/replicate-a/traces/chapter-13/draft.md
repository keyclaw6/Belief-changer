*You have lost nothing at all — sweetness is yours again, so close the book and live free.*

IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

If you opened the book here, stop here. This page cannot set you free by itself.

What you hold in your hands now is a handful of saved reminders, not the method. The method was the slow unwinding that came before — the seeing, the unmasking, the last ordinary treat taken with wide eyes, the mornings and aisles and evenings lived bright. A list cannot do that work for you. A list can only remind a free eater what sight already taught.

So do the kind thing. Close these last pages for an hour. Start at the first page with an open mind and read through as normal. Carry on exactly as normal while you read. Let each seeing land in order. Come back here when you are already free and want something small to keep in your pocket.

I tell you this as one who tried to cheat with endings myself. I loved endings. I flipped to the back for the answer, hoping to carry the answer away without the walk. The fact is freedom never lived in the back. It lived in the walk that made the back true.

You would not frame the last photograph of a holiday and claim you had swum the sea. You swim. Then the photograph means something. Read. Then this list means something.

If you have read through and taken your vow, stay with me a little longer. There is one last seeing to place in your hands, and then I will get out of your way.

THERE IS NOTHING MORE TO STUDY

“I know I feel free now,” a voice says in your head, “but how do I stay free? What do I study? What do I watch? What do I practice?”

Hear how old that worry sounds. We lived years as pupils of our own appetite. Rules on the fridge. Clocks for meals. Good foods and bad foods. Small penance salad followed by large secret dose. We studied ourselves and still woke prowling. Study never freed us because study was never what trapped us. A false belief trapped us. Belief seen through does not need studying.

Let me ask you plainly, and answer honestly.

Did you ever need a lesson to enjoy bread when you were truly hungry? Did a child need a course to push a peach away mid-bite and run off bright? Do you need a manual to feel relief when a dead echo passes and you smile?

Only honest answers stand here. Hunger needs no teaching. Satisfaction needs no guarding. Relief needs no practice.

We believed freedom would be vigilance. The fact is freedom is sight. You see bright packs for what they are. You see a passing thought for what it is. You see hunger met plainly leave you steady. Sight does not fade because you stop polishing it. Sight stays because it is true.

If you live with diabetes or take medicine that affects appetite or blood sugar, follow your clinician first and let this book change belief only.

I am stern with you here because I love you out of the last rope. Do not turn this book into another diet to carry. Do not count days. Do not weigh mornings. Do not make a project of being free. A free eater does not practice freedom. A free eater eats and lives and laughs and forgets the whole affair for hours at a time.

You are not a pupil any more. You are an eater.

THE PHOTOGRAPH ON THE FRIDGE

Keep something small. Not a course. Not a system. A photograph.

Copy these few lines in your own hand or take a picture of them on your phone and pin it where you will see it on a tired evening. When doubt whispers that there is a cost, read them and smile at how little there is to remember.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That was always the whole contract. Not effort. Not hunger. Not white heat. Follow the sight. Guard the sight. Let the body do the rest.

When we dosed daily, life was arithmetic around BAD SUGAR. How much. How often. How hidden. How excused. One small bag promised, three bought. One bite permitted, box emptied before regret could finish its sentence. That arithmetic always lost because craving was belief calling the hand before thought caught up.

That arithmetic is gone. Hunger is your clock now. Satisfaction is your bell. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that line slowly. It is not a rule. It is a description of what the body does when no coating shouts over it. The child with the peach lived it without words. You lived it this morning without effort. You will live it tonight without debate.

“But what if I forget a line?” That is the old fear borrowing wisdom’s voice. Answer it with your own day as witness.

Did forgetting ever place a dose in your mouth? Did remembering misery ever keep one out? What kept your hand light in the bright aisle — memory of a sentence, or clear seeing that bright cardboard held fog and prowl? If seeing kept you light once, will seeing need notes to keep you light twice?

Only honest answers survive here. Seeing keeps you free.

So keep the photograph for comfort, not for labour. Glance when you like. Forget for days. The list does not hold you up. You hold the list lightly because you no longer lean on it.

SWEETNESS HANDED BACK

Now the last seeing, saved for this hour. It changes nothing and it changes everything.

You think you have quit something sweet. You have not.

Think of the mornings dosed and sour, the afternoons buzzed then fogged, the evenings sealed with a fix that left thirst and second reach. Was that sweetness? Did the tongue awake taste more, or less? Did food glow brighter, or duller? Did love at the table grow fuller, or thinner? If those hits were sweetness, why did the sweetest years taste coated and leave you flat, foggy, prowling for more?

You know the honest answers. The coating dulled the tongue it claimed to delight. The fix borrowed joy from rest and company and hunger met, while digging the low that made joy feel thin.

Here is the truth to carry out of these pages. You have not given up sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you by the trap and has now been handed back.

Look at a peach now with a clean tongue. It cracks. It juices. It satisfies without starting a clamour. Look at bread with bite, soup with steam, berries bright after a savoury meal. Look at tea tasting like itself. This is sweetness as the body meant it — part of hunger met, part of satisfaction holding, part of flavour awake. The trap shrank all that vast brightness down to one narrow hit that never satisfied, then sold the narrowing back to you as treat.

This was never about quitting. It was about growing back into the eater you were before the narrowing began.

We all began as that eater. Hungry. Present. Done when done. No counting. No bargaining. No payment in coating for being good this week. Then came bliss-point packs and cartoon grins and checkout ambush, and the slow teaching that joy lived in a wrapper. We were conned, not foolish. We mistook relief from a self-dug low for a gift. Now the con is seen and the eater stands clear again, older, kinder, wider awake.

Ask which life you want to keep.

Do you want to dose and crash and crave for the rest of your life, paying every single day for something you do not even need, watching evenings end prowling and mornings begin sour?

Or do you want to eat and live clear for the rest of your life, waking bright, working steady through hours that used to dip and dive, sitting at tables with laughter remembered and sleep clean?

Only honest answers stand here. One rest-of-life is slavery. The other is yours by sight.

You are not losing. You are regaining.

GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

So go. Get on with enjoying your life.

Eat breakfast hungry. Eat lunch hungry. Eat dinner hungry. Stop at comfort. Notice salt, sour, warm, crisp, ripe returning like old friends. Walk the bright aisle slow with clear eyes. Pity trapped hands warmly, briefly, and walk on light. Sit on the evening sofa with tea and let the day settle. Laugh at the funny scene. Remember the talk. Sleep unloaded. Wake bright.

Change nothing else in your life. Same streets. Same shops. Same friends. Same work. Same birthdays — only present now, remembered now, slept off cleanly now. No new system. No void to fill. No calendar to white-knuckle. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own. Hunger comes clean. Satisfaction holds.

A thought will cross at times. A picture of gloss. A smell of bake. A memory of ease. Let it come. It has no hands. Only belief could give it hands, and belief is gone. Answer it at once with joy. Think how marvellous it feels to walk past bright packs wanting nothing. Think how wonderful evenings feel without prowling. Think how exciting mornings feel without sourness. Let pity rise for users still shuffling with bags of fixes, and relief rise for you.

I walked through those first ordinary days myself with wonder at how little there was to do. Breakfast. Work. Shop. Dinner. Sofa. Sleep. All the old rooms, all the old hours, lived bright. The sweet thought crossed thinner each month, and each time it brought the same bell.

You stand now where every free eater stands — clear that mornings satisfy, that aisles hold no orders, that evenings stay full, that crumbs teach and pass, that quiet ease recruits louder than preaching. Guard one thing and one thing only: the sight that there is nothing here you want. The body follows the sight. Keep the sight clear and no street, no shelf, no celebration can touch you.

Do not mourn. Rejoice. Do not preach. Shine. Do not wait to be free. You already are.

Close the book. Leave it shut. Go to your kitchen hungry and eat with pleasure. Go to your people and be fully there. Go to your bed clean and sleep deep. Tomorrow will ask nothing of you but living.
