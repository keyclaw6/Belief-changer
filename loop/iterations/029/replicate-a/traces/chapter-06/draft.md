Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

IN THIS CHAPTER
Saturday market with wet greens and loud colour, warm loaf in paper, noon kitchen with steam on the window, peach eaten hungry with juice on the chin, plate pushed back at satisfied

*Hunger met by real food is the feast you thought the wrapper held.*

THE GREY FEAR

You come to this room with the afternoon proven.

You have watched the drawer open by itself at ten to three. You have watched ten minutes of brightness thin and sink lower. You have felt with your own hours that the lift never built, it only paused. The fuel story is dead. Your hands are clean of blame and your eyes are clear.

And now a quieter fear speaks, in your own voice, and I honour it because it is honest:

"But without something sweet, eating will be grey. Dessert is love. Pudding is the point."

"Celebrating with chocolate. A treat when you feel you deserve one. That is what makes a meal a meal."

I do not laugh at that voice. That voice held birthdays together. That voice tried to pay you back after hard days. Of course it feels true. We all learned it before we could read a menu.

Let me sit with you at the table where that fear lives, because this chapter does not argue. This chapter eats.

You fear that when BAD SUGAR leaves the table, joy leaves with it. You fear plain food will sit flat on the tongue, that hunger will nag without a sweet answer, that satisfaction will never quite arrive, that you will chew and swallow and still look around for the real pleasure that used to come in a wrapper.

Do you truly believe joy lived in the wrapper?

Look at your own sweetest meals. The cake after the long week. The biscuits eaten standing up at the counter. The chocolate eaten in front of the late film. Did you eat slowly, tasting, glowing, stopping with a sigh? Or did you eat fast, half-noticed, hand to mouth while thinking of something else, mouth coated, stomach full and empty together, heart still asking?

Did the sweet leave you settled? Or did it leave you prowling for more?

Only honest answers survive here, and yours already know the shape. Sweet doses never settled hunger. They kept hunger awake. A real meal settles. That difference is everything, and your body never forgot it, even when we stopped listening.

I am not asking you to believe me yet. I am asking you to eat with me once, hungry, and watch what your own mouth does.

MARKET MORNING

Come with me on an ordinary Saturday, because freedom is ordinary and marvellous from the first step.

Morning air cool on the face. Canvas awnings damp from night. Crates stacked high. Lettuces wet and crisp, carrots with earth still on them, tomatoes warm from the sun under the cover, herbs smelling green and sharp when you brush them. Apples piled red and yellow. Pears with freckles. A stall with bread, loaves split on top, steam still leaving them, paper bags going soft with warmth in your arms.

We walk hungry. Not starving. Not faint. Clean hungry. The kind that makes the stomach give a small hollow call and the mouth water at smell. The kind children feel before lunch and answer without thinking.

You pick up a tomato and smell it. It smells like summer and soil. You tear a corner off the warm loaf and put it in your mouth. The crust cracks. The inside is soft and chewy and faintly sour and sweet in the way real things are sweet without shouting. You chew. You swallow. Your whole face wakes.

No one counts. No one bargains with Monday. No one says, I have been good this week so I may have this. The market does not speak that language. Hunger speaks, food answers, mouth thanks.

Watch what happens in your body when food is real and hunger is clean. The eyes brighten. The shoulders drop. The breath slows. Interest rises. You want to taste, not to chase. You linger. You laugh with the stallholder. You carry weight home in bags and feel glad of the weight.

We were taught to call this nothing. Shopping. Cooking. Plain eating. We were taught the excitement lived further down the aisle, in bright packets with cartoon promises. But who is excited here? The hands picking, smelling, choosing. The nose leading. The stomach calling clean. This is appetite alive, not managed, not feared, not drugged into silence and then whipped awake.

Bring that hunger home. That is all I ask. Bring it home and eat.

THE PLAIN FAVOURITE MEAL

Noon kitchen. Light high. Window steamed from the pot.

On the table, nothing clever. A bowl of soup made from those market vegetables, colour still loud, broth hot enough to warm the hands through the bowl. Fresh bread torn, not sliced, crumbs on the board. A plate of leaves with oil and lemon. Fruit for after, peaches with fuzz, skin blushing, juice ready. Water to drink because thirst is thirst and hunger is hunger and neither needs a costume.

Sit down hungry. Truly hungry. Not bored. Not twitchy from the last dose. Hungry because morning was lived and lunch is due. Feel that hollow call. Welcome it. Hunger is not an emergency. Hunger is the bell that makes food music.

Eat.

First spoon of soup. Hot. Salty. Sweet from carrots and onion cooked soft. You blow. You sip. Warmth runs down and spreads. You close your eyes without deciding to. The body says yes all the way down.

Tear bread. Chew slowly. Taste wheat. Taste chew itself, jaw working glad. Dip the crust and let it soak and soften. Eat another spoon. And another. Notice how taste grows with hunger met, not fades. The third mouthful tastes deeper than the first, because the body is waking to being answered.

Talk if company is there. Laugh. Be quiet if alone. Let the meal have its own pace. No screen to eat past. No wrapper to race. Hands moving between bowl and bread and glass, unhurried.

After a while, something quiet arrives. Not full to bursting. Not stuffed and sorry. Satisfied. The hollow is filled. The edge is gone. Taste is still lovely but the urgency has left it. The body settles. The breath comes easy. You push the plate back half an inch without thinking. You leave bread on the board and feel no pull to finish it to be tidy or to finish it because it is there.

Stop there.

That stop is the whole secret the sweet loop stole from you. Stop when satisfied and feel how clean it feels. No regret. No promise to be good tomorrow. No coated mouth calling for repair. Only ease. Warmth. Glad to sit a moment and do nothing.

Now the peach.

Hold it hungry still, but lightly hungry, the way dessert hunger feels after real food — a small bright corner left for freshness. Bite. Juice runs down the chin. Flesh gives. Sweetness floods, but the sweetness of a thing alive, with water and fibre and scent around it. You eat to the stone. You lick fingers. You wipe chin and laugh. And then — done. No second peach chased. No third. Satisfaction says enough, and enough feels like wealth, not denial.

Ask your own mouth what just happened.

Did eating feel grey? Did joy need a wrapper? Did hunger nag after being met? Did satisfaction hide?

You ate what you loved, when hunger called, and stopped when hunger was answered. That is eating as favourite. That is the eater you were before packets taught you to doubt your own bell.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

WHAT HUNGER AND SATISFACTION FEEL LIKE

Let me name the two friends you just met, because we lost their names for years under noise.

Hunger, clean, feels like emptiness with interest. A hollow in the middle, light, awake. Mouth watering at smell. Eyes brightening at sight. Stomach giving a small growl that feels almost cheerful. Thought turning to food with pleasure, not panic. No shake. No snapping at people you love. No I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling driving the hands. Only a bell ringing lunch.

We know the other feeling well, the one that wears hunger's clothes. Urgent. Twitchy. Head full of cotton and temper short. Mouth sour. Hands empty and prowling. That feeling arrives an hour after a dose, at the same hour the last dose wears off. It is not the bell. It is the echo. Clean hunger builds slowly and is answered for hours. The echo arrives fast and is never answered for long, however much you feed it.

Satisfaction, clean, feels like settling. Warmth spreading. Interest softening. Taste still good but no longer urgent. Body saying, enough, thank you, and meaning thank you. You could eat more, but you do not want to. The plate can wait. Food is forgotten for hours, not because you are being good, but because hunger is met and the mind turns glad to other things — work, walk, talk, play.

Think of a small body that stops mid-bite and runs to play.

That is the whole lesson in one picture. No lecture taught it. No rule enforced it. Hunger came, food answered, satisfaction closed the meal like a lid, and life resumed. No counting. No guilt. No second helping to chase a feeling that never arrived. The body led and joy followed.

Your body leads still.

Your body makes its own steady glucose from plain food, from meals eaten hungry and slept sleep and daily bread, keeping your brain lit through the night when no hand feeds you for hours. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that moves blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice. For the well body, clean baseline without BAD SUGAR is not deprivation. It is return. The machine is precise and generous and asks no flood to do its daily work.

Feel the difference with your own history. When did a real dinner eaten hungry ever make you need another dinner an hour later to keep the first dinner working? When did soup and bread and fruit ever leave you prowling the cupboard at ten at night, hand moving before decision? Never. Real hunger satisfied forgets itself. That forgetting is the proof of fuel and the proof of joy together.

BAD SUGAR never forgot itself. Not once. Every dose remembered itself louder and called for its own echo. That is how you know which was food and which was rent.

LOVE WAS NEVER THE WRAPPER

I hear the strongest objection before you speak it, because it deserves to be met face to face.

"But dessert is love. My mother’s pudding. Birthday candles. Cinema chocolate. You cannot tell me that was nothing."

I will not tell you it was nothing. I will tell you where the everything lived.

Think of the birthday table. Candles lit. Faces warm. Someone you love laughing with mouth open. Singing off key. The room holding its breath as wishes are made. A cake carried in glowing. You eat a slice among people who know your name. Sweetness on the tongue, yes — but where was the love? In the sugar? Or in the faces, the singing, the being known, the belonging?

Think of the sofa film. Lights low. Shared blanket. Funny scene coming. Hand finding hand in the same bowl. Laughter at the same second. Warmth spreading in the chest. Was the warmth the chocolate? Or was the chocolate riding on the warmth like a child on shoulders, claiming the view?

Think of the hard day paid back with a bar after virtue. I've managed to be good this week. I deserve one. The relief as teeth sink. The sigh. Was the sigh for the dose? Or was it for stopping at last, sitting at last, being kind to yourself at last after hours of driving?

Ask three quiet questions and answer with your own table, not with theory.

If the love lived in the dose, why did the same dose eaten alone standing at the counter in secret never bring the same glow? If the joy lived in the wrapper, why did the wrapper eaten fast without tasting, crumbs wiped, evidence hidden, leave flatness and not singing? If dessert was love, why did love never need a wrapper on days when hunger was met and company was full and laughter came free?

Only honest answers survive.

The love was the meal, the company, hunger met among your own. The dose only ever sneaked a ride. It took credit for the candles and the laughter and the rest after work, the way a loud guest takes credit for a party he did not host.

And here is the glad turn your heart is ready for. If the joy was never in the dose, no joy leaves when dosing leaves. The candles still glow. The laughter still comes. The sofa still holds. The hard day still ends and you can still be kind to yourself — kinder, because kindness that settles is kinder than kindness that prowls.

You do not lose celebration. Celebration is handed back clean. Fruit still sweet on a hungry tongue. Bread still marvellous torn warm. Soup still wonderful steaming. Company still sweetest of all. Eating itself is joy without a single packet, because hunger met is joy, and satisfaction is peace, and peace after hunger is the oldest feast we know.

Hunger met is joy. Hunger fooled is hunger kept.

See it once at your own table and the grey fear dies. Plain food is not grey. Grey was the fog between doses. Colour is hunger answered, taste noticed, enough recognised with thanks. You have just lived it for one noon. Imagine mornings, shops and meals lived that way as the rule, not the exception — hands quiet, mouth glad, mind free to turn to life because food forgot itself.

I speak as one who escaped that grey and found colour waiting where colour always lived. We were conned to look for love in bright packets. Love waited at the market, at the noon table, in the peach, in the company, in the bell and the answer. It never left. We only looked away.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

SUMMARY
- Food joy never lived in the wrapper, it lived in hunger met and company kept.
- Clean hunger feels hollow with interest, not urgent and prowling.
- Clean satisfaction feels settled and forgets food for hours.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes deeper with each mouthful.
- Stopping at satisfied feels like wealth, not loss.
- Fruit eaten hungry gives sweetness with freshness and leaves no coated call.
- The body keeps its own steady fuel from plain meals without any flood.
- Love at birthdays and films belonged to faces and laughter, not to the dose.
