Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

IN THIS CHAPTER
Friday kitchen with red-ringed calendar and tidy drawer, bright office drawer at four o'clock, cliff edge in sea wind with chalked line, biscuit tin moving hand to hand after dinner, midnight counter in a quiet house, seatbelt click on a grey morning road

*There is no careful little corner of BAD SUGAR you can keep and still be free.*

THE FRIDAY BARGAIN

You have walked far with me. You saw the afternoon lift for what it was. You saw the evening prowl for what it was. You saw the cinema bar and the birthday icing for what they were. The glow belonged to people and laughter and rest. The dose only borrowed it.

And now a reasonable voice steps forward with a calendar in its hand.

“All right. Daily grazing was slavery. I see that. Keep weekdays clean. Allow Fridays. Allow birthdays. Allow just one with coffee. Keep a small house rule and I will hold it.”

I know that voice because we all used it. I used it for years. Weekdays belonged to virtue. Weekends belonged to joy. One square proved control. Tomorrow would be cleaner if today were kind.

The fact is the bargain is the trap talking after losing every other argument. It cannot defend daily doses, so it defends one dose. It cannot defend the con, so it defends the date.

Look at what a Friday-allowance week does to a real eater, hour by hour.

Monday you feel virtuous. The drawer is cleared. The calendar is ringed. Tuesday you feel watchful. Wednesday you think about Friday at noon, uninvited. Thursday the thought grows louder and brighter and more golden than any sweet ever tasted on its own. Friday morning anticipation hums under work. Friday evening the first mouthful arrives loaded with five days of waiting, and you call that loaded glow proof that Friday sweets are special.

They are not special. They are starved for.

Ask your own week, and answer plain.

If the Friday dose is free choice, why does it occupy Tuesday? If the rule controls the dose, why does Thursday feel longer than Monday? If one evening is enough, why does Saturday morning start whispering for Thursday to slide?

A free pleasure does not need four days of rehearsal. A trap does.

We all lived this. We made BAD SUGAR precious by rationing it, then used the preciousness as proof we needed it. We polished the cage on weekdays and called the Friday opening freedom, though the door led back to the cage.

That is why totality is kindness, not harshness. The rule does not protect you from excess. The rule creates the glow that drives excess.

You do not need a stricter calendar. You need no calendar around BAD SUGAR at all.

ONE BITE CARRIES THE BINGE

“But just one,” you say. “Surely one small square cannot matter. I feel like i cant stop at home, I admit that — so let me keep just one careful one where love watches.”

Hear what you just told me. You described the signature truly, then asked to keep the signature room with prettier lighting.

“I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop.” That is how eaters say it in their own kitchens. A permitted sliver lights a chain that does not stop at one. The wrapper rustles. The hand moves before decision catches up. The box empty before the swallow ends.

I speak as one who tried every version of just one. The single biscuit with afternoon tea that became the tin passed round and round till shame sat in. The single square broken off for control that became the bar gone by the credits. The single dessert to be polite that became the midnight counter and the sour morning.

The craving, once triggered, carries the binge. That is the lived shape. Not because you are weak. Not because you are doomed to helplessness for life. Because one dose re-lights the belief that a dose gives something, and belief calls the hand before thought catches up.

Ask again, with eyes open.

When did just one ever stay one for long? When did a drawer cleared Monday to Friday stay cleared after the first Friday exception? When did a careful square quiet the evening, rather than open it?

You know the honest answers. Just one never stays one because one keeps the meaning alive. Keep the meaning and the hand follows.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Feel how that verdict closes the just-one door without force. If it did nothing FOR you often, it does nothing FOR you occasionally. If there is no benefit in chasing it daily, there is no point chasing it weekly. The rare dose is the same coating, the same load, the same call for another — only with added ceremony.

This is not a flaw in you. We were all conned in the same way. We believed rarity made poison precious. Rarity only made the con harder to see.

THE STARVATION GLOW

Monday to Friday clean, Saturday flood, Sunday gloom, Monday regret, Monday restart. We all rode that wheel and called it moderation.

The wheel is the trap form. Starve, crave, flood, blame. Restriction by day makes the evening volume grow. The clean days do not weaken the want. They ripen it. Each white-knuckle weekday teaches the mind that BAD SUGAR is scarce and precious, so Friday arrives frantic, not joyful.

This is why your Friday sweet tasted brighter than any weekday sweet ever did. Not because Friday sugar is better sugar. Because Friday hunger for it was five days deep. Lift away the five days of denial and the brightness falls away. Keep the denial and every Friday glows like a rescue — while digging a deeper low for Sunday.

Look at your own diary. The weeks you ruled hardest were the weeks you fell hardest. The mornings you swore virtue were the evenings you prowled longest. The rule did not fail because you broke it. The rule failed because the rule feeds the glow.

You were never weak-willed. You were strong enough to persist for days against hunger and instinct, then human enough to answer a need the rule had sharpened. Blame the wheel, not yourself.

We did this together, all of us in the Sugar Trap. We fasted, counted, earned, deserved, paid, promised. We called being good through Thursday proof we were free, then called falling on Friday proof we were broken. Both readings were wrong. The fasting made the falling. The falling proved the fasting was the feeder.

Do you want to keep polishing a wheel that throws you? Of course you want the wheel stopped — that is why you felt relief when you first saw starvation glow for what it was. Stay with that relief. Let it widen.

Free eaters do not glow on Fridays because Fridays are not loaded. Mondays taste clean. Thursdays taste clean. Fridays taste clean. Hunger comes, food satisfies, life moves. No rehearsal. No hum under work. No negotiation in the dark. That flat, easy sameness is what freedom feels like from the inside.

THE CLIFF

Come with me to a cliff, because moderation needs to be seen in daylight to be laughed at.

A friend takes you to a high cliff over rocks and sea. Wind tugs your coat. Gulls cry below. He points down, cheerful as a host, and says, “Jump. You may jump every Friday. Provided you fall only a few metres.”

You stare at him. You check his face for sense. Fall only a few metres? From this height? The condition is pretty. The drop is not.

He grows reasonable. “All right, not from the top. From a lower ledge. Just one small jump. Just on special occasions. Just after birthdays. Just with coffee. Just to prove you are in charge of cliffs.”

Would you call him wise? Would you call the small jump safe because it is rare? Would you climb down to argue about which ledge is safe, or would you step back from the edge and leave jumping to gulls?

You would step back. Not from fear. From sight. Cliffs do not offer safe falling. The height is the point.

BAD SUGAR living inside the Sugar Trap is that cliff. Friday-dessert allowance is the chalked line three metres down, drawn by the same mind that wants you to jump. Birthday-only, coffee-only, cinema-only, holidays-only — each is a lower ledge with the same rocks below. The date on the calendar does not shorten the drop. The smallness of the dose does not soften the belief that calls the next dose.

Do you see how the ledge moves? First it is Friday only. Then Friday and Saturday because Saturday felt flat after Friday. Then Thursday evening because Thursday felt long. Then mornings because the crash needs lifting. We all watched the chalk wash down the rock in rain. The line never held because the belief held.

“And I will wean,” you say. “Less each week. Lighter bars. Smaller squares till zero.”

Weaning is jumping slowly. It keeps eyes on the edge for weeks, deepens anticipation between smaller hits, and teaches the mouth to treasure each shrinking fall. No one leaves a cliff by practising falling. You leave it by seeing there is nothing down there worth falling for, then walking back to the field where dinner waits.

Ask the cliff its three questions.

If moderation is control, why does the controlled dose occupy days before it arrives? If the ledge is safe, why does the mind rehearse the fall all week? If the small fall proves mastery, why does mastery need proving again next Friday?

Only honest answers survive here. The safe sweet is a story told by the Sweet Con to keep one rope tied. Cut the rope and the cliff loses interest. Keep one rope and you stay tied to the edge, congratulating yourself for not yet falling.

I stood on that edge for years, negotiating ledges, chalking lines, praising my caution while wind pulled at my coat. The day I saw there was nothing down there to want, I stopped admiring my balance and walked away hungry for real dinner. No courage needed. Sight did the walking.

TOMORROW AND THE TIDY CUPBOARD

Two more soft doors, each with your voice on the handle. Let me close them with you gently, because each kept me prowling a year longer than need.

“I will start tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays.”

We all said tomorrow because tomorrow feels safer than now. Look at tomorrow. Tomorrow holds a birthday. The day after holds a dinner. Next month holds holidays. Summer holds travel. Winter holds comfort nights. The calendar never empties.

If the dose were harmless you would not be reading a book to escape it. If the dose is worth escaping, why pay another month of fog, thirst, load and prowling for a date that moves?

Ask: how many tomorrows have already passed with the same promise? How many birthdays were meant to be the last sweet one? Did waiting make the step easier, or did waiting make the belief heavier with each paid week?

Delay is the Sweet Con speaking. It says wait till ready while making ready impossible by keeping the belief fed. Readiness does not arrive on a quiet Tuesday with no cake in sight. Readiness is sight. You see there is nothing to lose, so now is easy.

“I do not keep it in the house. That proves I control it.”

I honour the tidy cupboard. We all cleared cupboards and felt clean till six o'clock. Then we walked to the shop in rain for one bar. Then we ate it on the bench before home. Then we bought another for the house we swore to keep clean. Then we kept a drawer at work, a tin at a friend's, a purse dose for emergencies.

The house rule did not remove want. It moved purchase, added walking, added secrecy, added romance to the hunt.

If the cupboard rule controlled the dose, why did the hand move before decision outside the house? Why did the office drawer, the petrol station, the friend's tin become cupboards by another name? A belief kept alive in one room prowls every street.

Control is not an empty shelf. Control is an empty want. Empty want needs no locked door. The free eater walks past bright packs with pity, light and clean, happy to be free, hungry for dinner, present for laughter without coating.

You have proved this to yourself already. The nights you saw clearly you passed the bright aisle without a flicker. The nights you negotiated you felt the pull from three aisles away. The aisle did not change. The belief did.

THE SWAP THAT KEEPS THE POSTURE

“I will swap. Fruit chews. Light bars. Sweet drinks. A less-bad version till I fade.”

A swap keeps the mouth in the same posture and the mind in the same story — hourly hit, brief lift, fast fade, need-something call. The label changes. The loop stays. Feed the Nibbler brown or white, solid or sipped, branded healthy or branded treat, and it grumbles for more on the same clock.

Ask: did the light version ever end the evening prowl? Or did it move the prowl to a larger box with a kinder name? Did sipping shorten the afternoon yo-yo? Or did it spread grazing through the whole day till hunger never cleared?

Only honest answers close this. Substitutes keep alive the belief that a sweet hit fixes a low the last hit dug. Remove the belief and no substitute is needed. Hunger, satisfaction and real food do the work cleanly, without ceremony, without counting.

We all tried the kinder packet. We read fronts, compared colours, paid more for less guilt. The mouth stayed coated. The belly stayed loaded. The mind stayed busy. Kindness was on the label. Roughness stayed in the work.

“Is not total abstinence extreme? You speak freedom yet forbid a single sweet.”

I welcome that challenge because it tests the method itself, not the calendar. Hear me flat.

I forbid nothing from power. I point to nothing worth wanting. Extreme would be forbidding a good. Seeing there is no good is not extreme. It is clear. If I told you never to drink puddle water on Fridays, would you call me rigid? Or would you thank me for showing the puddle?

This book does not brainwash you toward fear. Brainwashing installed the glow. Clear sight removes it. You remain free to dose any Friday you choose. I ask only: will you want to, once you see the Friday glow was starvation glow, the just-one was the binge trigger, and the cliff has no safe ledge?

That question is the whole escape. Answer it with your own weeks, not my voice.

Do you want to keep one shining dose on a high shelf and spend life looking up at it? Of course you want the shelf empty and the mind empty with it — that is why the negotiator feels so tiring to listen to now. Tiredness is the con running out of rooms.

THE SEATBELT

“But what if a crumb slips in? A sauce sweetened without my knowing? A bite taken before I see? Does one slip throw me back to slavery?”

No. And this matters, because panic is another rope of the same con.

Think of a seatbelt on a grey morning road. You slide in, pull the belt across, hear the click. Hedge rows pass. Radio low. You drive steady to the shops, to work, to dinner with friends. The belt rests light across you. You forget it.

You did not click it so you can drive erratically, aim for ditches, test the strap for sport. You clicked it for the accidental jolt — a sudden stop, a careless driver, a spill no one planned. The belt absorbs the shock and you drive on, steady, unshaken.

Your body is built that way with BAD SUGAR. It can cope with an occasional accidental crumb without ruin. Your mind cannot afford a deliberate swerve. An accidental taste, noticed, smiled at, left behind, revives nothing unless you let the old belief climb back in and say, “There, you have blown it, you may as well flood.”

A slip is a rumble lesson, not a crash. Notice the cloy, the coating, the faint call. Rejoice that you see it for what it is. Drive on. Never punish yourself. Never use the blip as licence for a planned Friday fall.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that moves blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry an eating-disorder history, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice above all here.

Guard the belief and the body follows. Guard the behaviour with panic and the belief sneaks back wearing fear. There is no fear here. There is sight, and sight is gentle.

You stand now where every free eater stands before the last step — clear that cut-down gilds the cage, that tomorrow never comes, that house rules move the hunt, that swaps keep the posture, that one kept dose keeps the whole meaning breathing.

The Sweet Con kept you negotiating because negotiation kept the benefit alive somewhere. Starve the negotiation and the Nibbler starves with it. Its grumble is small, brief, already fading. Its death is relief, not loss.

Picture the ordinary week that waits. Monday dinner eaten hungry to satisfaction. Tuesday shop walked with clear eyes. Wednesday afternoon bright without a drawer. Thursday talk without hum. Friday laughter without coating. Saturday morning light without sourness. Sunday rest without regret. No rehearsal. No hum. No midnight counter. That sameness is marvellous freedom, wonderful in its plainness, exciting because it lasts.

Rejoice at that picture. You lose no celebration. You walk into every celebration cleaner, lighter, more present, free to laugh without chewing, free to remember without fog, free to sleep without prowling.

SUMMARY
- A Friday-only rule makes the Friday dose glow by starving weekdays, not by adding joy.
- One permitted square re-lights the binge because it keeps alive the belief that a dose gives something.
- Clean-day weeks grow cravings by making BAD SUGAR scarce and precious, then flood follows restriction.
- Cliffs offer no safe ledge, and dates on a calendar do not shorten the drop.
- Tomorrow never empties, a tidy cupboard still prowls the streets, and swaps keep the same loop in kinder packets.
- An accidental crumb stays an accident unless old belief is allowed back to turn it into a flood.
