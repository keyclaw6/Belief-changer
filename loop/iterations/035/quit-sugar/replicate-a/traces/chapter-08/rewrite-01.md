Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THE WANT

IN THIS CHAPTER
Bright aisle at child eye-level, checkout tower, family mirror, open cell

*Your want was built for you, and the fear around it was built with it — neither proves you need BAD SUGAR.*

The Aisle That Wanted For You

Walk with me down the aisle where you do your ordinary shop.

You came in for bread and milk. The light is bright and flat. The shelves rise higher than your head. Down low, at trolley-handle height, the bright packs sit where small hands reach first. Cartoon tigers grin. Purple creatures dance. A bar the size of your thumb wears a cape and promises power. Further on, biscuits in family armour line up in twenty shades of gold. Bottles sweat. Every pack shouts crunch, goo, melt, new, bigger, now with more.

You tell yourself you chose. I know that story because we all lived it.

Stand still and watch what chose for you.

Food chemists spent months tuning crunch against melt, salt against sweet, soft against snap, till the bite hits a peak your mouth cannot argue with. Not hunger answering hunger. Tuning answering tuning. Designers tuned the rustle, the colour, the animal eyes that follow a child down the row. Managers paid for the end of the row, for the tower by the till, for the small bars at your elbow while you queue tired with a basket on your arm. Nothing there is accident. Nothing there is appetite speaking to appetite.

When did a peach ever hire a tiger to beg for you? When did soup ever pay to ambush you at the till? When did plain bread ever need a cape to be wanted?

You know the honest answers. The packs that need the loudest shouting are the ones your body never asked for.

We walked that aisle together for years, you and I. We went in steady and came out with a bag that rattled. We told ourselves everyone eats it so it must be fine. We called the extra pack a little reward for strain. We carried it home and called the carrying choice.

The fact is, the want arrived before you did.

You were taught it before you could read, then engineered at every turn after you could. Parents slipped a sweet for quiet and called it kindness. Grandparents pressed coins for the shop as proof of love. Teachers gave stars and sweets for good marks. Screens tied chocolate to laughter, to romance, to funny evenings, to being a good parent, to deserving rest. School prizes, office drawers, birthday tables — sweet arrived wherever warmth lived and claimed the warmth as its own work. No wonder the adult hears a hollow and translates it as I need my treat, my fuel, my comfort. The translation was taught before you could spell.

Then the makers tuned the dose to hold you. A lift sharp enough to notice. A dip fast enough to bring you back within the hour. Bright for ten minutes. Coated for hours. Hunting again before the wrapper settles, leaving you flat, foggy and never satisfied and pointing you to the next pack.

That last line is not poetry. It is the state you logged at the desk and at seven in the evening. Ten bright minutes bought with hours of coating. One square quieting for minutes the noise the last square laid down.

I place no blame on you for wanting in that aisle. You were conned in that aisle. I speak harshly of the makers who tuned the con, never of the shopper they aimed it at. A lone shopper was set against armies of tuning, testing, colour, placement. No wonder the hand moved. The hand was led.

You were never weak in that aisle. You were caught in the Sugar Trap.

And the voice that says you chose, that says you like it, that says you need it to get through — that voice is not your own reasoning. That voice is the Sweet Con talking, installed early, fed daily, quoting mother, teacher, advert, office, till.

Look at the tower by the till with clear eyes. Small bars, bright foils, two-for-one. Tired people queue with baskets on arms. Children whine. Hands reach without orders. Is that hunger? Hunger welcomes many foods. Hunger waits twenty minutes for a bench with a view. Hunger accepts bread or soup or fruit. That reaching wants sweet only, now, and now only. Same hands. Same bodies. Opposite behaviour at noon after a hungry meal, when the same tower is passed without a glance. The variable was never character. The variable was the low underneath plus the old reading on top.

Ask the aisle its plain questions.

If the packs fed hunger, why do fed bodies pass them calm after a hungry meal and hunt them hard at four after a dosed day?

If the tiger told truth, why does the peach with no tiger satisfy for hours while the bar with a cape calls again within the hour?

If everyone eating it proved it fine, why did your own evenings end coated, foggy, hunting, while your clearest mornings held no wrapper?

You know. The want was placed. The fear of passing it was placed with it. Neither proves need.

Mine Is Different

“But mine is different,” you say. “My family all have a sweet tooth. I have always been the addictive type. Other people can take one. I feel like i cant stop.”

Let that voice speak fully. I welcome it, because it kept me dosing long after joy left.

Look at it as an investigator, not as a prisoner.

If your nature caused the binge, why did the binge only start after regular dosing began? Did you empty boxes at six before sweet became daily? Did you hide wrappers before allow-then-forbid taught you to hide? If personality drove it, why do you take one slice of bread without chasing the loaf, one handful of peanuts without emptying the jar, yet one biscuit carries the box? Same person. Same character. Opposite behaviour. The variable was never your soul. The variable was the on-off pattern plus the dose.

Even in rats, when sweet arrives in bursts after gaps, binges follow. That does not prove you are built the same way. It cautions, and it mirrors the allow-forbid wheel we know — permit, binge, repent, crave, permit. Feed the wheel daily and the wheel turns daily. Break the belief that turns it and the wheel has no reason to turn.

And you are not odd in this. On checklists that ask about rich food, around 14 in every 100 adults and 12 in every 100 children tick enough boxes to match the pattern seen with drink and cigarettes. That is people answering questions, not doctors giving a diagnosis, and I cite it for one reason only: you are not uniquely broken. The trap is common because the building of it was common.

Watch how common shows in your own mouth. The first bar charmed. Then two were needed for the same note. Then the family pack, eaten standing, half turned away. That crowding — needing more for the same note — sits beside sharper bells. Smell fires before the bite. Wrapper fires before the bite. Three o’clock fires before the bite. The bell rings louder as the pleasure thins. Bell plus low underneath plus the old reading, and feet turn to the machine.

That crowding and firing are learned features of the trap. They do not prove your brain forces your hand. Hands move on belief.

Ask the bell its plain questions.

If smell compelled use, why do walkers fresh from a hungry meal pass the bakery and keep walking?

If three o’clock compelled dosing, why did the hungry morning pass noon without urgency?

If tiredness needed sweet, why does sleep answer tiredness while sweet only postpones it an hour and charges fog?

You know. Cues fire from habit. They do not compel. The firing thins when not followed. Monday the wrapper spikes. Tuesday the spike is shorter. Wednesday it is noted with mild amusement while you keep typing. Thursday it barely registers till afterwards, when you realise you did not register it. That is not years of training. That is days of lived rings without feed.

Your traits did not build the want. The want was built, then your traits were blamed for it. It is the other way around.

Think of the family mirror for a moment. Father takes sweet with tea. Mother keeps biscuits for visitors and eats three after they leave. Grandmother says we all have a sweet tooth and laughs. You hear that laugh and translate it as blood. Look closer. What was passed down? Not a tooth. A cupboard. A drawer. A rule — treat when you feel you deserve one — spoken so often it sounds like nature. A kitchen where love arrived in a foil. A house where strain was answered with a rustle. You learned the reach by watching, then taught your mouth the same low by dosing, then called the learned low inheritance.

If inheritance drove it, why do brothers raised at the same table dose so differently once kitchens part? Why does the same sweet tooth vanish for weeks on a busy farm summer, on a long walk, in weeks of plain meals eaten hungry, then return within days of daily dosing? Blood does not come and go with the cupboard. Learning does. Low does. Belief does.

And what of the phrase addictive type? We all carried it. I carried it into every Monday plan. I told myself other people had stronger character. Look at your own evidence against that story. You persisted for years in something that went against all your instincts — against teeth aching, afternoons fogging, money leaking, boxes emptying in shame. It takes a strong will to persist that long against the body’s own signals. Wilful, not weak-willed. The strength was never missing. The information was.

The Sweet Con loves the personality story because it hides the builder. If the fault is in your blood, you will never look at the aisle, the tiger, the tower, the tuning. You will blame the mirror and keep dosing. Turn the gaze outward where it belongs. Blame the tuning, never the shopper. Pity the trapped, never envy them. Your hand was led. Led hands can be unled by seeing.

The Treat That Predicts The Low

There is a harder fact to set beside the aisle, and I will set it flat because you deserve plain speech.

Follow men across years and those who ate more sweet food day after day were more likely to feel low later, even when strain and habit were taken into account. The daily treat did not fix mood. The daily treat went with later low mood.

I am not telling you this will happen to you.

Hear how I use it. I do not want you changing because you are frightened. Fear never loosened a trap; fear only tightens the grip it claims to loosen. I tell you because it completes the inversion you already lived: the rescuer was the perpetrator. The lift you bought for gloom laid down the next gloom. The comfort you bought for strain charged you strain with interest.

Test it against afternoons you have lived.

When did a dosed afternoon ever close clear? You dosed at three, buzzed at three ten, fogged at four, reached again at four thirty. When did an undosed hungry meal ever behave that way? You ate hungry at noon, closed satisfied, worked level till six. Which one steadied mood? Which one scheduled the dip?

You know. The dose never steadied. It borrowed from tomorrow to paint today, then sent the bill.

Men who dosed daily felt the bill years on. That does not sentence you. Bodies differ. Years differ. Strains differ. I speak of a pattern seen across many, not a verdict on one. If you live with low mood now, put this book aside for that part and speak to your clinician. This book changes a belief about BAD SUGAR. It does not treat mood.

Keep the shape, drop the scare. Lift, then dip. Lift, then dip. Short term lift then fast drop, as your own mouth says. The roller coaster you named — I want to get off the roller coaster — is not your personality. It is the dose schedule riding you.

The body you carry is a precision machine, not a dull bucket to fill. Its warning feelings are dials, not faults. True hunger rises slow, welcomes many foods, holds quiet for hours when met. The sweet low arrives sharp, wants sweet only, vanishes ten minutes after a square and returns sharper within the hour. One is information. The other is debt coming due. You do not fix a dial by unscrewing its bulb. You read the dial and answer what it points to.

Think of hunger as a fuel gauge and the sweet low as a warning lamp wired to the last dose. Gauge drops slow across the morning, welcomes fuel at noon, rises to full after a hungry meal, holds for hours. Lamp flashes sharp at three, points only to the packet, goes dark ten minutes after a square, flashes again at four. One tells you about the tank. One tells you about the wiring the last dose left. Unscrew the lamp and the wiring stays. Read the lamp as wiring and you stop feeding wiring with fuel.

Do not change from fear of a dark year. Change from seeing that the bright packet never gave what it claimed. The gloom you feared without it was gloom you only felt with it.

Ask mood its questions.

If sweet steadied mood, why did dosed weeks swing harder than undosed weeks of plain meals?

If sweet fixed strain, why did strain answered with a hungry meal and a walk close clear while strain answered with a bar called again within the hour?

If the daily treat protected mornings, why did mornings after box-empty evenings start coated while mornings after hungry suppers started clean?

You know. The treat predicted the low because the treat laid the low.

The Bell That Rings Before The Bite

There is one more voice to unmask, because it ambushes even convinced readers.

You walk past a bakery and the smell hooks. You hear a wrapper rustle two desks away and the mouth waters. You see the hour on the clock — three — and the feet turn toward the machine without orders. Pavement bins overflow with cups and foils. The bus shelter poster glows. You call it proof that cues own you, that the world will always pull you back.

I know that pull. I stood in queues I had sworn to pass. I took the long way round the shop to avoid the aisle and then found myself in the aisle with basket in hand. I told myself my brain was wired and choice was gone.

Look closer with me. Those pulls are learned firings, not commands.

We paired sweet with places, times, sounds, tiredness, birthday tables, strain, till the pairing fired on its own. Smell fires before the bite. Wrapper fires before the bite. Three o’clock fires before the bite. Tiredness fires before the bite. The low hum of the vending machine fires before the bite. That firing is real. It is also empty. A bell rung often rings in the head after the meal is over. The bell cannot feed you. The bell cannot move your hand. Hands move on belief.

Watch the bell at work on a plain day.

You pass the bakery after a hungry meal, satisfied, mouth clean. Smell fires. Mouth notes. Feet keep walking. No drama. No debate. The bell rang and nothing followed, because no low waited underneath and no belief answered the ring.

You pass the same bakery at four after a dosed day, coated and dipping. Smell fires. The Sweet Con translates: you need a lift, you deserve one, just this once, you cannot cope without, everyone takes one. Feet turn. That turn was not the smell compelling you. That turn was the translation you obeyed.

Same nose. Same smell. Opposite afternoons. The variable was never the cue. The variable was the low underneath plus the belief on top.

The Sweet Con loves to call the bell proof of slavery. Slavery would need a master that forces the hand. The bell forces nothing. It suggests, it nudges, it waters the mouth. Suggestion is not compulsion. Watering is not drinking. You have ignored a thousand suggestions — to buy a coat, to take a flyer, to hum a jingle — without doubting your mind. The wrapper is one more jingle that learned to sound like need.

Let the wrapper rustle. Let the smell roll out of the bakery door. Let the clock show three. Note the firing, name it kindly — ah, learned firing, nothing more — and feel it thin as you walk on. Each unfollowed ring weakens the pairing a touch. Each smiled-at grumble starves the echo a touch more.

We were taught that cues prove slavery. The fact is, cues prove learning, and learning updates. You learned the ring in months. You unlearn it in days by living through rings without feeding them. No struggle in the unlearning. Only noticing while you live.

Keep that noticing beside the drawer log, the ditch, the desk hours. They are witnesses that the lift was debt. Keep the market bag and the full stop. They are witnesses that meals close without help. The bell belongs with the first set, not the second. It announces debt coming due, not help arriving.

Watch one cue lose its voice in real time. Monday. Wrapper rustles. Mouth waters. Thought points. You note: firing, plus low underneath from lunch plus sweet drink. You drink water if dry, return to work. Firing thins by the time the kettle boils. Tuesday. Same rustle. Shorter spike. Wednesday. Rustle noted with mild amusement while you keep typing. Thursday. Rustle barely registers till afterwards, when you realise you did not register it. That is not years of training. That is days of lived rings without feed. The bell rang. No answer followed. The bell quiets.

Ask the cue its last questions.

If wrappers forced use, why do fed walkers pass rustling desks without turning?

If three o’clock forced dosing, why did the hungry market morning pass noon without urgency?

If tiredness needed sweet, why does sleep answer tiredness while sweet only postpones it an hour and charges fog?

You know. The bell rings. You decide what the ring means. When the ring meant need, you dosed. When the ring means dying echo, you smile and keep walking.

Hunger Is Information, Debt Is Not

Now set the machine beside the bell, because this distinction carries the whole middle.

Your body speaks in two tongues and we were taught to confuse them.

Hunger speaks slow. It rises across hours, a hollow that welcomes. Bread will do. Soup will do. Fruit will do. Leftovers will do. It does not stamp. It does not point to one packet. It waits while you finish work, while you walk to the bench with a view, while you queue. Answered hungry with plain food, it closes with its own full stop — a quiet, unforced enough — and holds quiet for hours. No coating. No hunting. No negotiation in the feet.

Debt speaks sharp. It arrives within the hour of a dose, mouth-level, sweet-only, now-now-now. I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, as your own mouth phrases it when the low bites. Bread will not do. Soup will not do. Fruit will not do. Only the packet will do. Answered with a square, it quiets for ten minutes, then returns sharper. Answered with a box, it quiets till morning coated, then returns with the light.

One is a gauge. One is wiring left by the last dose.

We lived inside the confusion for years. We called debt hunger. We dosed debt with fuel and wondered why fuel never held. We said sugary somethings to get me through and watched the through never arrive. We said treat when you feel you deserve one and watched deserving arrive daily. We said I have managed to be good this week and watched good collapse by Thursday. Good was allow. Thursday was forbid breaking. Collapse was the wheel turning, not character failing.

The Sweet Con thrives on that confusion. Call debt hunger and every dose looks like feeding. Call debt hunger and passing the packet looks like starving. Call debt hunger and fear of life without packets looks like wisdom. Rename debt as debt and the whole story flips. Feeding debt never fed hunger. Passing the packet never starved hunger. Fear of life without debt is fear of life without a low you only felt with the dose.

Learn to read the dials in real time.

Noon. Belly hollow, mouth clean, mind open to many foods, willing to wait. That is hunger. Answer it hungry. Eat what hunger welcomes. Stop at the full stop.

Three. Belly not empty — lunch only two hours past — yet mouth coats, attention narrows to one drawer, thought points sweet-only. That is debt. Note it. Drink water if dry. Keep hands busy if restless. Watch the wave crest and thin without feed. It thins. It always thins when unbelieved.

Seven. Dinner two hours past, belly easy, yet hollow under ribs, mouth restlessness, thought pointing to the packet and to nothing else. That is debt, not supper calling. Note it. Attend to supper if hungry. Talk, walk, wash a pan. Watch the second wave thinner than the first.

Midnight. Mouth calling while belly tight. That is debt at its plainest — a full belly behaving as if starved. No gauge behaves that way. Only wiring behaves that way.

We were taught to unscrew the lamp — dose the feeling away, quiet the bell with a square, fix tiredness with a bar. Unscrewing never fixed wiring. Reading does. Read debt as debt and you stop paying debt with fuel. Read hunger as hunger and you feed it once, well, and walk clear for hours.

The machine needs no pampering and no policing. It needs plain food when hungry, water when dry, sleep when tired, movement when stiff. It has run this way since you were small, before tigers taught it otherwise. Recall the child with the peach — eats hungrily, stops mid-bite, runs off. No counting. No guilt. Body leads. That leader never left. It was only drowned by bells.

Trust the gauge. Note the lamp. The gauge feeds. The lamp fades.

The Open Door You Fear

Now we come to the fear that holds even convinced readers, because it wears wisdom’s clothes.

“I might fail. And if I succeed, who will I be at birthday tables, at late tables, on tired evenings? Life without sweet will be grey.”

I know both ropes because I pulled both. One rope says you will crawl back. The other says you will stand outside the warmth. Pull either and the middle burns your hands. That burning has a name you will now carry in ordinary speech. That burning is the tug-of-war of craving and fear.

See the shape first in a picture you cannot unsee.

A cell door stands open. Light falls across the floor. Food, work, talk wait outside. The prisoner sits on the bunk, hands round knees, begging to stay. Outside is danger, he says. Outside I will fail. Outside I will be no one. He calls his fear wisdom. He calls the cell safety.

In the next cage, the door also stands open. The lion in it, loosed that morning, paces out two steps, turns, paces back, lies down by the bars from habit, not need. No chain holds him. Only the groove of years moves his feet.

We were both prisoners. We were both lions.

The prisoner’s dread does not prove the cell feeds him. The lion’s pacing does not prove the cage nourishes him. Both prove habit plus a story, nothing more.

Ask the door its questions.

If the cell protected you, why did you need this book in your hand to defend staying in it?

If the cage fed you, why did fed days without BAD SUGAR leave you clearer than dosed days with it?

If outside were grey, why were the brightest hours you lately lived — market bag heavy, mouth clean, hunger met, full stop reached — bright without a wrapper?

You know. Both ropes belong to the same hands. Both fears were installed by the same builder who installed the want. Fear of failing says the grumble will grow forever. It will not; it peaks in days and thins. Fear of succeeding says joy lived in the packet. It did not; joy lived in the scene, the company, the hunger met, while the packet only sneaked a ride.

The Sweet Con holds both ropes. It whispers, you will fail, so stay. It whispers, you will succeed and be dull, so stay. Stay is the only word it knows. It calls staying safety. It calls staying love. It calls staying normal because everyone eats it. Everyone eating it never made the low less low. Everyone hearing bells never made bells commands.

Picture the two rests of life laid side by side. Keep dosing to hold fear off, and you buy fear for the rest of your life — daily bargaining, nightly rustle, mornings coated. See the con and let the door stand open, and you walk level mornings for the rest of your life — shops passed without debate, meals closed without hunting, evenings held without pacing.

No new fear is needed here. The old fears collapse the moment you see whose hands hold the ropes.

A small guard belongs here, spoken plain. Your body can cope with an accidental crumb the way a belt can take a sudden jolt. The belt is there for the jolt, never for steering into walls. Talk to your clinician first if you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry medical risk around food. Guard the belief, and the odd jolt touches nothing.

Do not test the door by peeking back into the cell to prove you are strong. Strength was never the question. Seeing is. Walk out, keep walking, let the pacing thin the way the bakery smell thinned when unfollowed. Within days the feet learn the new groove: past the tower without slowing, past the till without reaching, home with bread and milk and nothing rattling.

Other voices will try to pull you back into the cell. The office drawer-keeper will say one will not hurt. The birthday-table host will say you are no fun. The screen will say new bar, must try. The well-meaning adviser will hand you rules, swaps, cut-down plans, special ones. Hear each as the builder’s echo, not as care. Care would hand you mornings clear. The builder hands you mornings coated.

They built the want. They built the fear that guards it.

SUMMARY
- The want was placed long before your hand moved, in tuning, colour, place and early lessons.
- Emptying the box after one shows the wheel turning, not a flaw in blood or character.
- Many ordinary shoppers share the pattern, which points to common building.
- Needing bigger packs sits beside sharper bells, and bells suggest without forcing.
- A daily sweet habit goes with later low mood rather than steadying mood, as dosed afternoons show.
- Hunger welcomes many foods and holds quiet for hours, while debt wants sweet only and returns within the hour.
- Both fears belong to the same builder, and an open door needs no courage once seen as open.
