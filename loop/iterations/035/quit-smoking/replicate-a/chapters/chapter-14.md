Chapter 14
FREE FOR GOOD

*You did not lose a friend when you stopped; you stopped feeding a hunger that never gave.*

### THE STAINED END

You remember that farewell table because you sat at it with open eyes.

We all sat there once, we smokers together. For years we lit without looking. Hand to pocket, paper to lips, flame, draw, talk, tap, draw again. This time you looked. You watched the paper burn short, the filter brown at the end, the taste turn sour by the third puff, the throat sting, the fingers mark, the grey ash lengthen and fall. You did not turn away. You finished it plain and stubbed it with joy, not sadness. That ordinary attention did what years of scolding never did.

I stood where you stand. I know what that stub means. It means the proof is no longer in these pages. It is in your mouth still your own, your nose clear, your hands with nothing to time, your chest moving without rattle on the stairs.

“What if a part of me still misses it?”

Miss what? The stain? The ash? The hurry to the door in rain? The half-leave of every meal and every story to tend fire outside while talk ran inside without you? The counting of how many left for the afternoon? The buying of two before a trip in case the shops shut? Ask plain and answer plain. If there was something to miss, why did you have to film your tongue to taste it and sour your mouth to crown it? If there was something to miss, why did you need Febreeze on coats and mints on breath and hands scrubbed and garage doors lifted to hide it from partner and kids?

There was nothing to miss. There was only hours trained to move together, and hours can be retrained by seeing.

If you live with illness, take prescribed medicine, or feel worry, speak to your clinician — I speak here of belief, not medicine.

Look back at that table once, not to mourn it but to measure it. A pinch of leaf rolled in paper with a plug to keep the leaf from the lips. Did that light tube ever steady an argument or carry a night? Or did brainwashing wrap years of story around dust and call the story a prop? You saw it small. Keep it small.

Did toast ever ask for ash to taste like toast? Did the kettle ever wait for lighting before it boiled? Did your legs ever refuse the stairs until smoke told them to move? Or did the dose only borrow the waking and the eating and the talking and call the borrowing a start, a crown, a pause?

You answered at the table. The answer holds because the seeing holds. No ember shortens the minutes now. No film lays itself over tea. No sour turn waits at the back of the throat. No sleeve smells of yesterday. No window is cracked to let the room out. The table is wiped. The tray is emptied. The coat smells of rain and shop, not of yesterday. That is not a gift given for good behaviour. That is baseline, returned because nothing now dulls it.

Stand a moment longer at that memory. Water hot. Face washed. Mouth rinsed and still your own. Look out at the street as it wakes, bins out, bus sighing, neighbour door thudding. You are in it without delay. There is no pause while you finish a dose behind the door. There is no watching the clock to fit the dose before the bus. You pick up the bag and go.

Keep that picture where you can find it. Not as a test, not as a relic, but as a measure. Whenever old hours whisper that paper once helped, put the whisper beside the stain and the sour mouth and the browned end. The whisper thins beside what eyes saw. Eyes saw paper burn to grey and leave nothing behind except a mouth wanting wash. That is what paper always left. Nothing else was ever there.

### THE LEDGER IN ONE LINE

We kept accounts for years. Harm on one side, pleasure on the other, fear doing the sums. That whole ledger was a con.

The fact is the dose never added. It took attention from food and called the theft flavour. It took calm from the nerves and lent a little back for minutes and called the lending relief. It took company from the night and stood in front of it and took the bow. It took breath from the stairs and lent a little back and called the lending a start. It took money, hours, taste, smell, closeness, and called the taking a little party.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Say it aloud and test it against any hour you lived. Morning. Did it start the day, or did it quiet the hollow unease planted by the last dose of the night before? Work. Did it crown finished work, or did the work taste louder once no ember cut it? Meal-end. Would you pour ash on pudding to crown it? Would you stir grey into tea to make it kinder? Would you spread ash on toast to improve it? Night drink. Did paper complete leisure and company, or was it only ever sneaking a ride while night and drink and company gave?

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

“Was the first light in heaven, then?”

Heaven for ninety seconds at the front, then film and sting and tighter chest on the next stair and mouth needing to wash. I know that voice because we all spoke it: the moment of lighting bright, the aftertaste grey. Was that heaven, or was that hunger pausing because hunger was fed? Would heaven need topping up within the hour? Would heaven leave you stale, breathless and chained between lights?

Ask what the hour lost and what it kept. Ash. Stain. Sour mouth. Hurry. Hiding the smell from those who do not smoke. What did it keep? Food. Drink. Talk. Lingering. Breath moving without rattle. The lingering is longer now because nothing cuts it.

That is the whole account, closed. Not balanced. Closed. There is no second column to keep. There is no small gift to weigh. There is taking, briefly quieted by more taking, until the taking is seen and stops.

Did the dose ever move one paper on the desk? Did it ever settle one bill, mend one quarrel, finish one paragraph? The problem on the desk sat untouched by presence as it sits untouched by absence. Paper never moved one paper. It only quieted the pocket for minutes while worry kept its seat. The work still waits, and you still do it, with mouth your own and head on the task.

Carry that one line with you and you carry the whole book. When doubt knocks it will come dressed as an exception. A bad day. A good night. A drink. A friend. Put the exception beside the line and watch it thin. The line held in the morning. It held at the desk. It held at meal-end. It held on the patio. It will hold wherever it is tried, because it was true wherever it was lived.

### THE CREATURE AND THE LIE

We were conned twice, and both cons are now seen through.

The first was small. A tiny physical hunger, an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug that faded within minutes and peaked for a few days and then sank. Not will. Not character. Not a mighty enemy. A fading echo from hours unfed. You do not argue with an echo. You smile and let it die unfed.

We called that echo the Nipper because naming makes it winnable. Feed the Nipper and you keep the ticking alive. Starve it and it dies. You starved it. In a week the postcards come fewer. In a month they come rarely. When they come they bring their own answer, because no belief feeds them.

Did the tug ever prove paper was your friend? Did hunger ever prove food was poison? The tug proved only that the last dose had worn off. The meter faded in minutes and asked for another coin. That is not pleasure. That is tenancy. You do not owe rent to a tug.

The second con was large. Years of story that taught you to read that little tug as proof that paper relaxes, rewards, steadies, completes. Family lighting. Mates lighting. Bright walls behind tills. Theatre of filter and mild promise. That story was the Smokescreen, and quitting was de-programming, not deprivation.

You lived inside the nicotine trap and called the inside normal. Did normal need Febreeze on coats and mints on breath and hands scrubbed and garage doors lifted to hide? Did normal need counting left for the afternoon and buying two before a trip in case shops shut? Did normal need stepping out of your own life every forty minutes to feed? Did normal need a window cracked in winter, a lighter flicked ten thousand times without hearing, a sleeve smelling of yesterday?

You also lived inside the tug-of-war of fear and called the pulling honest. Fear of failing. Fear of succeeding without identity. Both ropes belonged to the same hands. If paper calmed fear, why did fear grow across the years of smoking? If paper proved identity, whose identity was it — yours, or the factory counting doses and printing the colour to match? The rope falls because no one now holds the other end.

You left, easily, immediately and permanently, not link by link but whole. Not flat, not empty, but full, because attention is no longer taxed. Taste lasts. Talk lasts. Work lasts. The bus ride is looking out of the window, not counting to the next stop where lighting is allowed. The waiting room is magazine and clock, not pacing outside. The phone call is the call, not the dose after the call.

That ease was not luck. It was seeing. The small hunger unfed dies quickly when the large story no longer dresses it as need. The large story thins when the small hunger is felt plain for what it is. Each truth holds the other up, and together they hold you without effort. No strain keeps them. Seeing keeps them.

### THE WORDS YOU CARRY

Doubt will knock. It knocks for every reader in the first weeks. A break. A bill. A sharp word on the phone. A lit hand offering with, “one won’t hurt.” Old hours leave echoes.

Do not debate the echo. Do not weigh it. Debate keeps paper alive as if it had a case. Paper has no case left standing. Morning proved it. Shop proved it. Break proved it. Meal proved it. Night proved it.

When the thought crosses, lightly, almost fondly, answer with the lines you already own. They are not slogans. They are shorthand for hours you lived.

Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain when that offer comes. Is there a single hour since the stub that was better with ash in it? Was tea less hot? Was talk less whole? Was work less yours? Was the sky less blue without paper over it? The question answers itself. What would one more test teach that hundreds did not, except how fast the clock winds again?

Tell yourself what you saw at the table: It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you. Say it against morning, against work, against meal-end, against night. It holds in every hour because it was true in every hour. The dose took and called the taking giving. Seen plain, the giving vanishes.

Tell yourself what you saw in the body: It never fixed the itch. It caused it. The hollow unease was not life. It was withdrawal pharmacology with a short peak, not proof the dose was your real self. The sinking needle taught the blood to ask, then the puff quieted the asking for minutes and claimed the quiet as calm. You do not call a stone in the shoe a walking aid because removal brings comfort.

Tell yourself what you saw in the street and the shop: you lived inside the nicotine trap. The pack was a day’s supply box, the tube a squirt of nicotine, the factory counting doses while you counted puffs. Did that light tube ever steady an argument? Did the colour give identity, or did brainwashing wrap years of story around dust? You saw the seller behind the smoke. Keep him seen.

You ask, “Will I always have to look away?” Look straight. There is no need to look away and no need to stare. The packets are small when seen plain — a pinch of leaf rolled in paper with a plug to keep the leaf from the lips. Does the object match the weight we gave it? Do you test yourself when you walk past socks you do not need? You do not think of socks at all except to step past. Paper joins socks. Seen plain, small, priced, it has no voice unless you lend it yours.

Hold too what ease meant. You left easily, immediately and permanently. Easily because there was nothing to miss. Immediately because freedom was conferred at the stub, not earned by months. Permanently because one kept alive means all kept alive, and none kept alive means none to miss. That triad is not a boast. It is a report of what seeing does.

### THE TUG THAT NO LONGER FOOLS

Picture the whole dose watched plain when the postcard says “nice.” Not the imagined lighting bright at the front, but dry paper mouth by the third puff, filmed tongue, stung throat, grey ash, browned filter, fingers marked, chest tighter on the next stair. Is that what you want, or is that what hunger wants you to remember as want? If one at Christmas gave, why book it months ahead like a train you fear to miss? If one on a bad day steadied, why did bad days multiply across the years of smoking?

You answer inside, loud and glad. Each visit arrives pre-labelled as script. Each answer makes the next answer quicker, until the thought itself means gladness that you do not live there.

Name the tug for what it is: an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug. Nothing more. It fades within minutes. It peaks for a few days and sinks unfed because no belief feeds it. An echo proves the room was loud once, not that sound remains. Let the hand half-lift and find nothing and fall again without comment. That half-lift is not want. It is echo.

Name the feeder for what it was: the Nipper. Small, already dying, kept alive only by doses. You do not feed it to be kind. You do not argue with it to be strong. You smile and let it die. In a week the postcards come fewer. Then the hand stops half-lifting. The pocket stops being patted. The wall behind the till becomes background. Memory of tending fades like a bus route you no longer take. You know it was yours once. You do not travel it.

Name the story for what it was: the Smokescreen. Years of pairing errands until every errand had a second errand hidden inside it. Post the letter and light after. Pay the bill and light before. Buy milk and light outside because you are outside anyway. We called it pairing. It was feeding between errands. We called stepping out company while talk ran inside without us. We called hot dust and bite at the back of the throat a reward for finished work. Ask plain. If the dose crowned finished work, why does the work taste louder now without it?

Name the pulling for what it was: the tug-of-war of fear. Fear of failing and fear of succeeding, both ropes held by the same hands. Did normal need pity for the self left out? See plain who is left out of what. You hear the whole story. You taste to the bottom. You walk home with mouth still your own. Would heaven leave you stale, breathless and chained between lights? Would heaven need topping up within the hour?

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. And you already did. You kept the mind open. You finished the argument before borrowing another. You watched other smokers with clear eyes and kept your own judgment. You trusted the body to breathe and taste without being taught. You saw the seller behind the smoke. You let the favourite prove it gives nothing. You allowed no single or special one to rebuild the bottle. That is why the tug no longer fools. The belief that fed it is gone.

Do not turn the answer into a debate. Paper has no case left standing. The thought is not a summons. It is a postcard from a street you no longer live on. You smile, you answer, you turn back to the day in front of you.

### TWELVE LINES TO PHOTOGRAPH

Keep this photograph in the pocket and in the phone. No footnotes, no course to re-study. Twelve spoken lines. You lived them. You keep them.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Give this book a fair hearing and it will free you.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

3. BEGIN BY FEELING GREAT TO BE ESCAPING
There is no doom here, only freedom ahead.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
All you have to do is what this book asks.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the method, not you.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

10. MEET THE BEST CIGARETTE HEAD-ON
Let the favourite prove it gives nothing.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

Read them once in the morning and once at night until you do not need to read them. They are not rules to strain with. They are reminders of what you saw. You kept the mind open when the promise sounded too good. You smoked as normal until the vow so no dread closed the mind. You began with gladness instead of gloom. You did what the book asked, nothing more. You let this argument finish. You stopped measuring yourself by strugglers. You watched other smokers plain and kept your own judgment. You trusted tongue and nose and chest. You remembered the factory. You let the favourite fail. You allowed no single one. You closed the trap at an ordinary table with eyes open.

Ask without paper helping. If the wall gave comfort, why did comfort need topping up within the hour? If pellets helped driving, why did long drives need more pellets, not fewer? If pellets helped thinking, why did the paragraph need re-reading after the step outside? If the dose rested you, why did you return tighter and already counting to the next? Put it to yourself straight and the lines hold without effort, because hours hold them.

You will find the lines thin to one feeling. Morning tea to the bottom. Shop entered and left. Break with talk whole. Work done with head on task. Meal lingering. Night laughter heard to the middle. That feeling is not a rule kept. It is a day lived without tax. The photograph only reminds you what days already prove.

### THE ASHTRAY

Here is the last thing I have to show you, and it is not recap.

For years we pictured a full tray we had emptied by courage. Grey powder. Browned ends. Stain wiped. Will holding the lid down. That picture kept us sorry for ourselves and a little proud of missing. It made paper precious by making absence an achievement to be guarded day by day.

Look again with clear eyes. The tray was never full. The ashtray was already empty — you were the one who kept refilling it, and now you have put the habit down like a borrowed coat that was never yours.

We borrowed the coat at the first con on phoney information and wore it until we called it skin. It itched. It stank of yesterday. It sent us to the step in rain. It taxed every errand with a second errand hidden inside it. It cut talk to fit ember length. It narrowed eyes against the thin curl. It lifted the shoulder a little to coax the draw. It shortened the draw as the end warmed. We patched it with lights and milds and filters, with pellets for finished paragraphs and miles, with promises of tomorrow. We told ourselves sun and leisure and company needed paper to complete them. We told ourselves tea needed film to be strong. We told ourselves work needed interruption to be crowned.

None of it was skin. Take it off and the shoulders are still there. The arms move. The day holds itself.

“Will I have to keep remembering?”

You will remember less and less. At first the glad answer comes often because echoes are frequent. Then the hand stops half-lifting. The pocket stops being patted. The shop wall becomes background. The square becomes sky, not cue. Memory of tending fades like a bus route you no longer take. You know it was yours once. You do not travel it.

Keep nothing as a prop. No packet kept for courage. No lighter kept for luck. No last end kept in a drawer to prove you could keep it and not use it. A kept prop keeps the story alive as if paper were a friend sleeping, not litter thrown. Throw the litter. Empty the tray. Wash the coat you do own. Air the curtains. Let the rooms forget.

We washed a lot in the trap. Febreeze on coats. Mints on breath. Hands scrubbed. Garage door lifted to hide. Called it tidiness. It was tending’s hiding. Now no hiding is needed. Stand close. Speak close. Laugh with mouth open. The body was never on paper’s side. It coughed on stairs. It soured at meal-end. It woke grey. It knew, and now it shows it knows by wanting to stay as it is.

If an old hour knocks louder — argument, deadline, bills, sharp words on the phone — hear it as hour, not order. The desk problem sits there whether you tend or not. Tending never moved one paper. It only added sour to worry and stole minutes from solving. Solve, or rest from solving, or walk round the block and look at sky. The sky does not ask for coins.

Think of mornings now strung together. Cold floor, kettle, toast. Cold floor, kettle, toast. Not a chain to be broken link by link, but days to be lived one by one, each whole. Does the second morning need proof again? No. It repeats the first without effort, because nothing was endured in the first. What is not missed is not missed again.

Do not preach. Do not pity aloud. Let lightness in plain sight do what argument cannot. A friend who smokes will watch you laugh to the middle of the story without stepping out, taste to the bottom without filming, walk home without detour, and will wonder what you know. Tell them nothing until they ask. When they ask, tell them paper never gave, only took, and days hold themselves.

Look ahead with me a season. Spring errands without second errands. Summer nights without stepping out. Autumn stairs breathed to the bottom. Winter coats smelling of rain. Birthdays with candles blown and taste kept. Bad days met with head clear and hands still. Good days crowned by themselves, not by fire. None of it needs guarding. None of it needs counting. Each day repeats the proof without asking, because proof was never strain. It was absence of need.

Look at the tray once more. Empty. Wiped. No grey powder. No browned ends. No stain spreading. It was always empty until a hand refilled it. The hand no longer refills. The coat hangs on its hook by the door, borrowed, returned, not yours, not missed. Shoulders warm without it. Hands still enough to hold heat and feel it. Mouth your own to the bottom of the cup.

Say the glad line when it rises on its own, not as duty but as report. Let neighbours see no struggle to admire and no preaching to endure. The week fills itself because days were always full. Paper only stood in front and took the bow. Now the bow is withdrawn and the stage is plain: work, food, talk, night, sleep, waking. Enough. More than enough. Whole.

**SUMMARY**
- Paper did plenty to the body and breath and gave nothing to the hour; the account is closed
- The itch was made by dosing and quieted for minutes by dosing; it was never a gift above normal
- The tug was small and unfed and the story around it was large and now cleared
- Both fear ropes belonged to the trap; with neither held, the pulling stops
- The thought that crosses is echo and postcard, not order or evidence
- Twelve kept lines hold the whole method in the pocket without new rules
- The tray stands empty because nothing now refills it; the borrowed coat is laid down

Get on with enjoying your life.
