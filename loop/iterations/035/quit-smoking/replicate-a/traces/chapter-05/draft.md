Chapter 5
PARTIES, PAUSES AND MILDS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The classroom door still warm, the half-written paragraph, the grocery bags on the step, the pale pack and the mask with pinholes

*The little reward was never in the dose; the work threw the party and the mild only made you puff harder.*

### THE PARTIES YOU SWEAR YOU WOULD MISS

You have watched the desk after the row and seen the papers unmoved.

Part of you nods at that. Yes, the dose never opened the bill or mended the words. Yes, the coping was mine.

Then another voice steps forward, softer and more reasonable, and it says this:

"All right. Perhaps it does nothing for strain. But you cannot tell me it does nothing for pleasure. What about my little parties?"

I know that voice. I lived with it for years.

The morning one with the kettle. The one after the meal when the plate is cleared and the taste still sits. The one on the step when the work stops and the air is cool. The one you light because you have finished something and you want to mark it. Not to cope. To celebrate.

We all kept a private list. Mine was long. Yours will be longer because it is yours.

Ask it plainly, in your own words, and let me hear you:

"Do you mean to tell me there was no enjoyment in any of them?"

I do not mean that. There was enjoyment in those minutes. I am telling you where the enjoyment lived.

Think of the shape of your day. It is not one long grey stretch lit up by twenty bright doses. It is twenty small completions, each with its own lift, each then paid with a light as if the light made the lift.

That payment is the con.

### TEACHING, PARAGRAPHS AND GROCERIES

See one woman I knew. She could have been you.

"Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store."

Read that list again. Teaching a class and driving to the shops in the same list. A paragraph and five hundred miles. Does that look like a measured response to special pleasure? Or does it look like a stamp licked and stuck on anything finished, until finishing itself cannot feel finished without the stamp?

We did the same. We all did.

The bell goes. The classroom door shuts behind the last pupil. The noise drops. The shoulders drop. The work held, the hour went well, your body knows it before your mind names it. Hand to pack. Strike. Step to the window. Draw. And the Smokescreen says, this draw is the reward for teaching well.

The paragraph clicks. You read it back and it holds. A small clean thrill runs through you — the writer's thrill, the worker's thrill, the human thrill of a thing done right. Hand to pack. Strike. Lean back. And the story told afterwards is that the dose sealed the paragraph.

The car rolls into the drive. Bags on the step. Milk, bread, the small triumph of the errand done in rain without forgetting one item. Hand to pack before the bags are unpacked. And the errand, dull enough on paper, glows for three minutes because the gap since the last dose has closed.

Do you see the trick? The lift came first. The teaching lifted you. The paragraph lifted you. Getting home with the job done lifted you. The dose arrived second, closed its own gap, and took the bow for the lift that was already in you.

Take the dose out and what happens to the lift?

I stood at that window myself after I had stopped lighting, with the same classroom noise fading down the corridor and the same chalk on my fingers, and the lift was still there. Fuller, if anything, because there was no stale mouth laid over it and no hunt already starting for the next feeding. The work tasted of itself.

Have you ever finished something and felt the glow before you lit? Have you ever lit late, delayed by a call or a queue, and found the glow had already risen and steadied without smoke? Have you ever finished something and lit and found the dose hot and hurried and tasteless, while the pleasure of the finish stayed exactly where it was?

You lit on the bright hour because the feeder asks on schedule, bright hour or bleak hour. You glowed in the bright hour because doing holds its own joy. The dose sneaked a ride on each finish and claimed each finish as its party.

It is you that makes the party. Your skill. Your eye for the finished line. Your legs carrying the bags in. The dose stood beside the doing the way a stranger stands beside a workman and claims the wall.

THE PARTY WAS THE WORK.

### WHAT TASTE? WHAT PAUSE?

"But the taste. Surely the taste is real. And the pause. Surely the pause is mine."

Let us look at both without sneer, because this is where pleasure-keepers defend hardest.

What taste?

The first draws you ever took were foul. You coughed. Your eyes watered. Your throat burned. No child picks up a dose and says, what lovely flavour. We learned to call the burn taste the way a prisoner learns to call a cell home.

Watch yourself now, honestly. The rushed one on the stairs you do not taste. The wet one in the rain while others stay warm inside. The habit smokes lit without memory between tasks. Did those have taste? Or did they pass hot and bitter while you kept typing with one hand?

Even the one you swear you taste — after the meal, with coffee, on the step at dusk — what are you tasting? The meal still on the tongue. The coffee still warm. The evening air on the face. The loosening after hunger answered. Lift the dose out and the tongue still knows food. Non-smokers finish the same plate and sigh at the same fullness without once thinking a tube of shredded leaf would improve the flavour.

Ask your own mouth:

If doses tasted good, why do you spray and chew and Febreeze the hell out of myself after each one? Why does the morning mouth taste stale and sour from last night's pleasure? Why does the kiss after lighting turn away? Does real flavour leave that trail?

The taste story collapses the same way every time. We masked a bad taste with hunger paused, then called hunger paused a good taste.

And the pause?

I loved the pause. The step away. The hands freed from the task. The eyes on distance. The breath drawn deeper. Of course you love it. Any hard worker loves to stop and breathe.

But did the dose give you the pause, or did it sell you back a pause it had first stolen?

A non-smoker stops. He puts the pen down. He stands. He stretches. He takes air to the kerb and watches the street. No strike, no ash, no counting of minutes until he can slip out again. His pause belongs to him from first breath to last.

Your pause belonged to the feeder. You could not take it until you could feed. You cut the meal short to get to it. You left the table to stand alone. You left warm company to stand in rain. You watched the clock in meetings not for the end of work but for the start of feeding. And when you at last stood out there, the relief of the gap closing felt like the relief of resting.

It was not rest added. It was want paused.

Take twenty pauses a day and lace each with hunting, hiding, stale fingers, burning throat and the faint edginess between, and call the lace pleasure. That is what we did. Pull the lace out and the pause remains — clean, whole, yours — without the leash.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

### THE PALE PACK THAT PROMISED LESS

Then there is the pale pack. The mild. The light. The one with the white filter and the airy name that sounds like sense.

"I know the full ones grip me. So I smoke the milds. Less in, less harm, same little pleasure."

I hear the prudence in that. I felt it myself when I switched down and told my wife I was being careful.

Look at the object in your palm, not the promise on the foil.

A mild is not a gentler dose. It is the same tube with pinholes pricked around the filter, tiny vent holes meant to draw in empty air with the smoke when a machine puffs it in a laboratory. On paper the machine reads lower numbers. In a human mouth the story runs otherwise.

Put a gas mask on a man and prick pinholes in its side to let clean air leak in. Tell him the air inside is now milder. Then watch what his body does when he needs air. He presses closer. He sucks harder. He seals the leaks with lips and fingers without knowing he does it, because hunger does not read numbers. It reads want.

That is what human mouths do on vented filters. Lips close over the holes. Fingers cover them. The chest pulls deeper. The puff runs longer. The count creeps up because the throat waits for the familiar thump that says the Nipper has been fed. Not every pair of lips covers them exactly the same way, and I will not insult you by pretending all smokers puff to one clock. But human smoking defeated the machine on its own ground — the lower reading was machine air, not your intake.

Ask the questions whose honest answers close this door.

If milds gave less, why did you smoke as many or more after you switched? If they gripped less, why was the hunt between them just as sharp? Have you ever counted the deeper draws you take on a mild to find the throat-hit you miss, the way you turn up the volume when the radio fades? Did you switch to be free, or did you switch to keep feeding while feeling careful?

You know the answer because your fingers know it.

The pale pack did not loosen the chain. It painted the chain pale and left you to pull harder to feel held. The factory counted doses while you counted taste.

Does that look like help? A help that must be sucked harder to feel like help is not help. It is hunger taught to work for its feed.

### THE THEFT STOOD IN FULL

Step back and see the three thefts together, because together they leave no exception standing.

The reward theft: the work threw the party and the dose stole the credit.

The taste theft: the meal and the air and the rest gave the flavour and the pause, and the dose laid stale over both and called the stale pleasure.

The mild theft: the holes promised less and your own lips and fingers, without malice and without knowledge, made sure you took what the hunger asked.

Each theft uses the same glue. A real lift — finished work, full mouth, honest rest, the pride of a thing done — arrives. The gap since the last feeding closes at the same minute. The Smokescreen glues the lift to the closing and says, this came from lighting.

Pull them apart once and they stay apart.

Think of your own week and time the glow, not the story. The classroom glow rose before the match struck. The paragraph thrill ran before the lean-back. The grocery satisfaction settled while the bags were still on the step. The meal pleasure sat on the tongue before the chair scraped back. The evening air cooled the face whether smoke crossed it or not.

The dose never added one drop. It only arrived at the bright minute, paused its own whine inside the bright minute, and signed its name on brightness that was never its to sign.

I want to meet your last guard before it speaks, because it will speak here.

"Even if all that is true, why should I not keep the little ones? The after-meal one. The pause one. Surely one small pleasure can stay."

Would you keep a thief in the house because he steals only small spoons? Would you keep pinholes in your mask because the air feels milder while you suck harder?

One kept credit keeps the belief alive that lighting gives something. While that belief lives, the whine between doses will be read as proof you need the pleasure, and the pleasure will be read as proof the whine is worth it. The nicotine trap turns on that single kept cord. Pull it and the lamp goes out. Leave it and the room stays wired.

You do not need the stamp to finish the letter. You do not need the leash to take the air. Your mouth knows food. Your lungs know breath. Your work knows joy. Those powers were yours before the first foul draw you forced down to look grown, and they stayed yours through every year you paid to pause a want the last payment had renewed.

See the taking clearly and the keeping loses its charm.

**SUMMARY**
- The glow after finished work belonged to the work, the dose only closed its own gap at the same minute and took the credit
- Teaching, paragraphs and errands each carry their own lift, lighting added no taste and no rest to them
- The flavour you defend is food and air and hunger paused, not smoke, and the stale trail proves it
- Milds read lower on machines because vent holes dilute the puff, human lips and fingers close those holes and pull harder to feed the same want
- With reward and taste and mild seen as taking, no small exception remains worth keeping
