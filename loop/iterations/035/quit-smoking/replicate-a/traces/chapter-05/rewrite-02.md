Chapter 5
PARTIES, PAUSES AND MILDS

*The little reward was never in the dose; the work threw the party and the mild only made you puff harder.*

### THE PARTIES YOU SWEAR YOU WOULD MISS

You have watched the desk after the row and seen the papers unmoved.

Part of you nods at that. Yes, the dose never opened the bill or mended the words. Yes, the coping was mine. You felt that seeing land because you lived it. The hand reached, the match struck, the minute went quiet, and the trouble sat exactly where you left it.

Then another voice steps forward, softer and more reasonable, and it says this:

"All right. Perhaps it does nothing for strain. But you cannot tell me it does nothing for pleasure. What about my little parties?"

I know that voice. I lived with it for years.

Not the desperate lighting in a row. The chosen lighting. The morning one with the kettle when the house is still quiet and the day has not yet asked anything of you. The one after the meal when the plate is cleared and the taste still sits on the tongue and you lean back and tell yourself you have earned this. The one on the step when the work stops and the air is cool on the face. The one you light because you have finished something and you want to mark it. Not to cope. To celebrate in your own small way.

We all kept a private list. Mine was long. Yours will be longer because it is yours.

Ask it plainly, in your own words, and let me hear you:

"Do you mean to tell me there was no enjoyment in any of them?"

I do not mean that. There was enjoyment in those minutes. I am telling you where the enjoyment lived.

Think of the shape of your day. It is not one long grey stretch lit up by twenty bright doses. It is twenty small completions, each with its own lift, each then paid with a light as if the light made the lift.

That payment is the con.

We smoked between doings and called the between the reward. We finished and we lit. We paused and we lit. We ate and we lit. We talked and we lit. We drove and we lit. Each finish had a glow of its own, and each glow was signed over to smoke before we had time to taste whose glow it was.

I want you to keep the glow while we look. I am not asking you to call your work dull or your meal flat or your pause empty. Your work was bright. Your meal was full. Your pause was sweet. That brightness is precisely why the theft worked. A thief does not steal from an empty house. He waits until the house is warm and full, then slips in at the bright minute and signs his name on what was already shining.

You say little parties. I hear you. I used those exact words myself. The stick after the paragraph. The ciggies on the balcony after the shops. The habit smokes that stitched the day together. Let us pull one thread and see who sewed it.

Did you ever finish something hard and feel the lift rise before the hand moved? That half-second when the shoulders drop and the breath comes out and you know it held? That is the party starting without smoke. Did you ever get delayed, held by a call or a queue or a child tugging at the sleeve, and find the satisfaction still there ten minutes later, steady and whole, with no dose in it? That is the party continuing without smoke. Did you ever light at the bright minute and find the dose hot and hurried and bitter, while the pleasure of the finish stayed exactly where it was, untouched by the puffing? That is the party proving it never needed smoke.

We never checked because we never separated them. Finish and light arrived as one gesture. Hand to pack became part of finishing, the way tapping the desk became part of thinking. The Smokescreen glued the two together and said, this draw is what makes finishing feel finished.

Pull them apart once and they stay apart.

### TEACHING, PARAGRAPHS AND GROCERIES

See one woman I knew. She could have been you. She could have been me.

"Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store."

Read that list again. Teaching a class and driving to the shops in the same list. A paragraph and five hundred miles. A story nursed for months and a carton of milk. Does that look like a measured response to special pleasure? Or does it look like a stamp licked and stuck on anything finished, until finishing itself cannot feel finished without the stamp?

We did the same. We all did.

The bell goes. The classroom door shuts behind the last pupil. The noise drops down the corridor. Chalk dust hangs in the light. Your shoulders drop. The hour held. The difficult boy understood at last. The plan worked. Your body knows it before your mind names it. Pride, tiredness, warmth, the clean ache of work done well. Hand to pack. Strike. Step to the window. Draw. And the story told afterwards is that the dose sealed the teaching.

The paragraph clicks. You read it back and it holds. The sentence you hunted for an hour sits right at last. A small clean thrill runs through you — the writer's thrill, the worker's thrill, the human thrill of a thing done right. You lean back. The neck loosens. The eyes soften on the page. Hand to pack. Strike. Lean back further. And the story told afterwards is that the dose sealed the paragraph.

The car rolls into the drive. Bags on the step. Milk, bread, the small triumph of the errand done in rain without forgetting one item. The keys still in the hand. The door still to be opened. The legs tired in a good way. Hand to pack before the bags are unpacked. Three minutes of puffing on the step while the milk warms. And the errand, dull enough on paper, glows because the gap since the last dose has closed at the same minute as the coming home.

Do you see the trick? The lift came first. The teaching lifted you. The paragraph lifted you. Getting home with the job done lifted you. The dose arrived second, closed its own gap, and took the bow for the lift that was already in you.

Time it in your own week and you will catch it red-handed.

The classroom glow rose before the match struck. It was in the shutting door and the fading noise and the chalk on the fingers. The paragraph thrill ran before the lean-back. It was in the rereading and the nodding and the quiet yes under the breath. The grocery satisfaction settled while the bags were still on the step. It was in the rain on the coat and the list completed and the house reached. The dose never added one drop to those lifts. It only arrived at the bright minute, paused its own whine inside the bright minute, and signed its name on brightness that was never its to sign.

Take the dose out and what happens to the lift?

I stood at that window myself after I had stopped lighting, with the same classroom noise fading down the corridor and the same chalk on my fingers, and the lift was still there. Fuller, if anything, because there was no stale mouth laid over it and no hunt already starting for the next feeding and no need to leave the room to stand apart. The work tasted of itself. I stood over a finished paragraph with coffee gone cold and read it twice and felt the same writer's thrill from scalp to fingertips, with a tongue that still knew coffee and a chest that did not need to cough to mark the moment. I carried bags in rain up the same steps and felt the same small triumph, with hands that stayed on the bags instead of hunting for the pack.

Have you ever finished something and felt the glow before you lit? Have you ever lit late and found the glow had already risen and steadied without smoke? Have you ever finished something and lit and found the dose tasteless while the pleasure of the finish stayed exactly where it was?

You lit on the bright hour because the feeder asks on schedule, bright hour or bleak hour. You glowed in the bright hour because doing holds its own joy. The dose sneaked a ride on each finish and claimed each finish as its party.

It is you that makes the party. Your skill. Your eye for the finished line. Your legs carrying the bags in. Your voice holding the class. The dose stood beside the doing the way a stranger stands beside a workman and claims the wall.

THE PARTY WAS THE WORK.

### THE PELLET AFTER EVERYTHING

Look closer at the pellet chain, because it explains why the parties feel so many and so necessary.

A pellet is a small hard button pressed after every correct turn in a maze. Press, eat, run. Press, eat, run. Soon the running is not for the turning but for the button. We turned our days into that maze.

Finish the class, press. Finish the paragraph, press. Finish the call, press. Finish the drive, press. Finish the washing up, press. Finish the argument, press. Finish the wait, press. Twenty finishes, twenty presses, until the day cannot feel shaped without the press marking its edges.

Ask yourself when you started needing a marker to feel finished. Did the child need a dose to feel the game finished? Did you need one when you first taught well or wrote well or drove well, before the coughing weeks when you forced the first foul draws down to look grown? No. Finishing felt finished on its own. The run felt complete at the wall, not at the button.

Then the trap taught a second hunger that fades on schedule. Every hour or so the faint edginess stirs, the little tug whines for its squirt, and any finish that lands near the whine gets glued to the pause of the whine. The pause feels bright because the whine was dark. The finish feels brighter because the pause lands inside it. Soon we cannot tell which brightness is which.

That is why the list grows so absurdly long. Teaching a class and driving to the shops. A story and a paragraph and five hundred miles and milk. When the button can be pressed after anything, it will be pressed after everything. Not because everything is special, but because the whine returns after everything and asks to be paused.

Watch the chain across one ordinary day without changing anything you do, only watching.

Morning kettle. Hands around the mug. The house quiet. The first draw on lungs empty since night, burning, harsh, yet credited as the start of pleasure. Was the quiet less quiet before the match? Or was the quiet already there, in the warmth and the steam and the first light, and the dose laid harsh over it and called the harsh the start?

Mid-morning paragraph. Eyes tired. Back stiff. The line at last right. The lean-back. The strike. The lean-back would have eased the back without smoke. The rereading would have pleased without smoke. The eyes would have rested looking at distance without smoke. What did the puff add except heat and bite and the pause of a want that had been building since the kettle dose?

Midday shops. List done. Rain easing. Bags heavy. The step. The light before unpacking. The legs would have been glad to stop without smoke. The house would have smelled of itself without stale laid over it. The small pride of forgetting nothing would have sat warm without a tube. What did the dose bring that the bags and the door and the dry hall did not already bring?

Evening plate. Food done. Tongue still full. Chair pushed back. The move to the door to stand apart. The meal would have settled without smoke. The company would have stayed warm without your chair empty. The tongue would have kept flavour longer without ash laid over it. What did standing apart add to togetherness?

Each time the same answer, from your own mouth if you time it honestly. The doing gave. The pausing of the whine rode alongside the giving and took the credit.

Once you see the pellet as a second hunger hitched to real finishes, the chain loses its charm. You do not need a button to know the maze was run well. Your legs know. Your eye knows. Your tired back and full mouth and quiet house know. Those knowings were yours before the first foul draw and stayed yours through every year you paid to pause a want the last payment had renewed.

### WHAT TASTE?

"But the taste. Surely the taste is real."

Let us look at that without sneer, because this is where pleasure-keepers defend hardest.

What taste?

The first draws you ever took were foul. You coughed. Your eyes watered. Your throat burned. No child picks up a dose and says, what lovely flavour. No beginner sighs at the perfume. We choked, we spat, we felt dizzy and sick, and we went back because the others laughed and because we wanted to look grown, not because the tongue rejoiced. We learned to call the burn taste the way a prisoner learns to call a cell home — by living in it until the nose stops noticing.

Watch yourself now, honestly, dose by dose, not story by story.

The rushed one on the stairs you do not taste. You drag while climbing, hot and hurried, and reach the landing with no memory of flavour, only of having fed. The wet one in the rain while others stay warm inside, water on the hands, ash hissing — do you taste that? Or do you endure it to close the gap? The habit smokes lit without memory between tasks, typed with one hand while answering with the other — where is taste in those? They pass hot and bitter while you keep working.

Even the one you swear you taste — after the meal, with coffee, on the step at dusk — what are you tasting? The meal still on the tongue. The coffee still warm. The evening air on the face. The loosening after hunger answered. The body settling after work. Lift the dose out and the tongue still knows food. Non-smokers finish the same plate and sigh at the same fullness and linger over the same coffee without once thinking a tube of shredded leaf would improve the flavour. Children finish cake and grin with crumbs on the mouth and ask for nothing to be burned over it.

Ask your own mouth, not the old story:

If doses tasted good, why do you spray and chew and Febreeze the hell out of myself after each one? Why does the morning mouth taste stale and sour from last night's pleasure? Why does the kiss after lighting turn away? Why do the curtains and the coat and the car hold a smell you scrub on weekends? Does real flavour leave that trail?

The taste story collapses the same way every time. We masked a bad taste with hunger paused, then called hunger paused a good taste.

I hear the next whisper because I whispered it.

"You do not understand. I like the throat-hit. I like the scratch and the bite."

I understand. I liked it too. But ask when you learned to like it. Did you like the scratch on day one, when it made you cough? Or did you learn to read the scratch as the signal that the squirt had arrived, the way a bell becomes liked because food follows it? The scratch means the Nipper has been fed. The bite means the whine will go quiet for draws. We do not love ash. We love the pause the ash announces, and we call the announcer the pleasure.

Take the whine away and the announcer is only harsh air. Put the whine back and any harshness that pauses it will be called pleasure, whether it is smoke or pepper or ice water held too long. That is not taste. That is a hunger applauding its own pausing.

Your tongue knows real taste. Soup after hunger. Water after thirst. The first bite after a long morning. Those flavours need no teaching and leave no sour trail. Smoke never joined that company. It only stood beside food and drink at the bright minute and borrowed their clothes.

### WHAT PAUSE?

"And the pause. Surely the pause is mine. You cannot take the pause from me."

I loved the pause. The step away. The hands empty of the task. The eyes on distance. The breath drawn deeper. Of course you love it. Any hard worker loves to stop and breathe. Any parent loves two minutes on the step while the house still hums. Any driver loves to stand and stretch the legs.

But did the dose give you the pause, or did it sell you back a pause it had first stolen?

A non-smoker stops. He puts the pen down. He stands. He stretches. He takes air to the kerb and watches the street. He holds his mug and looks at nothing. No strike, no ash, no counting of minutes until he can slip out again, no watching the clock in meetings for the start of feeding. His pause belongs to him from first breath to last.

Your pause belonged to the feeder.

You could not take it until you could feed. You cut the meal short to get to it. You left the table to stand alone while others stayed and talked. You left warm company to stand in rain. You left the desk to hunt for corners. You watched the clock not for the end of work but for the start of feeding. You measured distance in where you could light. You chose coats with pockets for the pack. You chose seats near doors. You slipped, you hid, you excused yourself, you stood apart — all to take a pause that was supposed to be yours by right.

And when you at last stood out there, the closing of the gap felt like resting. It was not rest added. It was want paused.

Take twenty pauses a day and lace each with hunting, hiding, stale fingers, burning throat, coughing in the morning, washing the hands, spraying the room, and the faint edginess between, and call the lace pleasure. That is what we did. Pull the lace out and the pause remains — clean, whole, yours — without the leash.

Look at the pause you praise most. The after-meal one. The plate cleared, the body full, the talk low. You rise, you step out, you leave the talk to take the dose. What happens at the table while you stand out there? The talk continues without you. The child tells the funny part while your chair is empty. The friend pours more coffee you will drink half-cold. You return with a mouth that overpowers the coffee and fingers that carry the smell into the cloth. Did the dose deepen the meal? Or did it cut the meal short, remove you from its close, and lay stale over the tongue that had just known flavour?

Look at the work pause you praise. The paragraph done, the eyes tired, the need to look far. You could have stood, stretched, rolled the shoulders, looked at sky, drunk water, walked to the window and back. Five minutes, blood moving, eyes resting, mind settling. Instead you stood in one spot sucking heat, shoulders hunched against wind, eyes on the butt, mind on when the next one can come. Did that rest the eyes? Did that loosen the back? Or did it narrow the break to a single repeated gesture while the body asked for movement and air?

Look at the step-away you praise in strain. The row still warm, the need to cool before the next word. You slip out to light and call the minutes cooling. But the cooling would have come from stepping away, from breathing, from letting the blood settle, from choosing not to wound. The stepping away did the work. The lighting only paused the second hunger inside the first trouble, then added stale and guilt to the return.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

### THE PALE PACK THAT PROMISED LESS

Then there is the pale pack. The mild. The light. The one with the white filter and the airy name that sounds like sense.

"I know the full ones grip me. So I smoke the milds. Less in, less harm, same little pleasure."

I hear the prudence in that. I felt it myself when I switched down and told my wife I was being careful. It sounds like a compromise a sensible man can keep. Less smoke, less bite, less chain, same party.

Look at the object in your palm, not the promise on the foil.

A mild is not a gentler dose. It is the same tube with pinholes pricked around the filter, tiny vent holes meant to draw in empty air with the smoke when a machine puffs it in a laboratory. On paper the machine reads lower numbers. The needle moves less. The print on the pack can speak of lightness. In a human mouth the story runs otherwise.

Put a mask on a man and prick pinholes in its side to let clean air leak in. Tell him the air inside is now milder. Then watch what his body does when he needs air. He presses closer. He sucks harder. He seals the leaks with lips and fingers without knowing he does it, because hunger does not read numbers. It reads want. He will not tell you he sealed them. He will tell you the mask feels thin and he must pull to get anything from it. That pulling is the sealing made visible.

That is what human mouths do on vented filters. Lips close over the holes. Fingers cover them where the hand holds. The chest pulls deeper. The puff runs longer. The count creeps up because the throat waits for the familiar thump that says the little feeder has been fed. I will not insult you by pretending all mouths do it to one clock or to one measure. They do not. No two smokers hold quite the same way, and no single puff can be timed for every throat. But human smoking defeated the machine on its own ground — the lower reading was machine air, not your intake. The holes that dilute the laboratory puff are closed by living lips that want the squirt.

Ask the questions whose honest answers close this door.

If milds gave less, why did you smoke as many or more after you switched? If they gripped less, why was the hunt between them just as sharp, the hand just as quick to the pack after the meal, the edginess just as loud in a long meeting? Have you ever counted the deeper draws you take on a mild to find the throat-hit you miss, the way you turn up the volume when the radio fades, the way you drink longer when the cup is watered? Did you switch to loosen the chain, or did you switch to keep feeding while feeling careful?

You know the answer because your fingers know it.

The pale pack did not loosen the chain. It painted the chain pale and left you to pull harder to feel held. The factory counted doses while you counted taste. You paid the same, you hunted the same, you stood out in the same rain, you carried the same smell home and sprayed the same curtains — only now with the added story that you were being prudent.

Does that look like help? A help that must be sucked harder to feel like help is not help. It is hunger taught to work for its feed.

And mark this, because the Smokescreen will try to twist it. I am not telling you the full ones are the honest choice or the safer corner. There is no honest corner in the trap. I am telling you the mild promise is a mask with pinholes, and your own mouth, without malice and without knowledge, closed those pinholes to get what the hunger asked. To see that is not to choose another pack. It is to see that no pack ever gave less or more — each only fed the same whine and renewed it.

Think of the morning mild on empty lungs. Pale foil, clean print, the suggestion of lightness. The first draw still burns. The head still turns. The cough still comes if you pull deep. Where is mildness in that? Think of the after-meal mild you praise as delicate. Delicate beside what? Beside the full one that scratched more? That is like praising a smaller stone in the shoe because it bruises less. The shoe still holds the stone. The foot still limps to the next removal.

The holes did not make the dose kind. They made the machine read kind while the man pulled hard.

### THE THEFT STOOD IN FULL

Step back and see the three thefts together, because together they leave no exception standing.

The reward theft: the work threw the party and the dose stole the credit. Teaching lifted you. The paragraph thrilled you. The errand steadied you. The meal filled you. The pause rested you. Each real lift arrived on its own legs, and the gap closing at the same minute was glued to it and called the cause.

The taste theft: the meal and the air and the rest gave the flavour and the pause, and the dose laid stale over both and called the stale pleasure. The tongue knew food. The nose knew evening. The lungs knew depth. Smoke added heat and bite and ash, then claimed the food and the evening and the depth as its gifts.

The mild theft: the holes promised less and your own lips and fingers, without malice and without knowledge, made sure you took what the hunger asked. The laboratory read low. The throat read want. Want won.

Each theft uses the same glue. A real lift — finished work, full mouth, honest rest, the pride of a thing done — arrives. The gap since the last feeding closes at the same minute. The Smokescreen glues the lift to the closing and says, this came from lighting.

Pull them apart once and they stay apart.

Think of your own week and time the glow, not the story. The classroom glow rose before the match struck. The paragraph thrill ran before the lean-back. The grocery satisfaction settled while the bags were still on the step. The meal pleasure sat on the tongue before the chair scraped back. The evening air cooled the face whether smoke crossed it or not. The pause opened the chest whether a dose burned in it or not.

The dose never added one drop. It only arrived at the bright minute, paused its own whine inside the bright minute, and signed its name on brightness that was never its to sign.

I want to meet your last guard before it speaks, because it will speak here.

"Even if all that is true, why should I not keep the little ones? The after-meal one. The pause one. Surely one small pleasure can stay."

Would you keep a thief in the house because he steals only small spoons? Would you keep pinholes in your mask because the air feels milder while you suck harder? Would you keep a button that marks finishes you already know are finished?

One kept credit keeps the belief alive that lighting gives something. While that belief lives, the whine between doses will be read as proof you need the pleasure, and the pleasure will be read as proof the whine is worth it. The nicotine trap turns on that single kept cord. It does not need all your reasons. It needs one. Give it the after-meal one and it will rebuild the whole chain from that one, the way a single kept photograph can rebuild a whole longing.

You do not need the stamp to finish the letter. You do not need the leash to take the air. Your mouth knows food. Your lungs know breath. Your work knows joy. Your pause knows rest. Those powers were yours before the first foul draw you forced down to look grown, and they stayed yours through every year you paid to pause a want the last payment had renewed.

See the taking clearly and the keeping loses its charm. The parties were never in the pack. They were in the teaching and the paragraph and the bags on the step and the plate and the evening and the hands that did the work. The mild never made the chain kind. It only made the pulling harder and the story softer.

Let that stand as settled fact in you while we finish the hearing. Do not borrow another argument in the middle to soften it. Do not test it with one more careful lighting to be sure. Do not ask a friend who still lights whether your tasting was true. Let the seeing settle to its full weight. The method works as a whole because the trap works as a whole, and a whole seeing needs only a fair hearing to the end.
