Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The pack weighed in the palm, the filter held to the light, the two fears pulling in the night chest

*You did not grow this hunger like a tooth — it was packed for you, and so was the fear of living without it.*

### THE BOX IN YOUR PALM

You lived a morning with clear eyes. You ate and stayed at the table. You stood ten minutes with nothing burning and found the day was already full.

Yet the chest still tightens at night. Not the tug now. The thought behind the tug.

Take the pack in your hand and look at it as if you never saw it before. Twenty white tubes in a coloured box. Cellophane crackle. The flip top. The foil. The brand name written like a signature. We carried it like a wallet, counted it at midnight, patted the pocket to be sure it was there before keys, before money.

What is that box?

We told ourselves it was pleasure in a box. Company. Calm. A little luxury we had chosen.

Hold it longer. It is a day's supply box. Twenty doses measured for twenty falls in the drug, each fall timed a few minutes apart, each dose built to pause its own fall for three minutes and call the pause a gift. The factory did not pack flavour. In their own planning rooms they wrote it plain: the cigarette is the package, the product inside is nicotine. The tube is the squirt. The puff is the squirt delivered. The pack is the day counted out in advance.

Once you see it you cannot unsee it. That box is the nicotine trap in your pocket.

Who packed your day for you? Did you ever sit down and design a hunger that returns every forty minutes and needs paper and fire to quiet? Did you choose stale mouth in the morning and short breath on the stairs and hiding in the garage with mints? We did not choose it the way we choose bread or a coat. We smoked the first foul draws to look grown, coughed, persisted because everyone lit, and the drug closed the lid behind us. Then the sellers kept the lid closed with pictures of beaches and laughter and strong men on horses, until closing felt like wanting.

I am not angry at you. I was conned with you. I am angry at the hand that packed the box and sold the packing as pleasure. Look at that hand with clear eyes and you begin your escape from what it built.

### THE THEATRE OF MILD

Hold one cigarette up to the light.

White filter. Cork-coloured ring. Tiny holes round the paper if you squint. The word mild or light on the box. Smooth. Low-tar. We believed there was a cleaner smoke, a gentler dose for the careful smoker.

"I smoke lights, so I take less," you say. "The filter catches it. It tastes lighter, so it must be lighter."

I believed that while I coughed just as hard. Let me put it against your own mouth.

Have you ever smoked a mild and felt cleaner five minutes later, or did the mouth still taste stale and the chest still tighten? Does the non-smoker beside you breathe free air while you step out for the careful kind, or do you both know you stepped out to feed the same hunger? When the taste felt light, did you puff a little deeper, hold a little longer, draw to the butt to get the quiet you paid for?

The machine smoked it light. A metal mouth puffed neat puffs in a laboratory and measured less on paper. A human mouth is not metal. Lips and fingers cover the vent holes without knowing, lungs pull harder for the dose they miss, the body seeks the level it was taught to seek. That is why the theatre was built — holes you cover while you puff, a filter that stains brown to look useful, a word like mild to keep the careful smoker smoking.

Not every mouth covers in exactly the same way, and I speak of belief here, not medicine. If you feel ill, speak to your doctor. The point stands without a laboratory: the theatre did not make the dose gentle. It made the con gentle enough to stay in.

Did the filter ever filter the need, or did it only filter your doubt?

The need stayed. The doubt went quiet. That was its job.

That story is the Smokescreen talking.

### WHAT IF YOU FAIL

Now the first rope.

We have lived mornings and meals without signing them to smoke. The mind says, yes, but what if I cannot stay stopped? What if I am the one it does not work for? What if stress hits, drink comes, night patio laughs, and I fall?

"I have failed twenty times," you say. "Patches. Gum. White knuckles through January. What makes this time different? I will fail again and feel worse for hoping."

I hear you. I counted midnights and hid packs and promised children with tears and lit by ten. Shame sat beside every failure and said, you are weak.

Let me ask you who failed what.

Did you fail, or did the method you were given fail you? Did you ever fail to lock a door for lack of character, or did you simply use the wrong key twenty times? Have you ever watched a man try to hold his breath till the urge passes and called him weak for breathing? He fought a hunger the last dose planted while believing the hunger proved he needed the dose. No will held against that lie because the lie made the fight endless.

There is no test of strength here. There is only a trick seen or unseen. Seen, the tug is a small grumble leaving the body. Unseen, the same grumble is read as proof you cannot live without the box. The fear of failing is not your nature speaking. It is the con speaking through your memory of fights you were never meant to win.

What if the past proves nothing about you and everything about the key you were handed?

### WHO WILL YOU BE WITHOUT IT

Then the second rope pulls the other way.

Not fear you will fail. Fear you will succeed and lose yourself.

"Smoking is me," you say. "The break with the lads. The laugh with wine. The pause after love and food. The hand doing something while I think. Without it I will be flat. Boring. A stranger at my own table. I will watch them out on the patio and feel left out forever."

We all whispered that. I thought my wit lived in the ember, my calm lived in the draw, my belonging lived in the lit circle outside the pub.

Ask whose voice that is.

Were you born with a tube between your lips? Did you laugh less at eight, love less at seventeen before the first pack, work worse before the breaks were chained to flame? Does the non-smoker at your table tell fewer stories, taste less, belong less, because no ash falls from him? Have you ever stayed to the end of a meal without leaving and found the talk thinner, or did it run longer and warmer because no whine pulled you to the step?

The cigarette never gave you a self. It borrowed your best minutes — night, drink, friendship, done work — and signed its name on them until unsigned minutes felt naked. Take the signature away and the minutes remain. The laugh is the laugh. The night air is the night air. The pause is the pause. What you call identity is only habit wearing your clothes.

Would you call a man chained to a radiator warmer for the chain?

### PULLED BOTH WAYS

Feel both ropes now.

Fail and you stay a smoker, shamed. Succeed and you become a stranger, deprived. Damned to smoke, damned to stop. The chest pulls left and right through the night and calls the pulling proof the choice is hard.

It is not hard. It is rigged.

Who holds the left rope? The belief you cannot live without the dose. Who holds the right rope? The belief life without the dose is empty. Both beliefs were packed in the same box by the same hand. The fear of failing keeps you lighting. The fear of succeeding keeps you from seeing there is nothing to miss. Each fear feeds the other, tug against tug, while the drug ticks underneath and takes the coin.

We lived inside that pulling for years and called it our torn nature. It was never our nature. That pulling both ways is the tug-of-war of fear.

See the geometry plain. A tug-of-war needs two sides. Here both ends are held by the trap. The left says you are too weak to leave. The right says you are too empty to leave. Cut either belief and the other falls slack, because neither rope was ever tied to you. They were tied to the box that taught you to want it and taught you to dread unwanting it.

When the next wave comes, watch it as a witness. Emptiness under the ribs. Edge behind the eyes. Thoughts shouting fail, shouting flat. Say to it, you are not me, you are the echo of packing. Drink water. Move. Look far. The wave slaps, then thins. Each unfed wave is lower because no belief feeds it now.

You do not have to win the pull. You only have to see who is pulling and drop your end, and your escape begins the second you do.

### WHAT STAYING COSTS

I will not leave you with mist. Look at the price of staying, because staying has a price and it is paid daily.

Morning mouth stale before tea. Throat faintly raw before talk. Stairs climbed with a hand on the rail while the friend breathes easy beside you. Clothes washed and still sour by noon. Money burned for the privilege of burning. Skin grey in the mirror. Laugh cut by cough. Children kissed with turned head. Love paused for the step outside in rain. Nights shortened. Mornings narrowed. Years counted in packs.

That is not a lecture. That is the room we lived in. Morning after morning that leaves you stale, breathless and chained.

I lay it flat because it is true, and I tell you in the same breath not to stop from fright. If fear drove you, you would still believe the dose was worth fearing to lose. I want no shivering convert counting days. I want you seeing the box did plenty to you and nothing for you, and feeling the weight slide because there is nothing to miss. If your chest is ill or your dread is heavy, speak to your doctor for your body — I speak here to the belief that kept you paying.

Have you ever paid that daily price and received one true gift back, or did each dose only pause the want the last dose planted and leave the stain? Does the non-smoker pay it with you, or does he walk the same stairs, eat the same meal, stand the same break, and keep clean mouth and full breath while you feed the meter?

Staying costs everything and gives nothing. Leaving costs nothing and returns everything the box signed its name on.

**SUMMARY**
- The want was packed for you as daily doses, not grown in you as nature
- Mild and filter theatre kept doubt quiet without removing the hunger
- Past failures proved the key was wrong, not that you were weak
- The self you fear losing was your own minutes signed by smoke
- Both fears pull for the same seller, and seeing that loosens them
- Staying keeps a daily cost with no true gift behind it
