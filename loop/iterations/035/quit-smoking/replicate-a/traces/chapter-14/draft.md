Chapter 14
FREE FOR GOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the stained end on the farewell table. Twelve kept lines for the pocket. The empty ashtray and the borrowed coat.

*You did not lose a friend when you stopped; you stopped feeding a hunger that never gave.*

### THE STAINED END

You remember that farewell table with its yellow stain and grey ash because you sat at it with open eyes.

We all sat there once, we smokers together. For years we lit without looking. This time you looked. You watched the paper burn short, the filter brown, the taste turn sour at the third puff, the throat sting, the fingers mark. You did not turn away. You finished it plain and stubbed it with joy, not sadness. That ordinary attention did what years of scolding never did.

I stood where you stand. I know what that stub means. It means the proof is no longer in these pages. It is in your mouth still your own, your nose clear, your hands with nothing to time.

“What if a part of me still misses it?”

Miss what? The stain? The ash? The hurry to the door in rain? The half-leave of every meal and every story to tend fire outside while talk ran inside without you? Ask plain and answer plain. If there was something to miss, why did you have to film your tongue to taste it and sour your mouth to crown it?

There was nothing to miss. There was only hours trained to move together, and hours can be retrained by seeing.

If you live with illness, take prescribed medicine, or feel worry, speak to your clinician — I speak here of belief, not medicine.

Look back at that table once, not to mourn it but to measure it. A pinch of leaf rolled in paper with a plug to keep the leaf from the lips. Did that light tube ever steady an argument or carry a night? Or did brainwashing wrap years of story around dust and call the story a prop? You saw it small. Keep it small.

### THE LEDGER IN ONE LINE

We kept accounts for years. Harm on one side, pleasure on the other, fear doing the sums. That whole ledger was a con.

The fact is the dose never added. It took attention from food and called the theft flavour. It took calm from the nerves and lent a little back for minutes and called the lending relief. It took company from the night and stood in front of it and took the bow.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Say it aloud and test it against any hour you lived. Morning. Did it start the day, or did it quiet the hollow unease planted by the last dose of the night before? Work. Did it crown finished work, or did the work taste louder once no ember cut it? Meal-end. Would you pour ash on pudding to crown it? Night drink. Did paper complete sun and leisure and company, or was it only ever sneaking a ride while night and drink and company gave?

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

“Was the first light in heaven, then?”

Heaven for ninety seconds at the front, then film and sting and tighter chest on the next stair and mouth needing to wash. Was that heaven, or was that hunger pausing because hunger was fed? Would heaven need topping up within the hour? Would heaven leave you stale, breathless and chained between lights?

Ask what the hour lost and what it kept. Ash. Stain. Sour mouth. Hurry. Hiding the smell from those who do not smoke. What did it keep? Food. Drink. Talk. Lingering. Breath moving without rattle. The lingering is longer now because nothing cuts it.

That is the whole account, closed.

### THE CREATURE AND THE LIE

We were conned twice, and both cons are now seen through.

The first was small. A tiny physical hunger, an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug that faded within minutes and peaked for a few days and then sank. Not will. Not character. Not a mighty enemy. A fading echo from hours unfed. You do not argue with an echo. You smile and let it die unfed.

We called that echo the Nipper because naming makes it winnable. Feed the Nipper and you keep the ticking alive. Starve it and it dies. You starved it. In a week the postcards come fewer. In a month they come rarely. When they come they bring their own answer, because no belief feeds them.

The second con was large. Years of story that taught you to read that little tug as proof that paper relaxes, rewards, steadies, completes. Family. Mates lighting. Bright walls behind tills. Theatre of filter and mild promise. That story was the Smokescreen, and quitting was de-programming, not deprivation.

You lived inside the nicotine trap and called the inside normal. Did normal need Febreeze on coats and mints on breath and hands scrubbed and garage doors lifted to hide? Did normal need counting left for the afternoon and buying two before a trip in case shops shut? Did normal need stepping out of your own life every forty minutes to feed?

You also lived inside the tug-of-war of fear and called the pulling honest. Fear of failing. Fear of succeeding without identity. Both ropes belonged to the same hands. If paper calmed fear, why did fear grow across the years of smoking? If paper proved identity, whose identity was it — yours, or the factory counting puffs? The rope falls because no one now holds the other end.

You left, easily, immediately and permanently, not link by link but whole. Not flat, not empty, but full, because attention is no longer taxed.

### THE WORDS YOU KEEP

Doubt will knock. It knocks for every free reader in the first weeks. A break. A bill. A sharp word on the phone. A lit hand offering with, “one won’t hurt.” Old hours leave echoes.

Do not debate the echo. Do not weigh it. Debate keeps paper alive as if it had a case. Paper has no case left standing. Morning proved it. Shop proved it. Break proved it. Meal proved it. Night proved it.

When the thought crosses, lightly, almost fondly, answer with the lines you already own. They are not slogans. They are shorthand for hours you lived.

Remember, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain when that offer comes. Is there a single hour since the stub that was better with ash in it? Was tea less hot? Was talk less whole? Was work less yours? The question answers itself.

Answer, All you have to do is follow all the instructions, and you already did. You kept the mind open. You finished the argument before borrowing another. You watched other smokers with clear eyes and kept your own judgment. You trusted the body to breathe and taste without being taught. You saw the seller behind the smoke. You let the favourite prove it gives nothing. You allowed no single or special one to rebuild the bottle.

Picture the whole dose watched plain when the postcard says “nice.” Not the imagined lighting bright at the front, but dry paper mouth by the third puff, filmed tongue, grey ash, browned filter, chest tighter on the next stair. Is that what you want, or is that what hunger wants you to remember as want? What would one more test teach that hundreds did not, except how fast the clock winds again?

You answer inside, loud and glad. Each visit arrives pre-labelled as script. Each answer makes the next answer quicker, until the thought itself means gladness that you do not live there.

### TWELVE LINES TO PHOTOGRAPH

Keep this photograph in the pocket and in the phone. No callbacks, no course, no footnotes. Twelve spoken lines. You lived them. You keep them.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Give this book a fair hearing and it will free you.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

3. BEGIN BY FEELING GREAT TO BE ESCAPING
There is no doom here, only freedom ahead.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
All you have to do is what this book asks.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the method, not you.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

10. MEET THE BEST CIGARETTE HEAD-ON
Let the favourite prove it gives nothing.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
Close the trap with joy, not sadness.

Read them once in the morning and once at night until you do not need to read them. They are not rules to strain with. They are reminders of what you saw.

### THE ASHTRAY

Here is the last thing I have to show you, and it is not recap.

For years we pictured a full tray we had emptied by courage. Grey powder. Browned ends. Stain wiped. Will holding the lid down. That picture kept us sorry for ourselves and a little proud of missing.

Look again with clear eyes. The tray was never full. The ashtray was already empty — you were the one who kept refilling it, and now you have put the habit down like a borrowed coat that was never yours.

We borrowed the coat at the first con on phoney information and wore it until we called it skin. It itched. It stank of yesterday. It sent us to the step in rain. It taxed every errand with a second errand hidden inside it. We patched it with lights and milds and filters, with pellets for finished paragraphs and miles, with promises of tomorrow. None of it was skin. Take it off and the shoulders are still there. The arms move. The day holds itself.

“Will I have to keep remembering?”

You will remember less and less. At first the glad answer comes often because echoes are frequent. Then the hand stops half-lifting. The pocket stops being patted. The shop wall becomes background. The square becomes sky, not cue. Memory of tending fades like a bus route you no longer take. You know it was yours once. You do not travel it.

Keep nothing as a prop. No packet kept for courage. No lighter kept for luck. No last end kept in a drawer to prove you could keep it and not use it. A kept prop keeps the story alive as if paper were a friend sleeping, not litter thrown. Throw the litter. Empty the tray. Wash the coat you do own. Air the curtains. Let the rooms forget.

Do not preach. Do not pity aloud. Let visible lightness do what argument cannot. A friend who smokes will watch you laugh to the middle of the story without stepping out, taste to the bottom without filming, walk home without detour, and will wonder what you know. Tell them nothing until they ask. When they ask, tell them paper never gave, only took, and days hold themselves.

**SUMMARY**
- Paper did plenty to the body and breath and gave nothing to the hour; the account is closed
- The itch was made by dosing and quieted for minutes by dosing; it was never a gift above normal
- The tug was small and unfed and the story around it was large and now cleared
- Both fear ropes belonged to the trap; with neither held, the pulling stops
- The thought that crosses is echo and postcard, not order or evidence
- Twelve kept lines hold the whole method in the pocket without new rules
- The tray stands empty because nothing now refills it; the borrowed coat is laid down

Get on with enjoying your life.
