Chapter 11
ONE MEANS ALL AND TOMORROW MEANS NEVER

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The cut-down diary, the lamp on the shelf and the cliff edge, the morning five months on, the myths table

*There is no just-one dose — there is only the bottle built again and the clock wound again.*

### THE DRAWER YOU KEEP

You have left that midnight patio with drinks and lit hands without signing its name on paper. You have seen morning and meal and break stand up without a dose in them.

And still the mind keeps one locked drawer and polishes the key.

We all kept it. We said, very reasonably, very quietly, I see all that, but surely I can keep one for the wedding, one for the holiday, one for the bad night, one when the talk runs long. Surely I can cut to three a day and hold it there. Surely I can start properly tomorrow when work calms and the house is quiet.

Listen to what that voice is asking for.

Is it asking for pleasure? Or is it asking for permission to keep the hunger alive on a smaller leash? Did you ever meet a man who loved being struck and asked to be struck only on Sundays? Would you call that love, or would you call that hunger talking through a calendar?

Have you ever asked why the bargain always comes now, after the proof? Why it never came before you saw the patio plain? Is it because the proof left one true pleasure standing, or because the proof left the hunger with nowhere to hide except a number and a date?

We held the box in the palm and counted twenties as a day counted out. We watched the theatre of mild. We watched night and drink and company give while paper took. What is left to bargain with once the givers have been named? Only hunger, calling itself moderation.

Tell me plain. If three a day truly gave something, why would twenty have given more? If one at Christmas truly gave something, why would you need to book it months ahead like a train you are afraid to miss? Does a man book joy, or does he book a dose?

### CUTTING DOWN IS STILL FALLING

Take the cut-down diary and look at it without kindness.

Monday: four. Proud. Tuesday: six. Tired. Wednesday: three, with two borrowed and not written. Thursday: eight and a vow to be strict Friday. Friday with drink: eleven and the diary shut in the drawer.

We have all written that diary. We wrote smaller numbers and smoked larger wants. Each written number made the next dose more precious, more watched, more waited for. The hours between grew longer and louder. The tug grew a voice. The lighting felt bigger because the wait was bigger.

Did the smaller number make you less tied, or did it put the tie under a lamp and admire it? Did you think less about paper when you allowed three, or did you think about paper all day to time the three? Which man thinks more about smoking — the man who lights without counting, or the man who counts every light?

Cutting down does not loosen the knot. It tightens the hours around fewer knots until each knot feels like an event.

Picture a cliff. You stand at the top. Someone says, you can jump off as long as you do not fall more than a few metres. You would laugh. Jumping is falling. The height only decides how long before the ground.

Cutting down is that laugh turned solemn. Smoke a little and fall a little. Keep a little hunger and feed a little hunger. Does the ground care whether you called it moderation on the way down? Does hunger honour a limit, or does it use the limit as proof that each dose matters?

Ask three questions and answer without the pack helping you.

Have you ever cut down and stayed down without thinking about it every hour, or did the number creep while the thinking never stopped? Have you ever made a dose more precious and found it gave more, or did the preciousness only make the wait harder and the lighting shorter? Have you ever known a cutter-down who stopped counting, or did he count for years until the count broke?

That diary is the nicotine trap keeping its books.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

### THE LAMP ON THE SHELF

Now take the other bargain, the prettiest one. The special one.

The wedding dose. The holiday dose. The one after the funeral. The one with the old mate you have not seen in ten years. Kept on a shelf like a brass lamp. Not for hunger, you say. For the moment.

Rub that lamp and watch what comes out.

One light after weeks without. The hand stops reaching for a minute. The head says, ahh. Then the mouth turns dry paper. The fingers smell. The talk moves on while you tend ash. By night the pocket is tapped twice. By next day the shop queue feels different. By the week the shelf holds not one lamp but a row of lamps, all waiting for special moments, and every week grows more moments.

Is that prediction, or is that memory? Have we not lived it?

The lamp never held a moment. It held a bottle. Rub it once and the bottle rebuilds around you, wall by wall, until you stand inside again counting twenties and washing curtains and stepping out into cold squares to tend small fires. One rub rebuilds the room. That is not poetry. That is how hunger learns its hours again.

Think of hunger as a small fire in a grate. Leave it unfed and it sinks to ash. Puff on it once to see if it still burns and you have answered your own question. It burns because you puffed. Would you test ash with paper and call the flame proof you needed paper?

We say, I will control it this time. I am older. I know more. I will have just the one and walk away.

Did knowing more ever make hunger argue less? Did age ever make a dose give what it never gave at twenty? Was the last hundred times you said control a rehearsal, or was it evidence?

There is no special dose. There is only a dose that arrives wearing a special coat. Take the coat off and the dose stands plain — paper, dry tongue, sting at the back, tug quiet for minutes then louder by the hour. Would you book months ahead for dry tongue? Would you cross town for sting? Or would you only cross town because hunger has dressed them as memory?

Once the bottle is rebuilt you do not sip from it. You live in it.

### FIVE MONTHS WITHOUT, THEN ONE

“I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way, thinking after 5 months I could control myself and just have one. Nope.”

That line was written by someone who stood where you stand and tested the shelf.

Five months with no paper. Mornings with kettle and cold floor and hands that no longer pat pockets. Meals that end in taste, not in stepping out. Breaks with air on the face. Talk heard to the middle. Coats that smell of night and rain. Then a night, a drink, a hand extended, a kind voice, one won't hurt, and the old thought arriving in his own voice: I can control one now. I have proved it.

He lit. First draw hot. Second less. Third already edging back under the ribs. By the butt the mouth was film and the laugh inside had moved on. By night the pocket was tapped. By week the diary was back in the drawer.

Did five months make one safe, or did five months only make the hunger quiet enough to be misheard as control? Was the control proved by months without, or was it disproved by minutes after?

I tell you this not to frighten you about a slip. A slip is a rumble strip on the road, not the ditch. If your tyre hums on it you correct the wheel and drive on; you do not drive into the ditch to prove you slipped. What matters is the belief you carry after, not the shame you pile on. Guard the belief and a stumble does not become a home.

I tell you to close the shelf before the night arrives, so when the thought comes it arrives already named.

No one needs to test one to know it. You have tested hundreds. Did any single lighting ever stay single because you ordered it to? Or did each single only borrow the next until the row was rebuilt?

If your body worries you, speak to your clinician — I speak here of belief, not medicine.

Whether some found a prop helped for a while or most try with nothing in the drawer, neither count tells you what a dose does. Counts answer other questions for other rooms. Your question is simpler and sits in your palm: did paper ever give, or did it only quiet its own noise for minutes?

You know the answer because you have timed it on your own skin.

### TOMORROW NEVER LIGHTS

Then the last bargain, the softest. Tomorrow.

Not tonight, you say. Tonight the house is loud. Work is sharp. The mother-in-law lands Friday. The deadline lands Monday. I will smoke till Sunday and start clean Monday when life is kind.

We have all set that Monday. We set it with a full pack and a good heart. Monday came with its own noise, so we set next Monday. The pack counted our Mondays for us.

Does hunger calm while you feed it till a date? Or does each day till Monday teach the hands again that noise means lighting? What will Monday know that today does not? Will the hands be less trained, or more? Will the tug be weaker after twenty more lessons, or stronger?

Tomorrow is not a date. It is a dose wearing a calendar.

Think of cost while you wait, not to scare but to count. Money out in coins every day. Hours out standing apart while talk runs inside. Mornings out coughing on stairs. Nights out tapping pockets on hills home. Every single day till Monday for something you have already seen gives nothing. Is that waiting, or is that paying?

And fear? Look at the two ropes pulling.

“I will fail if I try now.” “I will lose myself if I succeed.” One rope says you cannot hold out. One rope says life without will be flat and strange. Pull left, pull right, you stay in the middle with a lit end.

Who holds those ropes? Did you tie them, or were they tied for you by years of lit hours and packet theatre and talk that called hunger pleasure? That pull in both directions is the tug-of-war of fear.

Both ropes belong to the same hands. Drop them and the war ends. Not by holding harder. By seeing there is nothing to hold.

Ask and answer without paper helping.

If tomorrow were truly kinder, would today’s dose make tomorrow easier or harder? If you fear failure, does feeding hunger till Monday train success? If you fear a flat life, did paper ever colour a day, or did it only pause its own grey for minutes and call the pause colour?

Tomorrow lights nothing. Today names it.

### FOUR LINES YOU WILL HEAR

These lines will arrive in your voice in the days ahead. They are not your reasoning. They are the old script knocking. Hear each as script and answer it once, and it loses its knock.

“I’ll just cut to a few a day. That’s sensible.”

Sensible for whom? Three a day means hunger watched all day and preciousness added to each lighting. You will think more, wait longer, and credit the lighting bigger for the wait. Sensible would be no hunger timed at all.

“I’ll keep just the wedding one, the holiday one. Special ones don’t count.”

They count most because they keep the belief whole. One kept pleasure means the lie stands. The lamp stays on the shelf and the bottle stays built around one kept lighting. There is no shelf outside the room.

“I’ll start Monday when stress lifts.”

Stress never lifts on order, and paper never lifted stress. It left the phone and the papers and the argument untouched while it quieted its own tug for minutes. Monday with twenty more lessons will not be kinder. Monday is today once you name the bargain.

“I slipped, so I’ve blown it. Might as well carry on.”

A hum on the rumble strip is not the ditch. Note where the wheel drifted — drink, offer, late hour — correct the wheel, hold the belief steady, and drive on. The only way to blow it is to invite the bottle back and call the invitation proof.

Hear them now and you will not obey them later. The thought will come dressed as you. You will know the dress.

### IS THIS JUST MORE BRAINWASHING

“You talk easy. You tell me to follow and not argue. That’s just brainwashing the other way.”

I welcome that challenge because it is honest. So test it like you tested the patio.

Have I asked you to believe without looking? Or have I asked you to look at mornings, meals, breaks, night squares, diaries, lamps, Mondays — your own hours — and time what paper added? Did I hand you a slogan, or did I hand you your own pocket tapped on a hill and your own mouth like film after three puffs?

Brainwashing told you paper calms, rewards, belongs on patios, comes in mild that spares, can be timed by numbers, can be kept for weddings, can wait till Monday. Who sold that? Who packed twenties as a day and cut holes to whisper mild? Who paid for the laugh to wear ash?

I sell you nothing to put in that place. No other glass for the same drink. No counting. No hiding from friends. No dodging the glass. Go out. Drink what you drink. Stand the cold square. Hear the whole story. Taste to the bottom. Change nothing except paper.

Does that read as a new chain, or as chains named one by one till the hands are empty?

I do not ask you to trust me. I ask you to trust what you timed yourself — heaven ninety seconds at the front, sour mouth two hours, missed middle gone for good. If that timing is false, keep the drawer. If that timing is true, what is left to obey except what you saw?

You are not being led. You are being shown where the leading was.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

**SUMMARY**
- Cutting down makes each dose more precious and the wait louder, it does not loosen hunger
- One kept for weddings or holidays keeps the whole belief standing and rebuilds the bottle
- A long stretch without does not turn one into something safe, the lighting restarts the clock
- Tomorrow is hunger wearing a calendar, today already holds the proof
- A slip is a rumble strip to correct from, not proof to carry on
- Future one-won’t-hurt thoughts arrive as old script, heard as script they lose force
