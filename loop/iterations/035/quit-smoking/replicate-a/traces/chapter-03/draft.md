Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY DO FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER
- The ledger on the kitchen table, two columns in your head
- A finished meal, a pause on the step, talk among friends
- Arms around you in the water that will not loosen

*Stop weighing what it costs you against what it gives you, and ask what it ever gave you at all.*

### THE LEDGER WE ALL KEEP

I know the page you have been adding up for years.

We all kept it. On one side the price, the cough on the stairs, the smell in the coat, the money at the till, the hiding and the mints. On the other side the reasons we told ourselves balanced it. It calms me. I like the taste. It is my little reward. It gives me a pause. It goes with food, with a drink, with friends. I know it harms me, but at least it does something for me.

I kept that ledger myself. I could recite it without thinking. And I believed that as long as there was anything on the pleasure side, the argument was close. Harm against benefit. Risk against reward. Perhaps I would stop one day, when the harm side grew heavy enough.

That weighing is the Smokescreen talking, and it keeps you inside the nicotine trap while you think you are thinking clearly.

Let me put it to you as one smoker to another, without a sneer and without a lecture. What if there is nothing on the other side to weigh?

I am not asking you whether smoking does more harm than good. Every smoker knows it harms. You do not need me to tell you about yellow fingers and stale rooms and mornings when the chest feels tight. You know the bill.

I am asking a different question. What good is there at all?

You entered this book beginning to see you were conned into needing doses. Keep that seeing. Now use it. Say your reasons out loud, one by one, the way you say them to yourself when no one is listening.

"I enjoy it." "It is my reward for getting through." "It helps the pause." "It completes the meal and the company."

Say them. I will not mock one of them. I felt each of them. The moment of lighting can feel like heaven, and I am not going to tell you it felt like nothing. It felt like something, for seconds, at the strike of the match. And a finished job can feel as if it calls for a small party.

Hold those two honest admissions with you while we look. Heaven at lighting, and only at lighting. A party that seems to attach itself to almost everything finished. If those are true, what did the dose add to the hour itself?

### AFTER THE MEAL, ON THE STEP, AMONG FRIENDS

Take three ordinary pictures from your own week. No laboratory. Just your life.

The meal finished. The plate cleared. The taste of food still in the mouth, warm and full. Then the hand moves and the dose is lit. Tell me truly. What did that dose add to the food? Did it deepen the flavour? Or did it cover it with heat and ash, sour the mouth you had just enjoyed, and leave you needing a mint to get the food taste back?

We tell ourselves the dose completes the meal. Look at what happened. The meal was complete before it. The pleasure was the food, the hunger met, the sitting down. The dose did not improve one bite you had already eaten. It only marked the end by spoiling the aftertaste.

Take the pause. The step outside, the kettle boiled, five minutes between tasks. You light and you stand and you stare. You say the dose makes the pause restful.

Was it the dose that rested you? Or was it the stopping, the air, the stillness, the five minutes of doing nothing? Non-smokers take that same five minutes and feel the same loosening in the shoulders. They do not need smoke to pause. The pause was pleasant. The dose only rode along.

Take company. Friends talking, laughing, the night warm around the table. Someone offers and you join them at the rail. You say it makes you belong.

Who made the warmth? The friends, the laughter, the stories, you yourself, quick and funny when you are at ease. Would that talk have been flat without smoke? Have you never laughed hard and long in places where no one lit? The company was the pleasure. The dose only stood beside it.

I hear you answer, because I answered the same way for years.

"But I feel it helps. The moment I light up I'm in heaven, but I feel shitty afterwards. Whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker."

I do not doubt the first half of that sentence. I do not call you a liar about your own mouth. That taken-care-of feeling at lighting is real as feeling. For a few draws the chatter drops. Something feels quieted.

Ask only what was quieted, and for how long.

Was the food made better for the evening, or only covered for minutes? Was the job made sweeter, or did the party turn out to be the lighting itself, not the work you had done well? Was the company made closer, or did you step away from the table to feed a hunger and call the stepping away belonging?

Does that short quiet at lighting look like a day-long gift? Or does it look like an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug pausing for seconds and then returning, while the meal and the pause and the friends were pleasant all along on their own?

It is you that enjoys the food. It is you that did the work well. It is you that laughs with friends. The dose sneaks a ride on each of them and takes the credit.

### THE HUG THAT WON'T LET GO

Let me give you the picture that undid the ledger for me.

You are out of your depth in water. You are frightened. Someone swims to you and puts strong arms around you and holds your head up. You gasp with gratitude. You cling to him. You thank him with all your heart.

Then you notice you cannot move. His arms are tight around your chest. When you try to swim toward the shore, he holds you where you are. When you try to breathe fully, his weight presses. He saved you, and now he will not let you go. And worse, you begin to understand what your gratitude would not let you see at first. He is the one who pushed you in.

That is what a dose does.

We threw grateful arms around the rescuer because at lighting the panic quiets. Shoulders drop. The Nipper stops whining for a minute. We say, it takes care of it. We mean the feeling. And the feeling does pause.

But look at the arms. The same dose that quiets the unease is the dose that made the unease certain to return in an hour. It feeds the little feeder so the little feeder lives to ask again. It leaves the fingers smelling, the mouth stale, the chest heavier, the pack lighter, the day cut into feedings. It leaves the problem on the desk exactly where it was.

A friend who helps you does not need you to see him twenty times a day. A pleasure that helps you does not leave you more in need after each helping. A rescuer does not tighten his grip each time you thank him.

Ask the only questions that matter here, and answer from your own throat, not from the story.

If the dose truly gave you calm, why are you less calm an hour later than a man who never lit? If it truly gave you pleasure, why does the pleasure live only in the lighting and turn to staleness by the half? If it truly gave you company and rest, why must you leave the table and leave the rest to get it?

You know the honest answer. You have always known it.

It never gave. It only paused the want it had made, and charged you for the pause.

Once that is seen, the ledger collapses. There are not two columns to balance. There is one column, and it is all on the wrong side. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Say that slowly to yourself, not as a slogan but as what you just watched with your own eyes at the table and on the step. The meal, the pause, the talk were FOR you. The dose did TO you what doses do. It covered taste, it broke rest into feedings, it chained the hour to the next hunger while letting you call the chaining a break.

That is the switch. Not less harm or more harm. No benefit left to defend.

You do not need to be miserable to see it. You are not losing a friend. You are seeing the arms for what they are, and a man who sees the arms begins to feel great to be slipping out of them.

3. BEGIN BY FEELING GREAT TO BE ESCAPING
There is no doom here, only freedom ahead.

SUMMARY
- The weighing of harm against pleasure falls away once pleasure itself is questioned
- Heaven belongs to seconds at lighting, the rest of the hour belongs to aftertaste and renewed want
- Food, pause and company please on their own, the dose only stands beside them and takes the credit
- The quiet after a puff pauses an inner want while leaving outside life untouched
- Grateful arms that tighten each time they hold are not rescue, they are the trap holding on
