### Reader journey comparison

**The real chapter's movement:** Carr’s withdrawal chapter moves the already-quit reader through the gap between physical abstinence and settled freedom. It names two trigger sources (nicotine “pangs” vs conditioned events), warns against willpower and deprivation, and reframes trigger moments—coffee, meals, social smoking—as brainwashed association, not real need. The end-state is practical confidence: triggers may still flicker, but the reader answers them with facts and joy (“little monster dying,” royal-flush certainty), treats smoking thoughts as moments to celebrate freedom, and expects ordinary life to become a non-issue quickly. Functionally: post-vow life, high freedom, no new demolition curriculum—handling the ordinary day without re-opening the trap.

**Our chapter's movement:** Ours receives C12’s free identity (“In the days after the stubbed end”) and walks the reader through a full ordinary week in scene: morning, shop queue, work break, afternoon errands, meal, evening drink, stray thought, then a consolidating week.
