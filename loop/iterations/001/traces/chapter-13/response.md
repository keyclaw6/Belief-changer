### WHY THE WORD SUGAR HAD TO BE RENAMED

Do you feel the wobble when you ask, what exactly must I avoid?

Of course you do. We have all felt it.

You leave the chapters that demolished energy, reward, taste, and cope, and you think, I see the trap. But what is the trap made of? Is it the spoon in my tea? The bread on my plate? The apple in my bag?

If you do not answer that clearly, the Sweet Brainwashing will answer it for you. It will blur the line. It will whisper, if you cannot eat an apple, this is impossible. If a biscuit is the same as rice, why bother? Is that honest confusion? Or is it the last defence of the brainwashing?

The fact is, the confusion was manufactured. The industry profits when sugar means everything and therefore means nothing. When fruit and frosting share one word, you cannot make a clean decision. And a decision you cannot make is a decision you cannot keep.

So we draw the line. Together. Now. Once. In a box you will never need to redraw.

Do you want a rule you can use in a supermarket aisle in three seconds, without a calculator and without shame?

You shall have it. Will you let me give it to you exactly as it will stay for the rest of this book?

Good. Keep an open mind and let the line become yours.

### THE BOX ON THE PAGE: WHAT COUNTS AS BAD SUGAR

Read this box slowly. Let it settle. From this page onward, every time I say sugar without qualification, I mean exactly this. This is our definitional decree.

> **BAD SUGAR = all refined sugar, all added free sugars, and processed junk carbs (including white flour/bread/pasta/rice, sugary drinks, sweets, chocolate, biscuits, pastries, most packaged snacks).**
>
> **Decree: When this book says "sugar," take it to mean BAD SUGAR.**

That's it. That's the line.

Do you see how short it is?

Does that short list leave you puzzled? Or relieved?

Ask yourself, does an apple need a factory to become an apple? Does a biscuit? There is your test before the test. BAD SUGAR is sugar that has been stripped from its natural package, concentrated, refined, and sold back to you as a hit. Good sugars — intrinsic in whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy — remain in their natural structure with water, fibre, and bulk that your body reads. They are not the target. They never were.

The fact is, there is an institutional ceiling you already exceed without knowing it. The World Health Organization sets a population ceiling for free sugars at less than ten percent of daily energy — about fifty grams for many adults — with additional benefit below five percent. Why? Because current consumption far exceeds both. Your normal is not healthy. The aisle is not neutral. The trap is everywhere.

Does that ceiling mean you must now count grams?

No. You have something better than counting. You have a name. When you see sweets, biscuits, chocolate, ice cream, sugary drinks, pastries, white bread, white pasta, white rice, most packaged snacks — you see the label your mind now applies automatically: BAD SUGAR. No maths. No moral debate. Do I eat this dose? That is the only question that matters.

Ask the trap question.

If BAD SUGAR did anything FOR you, why would you need a ceiling at all?

If BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you, why negotiate how much TO you is sensible?

There is only one honest answer. Zero is the only level where the con collapses. But zero of what? Zero BAD SUGAR. Not zero sweetness. Not zero fruit. Not zero joy. Do you feel the difference?

I know this is hard to accept at first if you have been taught that fruit sugar is the same as added sugar inside you. It is not. Inside you the molecule may look similar, but the package changes everything. Nature's Guide sends fruit slowly, with signals that say enough. BAD SUGAR hits fast, then drops you. Would you eat ten oranges as fast as ten sweets? Would your body let you?

No. Your body answers. Listen to it.

And here is the hard fact at full Carr force, then disowned exactly as Carr would disown it, so you do not quit out of fear.

Free sugars drive the acid that rots teeth. That is not a metaphor. Plaque bacteria eat free sugars and make acid. Acid dissolves enamel. Brushing and fluoride mediate it, but they do not erase it. The cavity is the body's receipt for BAD SUGAR. I want you to see it clearly. The sweet you call harmless is doing TO your teeth what no friend would do.

High added sugar also carries a deferred metabolic load. Population summaries link high added sugar with higher heart and vessel burden — liver overload, raised blood pressure, chronic inflammation — a lift that is not free energy but a loan with interest. The daily hit you call a treat predicts low mood months later, not relief.

That is frightening. It is meant to be frightening where it is true.

And now I ask you to put fear down.

The fact is, I do not want you to stop because you are frightened. Frightened quitters white-knuckle. Free people rejoice. Fear is another grip of the trap. Let the facts do their perception work, then let them go. You are not escaping to avoid a future punishment. You are escaping to recover taste, steady energy, and relief now.

Do you feel that shift? From dread to clarity? Keep it. Clarity is what keeps you free.

If you feel you need support, ask a clinician or trusted support service.*

That boxed advisory stays with every instruction that touches food. It is not a footnote. It is a promise. Your medical care stays in charge of your medical care. This book is in charge of the belief.

### WHAT IS NOT ON THE LIST — AND WHY YOU ARE NOT DEPRIVED

If BAD SUGAR is the target, what is not the target?

Whole fruit. Vegetables. Plain milk, plain yoghurt. Nuts. Beans. Eggs. Fish. Meat. Whole grains as nature grew them. Water.

Do you need to fear those?

No. They are not doses. They are food.

The fact is, your body manufactures glucose from protein and glycerol. Sugar is not an essential import. You are never without glucose. The brain runs on glucose your body makes whether you add BAD SUGAR or not. Does that land?

If sugar were truly essential as BAD SUGAR, would a child who never tasted sweets fail to grow? Would a hiker without sweets collapse for lack of import?

There is only one honest answer. You are not depriving yourself of a nutrient. You are removing a refined hit that was never nutrition.

Ask yourself, when you feared you would lose energy without BAD SUGAR, were you afraid of losing fuel? Or of losing the spike you had learned to call fuel?

It was the other way around. The spike creates the dip you then misread as need. Remove the spike and the dip goes. What returns is not flatness. It is the steady baseline you had before the trap taught you to chase a lift.

Do you see? Stopping BAD SUGAR is not nutritional deprivation. It is nutritional relief.

I want you to feel the relief physically. Think of-tight shoes. You wore them all day for the relief of taking them off. You called that relief pleasure. Unwrap the chocolate and you are only taking off the shoes BAD SUGAR tied on. The pleasure was the moment, not the dose. BAD SUGAR was sneaking a ride.

Is an apple less sweet after a month free of BAD SUGAR?

No. It is sweeter. Your taste recovers. We were not born needing frosting to taste peach.

So let the brainwashing's last nutrition scare dissolve. You are not giving up sweetness. You are recovering it.

### NATURE'S GUIDE AND HER THREE SIMPLE INSTRUMENTS

Now we install the positive authority that replaces the brainwashing's noise. I call her Nature's Guide.

She is not a diet. She is not a plan. She is the operating manual your body arrived with. She is older than any aisle. She reads hunger, fullness, and taste without numbers. We will give her three instruments so you can hear her clearly. These are tools, not prescriptions. You use them to check, not to obey perfectly.

**Instrument One: The Raw Test.**

Would you eat this food raw, plain, and unsweetened if you were calmly hungry? An apple — yes. A carrot — yes. Plain yoghurt — yes. Plain chicken, nuts, beans — yes.

Would you eat white bread raw? Dry pasta? A spoon of refined sugar? Frosting? No. Lonely carbs need company — butter, chocolate, cream, salt — to be palatable.

Listen. That test is not moral. It is mechanical. It tells you whether the food is food in nature's sense, or a dose inviting company over to fool your palate. BAD SUGAR fails the Raw Test. Good food passes. Use it in the aisle. Three seconds. Clear answer.

Do you feel how quickly it sorts the shelf? Have you already sorted half your kitchen in your head?

Good.

**Instrument Two: The 0–20 Hunger Gauge.**

Zero is empty and faint. Ten is comfortable, satisfied, steady. Twenty is stuffed, sluggish, tight waistband.

Most trapped eating happens at twelve to fifteen — not hungry, but restless. The Sugar Gremlin whines and the Sweet Brainwashing translates the whine as eat BAD SUGAR now.

So you check the gauge. Quietly. Where am I? Zero to twenty?

If you are at three, you need food — have a primary food. If you are at ten, you need no food — a glass of water, a stretch, a breath. If you are at sixteen, you are not hungry at all — you are full and the whine is pure habit.

Do you see? The gauge does not tell you what to do. It tells you what you are feeling, so you stop misreading a faint, empty, slightly restless, slightly off feeling as hunger or need for a hit.

Will you try it at your next pause? Just notice the number. No judgement. Numbers calm stories.

**Instrument Three: Primary and Secondary Foods.**

Primary foods are your steady base — foods that pass the Raw Test and satisfy the gauge: whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy, eggs, fish, meat, beans, nuts, whole grains as grown. They are what you build meals from. They end hunger and leave you steady.

Secondary foods are the rest of real food that is not BAD SUGAR — cooked dishes, sauces you make, breads you choose that are whole and simple, occasional treats you cook that still pass honesty. You enjoy them when you are hungry, as food, without the spike-crash of BAD SUGAR. They are not doses.

Do you hear the freedom in that?

There is no forbidden fruit. There is a clear enemy — BAD SUGAR — and a wide friendly world — everything primary, and secondary as food when you want it.

I say this with warm authority: Nature's Guide does not count. She notices. She is not a scold. She is relief. When you follow the gauge and favour primary foods, your body returns to its natural baseline on its own. Easily. You do not need to redesign your life. You change nothing else to prove you are free. You inhabit the same kitchen, same commute, same cinema — with new eyes.

Is that deprivation?

No. It is the end of bargaining.

Ask the trap question once more.

If a food needs sugar to be edible, was it food you loved? Or company that fooled you?

There is only one honest answer. And it is deliciously freeing.

### THE SEATBELT — YOUR MARGIN FOR AN ACCIDENTAL BLIP

Now the worry: what if I slip? What if the sauce has sugar? What if I am handed cake at work? Will one bite re-hook me forever?

Breathe. This is where the seatbelt lives.

Your body can cope with an occasional blip, but your mind cannot. Read that again. Your body can cope with an occasional blip, but your mind cannot.

An accidental ingestion — sugar in a sauce, a spoon you did not expect — is a non-catastrophic buffer. It revives nothing by itself. The body handles it and moves on. What revives the trap is not the molecule. It is the belief you let back in.

Do you see? Accidental blip is seatbelt. Deliberate repeat is swerving.

The seatbelt image is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. The belt does not license you to steer into walls. It protects you if a wall appears despite careful driving. If a blip happens, you notice it, you learn, you stay free. You do not say, I got away with it, so I can do it again. That sentence is the Sweet Brainwashing trying to reinterpret a rumble strip as permission.

We ate cake on phoney information as children. We were conned, not foolish. The Gremlin is small. The Brainwashing is loud. Guard the belief and the Gremlin starves. Guard the behaviour with fear and the Brainwashing grows.

So you guard the belief, not the behaviour. You keep the decision closed. You do not keep the sweets.

Ask yourself.

If a blip were truly catastrophic, would free people survive a hidden spoon of sugar in a restaurant sauce?

If a blip were meaningless, why would the thought I got away with it feel so cunning?

There is only one honest answer. The physics is forgiving. The psychology is not. So you are gentle with your body and strict with your story. You forgive a blip, you learn, you stay free. You never deliberately repeat it.

Does that feel stern?

It is warm sternness. It is the hand on your shoulder that says, you are safe, and you are staying safe.

The fact is, the margin-for-error doctrine exists so fear does not trap you. Fear of a slip is another rope. Cut it. You are not walking a tightrope. You are driving with a seatbelt, on a wide road, with Nature's Guide reading the map.

Can you feel the kindness in that strictness?

Good. Keep it.

You run the Raw Test. Would I eat an apple? Plain yoghurt? Nuts? Would I eat sugar off a spoon? Frosting alone?

You notice. The apple is calm. The frosting is a hit.

Name the Gremlin. Small. Faint. Restless. Brainwashing is loud, Gremlin is quiet. The whine peaks in days, not weeks, then baseline returns. You are not white-knuckling a mighty enemy. You are watching a trivial parasite lose its host.

You change nothing else. You stay in the kitchen. You make tea. You enjoy the evening. Do you feel the pride? The freedom is not elsewhere. It is here. Now.

And that is the promise of Nature's Guide. She does not replace sweets with substitutes that keep the loop alive. She returns you to food. Primary when hungry. Secondary as food. BAD SUGAR not at all. Simple. Operable. Kind.

No. Diets restrict amount. This removes a category of doses that never fed you. Diets say resist. This says rejoice — the con is gone. Diets count. This notices — gauge, Raw Test, primary base.

No. You have a name — BAD SUGAR — and a guide — Nature's Guide. The WHO ceiling is context, not a personal prescription. It tells you normal is excessive. It does not tell you to weigh fifty grams. The line is categorical, not numeric. BAD SUGAR is out. Good food is in. Your clinicians handle medical thresholds where needed. We handle the belief.
