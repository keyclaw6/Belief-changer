MOVE E33-M1: PARTIAL — Carr names the chemistry clearing in a few days while mind and body can take weeks to settle, and leftover smoking thoughts are normal for that short window; ours dramatizes habit traces easing over days and weeks but never states that the chemical withdrawal is already gone while thoughts may still drift. Evidence: "The first week after my last stubbed end, the feet still turned. Not from pain. From habit wearing my voice." and "Unanswered, that pull loses interest, and one morning you notice the morning asked nothing at all."

MOVE E33-M2: PARTIAL — Carr splits the two separate factors: the empty physical pang versus the conditioned psychological script tied to events; ours labels memory and habit wearing the reader's voice but does not teach that two-kind distinction or name the empty tug as its own category. Evidence: "If the old half-lift comes — hand half-rising before thought, eyes sliding to the pocket — see it for what it is. Memory wearing your voice." and "When an old picture drifts by in your own accent, you recognise the old uniform and stay with the day you are in."

MOVE E33-M3: PRESENT — Carr's point that staying stopped lets conditioned mechanical urgency fade without a lasting psychological programme is the chapter's lived spine: keep walking past the old turn, let the pull go unanswered, and ordinary days carry the proof with no new curriculum. Evidence: "I did not argue with the feet. I kept walking past the shelter to the corner and crossed where the light is better. By the third day the turn had softened. By the second week the shelter was just a shelter where other people stood, and my walk had its own line again." and "You do not need to debate it. You know that livery too well to walk behind it. Stay with the conversation, the task, the street you are on."
