Chapter 2
ARE YOU REALLY CHOOSING THIS

*You never decided to need cigarettes; you were caught, and your own hiding proves it.*

### The Spray Can In Your Pocket

Keep that open attention you brought from the meeting room, because I want to stand with you in a different room now.

It is late. The house is quiet. Your partner is already in bed. The children are asleep. You say you are just taking the rubbish out. You step into the garage and pull the door almost closed behind you, leaving a crack for the air.

The bulb is harsh. The car bonnet is cold. The concrete bites through your socks. You light up quickly and smoke with the door cracked, blowing toward the gap. You finish fast. You stub it in an old tin. You fan the air with your hand. Then you do the small ritual we all know. Mints. Spray. Febreeze the hell out of yourself. Hands washed. Coat shaken. You check your breath against your palm. You go back inside and sit down as if nothing happened.

I have stood in that garage. I know the cold on the fingers. I know the listening for footsteps. I know the careful casualness when you walk back in.

Now let me ask you plainly, as one smoker to another. Why does choice need a spray can?

Think of anything else you truly choose because you enjoy it. A meal you love. Music you love. A walk you love. Do you hide those in a garage? Do you lie about them with mints? Do you apologize for them before anyone has accused you? Choice does not creep. Choice does not whisper. Choice does not need to be Febreezed away.

We did creep. We did whisper. We hid from people we love in order to do something we claimed we loved. That split is not character. That is information.

I hear you, because I said it myself for years. "I enjoy it. It's my choice. Nobody forces me." And in the same week I said it, I was the closet smoker, standing in cold air so my own children would not know.

If this is choice, why the hiding? If you love lighting up, why do you hate being known as a smoker? If you choose it, why do you need a book to stop doing it? You do not need to answer yet. Only notice that both things live in you at once. You reach, and you conceal the reaching. That concealment is the first clear sight of the nicotine trap.

If you live with illness or worry, speak to your own clinician for personal care. I say this because I want you watching clearly, not anxiously.

### The Sofa Where You Pretend

You go back inside and sit down as if nothing happened. Let us stay there, because what happens on that sofa tells you more than the garage did.

The living room is warm. The television is low. Your partner is half asleep under a blanket, scrolling, not really watching. The cushions still hold the shape of the evening. You lower yourself onto your end of the sofa and pick up your own phone, or the remote, or the cold end of your tea. You arrange your face into evening.

Inside you are doing two jobs at once. One job is being here. Nodding at what is said. Laughing when the programme gets a laugh. Asking if the door is locked. The other job is covering. Did the spray cover it. Are your fingers clean. Is your breath safe if you lean in for the goodnight. Will the tin in the garage smell by morning. Will anyone open the garage before you can empty it.

We all know that double work. We sit with people we love and half our mind stands guard in another room.

Watch how small it makes you feel, and how unnecessary. You are not hiding an affair. You are not hiding a debt. You are hiding a paper tube you claim gives you pleasure. A pleasure that has to be laundered before it can sit on its own sofa.

Your partner shifts and says, did you put the bin out. You say, yes, just did it. It is not a big lie. That is what makes it heavy. You would not lie for a piece of chocolate cake. You would not chew mints and spray your coat and wash your hands for a cup of tea. You lie because some part of you already knows how this looks from outside, even while another part insists it is your right and your liking.

I do not tell you this to shame you. I will never look down on you for that garage or that sofa. I did both for years. I lied with the same casual voice. I sat with the same divided mind. I told myself I was protecting them from worry when I was protecting the habit from questions, including my own.

Ask yourself what a person who truly chose would do in that moment. Would they time the garage between programmes. Would they keep a tin that must never be seen. Would they keep count of sprays left in the can the way they keep count of cigarettes left in the pack. Would they feel a flash of irritation if their partner follows them to the door and talks too long, delaying the return inside.

That irritation is worth watching. It is not anger at them. It is the tug pulling while you smile. You love them. You also want to be back inside without a scene. So you hurry the talk. You cut it short. You feel mean afterwards. Then you tell yourself you smoked to be calm for them, when the edginess arrived because the last dose was fading and the next was calling.

Stay on that sofa a little longer. Notice your hands. They are empty now, washed, a little cold. They want something to hold. Notice your chest. Not pain, exactly. A thin restlessness under the ribs. Notice your thoughts offering the translation at once. Just habit. Just my little break. Just how I am.

Is that choosing? Choosing pauses. Choosing weighs. Choosing can wait until tomorrow without panic. What you feel on that sofa cannot wait without commentary. It narrates. It excuses. It bargains. It says one thing while your body does another.

A person choosing a pleasure does not need to pretend in front of his own family that the pleasure did not happen. We pretended. We all pretended. We Febreezed the hell out of ourselves and walked back in acting natural, and the effort to act natural is the proof that nature had left.

### The Hand That Moves First

Watch what your hand does today while you still smoke as normal.

The morning kettle has just clicked. You are standing in the kitchen in bare feet, waiting. The radio is on. Nothing hard has happened yet. No argument. No pressure. Your hand finds the pack on the worktop and turns it over once, the way you always do. You have not decided to smoke. You are waiting for water to boil. Yet the pack is already in your fingers.

Ask who moved first. You or your hand?

Later the phone goes down after a hard call. Your shoulders are up around your ears. The room feels tight. Your hand is already moving to the pack before you have decided anything. There is a familiar emptiness low down, a faint edginess at the edges, and thought arrives a half second later to dress it up. My nerves. My moment. My choice.

I am not denying what you feel in that second. "If anything stressful happens in my life, a cigarette takes care of it." I know that sentence from the inside. It arrives rapid and warm, as if someone had taken care of things. I felt it exactly as you feel it. I only ask you to notice the order. Tug first. Story second. Hand already moving while the mind supplies the words choice and need and liking.

Or take the doorway. You step out to fetch something from the car, a bag, a letter left on the seat. The air is cool. The street is quiet. You did not go out to smoke. You went out to fetch. But the hand has already slipped the pack from your pocket on the way. The lighter is already out. By the time you reach the car you are lit. You tell yourself you fancied one. Did you? Or did the movement happen before fancying had time to form?

We reach. We excuse. We hide.

A person choosing a pleasure anticipates, savours, remembers. A person in a con reaches, explains afterwards, hides the evidence. Which one did you do at the kettle, at the phone, at the car door?

Would a free chooser need to rehearse reasons before the hand has even stopped? Would he need to say, I have earned this, I need this, this is me, for something that truly gave? You do not rehearse reasons for scratching an itch. You scratch. You do not defend a glass of water. You drink. The need to call it choice, again and again, is itself the sign that choosing has gone while the word choice remains.

That is the point I want you to keep from today. Not a theory. A watched fact. Your hand moves. Your mind follows with a story. The story uses your voice, so you mistake it for you.

### The Car And The Queue

Let me walk you through two more ordinary reaches, because one example is easy to dismiss and three in a day are hard to argue with.

You are in the car at the lights. Engine idling. Heater blowing. The queue is long. You are not late, not calm, not upset, just waiting. Your left hand drums once on the wheel. Your right hand drops to the passenger seat where the pack lies. Click of the box. Tap of the filter. Lighter. Window cracked two inches. All done without a meeting in your head. No debate. No weighing of pleasure against risk. Just the sequence running.

Then at the red you catch your own face in the mirror, lit for a second, drawing in, and you think, I chose this. Did you? When? At what lights did you sit down and select breathlessness and hiding and counting as your pleasure? We never thought it over when we found it. We copied what we saw, we persisted through coughing and dizziness until we learned to inhale, and then we taught ourselves to thank the tube for pauses that belonged to the lights, the heater, the quiet minute alone.

Or the shop queue. You are standing with a basket, mints and milk, minding your own business. The person ahead argues about a receipt. The line does not move. A thin, empty feeling rises, so mild you would hardly call it discomfort. Your thoughts translate at once. Need air. Need a break. Need my brand. You step outside after paying and light before you reach the kerb. You call it taking the edge off a dull wait. Watch closer. The edge rose between doses. The dose covered it for minutes. The queue did not change. The till did not change. Only the tug quieted, briefly, and the mind called the quiet liking.

Three reaches. Kettle. Lights. Queue. Same order every time. An empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug first, so faint you almost miss it, then the hand, then the story in your voice saying choice.

I name that feeling in those exact words so you can spot it today without mistaking it for you. It is not hunger. It is not nerves. It is not character. It is a tiny physical signal between doses. It is not you.

And I name the story too, because you will hear it all day. It says grown-ups smoke to pause, to cope, to belong, to mark the moment. It borrows pictures from years ago, adults on steps, laughter in doorways, someone offering with a smile. That borrowed story is the Smokescreen. It takes the little tug and renames it as need and pleasure. Seen once, you cannot stop seeing it pass.

You were not foolish to believe it. You were conned on phoney information before you could judge, and then the trap kept you while leaving the word choice in your mouth.

That is why the garage needed spray and the sofa needed acting and the car needed the window cracked. Choice would need none of it. The nicotine trap needs all of it.

### The Two Keepers

There is a reason the hand moves and the spray comes out, and it is not weakness.

Once we are inside, two keepers hold us. The first is tiny and physical. Between doses you feel it as an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug, so mild you would hardly call it pain. It is the Nipper asking to be fed. It is small. It is already dying between every dose. It is not you.

The second keeper is larger and it lives in thought. It is the lifetime of stories that told you cigarettes calm you and help you cope and are your pleasure, the pictures of grown-ups pausing, the laughter that said everyone does it. That story is the Smokescreen. It takes that little tug and translates it as, you need a cigarette, you enjoy a cigarette, you choose a cigarette.

Together they make the nicotine trap. The little creature creates the tug. The big story misnames the tug as need and pleasure. The dose feeds the creature for minutes. The story keeps you feeding it for years.

I name them here only so you can see them passing in your own day. When the hand moves, that is the tug. When the mind says, this is my choice, my reward, my crutch, that is the smoke around it. You were not foolish to believe it. You were conned.

That is why blaming yourself never helped. No wonder past attempts ended with midnight vows and morning packets. You were never shown the trap. You were told to fight desire while still believing the desire was pleasure. This time we look first.

Think of the lighting second you know so well. The strike, the first draw, the brief flare that feels for seconds like heaven, then the stale mouth and the faint shame and the thought, I love smoking and yet I hate being a smoker. I am not mocking that flare. I felt it. I only ask you to place it where it belongs. Seconds at the tip. Not the day. Not the sofa. Not the garage made clean. It comes and leaves you where you were, only a little more chained.

Does a chooser call seconds of heaven followed by shame a choice he would recommend? Would you teach your child to choose it? Would you need mints after a true pleasure? The fact is, hiding is not choosing. Reaching before thought is not choosing. Needing a story in your voice to cover the reaching is not choosing.

It does not serve you. You serve it.

### Why I Am Not Asking You To Stop Yet

See what you now understand about your own garage moment.

You understand you hide what you claim to love. You understand your hand moves before decision. You understand a tiny tug and a large story keep you, not courage or character. You understand choice would not need hiding.

With that understanding, stopping now would only confuse the investigation. If you stop or cut down before you see clearly, every tug will feel like proof you love it, and every hard hour will be credited to missing it. You will be fighting in fog. I do not want you fighting. I want you watching with curiosity, smoking as you normally smoke, letting each dose show itself for what it is while we test what it actually does for you.

There is no doom here. There is only clear seeing while you continue, so that when the moment comes you walk out because you see, not because you forced yourself. You are looking at an escape, not a loss, and clear eyes are how we reach it.

Keep smoking as you do. Keep the pack where it is. Keep the garage tin if you must for now. Your job is not to change the pattern yet. Your job is to watch it with the new words in mind. When the hand moves, note the tug. When the mind says choice, note the smoke. When the spray comes out, ask why choice needs perfume. Let each ordinary reach show you the trap at work while we keep reading together.

I ask this because confusion helps the trap and clarity ends it. A smoker told to stop before he sees will credit every edgy hour to missing pleasure. A smoker allowed to watch while he smokes will see the edgy hour arrive between doses and quiet for minutes after, again and again, until the story wears thin. That watching is not effort. It is the open mind you brought from the meeting room, carried into the garage, the sofa, the car, the queue.

You have no need to test yourself today. You have no need to count or to hide more carefully or to confess. You have only to keep reading in order and let the next points finish. We will examine what the dose truly gives, one claim at a time, against your own mornings and breaks and meals. For now it is enough to see that reaching is not choosing, and that two keepers, one tiny in the body and one large in thought, have been doing the choosing for you.

I-02 — DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

SUMMARY
- Hiding with spray and mints shows the habit is not lived as open liking.
- The hand moving before thought shows the decision happens after the reach.
- Sitting divided on the sofa shows the cost is paid in covering, not only in smoking.
- Seconds of flare at lighting leave the day unchanged and bring shame after.
- A small tug in the body plus a large learned story keep the pattern in place.
- Watching steadily while smoking as normal lets clear seeing come before action.
