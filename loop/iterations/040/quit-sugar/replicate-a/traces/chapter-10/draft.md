Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

IN THIS CHAPTER
A Friday tin kept for Friday; a cliff edge with a promised short fall; a permitted biscuit with the box empty before the swallow ends; an empty cupboard at home; a calendar marked tomorrow; a swapped packet in a kinder coat; a seatbelt clicked for the jolt

*There is no safe sweet to keep, only a whole trap to leave.*

### THE FRIDAY TIN

Keep the week you know best in front of you, because it tells on the bargain you are still trying to strike.

Monday to Thursday you are good. You call it good. No packets at the desk. Fruit at lunch. Tea straight. You walk past the aisle with your chin high and tell yourself you have cracked it. Friday is allowed. Just Friday. A tin, a bar, a pudding after supper. A deserved close to a clean week.

Watch what Friday does to Monday.

Monday morning the tin already talks from the shelf. You do not open it. You picture it. Tuesday the picture sharpens. You plan which shop, which flavour, what time. Wednesday you are restless at three o'clock in a way you were never restless before you made the rule. Thursday night you lay out the evening to make room for it, you eat a little less at tea to earn it, you tell the room you are looking forward to tomorrow. Friday at last arrives heavy with ceremony. Hands quick. First bite loud in the head. Second bite already sad because the tin will soon be gone.

We all lived that week. We, who swore a limit would calm the want, found the limit taught the want to shout louder between allowances. The dose on Friday did not taste better because Friday food is better. It glowed because Monday to Thursday starved the eye and fed the fantasy. Denial by day, blaze by night. That is the timetable talking.

Ask flat, with the tin still shut on Monday.

Did the clean days feel calm — or did they feel like waiting?

Did Friday taste richer — or did hunger plus waiting paint richness onto paper?

Did the week end satisfied — or did Saturday morning start with Thursday creeping back in, a small extra on Saturday because Friday was so good, a small advance on Thursday because Friday is so near?

One honest answer runs through all three. The allowance did not contain the eating. It trained it. Anticipation fattened the glow. The glow pulled the hand. The hand reached beyond the line. The line moved.

That is why the Friday promise always creeps. Thursday joins. Saturday joins. The family-size bag arrives because the small bag feels too mean for a whole week of being good. The fact is plain. Cut down and you do not smoke less fire. You fan it.

The precious Friday is how the Sugar Trap stays hired.

Hear it short:

Kept sweet. Kept want.

### THE CLIFF WITH A SHORT FALL

A friend walks you to a cliff on a bright morning. Sea below. Gulls. He points to the drop, sheer and white, and says with a kind smile:

“Jump. You will only fall a few metres, I promise. Just a short fall. Just on Fridays.”

You would stare at him. You would check his face for drink. No one with sight believes a cliff can be jumped a little. The height decides the fall, not the promise made at the edge. To step off is to fall. The length of the fall is not yours to set once your feet leave rock.

That is the exact shape of the Friday-dessert allowance. The safe fall. The small exception. The little sweet kept like a pet.

BAD SUGAR is that cliff. A dose is a step off. You do not get to vote how far the drop runs once you are over. The hand decides after the first bite, not before it. The mouth films, the Nibbler grumbles, the old bell rings, and the fall runs its full length while the promise made at the edge flutters somewhere above.

Ventriloquise the negotiator in you, because he speaks in your accent:

“Just one on Fridays cannot hurt. A small treat once a week is fine.”

Print it whole to answer it whole.

If once a week were fine, why did once a week need a rule, a tin, a locked day, a week of thinking about it?

If a small treat were small, why did the small treat grow a ceremony, a shop route, a time, a mood, a second tin for safety?

If the edge were safe, why did every safe week end with creep — Thursday tasting, Saturday finishing, Monday planning?

One straight line answers all three. Safe is not a size. Safe is not a day. There is no ledge halfway down a cliff with tea and a view. There is rock and there is air.

The fact is settled. Inside BAD SUGAR there is no little fall. Leave the edge alone and you never need to measure the drop.

Hear the verdict for the cliff plain:

No ledge. No short fall.

### ONE MEANS ALL

Now take the smallest promise of all. Not Friday. One.

One biscuit with tea. One square from the shared bar. One spoon of the birthday slice to be polite. You tell yourself a single dose proves control. You tell yourself this time the hand will stop where the mouth says stop.

Watch that single with clear eyes, because your own evenings already ran the test.

A permitted sliver arrives on a saucer. Tea hot. Talk warm. First bite sweet to sting. Second half of the biscuit already in fingers before the first is swallowed. Wrapper rustle from the pack across the room. Fingers walk. Mouth films. Throat calls for water and hand calls for more at the same moment. Ten minutes later the pack lies light, the saucer holds crumbs, and a sour mouth asks why a grown eater with a full belly feels pulled to the cupboard again.

You know that pull. You do not starve the Nibbler by feeding it on Fridays.

Ask with crumbs still on the saucer.

Did one close the matter — or did one open it?

Did the hand stop kinder after a taste — or did the taste sharpen the hand?

Did control show itself in the single — or did the single show who holds control once the dose is in?

One honest answer ends all three. The craving, once triggered, carries the binge. That carry is the signature of the trap, not a sentence on you for life. It says nothing about your worth. It says everything about what a dose does after it lands.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That bar was never a genuine treat or fuel. It was a starter pistol.

Hear the verdict for the single straight:

One lit. All followed.

### THE EMPTY CUPBOARD AT HOME

“I don't keep it in the house.”

You say it with pride, and I hear the pride because I once said it the same way. Cupboards clean. Drawers bare. No tin. No bag. Willpower polished like a doorstep. You tell yourself the house proves you are nearly free.

Ask where the want went when the house went bare.

Did the evenings grow calm — or did the shop on the corner grow bright?

Did the hand rest — or did the hand learn new hours, the garage at ten, the till at lunch, the drawer at work?

Did the head go quiet — or did the head run a louder film about Friday, about parties, about other people's cupboards, about the office tin you now visit with a joke and a second visit without one?

One answer fits every clean cupboard that still keeps a kept sweet outside it. The trap did not leave. It moved address. A dose kept at arm's length is still kept. Distance makes it precious. Precious makes it loud. Loud pulls feet to wherever it lives.

We all tried the geography cure. We, who emptied shelves, found ourselves walking further for the same fix, paying more, hiding better, counting the walk as proof of control while the walk proved the leash. A prisoner who moves his cell to a nicer street is still locked.

The house-rule also breeds its twin lie. Home clean, so outside does not count. A biscuit with a neighbour is manners. Cake at work is team spirit. Ice cream on holiday is holiday. The line drawn round your own shelf leaks everywhere else, and every leak is forgiven because home stays clean. Soon the outside eats more than the inside ever did.

Hear the short truth for the bare shelf:

House bare. Mind full.

A sweet kept anywhere is kept everywhere. The only cupboard that matters is the one between your ears, and that cupboard stays stocked while one exception sits crowned as prize.

### TOMORROW ON THE CALENDAR

“I'll quit tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays.”

Tomorrow is the kindest sounding trap of all. It asks nothing today. It promises everything later. It lets you read on with a full mouth and a light heart, because the reckoning always sits one page ahead.

Look at tomorrow with me as an investigator, not as a mourner.

Take last month's tomorrow. What date wore it? The birthday came. Did the start come with it — or did the birthday bring its table, its corner piece, its tomorrow-after-the-leftovers? The holidays came. Did the start come with them — or did travel bring stations, stations bring kiosks, kiosks bring just-for-the-journey? Monday came. Did the start come — or did Monday bring a hard morning that needed getting through first?

Ask with the calendar still open.

If tomorrow were safer, why has every past tomorrow arrived with its own reason to wait again?

If the dose were harmless till the birthday, why are you reading a book to leave it?

If waiting helped, why do waited-for Fridays taste more frantic, not calmer?

One honest line answers all three. Tomorrow never thins the trap. It fattens it. Each delayed day adds another memory of glow, another rehearsal of anticipation, another proof to the whisper that life needs paper to mark time. Delay is not preparation. Delay is feeding.

That whisper is the Sweet Con talking, not you.

I state it flat because warmth without plainness would leave you waiting. There will always be a birthday somewhere ahead. There will always be a holiday, a dinner, a hard week, a good week that deserves marking. A line that moves for occasions is not a line. It is a ribbon in wind.

Hear the verdict for the calendar:

Tomorrow kept. Trap kept.

### THE SWAP IN A KINDER COAT

When the Friday tin shakes and the single fails and tomorrow slips, the negotiator tries a cleverer door. The swap.

“Fine. No sugar. I will switch. Sweeteners in tea. Diet drinks. Low-sugar bars. Fruit juice to wean. A kinder hit to taper down.”

It sounds scientific. It sounds moderate. It is the same cliff in a kinder coat.

Taste the swap truly. The pale tablet in tea still teaches the tongue to ask for cover before flavour counts as flavour. The diet bottle still keeps the hand on the cold ring-pull at three o'clock, the throat waiting for flood, the head waiting for lift. The low-sugar bar still keeps the rustle, the tear, the chew-chew-search, the standing at the sink after. The powder changes. The ritual stays. The bell stays. The pull stays.

Ask with the swapped cup still warm.

Did the swap close the three-o'clock reach — or did it keep the reach on a new leash?

Did the mouth learn clean — or did it learn to need sweet to call anything kind?

Did the evenings widen — or did they narrow round a different packet with the same clock?

One answer fits every swapped shelf. Swap the product and you remain a user, still hooked. The Nibbler does not read labels. It reads sweet on the tongue and ritual in the hand. Feed either and it grumbles for more. Starve both and its voice thins to nothing in days.

The fact is blunt. Sweeteners keep the belief breathing. Juice keeps the flood-and-drop lesson in place. Low means still. Diet means still. Kinder means still. The trap does not mind which coat the dose wears while the dose still teaches that empty can be filled from a packet.

We, who once lined swaps in a row like medicine bottles, found each bottle led back to the same drawer. The taper never tapered. It spaced hits to make each hit glow harder. Spaced hits teach harder. Harder glow teaches sooner return. That yo-yo of hold-and-flood is the very shape that locks the pattern tight. Break the pattern by breaking the belief that any sweet hit fills, not by changing the wrapper.

Hear the verdict for the swap plain:

Coat changed. Hook stayed.

### THE SEATBELT FOR THE JOLT

Now hear the margin truly, because totality scares only when it sounds like panic.

A seatbelt sits across your chest every drive. It does not drive the car. It does not steer you into walls. It waits, quiet, for the jolt no driver plans — the sudden stop, the stranger's error. No sane driver says, because a belt exists, erratic driving is now safe. No sane passenger begs to test the belt for fun.

Your body holds a like mercy for the accidental crumb. A sauce thickened where you did not ask. A biscuit taken in good faith at a table that swore none was in it. A mouthful before the label was read. The body copes with the blip. The stomach settles. The day goes on. No bell rebuilds its church on one unchosen crumb.

But the mind cannot afford a deliberate one. Mind steers. Body rides. Steer into the dose on purpose — just this Friday, just this swap, just to test — and the belief wakes that the dose fills. Belief waked rebuilds want. Want rebuilt refills hands. The belt was sewn for accidents, not for steering into walls to prove the stitching.

If changing what you eat could carry risk for you, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Mark the line clean. Accidental is accident. Deliberate is decision. Guard the belief and the behaviour follows. Panic over a crumb rebuilds the very fear that feeds the trap, and fear never kept anyone clear. Sight keeps clear. You saw the cliff. You saw the tin. You saw the single carry all. With that sight, a crumb stays a crumb. Without that sight, a crumb becomes a permission slip.

Ask inside the car, belt clicked, hands on the wheel.

Does the belt invite the crash — or does it free you to drive straight without dread of every bump?

Does one jolt prove belts useless — or does it prove their use while straight driving goes on?

Does safety live in testing the edge — or in holding the wheel steady while the road runs ordinary?

One straight answer ends all three. Totality is not terror of dust. It is refusal of steering into the wall. The wheel stays held. The road stays ordinary. The belt stays quiet.

Hear the verdict for the belt short:

Blip borne. Wheel held.

### A RULE OR A RELEASE

One last voice stands at the door, cleverer than Friday, kinder than tomorrow. It attacks not the sweet but the method.

“Isn't never just another strict rule? Another diet with a sterner face? You call it freedom but it sounds like prison. You tell me I am brainwashed. Isn't this brainwashing back?”

I print it whole because it deserves honour, not scorn. It is the final script of the old training, dressed as philosophy.

Test it against your own weeks, not against words.

Did the Friday rule free your mind — or did it fill Monday to Thursday with pictures of Friday?

Did the house rule free your feet — or did it send feet further to find what the house lacked?

Did the swap free your tongue — or did it keep the tongue asking for cover?

One answer fits all three. Those rules bound because they kept the prize crowned. They said the dose holds worth and you must circle it with fences. A fence round a crown makes the crown shine harder. That is prison with better paint.

This is the other way round.

We do not fence a prize. We unmask a con. There is no glow left to circle once sight shows the glow was waiting painted as taste, label painted as flavour, relief painted over a low the last dose dug. To stop circling a crown after the jewels show as glass is not discipline. It is waking. No one calls a waking man strict for leaving a ditch he now sees.

Ask the last honest pair, because they close the file.

If this were prison, why do prison weeks end flat, foggy, pulled to the drawer, while sighted weeks end with mouth clear and evenings long in a good way?

If this were brainwashing, why does brainwashing need bright packets, jingles, cartoon friends and daily repetition, while sight needs only your own saucer, your own tin, your own calendar to prove itself?

If never were loss, why does the thought of never being tricked again land light in the chest, not heavy?

One line answers. The heaviness was the tug-of-war you lived for years. Both ropes cut at once feels strange for an hour and then feels like morning.

You stand now where every negotiator stands before the lock clicks. The evidence of your own hand is in. Friday fattens want. One carries all. The bare house still dreams. Tomorrow never comes. The swap keeps the hook. The belt covers the blip but never covers steering in. No corner inside BAD SUGAR stays safe once the belief that any corner fills is left alive.

Hold that whole picture steady while the room stays ordinary. Kettle warm. Feet on lino. Tea in hand. The tin on the shelf holds no vote unless you hand it one. The thought of a special sweet may cross, thin as cloud, and pass while talk goes on, while supper cooks, while the evening runs its good length. A passing cloud is weather, not orders.

With that sight held, there is only one clean decision left to make about kept sweets, and you are ready to make it now because the argument is already won.

I-11 — NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

SUMMARY
- A Friday allowance fills the waiting days with pictures and makes the dose glow harder.
- A safe fall off a high edge is not a size the jumper gets to choose.
- A permitted single opens the evening rather than closing it.
- A bare shelf at home still leaves the mind stocked when the sweet lives elsewhere.
- A start dated tomorrow moves with the calendar and never arrives.
- A swapped powder keeps the ritual, the reach and the pull in place.
- An accidental crumb is a jolt the body rides out, a deliberate dose is steering back in.
- A total line is not a fence round a prize, it is sight that the prize was glass.
