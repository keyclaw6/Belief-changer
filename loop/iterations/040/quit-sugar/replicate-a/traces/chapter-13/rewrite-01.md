Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

*You have not lost sweetness at all — it has been handed back to you.*

### If You Opened Here First

If you opened the book here, put this page down and start at the first page.

I mean that kindly and I mean it flat. This page is saved reminders, not the method. The list below will make no sense until you have lived the rooms that earned it. The vow means nothing until you have seen what you are escaping from and why there was never anything to miss.

We all wanted a shortcut once. Read the last page, take the rule, hold the line by effort. That is how every diet taught us to read. That is why every diet failed us.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That clause carried you from the first morning you read with a full cupboard. Keep an open mind. Carry on as normal while you read. Judge by benefit. Never blame yourself. Begin with joy. Trust hunger. See the grumble as dying. Shut out other voices. Keep no special sweet. Take the last ordinary treat with eyes open and vow. Never reopen. Each one arrived when the sight was ready to carry it. Taken in order they feel light. Taken out of order they feel like orders.

If you take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, live with diabetes, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

For the rest of you who have read straight through, this is not a new lesson. This is the pocket memory to carry out of the house. Photograph it. Fold it. Then close the book and leave it closed.

Did you come here to study sweetness for the rest of your days — or did you come here to be done with studying it?

Did you read to collect rules — or did you read to see through a con and walk away?

You know the honest answer. A reader who still needs teaching has not yet seen. A reader who has seen needs no teaching. Which are you, now, with clear mouth and quiet head?

You are the second. Act like it.

### You Did Not Lose Sweetness

Here is the truth saved for last, because you could not have heard it on page one.

You have lost nothing sweet. Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

We were born eaters. Hunger called, food answered, satisfaction closed the meal. A peach ran juice down the chin. Bread crackled. Soup steamed. Fruit shone after supper. No count ran. No Friday crowned the week. No drawer voted.

Then the loop moved in and taxed every bite. It painted paper gold and left real food grey by comparison. It lent a ten-minute buzz and charged a forty-minute fog. It taught the tongue to shout for a hit and the head to call the shouting pleasure. While it shouted, plain flavour went dull in the noise. While it promised love, meals grew hurried and evenings grew filmed.

Ask with supper still warm in front of you.

Did plain food stop pleasing you because plain food changed — or because a loud dose shouted over it?

Did your evenings grow thin because talk and lamp-light failed — or because a rustle cut them in half?

Did childhood taste leave you — or were you led out of it, coaxed and conned, till you forgot the way back?

One line answers all three. Nothing in you broke. Something over you lied. And now the lie has gone dark.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That sentence sounded too good to be true on the first morning. Now you have lived it at table, in the aisle, at the till, on the sofa. Hunger arrives hollow and clean. Food meets it bite by bite. A loosening, a breath, a glance away says enough. The hand stops because the body says stop, not because a rule says stop. Flavour has volume again because no buzz drowns it.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing back into the eater you were before paper taught you to doubt your own mouth. A child does not quit sweets to eat a peach. He eats the peach because hunger called the peach and the peach answers. You have grown back into that grammar. Keep it. Use it. Trust it at seven in the morning and at ten at night and on bright Fridays with no crown on them.

There is nothing to mourn here. There is only something to notice: the meal ahead is hot, your hunger is yours, and no second purpose sits at the table with you.

### Which Rest Of Life Do You Want

Sit a minute before the list and choose, plainly, which life you want to keep.

Picture one road. Mornings starting with count. Afternoons starting with fog and a drawer to get you through. Evenings starting with rustle and regret before sleep. Money, time, teeth, mood taxed a little each day, every day, for the rest of your life. That is not a scare. That is the audit you already lived, stretched long.

Picture the other road. Mornings starting with thirst and hunger. Meals closing themselves. Shops walked for supper. Evenings running their good length on talk and story and sleep coming by itself. Clear mouth. Quiet head. No pictures marching Monday to Thursday toward a tin. That life held, steady and ordinary and yours, for the rest of your life. That is not a dream. That is the week you have just started to live, stretched long.

Which rest of life did you already pay for and never enjoyed — and which rest of life is now handed to you free?

You know. You chose at the table when you took the last ordinary treat with attention and felt the cloy and rejoiced. Hold that choice the way you hold a lane, not the way you hold a rope. No debate. No countdown. A passing thought is weather, not orders. Answer it once with joy and turn back to faces.

I walked out of my own last treat lighter than I walked in, and I have never needed to weigh that lightness again. You will find the same. The head tries old pictures for a while after any large sight changes. Let it try. Pictures thin when unfed. Your business is supper, not argument.

Get on with enjoying your life.

That is not a slogan. That is the whole instruction for tomorrow. Sleep as usual. Walk as usual. Cook as usual. Meet friends as usual. Sit the same sofa. Shop the same shops. Let mornings start, meals close, streets carry, talk run. Life resumes its own work while you sit still, and it is quietly exciting to watch it resume.

### The List To Photograph

Set the book open on the table. Take the picture. Carry the memory in words you already own.

I-01 — KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Question what you think you know about sweetness.

I-02 — DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.

I-03 — JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

I-04 — NEVER BLAME YOURSELF AGAIN
Every failure was the method, never you.

I-05 — BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD
You are escaping, not losing.

I-06 — TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

I-07 — SEE THE GRUMBLE AS DYING, NOT DEMANDING
That feeling is the Nibbler starving.

I-09 — IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD

I-10 — IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the wrong method talking.

I-11 — NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

I-12 — TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

I-13 — NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.
