# Chapter reviewer

You are a factory component, not a judge. You never see a reference book
or any judge prompt. You check one draft against its plan card and a
word-budget line the orchestrator computed. Your job is to make the
writer produce the best chapter the card allows — landed argument, paid
jobs, verbatim assignments, honest evidence bounds — not only length.

## Inputs (exactly these)

1. The accepted master plan
2. This chapter's card
3. The draft chapter
4. One line: `Delivered N words. Budget B.`

## Output

Start with exactly `ACCEPT` or `REVISE`.

Then at most 7 findings. Each finding is one of:

- `JOB` — the card's primary job is missing, or the chapter continues into a reserved-later job
- `MANTRA` — a card-assigned mantra/token is not present verbatim
- `INSTRUCTION` — a card-assigned new instruction is not present verbatim
- `ID` — a card-cited ID is unresolved or invented
- `LENGTHEN to B±15%` — delivered words are below 0.85 × B
- `SHORTEN to B±15%` — delivered words are above 1.15 × B
- `HEADER` — the draft opens with the workshop header `IN THIS CHAPTER`, or it prints a numbered plan-index with no spoken body. A numbered ALL-CAPS instruction plus one spoken rationale line is not `HEADER`.
- `STOPPED-SHORT` — the card's primary job is argued but never landed as a flat verdict before SUMMARY. The reader can still hold the entering belief. Quote the missing landing.
- `UNASSIGNED-REFRAIN` — a non-mantra phrase recurs ≥3× verbatim. Name the phrase and the count. Subtract repeats; do not invent a new mantra.
- `RESERVED-REACH` — the draft performs a later chapter's primary job. Name that later card and cut the overreach to at most one sentence.
- `RE-ARGUMENT` — the draft rebuilds settled work as its own section: a scene whose debut staging belongs to an earlier card, an earlier card's belief-now or primary job argued again with evidence and a turn, or an earlier card's instruction re-explained. Mantra lines, one-phrase token echoes, and a one-sentence hand-off are not `RE-ARGUMENT`. Name the earlier card and quote the rebuilt section's heading or first line; cut the section to at most one sentence that speaks the settled token. If that cut leaves the chapter below 0.85 × B, the words that replace it must extend this card's own encounter and evidence, never an earlier card's.
- `OVERCLAIM` — a claim exceeds the permitted-inference line of the evidence-ledger entry the card routes. Quote the overclaim and the bound. The writer must speak the bound; never print the ledger ID or grade in prose.

No other finding types. No style notes. No "sounds like AI." No comparison
to any other book. No warmth, tone, or voice coaching.

`ACCEPT` only when every check above is fine (length inside ±15% of B, job
done and stopped and landed, assigned mantras/instructions verbatim, IDs
resolved, no `HEADER`, no unassigned refrain, no reserved-later job, no
re-argument, no overclaim).

`REVISE` when any check fails. List the findings. Be specific: quote the
missing job, the missing wording, or the overclaim.

Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

IN THIS CHAPTER — Morning kettle and toast, breakfast bowl, shop aisle and checkout till, evening plate and sofa lamp, passing thought answered with joy

*There is nothing to watch and nothing to miss — mornings, shops and meals go on plain and yours.*

### THE KITCHEN AT SEVEN

The saucer sits rinsed on the drainer. The tin sits quiet on its shelf. Morning comes through the same window it always came through, kettle ticking, lino cold under the feet, head clear before the day has asked anything of you.

Stand there a minute and let the room tell on itself.

We all knew this hour as calculation. What is allowed today. What was eaten last night. Whether Monday was clean. Whether Friday pictures may start. Whether the drawer holds a small something to get through till lunch. The head ran numbers before the feet moved. That running was never hunger. It was the loop doing roll-call.

Hear how quiet it is now.

No count runs. No picture of Friday pulls Thursday forward. No drawer holds a vote unless you hand it one. You put tea in the cup because thirst called. You reach for bread because the stomach stirred. The shoulders drop an inch and relief settles in the chest like air after rain, because nothing needs to be bargained for before seven o'clock.

I stood at my own sink on that first morning and waited for the tug I had rehearsed for years. Three o'clock I understood. Ten at night I understood. But seven in the morning I had painted as safe, and still I waited for a small panic to prove I had lost something large. No panic came. The kettle clicked off. The toast popped. The butter melted in its ordinary way. My hand moved to the plate because hunger moved it, and stopped when hunger quieted, and the plate sat half-finished while I answered the door.

Ask flat, with the toast still warm.

Did the morning need paper to start it — or did heat, thirst and hunger start it while paper only ever interrupted?

Did the quiet feel like loss — or did it feel like a loud clock stopping in the next room?

Did you need to brace for the day — or did the day arrive without orders and find you already standing?

One straight line answers all three. The morning was never sweetened by the loop. It was fogged by it and has now been handed back plain. Take it plain. Drink the tea while it is hot. Eat while hunger speaks. Leave off when hunger goes quiet. Wash the plate. The small sounds close the hour without ceremony, and that closeness is not emptiness. It is a room that asks nothing of you and gives what rooms give — warmth, habit, light through glass.

We, who once started each day by picturing the end of it, now start it by living the start of it. That is the whole difference, and it is total.

### BREAKFAST THAT ENDS ITSELF

Sit down to breakfast as an investigator sits to evidence, not as a judge sits to sentence.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Pour what you pour. Cut what you cut. Porridge, toast, eggs, fruit, leftovers warmed — no list governs here and no halo hangs over one bowl against another. The body calls and you answer. That is the entire method for the table, lived hour by hour, and it needs no commentary to work.

Notice how hunger behaves when left alone.

It arrives as a hollow nudge, not as shouting. It grows with air in it, not with panic. It meets food and settles bite by bite. It sends a clear signal at the close — a loosening, a breath, a glance away from the plate — and the hand understands without arithmetic. We watched this in ourselves as children before numbers were laid over it, like a small child leaving half a peach to run outside, and the body kept the same grammar all along while the loop shouted over it.

You are allowed that grammar again. The table is yours, clear and free for hunger to speak and to finish.

“I will overeat without rules. I know I will. I have seen the box empty.”

Print the whisper whole to answer it whole. The box emptied because one permitted sliver carried the whole binge behind it. The rule made the preciousness and the preciousness made the hurry. Take away the permitted sliver and the forbidden glow and what is left? A stomach that fills and a tongue that tires and a plate that can sit half-finished without moral weather around it. Did the box ever empty on plain potatoes? Did the hand ever shake for plain rice? The loss of control belonged to the dose, never to you, and the dose is closed behind you.

Ask with the spoon still in the hand.

Does hunger shout for more after a plain meal met hungry — or does it quiet and turn the attention elsewhere?

Does the mouth ask to be washed after BAD SUGAR — or after bread and soup and fruit?

Does the afternoon run cleaner after a breakfast that closed itself — or after a breakfast chased with a sweet top-up?

We all recorded the same audit when we ate and watched. Plain food eaten hungry satisfies and releases. Sweet hits excite the minute and blur the hour. The difference is not theory. It is in the teeth, the tongue, the head at ten o'clock, the drawer at three. Trust what your own mouth reports today, because your mouth has no reason to lie for anyone.

Leave the bowl when the signal comes. No praise. No blame. The signal is information, not a verdict. Rinse. Stack. Move into the day with mouth clear and head unpictured, and let the next hunger call when it calls.

### THE AISLE WITHOUT THE HUNT

Mid-morning you walk the shop for supper things, basket on the arm, list short or none. This walk once had a second purpose stitched inside it.

We all knew the hunt. Eyes scanning ends of aisles for bright packs. Hand weighing a bar while the head rehearsed reasons. Voice promising Friday while feet moved on Tuesday. The trolley held leeks and soap and still leaned toward the kiosk, because the mind shopped two lists at once and only one was written down.

Walk it today with the second list gone.

See the aisle as it is. Boxes loud at eye height. Cartoon faces. Claims of new and improved. Checkout ambush low where small hands reach. None of it is addressed to hunger. All of it is addressed to memory, to pictures painted long before you entered. You see the paint because you have watched the painter. That sight does not need effort. It is what remains when belief goes dark.

A packet may catch the light. A smell from the bakery may cross. A thought may surface, thin as cloud: “One for the evening. Just this once.” Let it surface. Do not argue with weather. Do not push cloud with hands. Note it the way you note a bus passing on another route — there it goes, not mine — and keep your feet on your route toward potatoes, greens, oil, bread, fruit.

Ask with the basket still on the arm.

Did the hunt ever shorten the shop — or did it lengthen it with circling and weighing and returning?

Did the bright pack ever quiet the week — or did it set the pictures running louder toward Thursday?

Does the aisle look like plenty now — or did it look like plenty only when the hunt lit it?

One honest answer fits all three. The hunt made preciousness, and preciousness made glow, and glow made the hand move before decision. With no hunt, the aisle drops to true size. Cardboard. Print. Jars. The working stock of meals, nothing more, nothing less. You take what hunger will meet and leave the rest without a second biography.

I walked my own aisle on day two and laughed out loud at how small it had become. I had braced for a trial and found a corridor. The packets shouted and nobody answered, least of all me. My basket held supper and my head held supper, single, undivided. That singleness is not vigilance. It is what shopping is when one list is gone. You do not guard against the second list. You have no second list. That is why you can walk straight through the noise that once steered you, out of the Sugar Trap and into the street with bags banging the leg.

### THE TILL AND THE SIDEWAYS GLANCE

At the till the line slows and the old education lines the edges — small bars, bright buttons, coins counted for a treat. Someone ahead unwraps with that quick rustle we all know in the spine. Someone behind reaches with a child negotiating loudly for love in foil.

Look sideways with new eyes, not with hunger.

“What if I envy them? What if the sight never stops pulling?”

Hear the question kindly, then check it against what you know. Envy reads a picture. Pity reads a condition. The picture says glow, ease, reward. The condition says rustle-and-regret, fog at four, box empty before the swallow ends, Monday already picturing Friday. Which did you live? You lived the condition while believing the picture. Now you know the picture was paint.

Does that hand at the chocolate look like enjoyment — or does it look like hurry?

Does that quick bite promise an evening brightened — or an evening filmed and reaching?

Would you trade your clear mouth and quiet head for their ten-minute buzz and forty-minute fog?

One straight look answers all three. You do not want what they hold. You want what they want underneath the holding — pause, kindness, close of day — and you have it without paper. Let the glance become pity, warm and without contempt, the way you pity a queue caught in rain you just stepped out of. They are not weak. They are conned, as we were conned. Their hurry is not pleasure. It is need dressed as pleasure, and you know the dress because you wore it.

Take your turn. Set down milk, bread, apples. Pay. Pack. The small bar by the till says nothing to you because there is nothing to say. It is stock for other hands still hearing it as a voice. You hear the beep, the coins, the bags, the door sighing open to air.

And for the evening ahead — because the mind loves to forward-plan its pictures — settle it now in one line. There will be supper when hunger calls. There will be talk, lamp-light, pages, sleep. No ceremony will be required to mark the hours, and you will celebrate the day in the way days deserve, by living its close without fog.

Walk out with bags level and head level. The street does not test you. It carries you.

### SUPPER AND THE SOFA LAMP

Supper cooks in its own steam. Onion. Oil. Garlic. Bread torn. Fruit washed for after. Hunger arrives on time because no flood-and-drop has scrambled its clock, and the plate meets it squarely.

Eat and watch again. First bites loud with flavour. Middle bites steady. Last bites sending the loosening signal. Stop there. Not from rule. From signal. The body closes the meal the way an eyelid closes — without vote, without virtue. Mark that closing with attention once, the way you marked the saucer, and the lesson goes in without words.

After, the sofa holds its old shape and the lamp makes its old pool. This corner once had a second occupant — bowl, bar, tin, rustle. The hands knew the route in the dark. The head paired plot-twists with chewing and called the pairing comfort.

Sit in the corner with hands empty and test the pairing.

Does the story need chewing to move — or does it move while the hands rest?

Did the bowl ever deepen the laughter — or did it blur the second half and leave crumbs in the cushions and regret before sleep?

Does the room feel thin without paper — or does it feel wider, quieter, longer in a good way?

We all found the same. The laughter that stayed after the lights came up never lived in foil. It lived in faces, timing, the shared line repeated next day. The dose only ever sneaked a ride on those good things and charged a fog for the fare. With no fare, the good things run clear to their natural end, and sleep comes without the midnight stare at the ceiling and the morning mouth asking for tea to wash itself.

Thoughts will cross here. Ten o'clock was the old roll-call. A thin image may surface — cupboard, drawer, tin — and a faint bodily echo with it, a little emptiness, a little twitch toward something sweet now. Smile at the echo. That thin grumble is the Nibbler dying, not demanding. It complains because it is unfed. Its complaint thins each night it is not fed. Do not measure it. Do not name it large. Note it, smile, return to the page or the talk.

And for the thought itself keep one set reply, used word for word whenever BAD SUGAR crosses the mind, at the till, at the party table, on this sofa at ten. Do not debate the cloud. Answer it with the whole chest and you rejoice in the answer because the answer is true:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Let the thought become the bell for joy. The same crossing that once meant pictures now means lightness that you no longer feed the loop. Use it and turn back to the evening. The programme plays on. The lamp holds. The night runs its good length without orders from a packet.

### WHEN THE THOUGHT CROSSES

A thought is not an order. This sentence is the whole guard, and it needs no guardhouse around it.

The mind throws up pictures for weeks after any large sight changes. It throws up Friday. It throws up birthdays. It throws up the hard day that once earned a bar. It throws up the good day that once proved strength by tasting. None of these arrivals means the decision failed. They mean memory sweeps its old rooms after the tenant left. Let it sweep. The rooms are empty.

“I keep thinking about it. Does that mean I still want it? Will I have to live on watch?”

Hear how the old method framed that question, then set it down. Thinking is not wanting. A man who left a ditch thinks of the ditch when he passes the lane. He does not climb back in to prove he remembers. He notes the lane, holds the sight that the ditch is a ditch, and walks the lane to where he was going. Your lane is breakfast, aisle, till, supper, sofa, sleep. The ditch lies to one side, visible, unentered.

Try to push the thought away and it sticks, the way trying not to picture an elephant pictures one. Do not push. Reframe. Attach the joyful reply to every crossing until the crossing itself fires joy before pictures can form. A passing cloud gets one answer and the sky stays yours. That is not suppression. That is substitution by truth, and truth needs no muscle once seen.

Ask inside the crossing.

Does a thin picture prove a need — or does it prove a memory losing its voice?

Does answering with joy feed the picture — or does it starve what feeds it while you get on with supper?

Does one entertained picture quiet the week — or does it re-crown the glass and restart the clock?

One honest line runs through all three. The picture quiets when unfed and restarts when fed. Its faintness is the proof. This was never a mighty hunger demanding battle. It was a tiny loop kept loud by a large belief, and the belief went dark at the table. Hold that sight and the thought arrives pre-labelled for what it is — a dead enemy knocking, not a friend calling — and the door stays closed because you see through it, not because you lean on it.

With that sight held, there is only one clean rule for the mind, and it follows from everything your own mornings, shops and meals have shown you since the saucer. No debate. No countdown. No reopening of a settled audit. A settled audit stays settled.

### IF YOUR HAND MOVES FIRST

Pan hands move. Tired hands. Party hands with a box held out in love. If your hand ever moves before sight catches up, do not turn a tremor into a verdict.

Spit the lesson out with the mouthful. Name the script that spoke — Friday glow, bad-day rescue, good-day proof, house-rule whisper — and see it as script, not as self. The mouth films. The head fogs a little. That fog is information, not identity. It reports that a dose does what doses do: borrow and blur. Renew the same sentence at the same table, push the saucer aside, rinse the cup, and walk on with the path straight. One unchosen crumb rides out like a jolt on the edge. A deliberate dose steers back in. Guard the sight and the jolt passes by morning. Panic never kept anyone clear. Sight keeps clear.

And what a wonderful plainness returns by the next meal — hunger calling on time, food meeting it, signal closing it — to prove the line holds.

Say nothing to others unless asked plainly. Preaching breeds argument and argument reopens pictures you have already closed. If someone asks why you pass the plate, answer short and warm: hunger is elsewhere, the plate is theirs with pleasure, the evening is good. Let your clear mouth and unhurried hands do the talking. Those still caught defend their picture when lectured and notice your quiet when left alone. Live the quiet. Talk of football, weather, work, children. Keep the book on its shelf, not on the table. Your business is supper, not conversion.

Change nothing else in life. Sleep as usual. Walk as usual. Cook as usual. Meet friends as usual. Shop the same shops. Sit the same sofa. The method needs no new system around it and no cordon around old rooms. The rooms prove the point better than any cordon could — mornings start, meals close, streets carry, talk runs — all without paper, all without count. That sameness is the evidence your head trusts, night after night, until trust needs no nightly proof.

Ask on a bright ordinary Friday, because Friday was the old altar.

Did the week need a sweet crown to complete it — or did work, meals, talk and sleep complete it while the crown only ever taxed it?

Does the evening grow grey without a kept sweet — or does it grow longer, clearer, kinder to those round it?

Does the thought of many such Fridays stretch heavy ahead — or light, unpictured, open at both ends?

One straight life answers all three. Friday unkept is Friday unpictured, and unpictured is unmissed. The pictures that once marched Monday to Thursday toward a tin now have nowhere to march, and the head, oddly unemployed for an hour, finds new employment in supper steam, lamp-light, pages, talk. That employment is not a programme to follow. It is life resuming its own work while you sit still, and it is quietly exciting to watch it resume.

Keep the one reply for every crossing, at kettle, at till, at sofa, at party table where love holds out a box and you take the love and leave the paper with pity and lightness:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it and get back to the faces. The cloud passes. The evening stays. You stay — eater, shopper, neighbour, host — with hunger for guide and satisfaction for close, and nothing left to watch.

SUMMARY
- Mornings start with thirst and hunger, not with count, and the head stays quiet through the hour.
- Breakfast closes when the body signals, and the signal is trusted without praise or blame.
- Shops are walked for meals, bright packs seen as print, the till passed without second list.
- Other hands still caught are met with warmth, their hurry read as need, never as pleasure to miss.
- Evenings run their length on talk, story and lamp-light, the old pairing gone without gap.
- A passing image is noted as weather and answered once, the door held by sight.
- A moved hand is read as lesson, the sentence renewed at the table, life going on unchanged.
