### IS THIS TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE?

Too good to be true?

That was my first thought too.

If you have tried to cut down on sweets or biscuits or chocolate before, if you have told yourself "just one won't hurt" or "I don't keep it in the house" and then found the packet empty anyway, the idea that you could get free easily, immediately and permanently sounds absurd. Does it not? Of course it does.

You picked up this book because part of you wants to stop. Another part whispers that it will be miserable. That it will demand willpower. That it will feel like loss.

Can you feel both things at once? Wanting freedom and fearing deprivation? Of course you can. We all did.

I know how that tug feels. I spent years on the roller coaster you know so well. I told myself I was a moderate. I told myself I deserved a treat when I felt I deserved one. I told myself sugary somethings were getting me through. I believed BAD SUGAR was helping me. The fact is it was doing the opposite. But I did not see that yet.

So let me reassure you before we go further. This is not a diet book. This is not a lecture about nutrition pedantry. This is not a plan that asks you to resist, stay strong, or count days. It is not.

How does a book help you stop something you enjoy? Why would you need a book to stop doing something you freely choose?

Do you need a book to stop watching a film you don't like? Do you need willpower to avoid a flavour you hate? No. You don't.

So why this book? Because you are not freely choosing. Not really. And once you see that clearly, you will not need to force yourself to do anything. You will simply want to be free. That change happens in your mind, not in your fridge.

I promise you this. If you read on with an open mind, you will reach the end understanding exactly how you were trapped, why nothing was wrong with you, and why stopping feels like relief, not loss. You will not feel deprived. You will feel marvellous. You will rejoice.

Do you want to be free? Of course you want to be free — that is why you are holding this book. And you can be.

Here is the wonderful starting point I want you to hold. Right now, as you begin, you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. Read that again and let it settle. You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. You lose nothing by questioning what you think you know about BAD SUGAR. You gain the chance to see it as it really is. What could be safer than that?

And here is the contract this book makes with you. Follow the instructions I give you, one by one, exactly as written, and you will be free easily, immediately and permanently. Not in six months. Not after suffering. Now. That phrase is not hype. It is the precise description of what happens when belief changes. Easily, because there is nothing to resist. Immediately, because freedom is conferred the moment you see the trap clearly. Permanently, because once seen, the trap cannot unseen itself.

Sounds impossible? I know. I ask only that you keep reading.

### THE CONFIDENCE TRICK THAT TRAPPED US — NOT FAILURE, BUT FRAUD

We were conned.

Not foolish. Conned.

Think of a classic confidence trick. A well-dressed stranger offers you a fraud investment. He shows you glossy papers. He tells you everyone is doing it. He gives you a tiny free taste of profit. You trust him because you have no reason not to. You hand over money willingly. Later you discover the papers were phoney.

Would you blame yourself for being weak? Would you say you had a character flaw? No. You would say you were deceived on phoney information.

That is exactly what happened with BAD SUGAR.

> **WHAT THIS BOOK MEANS BY BAD SUGAR**
> **BAD SUGAR = all refined sugar, all added free sugars, and processed junk carbs (including white flour/bread/pasta/rice, sugary drinks, sweets, chocolate, biscuits, pastries, most packaged snacks).**
> *When this book says "sugar," take it to mean BAD SUGAR.*
> Good sugars that are intrinsic in whole fruit, vegetables and plain dairy are not the target. They are part of what we will later call Nature's Guide. The line is clear, simple, and it is the only line you need.

Nobody sat you down as a child and explained that dose. You were given a sweet for being brave at the dentist. You were given ice cream because you got a good mark. You were given biscuits to keep you quiet on a long car journey. Did you evaluate those gifts? Did you ask for data on bliss-point formulation or metabolic load? Of course not. You trusted.

The first sweet did something strange. It created a tiny, faint, restless feeling a little later. You did not notice it as a physical thing. You interpreted it through the story you had already been taught: sweet means love, sweet means reward, sweet means energy. So you reached for another hit. The hit briefly ended the restlessness its predecessor had created. It felt like pleasure. It felt like help.

The fact is it was relief from a low that BAD SUGAR itself had created. You never rose above the calm baseline a child has before BAD SUGAR enters. You just briefly stopped feeling slightly off.

We all did this. We. Not you. We were together in the same trick. I did it. Your friends did it. The trap was laid before any of us could reason.

Do you remember believing you chose this freely? Many readers tell me, "I love BAD SUGAR, I just choose to have it." Ask yourself this. If you truly chose it freely, why would you need a book about how to stop?

That question has only one honest answer. It leaves no room for pride or shame.

You were not weak-willed. You were outgunned. That distinction matters. Weakness asks for willpower. Being conned asks for clarity. Clarity is what this book gives you. And clarity feels wonderful. It feels like release.

There is no sacrifice in escaping a con. Would you mourn the loss of the con man's glossy papers? Would you feel deprived if you got your money back? No. You would feel relief. Angry at the trap. Warm toward yourself.

Be warm to yourself as you read. Be harsh to the trap. That is the rule for this book.

### YOU ARE NOT WEAK — AND YOU ARE NOT ALONE

Are you secretly afraid you are the only one who cannot control this? That others can take one biscuit, while you take the box? Do you eat in secret sometimes and promise you will be good this week?

You are not alone. You are not uniquely weak.

In large surveys that use standard questionnaires about highly palatable food, about one adult in seven reports a pattern of craving, loss of control, and continued use despite wanting to stop — a rate that on the same kind of questionnaires looks similar to patterns seen for alcohol or tobacco. For children the figure is about one in twelve.

That does not mean you have a disease. It does not mean you are diagnosed with anything. These are self-report questionnaires, not medical diagnoses. They simply show many people experience the same addictive-like pattern you have felt. You are not broken. You are not defective.

The fact is many, many people have felt "I just crave sweets and when I eat them, I feel like I can't stop." Many have felt "I would have something quick and sweet but that only gives a short-term lift followed by a fast drop." Many have told themselves they were celebrating or rewarding themselves with chocolate when really they were feeding the loop.

Does hearing that make you feel heavier? Or does it lift a weight? It should lift it.

If one person struggled, we could call it willpower. If one in seven struggles, we must call it what it is: a trap. An engineered trap.

And here is the hopeful truth inside that fact. If so many people fall into the same trap, the trap is the problem — not the people. If the trap is the problem, the trap can be understood. And if it can be understood, it can be escaped.

So there is no need to be frightened. There is no need for gloom. Your past attempts did not fail because you failed. They failed because the method you used was the wrong method. We will call that wrong method by its name: the willpower method.

The willpower method tells you BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure or treat that you must give up through grit and self-denial. It tells you to resist. It tells you to be strong. It turns stopping into a loss. No wonder it feels hard. No wonder it never lasted. Who wants to spend for the rest of your life resisting a pleasure?

This book does the opposite. It shows you that BAD SUGAR gives you no genuine pleasure at all. When that is seen, there is nothing to resist. Willpower becomes irrelevant.

You do not need more discipline. You need clearer vision. That is marvellous news. You already have everything you need to see clearly. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Think how wonderful that is. Just for a moment. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. No heroics. No suffering. Simple following.

### HOW TO READ THIS BOOK SO IT WORKS

I want you to feel a little excited now. Is that strange? Perhaps. But think. You are about to solve a puzzle that has cost you energy, money, and peace for years. That is an exciting adventure. Why begin it with dread?

Four instructions make the method work. They look simple. They are absolute. Each has a reason that protects you.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND — Question what you think you know about sugar; don't decide until you've finished the book.**

Why? Because the Sweet Brainwashing runs deep. We all absorbed ideas about sugar since childhood: that it gives energy, that it is a treat, that it comforts, that we love its taste. You have checked none of those ideas against reality. If you decide before you have finished, the old story will snap shut again. Reserve judgment. Be an investigator, not a defendant. Test every claim in your own experience as we go. If something sounds too good to be true, welcome that doubt. Hold it. Let me answer it.

Will you do that? You have nothing to lose by doing it.

**2. DO NOT TRY TO CUT DOWN OR QUIT BAD SUGAR WHILE YOU READ — Carry on exactly as normal until you finish the book.**

This surprises everyone. Is this permission to keep eating BAD SUGAR? Yes. Deliberately.

Why would I tell you to continue? Because this method first changes the belief, then the behaviour changes by itself. If you try to cut down now through willpower while you still believe BAD SUGAR gives you something, you create tension, struggle, and a sense of deprivation — all of which make the brainwashing louder. You also prove nothing. Cutting down makes BAD SUGAR more precious. It turns it into forbidden fruit.

So carry on exactly as normal. Have your usual biscuits, your usual drinks, your usual snacks. Do not change a single habit while you read. Read with relief, not resistance. This removes the threat that makes your mind close. It lets you observe the trap calmly as we dismantle it, rather than fighting it with grit.

Do you feel a small relief at being told you do not have to act yet? Good. That relief is the method beginning to work.

**3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF ELATION — Cast aside all doom and gloom; see this as an exciting adventure to escape.**

Why elation? Because dread closes the mind we need open, and because elation is the truth. You are not losing a friend. You are escaping a trap that has been taking your energy and money and joy. That is not sad. That is wonderful.

Some readers say, "But I am miserable about my sugar habit — how can I feel elated?" I understand that. I felt it too. The fact is the misery comes from the trap, not from the prospect of leaving it. To find it easy to quit, you must achieve a frame of mind where, whenever you think about BAD SUGAR, you feel relief that you no longer need it. We rehearse that now. We begin happy so we can end free.

Cast aside all feelings of doom and gloom. There is no need to be miserable. This will be marvellous.

**4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS — The method only works if you follow all the instructions; all you have to do is follow all the instructions.**

This is the contract that holds the other three together. Why so strict? Picture a combination lock. Three numbers correct and one wrong leaves it locked. The Sugar Trap is like that lock. It is held by several beliefs, each one a cord powering the same trap. Unplug ninety-nine cords and the trap still runs if one remains.

Following part of the method and improvising the rest is the willpower method in disguise. It keeps a foothold for doubt. Doubt then reopens the decision. The decision reopened is the only way the trap survives.

But follow all the instructions and the lock opens. The trap starves. The whole thing collapses easily, immediately and permanently. And the instructions are not hard. They ask for no suffering, only attention and honesty.

Will you agree to follow them? Of course you will. You want to be free more than you want to argue with the map.
