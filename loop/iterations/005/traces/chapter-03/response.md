### THE WRONG QUESTION

We left the last chapter with a question. Do you remember it?

Not "does BAD SUGAR do more harm than good?"

But "what good is it at all?"

Why does that change matter? Because the first question keeps you trapped. The second question frees you. Let me show you.

For years we asked the wrong question. We weighed harm against benefit. We said, "I know it is not perfect, but I enjoy it, I need it, it helps me." We put health on one side of the scales and pleasure on the other. And pleasure seemed to win. Does that sound familiar?

You say, "I know I should cut down, but it gets me through the afternoon." You say, "I know it is sugary, but it is my treat, I deserve it." You are negotiating. You are calculating. You are asking whether the damage outweighs the help.

That is exactly what the Sugar Trap wants you to ask. Why? Because as long as you argue harm versus help, help is assumed. The trap has already won. You have granted it a benefit it never had to prove.

Let me ask you plainly. Who told you there was a benefit?

Did you test it? Did you isolate it? Or did you simply feel a brief lift after a dose and call that help?

We do this without thinking. We feel low. We take a hit of BAD SUGAR. We feel a flicker of relief. We say, "See? It helped." We never ask whether the low was there before BAD SUGAR created it. We never ask what genuine pleasure or treat BAD SUGAR actually gave us.

The fact is we have been answering an unexamined premise. We assumed goodness and argued degree. That is like arguing whether a counterfeit note is worth more than the paper it is printed on. You miss the point. It is counterfeit.

So I want you to change the question with me now. Not "is the harm worth it?" But "is there any worth at all?"

Can you feel the difference? One question keeps you weighing. The other demands proof. One keeps you a customer. The other makes you an investigator.

We will not weigh harm against help in this chapter. We will demand that BAD SUGAR justify a single, irreplaceable good. Not a memory of relief. Not a story about reward. A genuine good that exists without BAD SUGAR taking credit for something else.

Is that fair? If BAD SUGAR is so valuable, it should survive that test easily. If it cannot, you are free.

You have nothing to fear from this test. You have everything to gain. Look with me.
