**The book's journey in five sentences:** The reader enters braced for loss but is offered an easy contract on the stairs, then watches hiding, hand-before-thought, and the Nipper/Smokescreen trap close before the TO/FOR axis empties every claimed benefit. Through the middle the book demolishes stress, reward, willpower, the nicotine meter, ordinary mornings and meals, industry packaging, the patio exception, and just-one/tomorrow bargains — each credit returned to food, air, work, and company. Chapter 12 confers freedom at the farewell table: belief dies at the stub, the vow is spoken, and YIPPEE! I'M FREE! becomes the replacement script. Chapters 13–14 walk an uncut ordinary day — kettle, shop, doorway, supper, bed — with empty hands and no maintenance frame. The book closes on photographed instructions, the empty-tray reframe, and an outward push to enjoy life already whole.

**Overall arc assessment:** Read as one cumulative Carr-method journey, this book takes a believing smoker to escape: the inversion completes, freedom is conferred before the final chapters, and the ending is relief and excitement rather than white-knuckle maintenance. Mantras debut and echo on schedule, all twelve instructions are issued through chapter 12 and recapped in chapter 14, and the saved ashtray reframe lands only in the finale. The largest cross-chapter weakness is structural repetition, not incomplete belief change: after the vow, chapters 13 and especially 14 re-stage the same ordinary-day rooms at full length instead of compressing to tokens, which flattens the late freedom crescendo slightly and accounts for most of the noted re-argument. None of that blocks the gate — a reader who followed through should hold no real benefit credit and should not expect to quit by willpower.
