Chapter 7
THE ITCH IT PRETENDS TO SCRATCH

*That lift never took you above normal — it only hushed for minutes the hunger it had made.*

## THE METER ON THE CORNER

We shut the drawer in the kitchen. We saw that twenty crosses on a calendar do not prove we are weak. They prove the method chained us and praised our shaking.

Good. That clears the past. It still leaves the fear in the flesh.

You say it plain in your head, and I honour you for saying it. Pleasures may be empty. Method may be wrong. But my body needs it. I feel the call in my hands and throat. What if the need is real?

Then let us watch one ordinary day together, coin by coin, and see what the flesh is really asking for.

Picture a parking meter on a grey corner. You park in the morning. You drop a coin. The needle jumps. You walk away. Ten minutes later you look back and the needle is already sinking. By lunch you have fed it four times. By night you have fed it twenty times. You never once bought the street. You only rented the needle off zero for a few minutes at a time.

That is our day with paper.

First dose with coffee. Hands steadier for half an hour. Second dose after the row with the boss. Shoulders drop for twenty minutes. Third dose waiting for the bus. Fourth after food. Fifth with drink. Each time the same small hush, each time gone before the hour turns, each time the hand moving again to the pocket for another coin.

I lived that meter for years. I believed I was buying calm and pause and reward. I was only paying to lift the needle off red for minutes, then watching it sink again.

Ask it with your own day in view.

If each dose truly settled you, why does the settling never last the morning?
If each dose truly filled you, why must the pocket always carry the next one?

We were not enjoying a street we owned. We were feeding a meter we rented.

## THE COIN FADES IN MINUTES

Here is the plain mechanism underneath the meter, stated flat because it is true for smoked doses as a rule.

When smoke enters the lungs, nicotine is carried in the blood to the brain within seconds. That is why the hush feels instant. The hand shakes, the puff bites, the shoulders drop — seconds. But the acute effect also fades within minutes, with the reward feeling going with it. That is why the hush never holds. The brain reads the fade as lack, and lack reads as want another.

No lecture in that. No scare. Only clockwork.

One coin buys minutes, not hours. The meter is built to sink. The sinking is not your fault and not your weakness. It is what a rapidly fading dose does in a human body.

Watch it in your own pocket today while we look together. I am not asking you to change what you do. Only to see.

Light. Note the second the bite hits. Note how complete it feels for three minutes. Note minute fifteen, when the completeness is gone and the mind is already tidying toward the next break. Note hour one, when yesterday's dose might as well never have been smoked. Nothing of it stayed with you. Not calm, not focus, not pleasure. Only the memory of a hush and the present need to buy it again.

That cycle is not every puff timed to the second for every smoker. Bodies differ. Days differ. As a rule with smoked paper, the pattern holds: seconds to hit, minutes to fade, all day to repeat.

So hear the honest accounting.

You never smoked twenty different pleasures in a day. You smoked the same fading coin twenty times to keep one needle off zero. The first coin of the morning did not lift a rested man higher. It quieted a night-starved nerve back toward where a non-smoker had slept all night. The coin after the argument did not touch the argument. Only the nip quieted while you looked away.

If the gift were real, one coin would last. A true gift does not need feeding twenty times before dark.

## THE GAP BETWEEN COINS

The coin is easy to see. The gap is where the fear lives. So stay in the gap with me and watch what actually happens there, minute by minute, while the needle sinks.

You stub one out at 10:00 outside the office. For three minutes you feel done. Mouth tastes of ash, fingers smell, chest feels a shade tight, but the mind says done, taken care of. At 10:04 you go back in. Mail, talk, kettle. Nothing in you thinks of paper.

At about 10:15 something changes, so faint you would miss it if I did not point. Not a pain. Not a hunger. A small emptiness opens under the ribs, as if a window had been left ajar. Hands feel a fraction too idle. Attention snags for a second and then lets go. If you were busy you would not name it. That is the start.

By 10:30 the ajar window is wider. What you feel between doses is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

Say those words slowly over your own skin, because they are the true size. Empty, as if something were missing but you cannot say what. Slightly restless, as if you should do something with your hands. Slightly edgy, as if the skin were a shade too tight. We have called it craving, hunger, nerves, need-a-minute. It is the same small signal underneath every name.

At 10:45 the tug is louder but still small. You shift in the chair. You re-read the same mail twice. You stand and sit. The body asks for movement. The mind, trained for years, offers its old translation instantly: I need a break, I need air, I need to think this through with paper, I cannot settle without it.

Watch that translation happen. It happens fast because it is practised, not because it is true.

At 11:00 the tug peaks as a mood. Not agony. Irritability. A thin impatience with the slow printer, with the mate talking too long, with yourself for fidgeting. The Smokescreen leans in here and speaks in your own voice: see, you cannot cope, you cannot focus, you are not yourself without it. And because the feeling is real, the sentence feels real.

It is not.

Separate the two lines and the whole day changes shape. Line one is physical and tiny: a fading dose leaving a small empty nip. Line two is planted and huge: cope, think, laugh, stay you. Line one is the Nipper stirring. Line two is the Smokescreen talking.

That is the Nipper stirring.

He is tiny. He is a trivial physical creature, born with the last dose, already half-starved while you read, dying a little more with every hour you do not feed him. He cannot think, cannot reason, cannot force your hand. He can only nip and ask to be fed.

That story about what the nip means is the Smokescreen talking.

The Smokescreen says the tug means you need smoke to cope, to think, to laugh, to stay you. It translates a small empty nip as a large human truth. It has done that translation for years, through family lighting up, mates lighting up, silver packs and smooth promises, until we hear nip and think self.

Put the two side by side in the gap and ask which does the heavy lifting.

If the tug were truly large, it would stop your hands. It does not. You still type, still drive, still answer, still carry shopping, still laugh at the joke while it nips. A real physical need — thirst, breath, sleep — interrupts act. This does not interrupt. It colours. It nags while you get on with everything. That is the signature of a small signal carrying a large false story.

If the tug were truly about coping, it would arrive only at trouble. It does not. It arrives after food when there is no trouble. It arrives on holiday when there is no trouble. It arrives in the middle of laughter when there is no trouble. It arrives on schedule, not on sorrow. Schedule means dose-fade. Sorrow would mean life.

If the tug were truly you, it would have been there before you started. It was not. No child sat at school feeling this exact empty edge and thinking, I lack paper. The edge began after the meter began. What begins with dosing belongs to dosing.

There is only one reading that fits all three. The nip is small. The meaning is added. The added meaning does almost all the suffering.

Now follow the gap to its end, because this is the part no smoker was ever shown and it ends the fear.

At 11:10, if you do not light, what happens? The mind predicts torment climbing forever. The body does something else. The tug, left unfed, crests like a wave and then loses shape. Attention moves to the next mail, the next call, the next mouthful of tea. For five minutes you forget it entirely. Then it flickers back, fainter. Then forget again. The wave does not climb to torture. It frets, peaks, passes, returns fainter.

I am not describing heroics. I am describing a starving nip with no orders left to give once you stop obeying the translation.

Think of a radio left on low in the next room. While you believe it is broadcasting orders, you strain to listen, you feel tense, you keep walking to the door to check. The moment you see it is only static from a dying battery, you still hear it, but you stop minding it. You make tea over it. You talk over it. It runs down while you live. That is the gap without the story.

And here is the backward clockwork that guarantees the next low.

When you do light at 11:15, the static stops in seconds. Wonderful, says the Smokescreen, proof you needed it. But watch what the hush actually installs. The dose tops the fade for minutes and, by topping it, re-sets the meter to full — which means it re-creates the exact fade that will open the same empty window at 11:30. Every hush lays down the next hunger. Every coin pays for the next sink. You never leave the corner. You only buy another fifteen minutes off red and call it help.

Stay with that coin past 11:15, because the day-tracking must not stop at one gap. See the second sink arrive on schedule and you will see the circuit, not just the return.

At 11:18 you are back at the desk with the taste still in the mouth. For three minutes the window is shut. Shoulders sit lower. Hands are quiet. The mind says settled. Nothing has changed in the room — same mail pile, same kettle cooling, same mate coughing at the next desk — but the needle stands off red and you mistake the needle for the room.

At 11:30 it starts again, with no new trouble to explain it. No fresh row. No bad news. No harder work. The mail you answered stays answered. The tea you poured stays poured. Yet under the ribs the same ajar feeling opens, faint as before. Hands feel a fraction too idle again. Eyes lift from the screen for a second too long toward the door, toward the pocket, toward the clock. If trouble caused it, where is the trouble? There is none. There is only schedule.

By 11:40 the schedule is louder. You re-read a line you already understood. You tap the pen. You stand to stretch and sit again without needing to stretch. The body asks for movement the way it did at 10:45, same small restlessness, same thin edge. The mind offers the same old translation: time for air, time to clear the head, time to step out. It sounds like wisdom. It is timetable.

At 11:50 the timetable turns to mood again. The printer is slow again. The mate talks too long again. Yourself fidgets again. Nothing is worse than at 10:50, nothing is harder, yet the impatience is back to the ounce. That exact repeat is the proof. Life did not get harder between hush and hunger. The coin wore off between hush and hunger. The same fade rebuilt the same tug to sell the same hush.

At 12:00 the pocket weighs again. Not because noon needs paper, but because 11:15 bought 11:30. The hand moves to the coins without a decision, the way it moved at 11:15 without a decision. Another coin goes in. The needle jumps. The window shuts for minutes. The Smokescreen says, see, you needed that to face the afternoon. What you faced was not the afternoon. What you faced was the sink scheduled by the last hush.

Feel that second round from inside and the belief shifts a second notch.

The first gap taught you the hush carries you back toward where the non-smoker lives all day. The second gap teaches you the hush also books the next fall. Each dose re-sets the meter to full and thereby writes the time of the next emptiness. Each quiet lays its own alarm. The meter does not just sink. It is re-wound by every coin to sink again.

Ask it over the two gaps together, and answer honest.

If the 11:15 dose solved something, why did the same emptiness need solving again at 11:30 with nothing new happening in between?
If the 10:30 tug meant you could not think, how did you think perfectly well from 11:18 to 11:28, then suddenly not think again at 11:40 over the same work?
If paper helped days, why do easy days need as many coins as hard days, and holidays as many as Mondays?

Put both windows side by side and the shape is plain. Relief is not only a brief return toward the quiet street. It is a self-repeating circuit. Hush schedules hunger schedules hush. The itch-hush-itch loop runs itself while the story on top calls it coping, reward, company, you.

That is why the day is twenty coins and never one. Not because days are hard and need twenty helps. Because each help manufactures the next need. The circuit runs itself while the story on top calls it life.

Feel that circuit from inside once and the belief shifts under your feet.

You are not a man with a large physical need and a small doubt. You are a man with a small physical nip and a large implanted doubt. The nip will fret for a while and then run down, as a rule with smoked doses left unfed, with the edge typically sharpest in the first few days and usually softening within weeks — typical, not every reader, not torture for all, bodies and days differ. The doubt is what kept you feeding it for years. Starve the nip by not feeding. Dissolve the doubt by seeing. The first is trivial. The second is this book's whole work.

Once seen, the gap stops being a threat and starts being evidence.

The 10:15 emptiness is not you breaking. It is a coin wearing off.
The 10:45 restlessness is not proof you cannot think. It is a dying creature nipping while the old story talks over it.
The 11:00 edge is not coming torment. It is a short wave that will crest and pass if you let it, and will return fainter until it forgets to return.
The 11:30 return with no new trouble is not life getting harder. It is the last hush scheduling its own hunger.

You do not have to fight the wave. You only have to stop translating it.

## WHAT THE TUG REALLY IS

Now name it clean, because fear made it huge and it is small.

Between doses there is no wound. There is a small empty signal with a short life. As a rule the restless edge is sharpest in the first few days without a dose and usually softens within a few weeks. It can begin within hours. That is the typical curve, not a sentence on you to the day, and not weeks of torment for everyone. I speak of belief here, not of your body's care — if you feel ill or you worry, speak to your own clinician for your own care. My point stands inside those bounds: the tug is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical peak, not proof paper was your real self.

Think of an itching arm in plaster. The itch nags. Scratching the plaster does not mend the bone. It only rubs the itch for seconds while keeping the arm caged. You would not say the plaster healed you. You would say the plaster kept the itch alive by covering it.

Paper is that plaster. It rubs the tug it keeps alive.

Kill the meaning and the nip has no orders to give. Keep feeding the meaning and even a faint nip will feel like fate. That is why two smokers can feel the same faint tug and live two different hours — one lighting in panic, one smiling and forgetting. Same Nipper. Different Smokescreen. Belief is the dominant driver. The flesh only whispers. The story shouts.

Ask it over your own arm.

If scratching healed, why does the itch always return louder within the hour?
If plaster helped the bone, why has the arm grown weaker every year you wore it?
If the tug were fate, why does it fade to nothing in days when unfed, while fate never fades?

You scratch. The itch hushes for minutes. The scratching roughens the skin, so the itch returns louder. You scratch harder, call the scratch healing, fear life without scratching. Then one morning you see the scratch never healed. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

There is the nicotine trap in one line.

## YOU NEVER ROSE ABOVE THE STREET

We come to the backward truth that ends this fear. Stay with me here, because this is the turn the whole book stands on.

We believed paper lifted us above normal. Stress came, dose came, calm came — therefore dose made calm. Work done, dose came, glow came — therefore dose made glow. Night out, dose came, belonging came — therefore dose made belonging.

It is the other way around.

A non-smoker lives on the quiet street. No meter, no coins, no tug. We left that street with our first doses and moved to the meter corner. From then on every dose only carried us from the red back toward the quiet street for minutes, then dropped us back to red. We called the ride up a lift. It was a return.

Picture two men at the same desk after the same harsh call. One never smoked. His shoulders are tight because the call was harsh. He drinks water, answers the next mail, tightness passes. The other is us. His shoulders are tight because the call was harsh plus his last coin faded twenty minutes ago and the Nipper is nipping. He lights, nip quiets for minutes, call still sits untouched on the pad. He says paper calmed him. What calmed was the nip, not the call, not the man. The non-smoker beside him was calmer all along with nothing lit.

I know the objection because it was mine for years. Let it speak in your words:

“But I feel it work in my body. My hands stop shaking. My head clears.”

Yes. You feel it work on the need it made. Hands stop shaking because the fading dose is topped for minutes. Head clears because the tug stops shouting for minutes. That is not a gift above normal. That is a debt paid down for minutes, with the next debt already ticking.

Answer three questions honest, with your own days as witness.

If paper truly raised you above normal, why do non-smokers sit steady all day with no paper at all?
If paper truly gave calm, why are smokers as a group the edgiest people in the waiting room, watching the door for the next break?
If the tug proved paper was your friend, why did the tug never exist before you started paying the meter?

Look at your own days and the answer is plain. The party was work, the pause was air, the company was company, the calm after the puff was the nip going quiet. Paper sneaked a ride on every good around it and charged you for the ride.

## OTHER SMOKERS ARE STILL PAYING

This seeing must hold when other smokers light around you. It will be tested within hours, and the test is simple.

You will stand with mates. Tray out. Laughter. Hand extended. “Go on. One won’t hurt.” Smoke curling. Faces soft in the light. Your Nipper will nip at the smell, your Smokescreen will translate on cue: they are enjoying, you are missing, one coin keeps you one of us.

Watch with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

What are you actually watching? Not enjoyment. Rent-paying. Each man there is feeding his own meter on schedule. The soft face after the puff is the nip going quiet for minutes. Ten minutes later the same face will tighten toward the next coin. The laugh is the drink and the company and the night air. Paper is sneaking a ride on all three while charging for the ride.

See them warm, not proud. You see men still chained to a corner you have left in your head. You do not envy the coin going in. You see the needle about to sink again.

And you do not need to argue them or copy them or hide from them. Hiding would say paper still holds power. Arguing would lend your eyes out before our case is done. Let them smoke while you watch. Let the smell come and go. Your work is not to police their hands. Your work is to keep your own translation true: tug means Nipper, story means Smokescreen, hush means return toward normal, never rise above it.

You walk out from it easily, immediately and permanently.

That is not boast. It is mechanics. Once the credit is returned — calm to water and next mail, glow to finished work, belonging to mates and night — there is nothing left for paper to sell you. The small tug, when it comes, arrives pre-labelled. Ah. There nips the dying creature. There talks the old story. No order to obey.

So when the hand extends tomorrow, hear it clean.

Their lighting does not prove paper gives. It proves the meter still rents them. Your not lighting does not prove you lose company. It proves you keep company with clear lungs and full pocket while the needle stays where the quiet street always kept it.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
You have watched the tray go round — leave their coins to them and keep your own eyes clear.

SUMMARY
- Each dose hits within seconds and fades within minutes, so the day becomes coin after coin.
- The fade is the dose wearing off, not proof the day needs dosing.
- The between-dose signal is a small empty tug with a short typical peak, not the real self speaking.
- A puff only carries the smoker from low back toward the non-smoker baseline for minutes.
- No puff ever raised life above the baseline non-smokers live on all day.
- The itch-hush-itch cycle is made by smoking, then sold as help with itch.
- Other smokers lighting nearby shows rent being paid, not enjoyment being had.
