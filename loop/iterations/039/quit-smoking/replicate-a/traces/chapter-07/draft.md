Chapter 7
THE ITCH IT PRETENDS TO SCRATCH

IN THIS CHAPTER — The parking meter, the pocket of coins, the itching arm, the quiet street underneath.

*That lift never took you above normal — it only hushed for minutes the hunger it had made.*

## THE METER ON THE CORNER

We have shut the drawer in the kitchen. We have seen that twenty crosses on the calendar do not prove we are weak. They prove the method chained us and praised our shaking.

Good. That clears the past. It still leaves the fear in the body.

You say it plain in your own head, and I honour you for saying it: pleasures may be empty, method may be wrong, but my flesh needs it. I feel the call in my hands and throat. What if the need is real?

Then let us watch one ordinary day together, coin by coin, and see what the flesh is really asking for.

Picture a parking meter on a grey corner. You park in the morning. You drop a coin. The needle jumps. You walk away. Ten minutes later you look back and the needle is already sinking. By lunch you have fed it four times. By night you have fed it twenty times. You never once bought the street. You only rented the needle off zero for a few minutes at a time.

That is our day with paper.

First dose with coffee. Hands steadier for half an hour. Second dose after the row with the boss. Shoulders drop for twenty minutes. Third dose waiting for the bus. Fourth after food. Fifth with drink. Each time the same small hush, each time gone before the hour turns, each time the hand moving again to the pocket for another coin.

I lived that meter for years. I believed I was buying calm and pause and reward. I was only paying to lift the needle off red for minutes, then watching it sink again.

Ask it with your own day in view.

If each dose truly settled you, why does the settling never last the morning?
If each dose truly filled you, why must the pocket always carry the next one?

We were not enjoying a street we owned. We were feeding a meter we rented.

## THE COIN FADES IN MINUTES

Here is the plain mechanism underneath the meter, stated flat because it is true for smoked doses as a rule.

When smoke enters the lungs, the nicotine reaches the brain within seconds. That is why the hush feels instant. The hand shakes, the puff bites, the shoulders drop — seconds. But the acute effect also fades within minutes. That is why the hush never holds. The brain reads the fade as lack, and lack reads as want another.

No lecture in that. No scare. Only clockwork.

One coin buys minutes, not hours. The meter is built to sink. The sinking is not your fault and not your weakness. It is what a rapidly fading dose does in a human body.

Watch it in your own pocket today while you still smoke as we look — no change asked yet, only eyes.

Light. Note the second the bite hits. Note how complete it feels for three minutes. Note minute fifteen, when the completeness is gone and the mind is already tidying toward the next break. Note hour one, when yesterday's dose might as well never have been smoked. Nothing of it stayed with you. Not calm, not focus, not pleasure. Only the memory of a hush and the present need to buy it again.

That cycle is not every puff timed to the second for every smoker. Bodies differ. Days differ. As a rule with smoked paper, the pattern holds: seconds to hit, minutes to fade, all day to repeat.

So hear the honest accounting.

You never smoked twenty different pleasures in a day. You smoked the same fading coin twenty times to keep one needle off zero. The first coin of the morning did not lift a rested man higher. It quieted a night-starved nerve back toward where a non-smoker had slept all night. The coin after the argument did not touch the argument. The papers still lay where you left them. Only the nip quieted while you looked away.

If the gift were real, one coin would last. A true gift does not need feeding twenty times before dark.

## WHAT THE TUG REALLY IS

Now name the nip itself, because fear has made it huge and it is small.

What you feel between doses is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

Not pain. Not illness. Not proof you cannot live without paper. A tug. Empty, as if something were missing. Slightly restless, as if you should do something with your hands. Slightly edgy, as if the skin were a shade too tight. We all know it. We have called it craving, hunger, nerves, need-a-minute. It is the same small signal underneath every name.

That is the Nipper stirring.

He is tiny. He is a trivial physical creature, born with the last dose, dying with the next missed feed, already half-starved while you read. He cannot think, cannot reason, cannot force your hand. He can only nip and ask to be fed.

That story about what the nip means is the Smokescreen talking.

The Smokescreen says the tug means you need smoke to cope, to think, to laugh, to stay you. It translates a small empty nip as a large human truth. It has done that translation for years, through family lighting up, mates lighting up, silver packs and smooth promises, until we hear nip and think self.

Separate the two and fear shrinks to size.

The nip is physical and brief. The meaning is planted and false. Kill the meaning and the nip has no orders to give.

I give you the body truth straight, with no dressing. As a rule the restless edge is sharpest in the first few days without a dose and usually softens within a few weeks. It can begin within hours. That is the typical curve, not a sentence on you to the day, and not weeks of torment for everyone. I am not giving medical advice here — if you feel ill or you worry, speak to your own clinician for your own care. My point is belief, and belief is enough: the tug is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical peak, not proof paper was your real self.

Think of an itching arm in plaster. The itch nags. Scratching the plaster does not mend the bone. It only rubs the itch for seconds while keeping the arm caged. You would not say the plaster healed you. You would say the plaster kept the itch alive by covering it.

Paper is that plaster. It rubs the tug it keeps alive.

## YOU NEVER ROSE ABOVE THE STREET

We come to the backward truth that ends this fear. Stay with me here, because this is the turn the whole book stands on.

We believed paper lifted us above normal. Stress came, dose came, calm came — therefore dose made calm. Work done, dose came, glow came — therefore dose made glow. Night out, dose came, belonging came — therefore dose made belonging.

It is the other way around.

A non-smoker lives on the quiet street. No meter, no coins, no tug. We left that street with our first doses and moved to the meter corner. From then on every dose only carried us from the red back toward the quiet street for minutes, then dropped us back to red. We called the ride up a lift. It was a return.

Picture two men at the same desk after the same harsh call. One never smoked. His shoulders are tight because the call was harsh. He drinks water, answers the next mail, tightness passes. The other is us. His shoulders are tight because the call was harsh plus his last coin faded twenty minutes ago and the Nipper is nipping. He lights, nip quiets for minutes, call still sits untouched on the pad. He says paper calmed him. What calmed was the nip, not the call, not the man. The non-smoker beside him was calmer all along with nothing lit.

I know the objection because it was mine for years. Let it speak in your words:

“But I feel it work in my body. My hands stop shaking. My head clears.”

Yes. You feel it work on the need it made. Hands stop shaking because the fading dose is topped for minutes. Head clears because the tug stops shouting for minutes. That is not a gift above normal. That is a debt paid down for minutes, with the next debt already ticking.

Answer three questions honest, with your own days as witness.

If paper truly raised you above normal, why do non-smokers sit steady all day with no paper at all?
If paper truly gave calm, why are smokers as a group the edgiest people in the waiting room, watching the door for the next break?
If the tug proved paper was your friend, why did the tug never exist before you started paying the meter?

There is only one reading that fits all three. The party was work, the pause was air, the company was company, the calm after the puff was the nip going quiet. Paper sneaked a ride on every good around it and charged you for the ride.

Here is the lived compression, felt in the arm before it is said in the head.

You scratch. The itch hushes for minutes. The scratching roughens the skin, so the itch returns louder. You scratch harder, call the scratch healing, fear life without scratching. Then one morning you see the scratch never healed. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

There is the nicotine trap in one line.

## OTHER SMOKERS ARE STILL PAYING

This seeing must hold when other smokers light around you. It will be tested within hours, and the test is simple.

You will stand with mates. Tray out. Laughter. Hand extended. “Go on. One won’t hurt.” Smoke curling. Faces soft in the light. Your Nipper will nip at the smell, your Smokescreen will translate on cue: they are enjoying, you are missing, one coin keeps you one of us.

Watch with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

What are you actually watching? Not enjoyment. Rent-paying. Each man there is feeding his own meter on schedule. The soft face after the puff is the nip going quiet for minutes. Ten minutes later the same face will tighten toward the next coin. The laugh is the drink and the company and the night air. Paper is sneaking a ride on all three while charging for the ride.

Pity in the right sense, which is warm, not proud. You see men still chained to a corner you have left in your head. You do not envy the coin going in. You see the needle about to sink again.

And you do not need to argue them or copy them or hide from them. Hiding would say paper still holds power. Arguing would lend your eyes out before our case is done. Let them smoke while you watch. Let the smell come and go. Your work is not to police their hands. Your work is to keep your own translation true: tug means Nipper, story means Smokescreen, hush means return toward normal, never rise above it.

You walk out from it easily, immediately and permanently.

That is not boast. It is mechanics. Once the credit is returned — calm to water and next mail, glow to finished work, belonging to mates and night — there is nothing left for paper to sell you. The small tug, when it comes, arrives pre-labelled. Ah. There nips the dying creature. There talks the old story. No order to obey.

So when the hand extends tomorrow, hear it clean.

Their lighting does not prove paper gives. It proves the meter still rents them. Your not lighting does not prove you lose company. It proves you keep company with clear lungs and full pocket while the needle stays where the quiet street always kept it.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

SUMMARY
- Each dose hits within seconds and fades within minutes, so the day becomes coin after coin.
- The fade is the dose wearing off, not proof the day needs dosing.
- The between-dose signal is a small empty tug with a short typical peak, not the real self speaking.
- A puff only carries the smoker from low back toward the non-smoker baseline for minutes.
- No puff ever raised life above the baseline non-smokers live on all day.
- The itch-hush-itch cycle is made by smoking, then sold as help with itch.
- Other smokers lighting nearby shows rent being paid, not enjoyment being had.
