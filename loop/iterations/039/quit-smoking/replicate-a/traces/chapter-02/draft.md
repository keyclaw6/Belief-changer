Chapter 2
ARE YOU REALLY CHOOSING THIS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The back step after midnight, the spray bottle and mint tin, the hand that lights before thought

*You were never choosing to stay chained like this; you were conned, and two small keepers hold the door shut.*

## The Hiding No Chooser Needs

Look at what we actually do.

Late. The house quiet. You tell your partner you are taking the rubbish out, checking the car, getting a breath on the back step. You close the garage door soft so it does not click. You stand in the cold in socks and drag hard, then chew two mints, then spray that sweet chemical cloud over your coat and hair, then wave your hands in the air like that will clear it. You Febreeze the hell out of yourself before you go back inside. You smile at the door as if you were never gone.

I know that smile. I wore it for years.

No one taught us that dance. We learned it because we had to. A man enjoying a true pleasure does not hide in a garage to take it. A woman making a real choice does not lie to her kids about where she was for six minutes. Choice does not need an alibi.

So let me ask you plain, as one smoker to another.

If these are your beloved little parties, why do you sneak them like a shame? If this is your friend, why do you wash him off your fingers before you hug your children? If you are in charge here, why do you stand outside in rain while the non-smokers sit inside warm, telling yourself you prefer it out here?

There is only one honest answer, and you already feel it.

We say, “the moment I light up I'm in heaven.” I will not mock that second. I felt it too. That flare, that first pull, that sudden sense of heaven as the match takes — it is real as a sensation. But notice where it lives. It lives in the lighting second only. Not in the middle of the dose. Not in the stale end. Not in the sour mouth and sour conscience ten minutes after. Heaven for a lighting second, then the chain back on. That is not a day-long gift. That is a spark that dies while the problem that sent you outside sits untouched on the kitchen table.

And we say in the same breath, “whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker.” Do you hear the split in that sentence? Love the lighting, hate the life. A chooser would not be split down the middle like that. A chooser would light and be done and feel whole. We light and feel divided, because part of us knows we are not choosing at all.

The fact is, hiding proves the choice left long ago. We were not foolish. We were conned into something that now makes us hide from the people we love most. That is not an insult. It is the start of seeing.

## The Hand That Moves First

Watch yourself today.

Phone down after a sharp exchange. Shoulders up. Papers still spread where the argument left them. And before you have decided anything, your hand is already moving to the pack. Tap, flick, light. Only after the first pull do you think, I needed that.

Did you choose that? Or did the hand choose before you?

I used to watch my own hand do it and laugh it off. Habit, I called it. My little prop. My way of coping. But look closer with me. The stressful moment comes, the hand reaches, the dose goes in, and for a minute you feel taken care of. The feeling is real. I am not denying you felt it. The credit is false. The bill, the row, the queue, the ache — none of it moved while you stood outside. What quieted was something else, something much smaller, something that was there before the row started.

Ask yourself three questions and answer them to yourself, not to me.

When stress hits, does a non-smoker sit frozen until someone hands him a paper tube, or does he deal, fumble, shout, laugh, cope in his own human way without lighting anything?

When you cannot light — in a cinema, on a long flight, in a hospital corridor — does your stress rise because life got harder in that hour, or because something in you started tugging to be fed?

And when you finally light after that wait, does the world get kinder, or does just that tug go quiet while the world stays exactly as it was?

You know. You have lived it a thousand times.

That tug is not character. It is not need. It is not proof you cannot cope. It is a physical whisper, not pain, not crisis, just an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug that asks to be fed and then, fed, asks again a little later. Once you name it for what it is, you stop mistaking it for yourself.

It is the other way around. You do not smoke because you are tense. You are tense in that particular way because you smoke.

## What Really Keeps You Lit

This is where the picture changes.

Think of what has held you not as one big need but as a trap with two keepers. That is how the nicotine trap closes and stays closed. One keeper is tiny and physical. One keeper is learned and loud. Together they look like choice. Seen apart, neither is choice at all.

We all started as non-smokers. We did not need doses to laugh, to argue, to drive, to pause. Then we tried the first ones to look grown, to belong, to try what everyone seemed to enjoy. Inhaling burned. We coughed. Our eyes watered. No one enjoys the first ones. We persisted because the con told us seasoned smokers got something we did not yet get. That was phoney information. The seasoned smoker got nothing but the quieting of a tug the previous dose had planted.

The tiny keeper lives in the body. Each dose feeds a nipping creature that wakes hungry minutes later. I call that small physical tug the Nipper because it nips and nags and then starves quickly if you stop feeding it. It is not mighty. It does not shake you like illness. It is a small, empty gnaw that you have mistaken for hunger, nerves, boredom, lack of focus. Feed it and it goes quiet for a little while. That quiet is what we called pleasure.

The loud keeper lives in the mind. It is every idea we were taught about what a dose does — it calms, it marks, it rewards, it belongs on patios and after meals and with drinks. I call the pile of learned ideas that feeds it the Smokescreen because it hides what is really happening behind a story about gifts. The story says the dose takes care of stress. The story says the dose completes company. The story says you are that kind of person. Without that story, the Nipper is just a faint tug you would shrug off by lifting a kettle or opening a window.

Do you see the pincer? The Nipper creates a tug. The Smokescreen teaches you that only a dose quiets it and calls that quiet a benefit. You light to quiet what lighting created. Each quiet guarantees the next tug. Round and round we went, calling the circle our habit, our pleasure, our choice.

It was never choice. It was a learned circle. A circle can be seen. What is seen can be broken by seeing. That seeing is an escape in the mind before anything changes in the pocket.

## Who Put You Here?

“I chose to start. No one forced me.”

True. And beside the point.

You chose the first one on false terms. You were a kid or young and hungry to belong, watching grown faces look calm with smoke curling, hearing laughter in the smoking corner, believing there was a grown calm in the tube. You did not choose to stand in a garage at midnight twenty years later spraying your coat. You did not choose to count your day in lights between tasks. You did not choose to pant on stairs or to dread a long flight because you cannot feed on time. No one would choose that menu if it were printed plain.

If you were choosing a hobby you loved, would you need a book to help you stop it? Would you hide it from your partner? Would your hand move before your mind agreed? The fact you are reading this with a pack in your pocket proves the choice was taken while you thought you were choosing.

That is good news, not shame. A flaw cannot be argued away. A con can. We were not weak. We were outgunned by a design that turns a faint tug and a loud story into a life of lights.

So hold your own life up to the light for a minute.

Did you need smoke to enjoy people before you smoked? Do small children need it to laugh without holding back? Do the non-smokers at your table get through the meal, the joke, the pause, the row, without collapsing? They do. Every day. Without effort. Without mints. Without standing in rain.

Then what does your dose add that the moment did not already own? Name one thing it adds that a non-smoker lacks in that same moment. One real thing, not the quieting of its own tug.

You cannot name it. No smoker can. That silence is the trap showing its edge.

We lit for years to quiet the Nipper and believed the Smokescreen that called the quiet a gift. Once seen, that gift dies. What is left is a paper tube, a chemical tug, and a story. Paper, tug, story. That is all that ever kept you.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

SUMMARY
- Hiding to smoke proves smoking stopped being choice long ago; choice would never need mints and lies.
- The hand often lights before any decision, which shows a tug and a story are acting, not a free will.
- The heaven second lives only in the lighting, not across the day, and leaves the smoker divided after.
- A tiny physical tug and a loud learned story keep the circle turning by feeding each other.
- The first cigarettes were tried on false ideas about calm and belonging, not on knowledge of garage nights and counted days.
