Chapter 11
NOT ONE, NEVER TOMORROW

*There is no safe single dose and no safe tomorrow — one only feeds what you mean to leave behind.*

### THE SMALLER DEAL

That late patio is settled — the glow was faces and talk, not leaf.

And the mind, seeing the favourite fall, reaches for a smaller deal.

We all did this. I did it for years. We said, very reasonable, very adult: I see the packet-a-day is a con, so I will be clever. I will smoke less. I will keep only the wedding one, the holiday one, the bad-day one. I will stop properly on Monday, after the deadline, in the new year.

Listen to that voice with clear eyes. It sounds like control. It is the same old hunger speaking in a lower voice.

Ask it plain.

If cutting down worked, why did every diary you ever kept creep back up?
If a special one did not count, why did you need a rule to protect it?
If tomorrow was a plan, why did tomorrow need another tomorrow behind it?

There is only one honest answer, and you know it. The bargain is not a way out. It is a way to stay in while sounding sensible about it.

### THREE A DAY IS STILL FALLING

Cutting down looks like sense because the numbers drop for a week. Ten becomes six. Six becomes three. The box lasts longer. The morning cough eases. You tell a mate, I have it under control now.

Watch what really happens in that week.

Each dose between longer gaps tastes stronger. The wait grows longer, the head-swim on lighting grows sharper, the little tug before lighting grows louder. You think about leaf more, not less. You clock-watch for the permitted hour. You guard your three the way a miser guards coins, and when the third is gone by four o'clock you borrow, you bum cigs, you count puffs to stretch the day. The diary on the kitchen side starts neat and ends scribbled, with arrows and extras and just-this-once in the margin.

We turned smoking into a timetable and called the timetable control. It was the opposite. We made each hit more precious by making it rarer.

I want you to see the mechanism straight, because this is where cutters-down fool themselves longest.

You never had a natural need for leaf between smokes. What you felt between smokes was the fading of the last dose. The body asks for another squirt because the last squirt wore off in minutes, and the mind reads that empty tug as hunger, boredom, tension, need-a-break. Then the next dose quiets the tug for minutes and takes credit for calming you.

It is the other way around. The dose does not quiet a natural need between smokes. It creates the need it then quiets for minutes.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

So what does cutting down do? It stretches the gap, so the tug grows louder. It makes the quiet on lighting feel bigger, so the credit grows bigger. It turns an hourly habit you barely noticed into an all-day negotiation you cannot stop noticing. When did you want leaf most — when you lit without thinking every forty minutes, or when you stared at the clock till twelve and told yourself you could have one at twelve?

Ask any cutter-down you ever knew, yourself included. Did fewer ever stay fewer? Or did fewer always mean thinking-more, wanting-more, then later-more, with interest?

Picture the cliff. A man stands at the edge and says, I will jump, but only a little way. I will fall three metres, not thirty. You would laugh. Falling is falling. The height you choose does not change the direction.

Cutting down is jumping off a cliff and hoping to fall only a few metres. You are still falling. You have only added the strain of deciding, each time, whether this ache deserves a dose yet. And the ache always finds a reason by evening — a phone call, a meal, a pause, a mate. The fourth dose hires the fifth. The weekday rule hires the weekend exception. The diary creeps because the tug was built to be fed hourly, not to be bargained with three times a day.

It was not control. It was rent paid in smaller coins, more often thought about.

### THE LAMP THAT REBUILDS THE HOUSE

The special one is prettier than the cut-down, and more dangerous.

I will give you mine straight, because I lived this bargain longer than any other. I had five months with no smoke. Five months. Morning kettle without the hand-search. Food tasting to the end. Stairs without the cage-rattle. Coat smelling of rain, not ash. I felt done.

Then a spring lunch, coats off, old mate with a pack tapped open. Laugh, sun, full belly. He held one out and said what hands have said a thousand times: go on, one will not hurt.

I heard my own head say what yours has said: I have proved I can leave it, so I can prove I can take one. Five months is control. One is nothing.

It was not nothing. I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way, thinking after 5 months I could control myself and just have one. Nope.

That first dose after months did not taste like heaven. It scratched. It made the head swim for seconds, then left the mouth dry and sour under good wine. But by evening the hourly nip was back, tapping the shoulder it had left alone for months. By next Friday I was buying a box for the weekend only. By the month's end the box was daily again, and I was out in the garage with mints, hiding the smell from the same people I had boasted to in spring.

Stay with that garage a minute, because this is what one really buys and no bargainer lets himself picture it beforehand.

Monday after the lunch, I told myself the weekend box would last. It did not last. I counted out Tuesday: two left for after dinner, nothing at lunch. By eleven the tug was pacing. I kept looking at the clock, at the door, at the drawer where the box sat. I lit at half eleven and told myself the count would restart tomorrow. The dose scratched, the mouth turned, the shoulders dropped for a minute and then the tug started again, a little louder for being fed.

Wednesday I bought ten to be sensible. Ten is sensible, the voice said. Ten means less. By Thursday night the ten were gone and I bought twenty because twenty was cheaper per dose and I was already smoking again so why pay more to smoke. That is the arithmetic the trap does for you.

Friday I smoked in the old slots and the new ones. Before kettle. After kettle. On the step with the phone. In the car with the window cracked. After food while the plate was still warm. Each lighting took ninety seconds and each wanting took an hour. The day became a string of permissions. Can I have one now? Has it been long enough? Will anyone smell it? Where is the mint packet? Did I Febreeze the hell out of myself enough for the sofa?

Money went the same way. Three pounds odd here, six pounds there, a fiver borrowed against Saturday. I stopped counting because counting meant seeing. At the end of the month the bank showed what the diary would not: over a hundred pounds burned for the right to be timed, soured and divided.

Mornings told it hardest. Before the single dose, mornings were kettle, piss, light through blinds, toast tasting of toast. After the rebuild, mornings were pocket-pat before feet touched floor, throat dry, head thick, coat on over pyjamas for the corner shop because the box was empty again. Shutters half up. The same till, the same nod. The first dose on empty lungs scratched and settled the hands, and the problem papers from yesterday sat exactly where I left them, untouched by leaf.

Evenings told it meanest. I had boasted in spring. I had said, I am done, I feel clear. Now I stood in the cold garage with the door cracked, blowing thin smoke into rain air, listening for footsteps in the hall, chewing a mint till the jaw ached, spraying the coat, washing the hands, rehearsing a face to wear back inside. Not because I am a liar. Because the dose makes a divided man: one voice in daylight saying no more, another voice in the yard saying just this one. Warm to myself, I can say it now — that hiding was not my character. It was the rent for keeping one brick.

And the body kept the ledger day by day. Tongue furred by ten. Fingers yellowed again. Chest tight on stairs I had climbed clear in April. Food shortened at the edges, wine soured for a swallow after each lighting, sleep broken by the three a.m. tug and the four a.m. cough. Nothing dramatic. Nothing a doctor would photograph. Just the slow return of stale, chained routine: pocket, doorway, tray, mint, shop, repeat.

That is what one does. It does not stay one.

Picture a brass lamp on a shelf. You rub it once for fun, to show friends there is nothing inside. Smoke pours out and builds the whole house again around you — walls, keeper, timetable, hiding place. You do not get to rub and then say, back in the lamp. The rub is the house.

One keeps the nicotine trap alive.

So hear this cold and keep it warm toward yourself: a long clear spell does not make one safe. The body forgets quickly. The belief is what remembers, and one re-teaches it. If you stumble and take a puff, do not turn it into a verdict on yourself. Put it out, see the scratch and sour for what they were, and keep the door shut behind it. A stumble is a rumble strip, not a crash. It warns. It never licenses another on purpose.

Never take a dose to test whether you are cured. The test is the trap.

### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP TALKING

Tomorrow sounds kind. It lets you keep the pleasure story while nodding at sense. After this box. After the holiday. When work calms. When I am ready.

We all stacked tomorrows. I stacked them for years. Each one cost what every smoked day costs — money burned, breath shortened, evenings timetabled by pocket and doorway, the sour mouth and the morning shop-run, the small divided self that says one thing in daylight and does another in the yard.

Count it honest. If leaf gave anything real, why would you need to quit tomorrow? And if it gives nothing, why pay another thirty, another hundred, another thousand doses to arrive at Monday?

Tomorrow is not a date. It is a mood. Fear of failing pulls one rope. Fear of succeeding without the old prop pulls the other. That pull both ways is the tug-of-war of fear.

See whose hands hold both ropes. Not yours. The con holds them, and it wins either way you stay. Fail and you smoke to soothe shame. Succeed and you smoke to mark nerves. Both ropes lead to the tray.

You ask, am I waiting to feel ready? Let me ask you back, as an investigator. What new fact will Monday give you that mornings, meals, work hours and watched nights have not already given? Will the next thirty doses teach you something the last ten thousand did not? Will the throat feel different on the 10,001st scratch?

You do not need another week of proof. Delay only rents more cost for no new information.

Walk that rented week with me, day by day, because tomorrow is never neutral waiting. It is active payment.

Say Monday and you say after this box. That box is twenty doses. Twenty lightings, twenty sourings of the tongue, twenty pauses to find doorways and trays. By night the box is soft at the corners and your coat carries the day. You tell yourself Tuesday will be cleaner. Tuesday buys ten to be sensible, then borrows three off a mate by evening because sensible ran out at lunch. Wednesday pays full price for twenty again because the ten-box arithmetic punished you, six pounds odd gone for the right to stay timed.

Count the week as a ledger, not a mood. A hundred and forty doses if you smoke your usual twenty a day. Forty-odd pounds burned in seven days for paper and ash. Forty-odd pocket-pats before you stand. Forty-odd doorway steps out while talk goes on inside without you. Seven mornings of furred tongue and dry throat before kettle. Seven nights of mint and spray and hand-wash before the sofa. One corner-shop run in pyjamas with shutters half up because Friday night emptied the box, the same till, the same nod, the same first scratch on empty lungs Saturday morning.

What did the week teach that you did not already know? Did the 8,000th doorway taste different from the 7,999th? Did the 140th ash add one fact about leaf? Did the till receipt make you wiser, or only lighter?

That is the cost of tomorrow. Not waiting. Paying — in coin, in breath, in divided evenings — to learn nothing new. Every tomorrow hires another week of pocket and tray and sour mouth and shop-run, and each week hires the next by the same two ropes: dread of trying, dread of living without. The hands holding those ropes are not yours.

Stop paying for proof you already own.

### STRAIGHT ANSWERS TO LAST EXCUSES

Put them on the shelf and answer them one by one, fast, in your own voice.

“I’ll just cut down to three a day.”
Then you will think about leaf all day to protect three. Three strong hits feed the nip harder than ten dull ones, and the fourth always finds a reason. No cutter-down stays cut down.

“I’ll keep only the special ones — Christmas, holidays, the big night.”
There are no special doses. There is only the same paper tube with brighter wallpaper behind it. Keep one occasion and the belief stays whole, and the whole house rebuilds from that one brick.

“I’ll stop tomorrow, after the wedding.”
Tomorrow never opens. The wedding smokes, the deadline smokes, Monday smokes, and each hires the next. The price is paid daily for nothing gained.

“If I slip after months, I’ve blown it, I may as well smoke.”
No. A puff after months proves one rebuilds the call. It does not prove you are broken. See it, shut the belief again, and walk on. What ruins people is not the puff. It is the story that one means all is lost.

“But the odds say most fail bare-handed. Don’t I need a patch or gum or vape?”
In neat trials a dummy can be beaten by a patch for a spell. Out in ordinary kitchens plenty of tries are made with bare hands and many do not hold first time. Neither fact decides you. Those numbers answer different questions about groups, not about what you see now. This is about seeing the credit was false, not about wearing a different glass for the same drink. If you live with illness or worry about stopping, speak to your clinician for your own care.

“One to prove I’m free won’t hurt.”
That is the lamp-rub. A person who has walked out does not need to prove it by feeding the old call. You prove it by keeping empty hands and tasting the whole night through.

Hear how each excuse arrives? “Go on.” “Just this once.” “Tomorrow.” When those lines come next week, you will know them. They are not your reasoning. They are the old script talking, pre-labelled, second-hand.

### THE OBJECTION TO THIS BOOK ITSELF

And one more voice, cleverer than the rest, will say: isn’t this book just brainwashing me the other way? You tell me it is easy, you tell me there is no pleasure, you repeat it till I believe it.

I welcome that question. Ask it hard.

Brainwashing put the pleasure there without your consent — films, hoardings, older hands lighting at every door, the timed nip misread as need. I am not asking you to take my word for anything. I am asking you to check with your own tongue, your own taxi home, your own tray at midnight, your own diary that crept up. Did the tube season the night or ash it? Did fewer stay fewer? Did months make one safe?

You are not being converted. You are investigating, and the evidence is your own life.

The method is not grit. No one here asks you to clench and suffer and count days. Past tries failed because the method starved you while leaving the belief fed. This time the belief is starved, and the little physical tug starves with it. All you do is see, and keep seeing.

Because the lamp rebuilds the whole house from one rub, because the cliff still drops if you jump it a little way, because tomorrow only hires another tomorrow at daily cost — there is only one rule that holds the door shut.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

**SUMMARY**
- Cutting down made each dose stronger in the mind and the wait louder, and the diary always crept back up.
- A special occasion did not change the dose, it only dressed the same tube in brighter lights.
- Five months clear did not make one safe, one woke the hourly call and rebuilt daily smoking within weeks.
- A stumble warns but never proves failure and never licenses another on purpose.
- Trial odds about props and street tries about bare hands answer different questions and neither decides what clear seeing does.
- Delay rents daily cost for no new proof, both fear ropes belong to the same hands.
