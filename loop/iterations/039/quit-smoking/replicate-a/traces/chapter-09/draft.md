Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

IN THIS CHAPTER
A pack weighed in the palm, a filter held to the light, two fears pulling opposite ways

*You never chose this hunger in the first place — it was built for you, and both fears that hold you were built with it.*

## The Box In Your Palm

You have lived the morning with the kettle, the plate left to linger, the doorway with cold on the face. You have seen those moments stand whole with nothing lit in them.

So why does the hand still move to the pocket?

Pick up the box and look at it with clear eyes, as if you had never seen it before.

Twenty white tubes in a row. Cardboard to keep them straight. Foil to keep them dry. Cellophane to keep them crisp. A flip lid that opens with one thumb. It fits the palm, the pocket, the bag. It goes where you go.

We learned to call that box a habit. It is not a habit. It is a day's supply box.

Count it. One for waking, one for the bus, two for the morning, one for coffee, one for after food, one for the call that ran long, one for the wait, one for the drink, one for the last thing at night. The factory counted your day before you lived it. It put the number in your pocket and taught you to call the counting yours.

Take one tube out. Paper, filter, brown leaf cut fine. It looks simple. It looks almost innocent.

In their own planning rooms, away from posters and slogans, one of their scientists wrote it plain: the cigarette should be conceived not as a product but as a package — the product is nicotine.

Read that again in your own terms. The paper is the wrapper. The tube is the squirt. The puff is the delivery. The box is the day's dosing kit.

Did you ever decide to carry a dosing kit? Did you go to a shop at fourteen, sixteen, twenty and say, I want to rent my mornings and my meals and my breaks to a drug, twenty squirts a day, every day, paid by me? Of course you did not. We were conned into the first ones by friends, by smoke in doorways, by posters that made lit hands look adult and calm, and then the dosing did the rest.

That box in your palm is the nicotine trap made visible.

Ask it plain.

Who chose twenty as your number — you, or the men who packed twenty?
Who chose the timing — your body, or the short fade that calls for the next squirt within the hour?
If this was your nature, why did it need a factory to teach it?

You did not build this want. It was built for you, and sold to you as you.

## The Holes In The Filter

Now hold the filter to the light.

Brown tip, white wrap, a neat ring of tiny holes round the paper. Mild. Light. Smooth. The promise printed in silver: less, cleaner, kinder.

We wanted to believe it. I wanted to believe it. Anyone with a cough and a stairs-breath pinch wants to believe there is a softer version.

Look closer. Those holes are theatre.

On a machine, with a rubber mouth drawing neat and steady, air slips in through the holes and the readings drop. Tar down. Nicotine down. Numbers to print.

A mouth is not a machine.

Put lips and fingers on that filter and you cover half those holes without knowing. Draw a shade deeper because the taste is thin. Hold a second longer because the hit feels missing. Take one more from the box because the last one left the old nip half-quieted. As a rule smokers pull harder on lights and take in what the machine said they missed — not to the millimetre the same in every smoker, bodies and days differ, but the direction is the same.

I speak here of how the trick works, not of any softer way to smoke. This does not make the hard-hitting kind a better choice.

That story that milder means safer is the Smokescreen speaking.

Ask it over your own hand.

If lights gave less, why did you smoke the same number, or more, and hold each deeper and longer?
If the filter protected you, why did the cough stay, the stairs stay hard, the morning throat stay sour?
If mild was mild, why did the timetable never loosen — why the same hourly call, the same pocket check, the same panic when the box ran low?

The holes did not change the drug. They changed the reading. They let the seller keep you dosing while telling you he was caring for you.

That is not care. It is packaging. The same dosing kit with a kinder costume.

## Afraid To Fail, Afraid To Succeed

This is where most smokers live now, once the mornings and meals have shown themselves whole.

Two voices. Opposite directions. Both loud at night.

“I have failed twenty times. Patches, gum, promises on Mondays. What if I try again and fail again? Better not to try than to prove I am weak.”

“I have smoked through my whole adult life. Breaks, nights out, work done, food finished. Who am I with empty hands? What if life goes flat and grey with nothing lit in it?”

I know both voices. I lived in both. Let me meet them straight, because they sound like wisdom and they are both the same lie.

First, failure. You did not fail. The method failed. A method that tells a conned person to white-knuckle through loss while still believing paper calms, rewards and completes will always produce shaking and relapse. That is not weakness in you. That is a wrong tool breaking in the hand. We all blamed the hand when the tool was blunt.

Think of the drawer of half-used boxes, the calendar crosses, the Monday vows. Did those attempts change what you believed paper gave you? No. They only demanded you suffer the missing of a supposed friend. Anyone would crack under that demand. The crack proved nothing about your spine.

Second, success. That flat grey stranger you fear becoming — where did you meet him? Not in life. In posters. In talk in smoking doorways. In the seller's old story that lit hands are lively hands and unlit hands are empty hands. You have already watched a kettle morning, a lingering plate, a cold doorway stand full with nothing lit. Was any of that grey? Was any of that missing? The stranger is not you without paper. The stranger is you with paper's credit removed from moments that were never paper's.

See the trick. One rope says you cannot stop. The other says you cannot live stopped. Pull on either and you stay at the post, lighting the next tube while arguing with yourself.

Did you tie either rope? Did you, as a child, sit down and decide that courage lives in paper and that joy needs smoke? Or were those ropes handed to you, one by the failed attempts the wrong method arranged, one by the seller who needs you afraid of your own clear days?

That pull both ways is the tug-of-war of fear.

Both ropes belong to the same hands. Not yours. The trap holds one end of each and lets you do the pulling. Drop the idea that the fear is information about you. It is information about the trap.

## What Staying Costs

It is time to look at what staying rents, because the seller never puts this on the box.

Stale. The mouth in the morning furred and sour. Clothes aired, windows opened, mints chewed, spray in the bag, the small lie about where you were for ten minutes. The kiss turned away. The child asking why you smell like that. We told ourselves no one noticed. Everyone noticed. We noticed most, and we carried the noticing like a second weight.

Breathless. The second flight taken slower. The laugh that ends in a cough. The chest a shade tight by evening. The swim in the head on the first drag called a lift, though clear heads never need to swim to stand. The body that was built to run and climb and sing taught to ration stairs. I speak of the trap's cost here, not of your body's care; if you feel ill or you worry, speak to your own clinician for your own care.

Chained. The box counted, the pocket patted, the shop run in rain, the evening cut short to buy for morning, the party stepped out of, the film missed, the train seat chosen for the next platform's doorway. Twenty decisions a day made by cardboard.

Put them together and stop looking away, because looking away is what the seller bought with silver words like smooth and mild. Mouth sour, chest tight, day timetabled by a box. Nights shortened. Money burned. Attention rented out hourly to a nip.

That is what staying looks like: stale, breathless and chained.

I lay that flat and I do not lay it to frighten you into stopping. Fear never freed anyone; it only makes the next tube feel like shelter. I lay it so you see who did this to you. You did not do this to yourself from weakness. A business that packs a drug as twenty social moments did this to you for profit, then taught you to call the doing your choice.

And here is the turn. Once you see the builder, the fear changes shape. The stairs pinch is not proof you need paper. It is proof paper took. The sour mouth is not proof you love lighting. It is proof lighting soured. The panic at an empty box is not proof of your nature. It is proof of a timetable packed by other men.

You are not a smoker by nature who must fear life unlit. You are a clear morning, a lingering plate, an open doorway person who was taught to pay a coin for what was already yours.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

**SUMMARY**
- The want did not start in me; the first tubes were conned, then dosing set the timetable.
- The box works as a day's dosing kit, not as company or character.
- The filter holes and mild promise change the machine reading, not the hourly need.
- Past attempts broke because the method kept the belief intact, not because will was missing.
- Fear of failing and fear of life unlit are both ropes held by the same trap.
- Staying keeps mouth sour, chest tight and days timetabled by cardboard.
