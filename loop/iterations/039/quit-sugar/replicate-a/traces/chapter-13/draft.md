Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

IN THIS CHAPTER — Photographed card, shut cover, clear morning table, market bag on the counter, lamp-lit sofa

*Sweetness was never lost; the Trap stole it and clear sight handed it back.*

### IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

I know that voice at once: “Just tell me what to do and I will pick the rest up as I go.”

I hear you. We all said it when hunt ran us. We wanted the short cut, the card in the pocket, the list on the phone, and to keep the evenings exactly as they were.

The fact is this page cannot do that work alone.

What changed you was not a list. It was seeing, room by room, that BAD SUGAR did plenty TO you and nothing FOR you, that the lift made the low, that the want was built and the hand moved before choice. A reminder cannot plant that sight. It can only keep sight you already own.

So if you opened here, shut this book now and start at the first page and read straight through. Read with a clear head and a full cupboard and let each picture do its job in order.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That is the whole contract. Not most of them. Not the ones that suit the week. Every one, in the order given, while you keep eating at hunger and stopping at satisfied.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice, and use this book for belief only.

Ask the short-cut straight, before you turn back to page one.

When did a tip sheet ever hold past nine at night — or did paper always burn first while the box emptied after? When did a picked rule ever close hunger — or did hunger always close best at a plain meal eaten hungry and left at satisfied?

Paper never held. Meals held. Sight held.

Short verdict for skimmers: the method first, the card after.

### CLOSE THE COVER AND EAT

You photographed the card. You shut the cover. The day waits exactly as it was.

That is the point. Nothing in the house needs moving. No drawer needs locking. No calendar needs marking. No talk needs rehearsing for the shop or the desk or the sofa. Change-nothing-else was never a slogan. It is lino under shoes, tap running, bags folded behind the bin.

We lived the other way. We set alarms for hunger. We counted brown squares. We promised small on Monday and paid family-size by Thursday. We told ourselves, treat when you feel you deserve one, and called the bill love. We called the fog help.

You eat another way now.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Hear how plain that sounds at a morning table. Kettle boils. Bread cuts. Apple sounds loud. Tea tastes of tea. Hunger talks, food answers, talk stops. No second voice bargains for one for the road. No hand lifts toward old height while the belly sits quiet. Supper will do the same tonight. Hunger will return because meals closed clean, not because a bell rang at sweet o’clock.

You will hear the old mutter once in a while, thin from far off. Mouth waters at wrapper rustle. Head says the hour needs brown help though the belly says nothing. Let it sound and pass unfed. It is learned firing, not orders. By cheese it dulls. By apples you have forgotten to listen.

Do you want to keep watching yourself? Of course you do not — that is why you read this book. Then do not watch. Keep meals, keep work, keep people, keep the walk to the window with tea.

When did watching ever close a day — or did days always close best when food closed hunger and hours filled themselves? When did a kept tin ever keep you — or did sight keep you while tins gathered dust?

Days you have lived give one reply.

Get on with enjoying your life.

### YOU DID NOT QUIT, YOU GREW

Here is the last turn, and it is saved for this page only.

You have not given up sweetness. Sweetness was taken from you.

Think when sweet tasted biggest. Small, hungry, summer fruit bursting, bread still warm, milk cold, mouth loud with it. No counting. No hunt. No fog after. Then brown doses taught the tongue that loud means brown, taught the belly that close means hunt, taught the head that love means wrapper. Taste narrowed to one note played too loud until other notes went dull. That was not your fault. You were conned out of a wide tongue into a narrow one, dose by dose, year by year.

Sight widened it back.

Now bread closes. Apple sounds. Soup steadies. Tea reports without story. Fruit, plain vegetables, plain meals eaten hungry satisfy the way they satisfied before the first dose taught doubt. Nothing was surrendered to get here. Something stolen was handed back.

I stood at my own counter and knew it with a spoon in hand. Soup hot, bread crust loud, belly closing quiet. No pull shouting. No bargain running. I laughed because the mouth I remembered from long before brown noise had walked back into the kitchen and sat down.

Ask that mouth straight.

When did brown stuff ever taste sweeter than hunger made plain food taste — or did the first square always promise most and the box always taste duller as it emptied? When did the dose ever add to a kind evening — or did kind always live in rest, warmth, people, while the dose billed after?

One answer fits evenings you have lived. The room gave. The dose took.

So choose which life runs on.

One road is dosing on schedule for the rest of your life, hunted mornings, bright aisles, three o’clock bells, boxes emptied before swallows end. The other is hunger met and left at satisfied for the rest of your life, clear tables, shops walked with new eyes, nights slept through, mornings woken hungry.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

You see any sweet thought now for what it is — Sweet Con script, not your reasoning — and you give it the one owned answer you keep:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!
