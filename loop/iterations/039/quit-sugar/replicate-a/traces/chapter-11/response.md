Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

### TOMORROW NEVER COMES

You have come far enough to see the whole shape.

You have seen the Friday rule fatten want. You have seen one open the hunt. You have seen the cliff own the fall. You have seen the house never hold the craving and the swap keep the hours alive under a new label. There is nothing left to fence, to date, to swap, to tidy.

And now the voice comes back in its kindest coat. It does not argue for Fridays. It does not argue for swaps. It says, very softly, in your own dialect, all right, I see it, so I will start after the holidays. After the birthday. After the travel. When work calms. When I am ready someday.

I hear you. We all said it. I said it myself on a Sunday night with a full drawer, meaning every word, telling my wife tomorrow would be clean. Monday noon the clean tomorrow needed one more day to be perfect. Tuesday needed the weekend out of the way. The drawer stayed full while the date slid.

Hear that date for what it is. It is not care. It is not wisdom. It is the Sweet Con talking with a calendar in its hand.

Ask tomorrow straight, as you have learned to ask everything else.

When did tomorrow ever arrive as today — or did tomorrow always arrive as today with a new tomorrow stapled to it? When did waiting ever thin wanting — or did waiting let the mouth rehearse and the picture grow brighter by promise? When did the birthday ever need BAD SUGAR to sing — or did people sing while the dose rode along and billed the evening after?

There is only one honest answer across all three. Delay feeds the very thing it claims to soften.

We lived that billing ourselves. One dose a day, ten minutes of hunt, thirty minutes of fog, one evening of sour belly, times three hundred tomorrows. That is not a small postponement. That is months of evenings paid for something that does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you. Teeth softened while you waited. Blood loaded while you waited. Hunger signals blurred while you waited. Wallet thinned while you waited. Mood thinned by evening while you waited. All TO you. Where in that waiting stands one thing done FOR you that people, meals, rest and laughter had not already done cleaner? None stands.

Do you want to start when life is quiet? Of course you do — that is why the date tempts you. Does life ever turn quiet on command, or does the Con always find one more loud week to protect? The second is true, and you know it from your own diary. Quiet never comes by waiting. Quiet comes by closing.

You can feel real relief in your chest when the calendar stops bossing you.

That is the whole move of this chapter. Waiting is not caution. Waiting is the Trap holding its door ajar with a smile. The only safe date is now, with eyes open, with an ordinary dose, with a vow that ends dating.

### ARE YOU CHAMPING TO CROSS?

Let us check readiness, not by mood but by sight.

Do you see that there is no benefit left to moderate? Yes. Do you see that one carries the binge? Yes. Do you see that Fridays, houses, tomorrows and swaps keep belief alive? Yes. Do you see that an accidental crumb never rebuilt a trap, while a deliberate date always does? Yes.

Then you are ready. Ready does not mean brave. Ready does not mean cheerful every hour. Ready means sighted. If you still feel pulled toward one more perfect date, do not force the moment. Read through again slowly and let sight settle. I tell you plain: you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by closing this tonight.

I make you no threat. I lay no deadline from outside. I ask only what your own audit already answered. Is there anything kind left in BAD SUGAR to say goodbye to? No. Is there any hour it closed hunger clean? No. Is there any Friday it kept? No.

So hear the contract as good news. This ends easily, immediately and permanently, not by stages. Not by taper. Not by shrine. Not by silver-wrapped stand-in. By sight, by vow, by an ordinary bite watched to the end.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That clause has carried you from the first page. Keep an open mind. Carry on as normal while you read. Judge only by what it does FOR you. Never blame yourself again. Begin with elation, not dread. Trust hunger and satisfaction. See the grumble as dying. Ignore voices that rebuild the Con. Ignore tales of struggle by the wrong method. Never keep a special sweet. Now this last one, spoken at dusk, closes the line clean inside which none is sought.

You picture your kitchen table bright and plain because you are free to taste food as food.

If that picture pulls you, you are champing. A horse at the gate does not need whipping. It needs the gate opened. I open it now.

### TAKE IT LIKE YOU ALWAYS TOOK IT

Do not make a laboratory of this. Do not buy a special farewell box. Do not pour a strange mixture to test yourself. Do not stage a last supper with candles and sorrow.

Take your last ordinary treat exactly as you always took it.

Go to the drawer as at ten at night. Take the ordinary bar, the ordinary biscuit, the ordinary handful you would have taken on any hunted evening. Sit at the kitchen table at dusk with lamplight on. No guests performing. No music swelling. Just you, the wrapper, the mouth, the hour you know too well.

This matters. A strange dose would let the mind say later, oh, that was not my kind, my kind still glows. An ordinary dose leaves nowhere to hide. Same brown stuff. Same timing. Same hand-to-mouth hurry. Same ten-minute lift while insulin works, same sour tail after. When the ordinary shows ugly, all kinds show ugly, because all kinds were the same bill.

We took thousands blind. We unwrapped while scrolling. We chewed while answering messages. We swallowed while planning the next. Tonight you will do the opposite. You will watch. You will taste on purpose. You will note. You will let the mouth tell truth without story, without cinema lights, without birthday song riding along.

Unwrap slowly. Look at it. Small, brown, tuned to bliss-point by people who never met you yet counted your hand. Smell it. Put the first square on the tongue and do not chase it with distraction. Let it melt. Note sweet, then too-sweet, then cloying at the edges. Note coat on teeth. Note thirst waking behind. Note belly talking though supper closed an hour ago.

Eat as you always ate, at your normal pace, neither dainty nor punishing. If you always took two bars, take two. If you always followed with cold cake over the sink, follow. Do not edit to prove control. Control is not the point. Sight is the point. Let the evening run its usual film while you watch from a new seat.

This is not loss; this is escape caught in an ordinary bite.

Ask the bite as it goes.

When does the first square close hunger — or does it open hunt? When does the second taste better than the first — or duller, furriner, more driven? When does the mouth say enough — or does the hand keep moving while the mouth complains?

Only one answer fits evenings you have lived. The start opens. The more dulls. The hand outruns the mouth. That is not character. That is schedule-plus-dose driving volume in your own kitchen, witnessed with lamplight on.

### THE UGLINESS UNDER ATTENTION

Stay with it to the tail. Most users never meet the tail because they leave the room before it speaks.

Ten minutes after the last swallow, sit still. Do not brush at once. Do not scroll at once. Do not tidy to blur the signal. Let the body report.

Tongue coats. A furry edge sits on teeth. Thirst stands up though water stands near. A sour note rises under the ribs. Head loses its bright edge and turns slow behind the eyes. The belly that closed quiet at supper now talks again, twitchy, hollow, asking for sweet to finish sweet. That empty, twitchy pull you once called need-something-sweet-now shows itself as echo, not call.

I name it plain so you feel steadied, not shamed. We read that echo as help for years. We said, I need sugary somethings to get me through. We said, treat when you feel you deserve one. We said, celebrating with chocolate. The echo never helped through. It only marked where the last dose dropped you. The rescuer was the perpetrator in a kind mask, the secret pusher offering a hand out of the ditch he dug at dawn.

Watch credit return where it belongs.

Was the ten-minute lift energy — or insulin working while the next low gathered? Was the evening glow love — or people, laughter, lamplight with a dose sneaking a ride? Was the calm after comfort — or tension briefly quieted that the dose itself had sharpened since three? It is the other way around. The dose creates the low it then lifts. The scene gives the glow it then claims. The hand thanks the pusher for the pull-out.

Plain tea after tastes wonderful and clean because the mouth tells truth at last.

Drink water now and taste metal. Eat a slice of apple and taste how loud real flavour sounds after brown noise. Note how ordinary food, eaten hungry, closes, while the brown stuff, eaten full, opens. Note how the roller coaster you once called mine never ran one hill. It always ran the full track to furry morning.

Ask that tail.

When did the tail ever sing — or did the song always live in the room, not in the wrapper? When did the dose ever leave you level — or did level always live before the dose, in hunger met, thirst met, rest taken? When did you ever need sweetening — or did sweetening need you, tuned, packed, shelved at till height to catch the hand?

One answer holds under lamplight. Nothing was given. Much was taken. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Let that land as arithmetic, not as scolding. Arithmetic needs no courage. It needs eyes.

### WHAT THE NEXT FEW DAYS HOLD

At this point a decent fear rises, and I meet it head-on because leaving it unnamed would leave it powerful.

“But what will the nights feel like? Will the pull be cruel? Will I lie awake hunted?”

Breathe. Hear the shape true, small as it is.

For a few days there may be a thin, twitchy want, strongest on the second or third evening, then thinning. Mouth waters at old hours. Hand opens the old cupboard from habit. Head says sweet o’clock though belly sits quiet. That is all. No high fever. No cruel siege. A days-long echo, small-scale, passing, not a lifelong fight to white-knuckle. The body absorbs and steadies. Hunger points to food. Food closes hunger. Thirst points to water. Sleep comes cleaner when the tail stops souring the night.

I say that to calm, not to frighten. The physical part is mild and brief. The belief part was the giant, and the giant falls tonight by sight. When belief goes, the echo starves unfed. When the echo grumbles, smile. A grumble unfed is a death rattle, not an order.

Both ends of the tug-of-war of craving and fear are held by the same hands.

See it. One rope pulls, have it, you need it. The other rope pulls, you cannot stop, you will fail. Both ropes belong to the Trap. Fear of missing and fear of failing are one trick with two ends. Cut the middle by sight and both ends fall slack at once. There is no outside to fear, no cage to miss — only habit pacing where a door stands open, like a lion pacing past an open cage.

So do not watch the days with dread. Watch them with curiosity. Morning one, tongue clearer. Shop aisle, packs loud yet oddly dull, cartoon faces shouting for a mouth that no longer answers. Evening sofa, hands quieter, tea tasting louder. Night, belly level, head level, no three o’clock bargaining. Your body steadies to a level hum within a few days.

If the hand moves from habit, note it without scolding. Habit is not need. A hand can open a cupboard and close it again while the head laughs at old wiring. If the mouth waters at a wrapper rustle, note cue firing, learned, not compulsion. A bell can ring though dinner never comes. Let it ring unfed and it dulls.

Change nothing else in life. Eat when hungry. Stop when satisfied. Keep bread, soup, markets, meals, tables, company. Keep mornings, shops, evenings exactly where they were, lived with new eyes. The baseline returns on its own. No props. No watches. No counting.

### THE TWO DOORS

Two doors will lie to you after tonight, and I mark them now so later each arrives already answered.

The first door comes on a hard day. Bills, quarrels, rain at the bus stop, a boss with cold eyes. The old script slides in, soft, reasonable: you have earned comfort, one kind dose will take the edge off, food is love.

Hear it as script, not as reasoning. When did brown stuff ever pay a bill — or did bills wait while fog thickened? When did it ever soften a quarrel — or did snap follow fog and shame follow snap? When did love ever live in foil — or did love live in people while the dose rode along? Only one answer fits hard days you have lived. The comfort never comforted. It billed.

Answer that door with the thought I give you now, spoken as lived fact in your own kitchen, not as slogan. When the sweet thought crosses at dusk or at the till, think at once, with a grin that reaches the chest: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it aloud tonight after the vow and feel how true it sounds against the furry tail. Say it tomorrow at three when the old hour rings. Say it at the cinema when foil rustles two rows down and pity, not envy, rises for hunted hands. Each time the thought comes, it will bring less pull and more grin, until one day you notice the thought has not come for weeks and you laugh at the quiet.

The second door comes on a bright day. Good news, sunshine, friends laughing, a table singing. The script slides in, light, almost playful: just one cannot hurt, you have proved sight, one to join in, one to show control.

Hear it as script, not as reasoning. When did one ever stay one — or did the sliver carry the binge every time schedule-plus-dose met? When did proving ever end — or did proof need proving weekly without end? When did joining ever need brown stuff — or did tables sing while the dose sneaked a ride? Only one answer fits bright days you have lived. One keeps belief alive. Belief keeps timing alive. Timing keeps hunt alive.

Answer that door the same way, louder, with lamplight still in mind. Mean it: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

I stood where you stand, bar half-melted, mouth coated, tail souring, calendar begging for one more slide. I spoke the vow aloud to my wife with the wrapper still on the table, ugly in sight, finished in mind. No sorrow rose. No hollow opened. A bell rang low in the chest, clear as a shop bell at opening. The date stopped sliding. The week stopped ticking toward Friday. Hunger talked and food answered. That bell rings for you tonight if you speak.

So speak now. Stand at the table. Look at the ordinary tail. Speak aloud, steady, cheerful, total: this was the last ordinary treat; the line around BAD SUGAR closes clean; no benefit remains to moderate; no date remains to wait for.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

Stay standing a minute after you have spoken. Do not tidy. Do not brush. Do not reach for the phone. Let the words hang in lamplight with the wrapper still ugly on the table.

Look at that wrapper. Torn, smeared, faintly sticky at the corner, smelling too-sweet now the mouth has reported. An hour ago that shine promised rescue. Now, watched to the tail, it promises nothing. Leave it there while you finish your water. Its ugliness is useful. It keeps sight warm while the chest catches up.

Look at the calendar on the wall. Tomorrow circled in red ink, birthdays underlined, holidays starred, all those sliding dates that bossed you. See what speaking did to it. The red circle has no job now. No proof to mount. No perfect morning to wait for. You uncap the pen and cross the circle out, slow, with pleasure. Ink over ink. The week lies flat again. Monday no longer points to Friday. Evening no longer points to hunt. The ticking toward the date stops in the middle of a tick, and the quiet after ticking feels wide.

Feel the chest. For years that ten-past-ten hour carried a tight band — belly talking though full, head bargaining, hand half-lifted. Notice the band slacken while belly still talks thin. The talk is only echo now, unfed, unobeyed. Breathe into it. Shoulders drop. Jaw unclenches. A low bell rings under the ribs, not loud, not solemn, clear and ordinary, like a shop bell at opening. That is not excitement you must pump up. That is weight off.

Sit down again at the same table, in the same dusk, as a changed eater. Put your palms flat on the wood. Wood cool, lamp warm, water glass sweating. No hunt to run. No second bar to fetch. No foil to hide before morning. The hour you know too well has lost its orders. You drink the rest of the water and taste how clean cold can be when the mouth tells truth. You rinse the furry edge and feel the tongue come back.

Walk to the sink with the wrapper. Drop it. Hear the small rustle as it lands on peelings and tea leaves. Wash your hands with soap. Watch brown melt off the fingers. Dry them on the towel. Those small doings, done without hurry, are the first doings of the new state. Nothing grand. Nothing strained. Hands washed, table wiped, lamp left on.

Evening runs on around you, ordinary and full. Kettle clicks. A neighbour laughs through the wall. Supper dishes wait. You eat nothing more because supper already closed, and the quiet belly under the thin echo proves closed can hold. You take tea and sit on the sofa where hunt used to sit. Hands lie still. No cupboard opening. No second opening to check the first opening. At nine the old hour rings faint — sweet o’clock — and you hear it as a bell with no dinner behind it. You grin in the lamplight of your own front room and think the thought I gave you, lived, not recited: BRILLIANT! I’M FREE!

Sleep comes without the sour tail. Mouth level. Belly level. Head level. No three o’clock bargaining in the dark.

Morning proves it before thought wakes. Tongue clearer, not coated. Thirst ordinary, answered by water that tastes of water. Hunger talks at breakfast time, plain and clean, and bread with butter with apple closes it whole. No furry return. No fog to lift. No star on the calendar to earn. You walk past the drawer and hear no pull from it, only the faint memory of a bell. The drawer has become wood with a handle. The aisle will be loud today and dull. The till will shine and miss. You are already on the other side of dating, living the hours that waiting always stole.

This is what now means. Not tomorrow promised. Now conferred.

**SUMMARY**
- Waiting never protected an evening; waiting trained want to swell toward the date.
- The last ordinary dose, watched to the tail, tastes cloying and sour, not kind.
- The ten-minute lift marks insulin at work while the next low gathers.
- A thin, twitchy echo for a few days passes and leaves no order behind.
- Both ropes of craving and fear belong to the same hands and fall together.
- A hard-day offer never paid a bill and a bright-day one never stayed one.
- A vow spoken tonight at the table closes dating and opens clear mornings.
