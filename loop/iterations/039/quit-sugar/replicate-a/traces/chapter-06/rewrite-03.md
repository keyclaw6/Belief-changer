Chapter 6
FOOD AS IT WAS MEANT TO TASTE

*Real food eaten hungry satisfies in a way BAD SUGAR never did.*

### THE GREY PLATE YOU FEAR

You come to this chapter with the fuel lie dead behind you. You watched the drawer write loans at three and collect at four. You felt the shoe pinch behind the sigh. Good. That was paper and clock.

Now a quieter fear speaks, and I want to hear it out loud because it keeps more hands in the packet than any drawer ever did.

It says food without sweet hits will be grey.

It says, "Pudding is love. A meal without something sweet at the end is unfinished. I am a comfort eater. Dessert is the part I live for."

We all said it. We lived for years with that sentence tucked under the tongue. No shame in hearing it. It is the talk the loop teaches when the bright wrapper has been praised so long the plain plate looks pale beside it.

Say it plain in your own dialect so we can look at it:

"I need a treat when you feel you deserve one."

"Without sugary somethings to get me through the meal feels flat."

"I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop — so food without them must be punishment."

I know this is hard to accept, but hear me as one who sat where you sit. I believed my favourite foods were all sweet. I believed a roast without pudding was work without pay. I believed hunger was an enemy to blunt with quick hits and satisfaction was a rumour other people talked about.

The fact is, hunger and satisfaction were never the enemy. They were the meal itself, drowned out so long you forgot their voices.

Ask three plain questions and answer without flinching.

When did a hurried sweet eaten standing at the cupboard leave you settled the way a hungry meal leaves you settled? When did the box emptied in ninety seconds close the evening the way a plate pushed away satisfied closes it? When did dessert ever love you back in the morning?

There is only one honest answer. The sweet never closed. It opened. It opened hunt though full, thought though tired, want though sour-mouthed. The plain meal closes. That closing is not grey. It is what you have been calling love without knowing it.

You think favourite must be a play on words. You think I mean you will learn to call plain food favourite the way a prisoner learns to call a cell home. You wait for the catch.

There is no catch. The pleasure is real.

I state this as settled fact because your own body will prove it before this chapter ends. Hunger makes flavour loud. Satisfaction makes it last. BAD SUGAR made both deaf.

Look at the shape we lived. Morning sweet tea and sweet cereal shouted so loud on the tongue that lunch arrived with ears ringing. Lunch plus pudding shouted again, so the afternoon arrived deaf. Evening box shouted all night, so morning arrived with a furred tongue and no appetite for breakfast. We called that dulled mouth normal. We called food grey. It was not grey. We were deafened.

Take the shout away and hearing returns. That is not poetry. That is how a tongue works when not shouted at three times a day.

### MORNING MARKET

Come with me to prove it, not in theory but in doing. This chapter is not a kill. It is a meal.

Saturday morning. No clock. You woke hungry, a low, empty, patient gnaw under the ribs, not loud, not panicked, slow. Plain hunger. You drink water. Mouth cool. You pull on shoes and walk to the market with a cloth bag folded under the arm.

Air cold on the face. Shutters rattling up. Canvas awnings still damp. The street smells of wet stone, coffee from a cart, earth from crates.

First stall. Vegetables heaped high. Dark green leaves with water beaded on them. Orange roots with tops still on, earth clinging to the skin. Red peppers glossy as paint. A crate of tomatoes that smell of vine even in cool air. You lift one. Weight heavy for its size. Skin tight, warm from the lamp. You hold it to the nose. Sharp, green, sweet in the grown sense, not the packet sense.

Next stall. Bread stacked in rows. Crusts cracked and dark. The smell rolls out when the back door opens — yeast, heat, toasted edge. You take a loaf still warm through the paper. Heat on the palm through the bag. Crust crackles under the thumb. The crumb inside will be open, chewy, sour in the good way that makes the mouth water.

Next stall. Fruit. Apples in boxes, blush and green, skins matte. Pears with necks that give just under the thumb. A tray of late berries, dark, dusty, fragrant. You try one grape offered on a wooden spoon. Juice bursts, sharp then round, seeds small. The mouth wakes and swallows by itself. No shout. A call.

You buy without counting, without bargaining with yourself. A bag grows heavy on the arm. Leaves brush the wrist. Bread warms the side. Fruit perfumes the bag from inside. Money handed over, coins cold, talk short and easy with the stall keeper about rain and what is ripe. You walk home slower than you came, bag bumping the hip, stomach still low and patient, pointing to the meal ahead, not to a drawer now.

Mark what has already happened before a bite. Eyes looked far and near. Hands weighed, thumbed, lifted. Nose led. Legs carried. Hunger pointed. That pointing is not pain to blunt. It is appetite tuning the instrument before it is played.

We shopped like this as children before talk taught us shopping meant hunting bright packs. We knew ripe by smell. We knew enough by weight. No label read our hunger for us.

You say, "But this is a pretty picture. My life is a Tuesday with bills."

Keep the basket. Tuesday eats from the same body. The market is not the point. Hunger is the point. The basket only shows what hunger does when heard.

I walked this market a hundred times dosed and a hundred times hungry, and I tell you cold what changed. Dosed, I bought bright packs first and fresh food as afterthought, chewed a bar on the way home and arrived with appetite blurred. Hungry, I bought what smelled ripe and arrived with appetite sharp. Same legs. Same stalls. Different ears. Hunger did that. Not virtue. Hunger.

### HUNGER IS THE SEASONING

Now answer the whisper underneath, because it will follow you from the table if not met head-on.

"Fruit is sugar too so nothing matters. Bread turns to glucose too. If the body runs on sugar, a bar is fuel all the same."

Hear your own sentence back. It mixes two different things and calls the mix proof.

The fact is, your body makes its own steady glucose from plain meals, bread, vegetables, fruit, protein, rest. That steady making is why a hungry meal carries you for hours without shout and drop. BAD SUGAR is refined sugar with added sugars with junk snack-carbs eaten as hits — sweet drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals, grazing doses that shout on the tongue and vanish underneath. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Fruit inside its skin, vegetables in their earth, a plain meal on wood are not the target. They belong on the table. Their sweetness can be enjoyed openly.

Ask the body, not the label.

When did an apple eaten hungry start a hunt for a second, third, tenth apple standing at the cupboard? When did bread with lunch demand bread again in an hour with drum fingers? When did a peach leave you sour-mouthed and hunting though full? When did a bar leave you settled till supper?

The honest answer concedes the point every time. Whole food eaten hungry satisfies and closes. The hit opens and nags. Same word sugar on paper, opposite work in your afternoon. Paper does not eat. You do.

I speak within strict care because honesty outranks force. I do not hand you a plan for what to weigh or when to chew. I do not divide the world into good food and bad food to fear. I describe what hunger does with real food and what the hit does to hunger. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry any medical risk around food, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Hear that limit clean and keep the table. For the eater this book speaks to, the message is simple and physical. Cutting BAD SUGAR is not cutting power. The body manufactures its own steady power from ordinary eating. You lose no fuel you owned. You lose shout and debt. What remains is not less. It is steady.

Think of hunger the way you think of sleepiness. Sleepiness is not a flaw to blunt with noise. It points to sleep and closes when sleep is met. Hunger points to a meal and closes when a meal is met. We would never call sleep grey because it ends in waking. Do not call hunger grey because it ends in satisfaction.

We were taught to blunt the point before it sharpened. Biscuit at eleven to blunt morning hunger. Sweet drink at three to blunt afternoon hunger. Box at ten to blunt evening hunger. Each blunting blurred the signal and called the blur normal. Then we said food does not satisfy. Of course dulled ears hear no music. Unblunt the ear and music returns.

You say, "But I loved the sweet taste itself. Plain food cannot match that shout."

True, and beside the point. Shout is not flavour. Shout is volume. A fire alarm is louder than a violin and no one calls it music. BAD SUGAR shouts. Real food plays. Once ears recover, you do not miss volume. You hear tone. Tone knows enough. Shout never knows enough.

It was hunger that did it. Not the dose.

### WHAT LOVE WAS ALL ALONG

Now take the last guarded sentence, the one you protect because it feels disloyal to question. Dessert is love.

We all lived that sentence. We ate pudding to pay ourselves after hard days. We handed chocolate to say sorry, to say well done, to say I love you. We sat on sofas with spoons and called the warmth love. No shame in that want. Wanting love is human. Misplacing where it lived is the con, never your character.

Strip the scene and watch where the warmth stays.

Birthday table. Candles lit. Faces round the wood. Someone you love laughs with mouth full. You laugh back. Light warm on skin. Talk overlapping. The cake stands in the middle with icing bright. You eat a slice fast, sweet on the tongue, then a second because hands move while laughing. Later the plates sit with crumbs and smears. Mouth furry. Stomach sour. Head already circling more though full.

Replay the same table without the hit, bite by bite in memory, and ask what carried the love.

Was it the icing, or the laugh with mouth full? Was it the sponge, or the faces round the wood? Was it the second slice eaten while talking, or the talk itself? When you picture the night a year later, what glows — the wrapper or the faces? When you tell the story, what do you describe — the sugar grams or the laugh?

Only one honest answer holds. The scene gave. The dose rode.

It was only ever sneaking a ride on your own life.

Think of cinema lights dimming, a shared sweet passed hand to hand at the funny scene, warmth in the chest as shoulders touch. Was sweet the warmth, or shoulders touching in the dark while laughing at the same line? Think of a hard day paid with a bar on the sofa. Was pay the bar, or feet up at last, door shut, body still, breath slowing? Think of a child offered a sweet for being brave at the dentist. Was courage the sweet, or the hand holding theirs, the praise, the pride?

We reassigned every good hour to the packet and left the hour itself unnamed. Take the packet out and the hour stands, brighter because not shouted through.

I stood at my own table for years and thanked the wrong guest. I thanked the pudding for the evening my wife laughed till she cried. I thanked the bar for the afternoon my work went well. I thanked the box for the night the house felt safe. The fact is, laughter did it, work did it, home did it. The dose stood in the corner taking bows for songs it never sang.

So with love. Food is love when hunger is met with care, when a meal is cooked and laid and eaten together, when hands pass bread and eyes meet over steam. That love lives in hunger met, not in shout added. A hungry table shared closes in talk and ease. A box shared standing closes in crumbs and hunt. Which taught you love? Which left you warm at midnight?

You fear plain eating will starve love. Watch the table and see the other way around. Sweet hits starved love into ninety-second pauses. Hunger feeds love into hours.

Hold the market basket beside the packet and judge by what each does for you in a life, not by volume on the tongue. Basket: hands busy, nose led, legs walked, hunger sharpened, meal noticed, satisfaction closed, talk easy, midnight quiet. Packet: hands tore, tongue shouted, hunger blurred, hunt opened, midnight loud. Which loved you back? Which left wrappers and want?

You already know. You lived both this week.

### THE HUNGRY TABLE

Now lay it down and eat it hungry. This is the encounter the whole book has been walking toward.

Kitchen midday. Light through the window on bare wood. Loaf torn, not sliced, crust scattering crumbs. Vegetables cooked plain — leaves wilted in a pan with oil and salt, roots roasted till edges char and centres turn sweet, tomatoes warmed till they slump. A bowl of apples and pears washed and piled. Water in a jug beaded cold. Nothing counted. Nothing labelled. Nothing earned.

You sit because hunger told you to sit. Not because the clock said. Not because the head chattered. Because the low gnaw grew round and clear and said now.

First bite. Bread crust cracks loud between the teeth. Crumb chewy, sour, warm. You chew slow because hunger makes you want to taste, not because a rule tells you to chew. Swallow. Warmth drops. Stomach notes it.

Second bite. Roasted root, edge crisp, centre soft. Salt, char, earth, sweet where heat made it sweet. Oil coats the tongue. You close the eyes without deciding to. The mouth waters and asks for the next bite. Not hunt. Invitation.

Third bite. Leaves, soft, salty, a little bitter in the grown way that makes the next bite of bread taste sweeter. You drink water. Cold cuts through warm. Mouth clears and returns sharper.

You talk between bites or you eat quiet. Someone beside you laughs about the morning. The room is warm from the oven. The plate empties at its own pace. Hands slow by themselves halfway through. Breath deepens. Shoulders drop an inch. The gnaw thins from the edges inward.

Then it happens, the thing BAD SUGAR never gave you. Satisfaction arrives as a plain signal. Not stuffed. Not fogged. A level line. Stomach full but light. Mouth calm. Head quiet on food. You push the plate a half-inch away without debate. You tear off one more small corner of crust because you want it, eat it slow, and stop because you want to stop. Fruit after, an apple cut in wedges, juice bright, crunch loud. One wedge. Two. You leave the third on the board because enough is enough, like a child leaving a peach half-eaten to run back to play.

Sit with that left wedge. In the old reading it proved weakness. In the true reading it proves the system works. Hunger opened. Satisfaction closed. No counting closed it. No fear closed it. The body closed it.

Ask it cold.

When did BAD SUGAR ever close like that, by itself, without a vow, without a lid slammed, without hunt an hour later? When did a packet ever leave a last wedge on the board from fullness rather than from shame? When did standing bites ever end in this level quiet?

Only one answer fits every kitchen you ever stood in. The packet never closed. It paused. The meal closes.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Hear that sentence as description, not as order. It describes what you just did at the table. You ate what you wanted from ripe food. You started when hungry. You stopped when satisfied. No punishment followed because none was needed. That is not a plan. That is hearing returned.

Stay at the table a minute longer with the plate pushed back. Do not hurry to clear. Hands rest on the wood. Cup warm under the palm. Breath low and even. Light moves a fraction across the boards. Talk drifts on about small things, the price of pears, rain coming, a key left in a coat. You listen without hunger pulling at the words. The body sits level, stomach full but light, mouth calm, head quiet on food. That quiet is the close working.

Now you rise. Chair legs scrape soft. You lift the plate. Crumbs gather under the thumb. Warm water runs in the sink, steam rising to the window. You slide the plate under, cloth in hand, oil lifting, wood of the board wiped clean. Hands move without hurry. No tearing of bright corners. No folding small to hide. Hands wash, dry, still. The kitchen smells of bread and washed leaves, not of sour mouth. The bin stays shut. That stillness in the hands is the first hour after satisfaction.

Walk back into the day and feel what level carries. The room you return to looks the same, papers where you left them, light thinning a little toward afternoon. You sit. Shoulders grounded. Feet flat. Eyes come back to near work without burn. You read a page to the end. You answer in three lines. You file. Hands type when typing is needed and rest when resting is needed. No circling thought pulls toward the cupboard. No loud now sounds in the head. Hunger has closed and will not speak again for hours. That silence leaves attention whole.

Mid-afternoon passes in that whole state. You stand to stretch, roll the shoulders once, drink water slow from a cool glass, look out past glass to sky and roofs, blink, return. Legs carry weight to the next room and back, solid through the soles. Talk comes easy with the person at the next desk about the morning job, a laugh, then back to work without the head trailing elsewhere. Mouth stays clean. Stomach stays level. The hours do not drag around food because food has finished its work.

Evening comes on that same level line. Kitchen lit warm. No dread of the sofa hours. You cook plain from what is left in the bag, leaves in oil and salt, roots charred, bread torn. Talk easy across the wood about the day. Steam rises between faces. Hunger has returned low and round to point to supper, and supper is met hungry and noticed. Hands slow halfway by themselves. Satisfaction closes again, level, plain. Plate pushed a half-inch. Hands still.

Night stays quiet after a closed day. No box opened on the sofa. No rustle under talk. Television plays, voices low, body full but light, mouth calm. Eyes grow heavy in the worked way. You climb stairs with legs steady, wash, mouth clean, bed cool. Sleep comes without sour stomach and without food-thought circling past midnight. The house ticks. Hands lie still in sleep.

Morning returns the proof. You wake with hunger low and patient under the ribs, clear, not panicked. Mouth clean, not furry. Head clear, not loud. Water cold on the tongue. Light through the blind. The low gnaw points to breakfast the way evening pointed to sleep, a signal to meet, not a flaw to blunt. You stand, pull on shoes, breathe air at the window, and feel appetite tuning before the first bite. That return of morning hunger is what a midday closed by satisfaction does to the hours after it. It hands the evening quiet, the night quiet, and the morning clear back.

Try it across three ordinary days so one table cannot be called luck.

Monday. Breakfast when hungry, not when anxious. Bread, fruit, water. Stop when level. Note the hour hunger returns, slow, low, patient. Meet it with a plain lunch. Stop when level.

Tuesday. Same. Hands reach at three from habit, not from hunger. Hear the reach as habit sounding. Drink water. Stretch. Look far. Work on. Hunger will point to supper later. Meet it then.

Live Tuesday afternoon through, hour by hour, with the table already in your body.

Three o clock passes with the drawer shut. The head murmurs half a sentence from old timing and then has nowhere to land because hands stay with keys and eyes stay with the page. You stand, roll the shoulders, drink a glass of water slow, look out past the car park to the far roofs, sit again. Work thickens. The murmur thins.

Four o clock. The inbox moves one, then another. Mouth dry, you drink again. Legs want movement, you carry paper to the next desk and back, feel weight through the soles, sit again grounded. No bright interval bought. No dull interval repaid. Attention stays present, not soaring, staying.

Five o clock. Screen dimmed. Desk clear. No wrappers to fold small into the bin. Stomach gives its first low, round signal, slow and patient. Not loud. Not panicked. You note it the way you note light thinning outside. Supper points, not drawer.

Home. Kitchen lit warm. The same plain food from the market, cooked plain. Leaves in oil and salt, roots charred at the edges, bread torn, apples washed. You sit hungry. Hunger tunes everything before the first bite.

First forkful noticed. Salt and oil and leaf-bitterness, chewed slow. Second forkful savoured. Root crisp outside, soft inside, sweet where heat made it sweet. Bread crust cracks. Water cold cuts through warm and returns the mouth sharper. Talk easy across the wood about the day, a laugh, steam rising between faces. Hands slow halfway by themselves. Breath deepens.

Satisfaction closes level. Plate pushed a half-inch. One small corner of crust taken because wanted, eaten slow, then hands still. Fruit wedges bright. One. Two. Enough. No lid slammed. No vow made. Body closed.

Evening sofa. No box opened. No rustle. Body full but light, head quiet on food, mouth calm. Television plays, talk drifts, eyes grow heavy in the worked way, not the hunted way. Midnight quiet. Bin without bright corners. Mouth clean. Sleep comes without sour stomach and without food-thought circling past midnight. Hands still in sleep.

Mark Tuesday whole. Morning hungry, midday level, afternoon present, supper noticed, evening closed, night quiet. Which hour was grey? The crust cracking was not grey. The juice bursting was not grey. The talk easy was not grey. The level close was not grey.

Wednesday. Same body, harder test, same hearing.

Morning. Wake with hunger low and clear. Water first, mouth cool. Breakfast when hunger says now. Bread warm, fruit cut, water. Chew slow because want to taste. Stop when level. Walk to work with legs carrying, bag on the hip, air on the face.

Midmorning. Hands reach from timing. Hear it as timing. Drink water. Look far past glass to sky, blink, return. Work on.

Midday. Birthday cake goes round at work. Talk is loud. Hands offer. Icing bright under strip light. You smile, hold your tea warm in both palms, let the talk wash past without debate in the head. No lecture held. No pang nursed. Hunger has not yet pointed to lunch, so you do not eat to the clock. You drink tea, finish the paragraph, file it.

One o clock. Hunger rounds and clears. Now. Canteen plain food, eaten hungry. Vegetables with bite still in them, potatoes roasted, bread, an apple. First bite loud in flavour because hunger tuned it. Chew, swallow, talk with the next table about rain, drink water, hands slowing by themselves. Level line arrives. Tray pushed. Hands still. Return to the desk present.

Three o clock. The old bell time. Drawer shut. Screen glowing. Murmur half a sentence, then work. Stand, stretch, window air, sit. Eyes read. Fingers type. The careful paragraph that stands gets written steady, not fast. Numbers line up. You save.

Four o clock to five. Steady staying. Shoulders tight, rolled loose. Jaw tight, loosened. Stomach quiet, pointing forward to supper, not backward to hunt.

Supper hungry again. Same wood, same steam, same noticing. Roots, leaves, bread, fruit. Savoured without hurry. Stopped at satisfied without argument. Evening talk easy. Midnight quiet again. Hands still again.

Two workdays lived without a top-up and without paleness. Monday proved hearing returns. Tuesday proved supper closes after a full desk day. Wednesday proved a loud cake round leaves no hole when lunch is met hungry later. Each day a little clearer in taste not because effort grew but because shout faded.

What fades when unfed was never food. What returns when heard was always yours. Hunger sharpens. Satisfaction closes. Flavour loudens in between.

Hold Tuesday supper beside Wednesday lunch and judge by a life. Hands that weighed tomatoes now hold work you made. Nose that led to ripe now leads to supper. Mouth that shouted all year now tastes char and salt and juice. Belly level at nine, head quiet at ten, midnight without hunt. Which part needed volume to matter? Which bite asked for shout to complete it?

Once seen, favourite changes meaning without a trick. Favourite is not the loudest hit. Favourite is the meal hunger makes sing and satisfaction lets end. Vegetables with earth still on them. Bread that crackles. Fruit that bursts. Water cold. Company warm. Hunger low and patient. Satisfaction level and plain. Noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied.

That is not a play on words. That is dinner.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.
That hungry table is how you live it.

**SUMMARY**
- Hunger is a tuning signal that sharpens flavour, not a flaw to blunt before meals.
- A plain meal eaten hungry and finished at level closes the evening in a way packets never close.
- The body makes steady power from ordinary eating, so life without hits is not missing fuel.
- Whole fruit and plain meals belong on the table and their sweetness can be enjoyed openly.
- Warmth at birthdays, cinemas and sofas lived in people and moments, not in the dose that rode along.
- Tuesday supper and Wednesday lunch prove ordinary workdays stay favourite when hunger is heard and satisfaction is allowed to close.
- Favourite means the food hunger makes sing and satisfaction brings to a natural stop.
