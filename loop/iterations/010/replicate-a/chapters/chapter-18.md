### WILL YOU REMEMBER WHEN MEMORY FADES?

Do you feel free? Really free?

If you do, wonderful. If a small voice says what if I forget, that is wonderful too. It means you care about staying free. Can we answer that worry in one page, so you never need to carry this whole book again?

We spent seventeen chapters taking the Sugar Trap apart. We saw how the trap was sold to you on phoney information as a child. We switched the question from does it harm to what good does it do at all. We found nothing. We watched each claimed benefit — energy, treat, taste, cope — do the same trick. It stood next to a good moment and went sneaking a ride. We named the two creatures that kept it alive. We exposed the willpower method that kept you failing. We redefined BAD SUGAR cleanly and gave you Nature's Guide to live by. You made a solemn vow. You walked the same aisle, sat at the same table, met the same stress, and found the moment of revelation was ordinary. Life without BAD SUGAR was not flat. It was steadier.

Is it not true that all of that lived as understanding, not as memory work?

Yet memory fades. That is normal. Vivid feelings soften. The shopping smile, the party ease, the evening without the graze — they become your normal and the old film gets dusty. Will the brainwashing use that dust to repaint the trap as pleasure again? Will one thought — just one won't hurt — find you unguarded?

It will try. That is its job. Your job is simpler.

You do not need to re-argue seventeen chapters on the spot. You need a pocket manual that holds them for you. One page you can keep. One thought you can think. One action you can take. Will you let me give you that?

We have already given you the thought. You know it by heart. But this chapter gives you the whole checklist in the exact words we used to free you, so whenever doubt flickers you do not debate. You open, you read, you rejoice.

And you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by keeping it close.

That promise was true on page one. Is it not truer now?

### WHAT THIS CHECKLIST IS FOR

This is not a diet sheet. It is not a set of rules to resist with.

Remember the ledger we drew together? Two columns. One headed doing TO you vs doing FOR you. On the TO side we wrote the tiredness, the roller coaster, the restless wanting, the money, the health load. On the FOR side we found nothing that survived ten honest minutes. That ledger is the truth about BAD SUGAR.

The checklist below is that ledger compressed into twelve lines you can carry. Each line is a frozen instruction we made together at its own climax in this book. Each line worked because the belief behind it had already changed. Now the lines guard the belief.

How will you use it?

First, you read it now, once, warmly, as a celebration. Not as study. As a reminder of what you already know.

Second, you keep it where you can see it for a week or two. Inside a cupboard. On your phone. Folded in your purse. You glance at it when you like. It steadies you the way a map steadies a walk you already know.

Third, when BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — and it will, faintly, as old habit does — you do not wrestle. You do not analyse. You do what free people do. You think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE FROM SUGAR! and you get on with enjoying your life.

Is that hard? No. It is relief reporting for duty.

I promised you the method would be easily, immediately and permanently true if you followed it. Do you remember how impossible that sounded in Chapter 1? Does it sound impossible now? Check. You are not resisting. You are not suffering. You are not counting days. You stopped. You are free. The contract is proved by you, in you, right now.

And notice what we do not do here. We do not ask you to give up sweetness. We do not ask you to stay strong. We do not ask you to avoid life. There is no willpower method hiding in these pages. There is only understanding, and the joy that follows it.

We were conned together. We escape together. The checklist keeps the escape clean. Will you keep it?

### THE TWELVE INSTRUCTIONS — WORD FOR WORD

Here they are. Exactly as we gave them. No new words. No softer version. All you have to do is follow all the instructions — that was our deal from the start, and it is still our deal at the end.

Read each one slowly. See its chapter behind it. Remember why it was wonderful news. Then let it do its job again.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND — Question what you think you know about sugar; don't decide until you've finished the book. (Chapter 1)**

You gave me this gift at the start. You let me show you the Sugar Trap before you judged it. Keep that openness now. When an old thought says but this time is different, meet it with curiosity, not debate. Ask: is that doing TO me or FOR me? The answer ends the thought.

**2. DO NOT TRY TO CUT DOWN OR QUIT BAD SUGAR WHILE YOU READ — Carry on exactly as normal until you finish the book. (Chapter 1)**

We removed pressure so belief could change first. That work is done. You have quit. There is nothing to cut down to. The line is clean. Free means zero BAD SUGAR — and plenty of everything good.

**3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF ELATION — Cast aside all doom and gloom; see this as an exciting adventure to escape. (Chapter 1)**

Dread closes the mind. Elation opens it. You began with excitement. You finished with pride. Keep beginning. Every time you think of sugar, return to elation. You are not losing a pleasure. You escaped a con.

**4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS — The method only works if you follow all the instructions; all you have to do is follow all the instructions. (Chapter 1)**

This is the contract that makes it easily, immediately and permanently true. Follow all twelve and you stay free. No picking. No editing. No private deals. The trap kept you by inviting private deals. Freedom keeps you by keeping the contract whole.

**5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD — Including diets, willpower tips, and stories from people who quit by suffering; if you have diabetes, an eating disorder, or are under medical nutrition therapy, continue to follow your clinician's advice on food and medication — this book does not replace medical care. (Chapter 6)**

The willpower method will knock on your door again through friends, magazines, and old habits. It says resist, restrict, suffer, count. That method is the enemy, not the cure. Smile and ignore it. It never freed anyone without misery, and misery never keeps anyone free. The tug-of-war of fear belongs to that method — both ropes were the trap pulling against itself. You stepped off the rope. Stay off. If you have diabetes, an eating disorder, are under medical nutrition therapy, or take medication affected by diet, continue to follow your clinician's advice on food and medication — this book does not replace medical care. If you feel you need support, ask a clinician or trusted support service.

**6. NEVER DOUBT YOUR DECISION TO QUIT BAD SUGAR — Once you make the vow, never reopen the question; referral to clinician exception as in I-05 still applies. (Chapter 15)**

The vow was solemn and final. You made it while looking clearly at what BAD SUGAR really is. Doubt is not analysis now. Doubt is the Sweet Brainwashing asking for its job back. You do not re-interview a dismissed liar. You keep the door closed.

**7. DO NOT USE SUBSTITUTES THAT KEEP THE ADDICTION ALIVE — If you have diabetes, an eating disorder, or are under medical care, follow your clinician's guidance on any substitute — this is not medical advice. (Chapter 16)**

Do not swap one dose for another that keeps the same belief alive. A substitute that says I need a hit, just a different hit, keeps the Gremlin fed with story even while you starve it of BAD SUGAR. Choose real food from Nature's Guide. Let the 0–20 gauge and the raw test be your guides. Primary foods satisfy. Secondary foods have their place. Factory fixes do not.

**8. REJOICE AT A DEAD ENEMY, DON'T MOURN A LOST FRIEND — Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind, think: FANTASTIC! I'M FREE FROM SUGAR! (Chapter 16)**

This is the heart of staying free. You cannot not think of BAD SUGAR on command — the pink elephant proves that. So you reframe, you don't suppress. The same fact — I don't do BAD SUGAR — can feel like loss or like victory. Choose victory. It is the truth. Feel the relief, say the words, smile. The Gremlin's faint, empty whine becomes your signal to celebrate its dying. Fantastic. It is dying. You are free.

**9. PITY, DON'T ENVY, THOSE STILL TRAPPED — See their fix as discomfort, not enjoyment. (Chapter 16)**

At the table or in the aisle you will see someone chasing the lift. Envy says they have the treat and you don't. Pity says they have the itch you are free from. Which is true? Look kindly. Is their face lit with genuine pleasure — or hurried, restless, a little disappointed as the hit fades? You know that face. It was yours. Pity keeps the story honest. Envy rewrites it.

**10. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL OCCASION EXCEPTION — One "just this once" keeps the Sweet Brainwashing alive. (Chapter 9)**

One exception rebuilds the whole trap. It says BAD SUGAR has a special place where it does give genuine pleasure. It does not. It was always sneaking a ride on the occasion. Birthdays were faces, music, sun through the window. The cake was scaffolding you no longer need. Keep the occasion. Leave the trap.

**11. FORGIVE A BLIP, LEARN, AND STAY FREE — A slip is a rumble strip, not a crash; if it happens, do not deliberately repeat it; if you have a medical condition, follow your clinician — this method does not override medical care. (Chapter 16)**

Your body can cope with an occasional blip, but your mind cannot if you let the story back in. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for erratic driving. If a blip happens — sauce, a bite, a confused label — you do not punish yourself. You do not say I've blown it. You learn, you see the thought that invited it, you laugh at the con, and you stay free. Deliberately repeating it is not forgiving. It is re-hiring the liar.

**12. ENJOY YOUR LIFE — Get on with enjoying your life; you didn't give up sweetness, you recovered your taste for it. (Chapter 18)**

This last instruction is the whole book in one line. You change nothing else in your life to prove freedom. You go to the same cafés, the same birthdays, the same evenings. Freedom is not a new life you must build. It is this life without the con. Nature's Guide walks with you — the raw test, the 0–20 gauge, primary and secondary foods — but life itself is the point now. Taste is brighter when it is not shouted over.

Do you see? Twelve lines hold seventeen chapters. Twelve lines guard for the rest of your life what took a few hours to see.

You kept them easily once. Will you keep them again?

So if you opened the book at the back hoping for a shortcut, honour yourself enough to take the full walk. You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading properly. All you have to do is follow all the instructions, in order, with an open mind and a feeling of elation. Then this checklist becomes what it is meant to be — a souvenir, not a struggle.

Will you?

### YOU DIDN'T GIVE UP SWEETNESS — YOU RECOVERED IT

I promised you one fresh reframe at the end. Not a recap. A revelation. Here it is.

We called this book Quit Sugar. The title is useful. It is also, at the deepest level, untrue. You did not quit sweetness. You recovered your taste for it.

Think of the pet rocks we carried.

For years we picked up a rock and told ourselves the rock was our sweetness. Our treat. Our reward. My chocolate. My biscuits. My thing at night. The rock became identity. We stroked it when life wobbled and called the stroking comfort. We envied others their rocks. We feared life without the rock would be flat and less kind.

The rock was BAD SUGAR. And BAD SUGAR was never sweet in the way we claimed.

True sweetness is subtle. A strawberry. A ripe peach. Milk. An apple that snaps. The body recognises that sweetness at the right volume. Nature's Guide shows you where it lives — in whole fruit, in vegetables, in plain dairy — alive, accompanied by fibre, water, structure, satisfaction.

BAD SUGAR is not that. BAD SUGAR is sweetness weaponised — refined, concentrated, multiplied, engineered to spike and crash, stripped of everything that tells the body enough. It shouts over true sweetness until true sweetness sounds quiet. You did not need more sweetness. You needed less shouting.

Do you remember the S-13 story from the previous chapter — the child with the pet rock who believed calm came from the stone, forgetting the hand was already capable? That child grows up and keeps carrying stones into every room. Love equals sweets. Rest equals biscuits. Celebration equals cake. One day someone gently says put the stone down and taste again. The hand feels lighter. The tongue wakes up. What was muted returns.

Is that not what has happened to you?

This is why we can say, with no deprivation, that you have everything to gain. Taste did not leave you for the rest of your life. It came back for the rest of your life. Energy did not leave. Steadiness came back. Evenings did not empty. They filled with the good that was always there — company, light, warmth, talk — now without a needy guest demanding attention.

There is a word that captures this better than quit. The word is burgeon. It means to grow, to bud, to push out new leaves. We did not strip a pleasure away. We stopped crowding a natural sense and let it burgeon again.

So when memory softens and the old film offers to repaint BAD SUGAR as genuine pleasure, answer it with this fresher truth. You didn't give up sweetness — you recovered your taste for it. You didn't lose the sweet life. You escaped the con that dulled it.

Say it and test it. Bite an apple tomorrow after a walk, when your 0–20 gauge says you are truly hungry, after days without the shouting. Does it taste sweeter than you remembered? Is that deprivation? Or is it return?

You know. Your mouth knows. Your life knows.

And whenever the old story flickers, what will you think?

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE FROM SUGAR!

That thought now carries this reframe inside it. It says I am free from shouting. Free to taste. Free to grow.

Do you feel the quiet pride of growth replacing the panic of loss? Keep it. Growth is yours for the rest of your life.

### GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

We are at the last line of our walk together. What now?

Now you get on with enjoying your life.

Not with a plan to stay strong. Not with a vow to resist. Those belong to the willpower method. You did not need strength to leave a burning building. You needed to see the fire. You saw it.

You go to the same places. You do the same things. You eat with appetite from Nature's Guide. You notice any faint, empty, slightly restless, slightly off feeling and smile — there is the Gremlin, small, starving, already dying. You hear any loud story about treat or reward and smile — there is the Sweet Brainwashing, the Big Monster that fed it, losing its voice. You see others still in the trap and feel warm pity, not envy. You do not preach. You live visibly at ease.

Will money and health improve? Yes. As bonuses. Not as motives. You did not escape to save pounds or to chase a number. You escaped because life is better free. The bonuses come along for the ride, the way health came back when you stopped wearing tight shoes for the pleasure of taking them off. The relief was the point. The healthier feet are the bonus. Keep the point the point.

Do you feel ready to close the book? I think you do. You have been ready since Chapter 15. You took the final dose, you spoke the vow, you woke up to steadier days. This checklist is just the string around your finger.

So take it. Keep it. Use it lightly.

And whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — tomorrow, next month, at the next birthday where laughter fills the room — think:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE FROM SUGAR!

Say it like you mean it. Because you do. You are not mourning a lost friend. You are rejoicing at a dead enemy. You are not missing sweetness. You have recovered it. You are not white-knuckling for the rest of your life. You are free for the rest of your life.

YOU ARE FREE.

GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE.
