### WHEN I SAY SUGAR, TAKE IT TO MEAN BAD SUGAR

Let me fix language so it can never confuse you again.

For the rest of this book — and for the rest of your life whenever you think of this trap — when I say sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

> **BOXED DEFINITION — BAD SUGAR = all refined sugar, all added free sugars, and processed junk carbs (including white flour/bread/pasta/rice, sugary drinks, sweets, chocolate, biscuits, pastries, most packaged snacks).**

Read it twice. Say it once out loud.

BAD SUGAR.

That CAPS name now carries the definition in every sentence. It does the work so you don't have to rehearse the list. When you see biscuits, you do not debate ingredients. You recognise BAD SUGAR. When you see white bread that could not exist without a factory, you recognise BAD SUGAR. When you see a sugary drink that hits your bliss point by design, you recognise BAD SUGAR.

Do you feel how different that is from the old, foggy word "sugar" that made fruit sound guilty and a chocolate bar sound like food?

The decree is simple. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

Good sugars — the intrinsic sugars inside whole fruit, whole vegetables, plain dairy where they come wrapped in fibre, water, structure and bulk — are not the target. They are not what trapped you. They are part of what frees you. Why? Because your body did not evolve craving an apple the way it was taught to crave a packet of biscuits engineered to dissolve with no chewing.

"Are you saying natural sugar is automatically safe in any amount?" No. I am saying the trap is not nature. The trap is refinement, concentration, and engineering. The apple still has chew, water and fibre that tell your body when you are satisfied. The biscuit was designed to bypass that telling.

"Do I have to quit fruit to be free from BAD SUGAR?" No. You do not. Nature's Guide keeps it. You would not tell a person to stop drinking water because factories sell sugary drinks, would you?

Let me state the fact plainly, as settled fact, so the Sweet Brainwashing cannot muddy it again.

The fact is you are not quitting sweetness. You are quitting BAD SUGAR.

Short verdict. Let it stand.

BAD SUGAR is what you escape. Sweetness is what you recover.

There. The line is operable. Does it feel cleaner already?

You might ask, "But isn't that line too strict? Isn't there a healthy amount of BAD SUGAR?" International guidance gives a population ceiling for free sugars because most people's normal intake sits far above it — around a tenth of daily energy, about fifty grams for many adults, with extra benefit seen below one twentieth. That is not a personal prescription for you. It is a simple institutional signal that normal is not healthy. The fact is your previous normal with BAD SUGAR lived well above any sensible ceiling, which is why you felt "fine" while slowly being loaded. You do not need to count grams to be free. You need to see the category and leave it.

One more fact, because it removes fear. Your body makes its own glucose. It manufactures it from protein and other building blocks whether you import BAD SUGAR or not. This is not theory. It is ordinary physiology. Stopping BAD SUGAR does not deprive your body of an essential nutrient. It returns your body to the baseline it knows how to run. I am not prescribing a diet. I am not telling you to eat ketogenically or to chase any threshold. I am telling you that freedom from BAD SUGAR is not nutritional deprivation. Isn't that a relief to know?

And one more, because it makes the "doing TO you" ledger concrete. Free sugars drive plaque acid. Plaque acid drives decay. That is the mechanism health bodies point to when they link free sugars to cavities and to the metabolic load that follows when sweetness is stripped from its natural package. Brushing and fluoride mediate the harm — they do not erase the mechanism. I tell you this not to frighten you. I tell you so you can see BAD SUGAR is doing TO you what it pretends to do FOR you. A genuine pleasure does not rot the teeth that taste it, does it?

Good. The line is drawn. Now you need something positive to walk toward.

### THE RAW TEST — WOULD YOU EAT IT RAW?

The Raw Test answers the shelf.

Pick up the food in your hand. Ask: would a person, if this food had not been engineered to be hyper-palatable, eat its core ingredient raw, or with nothing done to it, and enjoy it?

An apple? Yes. A carrot? Yes. Plain yoghurt? Yes. A handful of nuts? Yes. Fresh berries? Yes.

White bread that is mostly air and sugar? No. A biscuit that is flour, fat and sugar designed to melt without chewing? No. Milk chocolate that needs sugar and fat to hide the bitterness? No. A pasty that is white pastry wrapped around salt and fat? No. A fizzy drink that is sugar plus bubbles plus caffeine plus colour? No.

The fact is lonely carbs invite company to make themselves palatable. They gatecrash your senses with butter, chocolate, cream and salt because without the company they would be tasteless.

The Raw Test exposes the gatecrash in two seconds. You do not need a degree. You need honesty. If it only becomes delicious when sugar and fat and salt are invited over, it is BAD SUGAR sneaking a ride. Leave it on the shelf and feel marvellous.

"But what about bread I love?" Ask the test. Would you eat a spoonful of white flour raw and smile? You would not. You love what the butter and sugar and salt do on top of it. The love was for the company, not the carb.

Short sentence. Clear verdict. BAD SUGAR needs disguise. Real food does not.

Use the Raw Test as a game, not a grim duty. Watch how many packages fail it. Do you see how quickly the supermarket reveals itself as a factory aisle, not a food aisle? That is not deprivation talking. That is clarity.

### THE 0–20 GAUGE — ARE YOU HUNGRY OR IS THE GREMLIN WHISPERING?

The 0–20 Gauge answers the body.

It is a simple dial you read before you eat.

0 is truly empty — shaky, gnawing, keen for anything, even plain food. 10 is neutral — neither hungry nor full. 20 is stuffed. Most of us live between 7 and 14 and have forgotten what those numbers feel like because BAD SUGAR has been driving the numbers falsely up and down.

Here is how to use it.

Pause. Score yourself 0–20. Ask: would I eat plain primary food now with appetite? Plain apple, plain carrot sticks, plain yoghurt — would that genuinely please me?

If yes and your score is below 10, you are hungry. Eat. Enjoy primary food until you are satisfied.

If no — if only BAD SUGAR would do, if only a hit would do — that is not hunger. That is the Sugar Gremlin whispering. I want you to hear it correctly. It is not a command. It is a faint, empty, slightly restless, slightly off feeling, almost imperceptible, already dying because you stopped feeding it. The Sweet Brainwashing translates that faint feeling as I need something right now. The gauge translates it correctly as not-hungry, just echo.

Do you remember how we proved the translation is false? When you are absorbed in something lovely, does that feeling shout? No. When you stare at the wrapper, does it roar? Yes. The body did not get louder. The story did.

The gauge is not a prescription. It is a listening instrument. It does not tell you to discipline yourself. It tells you to notice whether you want food or a dose. If you are not hungry enough for primary food, you are not hungry. Wait. Drink water. Get on with enjoying your life. The echo passes within minutes. It has nowhere to feed.

Do you feel the kindness in that? You never have to resist hunger. Real hunger is wonderful and you meet it with real food, as much as you want until you are satisfied. You only stop feeding a whisper that pretended to be hunger.

### PRIMARY FOODS, SECONDARY FOODS, AND WHAT STAYS ON YOUR PLATE

The third instrument sorts your kitchen without counting.

Primary foods are what Nature's Guide recognises instantly: whole fruit, vegetables, legumes, plain dairy, nuts, seeds, whole grains in their intact form, fish, meat, eggs — food that passes the Raw Test or needs only simple cooking to be wonderful without needing sugar to be edible.

Secondary foods are useful, honest foods that may be lightly processed but are not BAD SUGAR: plain bread made from whole grain that you could imagine eating with nothing on it and still enjoying, plain cooking staples, tinned beans in water, simple sauces made without added free sugars.

BAD SUGAR is the third pile: everything that fails the first two — refined sugar, added free sugars, processed junk carbs, sweets, chocolate, biscuits, pastries, most packaged snacks, sugary drinks, white flour/bread/pasta/rice that behave like sugar in your body.

Do you see the abundance that remains? This is not a shrinking plate. This is a returning plate.

For years BAD SUGAR crowded primary food out, then told you primary food was boring. It was the other way around. BAD SUGAR dulled your taste. Primary food was wonderful all along. When you live without BAD SUGAR for a short while, an apple tastes sweeter than any sweet ever did — because your senses recover the sweetness that was drowned out.

I am not prescribing a diet. I am handing you a compass. You do not need thresholds. You follow Nature's Guide lightly and let your body find its weight, its energy, its teeth, its mood without you steering obsessively. Does that feel easier than the diets that told you to count, weigh and fear?

We change nothing else in your life except the one con. Same table. Same friends. Same celebrations. Different pile you push away with a smile.

### THE SEATBELT — WHY A BLIP IS NOT A CRASH

Now the worry that keeps honest people trapped longer than any craving: "What if I slip? What if a sauce has sugar? What if I take a bite at a party without thinking?"

Let me give you the seatbelt.

Picture a seatbelt. It is there for an accident, not for erratic driving. You do not put it on so you can swerve. You put it on so that if an accidental jolt happens, you do not crash.

Your body can cope with an occasional blip, but your mind cannot.

If you eat BAD SUGAR accidentally — a spoonful of sauce, a bite at a meal you did not know contained it — that blip revives nothing physical by itself. The physical Gremlin is small and still fading. One accidental exposure does not re-addict your body.

What can revive the trap is what you do with your mind after the blip. If you let the Sweet Brainwashing back in and reinterpret the blip as permission — "I got away with it, so I can do it again," "I've blown it now," "just one won't hurt" — then you feed the Gremlin deliberately and the brainwashing tightens again.

Do you see the release? Guard the belief, not the behaviour with anxiety.

If a blip happens, do not deliberately repeat it. Notice it. Correct the label. Say, "That was BAD SUGAR I did not intend, my body will handle the tiny echo, my mind stays free." Then get on with enjoying your life. No shame. No drama. No willpower. The seatbelt absorbed the jolt.

"Does the seatbelt mean I can keep a special occasion exception?" No. That is steering erratically and expecting the belt to save you. A kept exception is not an accident. It is a preserved belief that BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure or treat worth saving. Keep that belief and the trap stays alive.

The margin-for-error doctrine is kind but firm, exactly as a guide should be. Accidents do not make you trapped again. Deliberate repeats, licensed by the Sweet Brainwashing, do. So we forgive a blip, we learn, and we stay free. We never license a repeat.

Do you feel how that removes fear without removing clarity? You do not need to live as a detective. You live as a free person who prefers primary food, uses the Raw Test and the gauge lightly, and knows the seatbelt is there but has no intention to crash.
