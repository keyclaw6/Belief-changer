### LOCATE THE GNAWING NOW

Shall we do something together, right now, before we argue at all?

Do not change anything about how you feel. Do not try to be calm. Just notice.

Where is the craving when you think about BAD SUGAR?

Is it a storm in your head? Is it an iron willpower test? Is it a deep ache that proves you need a dose?

Or can you, if you sit for five seconds and locate it honestly, find something much smaller?

Close the book for a moment and ask your body, not your story about your body: what do you actually feel?

I will help you look, because we have lived in the Sugar Trap so long we add drama to a whisper.

We were taught to expect a monster. We were told craving is overwhelming, that without BAD SUGAR you will not function, that the need will grip you and not let go. Does that match what is actually happening inside you now, as you read?

Search gently.

Is there a slight restlessness? A faint emptiness? A little edginess behind the breastbone, a light insecurity, as if something is mildly off and not quite settled?

Do you feel it? That is all. Just that.

If you cannot find even that, wonderful. If you can, notice how quiet it is. Could that quiet sensation really be the terrible force you have spent years fearing? Could you have mistaken a whimper for a roar?

You have. We all have.

And I want you to see what you have just done. You located the entire physical part of the Sugar Trap. In seconds. Without instruments. Without willpower.

Is that the mysterious power that kept you hooked for the rest of your life?

No. That tiny feeling never kept anyone hooked. It is too mild. Something else did.

### TWO CREATURES, NOT ONE

The reason craving has felt mysterious, powerful, willpower-requiring is that we have fused two utterly different things into one dreaded word.

Let me split them for you, cleanly, so you never fuse them again.

Inside the Sugar Trap there are two creatures. Only two. And they are not equal.

The first is physical. I call it **the Squeak**.

The Squeak is the trivial, mild physical gnawing that BAD SUGAR leaves behind as it leaves the body. It is the faint emptiness you just located. It complains a little when unfed. That is all it can do. It cannot argue. It cannot reason. It cannot shout. It squeaks.

The second is mental. I call it **the Con**.

The Con is the belief-system that feeds the Squeak. It is the lifelong brainwashing that tells you the Squeak means you need BAD SUGAR, that the dose will give you pleasure, comfort, energy, reward, that you cannot be yourself without it. The Con interprets the whimper as an order and hands you the wrapper.

Do you see the trick? The Squeak is tiny. The Con is huge. The Squeak creates almost no suffering. The Con creates all the wanting.

We fell into the Sugar Trap together — we did — copying family, friends, advertisements, the bright shelf that says sweet means love, sweet means energy. We did not decide to believe it. We absorbed it. And once the Con is installed, it spends years feeding the Squeak and translating its dying whisper as "you need sugar."

Is it the Squeak that terrifies you? Or is it the Con speaking in your own voice?

Listen now to how you hear yourself before a dose. You do not hear a stomach growl. You hear a sentence.

"This will pick me up."

"I deserve it."

"Just a treat — once a week is fine."

"Once I start I can't stop, so I might as well start."

Have you heard those lines? Whose voice are they?

Not your stomach's. The Con's.

The fact is, if craving were only physical, you would have escaped long ago. A mild emptiness does not keep a grown adult in prison. A lie does.

I once believed the craving was one giant wall. When I saw it was two — a tiny dying creature and a loud liar feeding it — what happened to the need for willpower?

It vanished. There is nothing to wrestle. You starve one by doing nothing, and you stop believing the other. Both happen easily, immediately and permanently once seen.

Shall we prove how trivial the physical part truly is?

### HOW MILD IS MILD — THE TRUTH ABOUT THE PHYSICAL PART

I know this is hard to accept. You have felt urges that seemed enormous. You have felt the one-bite-becomes-the-box pull. You have felt "I need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW."

So let me give you the truth straight, with no hedging and no scare, because evidence honesty outranks force and you deserve plain scope.

In careful laboratory work where animals are given BAD SUGAR in an on-and-off schedule — sometimes available, sometimes not — something consistent happens. They binge when it returns. They show restlessness when it is removed. Each binge lights up the reward circuit briefly, then leaves a small, measurable dip afterwards — a low dopamine moment with a rise in the opposing signal, a real after-dip you can record. The loop also runs on the body's own opioid and cue systems, which is why a wrapper or a smell can make the mouth get ready before any dose.

The lab's own summary matters. They call the whole picture mild but well-defined. Mild in size. Well-defined in shape. Bingeing, craving, a dip, cue-reactivity — real — but mild.

Do you hear why I stress mild? The magnitude is far smaller than with drugs of abuse. This is not a disease label. The human picture is not a proven medical withdrawal syndrome. The shape translates cautiously from animal work, but the live pattern echoes what you know.

Why do I tell you this? To frighten you? No.

I tell you to shrink the monster you feared. The physical Squeak exists. It is well-defined. And it is mild.

Feel the reassurance in that. The thing you feared as overwhelming is, by the most careful measurement we have, small.

And now the well-defined part. Does mild mean imaginary? No. The dip after a dose is real but small. The binge pattern under intermittent access is real. The cue firing before the bite is real. You are not imagining your experience. You are misreading its size and its meaning.

Think. Have you ever had a cold that made you sneeze? The sneeze is real. Is it powerful? No. Do you need willpower to survive a cold?

The Squeak is smaller than a sneeze. It is a mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly restless gnawing.

Do you feel the weight lift? If the physical part is that mild, what kept you going back?

Not your body. The Con.

Have you not already proved this in your own life without noticing? Have you slept through an entire night without BAD SUGAR and woken craving-free? Have you been ill, or busy, or absorbed in a film, and gone hours longer than usual without thinking of sugar? Did your body collapse? Did willpower keep you alive?

No. The Squeak barely registered while you were not listening to the Con.

Is that mysterious, powerful, willpower-requiring?

No. It is mild. But well-defined. And once you name it correctly, you stop feeding it by accident.

### THE STARVING PARASITE — WHY YOU REJOICE WHILE IT DIES

Let me give you a picture that does the work, so you never again mis-see this feeling as a command.

Imagine a tiny, whimpering parasite. Not a lion. Not a demon. A small, pale creature that lives in your gut on BAD SUGAR and nothing else. When you feed it, it goes quiet for a little while. When you do not feed it, it squeaks.

That squeak is all it can do. It cannot pull your hand. It cannot open the cupboard. It gnaws a little, insecurely, restlessly, emptily. Can you hear it? A mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly restless gnawing.

Now, what happens if you simply do not feed it?

You would think it grows. That is what the Con has told you. "Feed me or I will get worse. Feed me or you will suffer for the rest of your life."

The fact is, the opposite happens. Starved, it dies. Quickly. In days.

You do not need to fight it. You do not need to resist it. You do not need to be strong. You starve it by doing nothing. By living your life. By letting the next hour pass.

And here is the inversion that makes you free. Its gnawing when you do not feed it is not its strength. It is its death rattle. Every little whimper is proof it is dying. Every faint empty moment is the Squeak shrinking.

Do you see why I tell you to rejoice at the very feeling you were taught to fear?

When you feel a mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly restless gnawing in the next days without BAD SUGAR, what does it mean?

Does it mean you need sugar?

Or does it mean the Squeak is dying and you are winning?

Ask yourself honestly. If a parasite whimpers only while starving, would you feed it to make it stronger? Would you feel deprived watching it die?

No. You would feel relief. You would feel, at last, that quiet is returning.

The fact is, there is a time-limited hump. The physical Squeak — the withdrawal, if you want to call it that — peaks in two to five days then passes. This is not theory sold as promise. Human measurements of this food-craving withdrawal found exactly that hump — small-scale, preliminary, retrospective, but consistent with the animal shape. Days, not life. A hump, not a sentence.

Does that scare you? Two to five days of a mild gnawing? Have you endured far worse for far less?

And note the limit honestly, because I will not sell you a guarantee that buys you disappointment. Not everyone feels the same hump. Some feel almost nothing. Some feel the whimper a little louder. But the direction is the same. It passes.

Can a mild, dying whimper that passes in days be the reason you stayed hooked for years?

No. The years were the Con keeping the Squeak alive with beliefs.

So I give you the posture that makes willpower irrelevant.

Do not wrestle the Squeak. Do not feed it. When you feel that mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly restless gnawing, do not say, "I must resist." Say, "Ah. There you are. You are dying. Wonderful."

Is that hard? To do nothing and rejoice?

It is the easiest thing in the world once you see it clearly.

### THE CON THAT FEEDS THE SQUEAK — WHO INSTALLED THE LIE

If the Squeak is so mild, why did it dominate your life?

Because the Con fed it.

The Con is the big lie. Not a feeling. A belief-system. The brainwashing that tells you BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure or crutch, that it helps with energy, taste, reward, sociability, stress, that without it life is less sweet.

Who installed it?

Not you, at the start. We did not choose it. We were conned.

Who told you sweet means love? Who handed you a lolly for being good? Who packed your lunchbox bright with wrappers? Who taught you that cinema needs chocolate, that birthdays need cake, that "I deserve it" ends with a spoon in a jar?

Family, with kindness. Friends, with habit. Advertising, with bliss-point labs and wrappers engineered to fire your cues before you taste anything. Everyone does it — that most hypnotic sentence of all.

Do you blame yourself for believing what everyone and everything told you was true? Of course not. You were not foolish. You were conned on phoney information. That is what a con does. The fraud sells you a coin that drains your wallet and then sells you the refill as help.

Repeat that to feel it.

The Con sold you BAD SUGAR as help for the low BAD SUGAR itself created.

Does that feel like your choice? Or like a trick seen through?

The Con does not need to shout to feed the Squeak. It only needs to re-label a neutral moment.

You feel a little empty — the Squeak's tiny gnawing — and the Con says, "You have low blood sugar."

You feel a 3pm dip — the insulin surge doing its job after a sweet lunch plus the dip after the dose — and the Con says, "You need sugar to function."

You feel a wrapping crinkle — a cue your body learned — and the Con says, "You want it. You love it. One won't hurt."

Have you heard that translation? Have you felt the cue fire before the bite and called it desire?

It was never desire for BAD SUGAR. It was the Con interpreting a learned cue.

And the Con has a clever cruelty. It borrows your finest moments and takes credit. It was there at the cinema, at the party, at the sofa after a hard day, whispering, "This is sweet because of sugar." Was it? Or was it sweet because you rested, were loved, laughed, belonged?

It was you that was sweet. Not the sugar.

Do you see the split now? The Squeak squeaks. The Con speaks. One is trivial and dying. One is loud and lying. Neither requires willpower to deal with.

What requires willpower to avoid watching a film that does not interest you?

Nothing.

That is what it is like when the Con dies. You do not have to battle thoughts about BAD SUGAR. You simply stop believing the lie that gave them weight, and the thoughts become like a pink elephant instruction you no longer follow — you see the elephant, smile, and think something true instead.

Was the Squeak hard? No. Mild but well-defined.

Was the Con hard? Only until you saw it. Now that you see it, what is left to fight?

Nothing. Just a small, starving creature and a big, discredited story. That is the whole of craving demystified.

### THE DIP, THE HUMP, AND THE CUE — WHAT REALLY HAPPENS AFTER A DOSE

Let me put the mild science plainly in your body, so your own experience confirms it and you are not asked to believe blindly.

First, the dip.

Each dose of BAD SUGAR releases a brief splash in the reward circuit, re-triggering that "have more" feeling. Then there is a small, real dip after — low dopamine for a little while with a rise in the opposing transmitter. You have felt this as the flat letdown after something sweet, the restlessness that seems to call for another dose.

Is that dip terrible? No. It is small. Smaller than the dip drugs of abuse produce. It is a real measured dip, not a metaphor, but mild.

Why does that matter? Because it explains why relief feels like pleasure. You are not lifted above normal. You are returned briefly toward normal from a little low, then guaranteed to dip again. The boost comes from the low. Do you feel the back-to-front error?

Ask: if the dip is small, why did the relief feel big?

Because the Con amplified the small dip into a big story. "You are crashing. You need fuel. Without sugar you cannot function." Did your body say that? Or did the belief?

Think of the fluorescent corridor at 3:47pm. Do you know it? The hum, the coins, the "rescue" that guarantees tomorrow's 3:47pm crash.

What actually happened there? You took a quick sweet lift. For twenty minutes you felt less flat. Then you felt flatter than before. Did sugar fix afternoon energy? Or did it borrow tomorrow's energy and charge you interest?

You know the answer. The lift–crash you described yourself is self-created. The very phrase recovered writers use — "I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop" — is the honest report. The lift IS insulin working. The same surge can produce a real after-dip. But everyday slumps are not clinically low glucose. Post-meal reactive lows can happen, but in healthy people they are rare and symptoms often appear without measured low sugar. That limit matters, because I do not want you diagnosing yourself with a disease you do not have. You have a trap, not a pathology.

The fact is, BAD SUGAR did not give you energy. It created the dip it pretended to solve.

Second, the hump.

If you stop feeding the Squeak today, what happens physically over days?

Human tracking of this exact withdrawal found the same shape as the animal work: symptoms, where they appear, peak in two to five days then pass. Not weeks. Not months. Not for the rest of your life. Days.

And again I hold the limit honestly. The human evidence is preliminary, small-scale, retrospective. It is not a drug-withdrawal syndrome. It is a mild, passing hump.

Is that frightening? Or reassuring?

Reassuring — because you expected a life sentence and you are being told the physical part complains for days, mildly, then stops.

Have you had days with headaches, or colds, or a poor night's sleep? Were they pleasant? No. Were they impossible? No. Did they require willpower to survive? No. You simply got on with enjoying your life around them.

That is the hump. Not a battle. A passing mild disturbance to live through while the Squeak dies.

Third, the cue.

Why can a wrapper, a smell, a petrol station aisle make you feel a sudden whimper even when you are not hungry?

Because the loop runs on your own opioid and cue systems too — a neural analogue for lingering, cue-driven wanting. The whimper can be triggered by sight and context alone, before any sugar touches your tongue.

Does that mean cues compel you?

No. They prompt. They fire. They do not force your hand. Lifting weights makes you stronger, but it does not make you punch people. Cue-reactivity makes a cue fire before the bite, but it does not compel the bite. You remain the one who decides what a firing means.

And what will you now decide it means?

When a wrapper crinkles and the Squeak whimpers, will you hear "I need it" from the Con? Or will you hear "There is the cue firing. The Squeak just squeaked. It is dying"?

One label re-powers the trap. The other starves it.

Which will you choose?

### TIGHT SHOES AGAIN — REMEMBERING WHAT RELIEF REALLY IS

Do you remember the tight shoes?

We have worn them together since Chapter 4. Do you recall?

We put on shoes two sizes too small and wore them all day, because we were told tight shoes give pleasure. At night we took them off. Ah. What relief. What pleasure.

Was that pleasure supplied by the shoes?

Or was it the ending of a pain we did not need to create?

We laughed then. We saw it for energy. Do you see it now for craving itself?

BAD SUGAR is the tight shoe you were told not to take off. The Squeak is the pinch. The Con is the voice that says, "Tight shoes support you. Tight shoes help you walk."

Does that voice deserve your belief?

For years you took a dose and felt a little relief from the mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly restless gnawing the previous dose created. You called that relief pleasure. You called that relief a crutch. You thought the relief proved the shoe helped.

It proved only that the shoe hurt.

Ask the three questions that make the Con concede.

If BAD SUGAR truly relieved a natural emptiness, why did you never feel that exact gnawing before you began taking BAD SUGAR regularly?

If the relief were genuine pleasure, why does it not lift you above how a child feels after running in a field, free and bright, with no dose?

And if the gnawing meant you needed sugar, why does it die in days when you stop feeding it, instead of growing forever?

It was never a need. It was a pinch. A mild, well-defined pinch that the Con sold you as need.

Feel the contempt for the trick. Warmly to yourself. Harshly to the trap. You were not weak. You were outgunned by a con that kept tightening the shoe and selling you the removal.

What happens now when you feel the pinch?

Do you quickly lace the shoe again? Or do you rejoice that you finally took it off?

You take it off. Easily, immediately and permanently. You feel the air on your foot and realize the foot was fine. The ache was the shoe. The acheIS the proof to remove the shoe, not to keep wearing it.

That is the whole demystification.

There is no mysterious, powerful craving that needs willpower.

There is the Squeak — trivial, mild but well-defined, whimpering as it dies, felt as **A MILD, EMPTY, SLIGHTLY INSECURE, SLIGHTLY RESTLESS GNAWING**.

And there is the Con — the big, lifelong brainwashing that fed it, interpreted it, and pretended it was "just a treat" or "I need sugar to function."

Kill the Con by seeing it, and the Squeak starves on its own. No discipline. No white-knuckle vigilance. No battle. Just understanding that lets desire collapse.

Do you feel that click? Intimidated, then "is that all it is?" then quiet contempt for the trick?

Hold it. That is the escape.

In the next chapter we will foreclose the last bargaining — why cutting down keeps the trap alive, and why an accidental blip cannot re-create a living parasite you have already starved unless you let the Con back in. But for now, fix this split and rejoice.

Whenever you feel that faint gnawing, think: the Squeak is dying. Whenever you hear a sentence that says "just one will help," think: the Con is speaking. One dies if you do nothing. The other dies if you stop believing it.

Do you need willpower for either?

No. You need sight. And you have it.
