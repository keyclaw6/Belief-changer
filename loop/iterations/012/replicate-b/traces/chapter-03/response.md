### THE SCOREBOARD THAT KEEPS YOU TRAPPED

Have you ever tried to bargain with BAD SUGAR?

Most of us have. We stand at the kitchen counter with a quiet calculator in our heads.

On one side: harm. On the other side: benefit.

We say, "I know it isn't great for me. But I like it. It helps. It makes life sweeter. So maybe the harm and the benefit balance out."

Do you recognise that calculator? Have you run those sums?

It feels sensible. It feels honest. It feels like the adult way to think.

It is also the exact scoreboard the Sugar Trap needs you to use.

Why? Because the harm-versus-benefit scoreboard lets the trap win even when it loses. If the harm looks big, you feel guilty but you still do it — because you believe the benefit is real. If the harm looks small, you feel reassured and you do it anyway. Either way you do it. The scoreboard never asks the only question that matters.

I want to show you that scoreboard for what it is. Not with shame. Never that. We were taught this scoreboard together. We all were.

But I will not debate harm versus benefit with you. That debate is a trap inside the Sugar Trap.

Think of it this way. If a friend said, "This coin makes me feel better," would you argue about whether the coin makes him a little ill? Or would you ask a simpler question — does the coin actually do anything good at all?

We have been asking the wrong question.

Is BAD SUGAR more harmful or more helpful? That question concedes that it helps.

What if it does not?

What if there is no benefit to weigh?

Then the whole scoreboard collapses. And with it, the need to bargain.

Do you see the shift? From "is the harm worth the benefit?" to "what benefit?"

That is the move we make in this chapter. One move. Complete. We will not yet demolish each claimed benefit — energy, taste, reward, comfort — those come next, one by one. Here we change the axis. We put a new test on the table and we ask the Sugar Trap to meet it.

Before I do, I want to be clear about something physical. Your body makes its own glucose from protein and glycerol. You are not importing something essential when you eat BAD SUGAR. That matters. It means stepping out of BAD SUGAR is not deprivation. Your body is not deprived of an essential nutrient. It has its own supply. Is this a prescription to eat no carbohydrates at all? No. Not at all. Whole fruit, vegetables, intact whole foods — they remain. They are not the target. The target is BAD SUGAR — the refined, added, processed dose. The fact that your body manufactures glucose simply removes the scare that you need BAD SUGAR to function. You do not. That is a fact. It is not a diet.

And another backdrop. Public health bodies set a ceiling for free sugars at less than ten percent of daily energy — about fifty grams — and note additional benefit below five percent. Those are population guide-rails. Why do they exist? Because current consumption sits far above both. I mention that only as context. This is not a personal prescription. I am not dosing you. I am saying the world you live in made BAD SUGAR feel normal while eating far past its own sensible ceiling.

So the question stands. Not "is normal harmful?" Normal is engineered. The question is, "what good is there at all?"

Do you feel the difference? One question keeps you bargaining. The other asks the trap to prove itself.

Shall we make it prove itself?

### YOUR TWO-COLUMN TEST

I want you to do a small exercise with me. Not as homework. As an honest look.

Take a sheet of paper. Draw a line down the middle. Two columns.

On the left, write FOR. On the right, write TO.

Wait. Which is which?

Left: What is BAD SUGAR doing FOR you? What genuine good does it add that nothing else would?

Right: What is BAD SUGAR doing TO you? What does it cost, take, or do to your body, your mood, your money, your time, your peace?

We have lived inside this trap so long that we write the two columns back to front. We fill the harm column easily — but vaguely. And we fill the benefit column quickly — but with phrases that sound like benefits.

Try it now. Honestly. In your own words.

What do you write on the FOR side?

"I need it for energy." Does that go there?

"I deserve it." That?

"It tastes wonderful." That?

"It comforts me when I am low." That?

"It is sociable. Everyone has cake."

"It is just a treat."

Write them. Do not censor. Write the lines you have told yourself, in the exact words you use when no one is listening. "I need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW." "Sugary somethings to get me through." "I've been good this week." That is the material.

Now look at the TO side.

What has it cost you?

Evenings of bargaining? Money, every single day, forever, for something you did not plan to buy? The packet you meant to share that disappeared? The 3pm crash that asks for another fix? The fog after? The teeth you worry about? The clothes? The hiding?

Do not write what you think you *should* feel. Write what you *do* feel when you are honest for ten seconds.

Here is what happens every time we do this exercise together.

The TO column fills fast. Too fast. It is concrete. It is lived.

The FOR column fills fast too at first — but with borrowed phrases. Slogans. Adverts. Family lines. "A little treat." "Quick energy." Half-sentences we never tested.

Is that a fair count? Do those slogans deserve to sit opposite real costs?

We must test them. Not by debating harm. That is the old scoreboard.

We test them by asking the trap to justify a single genuine good. One. Not a feeling that could be something else. Not a word like treat. A real benefit that is truly *from* the BAD SUGAR.

If it cannot, then the TO column is not the price of a pleasure. It is the damage of a con that never paid you.

Do you see why this matters? If BAD SUGAR were *a genuine pleasure or crutch*, you would need to sacrifice to stop. If it is not, there is nothing to sacrifice. There is only escape.

So we need to define that phrase and then hold the trap to it.

### SWITCHING THE AXIS

The phrase *a genuine pleasure or crutch* is our trap-namer for the benefit we think we get. I use it exactly as written because it gathers every justification into one object.

A genuine pleasure: something that adds pleasure you would not have without it.

Or a genuine crutch: something that truly supports you, relieves pressure, gives you strength you would not have without it.

We have believed BAD SUGAR is one or the other. Sometimes both. The whole Sugar Trap rests on that belief.

The axis switch is this:

Stop asking: does the harm outweigh the good?

Start asking: what good is there at all? Prove one item in the FOR column is *a genuine pleasure or crutch* that stands up to a simple test.

What test?

The ledger test. We call it **doing TO you vs doing FOR you**.

It is not my phrase to soften. It is the ledger itself. Every claimed benefit must survive one question: is this BAD SUGAR doing something FOR you that life would not have given you anyway, or is it doing something TO you and then taking credit for the momentary relief?

We have been crediting BAD SUGAR for pleasures that belonged to the moment.

Have you ever noticed how BAD SUGAR turns up with other good things? Rest after work. A film with friends. A laugh. A pause. A breath. Warmth. Company. The very things you love.

Do you see the trick? BAD SUGAR arrives at the party, eats all the food, and tells you it *was* the party.

Think of a house party. Music. Friends. Laughter. The kitchen is warm. You are relieved the day is done. Someone brings a tray of iced buns and you eat while you laugh. Later you say, "The buns made the evening."

Did they? Or did the people, the rest, the relief, the laughter? If you replay the same evening with the same people and the same relief but no tray, does the warmth vanish? Does the laugh get quieter? Or does the evening stay warm and the buns just take the credit?

That is the teaser for what comes next. We will strip BAD SUGAR out of each cherished scene and watch the pleasure remain. We will isolate the variable. We will reassign the credit to its true source. But we will not do it piecemeal here. That is for the next chapters.

Here we only install the lens. The lens is **doing TO you vs doing FOR you**.

The fact is we fell into believing BAD SUGAR was doing something FOR us because it coincided with moments that were sweet for other reasons. We fell in together. We all did. The industry engineered products to coincide with those moments — bliss-point sweetness, supersized cues, wrappers designed to fire before the first bite — so the mis-credit felt natural.

Do you feel the axis move? From harm-talk to benefit-proof.

I know what your mind says now. It says, "But it DOES do something for me. I feel it."

Of course you feel something. Feeling something is not proof of a genuine benefit. The tight-shoe feeling of relief when you take off tight shoes is intense. It is real. But did the shoes do something FOR you?

No. They did something TO you. Then you felt relief when the pressure eased.

Is BAD SUGAR different? That is the only question.

We must not concede a real benefit before we test it. Not one. Not yet. Not ever until it proves itself. If we concede one untested "good reason" — taste, energy, comfort — that one cord keeps the whole trap powered.

So we ask, plainly, without harm talk:

What does BAD SUGAR do FOR you that is genuine?

Name it. In one sentence.

Is it pleasure? We will test taste properly. Is it energy? We will test energy properly. Is it reward? We will test reward properly. Each will get its own trial where we isolate the variable and see who gets the credit.

Until then, the ledger stays on the table.

And the Sugar Trap is invited to fill the FOR column with one real entry.

Can it?

Are you willing to demand proof before you pay the price again?

### WHAT GOOD IS THERE AT ALL?

Let us make the demand harder to dodge.

It is not that BAD SUGAR relieves comfort. It creates the restlessness it then relieves.

It is not that BAD SUGAR gives energy. It takes energy and sells the pause as fuel.

It is not that BAD SUGAR tastes marvellous on its own. It masks and sweetens what we could have enjoyed cleanly.

Do you hear the inversion? "It's not that X relieves Y. It's that X causes Y."

We will prove each inversion in turn. Here we only state the rule: a benefit that is merely the ending of a discomfort the behaviour itself caused is not *a genuine pleasure or crutch*. It is the tight shoes.

Would you wear tight shoes all day for the pleasure of taking them off at night? Would you call that pleasure genuine?

Would you thank the shoes?

You would not. You would see the whole cycle as self-inflicted. You never rise above the comfort of someone who never put the shoes on.

That is what we need to see with BAD SUGAR. Until we see it, we keep thanking the shoes.

So I ask you again, quietly:

What good is there at all?

Do you have an answer that survives the ledger?

Is there a single entry on the FOR side that is not borrowed language, not a slogan, not a feeling that could be reassigned to rest, company, relief, or habit?

Take your time. Be honest. Be kind to yourself. We are not accusing you. We are accusing the con that taught you to write "treat" where you should have written "trigger."

If the answer is "I don't know yet," that is honest. Curiosity is enough. The defensive justification softens into curiosity the moment the ledger is on the table. That is the emotional turn this chapter is built to create — from "I must defend my treat" to "I wonder if there is anything in this column at all."

Do you feel it soften? Do you feel the question become interesting rather than threatening?

Good. Stay there. That curiosity is intelligence waking up.

The Sugar Trap told you the benefit was obvious. This ledger says: show me.

And the trap has no witness.

### THE EMPTY COLUMN AND THE QUESTION THAT FREES YOU

We need to land this. Not with a debate. With a verdict you can use.

You brought a scoreboard to this chapter. Harm versus benefit. That scoreboard treats benefit as granted and asks if harm outweighs it.

We replaced it with a ledger.

**doing TO you vs doing FOR you**

Read it as a command. Every time you think of BAD SUGAR, ask: is this doing FOR me or TO me?

Is this a genuine pleasure I would not have without BAD SUGAR? Or is it relief from a low BAD SUGAR created?

Is this a crutch that truly supports me? Or is it the hand that pushed me down offering to help me up?

We have not yet proved that every single FOR entry fails. We will. One chapter at a time. Properly. Fairly. With the variable isolated and the credit reassigned in plain view.

But the method of proof is decided here. And the burden is placed where it belongs — on the trap, not on you.

You have lived with BAD SUGAR as if it adds sweetness to your life. What if it never added? What if it only borrowed sweetness from moments that were sweet already and then made the next moment less sweet?

Is that possible? Could you have been crediting the dose for the company, the rest, the film, the laugh?

If that is possible, then the TO column is not a price. It is waste.

And the FOR column is not a benefit. It is a mislabel.

Do you see why we refuse the harm debate now?

Do you see why one empty column settles more than a hundred studies?

Do you see that the right question frees you from bargaining altogether?

Look at your paper again. Leave the TO column as it is. It is doing plenty TO you. Now look at the FOR column. Is there a single entry you are certain is *a genuine pleasure or crutch* — not a slogan, not relief from a low the dose itself caused, not pleasure that belonged to the moment?

If you cannot point to one that survives the ledger test, then the column is empty until proven otherwise.

Empty.

That is the verdict of this chapter. Not that BAD SUGAR harms — though it does plenty TO you — but that it does nothing FOR you that counts as *a genuine pleasure or crutch*.

Say it plainly. Try the words.

**IT IS DOING PLENTY TO YOU. IT IS DOING NOTHING FOR YOU.**

Do you feel the weight move? From "can I afford the harm?" to "why am I paying at all?"

From "I must give up something" to "there is nothing to give up."

That is not wordplay. It is the axis on which freedom turns.

We will test each FOR entry you wrote. Energy. Taste. Reward. Comfort. Sociability. Stress. Habit. Each will face the same ledger. Each will be asked: are you FOR me or did you just happen while I felt good? Each will be shown to be sneaking a ride.

But you do not need to pass those tests yet to benefit from this shift.

You can carry the ledger now.

Whenever you think of BAD SUGAR — the shop aisle, the advert, the desk drawer — run the ledger. Two words. TO or FOR.

TO is the trap using you.

FOR would be a genuine gift. Where is it?

Is it there? Can you name it without borrowing a slogan?

If not, you have your answer. Not as belief. As fact.

The fact is the Sugar Trap taught you to write benefit where there was only relief from a discomfort it caused. The fact is your body never needed BAD SUGAR to make glucose — it manufactures what it needs, and whole foods remain yours. The fact is the world eats far above its own ceilings and then calls that normal, so normal never proves choice.

Those facts do not demand sacrifice. They remove the idea of sacrifice.

Do you feel the curiosity hold? Will you keep the ledger up while we do the detailed proof?

You brought justifications to this chapter. You leave with a test.

**doing TO you vs doing FOR you**

Use it. Every time. It is the permanent question.

And when the next chapter says, "It gives me energy," you will know exactly where to put it. Not in debate. In the ledger.

Is that fair? Is that freedom thinking?

Of course it is. You have not lost a pleasure. You have gained a lens that shows there was no pleasure there to lose.
