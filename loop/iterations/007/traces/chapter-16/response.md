### "IS SUGAR ADDICTIVE OR NOT?" — THE HONEST SPLIT BOTH SIDES SHARE

> "But is sugar actually addictive? I've read that it's not proven. Maybe I'm not addicted at all."

Good question. Let's be precise.

The fact is, researchers still argue about the label. One camp points to animal work under a specific schedule — twelve hours of intermittent access to sugar — where rats binge, show a low after the binge, crave, and cross-react to other hits. They see dopamine rise in the reward centre on each binge and a dip afterwards. They call that a mild but well-defined dependency.

The other camp says, hold on. In those same labs, rats given easy, all-day access do not show that pattern. They argue it's the restrict-then-binge rhythm that drives the behaviour, not sugar itself at everyday human doses. They remind us that a dopamine flicker is not the same as pleasure — wanting is not liking — and that human proof for a full addiction diagnosis at normal eating is still thin.

Who is right? The argument is open.

The fact is, both sides grant the same behaviour you know in your own kitchen: when access is intermittent — diet, restrict, then binge — the bingeing appears. The schedule drives it.

Do you need the addiction label to be proven to see your trap? Do you need a diagnosis to know you binge after you restrict?

You don't. Have you ever told yourself "just a treat" and then found you needed another? Have you ever felt wrappers and smells fire the wanting before you were hungry?

That is the trap in action. The behaviour-level trap does not need a consensus on the word addiction to be real. The diet-binge yo-yo powers it. That is why cutting down keeps you trapped and why **the Sweet Con** interprets the whisper as "I need this."

So I will not tell you "sugar is a drug exactly like heroin." It is not. The size is much smaller. And I will not tell you "it's not real so you can moderate." You cannot. Why? Because one surviving belief — "it does something FOR me" — re-powers the whole circuit.

Is that clear? The science stays honest. The trap stays dead.

### "IS THE CRASH REAL OR JUST IN MY HEAD?" — KEEPING BOTH TRUTHS

The same surge then helps drive the dip you feel after. It is the other way around. BAD SUGAR did not give you energy. It created the high and the low in one movement.

Now the honest limit. That after-dip is real to feel. It is rarely a clinical low. Researchers measure everyday slumps and rarely find blood sugar at or below the medical threshold for hypoglycaemia. You can feel drained, foggy and flat without being medically low.

No. You felt the dip the hit created. You never beat the baseline of a person who never took the hit.
