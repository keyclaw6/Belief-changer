A few hours. What do you gain if it does? Freedom from the loop that has cost you money, energy, time and peace of mind for years. No downside. Only upside. That is not sales talk. You don't need a stronger tool. You need the truth about the job.

### THE CON YOU DIDN'T ASK TO JOIN

Let me show you why this trap is so convincing.

Imagine a well-dressed conman knocks on your door. He offers you an investment. "Pay me a little every day," he says, "and I will give you a boost. You will feel lifted. Steadier. More able to cope."

You pay. You feel a lift. You thank him.

What you don't see is that he stole that lift from you yesterday. He creates the low. He sells you the relief. He is the rescuer and the perpetrator. You are grateful to the hand that robbed you.

Does that make you foolish? No. It makes you conned. We were all conned. How were we conned? By family who meant well and passed on what they were told. By advertising that spent billions to make a dose look like love. By a world where BAD SUGAR is everywhere and not having it looks odd. By the Sugar Trap itself, which whispers, "You need me to get through." The fact is, you never needed it. You were taught to believe you did.

Do you remember touching a hot pan as a child? You didn't need a book to teach you not to keep touching it. The pain taught you. So ask yourself: if sugar really were a genuine pleasure you freely choose, why would you be reading a book on how to stop? Would you need a book to help you choose a hobby you enjoy? Would you need willpower to keep doing something that truly makes life better? Why would you hide it? Why would you feel shame after it? Why would you promise yourself "never again" on a Sunday night?

The only honest answer is this: you are not freely choosing. You are trapped. And being trapped is not shameful. It is common. It is human.

I want you to feel that for a moment. Not as defeat. As relief. If the trap is the problem, you are not the problem. Does that land? Are you willing to consider that your so-called lack of discipline is not a character flaw but a design feature of the con?

Hold that question. We will answer it together.

### IN HIS OWN WORDS — JON

Jon is 38. He works in sales. He came to this method after ten years of yo-yo dieting. He did not come as a believer. He came as a skeptic. This is his story, in his own words.

*"I was a classic grazer who turned into a night binger. Through the week I'd be 'good' — I tracked everything, I told myself 'just a treat on Friday.' Friday became Friday and Saturday. Saturday became the whole weekend. I was spending £60-£70 a week on biscuits, chocolate, ice cream, pastries — stuff I ate in the car so my partner wouldn't see. I weighed 94 kilos. Not huge, but I felt tired, foggy and drained every afternoon. That's the phrase that stuck — tired, foggy and drained. That's how I lived.*

*I'd done the Willpower Method — you know the one — apps, 30-day challenges, swapping to honey, swapping to 'healthy' bars that were just sugar in a green wrapper. Each time I lasted 8-12 days, then one bite became the box. Literally. One biscuit, then the packet. One row, then the bar. I told myself I lacked discipline.*

*I was sent this method by my sister. I rolled my eyes. I started reading with a big bag of sweets on the desk — deliberately — because I'd been told not to quit while reading. That threw me. 'Don't quit yet?' I kept eating exactly as I did. That removed the panic.*

*About a third of the way through, I had a weird moment. I ate a doughnut I'd been craving since 11am. I paid attention to it the way the book suggested. I noticed the cloy. The stick on my teeth. The instant need for water. The letdown ten minutes later. It wasn't even nice. And I thought: that's it? That's what I've been calling a treat?*

*I didn't have a perfect quit. I wobbled. On Day 3 after my final dose I had a terrible day — work stress, bad sleep — and I thought, 'Just one can't hurt.' I didn't have it. I remembered the instruction to think 'FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!' and I laughed. It felt silly. Then it felt true. I slept properly that week for the first time in years.*

*I'm now six months free. I weigh 82 kilos without dieting. I eat as much fresh food as I want. I enjoy food more. The weirdest thing is how little I think about it. I thought I'd be fighting cravings forever. I'm not fighting anything. The rollercoaster stopped. I finally got off."*

Read that again. Not as a miracle. As a pattern. Jon was not stronger than you. He was not different. He used a different method. And his wobble did not mean failure. It meant the old belief tried one last time to sell itself. He didn't buy it. You won't need to either.

Do you want that kind of freedom? Not perfect behaviour. Not a new diet identity. Just the quiet, steady feeling of not being driven any more? You can have it. And you can have it easily, immediately and permanently — not after months of earning it, but the moment you see the trap whole.

That phrase matters too. easily, immediately and permanently. Three words that sound outrageous together. Easy? Nothing about sugar has felt easy. Immediately? You've been told change takes time. Permanently? You've seen yourself go back. So how can I promise all three? Because easy is what happens when you lose the desire, not when you overpower it. Immediately is what happens when the decision is about understanding, not willpower. Permanently is what happens when there is nothing left to miss. We will prove all three. You don't have to take them on faith.

### THE ROLLERCOASTER YOU DIDN'T BUY A TICKET FOR

You know the ride.

You get a lift. A little buzz. A sense that things are steadier. Then — without you doing anything — the lift leaks. You feel a dip. A restlessness. A faint emptiness that says, "more." You feed it again. Lift. Dip. Lift. Dip. We call it the roller coaster because that is exactly what you told us it feels like. Up, down, up, down — with the queue for the next ride built into the last one.

We like to say we use sugar to get through. To get me through the afternoon. To get me through the meeting. To get me through the evening with the kids. But what if it's the other way around? What if the thing you use to get through is what made getting through feel so hard in the first place? Ask yourself honestly: did you need sugar to feel steady as a young child? Did you need a hit to enjoy a film, a birthday, a walk with a friend before the habit was installed? When did that need start — and who installed it?

The rollercoaster explains the exhaustion you call normal. tired, foggy and drained is not your personality. It is the state of a person riding that ride all day. Getting off does not deprive you of thrills. It ends the nausea.

Picture this for a second. Not a future where you are resisting. ### WHAT SUGAR MEANS — AND WHAT IT DOESN'T

Before those instructions, let's get brutally clear, so you never feel deprived.

> **WHEN THIS BOOK SAYS SUGAR, TAKE IT TO MEAN BAD SUGAR.**
>
> **BAD SUGAR = all refined and added free sugars — sucrose, glucose, high-fructose corn syrup, honey and syrups added — PLUS junk-processed carbs where fibre and structure have been stripped away: white bread, pastries, sweets, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, sugary drinks, confectionery, ultra-processed snacks.**
>
> **GOOD SUGAR / NOT-SUGAR = intrinsic sugars in whole fruit, vegetables and unrefined whole foods eaten as whole foods, and plain natural foods generally. They are not restricted. You are not quitting food. You are not quitting sweetness. You are not quitting eating.**

Read that box twice. Why does it matter? Because the fear says, "I'll lose food." You won't. You will eat as much of your favourite fresh foods as you want, whenever you want, without counting, without willpower and without feeling deprived. Your body knows how to handle real food. It is BAD SUGAR — the engineered, stripped, weaponised stuff — that never belonged there.

And what if you have diabetes, low blood sugar issues, an eating disorder, or a clinician-prescribed therapeutic diet? Then you follow your clinician's guidance, full stop. Nothing in this book overrides professional advice. This method is about belief, not medical prescription. If your clinician has set a plan, you stay on that plan. All instructions carry that exception.

### THREE INSTRUCTIONS — YOUR PART IN THIS

We are at the climax of this chapter. Everything else will build on what you agree here. I will state each instruction in its exact form, in capitals so you can find it again, and then give you the warm reason why it matters.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND. Question everything you think you know about sugar and let the evidence do its work — even when it sounds outrageous at first.**

Why? Because the hardest part is not learning something new. It is unlearning what you think you already know. If I told you the lift you feel is not a lift at all but the ending of a low that BAD SUGAR created, that will sound outrageous at first. Keep your mind open anyway. Let the next chapters test it. Question both sides — including me. Don't accept anything blindly. Just stay open long enough to let the evidence do its work. Can you do that?

**2. DO NOT TRY TO CUT DOWN OR QUIT WHILE YOU ARE READING THIS BOOK. Carry on eating exactly as you do now until you finish — unless your clinician has placed you on a prescribed therapeutic diet that you must continue to follow.**

Why? Two reasons — one practical, one psychological. Practically, you cannot prove the rollercoaster while you are stepping off it. We need you to see it working while you read, so each demolition lands on fresh, honest experience. Psychologically, this instruction removes fear. You are not being asked to give anything up yet. There is no sacrifice while you read. Carry on exactly as you do. Eat the biscuits. Drink the drink. Don't count. Don't be "good." Just read and notice. Does that lower the pressure? Good. That is the point. The change will happen because you want it to, not because you forced it.

Is there an exception? Yes — if your clinician has placed you on a therapeutic diet, you keep following it. This book never asks you to go against clinical guidance.

**3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF EXCITEMENT AND ELATION. You are about to escape — cast aside all feelings of doom and gloom.**

Why? Because you have been sold doom. You have been told quitting is hard, grim, a long grey corridor of resisting. It is not. It is an escape. Think of it like this: if you were about to walk out of a cage you have lived in for years, would you start by mourning the cage? No. You would feel excitement. Elation. Even if part of you is nervous, you can choose to lead with the exciting truth: this is the book where it stops. You are not starting a diet. You are starting your freedom. Can you allow yourself to feel that, even just a flicker?

Those three are your foundation. Keep an open mind. Don't quit yet. Begin elated.
