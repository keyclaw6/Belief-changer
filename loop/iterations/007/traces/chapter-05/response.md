### "I NEED SOMETHING QUICK AND SWEET"

"I need it to get through."

Do you hear how certain that sounds? How reasonable?

"To get me through the afternoon. I need something quick and sweet. I can't concentrate without it. The 3pm crash is real."

I hear you. We have all said it. We say it at the desk with the drawer opening. We say it in the car on the school run. We say it kindly to ourselves, as if we are being responsible — fuelling up.

And for the afternoon-crash rider, this is the load-bearing belief. Taste was a nice extra. This feels like function. You are not describing pleasure now. You are describing survival.

Is it true?

We were conned into the Sugar Trap, remember? We first reached for BAD SUGAR on phoney information, and we learned to read the brief quiet after the hit as "energy." Have you ever tested that reading against your own afternoon, hour by hour, with attention?

Let us do that now. Not with theory. With your day.

The fact is, your body already knows how to keep your blood glucose steady. We will look at that quietly, as settled fact, in a moment. First, let us map what you call energy.

When you say BAD SUGAR is a genuine treat or lift for energy, do you mean it lifts you above how a clear, well-fed person feels — or does it just lift you out of a hole you fell into an hour earlier?

Keep that question open. Your afternoon will answer it.

### YOUR AFTERNOON, MAPPED BEAT BY BEAT

Picture yesterday. Or today. A normal day, not a disaster.

Morning. You eat. Perhaps toast, cereal, juice, a pastry on the run. All BAD SUGAR, though you were told it was "energy food." You feel okay. Busy.

Late morning. A faint dip. Attention thins. You think, "I need something." You have a biscuit. A sweetened coffee. A bar from the drawer.

What happens?

For twenty minutes: a little lift. Sharper. Lighter. You think, "There, that's better."

Then what?

Heavier. Fog thicker. Eyelids heavier. Patience shorter. You feel tired, foggy and drained — not from work, but from inside. You tell yourself you didn't sleep well. You reach again. Another quick and sweet hit. Another brief lift. Another heavier fog.

By late afternoon you are irritable. By evening you are regretful. "Why did I eat like that again?" You promise tomorrow will be different.

Do you recognise that rhythm? Dose. Brief lift. Heavier fog. Next dose. Evening regret.

One woman put it plainly: "I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop." She is not diagnosing herself. She is describing the rollercoaster she used to call fuel.

Is that fuel? Does fuel make the engine heavier after it burns?

Ask the disarming question. If BAD SUGAR gives you energy, why are you more tired after it than before it? Why does the person who never took the hit not need your remedy?

Think of a child on a fresh-food day. Running, playing, eating an apple, a carrot, a handful of nuts, plain fish and veg when hungry, water when thirsty. Do they need a 3pm hit to concentrate on their game? Do they queue for a lift?

No. They have energy because nothing is dragging them down.

So what is dragging you down?

Look again at your map. Was the tiredness before the biscuit the same as the tiredness after the biscuit? Be precise. Before, it was a faint restlessness, a thin dip. After, it was a heavier drain, a fog that asked for more. Which one felt more like being tired, foggy and drained?

Do you see the pattern? The hit did not cure tiredness. It deepened it and renamed the deepening as proof you needed another hit.

We call that a genuine treat or lift. Is it? Or is it a loan at high interest, where you borrow a little spark and repay it with an hour of fog?

### IT'S THE OTHER WAY AROUND

The inversion is simple. And once seen, it cannot be unseen.

The fact is, the lift IS the surge. When you eat BAD SUGAR — refined sugar, white bread, pastries, sweets, sugary drinks — your blood glucose rises fast. Your body answers fast. It releases a surge of insulin to deal with the flood. That surge IS the brief lift you feel. Sharp. Bright. Quick.

And that same surge is what drives the dip.

It's the other way around. The dip is not proof you need more sugar. The dip is proof the last sugar is still acting on you.

Do you see? The rescuer is the perpetrator. The hand that lifts you is the hand that pushed you down. You thank the hit for relieving a low the previous hit created.

Is the after-dip a true medical low? No. The fact is, everyday 3pm slumps rarely measure as clinical hypoglycemia. Your blood sugar rarely drops to the low levels that doctors call hypoglycemia, and I am not diagnosing you. You do not need a diagnosis to feel the very real sag that follows the surge. The mechanism is real. The fog is real. The label is not needed. What matters is the direction: up fast, down heavier.

Ask yourself: did you need BAD SUGAR for energy as a child before you learned to use it? Did you need it on a day when you ate fresh food until satisfied and felt light and clear? Did you need it in the first hour after a satisfying meal of real food?

No. You needed it after the last hit of BAD SUGAR. That is the loop telling on itself.

Try the comparison test, in memory, not as a rule.

Recall a day you ate fresh foods freely when hungry — fruit, vegetables, nuts, eggs, fish, meat, beans, water — without counting, without BAD SUGAR. How did 3pm feel? Perhaps a gentle hunger, an honest signal that food would be satisfying. Not fog. Not drain. A clear body asking for fuel.

Now recall a day you grazed BAD SUGAR — cereal, juice, biscuits, bread, sweets, cola. How did 3pm feel? Restless. Flat. "I need something RIGHT NOW." Not hunger. A search.

Which day left you more capable? Which day made you reach again within the hour?

Do you notice? The two days had the same you, the same work, the same sleep. Only the pattern of hits changed. Which one is the cause of the energy difference?

Ask the one-answer question: if BAD SUGAR is fuel, why does the person who uses none of it not run out? Why do they finish the afternoon lighter than you do after your fuel?

Because it was never fuel. It was a flood.

And every flood needs a mop. Your insulin surge is the mop. You feel the mopping as a brief lift — "I'm back" — and then you feel the mopping as a dip — "I'm gone again." Same surge. Same story. Two feelings. One cause.

**IT'S THE OTHER WAY AROUND.**

The energy you credited to BAD SUGAR was relief from the tiredness BAD SUGAR was actively creating. The lift was the low leaving, briefly, before the next low arrived heavier. That is not energy. That is maintenance of a self-inflicted drain.

Feel that land. Short sentence. Clear verdict. No fog.

Do you still believe BAD SUGAR is a genuine treat or lift for energy?

### THE BODY YOU HAD BEFORE THE ROLLERCOASTER

Let me show you the quiet marvel underneath.

Your body already knows how to keep you steady. The fact is, you do not need to import sugar to have glucose in your blood. Your body manufactures glucose itself, calmly, from protein and from other parts of your food, as you need it. It has done this since you were born. It did it before you ever tasted BAD SUGAR. It will do it again the moment you stop flooding it.

This is not a diet prescription. I am not telling you what to eat. I am telling you how reassuring the truth is: you have a natural baseline that is not deprivation. Eliminating BAD SUGAR does not rob you of fuel. Your body IS fuel.

Think of that baseline as a wide, calm river. It moves steadily. It carries you. It does not spike and crash. Fresh food eaten as whole food — fruit, veg, nuts, beans, fish, meat, eggs — joins that river gently. Fibre, structure, chewing, digestion slow the entry. The river rises slightly, usefully, and returns. You feel satisfied. Light. Energetic. Not wired, not drained.

Now think of BAD SUGAR. Refined. Stripped. Engineered to dissolve fast. No fibre. No structure. It is not a river. It is a bucket thrown over the river all at once. A crude flood.

What does a flood do? It surges. It batters the banks. It forces a rapid response. Then it drains. Then you want another bucket because the banks feel empty again.

Do you see why the flood never beats the river? The river carries you all day. The bucket only makes you wetter, then drier, then desperate for another bucket.

This is the awe part. Quiet, almost reverent. Your tongue has thousands of taste buds that read sweetness in fruit as information, not as assault. Your gut has signals that say "enough" when real food lands. Your liver and muscles store and release steady glucose without your instruction. All of it sophisticated. All of it automatic. All of it designed to keep you comfortably between hungry and satisfied without a single hit of BAD SUGAR.

BAD SUGAR overrides that sophistication with a shout. The shout feels like energy for minutes. Then you pay.

Nine out of ten grazers I speak to say the same, in different words: "I thought it kept me going. Then I saw it kept me going back." Is that energy? Or is it engineering?

You did not need BAD SUGAR to concentrate as a child. You do not need it to concentrate when the river is calm. You need it only to relieve the drain the last flood left behind.

Hold that awe. It matters. When awe is present, the flood feels wrong — vandalism on a fine system — not temptation.

We were trapped, not defective. We mistook the mop for the power source.

### GETTING OFF IS NOT LOSING THE THRILL — THE ROLLERCOASTER

So let us name the ride you have been strapped to.

You call it "the roller coaster." You are right. That is exactly what it is.

You queue. You climb — the hit, the surge, the brief bright lift. You think, "Here we go, energy." Then you plunge — the dip, the fog, the restless search, tired, foggy and drained. Then you queue again. Dose. Lift. Crash. Queue. All day. Every day. For the rest of your life — if you stay on.

And what do you call the climb? A treat. A lift. A genuine treat or lift.

Is the climb thrilling? Or is it nausea you have learned to call thrill?

Step off with me, in imagination, for one minute. Not as sacrifice. As escape.

Picture stepping off the rollercoaster onto solid ground. The ground does not thrill. It steadies. Your stomach settles. The world stops tilting. You breathe. You look around and realise the thrill you thought you would miss was just the relief of stopping the nausea you were paying to create.

Do you feel the shift? From fear of missing energy to relief at not being drained?

The trap teaches you to measure energy by the height of the lift after the dip. That is a false measure. A true measure is: how steady, clear and capable do you feel between meals when no flood has hit for hours? How lightly do you move? How clean is your mouth? How calm is your mind?

BAD SUGAR never improves that measure. It borrows from it.

And the borrowing has a deferred cost far beyond 3pm. The fact is, a steady diet of high added sugar loads your liver, nudges blood pressure, stirs inflammation, and bypasses appetite signals that would otherwise tell you to stop. This is not about one biscuit and a heart attack. One hit does not give you heart disease. It is about the daily load, quietly, for years — the flood as habit, not as event. The lift you feel today is the load you carry tomorrow. That is not scare talk. It is flat fact. And I do not want you to stop from fear. See it, then put it behind you. You stop because freedom feels better, not because fear commands you.

Do you still want to call that deferred load "energy"?

Think again of the dearest memory you use to defend BAD SUGAR. Not now. Later chapters will meet that sweetest scene head-on. For energy alone, ask the colder test:

If BAD SUGAR gave you energy, would you need it more after using it or less? Would you feel more capable after a week of hits or less? Would you describe yourself as fresh or as tired, foggy and drained?

You already know the honest answer. You say it yourself when you are free of the wrapper: "I feel awful after it. I need more because I feel awful after it."

That sentence is the trap confessing.

You do not need willpower to step off. You need to see the ride was never energy. It was motion sickness you learned to sip.

Do you want another loop? Or do you want ground?

Decide. Clearly.

**IT'S THE OTHER WAY AROUND.** The tiredness you blame on the day is the cost of the last hit. The lift you praise is that cost briefly paused. Remove the hits and you remove the cost.

### WHAT REMAINS WHEN THE LOAN ENDS

You may worry: "If I stop BAD SUGAR, will I be flat forever? Will I have no lift at all?"

No. You will not be flat. You will be steady. There is a difference.

What leaves is not energy but the yo-yo. The search. The 3pm "I need something RIGHT NOW." The evening regret. What returns is the baseline you forgot — neutral, comfortable, lightly energetic, ready to feel hungry when fuel is needed and satisfied when it arrives. You will not feel superhuman. Do not expect fireworks. Expect something more valuable: you will stop paying interest on a loan you never needed.

I promise no superpowers beyond Carr amplitude. I promise you will not have to chase energy. Your body will provide it. Your days will feel longer because you are not queueing for the next lift.

Try this gentle experiment while you finish this book. You do not need to quit yet. Just watch.

Watch 3pm. When the pull comes, pause. Ask: "Would a plain raw apple, a carrot, a handful of nuts, or plain warm food satisfy me right now?" If yes, eat fresh food freely until you are comfortable. If no, and only BAD SUGAR appeals, notice that is not hunger but the cue firing — the rollercoaster calling you to queue. Do not fight it. Just label it. "There it is. The flood calling for another bucket."

Do you notice how naming it as trap, not need, already loosens it? That is not willpower. That is seeing.

And ask the childhood question that ends the debate, kindly, finally:

Did little you need BAD SUGAR to have boundless energy? Or did little you have boundless energy until BAD SUGAR taught you to call the dip "need"?

Which answer is true in your own body, today?

You came into this chapter believing BAD SUGAR carried you through the afternoon. You leave it seeing it carried you back to the queue.

That is one more FOR crossed off the ledger. One more power cord unplugged so it cannot run the machine. Taste was never the gift. Energy is not the gift either. Both were the Sugar Trap sneaking a ride on moments that were yours.

Hold the credit where it belongs. The climb was your relief. The steadiness is your nature. The flood was the intruder.
