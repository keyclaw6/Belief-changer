Chapter 6
EATING LIKE WE WERE BUILT TO

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A quiet kitchen at noon, true hunger arriving on its own, fruit warm in the hand, roast vegetables on a plain plate, bread that is food not confection, the clean finish of enough

*Real eating was never control and never bland — your body already knows how to want, enjoy, and finish without BAD SUGAR.*

### The Fear You Carry Into Freedom

You have let two cherished stories go.

You have seen that BAD SUGAR is not love and not kindness. You have seen that it is not lift, not fuel, not focus — that the lift was only relief from the dip it made. That took courage, and you showed it. You answered honestly when I asked you to watch the drawer, the tight shoes, the hand pushed in the river.

And now, in the quiet after those verdicts, a new worry arrives, softer and more reasonable-sounding than the others. It does not argue that BAD SUGAR is good for you. It says: "Fine. It gave me nothing. But what will eating *be* without it? Will every day be control? Will food be dull? Will I be managing and measuring for the rest of my life?"

I hear that fear. Of course you feel it. We were all taught that eating is control and that BAD SUGAR is the fun part — that without the confection, the treat, the sweet hit, food is discipline and discipline is grey.

The fact is, that is the last trick of the brainwashing. It cannot keep you with pleasure any more — you have seen through that — so it tries to keep you with the fear of blandness.

I will not argue you out of this fear. I will ask you to feel your way out of it, in your own body, with a real meal. Because the answer is not a theory about food. It is an experience of food you already know but have been taught to forget.

We have been living in the trap so long we have mistaken the gatecrasher for the house. The house is real food. It was there first. It is still there.

Come and sit down with me for one ordinary meal and notice what your body does when BAD SUGAR is simply not in the picture.

### When True Hunger Arrives

We have forgotten what hunger actually feels like.

Not that empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling that flicks on ninety minutes after something sweet — that thin, urgent, I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW chatter behind the eyes. We named that feeling together. It is not hunger. It is the whisper from the little creature when the tight shoes pinch.

True hunger is different. It does not nag. It arrives.

You know this arrival from childhood, even if you have not felt it cleanly for years. You are busy, you are living, and then — quietly — the body says *now*. Not a craving for a taste, not a picture of a packet, but a simple, open readiness. The mouth waters a little. The stomach gives a clear, hollow signal. The mind becomes interested in *food* — not in BAD SUGAR, but in food.

Notice how undemanding it is. True hunger does not panic. It does not bargain. It does not say "just this kind of sweet will do." It says: "I am ready to eat. Real food will be wonderful."

We have been taught to fear hunger, to pre-empt it, to graze before it can arrive so we never feel it. But when you let it arrive cleanly — because you have not been spiking and dipping all morning — hunger is a pleasure in itself. It is the appetite that makes the meal marvellous. It is your instinct doing its job, steady and kind, without any need for willpower or counting.

Sit with that for a moment. When did you last feel that clean, simple *ready*? Not the edgy need after a biscuit, but the calm opening that says the body is prepared to receive food and enjoy it?

That opening is not a problem to solve. It is the beginning of satisfaction. And BAD SUGAR never gave it to you. BAD SUGAR covered it over with a louder, false urgency so you would feed the urgency rather than answer the hunger.

Let the true hunger come. It knows what it wants.

### Real Food Tasting Like Itself

Now, with that true hunger alive, taste what real food does when you meet it without the con.

Pick up something plain and real. A ripe peach, or an apple that snaps when you bite it. A pan of roast vegetables — carrots and parsnips edged with brown, sweet in the way fire makes things sweet, not the way a factory does. A slice of bread that is food not confection, that smells of wheat and warmth, that you chew and it stays with you. Plain yoghurt, or cheese, or a handful of nuts. A simple meal with nothing engineered around it.

Eat it slowly. Not as a discipline. As attention.

What do you notice first? Chewing matters again. Flavour arrives in layers, not in one sharp, sickly blast. The fruit is sweet — truly sweet — but it is sweet inside a body of water, fibre, scent, acidity. You taste it, and the taste *belongs* to the fruit. The roast vegetables are sweet and savoury at once, grounded, deep. The bread satisfies as you chew it, not after you have crashed from it.

Do you feel how different this sweetness is? BAD SUGAR is weaponized sweetness — isolated, intensified, stripped of everything that makes sweetness part of food, blown up until it bullies your palate. GOOD SUGAR — the natural sweetness already inside whole fruit, vegetables, and plain dairy as part of real food — is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit. It is sweetness where it belongs, in food that feeds you.

We were told real food is bland. The fact is, we stopped tasting real food because our palate was dulled by the confection. When every day includes doses of processed sweetness in pretty paper, an apple cannot compete — not because the apple is dull, but because the tongue has been shouted at too loud to hear it. Take away the shouting and the hearing returns. Marvellously quickly.

You do not need a new palate. You need your own palate back. And it comes back on its own. Not by discipline. By absence of the bully.

We fell into the trap together, learning to call that bully flavour. You will walk out of it together with me by noticing that real flavour was always there underneath, waiting. Have you ever bitten into a peach at true hunger and felt the juice run and thought, without any instruction, *this is delicious*? You have. Every child knows it. That knowledge never left you.

This is not a diet lecture. I am not giving you rules, portions, or a system. I am asking you to trust the tongue you were born with. It already prefers real food when it can taste it.

And here is the quiet reassurance your body has been waiting to give you: your body makes the glucose it needs itself, steadily from protein and glycerol, so eliminating BAD SUGAR is not deprivation and does not starve the brain. You are not removing fuel. You are removing a con that rode on top of fuel and called itself fuel. Real food remains — fully, abundantly — and your body knows exactly what to do with it.

### Enough Finishing Cleanly

If hunger arrives cleanly, satisfaction finishes cleanly, too.

Watch the end of this ordinary meal. Not the end of a BAD SUGAR episode — that end was never an end. The packet crackled, the hand went back, the taste turned from pleasure to push, and you stopped not because you were satisfied but because you were tired, wired and crashing, or ashamed, or the packet was empty. That was not finishing. That was collapsing.

Real satisfaction is different. It is quiet.

You chew, you swallow, you feel warmth spread low and steady. The interest in food softens. The plate is not empty because you forced yourself to stop; it is empty or half-empty because the body says *enough, thank you.* There is no edginess after. No need to chase the taste with another taste. There is a clear, steady, settled feeling — not heavy, not wired, just *done* — and energy that stays with you hour after hour without a dip demanding another fix.

Have you felt that clean finish lately? The sigh that is not a crash but a completion? The moment you push the plate a centimetre away and think, without triumph or guilt, *lovely — that was enough*?

That is not control. That is the instinct we were built with doing exactly what it was built to do. Hunger, enjoyment, enough. No negotiation. No bargaining with yourself. No drawer opening by itself.

We were taught eating is control because BAD SUGAR made control necessary. When every bite spikes and drops you, of course you must manage, count, restrict, and then binge. Take BAD SUGAR out of the picture — keep the definition we set together, the line that protects real food — and the need for control falls away. Not because you are being strong, but because there is nothing to control. The body self-regulates when it is not being conned.

I want you to feel the relief in that. Freedom from BAD SUGAR is not a life of measuring your meals with a tight jaw. It is a life where meals measure themselves. You get to be hungry. You get to enjoy food fully. You get to be finished. Easily, immediately and permanently, that rhythm returns because it never left — it was only drowned out.

You do not have to learn to like this. You already like it. It is the most familiar feeling in the world once you notice it again.

### The Body You Can Trust

For years we put our trust in packets. Bright packets at child-eye height telling us we deserve a treat, we need energy, we should reward ourselves. We put our trust in slogans while our own body was trying to tell us the truth with every slump and every craving.

May I ask you, gently, to put your trust back where it belongs — in the body that has carried you faithfully through every one of those slumps without ever failing you?

Your body is a marvellous instrument. It asks for water when you are thirsty. It makes you sleepy when you need sleep. It makes you hungry when you need food, and it makes food delicious when you are hungry, and it makes you satisfied when you have had enough. That is not a theory to believe. That is an awe to notice.

Think of the elegance of it: you do not have to calculate your glucose. You do not have to instruct your stomach how to digest. You do not have to argue your tongue into liking a ripe pear when you are truly ready to eat. The system is already intelligent, already calibrated, already kind — far more sophisticated than any laboratory that tried to hijack it with a bliss point and a slogan.

BAD SUGAR did not improve that system. It overrode it, loudly, with a crude, isolated sweetness that gave you a ninety-second quiet, then left you emptier. Real eating does not override. It cooperates. It leaves you clear and steady.

You are not someone who must be taught how to eat. You are someone who was taught to doubt how you eat, so you would buy the thing that made eating feel broken.

Let that doubt go. Let the meal I described be not an instruction but a memory returned to you. A kitchen at noon. Light on the table. A plate of real food, eaten slowly because you are hungry and it is good, finishing in quiet satisfaction that stays with you through the afternoon without a crash chasing it. No packet rustle. No hand deciding for you. Just hunger, taste, enough — the trio we were built for.

If you have not lived that afternoon for a while, you will. Not by trying. By stopping the thing that kept interrupting it. When you stop feeding BAD SUGAR, the interruption stops, and the natural rhythm reappears on its own, as surely as hearing returns when the shouting stops.

And you will find — to your delight — that this is not bland. It is more delicious. Real flavour, clean hunger, steady satisfaction: these are not poor substitutes for the confection. The confection was the poor substitute for *them*, and it was stealing their place.

You have already demolished what BAD SUGAR pretended to give you. Now you have felt, in your own mouth and your own belly, what remains when it is gone. It is not emptiness. It is the house itself, steady and wonderful, that you get to live in and enjoy for the rest of your life — not as a sentence of control, but as freedom you can taste at every meal.

SUMMARY
- Eating without BAD SUGAR is not bland control; it is hunger arriving cleanly, real food enjoyed fully, and satisfaction finishing quietly.
- True hunger is a calm, open readiness for food, distinct from the empty, edgy, slightly needy feeling that follows sweetness.
- Real food tastes like itself when the palate is no longer dulled by weaponized sweetness, and its natural sweetness is part of nourishment, not the trap.
- Enough is a clear, steady finish, not a crash or a collapse; the body signals done when not spiked and dropped.
- Your body makes the glucose it needs itself, so removing BAD SUGAR is not deprivation and real eating remains fully available.
