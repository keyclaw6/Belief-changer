Chapter 10
THE WILLPOWER TRAP

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The Monday kitchen at 7 a.m. and the promise you make to the fridge, the phone app with its streak counter and red reset button, the drawer you try to walk past without looking, the colleague who brags about willpower and the friend who whinges about how hard it is, the cliff edge with its polite little sign about falling only a few metres

*You did not fail because you are weak — the method you were given was built to fail, and your staying power proves how strong you already are.*

### The Monday You Have Lived A Hundred Times

You know this Monday.

I know it too. I have lived it more times than I can count.

You wake up a little tired, a little wired, you look at the kitchen and you make a deal with yourself. *This week will be different. I will be good. Just a little. I don't keep it in the house. Once a week is fine.*

By ten the office drawer is already talking. By three you are negotiating with a biscuit packet like it is a person. By eight you are on the sofa with chocolate you swore you would not buy, eating it fast, hiding the wrapper, feeling that hot, flat shame that follows every willpower Monday.

If that is you, hear me clearly and warmly: you are not weak.

We all stood in that same kitchen. We all made that same promise. We all broke it and blamed ourselves.

The fact is the promise was not yours to break. It was the method that broke you.

I want to give that method its true name so you can see it plainly and stop carrying its failure as if it were your character. The name is `the Willpower Method`.

`the Willpower Method` is the idea that quitting BAD SUGAR is a test of grit. That you must brace, clench, walk past the drawer with white knuckles, count days, count grams, bargain for one small hit, and be proud of how hard you are trying. It tells you cutting down is sensible. It tells you a special one is safe if you are disciplined enough. It tells you failure means you lacked strength.

Every part of that is back to front.

`the Willpower Method` does not remove the desire. It preserves it. It keeps BAD SUGAR precious by making it forbidden, then asks you to live forever beside something precious while pretending you do not want it. That is not freedom. That is torture. And torture does not fail because you are weak. It fails because torture fails.

Think: if you wanted to stop watching a film you no longer liked, would you need an app to count how many days you had not watched it? Would you need to tell yourself to stay strong every time it was mentioned? Of course not. You would just not watch it, and you would not feel deprived.

Why does BAD SUGAR need grit when a boring film does not? Because you still believe, deep down, that BAD SUGAR is `a genuine pleasure, treat or lift` and that without it life is a little less sweet. As long as that belief lives, willpower is the only tool left — and willpower is not a tool for freedom. It is the trap's favourite method for keeping you in the trap.

Have you noticed how hard you have tried? That is not evidence of weakness. We needed a strong will to persist in something that goes against every instinct for years. It takes a strong will to keep queuing for the very thing that leaves you tired, wired and crashing. It takes a strong will to keep restarting on Mondays after dozens of Mondays that ended the same way. Wilful, not weak-willed — that is the truth about you.

I say it without flattery. You kept going back. That is not the action of a weak person. That is the action of a person who was given the wrong instructions and followed them loyally.

`the Willpower Method` taught you to measure your worth by how long you could stand next to the drawer without opening it. I am going to show you there is nothing in the drawer worth wanting, so you will not need to stand there at all.

### The Cliff You Cannot Jump A Little

Let us stage the moderation idea honestly, because this is where `the Willpower Method` sounds most reasonable and does its most damage.

You say to yourself, "I will just have a little." "I will keep the special ones." "I will allow it on Fridays." "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop — so I will control the starting."

We tried that, we all did. A permitted sliver, a single biscuit, a little square to be sensible — and then the familiar blur where the sliver becomes the box. I am not diagnosing you. I am describing the trap's signature, the one thousands of us report in the same words: one bite calls the next, not because you are greedy, but because one BAD SUGAR keeps the belief alive that there is something to get from the next one. Moderation does not make BAD SUGAR smaller. It makes it more precious.

Here is the image I want you to hold for the rest of the book. It does the whole moderation argument in one picture.

Imagine we stand at the top of a cliff. The drop is sheer. The sea is far below on rocks. Someone puts up a neat painted sign: YOU MAY JUMP OFF THIS CLIFF AS LONG AS YOU DO NOT FALL MORE THAN A FEW METRES.

You would laugh. Not because you are rude, but because the sentence is nonsense. The height is the fact. You cannot jump off a cliff a little. You are either off the cliff or you are not. The fall does not check your portion size.

BAD SUGAR is that cliff.

`the Willpower Method` points to the sign and calls it sensible. *Just a little off the cliff. Just on special occasions. Just the dark chocolate, just the one at the cinema, just Fridays.* The trap does not know the word just. It knows fed or not fed. One deliberate feed revives the little creature and re-inflates the big belief, and the belief brings back the wanting by tonight. An accidental crumb, a mistaken ingredient, eaten without belief, revives nothing — your body can cope with a blip — but a deliberate "just one" is not a blip. It is a decision to believe again that BAD SUGAR gives you something.

That is why cutting down feels harder than stopping. With `the Willpower Method`, every gap between hits is spent waiting for the next precious hit. The hit becomes a reward for having deprived yourself. The deprivation makes the hit glow before it even touches your tongue. You think you are learning control. In fact you are being taught to fantasise more intensely, to fetishise the very thing you want to be free of.

Ask yourself, flatly: have the weeks you tried to cut down felt calmer or more obsessed? Did you think about sugar less or more? Did the box get easier to leave or did you finish the box faster than when you gave yourself permission?

You already know the answer because you have lived it. Cutting down intensifies each hit's illusory relief and guarantees the next dip writes the next rule. That is not your failure. That is the schedule.

### Why Each Hit Hits Harder When You Ration It

Let me show you why, so the relief can land without a trace of self-blame.

When we eat BAD SUGAR steadily — grazing, sipping, snacking — the body rides a yo-yo you can feel at three in the afternoon. When we ration it — white-knuckle until Friday, then allow a treat — we do not soften the yo-yo. We wind it tighter. The wait makes the treat feel huge, the huge hit creates a deeper after-dip, the dip is misread as need, and the need becomes the excuse for the next treat. The pattern is the problem, not your portion.

This is why the same person can report, in the same week, "I hardly ate any sugar" and "I lost control and ate the whole packet." Both are true. Rationing raised the stakes so high that the first permitted bite carried the whole week's wanting.

`the Willpower Method` calls that binge proof you lack discipline. It is not. It is proof the method increases the very wanting it claims to control.

You might have heard conflicting chatter about what happens if you just force yourself off sweet drinks for three days by willpower — one small experiment says you feel worse, another says you feel better. I mention it only to be honest with you: short, narrow windows measured that way genuinely disagree, and they disagree in teenagers drinking sweet drinks, not in you reading this book about the whole BAD SUGAR line. That split does not decide your freedom. It only proves what we already know from your own Mondays: white-knuckling a small piece of the trap for a few days, while still believing BAD SUGAR is a reward, is a strain experiment — not a belief change. Strain will always give noisy, contradictory results. Truth goes quiet and stays.

I do not want you to white-knuckle any window. I want you to see the trap, so there is nothing to white-knuckle.

Have you ever watched a child eat real food when truly hungry? No app. No streak. No negotiation. Hunger arrives, food satisfies, the child stops. No one taught that. The body knows how to start and stop when BAD SUGAR is not shouting over it. `the Willpower Method` taught you to override that body with rules. Freedom is not a better set of rules. It is the end of needing rules for something that needs no ruling.

So ask: does "just a little" feel like freedom or like being told you may fall a little? If the sentence sounds absurd at the cliff, it is absurd in the kitchen. The same height, the same lie.

### The Braggers And The Whingers

If `the Willpower Method` fails so reliably, why do you know someone who claims it worked for them? And why do you know someone else who moans about how hard it is forever?

Because `the Willpower Method` produces two public characters, and both keep you trapped.

The Bragger says, "I did it. I used willpower. I cut out sugar. It was hard, but I stayed strong and I still miss it, but I manage." Listen to what is actually being said: *hard, still miss it, manage.* Does that sound like freedom to you? Does that sound like someone who no longer wants BAD SUGAR? Or does it sound like someone who still believes BAD SUGAR is `a genuine pleasure, treat or lift` and is spending every day not having it? You are not meant to envy that strain. You are meant to pity it. I say that kindly, not smugly — I was that bragger for a while, tight-jawed and quietly proud of my suffering, counting days on an app as if freedom were a sentence with parole.

The Whinger says, "It is so hard. I keep failing. I have no willpower. You have to cut everything out and be miserable." The Whinger is the Bragger on a bad day. Same belief, same method, same misery, only without the badge. Both chant the same hymn: quitting must be hard and needs grit.

I want you to hear both with new ears. When the Bragger boasts about strain, you are hearing someone confessing they still want BAD SUGAR. When the Whinger complains about suffering, you are hearing the same confession. Neither has killed the belief. Both are guarding it with effort.

The fact is they are not enjoying BAD SUGAR less than you did, and they are not enjoying freedom more. They are stuck in the middle — not fully in the trap, not fully out — performing control.

Would you swap places with someone who still dreams about the drawer while telling you how strong they are for not opening it? Or do you want to be the person who walks past the drawer and feels nothing but relief that you no longer pay to open it?

`the Willpower Method` sells suffering as proof of seriousness. I sell you ease as proof the belief is dead. One keeps you counting. The other lets you get on with enjoying your life.

And here is the foreshadow I must leave with you before we close this chapter, not to solve today but to name honestly: there are two ropes that `the Willpower Method` hands you and tells you to pull. One rope is fear of failure — *what if I cannot do it, what if I fail again?* The other rope is fear of what happens if you do — *what if life without BAD SUGAR is flat, joyless, less sociable?* You feel torn between them, hand over hand, as if you must choose which fear to obey. The fact is both ropes are held by the same hands. Both belong to the trap. We will collapse them together in the next room. For now, just see the geometry: a tug-of-war where every pull tightens the trap, and the only way to stop pulling is to see who is holding the rope.

### There Is No Finish Line

This is the sentence `the Willpower Method` can never say honestly, so I will say it for you:

With `the Willpower Method`, there is no finish line.

Think about that Monday again, but picture it stretched for a year. If quitting is grit, when are you free? After a week? A month? A hundred days on the app? Every counted day is a day you still miss it. Every "one day at a time" is a promise to re-fight the same fight tomorrow. The calendar becomes a staircase you built where there was no staircase. You are free the moment you stop wanting it — not the moment the counter says a nice number.

`the Willpower Method` has no finish line because it never ends the wanting. It manages the wanting. Managing is not escaping. You cannot manage your way out of a trap. You see it and step out.

I want you to feel the weight lift that comes with that seeing.

You did not fail. The method failed you. And because the method failed you, you kept the most valuable thing of all — your strong will intact for the escape that actually works. We need that will now, not to clench, but to be honest, to keep an open mind, and to follow the instructions that make escape inevitable.

Which is why I must give you a firewall for the next chapters, while your mind is still tender and the world's noise is loud.

While you read, the world will keep selling `the Willpower Method`. Friends will tell you how they suffered through it. Influencers will tell you to track, to resist, to earn a treat. People who still feed BAD SUGAR will offer you some with love, and their love is real while their information is not. If you listen to all of that at once, you will feel torn before the belief has fully died. So for this short reading period — not for the rest of your life — we close the noise.

7. **IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY THE WILLPOWER METHOD** — Their strain is not your method.
8. **AVOID THE INFLUENCE OF OTHER BAD-SUGAR USERS WHILE YOU READ** — Their chatter is the brainwashing talking.

I issue those not as harsh rules, but as warmth. You would not try to learn a new language while three other stations blare. Let this book finish speaking. When you are free, you can hear anyone and not wobble, because you will hear their strain or their craving as proof they are still inside the Sugar Trap, not as invitation. For now, give your mind the quiet it was never given when the trap was installed.

Do you feel the shift? Before this chapter, failure meant you lacked strength and quitting meant a life of effort. After it, failure belongs to `the Willpower Method` and strength belongs to you — the strong will that kept trying on bad instructions. The cliff has no safe little jump. The ration makes the hit more precious. The bragger and the whinger are the same person on different days, and you need envy neither. And there is no finish line to willpower — so you do not need to run its race a single step further.

You are not broken. You were badly coached.

Let the bad coaching go. Keep the will for what matters — seeing clearly — and let the wanting die with the lie that fed it.

- `the Willpower Method` keeps BAD SUGAR precious by forbidding it and then asking you to live beside it with effort, so desire grows instead of dies.
- Cutting down and keeping special ones intensifies each hit and guarantees the next dip, which is why one bite becomes the box.
- Past failures prove the method failed, not you — persisting on bad instructions took strong will, not weak will.
- People who quit by strain still miss it and are not free to envy — with `the Willpower Method`, there is no finish line.
