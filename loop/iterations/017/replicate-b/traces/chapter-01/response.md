Chapter 1
THE PROMISE — HOW TO READ THIS BOOK

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The kitchen counter after lights out, the crumpled packet pushed to the bottom of the bin, the safe that needs every number, the two impossible-sounding promises.

*You do not need a stronger will — you need to see the trap clearly, and then escape becomes easy.*

### BEFORE WE BEGIN

If you are reading this at the kitchen counter with the taste of something sweet still in your mouth, or with a wrapper tucked out of sight, or after telling yourself tomorrow you will be good — you are exactly where you need to be.

I do not know you yet, but I know how you arrived. You have tried. You have cut down, cut out on Mondays, hidden the evidence, made rules about not keeping it in the house, and watched the rules collapse. You have done the hard part — the negotiating, the counting, the private bargaining — and the thing you were trying to control is still controlling you.

If any of that feels familiar, let me say this plainly: there is nothing wrong with you.

There is something very wrong with the trap you walked into, and with the method you were sold to get out of it. We were all conned in the same way. That is why we will use we when we talk about how we fell into it — because we did, together — and you when we talk about the escape — because you are the one who will walk out.

You may be expecting another lecture. You may be braced for shame. Put that down. I am warm to you and vicious to the trap. I will never scold you. I will show you how the trick works, and once you see it you will not be able to unsee it.

I was where you are. For years I lived in the craving-snacking loop — the morning promise, the afternoon drawer, the evening sofa with something sweet I told myself I had earned. I believed I loved it. I believed I needed it to function. I believed life without it would be flat and joyless. I tried to be strong and failed, and the failure felt like proof I was weak.

Then I saw the inversion. I saw that the very thing I thought was giving me lift, comfort and reward was taking all three away — and selling them back to me for a few seconds at a time. When that belief changed, the want collapsed. Not after weeks of strain. Right then. That is the escape this book hands you.

And I want you to hear the contract up front, in the plainest words, because you have every right to be sceptical of an outrageous claim. This is that claim:

You can stop eating BAD SUGAR — not less, not more carefully, but free — and you can do it easily, immediately and permanently. That is not a boast. It is what happens when you kill the belief that keeps the trap alive. The body rights itself on its own. Your job is only to see.

I know how that sounds. Too good to be true. That was my first thought too. And that is exactly why we need to read this book in a particular, easy way.

You have guessed the truth already: most approaches fail because they start with strain. We will not. Freedom here is not a future milestone you earn by counting days. It is an immediate gain. You are not losing a pleasure. You are escaping a con.

All you have to do for now is this: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain by reading on with an open, curious mind. You risk nothing. If I am wrong, you will have spent a few hours and learned something. If I am right, you will be free.

### WHY THIS BOOK IS DIFFERENT — AND WHY THAT MATTERS

You have not failed because you lacked discipline. You failed because you were given the wrong instructions for the wrong problem.

The trap was not built by your appetite. It was engineered around you. Bright packets at eye level, the slogans that told you that you deserve a treat, the recipes that quietly doubled the sweetness year after year — the whole lobby that profits while you pay — taught you to read a craving as a need and a hit as a pleasure.

And the method you were handed after that — the strain, the bargaining, the white knuckles, the counting — kept you in the trap by selling strain as the solution and letting each collapse feel like personal proof. That method is a villain in its own right. We will dismantle it later, and you will feel the shame lift off you completely.

This book does not do that. It does not ask you to fight. It shows you there is nothing to fight. It changes the wanting, not just the behaviour. When you no longer believe BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, you do not need to resist it. You simply no longer want it. That is why this feels easy when it has always felt hard.

There is a practical point here that makes everything else work. While you read, carry on exactly as normal. Do not try to be good for me. Do not test yourself. The reason will be clear in a moment.

### WHAT WE MEAN WHEN WE SAY SUGAR

We need one clean definition, now, so the rest of the book does not wobble. From this page forward, one name carries that definition in every sentence.

> **BAD SUGAR = any added or refined sugar + any product engineered around it + any starchy junk carb that behaves like sugar in the trap:** sweets, chocolate, biscuits, cakes, pastries, puddings, ice cream, sugary drinks, sweetened yoghurts and cereals, white bread, white rice, chips, and other ultra-processed carbs eaten as snacks, lifts or treats. When this book says *sugar*, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. GOOD SUGAR — the natural sweetness already inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain dairy as part of real food — is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit.

Read that once more and let it settle. BAD SUGAR is not food. It is a processed poison in pretty paper, weaponized sweetness that was designed to be craved and re-craved. GOOD SUGAR inside real food is food. You are not here to quit fruit, or vegetables, or a bowl of plain yoghurt. You are here to escape the trap that pretends those things and a chocolate bar are the same. They are not.

From now on I will say BAD SUGAR, and you will know precisely what I mean.

Two small doctrines sit inside that name that will save you a lot of trouble.

First, inside the BAD SUGAR line there is no healthy level other than zero. One deliberate fix keeps the belief alive — that you still get something from it — and while the belief lives, the craving lives. This is not about perfectionism. It is about seeing clearly: if there is no benefit in doing it often, there is no point doing it occasionally either.

Second, the seatbelt. Your body can cope with an occasional blip — a mouthful that turns out to have sugar in it, a mistaken bite at a friend's table. Your mind will not cope with a deliberate exception you choose and romanticise. An accidental blip revives nothing if the belief stays dead. A deliberate just one feeds the little whine and revives the big lie. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the behaviour.

And because you did not pick up this book to quit everything that tastes sweet: you are not quitting GOOD SUGAR, dairy as food, or alcohol as part of this decision. Only BAD SUGAR. If freedom feels so good that later you want the upgrade and look at those too, the door is open and I will point you to help. It is a bonus, never a condition of success.

This book is not medical advice and does not treat any disease, and if you have diabetes, an eating-disorder history, take prescription medication for blood sugar, or are pregnant, speak to your own clinician before changing what you eat; if eating feels distressing, reach out now to a trusted person or your health service.

### THE SAFE THAT NEEDS EVERY NUMBER

Let me give you the picture that makes the contract we are about to make feel inevitable rather than odd.

Imagine a safe with ten numbers. Turn nine correctly and the door stays locked. Not because you were close, not because you tried hard — because the lock is the lock. One missing number is the same as no numbers. You do not prize the safe open with effort. You learn the combination, turn every number in order, and it clicks.

The trap you are in is that kind of lock. It is a set of beliefs, not a test of character. You did not lock yourself in by being weak; you were conned into turning the tumblers the wrong way on phoney information. And the way out is not to push harder against the door. It is to follow the combination in order, letting each number do its one job, until the mechanism releases.

That is why I am so insistent on order. Each chapter has exactly one job. It lands one belief move and then stops and hands you to the next. Skip ahead and you miss a number; miss a number and the door stays shut. Follow all the numbers and you do not need to be heroic. You just need to be willing to turn them.

This is also why so many honest, strong-willed people failed before. They turned one number beautifully — a week of clean eating, a perfect Monday — while leaving the other nine untouched. With the wrong method, there is no finish line. With the right combination, there is no struggle.

You are not being asked to believe this. You are being asked to test it. Bring your scepticism with you. Question everything you think you know about BAD SUGAR as we go. Be the investigator, not the defendant. You will supply your own evidence from your own life, and your own conclusion will be the one that lasts.

### YOUR TWO INSTRUCTIONS — THE ENTRY CONTRACT

We have come to the simple contract that makes the whole thing safe and easy. You have already heard it in pieces. Here it is as two clear commands. Follow them exactly while you read, and I promise the rest will do its work on its own.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND**
Question everything you think you know about BAD SUGAR.

**2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL THE VOW**
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.

Read those again. I mean them kindly and I mean them absolutely.

Keep an open mind does not mean believe everything. It means be willing to consider that every feeling you have called pleasure, treat or lift around BAD SUGAR might be a misreading — and to let your own experience decide. When a sentence rubs, do not slam the book shut. Ask, is that true in my life? The very next page will meet your objection and answer it.

Do not stop or cut down until the vow is not a trick. It removes the threat that always made these books impossible to read. Most books demand sacrifice on page one, so you read braced for deprivation and every word feels like a threat. I am asking the opposite. Keep feeding the trap exactly as before while we look at it together. No testing, no counting, no proving. If you are to escape, it will be because you see clearly and want to. Strain before sight only tightens the trap.

Those are the only two instructions until the next chapter, which will add one more. Together they are the combination we turn in order. Keep the mind open. Keep the behaviour unchanged until the moment we mark together. When that moment comes — a last ordinary bite taken with attention, not nostalgia, and a solemn vow made once — you will already be ready.

I will say once more what is at stake and what is not. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain by doing this exactly as written. You risk no sacrifice, no loss of joy, no flat future — because, as you will see, BAD SUGAR never gave you joy in the first place. It sold you relief from a discomfort it created. That is the whole inversion, and once you live it, there is nothing to miss.

So start as we mean to go on — with relief, not dread. You are not facing a punishment. You are about to be shown the exit to a cage you did not know you were in. The door has been open while you paced the old perimeter. Follow the numbers. Let each one click.

When you close this chapter tonight, you should feel three things: safe to keep reading, clear on what BAD SUGAR means whenever I say it, and willing to keep an open mind and change nothing yet. If you feel those, you are already doing it.

That is the promise. Keep reading. The next chapter names the trap.

### SUMMARY

- This book treats stopping as escape from a con, not loss of a pleasure, so the reader can feel safe and curious while the belief changes.
- BAD SUGAR is a defined trap set of refined and engineered sweets, drinks and junk carbs; ordinary natural sweetness in whole food is not part of it.
- Freedom here depends on seeing the trap in order, like turning every number of a combination lock, not on trying harder.
- The entry contract makes reading safe: the reader keeps an open mind and changes nothing about eating until a marked vow, which removes threat and lets sight come before action.
