Chapter 6
EATING LIKE WE WERE BUILT TO

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The kitchen at midday, the honest empty belly, the apple and the roast tray, the bread that is food, the clean finish that stays.

*Real eating was never bland control — it is the pleasure BAD SUGAR was blocking.*

### THE MEAL BEFORE THE STORY

You have spent two chapters watching BAD SUGAR do its trick.

You saw it sell itself as a treat and take the love with it. You saw it sell itself as energy and steal steadiness with it.

So a fair worry arrives, in your exact voice:

"Fine. It gives me nothing. But if I don't eat BAD SUGAR, what *is* eating? Is it control forever? Is it chewing joylessly while other people enjoy themselves?"

I am warm to that worry. It is the Big Lie talking, not you. It taught you that eating *is* control and BAD SUGAR is the fun part. Of course life without the fun part sounds grey.

Let me show you eating as your body already knows it — before any rule, before any packet.

Think of a child you have watched. Not a child being managed. A child let alone for a morning.

She runs. She plays. She forgets food completely. Then, at some honest moment that nobody announces, hunger arrives. Not a gnaw. Not panic. A clear, empty, willing feeling. The belly says, now. The mouth waters at the thought of real food. She eats — an apple that snaps, bread that is bread, potatoes, cheese, soup, a piece of chicken — and she eats with attention because she is hungry. And then — this is the part we forgot to watch — she stops. Not because she is being good. Because she is satisfied. Finished. Clear. She gets up and goes back to playing, without a wrapper, without a crash, without bargaining for more.

We were all that child.

That is not discipline. That is instinct. And instinct is not startled by BAD SUGAR. BAD SUGAR startled instinct.

You already own this. I do not have to teach it to you. I only have to invite you to feel it again, once, with your own mouth, at your own table, without a slogan in your head.

We fell into the trap together — we learned to call grazing "eating" and to call BAD SUGAR "food." You escape by feeling, in one ordinary meal, what food actually is.

Shall we eat that meal now, in words, so your body remembers before your next meal arrives?

### WHERE HUNGER LIVES

Sit with me at a normal kitchen. Midday. Not a celebration. Not a punishment. Just a Tuesday.

You have not snacked your way to this moment. You waited. You let a few hours pass doing ordinary things — work, errands, walking. And after a while you feel it: not that tight, edgy hunting we called "need sugar now," but a quieter thing lower down. An opening. An emptiness that is not scary. It is clean. It says, I am ready.

Do you feel the difference? That is real hunger. It does not shout. It beckons.

You put real food in front of you. Not a product engineered to hit a bliss point. Real food. A bowl of roast vegetables still warm from the tray, edges browned, salt, olive oil. A slice of bread that tears, not foam that dissolves. A piece of fruit that bites back with juice. Maybe cheese, maybe beans, maybe fish — I am not prescribing, I am describing what humans have always called satisfying.

You eat slowly. Not as a technique. Because hunger has made you interested.

The first bite is wonderful — not because sugar has flooded it, but because hunger seasons everything. The apple is sweet. Truly sweet — not weaponized sweetness, but clean, bright sweetness that belongs inside the fruit. The roast carrot is sweet in a different way. The bread is sweet underneath its salt. Real food is not bland. It is subtle and full and it changes as you chew.

Notice what BAD SUGAR never allowed you to notice: you taste now.

When every snack is processed poison in pretty paper, your palate lives under a foghorn. After a week of that foghorn, an apple tastes like nothing. You think, "I don't like healthy food. I need something more exciting." The fact was the foghorn. Turn it off, and flavour returns. I have watched this happen easily, immediately and permanently for people who simply stopped feeding the con and kept eating real meals.

You were never giving up taste. Your taste was being taken from you.

Keep eating, until satisfaction arrives. Not fullness as a stuffed feeling. Satisfaction. The body gives a small, definite signal: enough. The chewing slows on its own. The food in front of you is still pleasant, but not pulling. You could have more, and you do not want more. That is the other thing we were taught to distrust — that our own enough is enough.

Ask yourself, gently, as you eat:

Did you need to count anything to feel that?
Did you need to resist anything to stop?
Did you feel deprived because you did not add a dose of BAD SUGAR on top?

No. You felt — for the first time today without commentary — well. Clear. Steady.

This is what I mean when I say the meal itself is the proof. It does not argue. It shows.

You thought eating was control and BAD SUGAR was pleasure. The meal shows you the other way around. Control was BAD SUGAR making you graze, negotiate, hunt, bargain, crash, hunt again. Pleasure was this — hunger arriving, real food answering, satisfaction finishing — a rhythm your body has run since you were small, without your help.

A brief picture, because you will need it next time a bright packet shouts: real food was the house all along. BAD SUGAR was the gatecrasher who ate the host. You do not need a new house. You only need to stop opening the door to the crasher.

Feel how that lands differently from a rule? No one is telling you what you may not have. You are discovering what you already have.

### THE CLEAN FINISH THAT STAYS

The best part of a real meal is not while you eat. It is after.

You push the plate away. You stand. You feel something BAD SUGAR never gave you.

Steadiness.

Not a lift. Not a spike. A level, marvellous steadiness that does not ask for another hit in an hour. Your head is clear. Your mood is not pitching. Your belly is not calling you back to the cupboard. You can get on with enjoying your life — work, walk, talk, laugh — without the afternoon negotiation.

You call this normal. It is normal. And you had forgotten how wonderful normal feels after years of tired, wired and crashing.

"But," you say — and I want to hear you, verbatim, so I can answer you warmly — "but I love food. If I stop BAD SUGAR, will I still love food?"

I hear how tender that question is. It feels like identity. Of course you love food. You were built to love food. That is why BAD SUGAR could hijack you at all — because the love was already there.

You do not love BAD SUGAR the way you love food. You love food the way your body loves being fed. BAD SUGAR rode that love. It looked like food because it sat where food sits, in a packet near food, at the moment real hunger called.

Eat the roast, the apple, the bread that is bread, and tell me after the clean finish: did you love that less? Or more, because you could actually taste it, and because it left you steady instead of hunting?

The answer is already in your mouth.

And here is the quiet physiology that lets you trust this without a new diet plan.

Your body makes the glucose it needs.

You do not need to import BAD SUGAR to keep your brain lit. From the ordinary protein and fat in real meals, your system keeps glucose available, steadily, without a wrapper. So when you stop BAD SUGAR, you are not depriving your brain of fuel. You are removing interference. That is why the clean finish stays — not because you have imposed willpower, but because you have stopped imposing the thing that created the yo-yo.

No prescription follows from that. No keto rule. No ban on fruit, vegetables, plain dairy, plain grains that satisfy — that natural sweetness is not BAD SUGAR and is not quit. I am not handing you a regimen. I am pointing at a freedom your body already knows how to run when you let it.

So test this not as an idea, but as a doing.

At your next true hunger — not boredom, not the clock saying three, not the packet rustling down the hall — true hunger, that clean empty willing feeling — sit down and eat a real meal until you feel that calm satisfaction. Chew. Taste. Stop when the pull stops.

Then do what children do. Get up. Go live. Do not graze. Do not top up. Notice that steadiness carries you for hours without a fix, without a hit, without feeding anything.

That steadiness is not a reward for being good. It is the baseline. You never needed BAD SUGAR to reach it. You only needed to stop digging the hole you were trying to climb out of.

I promise you as an escaped eater, not as a lecturer: real eating is not control forever. It is relief. It is wonderful. It is freedom you can taste in one bite of an apple when your mouth is truly hungry — and feel for hours after, as clear, steady, enough.

BAD SUGAR did nothing FOR you. Real food does everything FOR you.

And once you have felt one real meal from hunger through to satisfaction and stillness, you will not need me to persuade you. Your body will have shown you. The meal is the reframe.

### SUMMARY

- Eating without BAD SUGAR is not bland control; it is hunger arriving, real food satisfying, and a clean, steady finish.
- Real food is sweet and delicious in its own way, and flavour returns when BAD SUGAR stops fogging your palate.
- Your body makes the glucose it needs, so stopping BAD SUGAR does not starve you of energy and does not require a special diet.
- One ordinary meal eaten to satisfaction proves freedom is not a rule but a return to how you were built to eat — steady, clear, and enough.
