Chapter 9
THE BODY KEEPS THE SCORE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The dentist's mirror up close, the tight cuff at 3pm, the quiet numbers on the scale, the familiar ache behind the eyes

*BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you — and seeing that clearly is relief, not a sentence.*

### THE PRICE YOU STOPPED COUNTING

You have seen who built the cage. Now let us look, without flinching and without dread, at what living in it has been costing your body.

We told ourselves it was harmless. A little sweet after lunch. Something to get through the afternoon. We called it a treat and we paid for it with steadiness, and then we called the unsteadiness proof we needed another treat. That is the whole con in miniature.

For a while we don't count. The packet is small. The drink is normal. Everyone does it. The body absorbs the loan and keeps going. And because the heat rose so slowly — you remember the Boiling Frog picture — we called the warm water normal life. That is not your fault. That was the design. The canyon was built to make far too much feel like just enough.

I want you to feel the reframe in your own body, not in a statistic. Stand in the three places where the body keeps the score honestly, even when the mind still repeats the Lie.

### THE MIRROR, THE CUFF, THE SCALE

First, the mirror.

Open wide. The dentist lifts the little mirror and you see a dark line at the gum, a chalky edge, a filling you had forgotten. The fact is… BAD SUGAR feeds the bacteria in plaque that make acid that dissolves enamel. That acid is what rots teeth. Brushing and fluoride matter, hygiene matters, but the mechanism is direct: free sugars → plaque acid → decay. Not one sweet guarantees a cavity tomorrow, not every cavity comes from sweets alone — but at population level the ticket is punched there, appointment after appointment, year after year. Those pretty packets do not list this on the front. Your mouth lists it for them.

Second, the cuff.

It is 3:07pm. The drawer, the biscuit, the lift that never lifts — you know it. Later in the year, in a quiet clinic room, a cuff tightens on your arm. The number is a little higher than last time. At population level, high intake of added sugar is linked with higher heart and stroke risk — through liver overload, raised blood pressure, chronic inflammation, and a bypassing of the appetite controls that were meant to keep you steady. The fact is… the hit you felt as energy was a deferred load. High intake, over time, is linked — not one dose causes a heart attack, not freedom from BAD SUGAR guarantees prevention — but the ledger is real: what felt like a lift was the body taking on work you never agreed to. If you have a medical condition or take medicines that affect blood sugar or pressure, speak to your clinician before changing what you eat — this is about seeing the trap, not a prescription.

Third, the scale and the feeling after it.

We step on, we step off, we tell ourselves not to care. The body keeps another score you can feel without any machine: you finish the sweet and a short while later you feel tired, wired and crashing — that edgy buzz that is not energy, the tired that is not sleepiness, the crash that asks for another fix to quiet itself. That is not weakness. That is the after-effect doing exactly what a trap does: it creates the low it then offers to relieve. You never rose above the steady baseline of a person free of BAD SUGAR. You were pushed down and sold the hand back up.

Stay with that for a second — not as fear, as information. Your body was informing you correctly the whole time. You read the warning lights backwards because the Big Sweet Lie taught you to.

### THE MOOD YOU THOUGHT IT MENDED

And there is a quieter score, the one we most want to pretend is rescued by a treat.

We reach for BAD SUGAR when we feel low or tense or flat, because we were taught it comforts. In long-running cohorts, men who got more of their sugar from sweets had higher later odds of common mental distress — daily sweets predicted worse mood later, not relief, and the researchers checked and did not find it was just low mood driving the sweet eating in the first place.

Read that carefully and kindly: that finding is men only, observational, sweets as the source — not proof that sugar causes depression in everyone, not a diagnosis about you, and not a promise that stopping fixes mood by itself. But it does one honest job: it breaks the promise that the treat comforts you. At group level, the comfort did not comfort. It predicted the opposite.

That is why the roller coaster feels so personal and so cruel. BAD SUGAR sells itself as the friend that soothes the fall, when the fall is the price of the friend.

Take the other quiet marker: the population ceiling for free sugars is less than ten percent of energy — about fifty grams — with additional benefit below five percent, because everyday life in the canyon already puts so many people far beyond it without feeling they are doing anything extreme. That number is not a personal prescription for you. It is a population anchor that tells you how engineered normal has become. We exceeded it while calling ourselves moderate. The Frog was not imagining the heat.

You do not need me to tell you that any one bar, any one bottle, ruins a heart or a mind or a tooth on the spot. It does not. The trap is not a cliff you fall off in one step. It is a slope you pay on every day, in small, plausible withdrawals — until the statements arrive and you call the statements your age, your genes, your character.

The fact is… BAD SUGAR was doing plenty TO you while doing nothing FOR you. That is not a scare to leave you in. It is the bill you were already paying, now correctly addressed.

### RELIEF, NOT FEAR

I say this at full force because you deserve the truth plain: high intake is linked with real stakes — teeth, heart and vessels, inflammation, mood. I say it and in the same breath I will tell you exactly what I do not want.

I do not want you to stop from fear.

Fear makes you white-knuckle, count, bargain, and wait to fail. Fear keeps the trap alive by making the thing you leave feel powerful. We are not using fear. We are using sight.

If you stay in the Sugar Trap you pay for the rest of your life in small, daily thefts of calm and steadiness. If you walk free you are free for the rest of your life — not by grit, but because you no longer do the thing that was stealing from you. Same phrase, opposite meanings. One is a sentence. One is a release. You choose which way that line reads by seeing the bill correctly once.

There is a tired old rule that says you can keep BAD SUGAR if you keep it small — you can jump off the cliff as long as you don't fall more than a few metres. The body does not work on that bargain. The schedule that taught you to binge, the cues that glow before the bite, the loan that comes due after the lift — none of that obeys a little exception. One deliberate exception keeps the Lie alive and the Worm fed. An accidental blip — a stray ingredient, a mistaken bite — revives nothing if the belief stays dead. Guard the belief, not your halo.

So do not leave this chapter frightened of your next meal. Leave it relieved that you finally know where the tired, wired and crashing came from — and that it was not your nature.

We fell into this together. You are not weak. You were willing to believe a kind-sounding lie from people who profited while you paid. Your body, beautiful and steady when left alone, kept flashing the warning lights and you were taught to misread them as hunger for the very thing that lit them.

Now you read them true.

And when you read them true, there is nothing to mourn. There is only a weight you can put down. The price was being paid whether you noticed it or not; the relief is available the moment you stop paying it. That is not sacrifice. That is escape.

You do not need to change from fear. You get to get out, wonderfully, because there was never anything in BAD SUGAR that was doing anything FOR you — and there is everything ahead when you stop doing so much TO yourself.

### SUMMARY

- BAD SUGAR was doing plenty TO you — linked at population level with tooth decay through plaque acid, and with higher heart and vessel risk through liver load, blood pressure and inflammation — while doing nothing FOR you.
- High intake over time is linked with those harms; one dose does not cause them and freedom does not guarantee prevention, but the lift you felt was a deferred load.
- Daily sweets predicting worse later mood in men does not prove cause in everyone, but it breaks the promise that the treat comforts you — the comfort predicted the opposite.
- Living far beyond the population sugar ceiling feels moderate because the environment was built to sit beyond it; the body kept the score even when you called the warmth normal.
- Seeing the score clearly is relief, not fear — you do not need to quit frightened, you get to escape because there is nothing real to miss.
