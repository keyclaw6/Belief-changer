Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THIS CAGE?

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The supermarket canyon at child-eye height, the lab coats behind the bright packet, the warming water you stopped noticing, the dashboard lights you were taught to call weakness

*You didn't build this cage from appetite — it was built around you and sold back to you as your own sweet tooth.*

### THE CANYON YOU WALKED WITHOUT SEEING IT

Walk it with me now, while you are clear.

You push the trolley in. The light is bright. The aisle is a canyon — floor to ceiling colour, every packet shouting. At child-eye height, bright cartoon faces. At hand height, the big family bags with a smiling farm on the front. At the till, the little singles, "for now," "for the road," "treat yourself."

We walked this canyon together for years, and we thought we were choosing.

We were not. We were being chosen.

I have seen it with you, because I lived it with you. We tell ourselves "I've got a sweet tooth," as if the tooth were born in us. But the tooth was trained. The packet was placed. The slogan was written for the exact moment you feel tired or tender and need a friend. "You deserve it." "Reward yourself." "Make today special." The words are not yours. They are advertising copy that moved in and learned to speak in your voice.

Look again at that canyon and see who it serves. The packets are not food that happens to be sweet. They are sweetness engineered to be food — calculated to hit a bliss point, to melt fast, to keep you reaching, to be cheap enough to buy without thought and expensive enough, over a lifetime, to build an empire.

You were not weak in that canyon. You were outgunned.

And that matters, because the whole of the Sugar Trap depends on you believing the opposite — that the trap is your appetite, your willpower, your character. If it is your fault, you stay quiet. You hide the wrappers. You start again on Monday. You blame yourself and you go back for another hit to soothe the blame.

See it now for what it is. You fell into the Sugar Trap the way anyone falls into a confidence trick — on phoney information, kindly delivered, from people you trusted, about what would make you feel better. And the lie that made the whole trick feel normal, that taught you to read the Worm's whine as love or need, was the Big Sweet Lie — fed to you by the very people who profit when you feel you need what harms you.

Warmly, I tell you this not to make you bitter, but to give you back your dignity. You were not greedy. You were conned. There is a world of difference.

### THE LABORATORY BEHIND THE PRETTY PAPER

Let me show you the room behind the canyon.

Not a kitchen. A laboratory.

White coats. Banks of dials. Trays of white powder weighed to the milligram. People who are brilliant, paid well, who do not eat the thing they are perfecting — because they know what it does. Their brief is simple and it is not cruel in the way a villain in a film is cruel. It is commercial. Find the point where sugar and fat and crunch and salt light up the wanting circuit most brightly. Make the texture vanish quickly so the mouth wants another bite before the last one is finished. Make the colour promise love. Put it where a tired hand will fall on it.

Armies of scientists weaponizing abundance. That is not a conspiracy sentence. It is the business model.

We lost awe for how clever our own bodies are, and we gave that cleverness to a factory.

Your body knows how to ask for food, how to enjoy real food, how to stop. The factory knows how to make a product that imitates food just enough to fire the same circuits but not enough to satisfy them — so you stay in the loop. And the loop has a schedule.

In the animal work that mirrors our way of eating, the trap does not appear when sweetness is there calmly, all the time. It appears when it is given intermittently — binge, restrict, binge, restrict — exactly the yo-yo we were taught: be good, be strict, count, deny, then "treat," then binge the box, then be good again tomorrow. That schedule drives escalation. The binge gets bigger. The wanting gets louder. Not because sugar is a mighty drug, but because the pattern teaches the brain to binge.

That is the schedule we were sold as discipline. "I don't keep it in the house." "Just a little once a week." "I'll be good and then I can have it." The schedule is the trap.

And the schedule trains cues. Once the loop is learned, the wrapper glows before the bite, the smell pulls before you are hungry, the sofa cues the hand. The brain becomes hyper-reactive to the signal — not because you are helpless, but because it learned that this signal once predicted the short rescue. The fact is… the glow is in the prediction, not in the product. The packet sells excitement; the eating never delivers it, except as brief quiet of the very agitation it created. That crowding of the reward circuit — needing the family size to feel what the small bag once gave — is not proof you love BAD SUGAR. It is proof the circuit is tired of being conned.

Now look at the scale. For a whole population, the recommended ceiling for free sugars is less than ten percent of energy — about fifty grams — with additional benefit below five. That number is not a personal prescription for you, it is a population marker. What matters is this: normal life in the canyon now puts almost everyone far beyond it without ever feeling they are doing anything extreme. Not because you are extreme. Because the canyon is extreme. The norm was engineered to sit above the ceiling and to call that "moderate." When everyone is over, no one feels they are in a trap. 

There is your Boiling Frog moment — not as a new argument, but as a picture you already own: the heat rose so slowly you called the warmth normal. The packet at child-eye height became furniture. The 3pm drawer became culture. You called it life while you were being cooked.

See who raised the heat.

### THE LIGHTS YOU CALLED WEAKNESS

This is the turn that gives you your anger back, in the clean form.

For years you read your own body perfectly and then translated it backwards.

The afternoon crash after the sweet — you called that "I need sugar to function."
The edgy restlessness when you hadn't had a hit — you called that "my sweet tooth talking."
The hand back in the packet after one biscuit — you called that "no willpower."

Read them again, correctly, as the body wrote them.

The crash is a warning light. Not character. Not need. Your steady system saying, "That spike was not food, that was a con — I am paying down the loan you just took." The empty feeling is a warning light. The Worm whining because it was unfed for an hour, not you wanting. The loss of control after one bite is not you failing at moderation — it is the trap doing exactly what the schedule taught it to do: once triggered, keep going.

Your body was not failing you. It was informing you. Patiently, in the only language it has — feeling — it kept flashing: this is not fuel, this is friction. This is not treat, this is trap.

We were taught to read those lights as proof we should feed the very thing that lit them. The manufacturer profits when you do. You pay — in money, in steady energy, in calm — and you thank the hand that pushed you in for hauling you halfway back.

No more.

You are not a person with a defective appetite. You are a person with a beautiful, steady appetite that was borrowed for a while by an industry that learned how to mimic satisfaction while removing it.

Feel the reframe settle. The shame you carried belongs elsewhere. The anger, if you want it, has a proper address — not yourself, not your body, not your past attempts. The trap. The pretty paper. The canyon built to harvest a whine and sell you the whine as desire.

You do not need to become someone else to be free of BAD SUGAR. You need to stop obeying instructions that were never yours. Your hunger, your taste for real food, your clean satisfaction that stays — those were never broken. They were drowned out. Turn down the canyon's noise and they return on their own. No discipline. No heroics. Just the return of the normal you had before the first bright packet taught you to misread yourself.

You were never the problem. The cage was built around you.

And a cage built around you can be walked out of.

### SUMMARY

- The craving and the canyon were not your invention; they were engineered and sold to you as normal choice.
- The binge-restrict schedule, not mere exposure, teaches escalation — which is why "being good" and then treating drives the very loss of control you blamed on yourself.
- Wrappers, smells and times glow before the bite because the brain learned they predict the short rescue; the glow is prediction, not proof of pleasure.
- Living far beyond the population sugar ceiling feels ordinary because the environment was built to sit beyond it — gradual heat you stopped noticing.
- Crash, craving and the hand back in the packet are not character flaws; they are the body's warning lights that BAD SUGAR gave nothing and took steadiness away.
