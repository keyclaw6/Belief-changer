## 1. BOOK CORE

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of BAD SUGAR — refined/added free sugars and junk carbs (sweets, biscuits, chocolate, ice cream, sugary drinks, pastries, white bread/pasta/rice, processed snacks) taken in the craving–snacking loop. Not taste, not cooking, not nutrition pedantry.

**One reader, 4 functional personas:**
* **P-01 — The loss-of-control binger** — one bite becomes the box, secret eating, shame/restriction cycle, failed diets.
* **P-02 — The in-denial moderate** — "just a treat," daily grazing that never reads as a problem, carve-out rules ("I don't keep it in the house").
* **P-03 — The comfort/identity eater** — sugar as reward/love/cope/nostalgia; "I deserve it."
* **P-04 — The energy-crash yo-yoer** — sugar as fuel for focus and the 3pm slump; lift–crash rollercoaster.

All four are one reader in different moments. Every chapter targets at least one persona explicitly via lexicon and scene.

**Load-bearing false belief (one sentence):**
*Sugar — especially BAD SUGAR — is a genuine pleasure, treat and energy-lift that makes life sweeter, and I would be deprived without it.*

**Through-line:** *Rescuer as perpetrator.* The thing you reach for as cure is the cause. Every chapter reassigns credit away from BAD SUGAR until the benefit collapses to zero.

**Format:** Full-length Easyway (~60k words), 18 chapters. Pocket-book sequence expanded with full-length architecture (B10). Front matter + instructions at both ends, verbatim recap lists.

**All five forks — explicit decisions for this book:**
* **F1 Inner monster: CARR DEFAULT — FULL PERSONIFICATION.** Two mechanism characters with original behavior-fitted names. Little Monster equivalent = **the Sugar Gremlin** (trivial, almost-imperceptible physical restlessness). Big Monster equivalent = **the Sweet Brainwashing** (lifelong belief-system that interprets the Gremlin's whine as proof sugar gives pleasure). Gremlin is small, starving, already dying; revel in its death throes. Never a mighty enemy demanding willpower.
* **F2 Outcome: CARR DEFAULT — TOTAL CESSATION of BAD SUGAR, commanded with cheerful certainty.** Moderation foreclosed by pincer and cliff-jump. The redefinition (BAD SUGAR line) IS where total abstinence lives; intrinsic sugars in whole fruit/veg/milk remain permitted and are part of Nature's Guide. Instructions are numbered commands issued with warm rationale, never apologized for.
* **F3 Science: CARR DEFAULT — SCARE-THEN-DISOWN at full Carr force.** Hard facts (dental caries, metabolic load, mood cost) delivered vividly, flatly, unhedged and frightening where true — then explicitly disowned as motivator. No hedge, no omission, no literature-review texture. Raw citations quarantined to back-matter appendix.
* **F4 Villain: CARR DEFAULT — TWO VILLAINS, both hit hard.** (1) The trap/industry — sugar engineers, bliss-point formulation, marketing that manufactured desire; (2) The named anti-method — **the willpower method** and its diet/restriction variants that kept the reader trapped. Anger channeled at both, never at the reader.
* **F5 Void: CARR DEFAULT — "Change nothing else in your life."** Body returns to its natural baseline on its own; reader inhabits old contexts confidently to prove freedom. No elaborate replacement system. For this consumptive behavior the positive authority (Nature's Guide) is an informational operating manual, not a Burgeon-style transmutation/identity rebuild. Root-craving replacement is not invoked — explicitly brief-level override not used.

**Redefinition and margin-for-error doctrine (B10):**
BOXED DEFINITION in Ch1 and Ch13, repeated as CAPS token thereafter: **BAD SUGAR = all refined sugar, all added free sugars, and processed junk carbs (including white flour/bread/pasta/rice, sugary drinks, sweets, chocolate, biscuits, pastries, most packaged snacks).** Decree: *When this book says "sugar," take it to mean BAD SUGAR.* Good sugars (intrinsic in whole fruit, vegetables, plain dairy) are not the target and are part of Nature's Guide.

Margin for error — frozen doctrine: *Your body can cope with an occasional blip, but your mind cannot.* Accidental ingestion in a sauce or at a meal is a non-catastrophic buffer — it revives nothing unless you let the Sweet Brainwashing back in and reinterpret it as permission ("I got away with it, so I can do it again"). Seatbelt image: the margin is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Guard the belief, not the behavior.

**Clinical / eating-disorder safety perimeter:**
Not medical nutrition therapy, not diabetes management, not eating-disorder treatment. No diagnosis, no prescribed carb thresholds for medical conditions, no weight-loss mechanics. Boxed advisory in Front Matter and Ch13 with frozen wording: *If you have diabetes, an eating disorder, are under medical nutrition therapy, or take medication affected by diet, continue to follow your clinician's advice on food and medication — this book does not replace medical care. If you feel you need support, ask a clinician or trusted support service.* Any instruction touching food change carries that exception verbatim. Practical fear-material disowned per Fork 3.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene assigned to Ch11:**
P-03/P-04 cinema/family celebration: warm cinema with chocolate, or sunny afternoon tea / birthday cake where everyone is laughing and you tell yourself dessert IS the point of the day. This is the book's idyllic garden coffee-morning equivalent — met head-on, pleasure reassigned to company/warmth/occasion, BAD SUGAR shown sneaking a ride.

**Destination state:** Reader feels *relief and freedom that they no longer do BAD SUGAR* — whenever BAD SUGAR crosses their mind they think **FANTASTIC! I'M FREE FROM SUGAR!** — so stopping feels like escaping a trap, not sacrificing a pleasure. Freedom available immediately after the final dose, not at a future milestone.

**One fresh ending reframe (saved until Close):**
*You didn't give up sweetness — you recovered your taste for it.* The whole effort reframed as growth/reclamation, not quitting: Burgeon = to grow. The reader didn't lose the sweet life; they escaped the con that dulled it.
