### Voice Gap 1: Research-report caveating drops out of Carr's single voice

**Our passage:** "We have all been conned in the same way. That is the good news. It means you are not uniquely weak. On validated self-report scales for highly palatable foods — not a medical diagnosis, just people's own answers about loss of control, craving, and use despite wanting to stop — about 14 in every 100 adults describe addictive-like patterns. About 12 in every 100 children do too. That figure is roughly comparable to the figures for alcohol or tobacco on similar scales."

**Real passage:** "No one likes to admit they're really an addict and it seems incredible to think that just about everybody on the planet is hooked on the same substance. But that is exactly how it is."

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr removes shame by assertion and shared experience — "just about everybody ... is hooked" — delivered as one person's plain conviction. The reader is reassured, rather than audited.

**Where our voice fails:** The instant the passage shifts from "You are not uniquely weak" into methodological hedging ("highly palatable foods ... self-report scales ... not a medical diagnosis, just people's own answers ..."), the reader stops being a person being comforted and becomes a data subject being caveated. It reads like a consent-form aside spliced into the chapter's warmest moment, creating distance exactly where trust and inclusion should peak.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** research — the evidence-graded stat was woven verbatim-adjacent into a warmth passage without being re-spoken in Carr's register.

### Voice Gap 2: Mindfulness-coaching imperatives manage the reader's inner state

**Our passage:** "Hold that lightness. We will need it."

**Real passage:** "See your journey through this book as it really is, an exciting, positive process. Just think how proud you'll feel when you're free."

**What Carr's voice does here:** Carr sets the desired frame plainly and confidently, then moves the argument forward; he never instructs the reader to "hold" a named feeling as a technique.

**Where our voice fails:** "Hold that lightness" is a meditation-app/coach register — it tells the reader to manage and preserve an emotion, which treats the reader as someone who must work at their mindset. That faintly contradicts the book's own promise (no effort, no discipline) and sounds more like a guide coaching a client than Carr stating truth.

**Initial suspicion (speculative):** writer-prompt — coaching-style emotional directives leaked into an otherwise direct, present-tense voice.

**Overall voice-effect assessment:** The single largest voice-effect gap is Gap 1: at the shame-removal peak, the clinical self-report caveating ("not a medical diagnosis, just people's own answers about loss of control, craving, and use despite wanting to stop") replaces Carr's plain, inclusive assertion ("just about everybody on the planet is hooked ... But that is exactly how it is") with the voice of a cautious research report, turning a moment of belonging into a moment of being audited. The warmth, mantras, instructions, and confrontation are otherwise strong and faithful — the chapter can pass on every assigned moment, but this unassigned register drift is material enough to withhold PASS.
