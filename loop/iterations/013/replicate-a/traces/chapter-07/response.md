Chapter 7
BLISS-POINTED: HOW THE TRAP WAS BUILT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
We walk the bright supermarket with you while the wrappers shout before you decide, we open the white-coated lab where the cannot-stop was dialled in, we watch lonely white carbs throw a house party and invite BAD SUGAR over, and we take the weight called lack of discipline off your shoulders and put it where it belongs.

*It was never your discipline that failed — the cannot-stop was built for you, and that uneasy feeling was you telling the truth.*

### THE AISLE THAT ALREADY KNEW YOUR NAME

You know this aisle, Maya.

Bright. Wide. Cold air humming. Colours engineered to shout. Wrappers cracking at the exact pitch your ear has learned to hear. The smell pumped forward before you see the trays.

And your phone buzzing in your pocket. Just for you, it says. Back in stock. You loved this last time. Fancy a little treat tonight?

We walk it together, exactly as you live it, and we pause. Before your hand moves, something has already fired.

That tightening, that glance, that mild pull toward the packet — you called it your lack of discipline. The fact is your body is exquisitely tuned to notice cues, and those cues were tuned to trigger it. The wanting can fire from a smell, a colour, even the crinkle before the taste ever hits your tongue. In the lab that same pattern shows on the creature's own opioid and cue circuits — a wrapper can light the circuit while the mouth is still empty. That is an animal analogue, not a diagnosis of you, and it does not prove a human withdrawal is happening in your body. I place it here gently, and only to show this: the aisle fires first, you answer second. The cue is not your character.

The Confectioner knows this. The Confectioner — the sweet story that has fed the Nibbler for years — whispers, *you have no willpower, you are the problem*. We have all heard it. We believed it because we were taught to believe it. It is a con.

You were not weak in that aisle. You were outgunned.

Ask yourself, plainly, and let the honest answer land:

If this were weak willpower, why does the smell hit before you have decided to want anything?

If this were true hunger, why does it fire when you have just eaten real food and are not hungry at all?

If this were genuine pleasure calling you, why does the wrapper do half the work before the first bite?

The honest answer is one short, flat verdict.

It was manufactured.

### THE LAB WITH THE DIALS

Let me take you behind the glass, just once, so you feel the anger in the right place.

In a clean, bright room, white coats stand around dials. One dial is sweetness. One is salt. One is fat. One is crunch that vanishes fast so you never feel done. They turn sweetness up — too much and you wince, too little and you forget. They turn again until subjects pause, taste, reach, and then cannot stop reaching. They call the spot they find the bliss point. It is not a metaphor. It is the made-to-measure point of cannot-stop.

That is how the Sugar Trap was built. Not by accident. Not by your appetite. By armies of food scientists weaponizing abundance, paid to make one more dose feel like incomplete relief. Your Nibbler — that trivial little sugar gremlin with its mild, empty gnaw — was never meant to be trusted. Your Confectioner — the big, clever story that says this is your treat, your comfort, your fuel — was written for you long before you walked the aisle. Warm to you, ruthless to the trap: you did not invite this. It was built to invite you.

And they built the schedule, not just the substance. In those same rooms they learned that an intermittent, on-off pattern — allow, forbid, allow — drives bingeing and volume. When access is steady, the same frantic pattern does not appear. That is an animal control-group finding, not a law stamped on you, and I do not use it to prescribe a diet. I use it to show what your "Fridays only" and "once a week is fine" were doing inside the Sugar Trap. The yo-yo schedule manufactures the binge. The rule you used to prove discipline was the very thing making one bite become the box.

There is another trick they rely on, and you have felt it. After a while the bite tastes less kind than it used to, so you need more to chase the same note, yet the sight of the packet still fires hard before you eat. That crowding and blunting of the reward circuit seems to explain why "not enjoying like before, but still pulled" is so common, and why a wrapper can blast wanting even when liking is falling. This is interpretation from brain imaging seen mostly in other contexts — not proof, and never brain damage — but the shape fits what you live: less liking, still wanting, triggered before the mouth.

It is not your tongue that is broken. It is the dial that was turned to mute it.

And because you have carried shame so alone, hear this one human number in its honest frame, not as a sentence on you. On validated self-report scales — not a medical diagnosis — about 14 in every 100 adults and about 12 in every 100 children score in an addiction-like range around food, a proportion seen with alcohol and tobacco in self-report. The grade is self-report, not a doctor's label, and it varies by measure. I give it only to lift the private myth that you are uniquely weak. You are not weak. You are not alone. The trap was laid for many, and you walked it as we all did. And I say that not to label you, but to un-label you.

You were never the flaw in the machine. You were the target.

### THE LONELY HOUSE PARTY

Here is the part they never tell you on the packet.

White carbs are lonely. They invite company.

White bread, pastries, crisps, those soft, quick-vanishing starches that behave like BAD SUGAR the moment you eat them — they are not food that settles. They hit, they vanish, they leave that mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly gnawing feeling behind. And a lonely bite looks around the house party for a friend to keep it company. Sugar walks in. Fat walks in. The house fills with the exact combo the dials found — fat plus sugar together seems to amplify the cannot-stop beyond either alone. That "seems to" matters — it is interpretation, not a law — but you have lived the shape: you did not plan dessert, you planned toast, and dessert planned you.

This is not greed. This is engineering meeting biology. Your body, with its instinct for real food, knows when it has eaten something that nourishes. It is calm, steady, done. The engineered plate is designed never to let it feel done. It is designed to produce the dip it then offers to relieve. That is the inversion we named together — the rescuer is the perpetrator — and that house party is where it happens nightly while the Confectioner says *you lack discipline*.

Stand back and look, Maya. Who is lonely here? Not you. The bite is lonely. You are already whole, with a body that knows how to read real food when the noise stops. The unease you felt after those lonely bites — the tired tug, the faint anxiety that something had taken hold — was not insecurity. It was accurate perception. Your body was telling the truth about a con, and the Confectioner taught you to hear truth as weakness. We turn it the right way around now. That feeling was warning, not flaw.

You were not failing the test. The test was rigged.

### THE CON THAT STOLE YOUR CREDIT

So we deepen the Con Trick, because this is where the weight comes off.

A confidence trick does not need a foolish mark. It needs phoney information presented warmly, repeatedly, and in company. "Everyone does it." "Just a treat." "You need something sweet to get through." We were sold the benefit before we ever chose the dose, and then each dose created the low the next dose relieved, so the story felt true. That is how the Sugar Trap removed your choice without ever asking permission.

You chose the first exposure on false pretences. Then the trap chose for you. The fact is you were not choosing freely between pleasure and forbearance. You were choosing between the discomfort the trap guaranteed and the brief relief it sold as pleasure. Never above the baseline of someone who never started. Always returning toward normal and paying for the round trip.

Feel the difference once, and it cannot be unseen.

You told yourself: I have no discipline, so I keep failing.

The truth, said flat: discipline was never the instrument. Belief was. And the belief was installed by the aisle, the lab, and the lonely house party that kept feeding the Nibbler so the Confectioner could keep talking. Starve no willpower muscle. Starve the story. The little creature's gnaw is trivial and small and it dies when it is not fed; the big story is the target we are killing together now. Both ropes of that tug-of-war you lived were held by the trap.

When you see it, name it — that's the Sugar Trap.

And when you see the story, pity it — pity the Confectioner — it's lost its voice when you stop listening. When you feel the gnaw, thank the Nibbler for dying — it is already less, because it is no longer ordered to eat.

You do not need to keep a pet to prove you are strong. You need to stop keeping the cord plugged in that powers the whole machine.

There is one surviving cord the Sugar Trap loves you to keep. The "special one." The Friday tin, the birthday slice that seems to be the celebration, the "I will just keep the soft white rolls and skip the sweet stuff." One cord lights the appliance.

NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL ONE — if there's no benefit often, there's no point occasionally

You are not mournful about this. You are relieved. If BAD SUGAR did nothing FOR you often — no genuine pleasure, treat or lift — then occasionally adds nothing real either. Occasional does not turn illusion into pleasure. It only re-teaches the belief.

**SUMMARY**
* Desire was not your character flaw — the cannot-stop was engineered at the bliss point and fired by cues before you chose.
* Your unease was accurate perception, not lack of discipline — the mild, empty gnaw and the pull of wrappers were warning lights, not weakness.
* Lonely white carbs invite BAD SUGAR over — that fat-plus-sugar house party seems to amplify the trap, which is why one kept "special one" keeps the whole Sugar Trap humming.
* You are not uniquely weak — many score high on self-report scales, not medical diagnosis — the shame belongs to the trap, not to you.
