### THE RAW TEST — YOUR FIRST INSTRUMENT

Your first instrument is **the Raw Test**.

Here it is, exactly as you will use it.

**the Raw Test** asks: *Would I eat this food as nature made it, raw, or with only simple cooking and no added sweetness? Would a hungry child recognize it as food before a packet told them it was?*

Say it plainly: If it needs added sugar or a factory to be edible, it fails. If it is wonderful as it grew, it passes.

Do you need a degree to use it? Do you need an app? Look at the food in your hand and ask the question. Your eyes and nose already know.

An apple — raw, fragrant, sweet without help — passes. A handful of berries — passes. Carrots, peppers, eggs, fish, meat, beans, lentils, nuts, plain yogurt, brown rice, potatoes eaten as potatoes — they pass **the Raw Test** because they are food before they are product.

A biscuit — would you eat its raw flour and fat and sugar by the spoonful? Would you crave plain white starch without the hit? A frosted cereal that is sugar with a grain costume? A fizzy drink that is water made undrinkable unless sweetened? A white loaf that is starch engineered to crave like sugar? They fail. Not because I forbid them. Because nature would not recognize them as food without the costume.

Do you feel the difference? This is not moral judgment. It is recognition. **the Raw Test** does not say "you are bad for wanting it." It says "that is not food your body asked for. That is a dose wearing a food mask."

Use it like this. In the shop, hold the packet. Ask, "Does it pass **the Raw Test**?" If yes, it is on **Nature's Guide** menu. Enjoy it freely when you are hungry. If no, you see it for what it is — BAD SUGAR in a different shape — and you feel relief, not deprivation, that you no longer need to feed **the Nibbler** with it.

Will this make you perfect? No. Perfection is not the aim. Clarity is. And clarity is immediate.

Does **the Raw Test** ban sweetness? No. Sweetness that grew — fruit, whole, with fibre and water and time — is not the trap. Your body meets it as food, not as a spike looking for a crash. Eat it when you want it, because you are hungry and it is good. That is not a slip. That is living.

Do you need to weigh your fruit? Do you need to fear an apple? Do not be absurd. The trap never lived in an orchard.

Short lines to carry:

If it passes **the Raw Test**, it is food. Enjoy it.

If it fails **the Raw Test**, it is a dose. Leave it with pity, not longing.

Ask it once and you have asked it a thousand times.

### THE HUNGER GAUGE 0–20 — YOUR SECOND INSTRUMENT

Your second instrument is **the Hunger Gauge 0–20**.

Hear it exactly as you will use it, for the rest of your life.

**the Hunger Gauge 0–20** is a simple 0–20 gauge for real hunger versus **the Nibbler** whimper. Zero is not hungry at all — satisfied, light, clear. Twenty is ravenous — shaky, gnawing, urgent. In the middle is pleasant hunger — alive, interested in food, calm.

Here is how you read it.

0–3: Overfull. Heavy. You ate past enough. Not ill. Just past.

4–7: Comfortably satisfied. No thought of food. Energy even.

8–12: Pleasant hunger. The clean baseline. Mouth alive. You could eat, and food would taste wonderful. This is **Nature's Guide** speaking clearly. This is the best place to eat.

13–16: Hungry. Urgency rising. Food occupies thought. Eat soon, and choose what passes **the Raw Test**.

17–20: Ravenous and edgy. Hard to think. Easy to mistake for an emergency. This is rarely true need in a well-fed person today. It is often the empty, slightly restless whimper misread by **the Sugar Story** as "need sugar NOW."

Do you see the trick we are undoing? For years you were taught to eat at 17 and call it hunger, and to fix 17 with a sugar hit that drops you back to 17 an hour later. **the Hunger Gauge 0–20** teaches you to notice 8, 9, 10 — the quiet, pleasant hunger that is satisfied by real food and stays satisfied.

How do you use it? Before you eat, pause two seconds. Ask: "Where am I on **the Hunger Gauge 0–20**?" No writing needed unless you like it. One number, honestly felt, is enough. If you are at 8–12, eat what passes **the Raw Test** until you are at 4–7, then stop, pleased. If you are at 0–7 and a packet calls you, you are not hearing hunger. You are hearing **the Nibbler** rehearsing **the Sugar Story** line: "treat when you feel you deserve one."

Do you need to be precise to the point? No. The gauge is a mirror, not a ruler. Over weeks it calibrates itself. Your body already knows 10. You are remembering how to hear it.

Try it now, as you read. Where are you on **the Hunger Gauge 0–20**? Not what the Story says you should be. What your stomach says. Is there a light, empty pleasantness? Is there comfortable satisfaction? Is there the faint, empty, slightly edgy, slightly restless hunger that is not hunger at all but the last whimper of a creature already dying? Name it. That naming is freedom.

You will notice something marvellous within days without BAD SUGAR. Hunger becomes rhythmic again. Not need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW. Rhythmic. You eat. You are free for hours. You eat again when pleasant hunger returns. The roller coaster flattens into a plain, a fruited plain where appetite comes and goes like daylight.

Do you need willpower to ride a plain? Or do you need to stop re-creating the hill?

There is only one honest answer.

**the Hunger Gauge 0–20** is how you prove to yourself, meal after meal, that you were never broken. You were jammed. Now the signal is clear.

### PERCEPTION HOMEWORK — THE HUNGER GAUGE AUDIT

Now we prove it in your body, not just your head. No restriction. No test of character. Just attention, warm and curious, the way a naturalist watches a quiet animal return.

This is perception homework number three. You will carry on exactly as normal until your final dose, except you will collect evidence with your two instruments. Three days is enough to hear the signal again. A week makes it unmistakable.

**AUDIT ONE — BEFORE YOU EAT, GAUGE.**

For the next three days, before every meal or snack, pause. Stand still. Breathe once. Ask: "Where am I on **the Hunger Gauge 0–20**?" Say the number silently. No judgment.

If you are 8–12, note it: "Pleasant hunger, true." Eat what passes **the Raw Test** until you reach 4–7, then stop and notice satisfaction arrive without a crash. Mark it: "Ate at 10, stopped at 6. Clear. Content."

If you are 0–7 and the urge still nags — especially that faint, empty, slightly edgy, slightly restless hunger — note it: "Urge at 5. Not hunger. **the Nibbler**." Do not argue with it. Do not resist it. Observe it like weather. Smile at it. Say, "Ah, you. Dying already." Watch it pass without feeding it BAD SUGAR. Notice that it does pass.

Do this for every eating moment for three days. One line per moment is enough in your phone or on paper: time, gauge before, what you chose, gauge after, one word for how you felt an hour later (clear, even, light vs heavy, edgy, wanting).

What will you find? You will find that true hunger is pleasant and periodic, not panicked and constant. You will find that many urges live at 3–6, not 13–16, and that they fade without a dose. You will find that when you eat at 9 or 10, a modest portion of real food satisfies you deeply, without the roller coaster.

Do you need me to tell you? Your body will tell you. That is **Nature's Guide** speaking. Listen.

**AUDIT TWO — THE RAW TEST SHOP WALK.**

Once in those three days, walk a shop aisle slowly with **the Raw Test** in mind. No trolley needed. Just look.

Pick up five things you once bought without thinking. Ask the question quietly for each: "Does it pass **the Raw Test**?" Feel how instant the answer is. Apple — yes. Plain nuts — yes. Sugary drink — no. Biscuits — no. Yogurt with added sugar — no, but plain yogurt — yes.

Count the nos. Not to shame yourself. To see the trap's real estate. See how much shelf is dose, not food. Does that look like choice? Or does that look like architecture?

Then put everything back, buy only what passes, go home, eat when **the Hunger Gauge 0–20** says 8–12, and notice the next hour. Even. Calm. No negotiation. No "I shouldn't have."

Do you see? The test is not a rule you must obey forever with effort. It is a lens you now have. Once seen, you cannot unsee.

**AUDIT THREE — THE AFTER-HOUR CHECK.**

Two hours after any eating — real food or dose — check **the Hunger Gauge 0–20** again and add one honesty sentence: "Where am I now, and what did that food do TO me versus FOR me?"

If you ate what passes **the Raw Test** at pleasant hunger, you will be at 5–7, steady, perhaps pleasantly ready again soon. No crash. No tug.

If you ate BAD SUGAR — even a small "just a treat" — clock what follows: a brief up, then familiar slide to edgy, restless 12–14 an hour later, plus the Story's voice: "See? You need something." You do not need something. You need the dose to finish dying so **Nature's Guide** can finish returning.

Write that down if you can. That line is the whole trap in miniature.

Use these audits not to be good. Use them to be clear. There is no pass or fail, only sight. Sight is what frees you, not scorekeeping.
