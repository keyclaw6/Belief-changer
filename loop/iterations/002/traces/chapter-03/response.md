### "I CHOOSE THIS" — AND OTHER STORIES WE TELL OURSELVES

Do you tell yourself you choose this?

Do you hear that calm, reasonable voice that says, "It's just a treat. I could stop if I wanted to. It's not even a problem. I don't keep it in the house, so I'm fine. Once a week is fine"?

I hear you. We all said it.

I said it too. And I meant it. The fact is, we need to mean it. The story that we choose keeps us dignified. It keeps us safe from the scarier thought — that something else is choosing for us.

Do you recognise your own lines among these?

"It's just a treat."

"I don't have a problem. I just like something sweet."

"I've managed to be good this week, so I've earned it."

"I need sugary somethings to get me through. But I control it."

Once I start I can't stop — but I decide when to start. So it's still my choice. Right?

If that is your voice right now, I am warmly with you. I am not here to argue with your character. I am here to question the information you were given before you ever believed you chose.

Have you ever wondered why you need this book if you genuinely choose it freely?

Think about that for a second. Do you need a book to help you choose your favourite film? Do you need a method to stop choosing the jumper you love? Do you lie awake negotiating with yourself about whether to watch the movie you claim to enjoy?

You don't. Choice that is free feels light. It does not need defence. It does not need secrecy. It does not need a book.

Why do you need this book, then? Why did you pick it up at all?

That question is not an accusation. It is a doorway. Walk through it with me.

We fell into this as children who did not know. We were handed sweets as love, as applause, as celebration. A good mark got sweets. A grazed knee got sweets. A birthday meant sugar before it meant people. We copied everyone. We believed what everyone believed. We thought we were discovering pleasure. The fact is, we were being shown one side of the bargain before we could ask about the other.

Do you remember your first sweet that mattered? Do you remember who gave it to you, smiling? Did you decide then, with full information, that you wanted a lifetime of craving, crashing, and secret binges for that hit?

No. You decided that sugar was a treat. You decided it was pleasure. You decided it was harmless. That decision was not yours. It was given to you.

And we stay with that decision because it still feels like choice. We say, "I love the taste." We say, "I need it for energy." We say, "It's sociable." Those justifications feel like our reasoning. The fact is, they are lines we were taught to say to defend a trap we did not see.

Do you feel the faint irritation rise as I say that? Does a part of you want to say, "No — I really do enjoy it"?

Good. Keep that irritation. Keep it in the light. I will not push it down. I will ask you to test it.

If you truly enjoy BAD SUGAR as a free pleasure, why does one bite so often become the box? Why does "just a treat" so reliably turn into "I just crave sweets and when I eat them I feel like I can't stop"? Why does the calm treat-talk flip in an instant into evening shame, with the packet empty and the promise broken again?

The P-01 binger knows that flip intimately. The P-02 moderate knows its quieter version — the carve-out, the "I don't keep it in the house" rule, the grazing that never reads as a problem but never truly stops either. Both are choosing. Both are also not choosing. How can both be true?

They can both be true because choice was removed after the first con. You were conned, not foolish. That warmth stays. The harshness belongs elsewhere.

I want to show you that con plainly, and then I want to give it its name. The name matters. Once you have it, you will see the whole book through it. That name is **the Sugar Trap**. I will earn that name in the next section, and then it is yours.

### THE CONFIDENCE TRICK YOU DID NOT SEE

Let me show you the trap as a confidence trick.

Not as a metaphor for fun. As the actual mechanism.

Imagine a con artist who does not pick your pocket in the street. He invites you to invest.

He shows you glossy pictures. He tells you this investment pays daily pleasure. It gives you energy. It comforts you. It rewards you for being good. Everyone does it. Your parents did it. Your friends do it. The pictures show laughing families, bright kitchens, kindness itself in a wrapper.

He never shows you the terms. He never shows you that the pay-out is relief from a discomfort his own product created. He never shows you the crash after the lift, the restlessness before the next hit, the way "just one" re-triggers wanting, the creep where last year's treat becomes this year's daily need.

Do you invest?

Of course you do. We all would. The information was phoney. The choice was not informed. The fact is, you cannot freely choose what you do not understand.

That is what happened with BAD SUGAR.

You were sold a fraudulent investment on phoney information. The con was installed before you had language to question it. Then, once you took the first hits, the trap did its quiet work.

How? Here is what careful work shows, translated cautiously and honestly.

In labs, rats given BAD SUGAR on an intermittent, on-and-off schedule escalated. They binged bigger and bigger. Rats with the same stuff available all the time did not. The schedule itself manufactured the loss of control. We will not inflate that — it is animal work, not a human diagnosis, and it translates cautiously — but you already know the human echo, don't you?

The restriction-binge schedule itself manufactures the binge. The "I've been good this week" rule creates the "once I start I can't stop" box. The trap takes your very effort to control it and feeds on it.

Do you see why willpower talk never helped? Do you see why the Willpower Method — that stern method that leaves desire completely intact and then asks you to fight it with discipline and stay strong — failed you?

You did not fail. The method failed you. That phrase is not comfort. It is diagnosis.

Now add what else we know honestly. On self-report scales that ask the same questions we use for alcohol or tobacco, about fourteen in every hundred adults and about twelve in every hundred children tick enough boxes to meet that addiction-like pattern for highly palatable food. That is roughly the same level as for alcohol and tobacco on the same kind of scale. I tell you that not to label you. There is no clinical diagnosis for you here, and this is not a self-report test that diagnoses a disease. I tell you to de-shame you. You are not uniquely weak. You are not unusually greedy. You are not a defective person who alone cannot handle a treat.

And — because honesty matters more than force — I will tell you the other side in the same breath.

A serious Cambridge perspective contests the whole "sugar is addictive" frame. Those scientists argue the bingeing is about the pattern — the intermittent access, the restriction, the loop — more than about sugar chemistry itself. Human evidence for a hard, drug-like addiction is thin, they say, and "sugar is addictive" remains an open scientific question, not a consensus. They also remind us that the brain chemical often called pleasure is really wanting, not liking. A bite can re-trigger wanting without proving pleasure. You can crave what you do not even enjoy.

Do I need the addiction label to be proven for this book to work?

I do not. The trap does not need a contested diagnosis to be real. Your lived experience already proves it. The pattern is enough. The schedule is enough. The con is enough.

Do you feel the shift? From "I am weak" to "I was conned"? From shame to clear, dignified anger at the con?

Keep that anger warm. Aim it at the trap, never at yourself. We were all conned. That is the confidence trick. And a conned person does not need shame. They need to see the trick.

That trick has a name. Say it with me once and let it land. This is **the Sugar Trap**.

Not a metaphor. A structure. A manufactured loop with a product engineered to spike and crave, a story engineered to make you call the spike a pleasure, and a schedule engineered to turn your control efforts into its fuel.

You entered this trap as a child who trusted. You stayed because the Story told you that you were choosing. You blamed yourself when the choice disappeared. That blame was never yours to carry.

It is the other way around. The trap chose you before you chose it.

Let me land that as a pair, so you feel it.

You did not create the craving. The craving created you.

You did not fail the method. The method failed you.

Hold those. We will need them.

### WHO'S REALLY IN CHARGE?

So I ask you directly, with warmth and with steel:

Who is really in charge?

If you are in charge, why does the thought arrive uninvited? Why does the sight of a wrapper fire wanting before you have decided to want? Why does a smell from a doorway pull you like a cord?

If you are in charge, why does the kind voice that says "just a treat" speak in exactly the same tone as the shaming voice that later says "what's wrong with you"?

If you are in charge, why does this book need to exist?

You know the honest answer. We both do.

Do you remember the tug you feel at the till, at the office kitchen, at the service station? Your hand hovers. Your mind says, "Don't. You've been good." Your other mind says, "One won't hurt. Just this once. You've earned it." Do you experience that as freedom? Or do you experience it as a tug-of-war you watch yourself lose?

That tug is not your freedom. It is the evidence the trap is live.

The Willpower Method teaches you to watch that tug and call it proof you are weak. It says, "Resist. Stay strong. Use discipline." It leaves the desire completely intact and then congratulates itself for telling you to fight.

Do you see the cruelty? The method asks you to stare at bait and not feel hunger. Then it blames you for feeling it.

I say the opposite, and I say it as fact: the hunger is not yours. The tug belongs to the Sugar Trap. You are the one being tugged.

Do you feel relief when you hear that? Does your shoulder drop a centimetre?

Let it.

You are not a person with a defective appetite. You are a person with an intact, healthy appetite that an engineered product learned to hijack. Your appetite is not broken. The product is designed to misfire it. Should you be ashamed of that? Or should you be angry at the engineer?

Think honestly. If you wanted to quit a hobby you truly enjoyed, would you need a method at all? Would you need to read carefully, follow instructions, argue each justification to pieces?

No. You would just stop. The need for help proves the choice was already not free. That is the logic trap whose only honest answer concedes the point.

And if it is not free, it can be restored. That is the good news. A trick seen is a trick weakened. A trap named is a trap you can walk out of.

Say the name again. Not as a scold. As a compass.

**the Sugar Trap**

Do you feel how that phrase gathers the whole trick into three words? The product. The story. The schedule. The tug. All of it, one object. Once you see it as one object, you can escape one object. You do not need to fight yourself piece by piece.

You have nothing to lose and everything to gain by seeing it that way. And you already hold the boxed line from Chapter 1: BAD SUGAR = refined sugar + all added free sugars + processed junk carbs / starchy carbs engineered to spike and crave. That line is the floor plan of the trap. **the Sugar Trap** is its name.

Who is really in charge — you, or **the Sugar Trap**?

If your answer is the second, even quietly, even with a wince — good. That wince is not defeat. It is the start of escape. We name the jailer to leave the jail.

### THE BOILING FROG — WHY YOU FEEL FINE WHILE THE TRAP TIGHTENS

"But I feel fine," you say. "Maybe this is not that serious for me. I function. I manage. Other people have real problems."

Do you say that? Does the moderate in you whisper that you are fine because you are not at rock bottom?

Of course it does. The trap needs you to feel fine while it tightens.

Let me show you the Boiling Frog. It is not a scare. It is an explanation for why scare is hard to feel.

A frog dropped into boiling water jumps. A frog left in cool water that is heated degree by degree stays. Not because it likes it. Because the change is so gradual it never gets a signal that jumps.

We are those frogs. Not stupid. Gradual.

Do you remember when one biscuit satisfied you? Do you remember when a small bottle was enough? Do you notice now that the family size is normal, that you need the top-up, that you think about sugar more, enjoy it less, and still reach? The harm did not arrive as a crisis. It arrived as a habit. By the time you looked up, the water was hot and you called hot normal.

When everyone's sick, no one calls it disease. When every office has a biscuit tin, every lunch a sweet finish, every celebration a cake, who calls it a trap? We call it life. We call it treat. We call it love.

Do you see why "everyone does it" is brainwashing, not safety? Do you see why "I function" is not proof you are free, but proof the trap is smooth?

I will not frighten you here. Fear is not the route out, and I will tell you so plainly when hard facts arise later. For now, just feel the frog image do its work: the reason you feel fine is the reason to look, not the reason to ignore.

Another piece of honesty belongs here. In labs, each BAD SUGAR hit lights up the brain's own wanting circuitry briefly, then dips. It is real. It is also small — smaller than for hard drugs — and the low that follows is not destiny or disease forever, but part of the loop that re-triggers the next thought of sugar. Why does that matter? Because it explains why one bite so often calls the next, and why the call can feel like need even while the body could happily go without.

Do you hear how that matches your evening? Tiny sliver permitted, then the box empty? The bite re-triggered wanting via your own circuitry. The circuit fired. The Story then said, "See — you love it."

It was not love. It was a re-primed loop.

And like an eagle raised as a chicken, you learned to live low because low felt normal, not because you were built low.

Do you want to stay in water that only gets hotter because it heats slowly? Or would you rather step out now while the door is open and the water is still only warm?

I will not keep you in fear. I will lift you into clarity.

### TRAPPED, NOT DEFECTIVE

Let me collect you. Let me hold the warm-to-person, harsh-to-trap line without a wobble.

You are not weak. You were outgunned by an engineered product and a lifelong story. That is not shame. That is the truth.

You are not greedy. You are a person whose appetite was taught to answer a false alarm.

You are not failing at control. The control schedule itself manufactures the loss of control. The harder you try to be good on the Willpower Method, the more the trap feasts on your effort. You were trying to open a lock by pushing harder on the hinged side. The door was never locked. You were pushing the wrong edge.

Do you feel how that lands differently in your body than "try harder"? Does your chest loosen when blame moves from you to the trick?

Keep that loosening. It is not an excuse. It is the only accurate assignment of responsibility. The con artist is to blame, not the conned.

Think with me. If you were weak, would you have persisted at all? Would you have dieted, counted, swapped, hidden packets, started again on Monday, and kept trying after each secret binge left you tired and shamed? That is not weak will. That is strong will misdirected against a trap built to absorb it. The fact is, it takes a wilful person to keep persisting in something that goes against all your instincts while believing it is pleasure.

Do you hear the credit inversion? Your long history of trying is not proof you cannot. It is proof you are wilful. That wilfulness, aimed at truth instead of fighting desire, is what sets you free.

So where does that leave us now, at the seam into the next chapter?

You entered this chapter saying, "I choose this; maybe it's not even a problem." You have walked through the confidence trick audit. You have asked who is really in charge. You have felt the boiling frog heat you called fine.

Are you still freely choosing a treat? Or are you a decent person caught in **the Sugar Trap** — brainwashed, not defective?

If you feel a flash of dignified anger at that trap, good. Keep it. Aim it cleanly. Let it replace shame. Shame is the trap's favourite glue. Anger at the con is its solvent.

I will ask you the trap question one more time, and I will ask you to answer honestly, inside yourself, not to me:

If you love BAD SUGAR, why do you need to defend loving it?

If it does anything real FOR you, why does "just a treat" need so many rules to keep it small?

And if you are free, why does freedom need a book?

There is only one honest answer. We know it. We were trapped.

That is not a sentence. It is a liberation. A trap can be escaped when it is seen. A defect cannot. You were trapped. So you can escape. Easily. The moment the belief goes, the behaviour follows. You do not need to be stronger. You need to see clearer. I will help you see.

Take this with you, verbatim, as the floor you now stand on:

You are not weak. You were conned.

You are not the problem. **the Sugar Trap** is.

You do not need to fight yourself. You need to walk out of a trick whose instructions you never wrote.

Will you?

The next chapter hands you the tool to do it. We will switch the axis. We will stop asking, "Does it do more harm than good?" That question lets the trap hide behind half-truths. We will ask, "What good does it do FOR me at all?" That question makes **the Sugar Trap** justify itself — and it cannot.

We are warm to you, harsh to the trap. We stay there.

We were trapped, not defective. We leave by seeing, not by struggling.
