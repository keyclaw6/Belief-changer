### WHY YOU CAN BELIEVE THE STRANGE PROMISE ON THE COVER

I have been where you are.

I was not a moderate. I was not a "just one biscuit" person. I was the person who said "just one" and then found the packet empty. The person who bought sweets for the children and ate them in the car before I got home. The person who promised Monday, promised New Year, promised after the holiday, and felt smaller every time the promise broke.

And then I walked free. Not by grit. Not by suffering. In one clear, joyful shift.

The fact is, that shift was not complicated. Once the belief changed, the behaviour followed. That is the whole method.

I tell you this at the start for one reason only. You deserve to know who is speaking to you and why you can trust him on this subject.

For more than thirty years I have done one thing only: help people escape traps they believed they chose. I have sat with thousands of people who swore they loved the thing that was draining them. People who were certain they would fail again. People who arrived certain the book could not work for them. I have watched the same relief dawn on faces that have nothing in common except this — they thought stopping would be deprivation, and discovered it was escape.

Has every one of them found it easy? Do you want to know the truth? Almost all of them did. Not because they were strong. Because the method removes the reason to want it. When the want goes, the struggle goes.

You do not need to take that on faith. You only need to keep reading to see for yourself.

Why does that matter now? Because you are about to be asked to do something unusual. You are about to be asked to question beliefs you have held for a lifetime. Beliefs installed by family kitchens, by school treats, by advertisements that taught you sugar equals love, by a world where everyone does it so no one calls it strange. You will not question them because I tell you to. You will question them because you will recognise them as you read.

Do you want to stop feeling controlled by sweets and junk carbs? Of course you do — that is why you picked up this book. And do you fear that stopping will mean losing pleasure, energy, or comfort? Of course you do — that is why you have not stopped already. Both feelings are normal. Both will be answered.

This book works like a safe with a combination lock — miss one number and it stays locked, hit them in order and it opens at once.

And like a lion that paces by its open cage out of habit long after the door is open, you may feel uneasy even when you are already free. That unease is habit, not need. You will understand that soon.

### TOO GOOD TO BE TRUE?

"Too good to be true?" That is exactly what I thought the first time someone told me I could stop easily, immediately and permanently.

It is the right question. It is the intelligent question. I welcome it.

How can stopping feel easy when every diet felt like a punishment? How can it be immediate when you have been told it takes months of white-knuckling? How can it be permanent when you have watched yourself start again after the smallest slip?

Those are not awkward objections. They are the heart of the trap. And the answer is simple. The hard way you tried before was hard because it left the belief intact while demanding the behaviour change. That method keeps the desire alive and then asks you to fight it. This method does the other way around.

The fact is, you have nothing to lose and everything to gain by testing that claim as you read.

Think what "nothing to lose" really means here. I am not asking you to hand over your treats, your comfort, or your energy on page one. I am not asking you to suffer. I am asking you to investigate. To let me make the case. To see whether BAD SUGAR does anything FOR you at all — or whether it does plenty TO you while you credit it as FOR you.

Do you have to believe me now? No.

Do you have to decide to stop now? No.

Do you have to hope it will be wonderful? You will feel more hopeful as you go, but you do not need hope to start. You only need willingness to follow the argument to its end.

A good friend of mine said exactly what you may be thinking. She was the skeptic who proves the method. She told me, flatly, "I don't believe a book can do this. I love sweets. I love baking. Chocolate is my reward for being good all week. Don't waste my time telling me it is poison." She read because a friend nudged her, not because she believed. She argued on every page. And on the last page she laughed and said, "I feel as if someone turned the lights on. I don't want it. I don't miss it. Why did I ever think it was helping me?"

She was not strong-willed. She was not converted by pressure. She was freed by understanding. That is the convert who matters — the reluctant one who succeeds against her own expectation. You will meet many like her in these pages, but you only need to remember one thing: they were not special. They were simply shown the trap clearly.

So let me ask you plainly, and answer it with you. Is it possible you have been misled about what sugar does for you? Who benefits if you keep believing it picks you up, rewards you, calms you, and connects you? Would you need a book to stop something you truly, freely choose?

If you truly chose it, why are you reading this sentence?

The fact is, the discomfort you call craving is not mysterious. When measured carefully, the edgy, restless after-effect after heavy sweet use is faint and brief. It peaks in the first two to five days and then passes. It is not large. It is not prolonged. It is not a fight for life. That is important because it means the physical part is trivial and already ending while the belief — the Story that says you need it — is the real trap.

And the human picture? On standard checklists for addiction-like eating, about one in seven adults ticks enough boxes to meet the pattern — roughly the same rate as for alcohol or tobacco. That is not a diagnosis for you. It does not label you. It simply reminds you that you are not uniquely weak. This is common. It is learned. And what is learned can be unlearned.

Other serious reviewers look at the same evidence and say the opposite: hold on, the clearest bingeing happens with intermittent access — restriction then binge — not because sugar itself addicts the brain by chemistry, and human evidence is still thin. They also stress that dopamine is about wanting, not liking. I agree we should be honest about that disagreement. This book does not need you to accept an addiction label to prove the trap. The wanting/liking gap is actually our opening — you can want something fiercely while not even liking it.

Do you see what that honesty gives you? You can stop arguing about labels and look at something simpler: does BAD SUGAR deliver the pleasure, treat, or lift it promises? Or does it only relieve the low it created?

You will answer that for yourself in the chapters ahead.

### HOW THIS BOOK WORKS — AND WHY YOU DON'T NEED TO STOP YET

This is not a diet book. It will not count your calories, weigh your sins, or measure your worth. It will not preach. It will not shame you.

It will do one thing. It will change the false belief that makes BAD SUGAR look like your happiest option.

We change belief the way a con is undone — by seeing it. We were all conned, warmly and innocently. We are not weak. We were misinformed. And once you see the con, you cannot unsee it.

Here is the contract I make with you, and I make it with absolute confidence.

First, you will not be asked to stop or cut down while you read. Carry on exactly as normal. The read itself is day one of your escape. That instruction lowers your guard so the belief can change before any behaviour is demanded.

Second, I will make the case plainly, with vivid, flat facts where they help, and then I will leave fear behind. You will understand the costs. Then we will put fear down and walk forward toward freedom, not away from punishment. The gain pulls you; fear does not push you.

Third, each chapter does one job. One belief falls. Then we stop. You will not be asked to hold ten ideas at once. This safe opens one tumbler at a time.

Will you keep an open mind? Will you begin the next chapter with a feeling of anticipation rather than gloom? Imagine, for a moment, six months from now. You are at the same birthday table, the same office kitchen, the same evening sofa. Someone offers sweets. You smile, not because you are being good, but because you simply do not want them. You think, with genuine delight, how marvellous it is to be free. That is the moment we are heading toward. Picture it now. Hold it lightly. Let the book walk you there.

You will not be told to be strong. Strength was never the problem. The Story was the problem. We will starve the Story. The rest starves with it.

And remember this going in: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain. Read as an investigator, not a defendant. Question what you think you know. Let the method do the work.

We fell into the trap together as children who did not know. You will walk out of it as an adult who does.

EASILY, IMMEDIATELY AND PERMANENTLY — that is the promise we will keep.
