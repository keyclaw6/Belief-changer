Chapter 7
THE NIBBLER AND THE SWEET CON

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The plain table still warm behind you, early evening in low kitchen light, the twitch low in the body watched and smiling, the ditch in the morning lane and the offered hand, tight shoes at midnight, the two voices pulled apart, the short hump of three days, wrappers and smells firing before the bite

*That ache you dread is tiny and already dying, and the loud voice that feeds it is a con, not you.*

### YOU ARRIVE FED

You walk in fed in the true sense.

We sat at the market table together and ate a plain favourite hungry and stopped glad at satisfied. Bread torn with both hands. Vegetables shining. Fruit cold and bright. Hunger opening. Satisfaction closing. No coating. No hunt after. You felt what you feared you would never feel again, that eating itself delights when the mouth is clear. Keep that table with you. No one can argue it away because your own mouth lived it.

And the moment that fear loosens, the old dread steps forward in its clothes. I know her well because I lived with her for years.

She speaks in the early evening, low and reasonable, in your own voice.

"Very well. Food can please me without sweet. I felt it. But night is coming. You know what night brings. That crawling restlessness. That gnaw that will not be named. If I take no sweet tonight, and none tomorrow night, and none the night after, that feeling will grow till I climb the walls. I have felt it. It frightens me more than any grey plate."

"I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. Short term lift then fast drop. I feel like i cant stop once I start. I want to get off the roller coaster, but I dread what happens when the car stops."

Let us meet that dread kindly, because it is honest, and then pull it apart into its two true parts. What you fear is not one thing. It is two things tangled. A tiny bodily echo. And a huge belief that feeds it.

Once you see the two separately, you will never fear the tangle again.

### EARLY EVENING AND THE TWITCH

Come to the encounter that proves it. Not theory. An evening watched with open eyes.

Six o'clock. Dinner eaten two hours ago, or dinner still an hour away. Light going low. House softening. You sit, or you move about the kitchen, putting a cup down, picking a cloth up. Work done. Day easing. And low in the body a faint signal starts.

Not hunger. You know hunger now. Hunger builds slow and plain and welcomes bread and soup and fruit. This is different. Thin. Edgy. Hollow with an itch on it. A little emptiness with a twitch. A whisper that narrows to one taste only. It does not say food. It says sweet. Now.

For years we read that whisper as order. As need. As proof the body cannot run without a dose. We answered fast with something quick and sweet to get us through, felt a brief brightening for ten minutes, then felt the fog come down an hour later and called for more.

Watch it tonight another way. Do not answer yet. Do not argue yet. Look at it.

Note where it lives. Low, vague, under the ribs, behind attention more than in the stomach. Note how small it is. Not pain. Not illness. Not shaking collapse. A grumble. A nudge. A hollow prod. Note how it comes in waves. Strong for a minute, gone for ten, back for a minute. Note how your mind rushes to dress it in drama the moment it arrives, adding pictures of sleepless nights and snapping at loved ones and failure at work, though the feeling itself has not changed size at all.

That dressing is the whole trick. The feeling is tiny. The story about the feeling is enormous.

I state it as settled fact. The physical echo of BAD SUGAR leaving the body is trivial. Days-long, small-scale, passing. A mild dip after the flood, then gone. The terror you feel when you picture nights without doses does not come from that echo. It comes from belief laid over the echo like a loudspeaker over a whisper.

We will pull them apart now, once and for all, so you meet the evening with clear eyes. The little creature starves. The big belief must die first, and you kill it by seeing.

### THE DITCH AND THE HAND

There is a man you must see plainly to feel the inversion in your bones.

Every morning before light he waits by the lane you walk to work. Quiet shoes. Friendly face. As you pass, still half asleep, he steps behind you and shoves you hard between the shoulders. You fall full length into the wet ditch at the side of the road. Breath knocked out. Knee scraped. Coat muddied. Shock running through you.

Before you can rise he is there above you with his hand stretched down, voice kind and concerned.

"Poor friend. Terrible fall. Here, take my hand. Let me pull you out."

And you take it. You thank him. You feel genuine warmth toward him for his kindness. You walk on shaken, telling yourself what a lucky thing it was he happened to be there.

Next morning the same. Shove from behind. Fall. Kind hand. Thanks. Next morning the same. After a year you cannot picture the lane without him. You tell others about his goodness. You fear the day he might not come, because then who would lift you from the ditch?

Ask what that gratitude proves, and answer slowly.

Does his hand heal you? No. His hand returns you toward the road you were walking before he touched you. Does his rescue give you anything you would not have had if he had left you alone? No. Without him there would be no ditch to leave. Is he kind? He is filthy. He manufactures the distress he pretends to relieve, then collects thanks for the relief. He is thief and rescuer in one coat.

You see through him in a second when the picture is clean. Why? Because you saw push and pull together.

That is what a dose of BAD SUGAR does inside you. Each flood lifts, then drops you a little lower than before. Low goes with high as night goes with day. The next dose lifts you out of the low the last dose dug, toward the normal you would have owned all along without any dose. You thank the rescuer. You never charge the pusher.

The fact is, the lift is the relief of a low the last hit created. The low follows the high in the body as surely as the ditch follows the shove. I speak of the sound body in general; if you live with diabetes, take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry risk around food, follow your clinician first.

Feel what that does to gratitude. You were never lifted above normal. You were dropped below normal, then brought back toward normal, then dropped again. The rescuer and the perpetrator wear the same coat.

And here is the short verdict that ends the picture. He never helped you.

Once seen, the ditch cannot be unseen. Every bright ten minutes after a dose is a hand in the ditch. Every fog an hour later is the shove. Every call for more is the voice of the lane saying thank him again.

Be angry at the pusher, never at yourself. We all thanked him. I thanked him for years. We were conned in the lane before we knew the lane had an owner. Shame belongs to the fraud, never to the fallen. You were not weak in the ditch. You were pushed.

### THE TINY ECHO

Name the little part rightly and fear leaves it.

Inside the Sugar Trap there lives a small physical loop. Call it by its true name. It is the Nibbler.

He is not mighty. He is not cunning. He is not you. He is a tiny creature with one note. Feed me. He was born from floods, he lives on floods, and when floods stop he grumbles for a short while and then starves. His grumble is that empty, twitchy, slightly shaky, need-something-sweet-now feeling. Learn that string by heart, because you will meet the feeling again and you must know it on sight.

Understand his size or he will borrow size from your fear.

After a flood the body dips. Wanting circuits that fired bright cool below their ease for a while. Mood thins. Attention thins. Mouth remembers. That dip is real and measured, and I do not deny it. It is also small. Mild but well-defined, the sort of echo you would hardly remark if a loudspeaker were not telling you it means collapse. In animals the shape shows clearest, binge then dip then prod for more, and honest science says plainly that men and rats are not the same and human proof on that word is thin. I give you that honesty freely. It changes nothing. Whatever name laboratories argue over, binge behaviours under the yo-yo pattern happen, and you have lived them, and the shape is shove then hand, hand then shove.

Ask your own evenings for the measure.

If that echo were mighty, why does it come as vagueness rather than pain? If it were need, why can you forget it completely for an hour in talk or laughter or work, then remember it in a flash when a wrapper rustles? If it were collapse, why did you sleep through whole nights without feeding it and wake alive? If it runs your life, why does plain hunger welcome any meal while this echo narrows to sweet only?

There is only one reading that fits your life. The echo is tiny. The reading of the echo is huge.

Expect its calendar and you will smile when it comes. For a few days after floods stop, two to five in most mouths, the Nibbler grumbles. Not torment. Not illness. A hollow prod in the evening. A thin thought at the old hour. A little edginess looking for a name. Then quieter. Then gone. A time-limited hump, small-scale, not a life sentence. You do not serve it. You outlive it.

Think of shoes a size too small worn till midnight. That is all any lift ever was. Tight sweet shoes squeezed all day for the relief of kicking them off. Relief mistaken for pleasure.

I warn you with authority because the old voice will dress that grumble in medical drama. Do not let it. You are not fragile. You are not detoxing from poison in a clinic. You are a sound eater hearing a dying whisper. If you ever feel faint, ill, or distressed around food, seek care promptly and put argument aside till you are cleared. Apart from that care, hear the whisper for what it is and rejoice. A dying enemy does not need obeying.

And here is the question that closes the fear of the body. If the physical part is this small and this brief, what has kept you in slavery for years? It cannot be the Nibbler. He dies in days. It must be something that never dies unless killed. It is.

### THE LOUD VOICE

Name the big part rightly and you find the real target.

Over the Nibbler stands a belief. Call it by its true name. It is the Sweet Con.

He is not tiny. He is vast and old and well-dressed. He tells you BAD SUGAR is a genuine treat or fuel, love in a wrapper, reward after virtue, comfort after stress, brightness in grey days. He tells you evenings cannot close without a close, celebrations cannot shine without chocolate, afternoons cannot run without a fix. He tells you the grumble means need, the dip means danger, the hump means you cannot live without the lane and its kind hand.

He was not born in you. He was installed.

In seed, because full unmasking belongs to the next widening, hear who built him. Sweet talk at home when you were small, good means sweet and love means dessert. Laughter and cartoons selling bliss by the packet. Shops built to ambush, shelves at the eye of the young, paypoints lined with bright doses. Normal everywhere, so no one questions normal. Brainwashed years before you chose, conned before choice began. We all breathed him in. I breathed him. You breathed him. The air was sweetened before we learned to smell.

That Con feeds the Nibbler with meaning. Without the Con, the grumble would be a passing hollowness, hardly remarked, gone by the next hour. With the Con, the same hollowness reads as emergency. Need-something-sweet-now. Proof you function on sugar. Proof tonight will be unbearable. Proof tomorrow will be worse. The whisper arrives, the loudspeaker translates, the hand moves before decision.

See how cue firing serves him. Wrapper rustle before the bite. Smell from a bag opened across the room. Three o'clock on the clock. The sofa at eight. The film starting. The petrol station lights. Learned firings, not orders. The body learned when floods usually came and proded a little before the hour, the way the mouth waters before dinner. A firing is information. It compels nothing. The Con turns information into command by adding the old sentence, this means have it. Remove the sentence and the firing passes like a bell heard from another street.

Ask till the Con confesses.

Does a wrapper command a hand, or does belief about the wrapper move the hand? If smell compelled eating, why do non-users smell the same sweet air with indifference or distaste? If three o'clock drove dosing by clockwork, why does the same hour pass clean on holidays when hunger is trusted and attention is full? If the firing were need, why does knowing it is dying make it flattering rather than frightening?

There is only one pattern that fits. Firing proposes. Belief disposes. Kill belief and the body echo starves unfed.

That is the mechanism whole. Trivial physical loop fed by dominant belief. Starve the belief and the loop has nothing to live on. You do not battle the Nibbler with grit. You do not white-knuckle evenings. You see the Sweet Con talking, you refuse his translation, you smile at a dying grumble, and you get on with enjoying your life.

And here is the flat verdict on the two together. The ache is small. The story is false.

### WHY YOU FEARED THE UNBEARABLE

Voice the strongest objection fully so it cannot return whispering.

"But I have felt that evening feeling take me over. I told myself one square, one biscuit, and the box emptied before the programme ended. If the physical part is trivial, why do I lose all control once I start? Surely that proves mighty need."

We all lived that box. I lived it. Hand moving in lamplight. Rustle louder than voices on the screen. Promise made to the first square broken to the second. Regret arriving before swallowing ends. Morning shame. That collapse felt like proof of power in the substance.

It proves the opposite. Ask and answer from your own sofa.

If one square satisfied need, why did need grow with feeding? True hunger softens as food arrives. Why did this hunger sharpen as doses arrived? If the first bite met the body, why did the body call louder for the tenth? If control lived in the dose, why did the most controlled eaters lose control exactly when they permitted a little?

There is only one reading that honours the box. The craving once triggered carries the binge. The loss of control is the signature of the Sugar Trap, not the measure of your will. One dose re-fires want, want borrows the Con's old sentence, the sentence orders another dose. Push, hand, push, hand, faster, till sleep or sickness or empty cardboard stops the lane. You never lacked strength. You fed a loop that grows by feeding.

Feel how light that reversal is. You are not a person with mighty need and weak will. You are a sound eater with a tiny echo and a huge implanted reading. Remove the reading and the echo has no orders to give. The box empties no more because no first square is ordered to open it.

Consider what this means for the nights you dread. You feared nights without sweet would be unbearable because nights with sweet felt unbearable in their own coated way, restless, hunting, never closed. You mistook the pain of the lane for the pain of leaving the lane. Leaving is quiet. Staying is noise.

Will the grumble come? Yes, for a short while, faint and wavy. Will it grow till you break? No. unfed loops shrink. What grows with attention is belief. Withdraw attention from belief and the feeling has nowhere to grow. You will note the twitch, name it rightly, smile, and find ten minutes later you forgot to remember it. That forgetting is the Nibbler starving. Rejoice each time you catch it.

Do you want proof you already know how to do this? Recall the peach. The small eater takes fruit hungrily, stops mid-bite, runs off laughing, juice on the chin, half the peach abandoned without loss. No counting. No mourning. Hunger met, satisfaction obeyed, attention called louder by play. That child lives in you still. Hunger and satisfaction proved their office at the market table. Let them prove it at night. Evening hunger welcomes dinner. Satisfaction closes dinner. The grumble after is not hunger calling. It is echo dying.

You entered fearing physical need. You leave knowing the physical part is a mild hump and the real target is belief. The Con dies by seeing. The Nibbler dies by starving. Seeing is your work. Starving happens on its own.

### 7. SEE THE GRUMBLE AS DYING, NOT DEMANDING
That feeling is the Nibbler starving.

**SUMMARY**
- The evening twitch is small and passing, not mighty need.
- Each lift only returns you toward the normal the last dose took.
- The loud dread comes from an implanted reading, not from the body.
- Faint signals, smells and old hours propose, they never command.
- One permitted bite re-fires want and carries the box with it.
- Hunger welcomes food and closes glad, the echo narrows to sweet and never closes.
- Noted and smiled at, the grumble fades in days and leaves freedom.
