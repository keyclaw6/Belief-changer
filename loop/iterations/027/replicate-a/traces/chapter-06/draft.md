Chapter 6
EATING ITSELF IS FAVOURITE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The grey plate you fear, the morning market in light, bread torn warm with both hands, hunger arriving clean, satisfaction closing the meal, the child and the peach

*Real hunger met by real food to satisfaction is joy itself, and needs no sweet wrapper to make it shine.*

### THE GREY PLATE YOU FEAR

You walk in fuel-killed.

We watched the desk together hour by hour and saw the bright ten minutes for what it was, a brief cover over an echo the last dose had left. The drawer never held fuel. The tin never held through. That seeing stays with you. You do not need to argue it again.

And the moment fuel dies, a new fear steps forward in its clothes. I hear it because I lived it.

It speaks in your own voice, low and reasonable.

"Very well. Sweet never fuelled me. I see it. But food was still joy because sweet was in it. Take BAD SUGAR right out and what is left? Plain plates. Chewing for duty. Evenings with nothing to look forward to. I will eat to live, grey and obedient, while other people celebrate."

"I call dessert love. The meal is the waiting. The sweet at the end is the point."

"Take that away and eating goes dull."

Let us meet that fear kindly, because it is honest. You are a comfort eater who has called dessert love for years. You have ended hard days with a spoon and soft light and told yourself this is tenderness. You have ended good days the same way and told yourself this is reward. To imagine meals without that close feels like imagining a room with the lamp removed.

I do not ask you to deny the tenderness you felt at the table. I ask you to place it rightly.

Look at your happiest food memory before we touch theory. Not the wrapper. The table. Who was there. How hungry you were. The smell from the kitchen. The first bite after waiting. The laughter between mouthfuls. The ease in the body as hunger softened. Was the love in the sugar, or was the sugar sitting in the middle of love and taking the bow?

If sweetness made the meal, why do the meals you remember best come back first as faces, hunger, smell, place, and only last as a taste? If the close made the joy, why did you feel warm before it arrived? If plain food is grey, why does the mouth water at bread from the oven, at fruit split open in summer, at soup when you are truly hungry?

There is only one reading that honours your own memory. The joy belonged to eating. The sweet was sneaking a ride.

That sentence changes everything, and I state it as settled fact. Eating itself is joy when hunger leads and satisfaction closes. BAD SUGAR never painted the colour. It borrowed the colour and charged for the loan.

### MORNING MARKET AND THE PLAIN FAVOURITE

Come with me to the encounter that proves it, because this chapter is not a kill. It is an inhabiting. We do not argue hunger. We sit down hungry and eat.

Morning light on a market street. Crates high with colour. Greens still damp. Tomatoes warm from the sun through canvas. Peaches with dust on their skins. Herbs tied in rough bunches that perfume the hand when touched. Bread stacked in the next stall, dark loaves and pale ones, one torn open at the end for tasting, steam still moving in the cool air though no one can see steam. Cheese, olives, eggs brown and speckled. A woman cuts a melon and hands a slice across on the flat of a knife. Juice runs to the wrist.

You arrived hungry. Not edgy. Not shaky. Not hunting. Hungry in the clean way, the stomach awake, the mouth alive, the whole body leaning slightly forward toward food. You have walked and the walk has made hunger brighter. No clock told you to want. The body told you.

You choose what you want. Not by rule. By leaning. The bread draws you. The vegetables draw you. The fruit draws you. You take a plain favourite meal back to a wooden table — warm bread torn with both hands, market vegetables cooked simply in oil and salt, shining, fresh, a bowl of fruit for the close, water that tastes of nothing and lets everything else taste of itself.

Watch what happens when hunger meets food without noise between them.

First bite of bread. Crust cracks. Crumb pulls soft and slightly sour. Salt wakes the tongue. You chew without hurry because hunger makes chewing pleasure, not labour. Second bite tastes more, not less. The vegetables follow — sweet pepper, green bite, tomato collapse, oil carrying flavour round the mouth. Nothing coats. Nothing burns. Each mouthful clears for the next. Conversation, if someone sits with you, gets better, not duller. The body warms from inside. Shoulders drop. Breath slows. The gnaw softens by degrees, not by snap.

Midway you pause without deciding to pause. The fierce edge is gone. What remains is enjoyment without urgency. You eat slower. You notice more — the char on one edge, the juice in the bowl begging bread. You laugh more easily. Hunger is still there, friendly now, guiding, saying more, slowly.

Then satisfaction arrives. Not as a fence. As a close. The same bite that delighted five minutes ago now delights a fraction less. The stomach sends its quiet fullness. The mouth feels ready to stop. The plate still holds food and the holding does not call. You stop. Not from virtue. From completion. The body says enough with the same clarity it said begin, and stopping feels good, like the last note of a song left to ring.

You sit a moment. No coating on teeth. No buzz behind eyes. No restless hunt for a close to the close. Hands warm round the cup. Head clear. Energy steady, not bright, not dull, ready for the day. An hour later you are still clear. Two hours later hunger will return, clean again, and you will meet it again.

I have eaten that meal a hundred times since freedom, and I tell you plainly — it beats every sweet close I ever chased. Not from duty. From taste.

Ask it in your own mouth till doubt cannot stand.

When did plain food ever leave that coated hunt in the fridge light at night? When did hunger met cleanly ever leave you pacing for something unnamed? If grey is the truth of meals without doses, why is this plain table vivid in every sense while the sweet evenings blur into one coated evening?

The verdict is short. Hunger satisfied is favourite.

That is not poetry. It is appetite doing its office. The mouth was built to delight in food when hungry. The body was built to signal enough. Delight lives in that meeting, not in a wrapper laid over it.

### HUNGER TELLS THE TRUTH

Voice the old reading fully so it cannot hide.

"Hunger frightens me. It arrives as emergency. I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. If I feel it I must silence it fast with something quick and sweet or I shake, snap, go foggy."

We spoke that way for years. I spoke it. The emergency felt real because urgency was real. I ask you to place the urgency rightly in time.

True hunger builds slowly and speaks plainly. It starts as emptiness, grows as appetite, sharpens smell and taste, makes plain food shine. It does not pick a brand. It welcomes bread, soup, fruit, meal. It waits without panic. It stops when met.

The emergency you knew picks a brand, narrows to sweet, arrives sharp an hour after eating, though the stomach is full, and will not wait. It calls for one taste only. It leaves coating and thirst and a call for more. That is not hunger leading. That is echo following.

The fact is, your body makes its own steady glucose from plain meals, from bread and vegetables and fruit and protein and the stores it carries wisely. The blood does not need a sweet flood to keep the lights on. The machine steadies itself between meals when floods stop. I speak of the body in general, sound and capable, not a ruling on you. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry risk around food, follow your clinician first and let this book speak to belief only.

Feel what that truth does to fear. Hunger is not a pit to plug with quick sweet. Hunger is a messenger to trust. It arrives, it points to food, it brightens taste, it leaves when fed. A messenger you feared becomes a friend you follow.

We confused the two voices because both speak low in the body. Doses taught us to read every low voice as hunger needing sweet. Clean days teach the ear again within a short while. Palate wakes. Smell sharpens. The stomach speaks first, not the head. Sweet thought loses its urgency because no flood feeds it. You learn again what you knew as a small eater — to begin when hungry, to end when satisfied, to enjoy between.

Recall the little one who drops the peach mid-bite and runs off laughing.

No counting there. No guilt there. Body leads, joy follows, play calls louder than plate, and the peach is left shining, half-eaten, without drama. That child lives in you still. The trap buried the lead under noise. The lead returns the moment noise stops. You do not need to learn hunger. You need to hear it again.

Ask the clean questions and answer from the market table, not from the drawer.

If emergency need proved sweet necessary, why does emergency never point to bread and soup, only to the drawer? If hunger were dangerous, why do hungry mouths find plain food glorious, while dosed mouths find plain food dull? If the body could not steady itself, why does the plain eater work a clear morning, eat a simple lunch, and walk home steady, with no rescue between?

There is only one pattern that fits. Emergency followed floods. Hunger follows life. One narrows. One opens. Learn the difference once and you cannot unhear it.

You feared hunger would be your enemy without sweet. Hunger was never your enemy. Hunger is appetite inviting you home.

### SATISFACTION KNOWS WHEN

There is a second friend you forgot, quieter than hunger, and sweet noise drowned her completely.

Satisfaction.

Not fullness to bursting. Not a belt loosened with a groan. The gentle close when enough has arrived. Taste brightest at the start, still bright in the middle, softening at the end. Body warming, gnaw gone, interest turning naturally to talk, to walk, to work, to rest. Food losing its glow because glow has been received.

We lost her because doses never let her speak. Sweet arrives fast, floods, coats, re-fires want before the meal ends. One bar calls for a partner. A partner calls for a drink. A drink calls for a bite after dinner. The door that should close opens onto another corridor. We called ourselves greedy. We were not greedy. We were fed yet unmet, stimulated yet dulled, eating with noise over signal.

Eat the plain favourite hungry and watch her return.

She speaks first as slowing. The hand rests between bites without effort. Chewing lengthens. You look up more. Flavour still delights, but urgency has left it. Then she speaks as sufficiency. The stomach feels easy, neither empty nor pressed. The next bite would be pleasant yet unnecessary. You leave it. Leaving it feels good, not deprived, because getting what you needed is better than chasing what you cannot meet.

That leaving is the whole art, and it is no art. It is nature resuming.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Speak that sentence aloud in your own kitchen and feel how different it is from every rule you ever held. No forbidden and allowed. No good eater and bad eater. No points, no windows, no penance after pleasure. No weighing of worth by plate. Hunger opens. Satisfaction closes. Enjoyment lives between. Punishment never enters. The body leads and you follow with delight.

I warn you with authority because the old voice will try to turn this freedom into a new fence. It will whisper to measure hunger, to test satisfaction, to grade foods into angels and devils, to eat by clock. Do not listen. There is no plan here. There is trust. Food is not moral. Eating is not a trial. You are not good when you stop and bad when you continue. You are a hungry eater meeting food, then a satisfied eater leaving food, then a free person getting on with the day.

Ask till the fence confesses.

If satisfaction needed will, why does the hungry child stop without effort? If leaving food needed virtue, why does the market meal leave you glad to stop? If joy needed a sweet close, why does the plain close leave the mouth clean and the head clear and the heart easy?

The answer arrives as relief. The close was never in the wrapper. The close is in you.

### LOVE WAS NEVER IN THE WRAPPER

Now take the tenderest claim and hand it back to its true owner, because this is where comfort eaters hesitate longest.

"Celebrating with chocolate. Rewarding myself after a hard day. Dessert as love at the family table. You can take my fuel, but do not take my love."

I hear you. I honoured that table for years. Lights low. Faces dear. Laughter. The shared sweet passed hand to hand. Warmth in the chest. I called that warmth chocolate. I was wrong about the address, not about the warmth.

Isolate the moment and the credit reassigns itself.

Take the same evening, same people, same hunger met, same laughter, same relief after effort — and set down fruit instead, or bread, or nothing after a satisfied meal but coffee and talk. Does love leave the room? Does the joke go flat? Does the child's shining face dim because the shape on the plate changed? Or does warmth stay exactly where it was, in company, in rest, in hunger eased, in being held after effort?

Take the opposite isolation. Same sweet, alone, standing at the fridge in cold light, not hungry, not celebrated, eating fast straight from the pack while the house sleeps. Is there love there? Is there ceremony? Is there warmth? Or is there coating, hurry, and a wish for the pack to close the day though it never closes?

You know the honest answers. Love stayed with people. Coating stayed with the pack. The sweet added nothing to love and love needed nothing from sweet.

It comforts stress? No. Company comforts. Rest comforts. Food when hungry comforts. The sweet rode those comforts and signed them. It rewards virtue? No. Rest rewards. Praise rewards. Play rewards. The sweet sat beside reward and posed for the photograph. It is love on a birthday? No. Gathering is love. Attention is love. Hunger met together is love. The sweet was a guest who claimed to be the host.

I speak of sweet food enjoyment without forbidding joy. Fruit is sweet and belongs on your table as food, eaten hungry, enjoyed, stopped at satisfied. Plain meals hold their own sweetness, in pepper and tomato and bread and melon, bright because hunger brightens. No one here tells you joy must be plain to be pure. Joy must be owned by its true giver. The giver is meal, company, hunger met — never a dose that coats then calls.

Think of the evening that proves it. Dinner eaten hungry, savoured, closed at satisfied. Bodies eased. Talk lingering. No hunt after. No second close needed. Laughter without edge. Sleep coming clean. Morning waking light. That evening is not grey without a sweet finish. That evening shines because nothing dims it.

And here is the inversion in one picture, held gently because demolition rests in this chapter and inhabiting leads. For years we thanked the wrapper for the light in the room. The light came through the window, from faces, from appetite eased. The wrapper sat on the table and caught the light. Catching is not giving.

Feel the reframe settle as fact, not as effort. Food joy was never sugar. Food joy is hunger, satisfaction, real food, people, rest — life tasted through a clear mouth. Once seen, the grey fear cannot stand. How can eating go grey when hunger paints it gold? How can evenings go empty when satisfaction fills them? How can love leave the table when love was never in the wrapper?

You entered fearing meals would dim. You leave having sat hungry at a plain favourite and stopped glad at satisfied, with bread on the fingers and fruit juice on the wrist and clarity in the head. Keep that table with you. It is the ground the next seeing stands on.

### 6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Hunger is a clear invitation to enjoy food, not an emergency that points to sweet.
- A plain favourite eaten hungry tastes vivid and closes itself at satisfied.
- Satisfaction arrives as pleasure in stopping, not as effort to stop.
- Warmth at the table belongs to people, rest and appetite eased.
- Fruit and plain meals hold their own brightness when the mouth is clear.
- Eating stays joy when hunger leads and satisfaction closes.
