Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — bright kitchen at seven, bread and fruit on a plain table, supermarket aisle at noon, checkout queue with bright packets, evening sofa with hot tea, quiet street with bakery smell

*You are already free, so mornings, shops and meals feel simple and light.*

### THE FIRST MORNING TASTES CLEAR

You wake with sweet taste washed from your mouth and a clear head on your shoulders.

No tin to open before thought starts. No debate in the kitchen. No promise made to a mirror. You sleep through the old prowling hour and rise hungry, truly hungry, the clean hollow that asks for breakfast.

Make breakfast and eat it hungry. Fresh bread. Fruit. A plain warm meal that fills you and leaves you satisfied. Taste it. Real hunger makes plain food sing. The mouth wakes up. The head clears while you chew. Energy comes up quiet and steady and stays to do the day.

We used to wake fogged and reach for a bright packet to lift the fog the night packet left behind. We called that fog morning and the packet breakfast. We chased a blaze that thinned with each bite and left a coat on the tongue before the day began. You know that chase now for what it was. A wheel turning before feet touched floor.

That wheel has stopped. Hunger leads now. Satisfaction closes the meal. The body knows its work and does it gladly.

I woke on my first free morning and laughed at how little had changed and how much had changed. Same kettle. Same chair. Same light through the same window. Only the rustle gone. Only the reach gone. Only the low hum of bargaining gone. Porridge tasted sweet with nothing added. An apple cracked loud and clean. I ate till satisfied, put the bowl down mid-appetite gone, and walked out bright. Like a child with a peach who stops mid-bite and runs off laughing, the body said enough and joy obeyed.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician and follow their advice first.

You change nothing else in your life. Same walk to work. Same cup of tea. Same people at the table. Freedom does not ask for a new kitchen, a new shop, a new self. Freedom asks only that seeing stays clear. Eat when hungry. Stop when satisfied. Drink water when thirsty. Rest when tired. Meet true signals straight and let false signals starve unfed.

Do you feel a faint stirring as the old hour comes round? A light gnaw, a vague reach? Smile. That is not need. That is the Nibbler dying. Its grumble grows thin when unfed and passes in minutes while you get on with enjoying your life. Note it. Welcome its dying. Keep hands on breakfast and mind on the morning. Minutes pass. Freedom stays.

The fact is, mornings without BAD SUGAR are not empty. Mornings with BAD SUGAR were empty and blurred. You have not lost brightness. Brightness has returned to you.

### THE SHOP AT NOON

At noon you walk the same aisle you always walked. Bright packs stand shoulder to shoulder. Cartoon faces shout. Checkout shelves crowd close with small bars built to catch a tired hand.

Walk through easy. You have no vote to cast here.

We once walked this aisle as hunters and hunted both at once. Eyes pulled to paper we swore we would pass. Hands slow by the shelf. Head full of arithmetic — allowed, earned, good all week, just this once, Friday only. The heart beat a little fast at the rustle. The basket filled before decision formed. That was not choice. That was circling inside the Sugar Trap, with desire manufactured long before we arrived.

You see the aisle now with clear eyes. Bliss-point packs engineered to blaze then coat. Smells pumped to pull. Colours tuned to shout over hunger. Anger belongs to the makers, never shame to you. Poor engineers of craving, still oiling a wheel you have stepped off. You pity the trap and the hands still caught, you do not envy them.

Let others load their baskets. Feel relief, not longing. Does that trolley look like enjoyment? A grown adult scanning labels for permission, weighing a bar against a week of being good, promising tomorrow while reaching today. That is not pleasure. That is slavery in bright paper. You are happy to be free of it.

I stood in that queue on my second free day behind a man with three bars balanced on milk. He turned them over, put one back, took it again, smiled thin at me as if we shared a secret. We once shared that secret. I felt no pull to join him. I felt a warm ache that he still must. I paid for bread, cheese, apples, left the lights and noise behind, and stepped out marvellous light. No fight fought. No victory clutched. Only clarity doing its quiet work.

When the smell from a bakery doorway crosses you, when a wrapper flashes by a bench, when lights dimming at the cinema once meant chocolate in hand, let the memory cross and pass. A memory is not an order. A smell is not a command. A bright packet is not proof of joy. Joy lived in company, in laughter, in hunger met, never in the dose that sneaked a ride.

What rises in your chest at the sight? Relief. A clean lift. A glad knowing that you no longer feed the wheel. Think at once, loudly, gladly:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the head. Say it aloud when you can. Let it arrive with a smile. The thought converts to celebration the second it is reframed. You do not push the thought away. You cannot push a thought away by ordering it gone. You answer it with joy and it leaves you brighter than it found you.

Ask the aisle plainly and answer from your ribs.

If those packs gave energy, why did evenings fed with them end fogged on the sofa? If those bars gave reward, why did reward always ask for another within the hour? If passing them by were loss, why does passing them by feel like relief?

You know the answer. Loss never lived here. Escape lives here.

### THE EVENING SOFA

Evening was the stronghold. Chair. Film. Low light. Hand to mouth while mind went soft.

Live this evening in the same chair with new seeing.

Eat supper hungry. Cook plain food you love. Market vegetables. Fresh bread. Meat, fish, beans, rice as you like them. Fruit after that tastes bright when hunger has been met true. Eat till satisfied and stop. Satisfaction is deep and still. It does not rustle. It does not prowl. It does not send you back to the cupboard before credits end.

Then sit. Hot tea in your hand. Warmth in the room. Talk, film, book, quiet — take what you always took, minus the blur. Notice how much more of the film you follow. Notice laughter arriving clear. Notice hands still and mouth clean. Notice the hour after supper staying kind, no hollow, no second reach, no regret before swallow ends.

We feared evenings would stretch grey and long without grazing. The other way round was true. Grazing made evenings grey — head fogged, body heavy, attention split between screen and packet, joy thinned with each chew. Freedom hands the evening back whole.

I spent my first free evening waiting for the old gnaw to roar. It knocked once, light, at half past eight, by the old chair. I noted it. Named it dying. Rejoiced that it died. Made tea. By the time the cup cooled the knock had gone and did not return. Hunger never came that night because supper had satisfied. Thirst came and water met it. Tiredness came and bed met it. Straight signals, straight answers, no wheel between.

If a wave comes to you, do the same. Note it. Name it: Nibbler dying. Drink if thirsty. Eat if hungry till satisfied. Rest if tired. Keep eyes on the film and heart on the room. The wave peaks and thins whether fed or not. Unfed, it never returns with the same voice. Rejoice while it passes. Relief is available now, tonight, in this chair.

Do old tapes play? A hard day may slide a kind voice forward: you deserve one, life is hard, a little comfort will help. Hear it cold. That voice is not wisdom. That voice is an old recording. Did a dose ever soften a hard day? It blurred the evening and lent the morning a heavier head. Comfort lives in rest, in food that satisfies, in company, in trouble met with clear head. Pity trapped users still answering the tape. Turn to your own evening with relief.

A bright day may slide a clever voice forward: one to celebrate, just to taste, to show mastery. Hear it colder. A free eater does not prove freedom with a dose. A free eater proves freedom by having no question to answer — pity for the circling, relief in the chest, ease in the aisle.

You do not need to be on guard all night. Guarding is not vigilance. Seeing is rest. You do not patrol thoughts. You answer them once with joy and live on.

### IF A CRUMB FINDS YOU

Hear me on slips, so no accident can grow teeth.

Your body can cope with an accidental blip — sauce sweetened without your knowing, a bite taken before you saw. Your mind must not steer toward it. Note the rumble. Straighten the seeing. Drive on happy to be free.

A crumb unchosen revives nothing unless belief is let back in. Do not wail. Do not confess. Do not start counting from zero. Shame is a relapse engine and you have left shame with the Trap. Wash the mouth with water. Eat next when hungry. Think BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! and mean it — the wheel did not restart because dust touched it.

But never book accident as permission. A chosen one is not a crumb. A chosen one nurses fantasy, rehearses anticipation, fattens glow for Thursday toward Friday, and wakes the very echo you starved. Got away with it never means do it again. Rumble strip never means turn the car toward the ditch.

Ask the slip plainly if it brushes you.

Did the sweetened sauce taste like love? Did the unchosen bite lift the day? Did joy rise with the coat on the tongue, or did attention catch blaze, then thick, then sicken, then hollow? You already took your last ordinary treat with eyes open. You saw the ugliness under shine. One blind bite cannot unsee what seeing saw — unless you hand belief back its keys.

You will not hand them back. Belief dead means war over. Keep belief dead and the body follows glad.

I tell you this not to frighten you. Fright never freed anyone. I tell you so you walk light, knowing the road holds no hidden cliff. The seat of your freedom is seeing, not perfect footsteps. Seeing holds for anyone who guards it once.

### LEAVE THEM TO IT AND LIVE

Do not preach. Do not convert the house.

Fresh freedom wants to shout from the table and rescue all it loves. Hold your joy quiet and let ease do the talking. Lecture breeds defence. Defence reopens debate in your own head. Debate feeds the very doubt you starved.

When someone unwraps beside you, pity, do not envy. Poor soul, still paying to blur what freedom meets straight. When someone says you have amazing will, smile and tell the truth plain: no iron was needed, only seeing. When someone offers cake with kindness, answer kind and plain: no thank you, I am happy without it. No speech. No scorn. No fear of offence. A free eater needs no excuse and gives no lesson unless asked with open heart.

Change nothing else. Same parties. Same birthdays. Same cinema seats. Same tired Tuesdays and bright Saturdays. Inhabit every old context confident and prove freedom where the Trap once ruled. Laughter needs no wrapper. Company needs no packet. Hunger needs real food and satisfaction closes it. The body returns to its natural ease on its own when the wheel stops forcing it round.

We spent years rearranging life around doses — drawers stocked, routes planned past good shops, evenings timed to grazing, mornings timed to lifting. That rearrangement was the slavery, not the cure. Freedom rearranges nothing. Freedom removes the rearranger.

Look at the week ahead with excitement, not caution. Mornings bright. Shops easy. Meals eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied. Work met with clear head. Evenings owned whole. Money drip stopped. Hours circling returned. Teeth spared acid. Head spared fog. All bonus beside the great gain: no more wheel, no more debate, no more hollow asking for more.

You entered this book fearing life would demand watchfulness forever. Watchfulness is the old method talking. Freedom is not a sentence to serve. Freedom is an identity to enjoy. Thoughts will cross — wrapper, smell, old chair — and each crossing becomes a small festival when answered with joy. The dead enemy knocks from habit. Rejoice that it is dead.

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

Speak that when the thought comes and walk on light. The decision was made with clear eyes on an ordinary evening and needs no second hearing. Doubt is not wisdom returning. Doubt is habit pacing by an open door. Smile at the pacing. It passes.

I congratulate you not for effort but for seeing. You did not climb. You stepped out. Mornings prove it. Shops prove it. Supper proves it. The sofa proves it. Each ordinary day lived free writes the truth deeper till thought itself turns glad by nature.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

**SUMMARY**
- Mornings are clear and hunger is clean, breakfast eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied leaves brightness for the day.
- Shops are easy to walk with no vote to cast, bright packets seen as engineered noise while relief answers pity for trapped hands.
- Evenings stay whole in the same chair, supper satisfies and tea warms while film and laughter carry the joy.
- A passing memory is answered at once with joy and never pushed away, the thought becomes celebration.
- An unchosen crumb revives nothing when seeing stays straight, a chosen one is never booked as permission.
- Freedom is lived by changing nothing else, with no preaching and no patrol, ordinary days proving ease.
