Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — dawn kitchen with kettle steam and plain bread, bright noon aisle with shouting packets, checkout belt with bread and apples, hot supper table with market vegetables, evening chair with tea going cool, bakery doorway on a quiet street

*You are free already, so an ordinary morning, an ordinary shop and an ordinary meal prove it.*

### THE FIRST MORNING TASTES CLEAR

You wake with a clear head and a clean mouth.

No rustle before thought. No bargain before feet touch floor. No tin calling from the kitchen while you promise the mirror a better day. The old prowling hour passed in sleep and left no mark.

Hunger comes, honest and hollow, asking for breakfast. Not a gnaw for brightness. Not a twitch for paper-wrapped blaze. A true emptiness that food meets and leaves.

Make breakfast and eat it hungry. Fresh bread with butter melting in. Fruit cracked open and juicy. Porridge warm to the ribs. Eggs, cheese, plain yogurt — take what you love from real food. Taste it. Real hunger makes plain food sing. The mouth wakes. The head lifts quiet and steady and stays to do the day.

We used to wake fogged and call the fog normal. We poured a bright packet onto the fog to lift the fog the night packet left behind, and we called that packet breakfast. The first bite blazed, the second blazed less, the third left a coat on the tongue before work began, and by ten the head hummed for more. We called that hum morning. It was never morning. It was the wheel turning before the day began.

That wheel has stopped. Hunger leads now. Satisfaction closes the meal. The body knows its work and does it gladly.

I woke on my first free morning and laughed at how little had changed and how much had changed. Same kettle. Same chair. Same light through the same pane. Only the rustle gone. Only the reach gone. Only the low bargaining gone. Porridge tasted faintly sweet with nothing added. An apple cracked loud and clean and the juice ran down my chin. I ate till the hollow filled and the pleasure settled, put the spoon down, and walked out bright. Like a small child with a peach who eats hungry and stops mid-bite and runs off laughing, the body said enough and joy obeyed.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician and follow their advice first.

You change nothing else in your life. Same walk to work. Same cup of tea. Same people round the table. Freedom asks for no new kitchen and no new self. Freedom asks only that seeing stays clear. Eat when hunger calls. Stop when satisfaction settles. Drink water when thirsty. Rest when tired. Meet true signals straight and let false signals starve unfed.

Does a faint stirring come as the old hour comes round? A light gnaw, a vague reach for something bright? Smile. That is not need. That is the Nibbler dying. Its grumble grows thin when unfed and passes in minutes while you get on with enjoying your life. Note it. Welcome its dying. Keep hands on breakfast and mind on the morning. Minutes pass. Freedom stays.

The fact is, mornings with BAD SUGAR were empty and blurred. Mornings without it are not empty. You have lost no brightness. Brightness has come back to you.

Ask the morning plainly and answer from your ribs.

If the bright packet gave energy, why did packet mornings always sag by ten? If the sweet start gave joy, why did joy need a second start an hour later? If this clear hunger is loss, why does loss feel so light and clean?

You know the answer. The packet never fed the morning. The morning feeds you now.

### THE SHOP AT NOON

At noon you walk the same aisle you always walked. Tall shelves either side. Bright packs shoulder to shoulder. Cartoon faces shouting. Small bars crowded round the till to catch a tired hand.

Walk through easy. You have no vote to cast here.

We once walked this aisle as hunter and hunted both at once. Eyes pulled to paper we swore we would pass. Feet slow by the shelf. Head full of arithmetic — been good all week, earned one, allowed one, Friday only, just this once. Heart beating a little fast at the rustle. Basket filling before decision formed. That was not choice. That was circling inside the Sugar Trap, with desire built long before we arrived, by bright paper and bliss-point blaze and repetition till repetition felt like nature.

You see the aisle now with clear eyes. Blaze engineered to shout over hunger. Smell pumped to pull. Colour tuned to promise love and rest and fuel in a wrapper. Anger belongs to the makers, never shame to you. They oiled a wheel you have stepped off. You pity the hands still caught. You do not envy them.

Let others load their baskets. Feel relief, not longing. Watch close. Does that trolley look like enjoyment? A grown adult turning a bar over for permission, weighing paper against a week of being good, promising tomorrow while reaching today, smiling thin as if sharing a secret. That is not pleasure. That is slavery in bright paper. You are happy to be free of it.

I stood in that queue on my second free day behind a man with three bars balanced on milk. He put one back, took it again, put it back, took it again, laughed short at himself. We once shared that dance. I felt no pull to join him. I felt a warm ache that he still must. I paid for bread, cheese, apples, fish for supper, left the lights and noise behind, and stepped out marvellous light. No battle fought. No prize clutched. Only clarity doing its quiet work.

When a wrapper flashes by a bench, when bakery air crosses the pavement, when the till shines at tired eyes, let the memory cross and pass. A memory is not an order. A smell is not a command. A bright packet is not proof of joy. Joy lived in company, in laughter, in hunger met true, never in the dose that sneaked a ride. The lights dimming at the cinema once meant chocolate in hand, and the warmth in the room got credited to the bar while the film and the laughter did all the work.

What rises in your chest at the sight? Relief. A clean lift. A glad knowing that you no longer feed the wheel. Think at once, loudly, gladly:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the head. Say it aloud when you can. Let it arrive with a smile. You do not push the thought away. You cannot push a thought away by ordering it gone. You answer it with joy and it leaves you brighter than it found you. The dead enemy knocks from habit. Rejoice that it is dead.

Ask the aisle plainly and answer from your ribs.

If those packs gave energy, why did evenings fed with them end fogged on the sofa? If those bars gave reward, why did reward always ask for another within the hour? If passing them by were loss, why does passing them by feel like relief?

You know the answer. Loss never lived here. Escape lives here.

You buy plain food you love and carry it home light. No arithmetic at the belt. No secret added under the bread. No promise to start again Monday. The shop is a shop again. The food is food again. You are you again, only clearer.

### THE MEAL THAT SATISFIES

Hunger is your friend now. Satisfaction is your signal. Both were drowned for years under blaze and coat. Both return fast when the wheel stops forcing them round.

Cook supper hungry. Keep it plain and loved. Market vegetables bright in the pan. Fresh bread torn, not counted. Meat, fish, beans, rice, potatoes — take what your house loves. Soup steaming. Salad crisp. Fruit after that tastes bright when true hunger has been met first. Eat till the hollow fills and pleasure settles deep and still, then stop. Satisfaction does not rustle. It does not prowl. It does not send you back to the cupboard before the plates are cleared.

We feared food would turn grey without bright packets. The other way round was true. Bright packets turned food grey. They coated the tongue, dulled hunger, blurred satisfaction, and left us flat, foggy and never satisfied an hour later, reaching again for brightness to lift the flatness brightness left. Real meals waited patient while we chased paper, and we called the chase appetite. It was never appetite. It was echo fed by belief.

Taste now with a clean tongue. Notice how hunger seasons. Notice how the first mouthfuls blaze in their own way — bread crust, apple snap, broth warmth — and how the blaze deepens toward fullness rather than thinning toward sickness. Notice how stopping feels, not stuffed, not punished, simply done. The body says enough. Joy obeys. No counting. No bargaining. No good-all-week arithmetic humming under the chew.

I ate my first free supper slow and kept noticing. Carrots sweet without shine. Bread chewy and honest. Fish flaky and full. I waited for the old call for something after, the prowl that used to start before swallow ended. It knocked once, light, and passed while I drank water. I ate an orange after, section by section, juice on my fingers, and put the peel down satisfied. No hollow followed. No second reach rose. The evening stayed kind.

Do you hear the old tape that food is love and love needs a wrapper? Hear it cold. Love never lived in paper. Love lived in the table, the talk, the hands cooking, the hunger met together. A birthday table glows because people gather and laugh and mark a life, never because a glaze sits on a sponge. The dose only sneaked a ride and charged for the journey. The scene gave everything. The packet gave nothing.

Ask the plate plainly and answer from your ribs.

If sweetness at the end completed the meal, why did completed meals always unravel into grazing? If the packet crowned hunger, why did crowned hunger never rest? If plain food is dull, why does dull food leave you bright and settled for hours?

You know the answer. The crown was paste. The meal is king.

Meet hunger straight. Meet thirst with water. Meet tiredness with bed. Straight signals, straight answers, no wheel between. The body is a precision instrument when belief stops shouting over it. Trust it and it carries you. You do not need a new menu and a new rule. You need the old hunger and the old stop, heard clear at last.

### THE EVENING SOFA

Evening was the stronghold. Chair. Film. Low light. Hand to mouth while mind went soft.

Live this evening in the same chair with new seeing.

Eat supper hungry as you have learned, till satisfied, then sit. Hot tea in your hand. Warmth in the room. Talk, film, book, quiet — take what you always took, minus the blur. Notice how much more of the film you follow. Notice laughter arriving clear. Notice hands still and mouth clean. Notice the hour after supper staying kind, no hollow, no second reach, no regret before swallow ends.

We feared evenings would stretch grey and long without grazing. Grazing made evenings grey — head fogged, body heavy, attention split between screen and packet, joy thinned with each chew, then shame humming low under the credits. Freedom hands the evening back whole. Time slows kind. Talk goes deeper. The room feels warmer because you are in it, not circling its cupboards.

I spent my first free evening waiting for the old gnaw to roar. It knocked once, light, at half past eight, by the old chair. I noted it. Named it dying. Rejoiced that it died. Made tea. By the time the cup cooled the knock had gone and did not return. Hunger never came that night because supper had satisfied. Thirst came and water met it. Tiredness came and bed met it. No wheel turned between signal and answer.

If a wave comes to you, do the same. Note it. Name it: the Nibbler starving. Drink if thirsty. Eat if hungry till satisfied. Rest if tired. Keep eyes on the film and heart on the room. The wave peaks and thins whether fed or not. Unfed, it never returns with the same voice. Rejoice while it passes. Relief is available now, tonight, in this chair. That lift in the chest is not a dose. That lift is you, free, happy to be free.

Do old tapes play? A hard day may slide a kind voice forward: you deserve one, life is hard, a little comfort will help. Hear it for what it is. That voice is not wisdom. That voice is an old recording from inside the Sugar Trap. Did a dose ever soften a hard day? It blurred the evening, fogged the head, hollowed the hour after, and left the hard day plus a heavier head to meet in the morning. Comfort lives in rest, in food that satisfies, in company, in trouble met with clear head. Pity trapped users still answering the tape. Turn to your own evening with relief.

A bright day may slide a clever voice forward: one to celebrate, just to taste, to show mastery. Hear it colder. A free eater does not prove freedom with a dose. A free eater proves freedom by having no question to answer — pity for the circling, relief in the chest, ease in the chair. The thought converts to celebration the second it is reframed.

You do not need to patrol all night. Guarding is not vigilance. Seeing is rest. You do not sit on watch over thoughts. You answer a thought once with joy and live on. Doubt is not wisdom returning. Doubt is habit pacing by an open door. Smile at the pacing. It passes.

Ask the sofa plainly and answer from your ribs.

If grazing completed the evening, why did grazed evenings always end heavy and thin? If the packet relaxed you, why did relaxed nights always need a heavier morning lift? If this still chair is loss, why does loss feel so rich and owned?

You know the answer. The packet never owned the evening. The evening owns you now.

### IF A CRUMB FINDS YOU

Hear me on slips, so no accident can grow teeth.

Your body can cope with an accidental blip — sauce sweetened without your knowing, a bite taken before you saw, a host's dish dressed brighter than you knew. Your mind must not steer toward it. Note the rumble. Straighten the seeing. Drive on happy to be free.

A crumb unchosen revives nothing unless belief is let back in. Do not wail. Do not confess. Do not start counting from zero. Shame is a relapse engine and you have left shame with the Trap. Wash the mouth with water. Eat next when hungry. Think BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! and mean it — the wheel did not restart because dust touched it. A rumble strip wakes the driver. It does not crash the car.

But never book accident as permission. A chosen one is not a crumb. A chosen one nurses fantasy, rehearses anticipation, fattens glow for Thursday toward Friday, and wakes the very echo you starved. Got away with it never means do it again. Rumble never means steer toward the ditch.

I tell you this not to frighten you. Fright never freed anyone. I tell you so you walk light, knowing the road holds no hidden cliff. The seat of your freedom is seeing, not perfect footsteps. Seeing holds for anyone who guards it once. Belief dead means war over. Keep belief dead and the body follows glad.

Ask the slip plainly if it brushes you.

Did the sweetened sauce taste like love? Did the unchosen bite lift the day? Did joy rise with the coat on the tongue, or did attention catch blaze, then thick, then sicken, then hollow? You already took your last ordinary treat with eyes open. You saw the ugliness under shine. One blind bite cannot unsee what seeing saw — unless you hand belief back its keys.

You will not hand them back. Why would you? What would you buy? A brief blaze that thins by the third chew, a coat on the teeth, a fog in the head, a hollow an hour later asking for more. That is not a treat. That is a bill. You have stopped paying it.

We all feared a single crumb would prove us broken. The other way round is true. A free eater meets a crumb, smiles at the coat, drinks water, and forgets it by the next meal. A trapped eater meets a crumb and makes it a verdict. You are not trapped. You are free. Let the crumb stay a crumb and let seeing stay king.

If waves still knock in the first days, meet them as dying, not demanding. Note the time — late afternoon, evening chair, shop doorway — and note how short the knock lasts. Minutes. Always minutes. Hunger lasts till fed true. Thirst lasts till drunk true. The false gnaw thins while you live on. Welcome each thinning as proof the little creature starves. Its death throes are to rejoice at, never orders to obey.

You change nothing to host this dying. Same kettle. Same route. Same table. The echo dies in the corner while life goes on in the middle of the room. That is how small it is. That is how great you are without it.

### LEAVE THEM TO IT AND LIVE

Do not preach. Do not convert the house.

Fresh freedom wants to shout from the table and rescue all it loves. Hold your joy quiet and let ease do the talking. Lecture breeds defence. Defence reopens debate in your own head. Debate feeds the very doubt you starved. Live free loud and talk soft.

When someone unwraps beside you, pity, do not envy. Poor soul, still paying to blur what freedom meets straight. Does that really look like enjoyment — the rustle, the gulp, the hollow an hour later? You are not being deprived. They are. When someone says you have amazing will, smile and tell the truth plain: no iron was needed, only seeing. When someone offers cake with kindness, answer kind and plain: no thank you, I am happy without it. No speech. No scorn. No fear of offence. A free eater needs no excuse and gives no lesson unless asked with open heart.

Change nothing else. Same parties. Same birthdays. Same tired Tuesdays and bright Saturdays. Inhabit every old context confident and prove freedom where the Trap once ruled. Laughter needs no wrapper. Company needs no packet. Hunger needs real food and satisfaction closes it. The body returns to its natural ease on its own when the wheel stops forcing it round.

We spent years rearranging life around doses — drawers stocked, routes planned past good shops, evenings timed to grazing, mornings timed to lifting. That rearrangement was the slavery, not the cure. Freedom rearranges nothing. Freedom removes the rearranger. Same street. Same kitchen. Same chair. Only the circling gone.

Look at the week ahead with excitement, not caution. Mornings bright. Shops easy. Meals eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied. Work met with clear head. Evenings owned whole. Money drip stopped. Hours circling returned. Teeth spared acid. Head spared fog. All bonus beside the great gain: no more wheel, no more debate, no more hollow asking for more.

You entered this book fearing life would demand watchfulness forever. Watchfulness is the old method talking. Freedom is not a sentence to serve. Freedom is an identity to enjoy. Thoughts will cross — wrapper, smell, old chair — and each crossing becomes a small festival when answered with joy. Think at once, loudly, gladly:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Let that answer be your guard. Not a chant to fight with. Not a shield to clutch. A joyful response that replaces craving the second it crosses the mind. A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order. Rejoice that you no longer feed the Sugar Trap. Pity trapped users still oiling the wheel. Get on with enjoying your life.

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION

Speak that when the thought comes and walk on light. The decision was made with clear eyes on an ordinary evening and needs no second hearing. You do not debate a con once seen. You do not test freedom with slavery. You do not mourn an enemy. You celebrate.

I congratulate you not for effort but for seeing. You did not climb. You stepped out. Mornings prove it. Shops prove it. Supper proves it. The sofa proves it. Each ordinary day lived free writes the truth deeper till thought itself turns glad by nature.
