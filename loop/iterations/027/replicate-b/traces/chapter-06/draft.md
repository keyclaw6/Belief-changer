Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — dawn market with wet leaves and stacked greens, warm bread torn with bare hands, peach juice down the chin, a quiet plate pushed back half-finished, that half-eaten peach left to run

*Hunger itself makes plain food wonderful, and your body already tells you clearly when enough is enough.*

### THE GREY FEAR

You have seen the afternoon clearly now. The bar borrows ten minutes and bills an hour. The brightness fades into fog. The hand moves again on its own. That seeing stays with you.

And with it comes a small cold fear, spoken softly. I hear it because I spoke it myself.

"A meal with no pudding will be grey. Dessert is love. Take the sweet away and what joy is left in eating?"

I do not scold that fear. I know where it was learned. We all learned it together. We were told for years that joy sits in bright paper and a bowl with a spoon, that grown people earn love in sugar, that a table without a sweet close is unfinished.

Look at that fear with me as an investigator, blame off the table. You have proved your strength already in those white-knuckle weeks. This is not about strength. This is about where joy lived all along.

When you say dessert is love, which part do you mean? The warm room, the faces you like best, the hunger met after a long day, the laughter that loosens the shoulders — or the white mound that left the mouth coated and called for more an hour later?

There is only one honest answer, and your own evenings wrote it. Love never lived in the dose.

We were conned into reading the whole evening backwards. We gave the credit to the sweet because the sweet arrived at the same hour as warmth, company, rest, hunger met. It sneaked a ride on a happy hour and charged the hour for the ride.

A free eater does not eat grey food in a grey life. He eats with appetite and stops with pleasure. You did once. You can again.

### MORNING AT THE MARKET

Come with me somewhere ordinary, not theory. I want you to inhabit something, not argue it.

Early morning. Rain just off. The market hall smells of wet leaves and earth and warm yeast. Greens stacked high, still beaded. Apples with cold skins. Loaves piled behind glass, crusts crackling as they cool. A woman breaks a heel off a loaf for you to taste. Steam rises. The crumb is open and chewy. Salt sits honest on the tongue.

You have walked a while. You skipped no joy to earn this. You are simply hungry, truly hungry, the wide unhurried hunger that grows slowly and waits kindly. No panic in it. No pointing at one packet. Bread will meet it. Soup will meet it. Fruit will meet it.

You sit on the low wall outside with paper around warm bread, a small bag of plums, a wedge of cheese. You bite. Juice runs. The crust shatters. The crumb pulls. You chew slowly because chewing is pleasure when hunger is real. You notice. The plum skin snaps, the flesh gives, sweet and sharp together, clean in the mouth, gone when swallowed, asking nothing after.

You eat till the edge lifts. Then something quiet happens. The next bite does not call as loudly. The shoulders drop. Breath deepens. The plate, the paper, the bag — enough. You push back a little, half a plum left, half a heel left, and you forget it because play, work, talk call louder. No counting. No scolding. No vow. Body led, mind followed, joy done.

Ask it plainly of that wall, investigator to investigator.

When hunger was real and food was plain, did eating feel grey or marvellous? When you stopped at satisfied, did you feel deprived or complete? When no bright packet appeared, did you miss it for one second, or did the morning taste whole without it?

There is only one honest answer. Hunger makes plain food sing. Satisfaction closes the song on time.

I lived years believing only a sweet close could finish a meal. I know now I mistook fullness for fulfilment. The evening dose never finished anything. It opened the next want. The market morning finishes clean because hunger met with real food ends.

### HUNGER KNOWS THE WAY

Your body is not a dull machine that needs a manual. It is a precision instrument you were taught to distrust.

True hunger arrives like daylight, slow and broad. The belly hollows gently. Thought turns to food in general, not one bright thing. Time can pass. A plain meal answers it for hours and leaves you quiet.

The other call arrives like a pinch, fast and pointed, an hour after eating, aimed at one thing only. It cannot wait. It cannot be met by bread. It returns soon after it is fed. Learn to know the two apart, because the trap needs you to confuse them.

The fact is, your body makes its own steady fuel from ordinary meals. Plain bread, vegetables, fruit, soup, a full dinner — the body takes what it needs and keeps the blood line steady. Cutting BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. There is no gap for will to bridge.

If you live with diabetes, carry a food-related condition, take anything that touches blood sugar or appetite, or are pregnant, follow your clinician first. This book changes a belief. It does not give medical care.

Feel the relief in that. You were told your legs would wobble without hits. Your own life proves otherwise. Remember the busy morning you forgot sweet entirely and worked clear till one. Remember the holiday lunch, far from packets, after which you walked light for hours and never thought of sweet. Remember that peach left half-eaten while he ran to play. Those hours were not accidents. They were your natural line showing through.

You power you. Food powers you. The dose only rents you your own steadiness for minutes.

So trust the signals you were born with. Hunger is the invitation. Satisfaction is the full stop. Between them lies all the pleasure eating was meant to hold — smell, texture, chew, warmth, quiet. No packet ever added to that. Every evening box only blurred it.

That is the verdict for today, short and flat: hunger is joy's doorway.

### WHERE LOVE REALLY LIVED

Now meet the strongest whisper head-on, because it guards the last corner.

"But pudding is the love. The birthday table, the shared bowl, the laugh with spoons. Surely that sweet is different. Surely that is real."

I sat at those tables. I loved those people. I am not asking you to love them less or to leave the table early. I am asking you to see where the sweetness sat.

Lights low. Faces warm. The day done. Everyone hungry together. The main plates cleared with appetite. Laughter loosening the jaw. Relief in the room that work is over. Then spoons, bright and cold. For ninety seconds sugar floods the mouth while love floods the eyes, and the mind staples the two together.

Replay that table without the dose and keep everything else. Same hunger. Same people. Same laughter. Same rest. Same fine main food eaten with appetite. Would the warmth vanish? Would love drain from the room if spoons held fruit and cream, or cheese, or nothing but coffee and talk? Would you look at those faces and feel grey?

There is only one honest answer. The scene gave everything. The dose sneaked a ride.

And hear the tired lawyer in the head: "Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. You say BAD SUGAR poisons and then you praise a peach. It is all the same."

No. It is not the same, and your mouth knows it.

Sugars locked inside whole fruit, vegetables, plain meals arrive wrapped in water, fibre, chew, bulk — met by hunger, ended by satisfaction. They belong to the menu of real food, to hunger met and pleasure closed. BAD SUGAR means the refined and added free hits and the junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits — drinks, bars, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses of the same. When this book says sugar, take it to mean that. A peach eaten hungry, noticed, stopped at satisfied, asks for nothing after. A packet eaten pinched asks for its fellow within the hour.

I do not forbid joy and I do not rank foods as virtue. Enjoy sweetness where life put it — in fruit, in a meal, in company. Celebrating with chocolate was never proof the chocolate made the celebration. The celebration made the evening glow. The chocolate borrowed the glow and left fog for interest.

Give the credit back where it belongs. The meal fed you. Hunger seasoned it. Company warmed it. Rest closed it. The dose did nothing for that table that the table did not do for itself.

### EAT FREELY, STOP AT ENOUGH

Here is the daylight to carry into kitchens and shops and ordinary days.

You do not need rules to eat well once the con is seen. You need hunger and satisfaction, the two voices you were born hearing.

Eat when you are truly hungry. Not by the clock. Not by the drawer. Not to fill a dull hour or to pay yourself for virtue. When the belly hollows kindly and plain food sounds good, eat.

Eat what you love among real food, slowly enough to notice. Taste. Chew. Put the fork down between bites. Talk. Breathe. Let the meal do its work.

Stop when you are satisfied. Not stuffed. Not punished. Not polished to the last crumb from habit. When the next bite calls less loudly, when breath deepens and shoulders drop, push back. Enough is a feeling, clear and quiet. Trust it.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as freedom, not as a diet. There is no forbidden list here, no weighing, no penance after pleasure, no good eater prize. Punishment built the yo-yo. Permission lets the body speak. A body heard stops clamouring.

I lived the other way for years. I ate by rules by day and by flood by night. Monday tight, Tuesday tighter, Wednesday crack, Thursday flood. I called dessert love while love sat uneaten beside the plate — hunger ignored, satisfaction shouted down, company half-heard through the rustle. Now a plain dinner eaten hungry tastes favourite. Morning bread tastes favourite. Fruit tastes favourite. Favourite was never a brand. Favourite was hunger met.

You came in fearing food would turn grey without hits. Leave this hour knowing hunger colours it. A hungry mouth finds marvels in a crust. A satisfied body leaves food without grief. That is eating as it was meant to be — noticed, savoured, finished.

So walk into the next meal hungry on purpose, living exactly as normal while we talk. Clear nothing. Fight nothing. Notice the first bite. Notice the middle quiet. Notice the gentle full stop. Let real food prove itself in your own mouth. Free eaters do not miss the fog. They get on with enjoying their life.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Hunger is a kind, broad invitation and satisfaction is a clear, quiet close, and both can be trusted.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes complete, and stopping at enough feels like pleasure, not loss.
- The body makes steady fuel from ordinary food, so life with real meals holds no missing piece.
- Love at the table lived in hunger met, company, rest and laughter, and the dose only borrowed that glow.
- Fruit inside whole fruit belongs to real food and a hungry meal, separate in life from bright packets eaten as hits.
- Punishment never taught eating, and eating noticed and finished leaves no restless call behind.
