Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — wet stone at dawn and stacked greens beaded with rain, warm bread torn open with bare hands, plum juice on the wrist, a quiet plate pushed back half-finished, lamplight on an evening table with faces you love

*Hunger itself makes plain food wonderful, and your body already tells you clearly when enough is enough.*

### THE GREY FEAR

You have seen the afternoon plainly now. The drawer opens on its own. Ten bright minutes lift the head. The fog comes back heavier. The hand moves again. That seeing does not leave you.

And close behind it comes a small cold whisper. I know it because I spoke it myself for years.

A meal with no pudding will be grey. Dessert is love. Take the sweet away and what joy is left in eating?

I do not scold that whisper. I hear the kindness in it. We all learned it together. We were told from childhood that joy sits in bright paper and a cold bowl with a spoon, that grown people earn tenderness in sugar, that a table without a sweet close is unfinished. Of course you fear a grey table. Any loving eater would.

Look at it with me as an investigator, blame off the table for good. You have proved your strength already in those white-knuckle weeks when you held Tuesday by the teeth and began Monday with a sincere heart. No weak person does that. This is not about strength. This is about where joy lived all along.

When you say dessert is love, which part do you mean? The warm room after work, the faces you like best in the world, the hunger met after a long day, the laughter that loosens the shoulders — or the white mound that left the mouth coated and called for more an hour later?

When you remember the happiest table of last winter, what rises first in the body? The light in her eyes across the candles, the talk running over itself, the relief that the day is done — or the spoon?

There is only one honest answer, and your own evenings wrote it. Love never lived in the dose.

We were conned into reading the whole evening backwards. We gave the credit to the sweet because the sweet arrived at the same hour as warmth, company, rest, hunger met. It rode along with a happy hour and charged the hour for the ride. Take the sweet from that hour and the hour stands. Take the hour from the sweet and the sweet stands revealed: cold, loud, brief, followed by want.

A free eater does not eat grey food in a grey life. He eats with appetite and stops with pleasure. You did once. You can again, because appetite and pleasure were never taken from your body. They were covered by noise.

Stay with me through one ordinary morning and one ordinary evening, lived slowly, and let your own mouth settle the fear. I am not asking you to clear cupboards. I am not asking you to fight anything. Carry on exactly as normal while we talk. I am asking you to inhabit hunger and satisfaction once, on purpose, and notice what they do to plain food.

### MORNING AT THE MARKET

Come with me somewhere ordinary, not theory. I want you to inhabit something, not argue it.

Early morning. Rain just off. The air smells of wet stone and earth and warm yeast. The market hall breathes cool at the doors and warm at the ovens. Greens stand stacked high, leaves beaded, stems snapping clean. Apples sit cold-skinned in crates, blushing where the light touches. Behind glass the loaves pile, crusts crackling as they cool, steam threading up. A woman in a floury apron breaks the heel off a loaf and holds it out. The crumb is open and chewy. The crust shatters faintly between the teeth. Salt sits honest on the tongue.

You have walked a while. You skipped no joy to earn this. You are simply hungry, truly hungry, the wide unhurried hunger that grows slowly and waits kindly. No panic in it. No pointing at one packet. Bread will meet it. Soup will meet it. Fruit will meet it. Cheese will meet it. That breadth is the signature. True hunger says food. Only the pinch says one bright thing.

You sit on the low wall outside with paper around warm bread, a small bag of dark plums, a wedge of pale cheese. You bite. Juice runs down the wrist. The crust cracks. The crumb pulls. You chew slowly because chewing is pleasure when hunger is real. You notice. The plum skin snaps, the flesh gives, sweet and sharp together, clean in the mouth, gone when swallowed, asking nothing after. The cheese is cool and salty against the warm crumb. The air moves. Pigeons argue at the drain. A bus sighs past. Ordinary life goes on, and the mouth is happy inside it.

You eat till the edge lifts. Then something quiet happens, something no rule ever taught you. The next bite does not call as loudly. The shoulders drop. Breath deepens. The paper lightens in the hand. Half a plum left, half a heel left, and you forget them because work and talk and the walk home call louder. No counting. No scolding. No vow. Body led, mind followed, joy done. You push back a little and stand, light and complete.

Ask it plainly of that wall, investigator to investigator.

When hunger was real and food was plain, did eating feel grey or marvellous? When you stopped at satisfied, did you feel deprived or complete? When no bright packet appeared, did you miss it for one second, or did the morning taste whole without it?

There is only one honest answer. Hunger makes plain food sing. Satisfaction closes the song on time.

I lived years believing only a sweet close could finish a meal. I know now I mistook fullness for fulfilment. The evening dose never finished anything. It opened the next want. The market morning finishes clean because hunger met with real food ends. The mouth quiets. The belly quiets. The mind turns gladly to other things.

That is eating as it was meant to be. Not measured. Not earned. Not punished. Noticed, savoured, finished. You have not lost that eater. You have only covered him with rustle and glare. Watch him appear on the low wall in ten minutes with paper and plums, and know him for yourself.

### BREAD, SOUP, FRUIT, QUIET

Stay on that wall a little longer, because the body has more to show you when you give it time.

Notice the order of pleasure. The first bite is brightest. The second deepens it. The third steadies it. The fourth is still good, but quieter. The fifth is pleasant and already less needed. By the sixth or seventh, pleasure thins on its own, without decision, without force. The body says, enough, in its own language: breath slower, jaw slower, belly warm, interest drifting toward the street and the day. That drift is not failure of appetite. That drift is appetite complete.

Compare that with the packet eaten in the pinch. The first bite is loud. The second is louder to finish the mouthful properly. The third levels the row. Pleasure does not thin. Want sharpens. The belly fills while the mouth asks on. The hand finishes the row while the mind says, I feel like i cant stop. No drift toward the day. No quiet. Only rustle and coated tongue and a vow to be good tomorrow.

Which of those two progressions looks like hunger? Which looks like echo?

True hunger grows slowly. It is broad. It is patient. Bread satisfies it. Soup satisfies it. Fruit satisfies it. A meal ends it for hours and leaves you quiet. You can wait with it. You can talk through it. You can walk with it and forget it for a while, then meet it gladly at the table.

The other call arrives fast. It points at one thing only. It is narrow, twitchy, urgent, loud. It returns an hour after it is fed. It is never satisfied by a meal because it was never hunger for food. Learn to know the two apart, because the trap needs you to confuse them. It needs true hunger to wear the mask of the pinch, so you feed echo with food and food with echo and call the blur appetite.

I hear your objection before you speak it, because it was mine for years. You say: I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, is that not hunger? That shake, that hollow urgency, are you telling me I imagined it? No. I am telling you you felt something real and read its address wrong. The shake was real. The urgency was real. The reading that said plain food cannot meet it was false. Offer that urgency bread and soup and watch what it does. If it turns its nose at bread, it was never hunger for dinner. Hunger for dinner says yes to dinner.

Ask three plain questions over your own week, and answer from your own mouth, not from the advert.

When the call says any food will do and a bowl of soup quiets you for three hours, what was that? When the call says only a bright packet will do and a full lunch leaves it shouting, what is that? When you eat hungry at noon with no sweet near and walk light till evening, what carried you those hours if not food and blood and breath?

There is only one honest answer. One voice is hunger. The other is echo. Hunger ends. Echo repeats.

That distinction changes everything at the table. You stop chasing quiet with loudness. You stop answering broad need with narrow hits. You let hunger call, you meet it with real food, you let satisfaction close. Simple. Human. Complete.

I lived the blur for years. Midday pinch answered with a bar, evening hunger answered with grazing, night emptiness answered with a bowl, morning heaviness answered with skipping and coffee and rules. I called it appetite. It was static. The day I learned to hear the two voices, eating simplified on its own. Broad call, broad answer. Narrow call, smile and let pass. No battle. No arithmetic. The body spoke. I listened.

You can hear it by supper. Eat a little hungrier than habit. Wait till the belly hollows kindly and plain food sounds good. Then eat slowly enough to hear the thinning at bite five or six. Put the fork down. Breathe. Talk. When the next bite calls less loudly, stop. That less loudly is the full stop you were born reading. Trust it once and it speaks louder next time.

### HUNGER KNOWS THE WAY

Your body is not a dull machine that needs a manual from outside. It is a precision instrument you were taught to distrust so you would buy directions.

The fact is, your body makes its own steady fuel from ordinary meals. Plain bread, vegetables, fruit, soup, cheese, a full dinner eaten hungry — the body takes what it needs and keeps the line steady for hours. Protein and the glycerol backbone give it what it needs to hold blood sugar between meals. Cutting BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. There is no gap for will to bridge. Clean eating without hits is physiologically coherent. That is daylight, not dread.

If you live with diabetes, carry a food-related condition, take anything that touches blood sugar or appetite, or are pregnant, follow your clinician first. This book changes a belief. It does not give medical care.

Feel the relief in that. You were told your legs would wobble without hits. Your own life proves otherwise. Remember the busy morning you forgot sweet entirely and worked clear till one, spoke clearly, typed fast, laughed at noon. Remember the holiday lunch, far from packets, after which you walked light for hours and never thought of sweet. Remember the evening you were absorbed in work or play and the drawer stayed shut without effort because the mind was elsewhere. Those hours were not accidents. They were your natural line showing through the noise. The line is steady. The hits are the dip.

You power you. Food powers you. The hit only rents you your own steadiness for minutes and sends a bill for hours.

So trust the signals you were born with. Hunger is the invitation. Satisfaction is the full stop. Between them lies all the pleasure eating was meant to hold — smell, texture, chew, warmth, quiet. No packet ever added to that music. Every evening box only blurred it with static.

I do not give you a plan. I give you back your ears. There is no weighing here, no forbidden list, no penance after pleasure, no good eater prize. Punishment built the yo-yo. Permission lets the body speak. A body heard stops clamouring. A body shouted at shouts back at night.

Watch where steadiness already lives in your day, so you trust the ground we will inhabit fully from here. Watch the first hour after waking, before the first hit. Clearer than late afternoon. Watch the hour after a plain meal eaten hungry, before sweet. Quieter than mid-morning. Watch the walk after supper when you ate to satisfied and left the table light. No pinch at all. Those hours are not luck. They are you without static.

Ask it plainly, and let the answer settle for good.

If your body needed hits to function, how did you sail through a rushed morning and forget sweet entirely till the clock reminded you? If hits gave energy, why did energy always fall after the gift? If afternoons were naturally broken, why were holiday afternoons without packets whole?

There is only one honest answer. Steadiness was yours all along. The hit sneaked a ride on your break, your breath, your pause, and charged you fog for the ride.

Hold that answer at the table. When the broad call comes, meet it gladly. When the narrow call comes an hour after food, greet it as information, not orders. Ah, the echo. Bread would not interest it. Soup would not quiet it. It wants its fellow, not dinner. Smile and let the hour pass. Echo thins when unfed, because it was never hunger, never need, never fuel.

You are not losing help. You are losing static.

Think of the child who drops the half-eaten peach to run. No lesson there, only life: hunger called, fruit answered, play called louder, eating ended without grief. That ease is not childish. That ease is human, and it waits in you.

### WHERE LOVE REALLY LIVED

Now meet the tenderest whisper head-on, because it guards the last corner.

But pudding is the love. The birthday table, the shared bowl, the laugh with spoons. Surely that sweet is different. Surely that is real.

I sat at those tables. I loved those people. I am not asking you to love them less or to leave the table early or to lecture anyone about packets. I am asking you to see where the sweetness sat, so fear stops guarding a shadow.

Picture it as you lived it. Lights low. Faces warm. The day done. Everyone hungry together. The main plates cleared with appetite, the table littered and happy. Laughter loosening the jaw. Relief in the room that work is over and no one must perform. Then spoons, bright and cold. For ninety seconds sugar floods the mouth while love floods the eyes, and the mind staples the two together and calls the staple love.

Replay that table without the hit and keep everything else. Same hunger. Same people. Same laughter. Same rest. Same fine main food eaten with appetite. Fruit on the table, cheese on the board, coffee steaming, talk running long. Would the warmth vanish? Would love drain from the room if spoons held berries and cream, or baked apples, or nothing but talk? Would you look at those faces and feel grey?

There is only one honest answer. The scene gave everything. The hit sneaked a ride.

And hear the tired lawyer in the head, the voice that sounds clever at midnight: Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. You say BAD SUGAR harms and then you praise a plum. It is all the same.

No. It is not the same, and your mouth knows it before any book tells you.

Sugars locked inside whole fruit, vegetables, plain meals arrive wrapped in water, fibre, chew, bulk — met by hunger, ended by satisfaction. They belong to the menu of real food, to hunger met and pleasure closed. A plum eaten hungry, noticed, stopped at satisfied, asks for nothing after. An apple crunches, juices, ends. Soup warms, fills, quiets for hours.

BAD SUGAR means the refined and added free hits and the junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits — drinks, bars, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses of the same. When this book says sugar, take it to mean that. A packet eaten pinched asks for its fellow within the hour. The form is different. The course is different. The ending is different. Your own evenings proved the difference long before these pages named it.

I do not forbid joy and I do not rank foods as virtue. Enjoy sweetness where life put it — in fruit, in a meal, in company. I do not moralise the table into good and bad. I ask only that credit fall where it belongs. Celebrating with chocolate was never proof the chocolate made the celebration. The celebration made the evening glow. The chocolate borrowed the glow and left fog for interest. That is not love. That is hire-purchase on love.

Give the credit back where it belongs and feel the table brighten. The meal fed you. Hunger seasoned it. Company warmed it. Rest closed it. The hit did nothing for that table that the table did not do for itself. It does plenty TO the evening. It does nothing FOR the evening.

You came in fearing the table would dim. Leave this hour knowing the lamp was never the packet. The lamp was hunger met among people you love. That lamp burns clearer without static.

### EAT FREELY, STOP AT ENOUGH

Here is the daylight to carry into kitchens and shops and ordinary days.

You do not need rules to eat well once the con is seen. You need hunger and satisfaction, the two voices you were born hearing.

Eat when you are truly hungry. Not by the clock. Not by the drawer. Not to fill a dull hour or to pay yourself for virtue after a hard day. When the belly hollows kindly and plain food sounds good, eat. That kindness is the invitation. Trust it.

Eat what you love among real food, slowly enough to notice. Taste. Chew. Put the fork down between bites. Talk. Breathe. Let the meal do its work. Notice the first bright bite. Notice the deep middle. Notice the gentle thinning when enough nears. That noticing is pleasure itself. Hurry never tasted anything.

Stop when you are satisfied. Not stuffed. Not punished. Not polished to the last crumb from habit or from childhood training. When the next bite calls less loudly, when breath deepens and shoulders drop, push back. Enough is a feeling, clear and quiet. Trust it. It will speak louder each time you obey it.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as freedom, not as a diet. Punishment built the yo-yo: Monday tight, Tuesday tighter, Wednesday crack, Thursday flood, Friday truce. Permission lets the body speak. A body heard stops clamouring at night. A mouth noticed stops asking for loudness. A belly met stops echoing.

I lived the other way for years. I ate by rules by day and by flood by night. I called dessert love while love sat uneaten beside the plate — hunger ignored, satisfaction shouted down, company half-heard through the rustle. Now a plain dinner eaten hungry tastes favourite. Morning bread tastes favourite. Fruit tastes favourite. Soup tastes favourite. Favourite was never a brand. Favourite was hunger met.

You say: I have managed to be good this week. Surely a small reward cannot hurt. Treat when you feel you deserve one. I know that voice. It sounds like kindness. Test it against your own evenings. When you paid virtue with a hit, did the evening close in quiet or open into rustle? When you ate hungry and stopped at satisfied, did you feel cheated or complete? When love was present, did the packet deepen it or blur it?

There is only one honest answer. Reward in bright paper returns as want. Hunger met returns as peace.

So walk into the next meal hungry on purpose, living exactly as normal while we talk. Clear nothing. Fight nothing. Notice the first bite. Notice the middle quiet. Notice the gentle full stop. Let real food prove itself in your own mouth. Free eaters do not miss the fog. They get on with enjoying their life.

You came in fearing food would turn grey without hits. Leave this hour knowing hunger colours it. A hungry mouth finds marvels in a crust. A satisfied body leaves food without grief. That is eating as it was meant to be — noticed, savoured, finished. Hunger is joy's doorway. Satisfaction is joy's close.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Hunger arrives broad and patient and satisfaction arrives quiet and clear, and both can be trusted to lead eating.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry tastes complete, and stopping at enough feels like pleasure closing on time.
- The body holds steady on ordinary food, so a day of real meals carries no missing piece.
- Warmth at the table lived in hunger met, company, rest and laughter, and the hit only borrowed that glow.
- Whole fruit and plain meals answer hunger for hours, while bright packets answer echo for minutes.
- Eating noticed and finished leaves quiet behind, with no restless call and no penance.
