Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — kitchen calendar with Friday circled red, single bar waiting in a bright drawer, weekday pride turning to Thursday glow, cliff path with a short-fall dare, seatbelt click in a family car, bare shelf and shining doorways, birthday table laid for tomorrow

*There is no little sweet that stays little; keep one and you keep it all.*

### THE DEAL YOU ARE ABOUT TO MAKE

You have seen the cinema with clear eyes. You have seen the sofa and the birthday table with clear eyes. The love stayed. The laughter stayed. The glow stayed.

Now a polite voice steps forward with a pen and a small contract.

All right, it says. I see it gives nothing. I see the scene did the work. So I will be sensible. No daily grazing. No box in the cupboard. No morning dose. Only Fridays. Only birthdays. Only one. Only when truly deserved. Only till the holidays pass, then I will stop clean.

I honour you for saying it plainly. Every honest reader makes this deal at this exact point. I made it myself for years. We all made it. It sounds mature. It sounds balanced. It sounds free.

It is the voice of the Sugar Trap, and it will keep you circling for years if you sign.

The fact is, inside BAD SUGAR there is no sensible corner. There is no safe cut-down, no safe special, no safe tomorrow, no safe swap. There is total freedom on one side and the whole wheel on the other. One kept sweet keeps the belief alive, and belief alive keeps wanting alive.

You say, just a treat once a week is fine. You say, I do not keep it in the house so I am safe. You say, I will stop tomorrow, after the birthday, after the holidays. I print your lines because I love you, and I answer because I know where each line leads.

If the Friday dose was harmless, why did Thursday start to shine? If one was ever enough, why did one ever end with a hollow and a second reach? If tomorrow was truly safer, why has tomorrow never once arrived on its own?

You know the answer before I speak it. The exception is not the exit. The exception is the chain.

### FRIDAY MAKES IT PRECIOUS

Live one Friday-allowance week with me, hour by hour, and watch what sensible does to a free man.

Monday you are proud. Cupboards bare. Head clear. You tell the mirror you have cracked it. Moderation. Control. A grown-up rule.

Tuesday the rule still holds, but talk starts. Only three days to Friday. You picture the bar you chose. You rehearse which shop, which brand, which hour. Ordinary food tastes a little greyer, not because ordinary food changed, but because a bright picture hangs over Friday and steals light from today. Work is done with half a mind. The other half counts sleeps.

Wednesday you are virtuous and restless at once. You eat lightly at lunch to save room. You tell a friend you were good this week, and the friend praises you, and praise makes Friday shine harder. A faint hollow stirs in the late afternoon, a restless gnaw for something sweet now, and the Sweet Con translates it at once: see, you need it, Friday will fix it. You smile at the translation because you have learned who speaks.

Thursday anticipation is now hunger. You plan the evening. Film picked. Sofa ready. Bar waiting in the drawer you swore would hold only one. The day drags because joy has been moved to tomorrow. You snap at small things. You dream of sweetness the way a thirsty man dreams of water, though water stands beside you untouched and plain meals wait uneaten with joy in them. You check the drawer twice to make sure the bar is still there. You tell yourself checking proves control. Checking proves the bar has your evenings on a string.

Friday comes. Hands a little quick. Paper torn fast. First bite blazes bright. Second bite coats thick. Third bite sickens faintly, yet the hand reaches, because Friday must be savoured to last a week. Wrapper rustle, box empty before the credits end. You promised one and took three. You promised evening and stole the night. Regret rises before swallow ends. Fog follows. Hollow an hour later. Wheel oiled for tomorrow.

Saturday you tell yourself Friday was too much, so you add Saturday to use up the craving, and Sunday to say goodbye properly, and Monday to start again clean. The rule that was meant to shrink the trap has fed it for seven days. The week that was meant to prove mastery proves slavery.

We all lived that week. I lived it with chocolate Fridays and cake Sundays and cola at parties only. The week always ran the same way, whatever the brand, whatever the day circled red.

The rule did not make the sweet small. The rule made the sweet huge. Forbidden fruit glows. Waiting fattens fantasy. Each short fast makes the next dose blaze brighter, and the blaze is then misread as proof the sweet is precious.

It is the other way round. The blaze proves the fast was the trap.

Cutting down does not loosen the Sugar Trap. Cutting down tightens it. On-off dosing drives fast wanting in ordinary people, and wanting thins when unfed. Keep the on-off and you keep the wanting. Kill the on-off and the wheel stops.

Ask your own Friday and answer from your ribs.

If moderation worked, why did each allowed dose feel more urgent than daily grazing ever did? If one was control, why did control need rehearsing, saving room, counting sleeps, and forgiving creep to Thursday and Saturday? If the rule freed you, why did you feel more trapped in the rule week than in any binge week?

You know the answer. The rule was the trap wearing a sensible coat.

### ONE IS NEVER ONE

Now the smallest deal of all. Just one. One square. One biscuit. One sip for taste. Surely one cannot hurt.

I speak as settled fact, with love for you and contempt only for the con: inside BAD SUGAR one never stays one, because one re-fires the very want it pretends to satisfy.

You know this from your own hand, not from my words. A permitted sliver wakes fresh wanting. The mouth primed by the first blaze asks again before swallow ends. Thirst that water does not clear. Hand moving before decision. The packet empties, not because you are weak, but because a dose re-fires wanting circuitry while packets last. That carry-on is the signature of the trap, not the measure of your character.

Hear me clearly: you are not condemned to helplessness. You are not broken. You are describing what a hit does while belief in the hit lives. Kill the belief and the carry-on dies with it. Starve the tiny physical echo and the hand stops moving on its own.

I feel like i cant stop, you say in the evening, low and ashamed. I print the line because it is honest, and I lift the shame off it because shame lies. You felt you could not stop after the first bite because the first bite ordered the second. The order came from the dose, not from your nature. A free eater with no belief in BAD SUGAR feels no such order. Put a bar before a child full from a peach and watch how little orders sound.

Ask plainly.

If one was enough, why did one ever leave you prowling for more? If taste was the point, why did the fourth bite, which coated and sickened, still get eaten? If you chose freely, why did choice vanish after the first chew?

You know the answer. The craving, once triggered, carries the binge. One is the whole box wearing a tiny mask.

I tell you this not to frighten you, and I do not ask you to change from fear. Rejoice at the seeing. If one kept the wheel turning, zero lets the wheel stop. Zero is not bigger effort. Zero is no effort, because there is no blaze to rehearse, no Thursday glow to nurse, no wrapper to answer. Nothing to count. Nothing to forgive. The tiny echo starves in days when unfed, and belief dead means no voice to wake it.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That line is your guard here. Plenty TO — blaze then coat then sicken, thirst, fog, hollow, Thursday stolen for Friday, Monday started with regret. Nothing FOR — no love added, no rest added, no joy added that the evening did not already hold. Once seen, that line cannot be unseen.

### THE YO-YO PROVES THE RULE

Step back and see the shape you have lived for years. Starve by day. Flood by night. Good by week. Bad by weekend. Clean in January. Loaded by February. Tight rules and white-knuckle evenings, then flood and shame and Monday restart.

You called that your failure. I tell you as settled fact: that yo-yo is the trap form. Restriction by day drives flood by night. Flood by night demands restriction by day. The wheel needs both halves to turn.

The Willpower Method built that wheel and then blamed you for riding it. It told you to want hard and hold hard, to count and forbid and earn. Forbidding made precious. Precious made blaze. Blaze made flood. Flood made shame. Shame made fresh vows. Fresh vows made fresh forbidding. Round and round, with you in the middle thinking you lacked iron.

It takes no iron to see a con. It takes seeing.

We were never weak for yo-yoing. We were obedient to a wrong method that guarantees yo-yoing. Diet-binge, allow-deny, weekday-weekend — different names for one wheel. Stop oiling either half and the wheel stops. You do not need a kinder wheel. You need no wheel.

Look at your own diary. The cleanest mornings were followed by the loudest evenings. The strictest weeks were followed by the heaviest weekends. Was that accident? Was that character? Was that hunger? Hunger does not behave that way. Hunger met by real food quiets. Hunger denied by rules shouts, then flood answers, then rules return to punish flood. The pattern is too neat to be yours. It is the pattern of on-off dosing in ordinary people, restriction manufacturing the very flood it claims to cure.

Do not let any voice sell you weaning as wisdom. Tapering a blaze that lives on anticipation only fattens anticipation between hits. Sipping a little to soften the landing keeps the landing coming. The little grumble you fear is small and short, days-long, already dying when smiled at. Stretch it over weeks of Fridays and you turn a passing cloud into a season. Get it done whole. Starve the Nibbler at once by killing the belief that feeds it, and the body echo passes while you get on with enjoying your life.

The Sweet Con will whisper that daily small hits would be calmer than on-off. Do not listen. Calm never lived in the dose. Calm lives in hunger met by real food, thirst met by water, tiredness met by rest, life met straight. Daily hits only load slow water without pain while feeling fine misleads. On-off hits only blaze brighter while feeling controlled misleads. Both halves charge. Neither half pays.

Which half will you keep oiling? The starve half or the flood half? The weekday half or the weekend half? The morning half or the evening half?

You know the answer. Both halves are one wheel. Leave the whole wheel.

### I DO NOT KEEP IT IN THE HOUSE

Now the house-rule defence, spoken proudly.

I do not keep it in the house. I only have it out. At parties. At work. When offered. So I am safe.

Are you? Follow your own feet.

Monday the shelf is bare and you are proud. Tuesday a colleague waves a tin and pride wobbles. You take one to be polite, then three because one re-fired wanting. Wednesday you walk past the shop you swore to pass and feel virtuous for passing, though passing took a whole argument in your head. Thursday tiredness talks: you deserve one, you were good, just this once out will not count. Friday you buy a bar for the film, oath intact because the house stays bare eleven hours a day.

The house-rule did not remove the trap. The house-rule moved the trap to every doorway outside the house and made each doorway glow. Outside sweets now carry extra shine because inside sweets are banned. Offered sweets cannot be refused because refusal would prove you still want. Tired evenings cannot walk straight home because home holds no dose and the shop on the corner holds bright promise.

Who is in charge in that picture? The man or the drawer?

If the drawer is empty and the mind is full of drawers, the drawer is not empty. A free man does not need a bare shelf to stay free. A trapped man carries the shelf in his head wherever he walks.

True freedom is not a clean cupboard. True freedom is a clean belief. When BAD SUGAR is seen as poison in sweet coat, doing plenty TO and nothing FOR, you can stand in a bright aisle, at a birthday table, by an open tin at work, and feel relief that you no longer feed the Sugar Trap, pity for trapped users still circling, and ease in your own skin. No argument. No brave face. No counting. The shelf, full or bare, has no vote.

Do not clean the house to get free. Get free and the house cleans itself.

Ask your own house-rule and answer straight.

If absence at home was freedom, why did presence everywhere else feel louder? If outside-only was safe, why did outside feel like a test every single day? If the bare shelf proved mastery, why did mastery need daily proof?

You know the answer. The shelf was swept. The belief was kept. Kept belief keeps the trap.

### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP'S FAVOURITE WORD

Now the softest deal, spoken kindly.

I will stop tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays. When work eases. When I am ready. Today is bad timing.

I answer with total authority and total warmth: tomorrow is not timing. Tomorrow is the belief keeping itself alive.

If sweets were harmless, why stop tomorrow at all? If sweets were precious, why stop ever? You cannot have both. The fact you hold this book proves you know the wheel charges nightly. Delay proves you still hear a whisper that stopping costs joy. That whisper is not wisdom. That whisper is brainwashing, installed by bright packs and kind people repeating scripts until repetition felt like nature.

Count what tomorrow costs, not to scare you, but to free you. Money drip. Hours circling shops and cupboards. Teeth acid after each hit. Liver loading surplus. Pressure creeping. Appetite readings blurred. Daily comfort borrowing from later mood. Evening fog where clear laughter could live. Add them nightly hooked, or picture them gone free. Both futures are on offer. Which future do you want?

I do not leave you in dread of that load. If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with food distress past or present, or carry medical risk, your clinician leads and you put this book aside until cleared to continue. For the ordinary reader the seeing is enough: feeling fine never meant intake was low, and seeing the load is not the reason to walk out. Walking out into brightness is the reason. Fear never freed anyone. Escape frees at once.

Ask tomorrow plainly.

If after the birthday was safer, why did the last ten afters never come? If holidays were the block, why did quiet weeks after holidays fill with the same wheel? If readiness comes with time, why does time only fatten the picture and thin resolve?

You know the answer. Waiting is the trap. Freedom is now.

You do not need a perfect week. You need a true seeing. When the seeing is true, the day is any day, and any day is today.

### THE CLIFF WITH THE SHORT FALL

Hear the whole moderation lie in one picture, and it will never fool you again.

A smiling friend walks you to a high cliff over hard rocks. Jump, he says. Fun awaits. You step back. He laughs. Only jump a little way, he says. Fall just a few metres. Show control. Be sensible. Keep the thrill and skip the smash.

You stare. What does a little fall mean off a cliff? Gravity does not do sensible. A little fall is the whole fall started. The only safe move is not to jump.

Friday-only is that jump. One-only is that jump. Birthday-only is that jump. Wean a little is that jump. The Sweet Con stands at the edge in bright paper, promising a short fall. The Nibbler squeaks below for a feed. Physics does the rest. One blaze re-fires wanting. Wanting drives the packet. The packet drives the week. The week drives the vow. The vow drives the next short fall.

Would you admire the jumper who boasts he fell only halfway before rocks? Would you call him controlled? Would you envy him? You would pity him and warn the next walker to step back from the edge.

Step back now. Pity trapped jumpers, never envy them. They are not enjoying a safe thrill you miss. They are falling and calling falling flying while the ground rushes up nightly.

There is no safe sweet inside BAD SUGAR. There is no healthy level other than zero sought. The body can cope with an accidental crumb, but the mind cannot afford a deliberate one. Guard the belief and the body follows. Bargain with the belief and the body follows the bargain off the edge.

Some voices will call this extreme. Extreme is jumping off cliffs for a blaze that borrows brightness and returns dimness. Sensible is walking the safe path with clear head, real hunger met, real satisfaction kept, mornings bright, shops easy, evenings whole. Which picture is extreme? Your own ribs know.

Do not argue with gravity. Do not negotiate with the edge. Do not test the drop with a toe. Walk back onto the field where children play and meals are eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied and laughter needs no wrapper. That field was always yours. The cliff was built to charge admission.

### THE SEATBELT, NOT THE STEERING WHEEL

Now I give you the margin plainly, so no fear remains and no licence sneaks in.

You wear a seatbelt in a car for the accidental jolt — a sudden stop, a careless driver, a bump you never chose. The belt saves you from harm you did not steer toward. No sane driver buckles up in order to drive erratically. No sane driver aims at walls because the belt exists. The belt is for accident, never for steering.

So with eating. Your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip — a sauce sweetened without your knowing, a crumb in a shared kitchen, a polite bite taken before you saw. The body absorbs and clears. The mind need not panic. A slip revives nothing unless the Sweet Con belief is let back in. Panic is the Willpower Method talking. Smile, note the rumble strip, drive on straight, happy to be free.

But the mind must never steer into the wall. A deliberate Friday bar is not a blip. A deliberate birthday exception is not a blip. A deliberate just-one to test freedom is not a blip. Steering into the dose on purpose, belt on or not, re-feeds the Nibbler and re-waters the belief, and belief alive re-fires wanting while packets last.

Hear the difference clearly and fear leaves.

Accident: crumb unchosen, mind clear, no glow nursed, no rule broken because no rule of iron exists, only a free belief intact. Lesson noted. Joy intact. Freedom intact.

Deliberate: glow nursed for days, anticipation rehearsed, blaze chased, flood forgiven in advance because the belt was booked as permission. That is not accident. That is the trap driving with your hands.

Never book the belt as permission. Never test freedom with a dose. A free man does not test open doors by jumping. He walks through into light and gets on with enjoying his life.

If doubt whispers that one booked blip proves you blew it, answer at once. One unchosen crumb proves nothing. One chosen exception proves the belief wobbled. Straighten the belief and drive on. Shame is a relapse engine. Seeing is a freedom engine. Choose seeing.

### THIS BOOK IS NOT A TRICK

Now I meet the last whisper, the one aimed not at sweets but at this method itself. Hear it plain.

This sounds like brainwashing, it says. Totality or nothing. No moderation. No tomorrow. No house-rule. Just obey. How is that freedom? How is that different from every diet that chained me?

I thank you for the courage to think it. Here is total honesty in return.

Diets chained you with rules against instinct: starve hunger, count joy, earn food, fight your own body with iron. This book frees you with truth with instinct: trust hunger, stop at satisfied, enjoy real food as favourite, starve only a tiny echo by seeing the con that fed it. Diets said fight yourself. I say see the trick and walk out. Diets made sweets precious by forbidding. I make sweets worthless by unmasking. Diets left belief intact and attacked behaviour. I kill belief and behaviour follows.

Obeying me is not the point. Seeing clearly is the point. If BAD SUGAR does plenty TO and nothing FOR, what sane use remains for a little? If the wheel turns on on-off, what sane mercy lives in half a wheel? Totality is not my demand. Totality is the shape of the trap once seen. You do not bow to totality. You rejoice in it, because totality means no counting, no bargaining, no Thursdays lost to Fridays, no drawers carried in the head.

You are not asked to believe blind. You are asked to check with your own evenings. Did one stay one? Did Friday stay Friday? Did tomorrow come? Did the house-rule free the mind? Your diary already testified before I spoke. I only gathered its pages.

So do not call seeing a chain. Chains made you count. Seeing lets you stop counting. Chains made you fear shops. Seeing lets you pity trapped circling and walk straight. Chains promised happiness after hard weeks. Seeing gives relief now, bright head tonight, clear morning next.

You entered negotiating carve-outs. Leave knowing carve-out keeps belief alive, and belief alive keeps the Sugar Trap alive. Kill the carve-out and the trap dies for want of food. What remains is you, hunger, satisfaction, company, mornings, shops, meals — marvellous, wonderful, exciting in their plain truth, yours free.

Rejoice. The edge is behind you. The open road is ahead.
