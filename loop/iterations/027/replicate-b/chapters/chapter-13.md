Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — closed book on the kitchen table, phone picture of a short list, morning market light, bread and apples on the belt, steam over the supper pot, the meal ahead

*You have lost no sweetness at all. Sweetness has been handed back to you.*

### IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

If you opened the book here, close this page for now and start at the first page and read straight through.

This page is saved reminders, not the method. The method is the seeing you do from the beginning, with an open mind, while you live normally. What follows will make full sense only after that seeing is done.

You who have read through, stay with me. This is short. Then you live.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You have followed them. Now keep the picture and walk out light.

I do not ask you to study anything more. I do not ask you to remember arguments. I ask you to photograph a short list, put the book down, and go and eat supper hungry with joy.

### THERE IS NOTHING TO MISS

We all entered this book with the same grey fear humming low.

Life without bright packets would be duller. Joy needed a wrapper. Remove the blaze and brightness goes with it. Evenings would stretch long. Mornings would drag. Food would turn grey. Celebration would lose its point.

Ask that fear plainly now, with clear eyes, and answer from your ribs.

If bright packets made food joyful, why did the most packet-fed meals leave you flat, foggy and never satisfied an hour later? If they gave energy, why did packet days always sag by mid-afternoon and beg for more? If a clear tongue and a settled belly is loss, why does loss feel so light and clean and rich?

You know the answer. There was never anything to mourn.

The fact is, BAD SUGAR never fed you. It prodded you, hollowed you, blurred you, then sold you back a flash of relief from the hollow it left and called the flash a treat. We lived inside that wheel so long we called the wheel appetite. We called the flash energy. We called the rustle reward. We called the fog normal.

You see it now. The film gave the warmth. The laughter gave the joy. The company gave the love. The hunger met true gave the feast. The dose only sneaked a ride and charged for the journey.

What did it ever do for you? Name one real benefit that stays standing in daylight. The lift that thinned by the third chew? The buzz that sagged within the hour? The comfort that fogged the evening and left the trouble plus a heavier head for morning? The celebration that needed repeating before the plates were cleared?

It does nothing for you. It did plenty to blur the very joy it claimed to bring.

I hear the last whisper that rises in every free eater. “But surely there was something there. Surely I felt something.”

Yes. You felt something. You felt relief from a gnaw the last dose left behind. You felt a flash while the tongue still blazed, before the coat settled on the teeth. You felt the room laughing and credited the bar in your hand. That feeling was real. The reading of it was wrong.

Would you call it pleasure to wear shoes a size too small all day for the ease of kicking them off at night? Would you thank a hand that steadied you after pushing you over? Would you pay for a window to be opened after paying for it to be shut? That is what we paid for, bright packet after bright packet, year after year.

You are out of that bargain. Rejoice.

### THE BRIGHTNESS THAT WAS BORROWED

Think of an ordinary packet-fed day as we lived it.

Sweet start to lift the fog. Brief blaze. Coat on the tongue before work began. Ten o’clock hum. Something quick and bright to get through. Short lift. Fast drop. Lunch blurred. Afternoon drag. Another bright something to push through the hours. Evening chair. Hand to mouth while mind went soft. Box lighter than memory allowed. Regret before swallow ended. Morning fog again.

We called that living. It was circling.

Now think of yesterday lived free.

You woke with a clean mouth. Hunger came hollow and honest, asking for breakfast. You ate bread with butter melting in, fruit cracked open juicy, porridge warm to the ribs, eggs, cheese, plain yogurt — take what your house loves. You tasted it. Real hunger made plain food sing. You stopped when satisfaction settled, put the spoon down, and walked out bright.

You walked the noon aisle with no vote to cast. Bright packs shouted. Cartoon faces pulled. Small bars crowded round the till to catch a tired hand. You saw engineered noise and walked through easy. No arithmetic. No been good all week. No earned one. No Friday only. You bought bread, cheese, apples, fish for supper, paid light, and stepped out into daylight marvellous clear.

You cooked supper hungry. Market vegetables bright in the pan. Fresh bread torn, not counted. Soup steaming. Salad crisp. Meat, fish, beans, rice, potatoes — take what you love. You ate till the hollow filled and pleasure settled deep and still, then stopped. Fruit after tasted bright because true hunger had been met first. No hollow followed. No prowl started before the plates were cleared.

You sat in the same chair and followed the film to the end. Hot tea in your hand. Warmth in the room. Talk, book, quiet — take what you always took, minus the blur. Laughter arrived clear. Hands stayed still. Mouth stayed clean. The hour after supper stayed kind.

Which day held more brightness? Which day held more sweetness? Which day left you more you?

You know. The packet day borrowed brightness from the free day and charged interest. The free day owns its brightness outright.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or have any medical condition where changing what you eat could carry risk, talk to your clinician and follow their advice first.

### GROWING BACK, NOT QUITTING

Hear the fresh reframe that makes the whole journey plain.

You have lost no sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you by years of blaze and has now been handed back.

A clean tongue tastes bread crust deep. It tastes apple snap loud. It tastes broth warmth, berry sharpness, orange juice on the fingers. Hunger seasons them bright because hunger is clean again. Porridge tastes faintly sweet with nothing added. Carrots taste sweet without shine. An orange eaten section by section after supper satisfies where a box of blaze never could.

We believed joy needed brightness poured on top. The other way round was true. Brightness poured on top dulled joy underneath. It coated hunger. It blurred satisfaction. It left the mouth coated and the head fogged and called the next flash rescue.

Like a small child with a peach who eats hungry and stops mid-bite and runs off laughing, the body knew its work from the start. No counting. No guilt. Hunger leads. Joy obeys. Enough says stop. You were that eater once, before paper taught you to doubt hunger and chase blaze. You are that eater again now.

This was never about losing a pleasure. This was about growing back into the eater you were before the wheel started turning.

Feel the weight lift off that sentence. No emptiness to fill. No new life to build. No new kitchen. No new self. Same street. Same table. Same people. Same walk to work. Same cup of tea. Freedom rearranges nothing. Freedom removes the rearranger.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again. Drink water when thirsty. Rest when tired. Meet true signals straight and let false signals starve unfed. The body is a precision instrument when belief stops shouting over it. Trust it and it carries you glad.

Think what the choice really was. Staying in the wheel would have meant staying prodded and hollowed for the rest of your life. Walking free means eating, laughing, working and resting bright for the rest of your life. Same days. Two opposite lives. You have chosen brightness.

That is why elation is the only sane response. Not caution. Not watchfulness. Excitement. Pride. Relief available now, tonight, in this chair. The lift in your chest is not a dose. That lift is you, free, happy to be free.

### GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

So get on with enjoying your life.

Do not study freedom. Live it. Close the book. Cook supper hungry. Carry home plain food you love. Sit in the same chair and follow the film. Meet mornings clear and let hunger lead and satisfaction close. Let money drip stop. Let hours circling return. Let teeth be spared acid. Let head be spared fog. Those are bonus beside the great gain: no more wheel, no more debate, no more hollow asking for more.

Look at the week ahead with excitement, not caution. Mornings bright. Shops easy. Meals eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied. Work met with clear head. Evenings owned whole. Birthdays laughed through with people, not paper. Tired Tuesdays met with rest and food that satisfies. Bright Saturdays walked light with no bargain humming.

We spent years rearranging life round doses — drawers stocked, routes planned past good shops, evenings timed to grazing, mornings timed to lifting. That rearrangement was the slavery. Freedom needs no rearrangement. Same parties. Same birthdays. Same tired evenings and bright mornings. Inhabit every old context confident and prove freedom where the wheel once ruled.

Do not preach. Do not convert the house. Fresh freedom wants to shout from the table and rescue all it loves. Hold your joy quiet and let ease do the talking. Lecture breeds defence. Defence reopens debate in your own head. Live free loud and talk soft.

When someone unwraps beside you, pity, do not envy. Poor soul, still paying to blur what you meet straight. Does that really look like enjoyment — the rustle, the gulp, the hollow an hour later? You are not being deprived. They are. When someone says you have amazing will, smile and tell the truth plain: no iron was needed, only seeing. When kindness offers cake, answer kind and plain: no thank you, I am happy without it. No speech. No scorn. No fear of offence. A free eater needs no excuse and gives no lesson unless asked with open heart.

I congratulate you not for effort but for seeing. You did not climb. You stepped out. Mornings prove it. Shops prove it. Supper proves it. The sofa proves it. Each ordinary day lived free writes the truth deeper till thought itself turns glad by nature.

### WHEN THE THOUGHT CROSSES

Thoughts will cross. Let them.

A wrapper by a bench. Bakery air on a quiet street. The old hour by the old chair. A hard day sliding forward a kind voice: you deserve one, life is hard, a little comfort will help. A bright day sliding forward a clever voice: one to celebrate, just to taste, to show mastery.

Hear each for what it is. An old recording. A dead enemy knocking from habit. A memory, not an order. A smell, not a command.

Did a dose ever soften a hard day? It blurred the evening, fogged the head, hollowed the hour after, and left the hard day plus a heavier head to meet in the morning. Comfort lives in rest, in food that satisfies, in company, in trouble met with clear head. Does a dose prove mastery? A free eater proves freedom by having no question to answer — pity for the circling, relief in the chest, ease in the chair.

Do not push the thought away. You cannot push a thought gone by ordering it gone. Answer it at once with joy, loudly, gladly:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the head. Say it aloud when you can. Let it arrive with a smile. You do not fight the thought. You reframe it. The thought becomes a small festival. Pity those still caught, still paying to blur what freedom meets straight. Rejoice that you no longer feed the wheel. Then walk on light to the meal ahead.

If a faint stirring comes as the old hour comes round, a light gnaw, a vague reach for something bright, smile. Note it. Welcome its passing. Keep hands on life and mind on the room. Minutes pass. Freedom stays. Hunger lasts till fed true. Thirst lasts till drunk true. The false gnaw thins while you live on.

Hear me on slips, so no accident can grow teeth.

Your body can cope with an accidental blip — sauce sweetened without your knowing, a bite taken before you saw, a host’s dish dressed brighter than you knew. Your mind must not steer toward it. Note the rumble. Straighten the seeing. Drive on happy to be free.

A crumb unchosen revives nothing unless belief is let back in. Do not wail. Do not confess. Do not start counting from zero. Wash the mouth with water. Eat next when hungry. Think BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! and mean it — the wheel did not restart because dust touched it. A rumble strip wakes the driver. It does not crash the car.

But never book accident as permission. A chosen one nurses fantasy, rehearses anticipation, fattens glow, and wakes the very echo you starved. Got away with it never means do it again. Rumble never means steer toward the ditch.

You will not steer there. Why would you? What would you buy? A brief blaze that thins by the third chew, a coat on the teeth, a fog in the head, a hollow an hour later asking for more. That is not a treat. That is a bill. You have stopped paying it.

I tell you this not to frighten you. Fright never freed anyone. I tell you so you walk light, knowing the road holds no hidden cliff. The seat of your freedom is seeing, not perfect footsteps. Keep seeing straight and the body follows glad.

Guarding is not vigilance. Seeing is rest. You do not sit on watch over thoughts. You answer a thought once with joy and live on. Doubt is not wisdom returning. Doubt is habit pacing by an open door. Smile at the pacing. It passes.

Ask the future plainly and answer from your ribs.

If passing bright packets by were loss, why does passing them by feel like relief? If one bright packet completed celebration, why did completed celebrations always ask for another within the hour? If freedom needs endless teaching, why does a single joyful answer leave you brighter than an hour of debate?

You know the answer. Freedom teaches itself by living.

### THE PICTURE TO KEEP

Keep one picture and let the rest go.

Photograph this list. Keep it on your phone, on the fridge, where tired eyes can find it. These are the agreements that freed you, in the order you made them. If doubt ever whispers cost, read them and remember you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain and the change came easily, immediately and permanently because seeing did the work.

Read them slow. Each holds a room you walked through. Each holds a wheel you stepped off. No more teaching needed after this. Only living.

Question what you think you know about sweetness.
2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.
3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.
4. NEVER BLAME YOURSELF AGAIN

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!
