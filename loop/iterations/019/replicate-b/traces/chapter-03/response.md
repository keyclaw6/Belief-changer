Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The kitchen table strewn with yesterday's wrappers
The bathroom mirror and the dentist's lamp
The wallet, the clock and a blank column on a plain page
A hand held out after a push in the dark

*Forget for a minute what it costs you and tell me straight what it ever gave you.*

**THE WRONG FIGHT**

You have already seen the hand move before the mind in a quiet kitchen. You have felt shame slide off your shoulders to its rightful address. Good. Keep that lightness. We carry it into a sharper question.

Most sweet talk is a harm argument. The dentist warns. The doctor warns. The mirror warns. And the trapped eater answers, half-defiant, half-apologetic:

"Yes, I know it is bad for me. But I like it. It gets me through. It is my little treat when I feel I deserve one."

I know that voice. I lived inside it for years. I said it with a laugh at birthdays and with a low mutter at midnight. I believed harm was the price and pleasure was the purchase, and as long as I was willing to pay, no one had the right to lecture me.

Let us leave the price to one side. I am not here to frighten you with harm. Harm talk never freed anyone, because harm can always be bargained with. Tomorrow I will eat less. Next week I will be good. One small dose cannot hurt. We all know how to answer harm with delay.

I ask a different question, a fair one, investigator to investigator.

If BAD SUGAR is your pleasure, your help, your comfort in a grey day, what exactly does it do FOR you?

Not what does it cost. What does it give?

Look at the honesty of that switch. Harm versus benefit is a fight you can wriggle through forever. Benefit alone has nowhere to hide. Either there is something real on the table, or there is not.

If it gave you something real, could you name it right now without naming a low it first relieves? If the pleasure were solid, would you need this book to defend it? And if after an ordinary day the benefit column stands empty, what are you still paying for?

**THE DAY ON THE TABLE**

Take an ordinary day, not a bad day, not a binge day. A normal moderate day. Lay it out on the table the way an investigator lays out evidence.

Morning. A sweetened coffee, a pastry on the way to work, a spoonful here and there. Bright packets, quick bites. Noon. A sandwich that is more bread than food, a fizzy drink, a biscuit with tea because everyone else reaches. Afternoon. The roller coaster dips. Sugary somethings to get me through. Evening. The permitted sliver after dinner that widens. Night. The rustle you promised would not happen.

Now set four plain witnesses beside the wrappers.

Mood. Write how you felt at eight in the morning, at three in the afternoon, at ten at night. Not theory. The actual weather inside. Bright? Flat? Twitchy? Foggy? Short term lift then fast drop?

Teeth. Run the tongue along the back molars. Remember the last visit, the lamp in the eyes, the scrape, the bill, the lecture about sweets you already knew by heart.

Wallet. Add the coins. The bar here, the bottle there, the bakery bag, the checkout extras that were never on the list. Small money daily becomes large money quietly.

Time. Count the minutes spent thinking about sweets, getting sweets, hiding sweets, regretting sweets, promising about sweets. Not eating. Thinking.

Two columns. Left: what it did TO you. Right: what it did FOR you.

Left fills fast. Fog after the buzz. That empty, gnawing urge returning on schedule whether hungry or not. Money leaking. Minutes leaking. Teeth paying.

Right stays white.

I want you to feel the whiteness of that right column, because we have all stared at it and looked away. We write "comfort" and remember we felt worse after. We write "energy" and remember the 4pm slump that needed a second hit. We write "treat when you feel you deserve one" and remember chewing fast over the sink without tasting. The words rub out under their own weight.

We were taught to call this accounting pleasure. It was never pleasure. It was traffic.

And here is the question that seeds the whole book. If a man tripped you on your own path each morning and then held out his hand with a smile to lift you, would you call him your rescuer and thank him for his kindness?

Hold that picture lightly. We will meet him properly later. For now let the question do its work on the blank column.

**WHAT THE BODY PAYS**

Let me speak plainly about the body, as settled fact, without lecture and without gloom.

Free sugars in frequent doses feed the acid on the teeth that rots them. That is mechanism, not moral. Brushing and fluoride matter, other factors mediate, mouths differ — and still the frequent sweet bath drives plaque acid that eats enamel. The dentist's drill is not bad luck. It is arithmetic with many authors, and BAD SUGAR holds the pen often.

Across populations, ordinary intake sits far above guidance. The public ceiling is below ten percent of daily energy from free sugars, about fifty grams, with further benefit below five percent, because current habits run far past both. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low; the slow warming is why the frog never notices. I am not writing your medical fate here, only the shape at population level — and if you live with diabetes, take medication that moves blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry any condition where food changes carry risk, your clinician leads on that and this book is belief work only.

Read those facts and feel their weight. Then set them aside as motive.

I do not want you to walk free from fear. Fear makes the sweet glow brighter in memory and turns freedom into trial. I want you to walk free because the glow was never there.

Harm is what it does TO you. Plenty, as you see. The trap loves harm talk because harm talk keeps the debate on price. Price can be haggled. "Just one." "Just Fridays." "Just after I have been good." Haggle all your life and you haggle for the rest of your life hooked, paying daily for something you do not even need.

Or you can ask the other question and be free for the rest of your life, never paying at all for a hunger that was never yours.

Two rests-of-life. Same years. One spent feeding a loop. One spent living. Which would you sign?

**A TREAT THAT TREATS NOTHING**

We called it a treat since childhood. Sticker for brave. Chocolate for celebrating with chocolate. Something sweet after being good. The word stuck like sugar on fingers.

We called it fuel as adults. I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling at three o'clock. A quick hit to get through the meeting, the drive, the homework hour. Short lift, fast drop, second reach. We read the lift as proof and forgot the drop was part of the same dose.

I believed both names. I defended both. I told myself life without sweet hits would be greyer, slower, less kind. I told myself a grown person with a hard week had earned a little glow.

Watch what those names cover in lived life.

A treat you hide is no treat. A treat that hurries past taste is no treat. A treat that leaves you flatter ten minutes after, reaching again before the hour ends, has treated nothing. Real treats close. They satisfy and release you back to the day. This one opens and holds you in the doorway, nagging for repeat.

Fuel that borrows from the next hour is no fuel. Real fuel steadies and carries you. This one lifts and drops and charges interest in fog. You never rise above the normal of a body left alone. You sink below it and pay for the ladder back to level.

Do you see the shape now? The rescuer and the perpetrator wear the same bright coat. The low arrives on schedule. The dose quiets it briefly. Gratitude flows to the hand that quieted what the same hand had stirred. Round and round, kind to the pusher, harsh to yourself.

I ask you again, without scolding, without scare, as one conned eater to another. Name one solid thing it added to yesterday that the day itself did not already hold. The laugh at the table. The sun on the walk. The hunger met by food. The rest after work done. Which of those needed sugar to exist? Which was not paler for being chased with a wrapper?

The fact is, there is nothing to name. Yesterday proves it. Today will prove it again if you watch with clear eyes. It was never a genuine treat or fuel.

Say that sentence in your own mouth and feel how clean it sounds when the evidence of your own table sits behind it. Not a slogan. A description. The benefit the whole slavery rests on dissolves under its own diary.

**IT DOES PLENTY — AND NOTHING**

We have reached the hinge of this book, and I want it to land like a door closing on noise.

We entered this room conceding harm and guarding benefit. We laid an ordinary day on the table with its wrappers, its mood weather, its teeth, its coins, its stolen minutes. We spoke of population guidance and tooth acid without making them your sentence. We asked what a rescuer is who first creates the fall.

There is only one honest ledger left to keep.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Read it again slowly, because everything after hangs on it. Not less for you. Not little for you. Nothing. The left column crowded. The right column white. That whiteness is not deprivation. It is discovery. You cannot lose what never gave.

Does that frighten you? Good, feel it fully for a second. The idea that a lifelong friend gave nothing stings. Let it sting. Then feel the relief flood under the sting. If it gave nothing, there is nothing to miss, nothing to mourn, nothing to crave with love. There is only a con to leave and a life to enjoy with clear hunger and clear satisfaction.

So at the peak of this chapter I lay one clear command, to be followed exactly as spoken. It guards the new ground while the rest of the demolition follows in its own rooms. It asks nothing of will, only of attention.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

**SUMMARY**
- Arguing about harm keeps the loop alive because harm can always be haggled with tomorrow.
- An ordinary day laid beside mood, teeth, money and time leaves the benefit side blank.
- Frequent sweet doses feed tooth acid and ordinary intake runs far above guidance, while brushing, fluoride and other factors still matter.
- Population facts describe the shape of the trap, not a personal fate, and medical care stays with clinicians where risk lives.
- The low that calls for a dose arrives on schedule and the dose only quiets what the loop had stirred.
- Life holds warmth, hunger and satisfaction on its own, and the day's pleasure belongs to the day itself.
