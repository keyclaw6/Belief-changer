ORDINARY DAYS

*You are already free, so ordinary days are simply lived, not watched.*

**THE FIRST MORNING**

Wake up and notice what is not there.

No arithmetic. No promise made to a box. No bargain about being good this week. No low hum of hunt underneath the morning.

You slept out of the cell and you woke in your own kitchen. Make breakfast hungry. Eat it sitting down. Fresh bread, eggs, fruit, porridge, leftovers warmed — whatever real food you love when hunger calls clean.

We used to start the day with fog and a fix, chasing a lift that dropped us by mid-morning, then calling that drop a need for another hit. That was life inside the Sugar Trap. That noise is gone.

Eat when you are hungry. Stop when you are satisfied. Notice the signal land warm and plain, like a small child dropping a half-eaten peach to run off and play. No counting. No guilt. The body knows its work when the con stops shouting over it.

I eat slowly now on free mornings. I taste the crunch and the salt and the juice. I stop mid-bite because satisfaction has arrived, not because a rule tells me to stop. Do you feel how light the head is without the spike-then-drop? That clarity is not a reward you earned. That clarity is your natural baseline, returned.

This is marvellous news. Nothing has been taken from the morning. The morning has been handed back.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry medical risk, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

**THE SHOP AT MIDDAY**

Go to the shop today. Go at the ordinary hour. Walk the ordinary aisle.

The packs still shout. Bright wrappers. Cartoon promises. Bliss-point crunch in a bag built to be finished in one sitting. Checkout ambush at tired-eye height. We walked this aisle for years feeling a pull in the chest and calling it love. It was never love. It was a learned hunt wearing a friendly face.

Walk it now as a free eater.

You will see trapped users hurrying past with their fixes, arms full of doses for tonight. Look at them warmly. Does that hurried grab really look like enjoyment? Does that flat stare at the shelf look like a treat? You are not being deprived — they are. They are still paying for a low they mistake for a lift. Pity them and walk on with ease. Envy has no foothold because there is nothing to envy.

Pick up what you came for. Real food for hungry people. Bread that smells of bread. Cheese. Vegetables from the market pile. Fruit that shines. Put them in the basket and feel the relief in the shoulders. No tug. No whisper that one small dose for the road would make the day sweeter. The day is already sweet with freedom.

At the checkout, the small bars wait where tired hands reach. Let them wait. You have no business with them and they have no business with you. Smile at the bright colours and pay for your food and step out into the daylight. Happy to be free while the hunt goes on behind you.

That ease is not luck. That ease is truth seen.

**THE EVENING SOFA**

Evening comes. This is the hour the old script loved best.

Lamplight. Sofa. Quiet after noise. Tired body asking for comfort. For years we answered that hour with rustle and chew, a box emptied before the swallow ended, regret before pleasure ever arrived.

Sit in the same place tonight and live it free.

Eat supper hungry. A plain favourite meal, noticed and savoured. Company if you have it. Quiet if you need it. Stop satisfied and let the warmth settle. Then sit on the sofa with tea, with a book, with talk, with nothing at all. Feel the evening hold without a wrapper in it.

Does a faint memory drift by — the dim lights and shared chocolate, laughter in the room, love close? Let it come. It is only a memory of a happy scene, and the happiness lived in the scene, in the people, in you. The dose was only ever sneaking a ride on that warmth. The warmth remains. The rider is gone.

The sofa is softer without the noise. The night is longer in a good way. No second reach. No cupboard check. No full-belly emptiness. You get on with enjoying your life while the evening simply passes, marvellous and ordinary.

**WHEN THE THOUGHT DRIFTS BY**

Sooner or later a thought will drift by. It always does in the first days.

A picture of a biscuit. A smell from a bakery door. A tired moment whispering, “just one little dose to get through.”

Do not push the thought away. Do not argue with it. You cannot keep a thought from arriving, and you do not need to. A thought is not an order. A thought is a dead enemy walking past the window.

We spent years fearing those moments, believing each pang proved we still needed the fix. It never did. That empty, twitchy tug is the Nibbler dying, starved of belief, grumbling as it fades. It asks nothing heroic of you. It passes on its own while you sip tea and smile.

Answer it with joy, not with debate. The moment the memory crosses your mind, think bright and loud:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Feel the lift in the chest as you think it. Relief that you no longer feed the hunt. Pity for the trapped version of you that once obeyed that whisper. Celebration that the door stands open and you have walked through.

Ask yourself flat in that moment.

Does this thought bring pleasure or bring pity for a con seen through? Does it order you, or does it remind you that you have escaped? Is there any honest answer except laughter and relief?

There is none. The thought converts to joy the instant you meet it. Each time it arrives pre-labelled, each time it leaves weaker, until one day you notice you have forgotten to notice. That forgetting is freedom settling into bone.

**IF YOU STUMBLE ON A CRUMB**

Hear this warmly, because fear of a stumble keeps more minds hovering than any craving.

An accidental crumb in a sauce, a label misread, a bite taken in trust at a friend’s table — the body copes and the moment passes. Nothing revives unless the belief is invited back. A rumble strip on the road wakes you; it does not drive you into the ditch.

What must never happen is a deliberate special sweet. One exception kept on purpose tells the mind treasure still lives in the trap, and the hunt wakes with a calendar attached. Friday. Birthday. Holiday. Just-one-to-prove-I-am-free. Each kept sweet keeps the whole belief alive.

So guard the belief, not the behaviour with panic. If you stumble by accident, smile at the lesson, see the rumble for what it was, and walk on free. If the whisper suggests a deliberate taste, see it as the old script talking, pity it, and think your bright thought again: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

You have not blown anything. You cannot blow freedom with a crumb. You only lose it if you reopen the question and start mourning a friend that was always an enemy. Keep the question closed and the enemy stays dead.

**LEAVE THEM ALONE**

Do not preach. Do not convert the house. Do not lecture friends over cake.

We know the glow of escape and want everyone we love to feel it. But preaching breeds defence, and defence drags you back into arguments that reopen your own doubt. Let visible ease do the recruiting.

If someone offers you BAD SUGAR, answer simply and kindly. “No thanks, I don’t eat that now. I’m happy to be free.” No sermon. No warning about health. No tale of the con. Eat your real food with relish and leave them to their own seeing.

If someone eats it beside you, pity, don’t envy, and change the subject to life. Talk of work, of holidays, of the meal itself. Your freedom is loudest when it is quiet. One day they will ask why evenings look so easy on you. Then you can tell the story — warm to the person, harsh to the trap that conned us all.

Freedom needs no pulpit. It needs a lived day.

**CHANGE NOTHING ELSE**

Change nothing else in your life.

Keep your mornings. Keep your shops. Keep your meals, your routes, your sofa, your celebrations. Go everywhere you went. Sit everywhere you sat. Freedom proves itself by inhabiting every old context with ease, not by avoiding them.

We once believed quitting meant vigilance forever — watching, counting, dodging. That was the old method talking, the one that made leaving feel like a sentence. This is escape, not a sentence. There is nothing to watch. There is everything to enjoy.

Hunger calls. Satisfaction lands. Real food tastes like favourite food again. Energy runs level through the afternoon without the roller coaster. Evenings close soft without rustle and regret. Wonderful days stack into a wonderful life, exciting in their plainness, brilliant in their relief.

You walked out of the Sugar Trap with one vow. You stay out by living, not by guarding. Rejoice at the dead enemy. Pity the trapped. Forgive a rumble. Leave others in peace. Get on with enjoying your life.
