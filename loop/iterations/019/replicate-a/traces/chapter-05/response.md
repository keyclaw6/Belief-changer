Chapter 5
THE LIFT THAT MAKES THE LOW

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The desk at ten to three — The drawer, the buzz and the four o'clock fog — Shoes a size too small all day

*That lift is not fuel going in. It is the low from the last dose switching off for ten minutes.*

**THE AFTERNOON YOU KNOW BY HEART**

You know this afternoon by heart, because we all lived it.

Ten to three. The screen blurs. The shoulders ache. The morning feels a long way behind. A heaviness settles behind the eyes, that slow sinking that says the day has stalled.

And the thought arrives on cue, in your own voice:

“I need sugary somethings to get me through.”

We opened the drawer. I did. You did. We all did the same small ritual. The biscuit. The chocolate bar. The cola, the sweet tea, the handful from the jar by the till. A quick dose, eaten standing or half-hidden, barely tasted because tasting was never the point. Getting through was the point.

For ten minutes, buzz. A brightening. Fingers move faster. The meeting seems bearable. You tell yourself, there, you see, I needed that. Without it work stops.

Then half past three, quarter to four. The brightness thins. The mouth is louder than before. Concentration frays. At half past three it arrives as that empty, twitchy, slightly shaky, need-something-sweet-now feeling.

And by four o'clock, fog. Heavier than at three. Eyes gritty. Temper short. The same drawer calls louder the second time. The second dose is not a treat now. It feels like need. I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, urgent and righteous, as if the body is issuing an order.

Tell me honestly. Did that second call feel like pleasure?

We call it fuel. We say it gets us through the afternoon. I said it for years. Of course you believe it — that is why you reach. And of course the ten-minute buzz seems to prove it.

It proves nothing of the kind.

Look at the hour with clear eyes. At ten to three you were low. At five past three, after the dose, you were almost normal for ten minutes. At four you were lower than at three. That is not fuel going in. That is a low being switched off, then switched back on deeper.

The fact is, the lift-then-drop is how the loop describes itself. “I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop.” You have said some version of that yourself, after every yo-yo day.

If the dose gave energy, why did the afternoon cost more energy than it gave? If it helped concentration, why did concentration get worse across the day, not better? And why did the colleague at the next desk, doing the same dull work on water and a proper lunch, sail through the same hour without any buzz or fog at all?

There is only one answer that fits all three. You did not top up a tank. You quieted a disturbance the last top-up had left.

**SHOES A SIZE TOO SMALL**

Let me show you with feet.

Imagine you agree to wear shoes a size too small from nine in the morning. Tight across the toes. Pinch at the heel. Every step nips. You limp to meetings. You wince on stairs. At lunch you sit and prise them off for ten minutes and sigh with relief. Marvellous relief. Real relief. You tell everyone taking them off is one of life’s great pleasures.

Then you lace them back on for the afternoon. By three the pinch is agony. By four you can think of nothing but taking them off again. The evening removal feels like bliss.

Would you call those shoes a genuine pleasure? Would you say they give your feet energy? Would you thank them for the relief?

Of course not. The relief is real, but the shoes created the pain the removal relieves. You never rise above the comfort of a person who wore fitting shoes all day. You sink below it, climb briefly toward it, then sink again.

BAD SUGAR does the same in the afternoon. The morning dose tightens the shoe. The three o’clock dose loosens it for ten minutes. The loosening feels like fuel. It is not fuel. It is ten minutes of not-pinching, bought at the price of pinching all day.

I know this is hard to accept, because the buzz feels so physical. Keep an open mind and watch what happens without the story. The buzz does not add anything you lacked at nine before the first dose. It returns you for a few minutes toward the state you had before the loop taught you to need the return. Then the fog returns, and you are left flat, foggy and never satisfied.

Read that last line again in your body, not your head. After the buzz, after the hurry, after the wrapper, what is the permanent state across weeks and months? Not bright. Not powered. Flat in mood by evening. Foggy in head by afternoon. Never satisfied, always listening for the next call from the drawer.

That is the cost wearing the mask of benefit. The shoe never gave the foot anything. The dose never gave the afternoon anything.

**IT IS THE OTHER WAY AROUND**

You were told it was a genuine treat or fuel.

Let us test that claim the way an investigator tests any claim — by isolating the facts.

You say: I need it to function.

I ask you, with warmth and with total certainty: function at what?

Did you need it to run and laugh as a child before the loop began? No. Did the non-user in your office need it to finish the same report? No. Do you function better at five after two doses than you functioned at nine before the first? No — you function worse. More tired. Less patient. More fogged.

So what did the dose do?

For some of us the surge brings its own aftermath and the dip after is physical, insulin doing its work after the rush. That afternoon fog is not clinical low blood sugar, and I am not diagnosing you. Each dose also re-fires the wanting — a small echo that says again, not pleasure, but want more. That is why the second reach feels stronger than the first.

Do you see the shape? The rescuer is the perpetrator. The hand that lifts you out in the morning is the hand that pushed you low by afternoon. We thanked the rescuer because we never saw the push.

It is not that you concentrate because of the dose. You struggle to concentrate until the dose, because the last dose taught the hour to demand the next. It is not that the dose relieves the aggravation of the afternoon. It causes the aggravation, then briefly mutes it.

It is the other way around.

The lift makes the low. The low sells the next lift.

Once seen, it cannot be unseen. No dose ever carried you above the line of a free eater. Every dose dragged the line down, lifted you for ten minutes toward where you started, then dropped you a little lower to guarantee the next sale.

That is the whole fuel story. One short verdict: the buzz is borrowed comfort, and the repayment is fog.

**NOT FREE ENERGY, A DEFERRED LOAD**

There is a heavier truth here, and I will state it flatly because you deserve plain speech.

The fact is, at population level, diets heavy in added sugar are linked with a heavier load on the body — liver overload from the constant surplus, raised blood pressure, chronic low-grade inflammation, appetite signals bypassed so hunger and fullness misfire, and with that pattern a higher risk of heart and circulation trouble over time. Frequent free-sugar doses also feed the acid on the teeth that rots them. Feeling fine in the buzz does not mean the intake is low. The water can warm slowly while you swear it is comfortable.

I do not want you to change because that frightens you, and I am not saying one bar writes your future or diagnosing you in any way. I am not giving medical advice, so if you live with diabetes or take anything that affects blood sugar, follow your clinician first.

Hear the relief inside the hard fact: you do not need to be frightened into escape. Fear never freed anyone. You change because there was never free energy on offer — only a deferred load sold as a lift. Seeing that, you lose nothing by walking away. You regain mornings that do not start in deficit and afternoons that do not end in fog.

Ask yourself the closing questions and answer honestly.

If it were fuel, would quitting it leave you with more energy, as every free eater reports? If it powered work, why does work collapse without it only for the user, never for the non-user? If it were food, why does it leave you hungrier, twitchier, emptier than plain hunger ever did?

You know the answers because you lived them at the desk. The drawer did not power you. The break, the stand, the stretch, the glass of water, the breath at the window — life powered you, and the dose merely sneaked a ride on that ten-minute pause and claimed the credit.

Give the credit back where it belongs. The lift was yours. The low was the dose’s. Wonderful relief waits on the side where no shoe pinches at all.

**5. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD**
You are escaping, not losing.

**SUMMARY**

* The three o’clock dose does not add power, it mutes for ten minutes the heaviness the earlier dose left behind.
* Buzz followed by fog followed by a louder second call is the signature of relief mistaken for fuel, not proof of need.
* Like tight shoes, the loop creates discomfort all day and sells brief removal as pleasure, while the wearer never rises above normal.
* Each dose re-fires wanting rather than giving pleasure, and for some the surge brings an after-dip, while everyday fog is not a medical low.
* Heavy added-sugar patterns carry a deferred bodily load at population level, and feeling fine in the buzz does not mean intake is low.
* Daylight energy belongs to the body and the break, and the afternoon proves it hour by hour once the credit is returned.
