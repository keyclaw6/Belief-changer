Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The photographed list on the table — The closed book — The meal ahead, eaten hungry — The morning mirror — The open day

*You need no more teaching to stay free; sweetness has been handed back and life is waiting to be enjoyed.*

**YOU NEED NOTHING MORE**

You are free. Not nearly free. Not free if you are careful. Free.

I know the whisper that comes at this point, because we all hear it on the edge of a good life:

“But how will I stay free without more help? Surely I need ongoing teaching? Surely I need to keep studying, reminding, preparing?”

You do not. That whisper is the last echo of the old belief that freedom is fragile and must be guarded with effort.

The fact is you stay free the way you stay awake after waking. You do not practise waking. You live it.

We spent years inside a loop that told us sweetness was love and fuel and comfort, then left us flat, foggy and hungry again within the hour, reaching again before the swallow ended. We called that living. We blamed our character when moderation rules collapsed. It was never character. It was a con doing its ordinary work, creating the emptiness it pretended to solve.

Now you see it. Seeing does not wear off. Once you have seen that BAD SUGAR does nothing for you, you cannot unsee it.

Ask yourself plainly:

If BAD SUGAR truly gave you something real, why did every dose leave you wanting the next one?

If life without it were grey, why does food taste louder, mornings feel cleaner, evenings feel yours again?

If you needed more teaching, why does the thought of another dose feel shabby beside the relief you feel now?

You know. The teaching is done. The relief is you.

**THE PICTURE TO KEEP**

Take a photograph of this page with your mind, and with your phone if you wish. This is your portable memory. Not a course to study. A clear glass to look through whenever noise rises.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You have followed them. Keep them as settled knowing, not as rules to wrestle.

Here they are, bare and whole:

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Question what you think you know about sweetness.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

4. NEVER BLAME YOURSELF AGAIN
Every failure was the method, never you.

5. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD
You are escaping, not losing.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

7. SEE THE GRUMBLE AS DYING, NOT DEMANDING
That feeling is the Nibbler starving.

8. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Other voices rebuild the Con.

9. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the wrong method talking.

10. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

11. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

12. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

Read them once more and feel how light they are. No counting. No fear. No vigilance as a way of life. Clear seeing lived as ordinary ease.

When a sweet thought crosses your mind next week, bright and quick, you will not need a lecture. You will think, at once, laughingly, from the heart: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

That glad answer is your thought for life. It turns each echo into a fresh hit of relief. The wire thins. Soon weeks pass without a flicker. When a flicker comes, you smile because it lets you feel freedom again.

**CHOOSE WHICH LIFE**

Every eater chooses, whether they know it or not, what they want for the rest of your life.

There is one rest-of-life on the old road: the lift-crash-repeat for the rest of your life, the 3pm fog and the 10pm rustle, the box emptied before pleasure arrived, money and hours and toothache and tiredness paid daily for something that never gave.

And there is the other rest-of-life: mornings, shops and meals lived clear for the rest of your life, hunger met and left at satisfied, afternoons worked through without a dip to top, evenings owned, food enjoyed as favourite.

Which do you want? It is no contest once seen.

We were told freedom would be deprivation, a greyer plate, a duller party. The reality is the other way around. The grey was the fog after the dose. The colour is what returns when the fog lifts.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is not a diet rule. That is eating as the body designed it, as the child lives it, as you lived it before the con installed its noise. Hunger is clean. Satisfaction is complete. Real food — bread warm, fruit bright, a proper meal eaten hungry and left at enough — is pleasure enough without a wrapper’s promise on top.

You change nothing else in your life. Drink your tea as you always drink it. Go where you go. See who you see. Walk the same aisle with your head high. The packs still shout to the trapped. To you they are bright paper around a shabby trick. You feel relief and quiet gladness that you no longer feed it. Pity the hurried hand in the bag, warmly, privately, without a word. You know the hand is not enjoying. It is feeding a low the last dose created.

That knowing is marvellous, wonderful freedom. Celebrate it. Rejoice in it daily by living.

**SWEETNESS HANDED BACK**

Here is the fresh truth I saved for this last hour together, and I want you to let it cheer you to the bone.

You have lost no sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

Think how a child tastes a peach. Juice down the chin. Eyes wide. Then halfway through, done, running off to play. No counting. No guilt. No second reach to chase a fading buzz. The sweet is part of life, met hungry, left at satisfied, wrapped in sunshine and play.

We lived that once. Then the Trap taught us to chase sweetness in packets that coat the mouth and dull the tongue, that lift for ten minutes then drop us lower, that train us to call fog pleasure and low hunger. It stole true sweetness and sold us a copy that leaves us flat, foggy and never satisfied.

Now the copy has gone. The original remains.

This was never about quitting life. This was about growing back into the eater you were.

You do not leave the table poorer. You return to the table with clean taste, true hunger, full satisfaction. Fruit sings again. A meal satisfies. Water refreshes. The evening’s laughter — the company, the story, the rest held close — stays yours, brighter, because no fog settles over it.

So close the book. Leave my voice behind. You do not need me whispering in your ear.

Get on with enjoying your life. Eat hungry. Stop satisfied. Laugh fully. Walk brightly past the shouting shelves. Meet the morning kitchen with pleasure. When memory knocks, answer with joy. When others eat fog, pity them and live your ease where they can see it without a speech.

You are not missing. You are meeting. You are free, happily free, for good.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!
