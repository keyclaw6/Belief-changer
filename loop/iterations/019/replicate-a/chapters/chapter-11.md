Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The ordinary kitchen at an ordinary hour — A single biscuit on a plain saucer — The spoken vow across the table — The clean mouth and the lifted evening

*There is no safer tomorrow to get free; the next ordinary dose seen clearly is the door out.*

**TOMORROW IS THE CON**

You know there is no safe corner kept on purpose. You have watched the Friday square glow and spread waiting through the week. You have seen the clean cupboard with a busy mind. You have seen one kept taste carry on past stop.

And still a soft voice lingers, almost tender now.

“I see it all. I will do it properly. After the holidays. After the birthday. After this hard week. When I feel ready someday. Tomorrow will be cleaner.”

I hear you with warmth. We all said it. We believed delay was care, prudence, kindness to ourselves. We believed jumping now felt hasty and waiting felt wise.

The fact is waiting is not wisdom. Waiting is the last working part of the trap.

Delay does not weaken want. Delay feeds it while calling it preparation. Every further dose deepens the low that calls for the next. Every further day adds its bill in money, teeth, fogged afternoons, rustled evenings, for something that never once gave you a genuine lift that lasted. The mind that waits keeps the belief alive that there is something to say goodbye to. There is nothing to say goodbye to. There is a con to see through.

Ask yourself, kindly, as the investigator you have become:

If tomorrow were safer, why has every yesterday with the same promise delivered you to the same evening with the same gnaw?

If waiting prepared you, why do you feel no more ready after thirty more doses than you feel now?

Will the voice that says wait ever one morning say now?

You know the answers. The voice never says now. That is its job. It keeps the tug-of-war of craving and fear alive by pulling both ropes at once — fear of staying hooked and fear of living free — so you hover in the middle and eat.

See delay for what it is and the hover ends. You do not need a perfect calendar. You need a clear eye now.

**ARE YOU CHAMPING AT THE BIT?**

Let me gate you honestly, because I will not push you with will and I will not coax you with fear.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit. After all you have seen — the lift that makes the low, the bright room that gave nothing, the rule that made the want precious — you should be impatient to close the door and get on with enjoying your life.

If you are not, do not summon force. Force was the old method talking. Go back and read again with an open mind until the picture is bright. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

If you are ready, rejoice. And remember you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

I say this as one who stood where you stand, biscuit in hand, heart thumping, wanting certainty. I promise you certainty comes not before the vow but with it. You leave this trap easily, immediately and permanently, the moment belief goes, not after months of proving.

There is no test to pass. There is a truth to see. You have seen it.

So do not wait to be free. You become free now.

**TAKE IT WITH OPEN EYES**

We will not make a laboratory of this. No ceremony with special sweets, no last binge, no mournful farewell tour of favourite shops. Take your last ordinary treat exactly as you would have taken it anyway.

This afternoon or this evening, at your normal hour, put one ordinary dose on a plate — a biscuit with tea, a square you would have eaten without thinking, whatever is ordinary for you. Sit down. Eat it normally. But keep your attention on, wide awake for once.

Notice the cloy. Notice how sickly sweet it is when you are not half-asleep in habit. Notice how it coats the mouth, how the second bite is duller than the first, how pleasure slides off within seconds and want slides in behind it. Notice the faint nausea under the glow, the fog gathering at the edges, the old I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling already rehearsing its return before you have swallowed.

We ate for years without looking. We called that blur enjoyment. Looked at straight, it is ugly. Not tragic ugly. Shabby ugly. A trick seen in daylight.

Is this the great reward you were afraid to lose?

Is this the fuel that carried your afternoons?

Would a true pleasure need you to switch your attention off to survive it?

You know it would not. The dose does plenty to the mouth, the mood, the evening. It does nothing for the joy. The room holds you. The tea warms you. The pause rests you. The dose rode along and sent the bill.

And the little echo after — that empty, twitchy stir — see it for what it is. It is tiny. It is mild and brief, a days-long hump that peaks and passes, not a dread to arm against. Smile at it as a dying grumble. It has no power to compel you. It only has power if the belief feeds it.

You are not losing a friend. You are spotting a fraud with your mouth full, and spotting is freedom.

**THE TWO WHISPERS YOU ALREADY KNOW**

Before you vow, let me pre-play the two thoughts that will knock later, so they arrive already answered. They are not orders. They are echoes. When they come, you will smile.

First: the bad-day rescue.

“You have had a dreadful day. You are tired, hurt, wrung out. One sweet mouthful will soften it. You deserve comfort.”

We believed that for years. We ate on tired evenings and called the blur comfort. Did the dose mend the day? Or did it add fog to tiredness, shame to hurt, a second reach to the first, and leave the original ache untouched with interest charged?

Ask: if it comforted, why did every comforted evening end flatter, foggier, less satisfied?

If it helped, why did the helped days need helping again the next night?

You know comfort lived elsewhere — in rest, in food eaten hungry and left at satisfied, in company, in sleep. The dose never comforted. It interrupted. Pity the thought when it knocks. It is the old script, not your reasoning.

Second: the innocent one.

“Just one will not hurt now. You are free. Prove it with one, taken calmly. One square cannot rebuild anything.”

Listen with kind eyes, because brave people have fallen for gentleness.

We tried the proof. One ordinary bite re-wakes the whole circuit — the tiny echo and, far louder, the belief that relief lives in the wrapper. The craving, once triggered, carries on past stop. That is not proof you are weak. That is the trap doing its ordinary work when fed.

Ask: if one were nothing, why in your own true history did one never stay one?

If one proved freedom, why did every proved one leave you less free?

A door left ajar does not prove the house is warm. It lets the draught back in.

You do not prove freedom with a dose. You prove it with relief. Tomorrow morning a sweet thought will cross your mind, light as a passing van. You will not wrestle it. You will not mourn it. You will feel a lift of gladness that you no longer feed it. Like a freed lion pacing beside an open cage for a moment from habit, you may feel a strange wobble — then you see the door is open and you walk on, glad.

And in that moment you will have your new thought, the one you will keep for life. It is not a rule to remember. It is joy speaking: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it now under your breath and feel its shape. It is the sound of escape, not loss. Marvellous, wonderful relief. Whenever sweetness crosses your mind for the rest of your days, that is what you will think, and each thought will make the next freer.

You are ready. The calendar has no power. The cupboard has no power. The echo has no power. Only belief had power, and belief has changed.

**11. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM**
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

**SUMMARY**

* Waiting never made quitting safer, it only kept the belief fed while feeling like care.
* Readiness is impatience to be free, not perfect calm, and certainty arrives with the vow.
* One ordinary dose watched closely tastes cloying and shabby, not precious.
* The low after the bite is a tiny days-long echo that passes, not a force to fear.
* A bad day is not mended by a dose, comfort lives in rest and real hunger met.
* One deliberate taste re-wakes want and carries past stop, which shows the trap working.
* Freedom is conferred now, with relief that lifts whenever the old thought passes.
