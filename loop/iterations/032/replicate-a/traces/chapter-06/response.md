Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD
*Hunger met with real food is pleasure itself, and you have lost nothing you ever needed.*

YOU FEAR THE GREY PLATE

You bring no shame into this room. You bring the clear eyes that watched the afternoon tell the truth and the steady hands of an investigator who no longer blames herself. Keep them with you. We need them for something kinder now.

We have lived the tight hours together. We have seen the bright ten minutes thin and the fog return. We know the hand moving to the drawer before the mind has voted. That sight stays with you. I am not asking you to revisit it. I am asking you to eat with me.

You say it quietly, almost embarrassed to say it out loud:

“I see the lift made the low. But tell me straight — without sweet hits, will eating not turn grey? Will food become duty? Pudding was the warm part of the day in my house. Dessert was love.”

I hear you. I honour the fear. Every comfort eater who called dessert love has stood where you stand. We all believed food joy was sweet joy. We believed hunger was an enemy to be silenced quickly and satisfaction was a rumour other people talked about.

The fact is, hunger is not your enemy. Hunger is the beginning of pleasure. Satisfaction is not a rumour. Satisfaction is the clean stop your body built into you long before packets taught you to ignore it.

You do not believe me yet. Good. You are an investigator now, not a believer. Let me take you to eat, and let your own mouth answer.

Ask yourself as we walk, and answer from life, not from slogans:

When did a sweet afternoon ever leave you quiet and done the way a hungry meal leaves you?

When did more bright packets ever finish a want, and when did they only postpone it by minutes?

If dessert were love, why do the sweetest evenings so often end with a sticky mouth and a heavy sleep and a stale morning?

There is only one honest answer. Eating itself is joy. Sweet only shouted over it.

A MARKET MORNING

Come with me on an ordinary Saturday, hungry in the honest way.

You slept. You woke clear because the night had no hurry in it. No late grazing. No sticky mouth at midnight. You drank water. You pulled on a coat and walked to the market with an empty belly that feels open and calm, not panicked, not chanting. There is a difference you will soon know in your skin. Open hunger welcomes. It waits. It does not stamp its foot for one bright thing.

Stalls bright in cool air. Tomatoes still warm from the van, skins tight, smelling of vine and summer. Greens with water beaded on them. New potatoes earthy and rough. Apples firm and cold, squeaking in the hand. A loaf with a dark crust that cracks when you press it, steam still inside the crumb. Cheese with a rind that smells of cellar. Eggs brown and speckled. A peach that smells like itself from a foot away, fuzz catching light.

You buy simply, what catches eye and nose. No list from a plan. No counting. No bargaining with yourself. Your body leads and your eyes follow. You carry the bag home and it bumps your leg and the bread warms your arm through paper.

Home. You slice the bread thick. Butter melts in and pools in the holes. Tomatoes with salt and oil, sharp and warm together. Greens wilted in a hot pan with garlic, still bright at the edges. A little cheese. Water, cold. Then the peach for after, juice down the chin.

You sit down properly, hungry. You do not stand by the cupboard. You do not eat with one eye on the clock. You sit. First bite brightest. You notice. Crust crack, crumb soft, tomato sharp, oil round, greens clean and warm. Second bite steady. Third bite slower. You talk. You laugh. You pause. You drink water. You look out of the window. Middle of the plate the urgency softens. Later bites less urgent. Then it comes, unforced, almost unnoticed, like dusk: enough. You stop. Not because a rule says stop. Not because you are being good. Because satisfaction arrived on its own and there is nothing left to chase.

An hour later belly calm, head clear, hands willing. Two hours later steady still, ready for the next thing, not fidgety for the same thing. No buzz to remember. No fog to pay for. No second reach.

Did that meal need a bright packet to be joy?

Did hunger make the food grey, or did hunger make the food sing?

When did you last stop like that after sweet — stopped because done, not because the packet ended?

There is only one honest answer. That stop was favourite. Not a word trick. A bodily fact.

I lived the opposite for years. Drawer full, belly loud, head thick. Hundreds of sweet calories at three and emptier at four. Mouth busy. Body unfed. How could numbers in leave power out unless the numbers were noise? A car filled with the wrong liquid does not run better for being full. It runs worse. So did we.

Your body is a marvellous precision machine. It knows hunger. It knows enough. Hear that with warmth, not as lesson. You do not need a new system to eat. You need the noise turned down so the signal can be heard.

HUNGER OPENS, THE LOOP NARROWS

We lost the difference because we stopped listening.

We learned to call every urgent mouth-noise hunger. We learned to read I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW as proof the body needs power. We learned to answer both open hunger and narrow urgency with the same bright thing, and then we wondered why we never felt fed.

Learn the difference again in your own skin. It takes one hungry day to remember.

Hunger opens. It is calm, patient, wide. It welcomes soup. It welcomes bread. It welcomes vegetables, fruit, water, a pause. It can wait twenty minutes while you chop and it grows kinder as you chop. It settles when fed and says thank you by going quiet for hours.

The sweet urgency narrows. It is fidgety, specific, hurried. It does not want soup. It does not want bread. It does not want water and a stretch and air. It wants the bright packet and nothing else will do. Feed it sweet and it returns louder in the hour. Feed the body real food when hungry and it goes quiet, because there is no tightening left to relieve.

Watch it in your own afternoon. True hunger at one o'clock says, food would be good, many foods would be good, let me sit and eat. The narrow call at half past four says, now, bright, sweet, quick, nothing else counts. One is wide as a field. The other is narrow as a keyhole. Does a body asking for nourishment speak through a keyhole? Or does a loop asking to be repeated speak that way?

I lived deaf to this. I called both hunger. I answered both with sugar. I told myself honest workers need fuel and concentration lives in packets and afternoons prove the need. I stood in kitchens and at station shops obeying the chant like an order.

Ask with me now, as the forgiven investigator you are:

Does open hunger ever stamp its foot for one bright thing and refuse all else?

Does a hungry meal eaten hungry leave you chanting an hour later, or does it leave you quiet and ready?

When people who do not live on sweet hits reach three o'clock, do they collapse without sweet, or do they dip a little as humans do, drink water, stretch, and steady again?

There is only one honest answer. The chant is not the body asking for power. The loop is asking to be repeated.

Your body makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food eaten hungry — bread, potatoes, vegetables, fruit, meals — so living without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing essential missing, if you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only. For the rest of us the clean baseline without BAD SUGAR is physiologically coherent. Nothing essential is gone. The noise is gone. The food remains.

Does that sound like loss? Or does that sound like ears unblocked?

SATISFACTION IS A PLACE YOU STOP

We were taught satisfaction lives at the bottom of the packet. It does not. It lives in the body.

Eat hungry and watch the signals work, because they still work in you. First bites brightest. The mouth wakes. The nose wakes. Flavour loudest at the start, as it should be. Middle bites steady. You chew slower. You talk more. You put the fork down between bites without thinking. Later bites less urgent. You breathe out. You notice warmth spreading, belly calming, head clearing. Then done arrives. You push the plate away half-finished without triumph and without regret. You walk back to life light. Not stuffed. Not foggy. Done is a clean place. Done stays with you for hours.

Eat a sweet dose mid-afternoon and watch the opposite. First bite brightest for seconds. Second bite hurried. Third bite standing. Stop never arrives — only pause when the packet ends or eyes sting with reproach. An hour later belly loud, head thick, hands fidgety. Mouth full, body unfed. Numbers in, nourishment out.

Which taught you you are steady? The meal that left you steady taught you you are steady. The dose that left you foggy taught you you are foggy. One told truth. The other installed a lie and charged you for it.

Think of the body you were given before rules fenced it. Like that child who stops mid-bite and runs off to play, you knew done without being taught done. No counting. No guilt. No vow to be good tomorrow. Body led. Body said enough. Play called louder than more.

We laughed at that waste. We should have envied that ease. She had what we lost: a stop that arrives on its own.

You still have it. It was only drowned.

Try it hungry at supper. Soup hot, bread torn, not sliced neat. You blow, you sip. Warmth spreads. You chew slowly because there is time. You notice salt, pepper, oil, the crack of crust. You drink water. You pause. You have half left and the hurry is gone. You eat a little more. The brightness of first bites softens into steadiness. You put the spoon down. You breathe. Done. No ledger. No payback hunger tomorrow. No sweet penance.

Ask the plain questions and let your own suppers answer:

Has a sweet hit ever left you quiet and done for hours the way a hungry meal leaves you?

Has more sweet ever finished the want, or has more sweet only postponed the next want by minutes?

If satisfaction were in the packet, why does the packet never say enough?

There is only one honest answer. Satisfaction was never in the packet. Satisfaction is hunger met and left at done.

FAVOURITE IS NOT A TRICK OF WORDS

Now I will meet your suspicion head-on, because you are thinking it and I want you to hear it in your own voice:

“Favourite food. That is a play on words. You mean worthy food. You mean dull food I should love. You are dressing up loss as gain.”

I understand why the sentence sounds like a trick. We have been conned so often by worthy talk. I say it flat, with all the warmth I have: there is no catch. The pleasure is real.

I do not mean you must love boiled greens from duty. I mean hunger makes real food favourite in the mouth, not in the moral. Think of that bread, crust cracking, eaten hungry. Think of tomatoes salted, sharp and warm. Think of the peach, cold and fragrant, juice running. Did you need to persuade yourself to enjoy them? Did you need a lecture? Or did the mouth wake and the nose wake and the belly warm and the mind say, more of this life, please?

That is favourite. Not favourite as prize for being good. Favourite as eaten hungry, noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied.

Ventriloquize the rest of the doubt with me, fully, as you speak it inside:

“But I love the taste of chocolate. I love the cream, the crunch, the melt.”

Did you love the taste, or did you love the moment around the taste? Mouth novelty loves first seconds of anything bright. After seconds comes hurry. After hurry comes fog. After fog comes second reach. Loving first seconds that charge an hour is not love. It is a loan. When did that love ever leave you clean and done? When did it ever say enough?

“Celebrating with chocolate was love in our house.”

I honour that memory. Celebrating was love. The laughter, the company, the late light, the funny scene, the table full of people who know your name — that was love. The dose rode along. Meal, company, hunger met — that was the warmth you called dessert. You will see this whole when we meet your sweetest moment face to face. For now, notice this: the warmth stayed in the room after the wrapper left. The wrapper never held it.

“I've managed to be good this week. Food joy is my reward.”

Good weeks that end in foggy Fridays never proved food joy lives in sweet. They proved tight weeks manufacture Friday glow. Notice the ledger. Good means tight. Tight means edge. Edge means glow around the forbidden thing. True reward after hard work leaves you more able, not less. Rest leaves you more able. Food that feeds leaves you more able. Laughter leaves you more able. Air leaves you more able. A loan leaves you less.

Ask the three that close this room:

If food joy were sweet, why do the sweetest days end with the dullest mouths and the stalest mornings?

If dessert were love, why does love eaten hungry with bread and fruit and company feel warmer and cleaner and last longer?

If favourite were wordplay, why does the body say done with a quiet sigh after a hungry meal and never say done after sweet?

There is only one honest answer. Eating itself is joy. Sweet only shouted over it.

You fear a grey plate. Look at the market bag. Look at the peach. Look at the steam rising from soup. Where is the grey? The grey was never the absence of sweet. The grey was the weather after years of loans. Drain the loans and colour returns to the table that was there all along.

FRUIT IS FOOD, NOT A LOOPHOLE

You ask the clever trap question, and I welcome it:

“Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Bread turns to glucose too. It is all the same.”

No. It is not the same, and your body knows the difference before your mind argues it.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined sugar and added free sugars and junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses. Natural sugars inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals are not the target. They belong to the menu of hunger met.

See the difference in use, not in chemistry talk. A peach eaten hungry when you are hungry: noticed, savoured, stopped. Fibre, water, chew, pause, signals working, belly calming for hours. You eat one, perhaps two, and done arrives with juice on your chin and a quiet belly. A bar eaten fidgety at four: hurried, standing, stop never arriving, belly loud in the hour, hands edgy for more. You eat one and want its fellow.

One feeds hunger. The other bypasses hunger and shouts over the signal. One leaves done. The other leaves want.

Enjoying sweetness in a peach, in ripe tomatoes, in fresh bread, in a meal cooked with love, is not a lapse and is not forbidden. This book does not forbid enjoyment. Dessert as self-payment for virtue kept the loop alive because the frame said virtue must be paid in bright packets and stress must be paid the same way. “Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate” — that frame named the paymaster truly. Joy in a meal eaten hungry needs no payment and keeps no loop. The mouth was made to brighten at first taste. The body was made to steady after. Let both do their work.

Taste it to prove it. Eat an apple hungry, slowly, at the market, cold from the stall. First bite crack and flood. Second bite sweet and sharp together. Third bite quieter. You stop at the core without debate. Now remember a sweet drink drunk fidgety at the desk. First sip bright. Second sip hurried. Third sip gone. Want louder after than before. Which behaved like food? Which behaved like a call for its fellow?

I say this as settled fact: you were never made to live on hits. You were made to eat when hungry and stop when satisfied and get on with enjoying your life with a quiet mind.

No counting lives here. No good-food sermon lives here. No fear lives here. Hunger leads. Satisfaction stops. Pleasure follows both like a dog that never left.

THE ORDINARY LUNCH THAT PROVES IT

Let me stay with you for one ordinary lunch, so you feel it rather than agree with it.

One o'clock. True hunger, open and calm. Not the keyhole call. The field call. You sit. Soup hot, bread torn, not sliced neat. You blow, you sip. Warmth spreads from throat to belly. You chew slowly because there is time. You notice salt, pepper, oil, the crack of crust, the steam on your face. You drink water. You pause. You look out. You have half left and the hurry is gone.

You eat a little more. The brightness of first bites softens into steadiness. Talk flows easier. Shoulders lower. You put the spoon down. You breathe. Done. You push the bowl away half-finished without triumph and without regret. You walk back to work light, not heavy, not buzzing, not foggy. At three you dip a little as humans do. You stretch, drink water, open air. You steady again. At four you look up and notice you worked. At five you are still you. Supper hungry again, open again. Evening table lighter because the day collected no loans. Morning clearer because the night digested no hurry.

Compare dose by dose. Sweet top-up, brief buzz, fog and second reach. Hungry meal, warmth, steadiness, stop. Which behaved like energy? Which behaved like love?

I lived both. Sweet days: bright ten, scattered sixty, second dose, scattered thirty, fog, home stale, evening grazing to lift the evening low. Fed days: plain lunch eaten hungry, water, short walk, back to work, steady two hours, true tiredness at a true hour, home clear, morning clear. The difference was not subtle. One rode a coaster. The other walked a road.

You say, but my evenings need softening, my family table needs pudding to feel complete. Watch the table, not the pudding. Children talking over each other. Partner tired and funny. Steam rising. Bread passed hand to hand. Hunger met together. Stories told. Day laid down. Where is the grey? The grey you fear is not waiting in a meal without a bright packet. The grey was the permanent weather after loans — dull mouths, heavy eyelids, fidgety hands. Fed tables are loud with colour.

You say, but I want to get off the roller coaster and I fear the track is all I know. The track is not all you know. The road is older in you than the track. You walked it as a small eater before packets taught you hurry. You walk it every time you eat hungry and stop at done. One lunch proves it. One supper proves it again. One market morning proves it a third time until doubt looks thin.

Hear the verdict and let it stand: hunger met with real food satisfies in a way sweet never touched.

EAT AS THE BODY ASKS

You are ready for the rule that is not a rule. It is a return.

We fenced eating with clocks and ledgers and good-week accounting. Eat at noon whether hungry or not. Skip to be good. Graze to be kind. Pay virtue with sweet. Punish hurry with hunger. No wonder hunger went silent. We stopped asking it.

Ask it again. It answers at once.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that line as permission, not pressure. Want means real food you truly want when hungry — market vegetables, fresh bread, fruit, soup, cheese, eggs, meals that warm — noticed and enjoyed, not counted. Hungry means open hunger, not the narrow twitch for one bright thing. Satisfied means done, the quiet sigh, the fork down. Never punish yourself again means no payback hunger, no skipped meal to pay for pleasure, no sweet penance, no ledger. The body does not need correction. It needs hearing.

I promise you this as one who lost it and found it: the first hungry meal tastes almost startling. Flavours louder. Noses keener. Bellies wiser than we remembered. You stop earlier than fear told you and feel fuller than rules ever left you. You walk away without triumph because there was no battle. That absence of battle is freedom warming.

You will have mornings now that teach you. Breakfast hungry, eaten sitting, tasted. Midday steady. Afternoon human, not walled. Shop aisles easier because hunger shops for food and the keyhole call shops for bright packets, and you know the difference in your skin. Evening table warm because hunger met together is warm. Night quiet. Sleep deep. Wake clear. Day after day the same road, same steadiness, same quiet surprise that the hour you dreaded most passes like any hour.

Begin this glad, not grim. You are not losing the warm part of the day. You are finding where the warmth always lived.

So here is my sixth instruction:
TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

SUMMARY
* Food joy was never sweet joy; hunger met makes real food favourite without any catch.
* Open hunger welcomes many foods and settles for hours while narrow urgency wants one bright thing and returns in the hour.
* Satisfaction is a clean stop that arrives on its own after a hungry meal.
* The market meal proved pleasure is real — noticed, savoured, stopped — with no grey waiting.
* Fruit and plain meals belong to hunger met; they feed and leave done where bright hits bypass and leave want.
* The body makes what it needs from ordinary food, so living without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing essential missing.
* Eating when hungry and stopping at done returns steady afternoons and clear evenings without battle.
