Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD
*Eating was never grey without sweet; hunger met with real food is the favourite you thought the packet gave you.*

YOU FEAR THE GREY PLATE

You carry no shame into this room. You carry the dead lift and the clear investigator eyes that watched three o'clock tell the truth. Keep them. We need them for something kinder now.

You say to me, quietly, almost embarrassed to say it out loud:

“I see the lift made the low. I see the shoes. But tell me straight — without sweet hits, will eating not turn grey? Will food not become fuel and duty? Dessert was love in my house. A pudding was the warm part of the day.”

I hear you. I honour the fear. Every comfort eater who called dessert love has stood where you stand. We all believed food joy was sweet joy. We believed hunger was an enemy to be silenced quickly and satisfaction was a rumour other people talked about.

The fact is, hunger is not your enemy. Hunger is the beginning of pleasure. Satisfaction is not a rumour. Satisfaction is the clean stop your body built into you long before packets taught you to ignore it.

You do not believe me yet. Good. You are an investigator now, not a believer. Let me take you to eat.

A MARKET MORNING

Come with me on an ordinary Saturday, hungry in the honest way.

You slept. You woke clear because the night had no hurry in it. No late grazing, no sticky mouth at midnight. You drank water. You walked to the market with an empty belly that feels open and calm, not panicked, not chanting. There is a difference you will soon know in your skin. Open hunger welcomes. It waits. It does not stamp its foot for one bright thing.

Stalls bright in cool air. Tomatoes still warm from the van. Greens with water on them. New potatoes earthy. Apples firm and cold. A loaf with a dark crust that cracks when you press it, steam still inside. Cheese with a rind. Eggs brown and speckled. A peach that smells like itself from a foot away.

You buy simply, what catches your eye and nose. No list from a plan. No counting. No bargaining with yourself. Your body leads and your eyes follow.

Home. You slice the bread thick. Butter melts in. Tomatoes with salt and oil. Greens wilted in a hot pan with garlic. A little cheese. Water. Then the peach for after, juice down the chin.

You sit down properly, hungry. First bite brightest. You notice. Crust crack, crumb soft, tomato sharp and sweet together, greens warm and clean. Second bite steady. Third bite slower. You talk, you laugh, you pause. Middle of the plate the urgency softens. Later bites less urgent. Then it comes, unforced, almost unnoticed, like dusk: enough. You stop. Not because a rule says stop. Not because you are being good. Because satisfaction arrived on its own and there is nothing left to chase.

An hour later belly calm, head clear, hands willing. Two hours later steady still, ready for the next thing, not twitchy for the same thing. No buzz to remember. No fog to pay for. No second reach.

Ask yourself now:

Did that meal need a bright packet to be joy?

Did hunger make the food grey, or did hunger make the food sing?

When did you last stop like that after sweet — stopped because done, not because the packet ended or shame interrupted?

There is only one honest answer. That stop was favourite. Not a word trick. A bodily fact.

HUNGER OPENS, THE LOOP NARROWS

We lost the difference because we stopped listening.

We learned to call every urgent mouth-noise hunger. We learned to read I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW as proof the body needs power. We learned to answer open hunger and narrow urgency with the same bright thing, and then we wondered why we never felt fed.

Learn the difference again in your own skin. It takes one hungry day to remember.

Hunger opens. It is calm, patient, wide. It welcomes soup. It welcomes bread. It welcomes vegetables, fruit, water, a pause. It settles when fed and says thank you by going quiet for hours.

The sweet urgency narrows. It is twitchy, specific, hurried. It does not want soup. It does not want bread. It does not want water and a stretch. It wants the bright packet and nothing else will do. Feed it sweet and it returns louder in the hour. Feed the body real food when hungry and it goes quiet, because there is no tightening left to relieve.

I lived deaf to this for years. Drawer full, belly loud, head thick. I ate hundreds of sweet calories at three and felt emptier at four. Mouth busy. Body unfed. How could numbers in leave power out unless the numbers were noise? A car filled with the wrong liquid does not run better for being full. It runs worse. So did we.

Your body is a marvellous precision machine. It knows hunger. It knows enough. It makes the glucose it needs from ordinary food eaten hungry — bread, potatoes, vegetables, fruit, meals — so leaving BAD SUGAR out is not leaving fuel out. If you live with diabetes or take anything that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice, and use this book for belief only. For the rest of us, the clean baseline without BAD SUGAR is physiologically coherent. Nothing essential is missing. The noise is missing. The food remains.

Does that sound like deprivation? Or does that sound like ears unblocked?

SATISFACTION IS A PLACE YOU STOP

We were taught satisfaction lives at the bottom of the packet. It does not. It lives in the body.

Watch that small child with a peach on a summer step, juice to the elbows. She eats hungrily, bright-eyed, humming. Then — that small child who stops mid-bite and runs off to play — she stops mid-bite and runs off, half the peach left, no counting, no guilt, no vow to be good tomorrow. Body led. Body said done. Play called louder than more.

We laughed at her waste. We should have envied her freedom. She had what we lost: a stop that arrives.

You still have it. It was only drowned.

Eat hungry and watch the signals work. First bites brightest. Middle bites steady. Later bites less urgent. Stop arrives unforced. You put the fork down. You breathe out. You feel warm, steady, done. Not stuffed. Not foggy. Done is a clean place. Done leaves the table with you and stays for hours.

Eat a sweet dose mid-afternoon and watch the opposite. First bite brightest for seconds. Second bite hurried. Third bite standing by the cupboard. Stop never arrives — only pause when the packet ends. An hour later belly loud, head thick, hands restless. Mouth full, body unfed.

Which taught you you are steady? The meal that left you steady taught you you are steady. The dose that left you foggy taught you you are foggy. One told truth. The other installed a lie and charged you for it.

Ask the plain questions:

Has a sweet hit ever left you quiet and done for hours the way a hungry meal leaves you?

Has more sweet ever finished the want, or has more sweet only postponed the next want by minutes?

If satisfaction were in the packet, why does the packet never say enough?

There is only one honest answer. Satisfaction was never in the packet. Satisfaction is in hunger met and left at done.

FAVOURITE IS NOT A TRICK OF WORDS

Now I will meet your suspicion head-on, because you are thinking it and I want you to hear it in your own voice:

“Favourite food. That is a play on words. You mean worthy food. You mean dull food I should love. You are dressing up loss as gain.”

I understand why the sentence sounds like a trick. We have been conned so often by worthy talk. I say it flat, with all the warmth I have: there is no catch. The pleasure is real.

I do not mean you must love boiled greens from duty. I mean hunger makes real food favourite in the mouth, not in the moral. Think of that bread, crust cracking, eaten hungry. Think of tomatoes salted, sharp and warm. Think of the peach, cold and fragrant, juice running. Did you need to persuade yourself to enjoy them? Did you need a lecture? Or did the mouth wake and the nose wake and the belly warm and the mind say, more of this life, please?

That is favourite. Not favourite as prize for being good. Favourite as eaten hungry, noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied.

Ventriloquize the rest of the doubt with me:

“But I love the taste of chocolate. I love the cream, the crunch, the melt.”

Did you love the taste, or did you love the moment around the taste? Mouth novelty loves first seconds of anything bright. After seconds comes hurry. After hurry comes fog. After fog comes second reach. Loving first seconds that charge an hour is not love. It is a loan.

“Celebrating with chocolate was love in our house.”

Celebrating was love. Chocolate rode along. The laughter, the company, the late light, the funny scene, the table full of people who know your name — that was love. The dose sneaked a ride. You will see this whole when we meet your sweetest moment. For now, notice: meal, company, hunger met — that was the warmth you called dessert.

“I've managed to be good this week. Food joy is my reward.”

Good weeks that end in foggy Fridays never proved food joy lives in sweet. They proved tight weeks manufacture Friday glow. True reward after hard work leaves you more able, not less. Rest leaves you more able. Food that feeds leaves you more able. Laughter leaves you more able. A loan leaves you less.

Ask the three that close this room:

If food joy were sweet, why do the sweetest days end with the dullest mouths and the stalest mornings?

If dessert were love, why does love eaten hungry with bread and fruit and company feel warmer and cleaner and last longer?

If favourite were wordplay, why does the body say done with a quiet sigh after a hungry meal and never say done after sweet?

There is only one honest answer. Eating itself is joy. Sweet only shouted over it.

FRUIT IS FOOD, NOT A LOOPHOLE

You ask the clever trap question, and I welcome it:

“Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. Bread turns to glucose too. It is all the same.”

No. It is not the same, and your body knows the difference before your mind argues it.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR — refined sugar and added free sugars and junk processed carbs eaten as sweet hits, drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and grazing doses. Natural sugars inside whole fruit, vegetables and plain meals are not the target. They belong to the menu of hunger met.

See the difference in use, not in chemistry talk. A peach eaten hungry when you are hungry: noticed, savoured, stopped. Fibre, water, chew, pause, signals working, belly calming for hours. A bar eaten twitchy at four: hurried, standing, stop never arriving, belly loud in the hour, hands restless for more.

One feeds hunger. The other bypasses hunger and shouts over the signal. One leaves done. The other leaves want.

Enjoying sweetness in a peach, in ripe tomatoes, in fresh bread, in a meal cooked with love, is not a lapse and is not forbidden. This book does not forbid enjoyment. Dessert as self-payment for virtue kept the loop alive because the frame said virtue must be paid in bright packets. Joy in a meal eaten hungry needs no payment and keeps no loop. The mouth was made to brighten at first taste. The body was made to steady after. Let both do their work.

I say this as settled fact: you were never made to live on hits. You were made to eat when hungry and stop when satisfied and get on with enjoying your life with a quiet mind.

THE ORDINARY LUNCH THAT PROVES IT

Let me stay with you for one ordinary lunch, so you feel it rather than agree with it.

One o'clock. True hunger, open and calm. You sit. Soup hot, bread torn, not sliced neat. You blow, you sip. Warmth spreads. You chew slowly because there is time. You notice salt, pepper, oil, the crack of crust. You drink water. You pause. You have half left and the hurry is gone.

You eat a little more. The brightness of first bites softens into steadiness. You put the spoon down. You breathe. Done. You push the bowl away half-finished without triumph and without regret. You walk back to work light, not heavy, not buzzing, not foggy. At three you dip a little as humans do. You stretch, drink water, open air. You steady again. At four you look up and notice you worked. At five you are still you.

Compare dose by dose. Sweet top-up, brief buzz, fog and second reach. Hungry meal, warmth, steadiness, stop. Which behaved like energy? Which behaved like love?

I lived both. Sweet days: bright ten, scattered sixty, second dose, scattered thirty, fog, home stale, evening grazing to lift the evening low. Fed days: plain lunch eaten hungry, water, short walk, back to work, steady two hours, true tiredness at a true hour, home clear, morning clear. The difference was not subtle. One rode a coaster. The other walked a road.

You say, but my evenings need softening, my family table needs pudding to feel complete. Watch the table, not the pudding. Children talking over each other. Partner tired and funny. Steam rising. Bread passed hand to hand. Hunger met together. Where is the grey? The grey was never the absence of sweet. The grey was flat, foggy and never satisfied as permanent weather after years of loans. Drain the loans and colour returns to the table that was there all along.

Hear the verdict and let it stand: hunger met with real food satisfies in a way sweet never touched.

EAT AS THE BODY ASKS

You are ready for the rule that is not a rule. It is a return.

We fenced eating with clocks and ledgers and good-week accounting. Eat at noon whether hungry or not. Skip to be good. Graze to be kind. Pay virtue with sweet. Punish hurry with hunger. No wonder hunger went silent. We stopped asking it.

Ask it again. It answers at once.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that line as permission, not pressure. Want means real food you truly want when hungry — market vegetables, fresh bread, fruit, soup, cheese, eggs, meals that warm — noticed and enjoyed, not counted. Hungry means open hunger, not the narrow twitch for one bright thing. Satisfied means done, the quiet sigh, the fork down. Never punish yourself again means no payback hunger, no skipped meal to pay for pleasure, no sweet penance, no ledger. The body does not need correction. It needs hearing.

I promise you this as one who lost it and found it: the first hungry meal tastes almost startling. Flavours louder. Noses keener. Bellies wiser than we remembered. You stop earlier than fear told you and feel fuller than rules ever left you. You walk away without triumph because there was no battle. That absence of battle is freedom warming.

So here is my sixth instruction:
TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

SUMMARY
* Food joy was never sweet joy; hunger met makes real food favourite.
* Open hunger welcomes many foods and settles for hours; narrow urgency wants one bright thing and returns in the hour.
* Satisfaction is a clean stop that arrives on its own after a hungry meal.
* The market meal proved pleasure is real — noticed, savoured, stopped — with no catch.
* Fruit and plain meals belong to hunger met; they feed and leave done where bright hits bypass and leave want.
* The body makes what it needs from ordinary food, so living without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing essential missing.
* Eating when hungry and stopping at satisfied returns steady afternoons and clear evenings without battle.
