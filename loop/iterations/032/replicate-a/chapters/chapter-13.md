Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE
*You are free now, so close the book and live — sweetness is yours again.*

START AT THE BEGINNING

If you opened the book here, put this page down and start at the first page.

I mean that kindly and firmly. What follows is not the method. It is a handful of reminders for a free eater to photograph and carry. The freedom itself happens while you read, in order, with an open mind, while you carry on exactly as normal until your last ordinary treat.

You cannot borrow the ending without the seeing. You cannot keep the seeing without the whole argument. We believed for years that BAD SUGAR was a genuine treat or fuel that made hard days bearable and bright days brighter. We learned, drop by drop, that it does plenty TO us and nothing FOR us, that the lift makes the low, that the want was built and the fear was borrowed. That learning is the escape. Skip it and the list below is only words.

So start at the first page. Read it easily, willingly, all the way through. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You will not need courage to finish. You will not need time to recover after. You will turn the last page lighter than you turned the first, because each chapter takes something off you, not something from you. The chant gets quieter. Food gets brighter. Mornings get clearer. Then the vow comes and you are done.

If you have read it all, stay with me for a few minutes more. This is our goodbye, and it is a happy one. Photograph the list at the end if you like. Keep it in your pocket until you know it by heart. Then forget the list and remember the joy.

THIS WAS NEVER ABOUT QUITTING

You feel free. You feel a little amazed to feel free. Let me tell you what just happened, because it is marvellous and you will want to remember it.

You have not lost sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

Think back. We were born eaters. Hunger came, we ate, done came, we stopped, gladly. An apple was sharp. Bread was nutty. A peach eaten hungry, stopped mid-bite to run off and play, was joy without maths. No craving, no courtroom, no evening hunt. No voice counting treats or promising to be good tomorrow. Hunger spoke. We answered. Done spoke. We stopped.

Then the Sugar Trap closed around us. Daily doses, bliss-point crunch and melt, checkout shine, morning fog answered with more fog. The mouth got coated. Hunger got drowned under chant. Real food went grey — not because food went grey, but because blast blinded taste and the low that followed each hit was read as need. We called the blast pleasure. We called the low hunger. We called the hunt life. Sweetness itself was stolen by noise.

Do you see it now? The grey was never in the food. The grey was in the coating. The empty was never in the morning. The empty was in the echo of last night's doses calling for their brother. Take away the doses and the coating lifts. Take away the coating and flavour returns on its own. No training. No replacement. No hole to fill.

Now sight has returned it. Hunger is honest again. Done is glad again. Fruit is clean and bright again. Dinner eaten hungry tastes like dinner again. Bread torn hot is nutty again. Soup steams the glasses again. You stop when done comes and done stays done for hours. You did not shrink your life to a smaller plate. You grew back into the eater you were before the con.

That is why there is no hollow place to fill. We change nothing else in our lives. We eat. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

I stood where you stand, braced for loss, and found gain. I tell you flat: there was nothing to mourn. The packet took, never gave. The evening without rustle is wider. The morning without hunt is brighter. The shop without bargaining is quieter. The belly met with food stays met for hours.

Ask your own mouth, not your old memory:

Did real food taste duller this week, or brighter than it has in years?

Did evenings feel emptier without the box, or larger, calmer, yours again?

When a sweet thought drifted in and you answered with joy, did it grow teeth or die within a minute?

You know the honest answer. Bright. Large. Dead within a minute.

Which life do you want for the rest of your life? The chant, the fog, the box emptied before regret ended, hooked for the rest of your life? Or hunger, satisfaction, clear head, glad done, rejoicing for the rest of your life?

There is only one honest answer. You have already chosen it.

GET ON WITH ENJOYING YOUR LIFE

So close the book and live.

You need no further teaching to stay free. You need no watching, no counting, no armour for parties, no dread of birthdays. A free eater does not rehearse freedom. A free eater eats, works, laughs, rests, loves, and leaves packets to those still caught, with pity and without envy.

Tomorrow morning you will wake and eat because you are hungry. You will shop in the afternoon and walk the bright aisle quiet, buying real meals for real hunger. You will cook in the evening and taste dinner all the way through, and stop at glad done. You will sit on the sofa and hear the jokes because no rustle talks over them. Ordinary hours. Marvellous hours. Hours that used to narrow to blast and next blast now widen into life.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, live with an eating disorder past or present, or carry medical risk, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

From now on, whenever sweet craving or sweet memory crosses your mind, do one thing. Do not argue. Do not weigh. Do not test. Smile and think, loudly, gladly, on purpose:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it in the aisle. Think it at the birthday table. Think it on the bad day and on the bright day. At first you will think it to remind yourself. Very soon you will think it because it is true and sweet to say. The thought that once meant reach becomes the bell that rings relief. Bells need feeding to ring. Yours is starved. It will ring less and less until you forget you ever hunted.

Do not preach. Live visible ease and let ease do the recruiting. Eat your dinner with appetite. Stop at done with a smile. Walk past shine without hunt. Wake clear without fog. When they ask — and they will ask when they see mornings sit light on you — speak warm and short. I was caught, I saw the con, eating is joy again, I am happy to be free. Then talk about the film, the walk, the work, life. Freedom that walks easy among packets and smiles is freedom.

You came hovering at an edge. You crossed on an ordinary treat seen true. You woke to ordinary mornings. Ordinary is not grey. Ordinary is marvellous, wonderful, exciting in its quiet way — hunger clean, food bright, head clear, time uncounted.

Photograph the list below. Keep it in your pocket until you know it by heart. Then forget the list and remember the joy. The book is done. Life is ahead. The meal ahead is yours. The walk ahead is yours. The evening ahead is yours.

Rejoice. You are free. Get on with enjoying your life.

SUMMARY
1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Question what you think you know about sweetness.
2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH
Carry on exactly as normal while you read.
3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.
4. NEVER BLAME YOURSELF AGAIN
Every failure was the method, never you.
5. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD
You are escaping, not losing.
6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.
7. SEE THE GRUMBLE AS DYING, NOT DEMANDING
That feeling is the Nibbler starving.
8. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Other voices rebuild the Con.
9. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the wrong method talking.
10. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.
11. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.
12. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.
