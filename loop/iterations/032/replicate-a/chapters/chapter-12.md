Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS
*Life without BAD SUGAR is not a watch you keep; it is ordinary hours lived with ease.*

THE FIRST MORNING IS ORDINARY

Wake up. That is all.

No maths on the bedside table. No vow to be good today. No quiet audit of last night's packet. You slept, you woke, your belly will speak when it is ready, and you will answer it like an adult who trusts hunger.

I stood in my own kitchen on my first free morning and waited for the battle to start. I had fought a battle there for twenty years. Coffee, then hunt. Toast, then hunt. The drawer pulled open before thought finished. The mouth asking for blast while the body asked for breakfast. I stood with the kettle loud and braced for noise.

There was no noise.

I made eggs. I made toast with butter that tasted of butter. I ate because I was hungry. I stopped because I had had enough, the way a child stops mid-bite and runs off to play when done. No counting. No bargaining. No second voice narrating each mouthful. The plate empty, the belly calm, the morning mine.

Yours will be the same. Not because you are strong today. Because there is nothing to be strong about. The chant that used to start at dawn was never morning hunger. It was the echo of last night's doses calling for their brother. With no new dose to call, the echo has no orders left. It sounds faint for a little while, then it stops.

Eat your breakfast. Eat it seated if you can. Taste it. Real food has flavour when the mouth is not coated. Bread is nutty. Fruit is sharp and clean. Coffee is bitter in a good way. Porridge holds you for hours. Notice how long satisfaction lasts when it comes from food, not from blast. Blast lasts minutes and leaves a bill. Food lasts hours and leaves quiet.

We used to call that quiet boring. We were conned. We thought morning needed a lift, a hit, sugary somethings to get us through the hours till lunch. We mistook the fog after the fix for morning itself. Clear morning is not grey. Clear morning is bright at the edges, hunger honest, head uncoated, tongue clean.

If you take medicine that touches blood sugar or you live with diabetes, follow your clinician first — this book frees belief, it does not replace care.

Do you miss anything this morning? Look. The kettle, the plate, the light at the window. What exactly would a dose add to this table? Would it make hunger truer? Would it make toast taste more like toast? Would it make you brighter at ten o'clock, or foggier by eleven and hunting again? You know the answer from your own mouth, not from my lecture. A dose would coat, drop, and call again. Breakfast feeds. That is the whole difference, lived before nine.

Change nothing else in your life. Same kitchen. Same chair. Same shop bread. Same work to go to. Freedom does not need a new house, a new diet, a new routine. Freedom needs the old life without the old noise. Live it.

Put the dishes in the sink. Drink water. Go to work. The morning does not need ceremony. The morning needs living. You will find yourself humming while the toast browns. You will find yourself noticing weather. Small things return when hunt leaves — birdsong, cold air at the door, the clean scrape of butter. We thought sweet brightened mornings. Mornings were bright already. We had coated them.

THE SHOP WITHOUT A COURTROOM

You will go to the shop today. Go as a free eater.

We used to enter that aisle like a courtroom. Eyes forward. Jaw set. Bargaining with packs. I will be good this week. Just a small one for the kids. Just a Friday one. Just a dark one, that counts as better. The heart beating a little faster by the till. The hand moving while the mind voted no. The packet in the basket before decision finished.

Walk that aisle now and hear how quiet it is.

The packs still shout. Bright colours, big promises, cartoon smiles, checkout ambush at child height. I am not asking you to admire them or to hate them with clenched fists. See them true. They are doses dressed as love by men who profit when you hunt. Bliss-point crunch, melt, chew, blast. Designed to pall fast and call again. That is not food calling your body. That is engineering calling your old belief.

You walk past because you have no order to obey. Not because you are being good. There is no good to be. There is only free.

Look at the baskets around you with kind eyes. A woman loading family-size bars with tired apology in her face. A lad with two energy drinks and a chocolate roll for the bus. A father adding biscuits he swore last night he would skip. Do you envy them? Ask yourself honest, from your own evenings bent around a packet:

Do they look like they are enjoying a pleasure you have lost?

Do they look calm, satisfied, done when done?

Do they look free to leave the pack half-eaten and forget it for weeks?

You know the honest answer. They look hunted, hurried, a little ashamed, already reaching while promising control. They are still inside the Sugar Trap, still paying blast with fog, still mistaking relief of a low for joy. You are not missing their joy. They are missing yours.

That is pity, not envy. Pity without preaching. Pity without superiority. We were them last week. We know the chant, the maths, the evening box emptied while the mouth still promised one. We do not look down. We look across with warmth and walk on glad we no longer pay.

Buy what your hunger asks. Vegetables, bread, cheese, meat, fish, fruit, plain yoghurt, rice, potatoes, oil, coffee, tea. Real shopping for real meals. No rules from me about brands or plans. Your body knows hunger and fullness better than any label. Buy enough to eat well. Pay. Leave. The checkout sweets will shine under the light. Let them shine. Shine is not value. You smile on your way out because no voice follows you home.

And tomorrow you will do the same, and the day after, and the quiet grows. Not a test to pass. Not a calendar to guard. Just life, shopped, cooked, eaten, forgotten.

Notice how cheap the shop feels now. We used to pay twice — once at the till, once in fog, thirst, film on teeth, evening sag, morning hunt. Now you pay once and leave with food. The till girl does not know you are free. The automatic doors do not applaud. Better. Freedom does not need applause. Freedom needs potatoes, bread, apples, dinner.

DINNER YOU CAN TASTE

Cook. Sit. Eat.

Evening was our hardest hour. Work done, will thin, sofa deep, lights low. The permitted single biscuit that carried its brother. The wrapper rustle that meant the box would be empty before regret ended. We told ourselves food is love and love means sweet at night. We called grazing unwinding.

Unwinding never came. The mouth blasted, the belly sagged, the head fogged, sleep thinned, morning paid. Love was never in the packet.

Tonight make a plain favourite meal and eat it hungry. Soup that steams the glasses. Chicken that falls off the bone. Potatoes crisp at the edge. Salad bright with oil and lemon. Bread torn, not sliced. Fruit for after, cold from the fridge. Eat slowly enough to taste, fast enough to honour hunger. Stop when done comes — that deep easy signal that says enough, gladly.

Notice what we never noticed while feeding the chant. Hunger makes food marvellous. Satisfaction is a real bodily joy, deep, calm, complete. No second hunt behind it. No thirst under it. No film on the teeth. You lean back. You are done. Done stays done.

The house is the same house. The sofa is the same sofa. The plates are the same plates. Only the noise is gone. Talk is clearer with a clear head. Jokes land better. You chew, you swallow, you listen. No hand drifting to a bowl between sentences. No mind half in the cupboard while the body sits at the table. Presence is pleasure. We chased flavour and missed dinner. Now dinner is back.

Cook for others if you cook for others. Same pot. Same table. No speech about your freedom. No separate meal to prove a point. Let them have pudding if they want pudding. Your plate is your business and your joy. You eat your roast, your rice, your fruit. You stop glad. They will see appetite, ease, good humour. That teaches more than warning.

Wash up. Wipe the counter. Feel the evening stretch ahead, unbent around a packet. Time used to narrow to blast and next blast. Now time widens. An hour is long when it is not spent hunting. Read ten pages. Call your mother. Mend the drawer. Walk round the block and feel cold air clean the lungs. Small hours feel large when hunger is met and chant is dead.

THE EVENING SOFA IS YOURS

Sit down. The day is done.

This hour owned us. Ten o'clock, lights low, film on, hand in packet. We called it reward. We called it me-time. We called it the only quiet minute of the day. Then the minute ate the evening. Rustle over dialogue. Chew over jokes. Trip to the kitchen during adverts for more. Belly loud. Head loud. Sleep thin and sugared.

Tonight the film runs without rustle. The talk runs without crunch. You remember the jokes next morning because you heard them.

I think of dimmed lights and shared laughter we once blamed on chocolate, and I laugh now to see how little chocolate ever did.

The warmth was faces, jokes, shoulders touching, story unfolding. The dose only sneaked a ride and claimed the credit for the evening. Tonight laughter is clearer, attention undivided, belly quiet. You lean back at the credits and feel done, not dragged. That is reward. Real reward does not bill you at midnight.

If tiredness comes, go to bed. Free eaters sleep when tired. We used to dose tiredness with blast and call it energy, then lie awake coated and wired and call it insomnia. Tired is not a fault. Tired is a signal. Soup, bread, warmth, bed. Morning will be clear because night was clean.

If restlessness passes through, greet it kindly. Ah, the old hour chimes. The body remembers an hour the way a tongue remembers a song. A faint empty tug may pass. It is not hunger. Hunger points to food and is glad of dinner. This points to nothing and is glad of nothing. It is a dying echo.

That echo is the Nibbler starving. A small creature making a small noise as it loses its last servant. Do not argue with it. Do not feed it to shut it up. Smile at it. Each murmur is death, not demand. Rejoice the way you rejoice when a distant engine dies out and the street turns quiet again. Wonderful. It is going. You are staying free and getting on with enjoying your life.

You do not need to fill the evening to prove freedom. Read. Talk. Doze. Wash socks. Stare out of the window. Change nothing to prove freedom. Freedom proves itself by needing no proof.

WHEN THE THOUGHT DRIFTS IN

It will drift in. Let it.

A packet shines in a meeting. A smell drifts from a bakery. A child offers cake with sticky hands. A bad day breaks and the old voice slides close, warm, reasonable: treat when you feel you deserve one. A sweet day shines and the voice says softer still: celebrating with chocolate is the point, surely one is love.

You cannot stop thoughts arriving. No free mind censors its weather. Do not try to push the thought away. Do not scold yourself for thinking it. Do not turn the thought into a test of character. A thought is not an order. A thought is weather passing over a free field.

We spent years believing every sweet thought meant want, and every want meant fight, and every fight meant either guilt or dose. That chain was the Trap talking. Break it at the first link and the rest falls.

When the thought comes, answer it with joy, loudly, on purpose, until joy is the louder voice. You do not suppress the memory. You reframe it. The packet that once meant rescue now means prison seen from outside. The smell that once meant pleasure now means coating remembered true. The hour that once meant hunt now means relief that hunt is over.

You catch the shine. You smile and think:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it every time. At first you say it to remind yourself. Very soon you say it because it is true and sweet to say. The thought that once triggered reach becomes the bell that rings relief. That bell will ring less and less, not because you train yourself, but because a dead belief stops speaking. Thoughts need feeding to live. Yours is starved.

Ask and answer from your own week, not from fear:

When a sweet thought passed yesterday and you answered with joy, did the want grow or did it die within a minute?

When you saw trapped eaters grazing and sagging, did you feel deprived or did you feel relief you no longer pay?

When you ate a real meal hungry and stopped at satisfied, did you feel something missing or did you feel something returned?

You know the honest answer. Joy kills want. Relief kills envy. Food met with hunger needs no blast after it.

This is how you guard belief without effort. Not by watching yourself. By answering weather with celebration until celebration is habit and weather is just weather.

The Sugar Trap lives only in belief now. You killed belief last night with open eyes and a solemn vow. What walks the street today is memory without power, habit without master. Let memory walk past. You do not follow. You stand in your marvellous ordinary hour and rejoice.

IF A DOSE SLIPS IN

Hear me kind and clear about the rumble strip.

You may take a dose by accident. A sauce sweetened beyond taste. A biscuit taken without looking at a meeting table. A bite of your child's ice cream from love, not hunt. Your body can cope with an accidental crumb. Your mind must not steer into it on purpose.

Do not panic. Do not tell yourself you have blown everything and may as well fetch a packet. That all-or-nothing collapse was the Willpower Method talking, and the Willpower Method lied about everything, including this.

A slip teaches; it does not erase. Pull over mentally the way you slow when tyres hum on the road edge. Taste what it truly was. Was it marvellous? Or loud, cloying, thirsty, already calling for its brother while enjoying less? Let mouth correct memory. The lesson is not shame. The lesson is sight: blast, sag, call again, nothing added.

Then drive on. Free. The same hour. No punishment, no extra rules, no heavy vow to make up for it. Punishment would re-make precious what mouth just proved worthless. Just see true and keep living. One accidental dose does not rebuild a belief. Only inviting the belief back rebuilds it — by saying the dangerous sentence: well, one was fine, so a few are fine, so Fridays are fine.

Never invite it back. Deliberate exception keeps the whole belief alive. Accident forgiven, design refused. Guard the belief, not the behaviour with panic, and the behaviour looks after itself.

If the little echo grumbles louder for a day after a slip, smile wider. That grumble is the Nibbler rattled, not mighty. It will fade again in days, small, brief, weather-like. Millions have walked through that murmur without pain worth naming. You will too. Each morning without maths, each aisle without courtroom, each evening without rustle confirms you again.

You are not fragile. You were conned, and now you see. Seeing does not un-see because a crumb passed the lips. Seeing un-sees only if you agree to believe the lie again. Do not agree.

Tell me true from mouth, not memory: did that accidental bite taste like love? Did it calm the day? Did it add one true thing that dinner, rest, laughter, sleep had not already held? There is only one honest answer. Loud, brief, thirsty, calling. Nothing added. Lesson learned. Drive on free.

LET THE TRAPPED EAT IN PEACE

Do not preach.

I know the urge. You walk clear for three days and want to shake your sister, your mate, your husband. You want to empty their cupboards, quote the empty benefit column, save them by Tuesday. Love says warn them. Freedom says wait.

Preaching rebuilds the very walls you left. The trapped hear lecture as attack and grip their packet tighter. They hear ease as envy and start their own defence: I don't keep it in the house. Just a treat. I need it to get through. Everyone eats it. Fruit is sugar too. Tomorrow after the birthday. You know every line because you spoke every line. Argument never freed you. Sight freed you, in your own hour, when you were ready.

Live visible ease and let ease do the recruiting. Eat your dinner with appetite. Stop at done with a smile. Walk the aisle without hunt. Laugh at the film without rustle. Wake clear without fog. When they ask — and they will ask when they see mornings sit light on you — speak warm and short. I was caught, I saw the con, eating is joy again, I am happy to be free. No sermon. No pity face. No mission.

If they mock, do not wobble. Still off sugar? Go on, one won't kill you. Hear the Sugar Trap speaking through a kind mouth. The speaker is not cruel. The speaker is chanting. Pity the chant, not the friend. Answer light: I have no want to feed, I am celebrating my escape, have yours if you want it, I am glad where I am. Then talk about the film, the walk, the work, life. Refuse to turn dinner into a courtroom.

Keep your house as your house. You need no locked drawer, no warning sign, no announcement. If others under your roof still eat BAD SUGAR, let them without policing and without tasting to test yourself. Their doses are not your orders. Your freedom does not need their permission and does not demand their conversion. Change nothing else. Live together, eat together, love together, with your own plate clear and your own mind quiet.

Freedom that needs everyone else to change is not freedom. Freedom that walks easy among packets and smiles is freedom.

So here is my twelfth instruction:
NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

Speak that decision closed in your heart tonight. Not as a threat to carry. As a fact to enjoy. You do not weigh packets any more. You do not negotiate Fridays, parties, bad days, sweet days. The question is answered. Weighing would re-make precious what you have seen is worthless. Closed questions stay quiet. Open questions chant.

From now on there are no big days and hard days, only ordinary days lived free. Morning hunger met. Shop quiet. Meal tasted. Evening yours. Thought answered with joy. Slip learned from without panic. Friends left in peace. Body trusted. Satisfaction deep. Relief fresh each time the old shine passes and you hear your own glad voice answer it.

You came hovering at an edge. You crossed on an ordinary treat seen true. You woke to ordinary mornings. Ordinary is not grey. Ordinary is marvellous, wonderful, exciting in its quiet way — hunger clean, food bright, head clear, evenings unbent, money unhunted, teeth uncoated, time uncounted.

You have not lost sweetness. Sweetness was stolen by noise and handed back by sight. Fruit is sharp again. Bread is nutty again. Hunger is honest again. Done is glad again. Rejoice. You are free. Get on with enjoying your life.

SUMMARY
* Mornings are clear and hunger is honest, and breakfast satisfies for hours without a second hunt.
* Shops are quiet aisles to walk with kind eyes, and trapped baskets deserve pity, never envy.
* Real meals eaten hungry taste bright, and done comes deep and stays done.
* Evenings belong to film, talk and rest, with no rustle over dialogue and no bill at midnight.
* A passing sweet thought is weather to answer with joy until it fades on its own.
* An accidental bite teaches by its cloy and never erases sight unless belief is invited back.
* Friends are left in peace, and visible ease speaks louder than any warning.
