## CONTESTED — Claims the book MUST NOT overstate

1. **"Sugar is as addictive as cocaine"** — The Lenoir rat study shows sweetness SURPASSED cocaine reward, but this is an animal preference study, not a demonstration of equivalent addiction liability. The book must not claim equivalence.

2. **"Sugar addiction is a proven medical diagnosis"** — Sugar/food addiction is not recognized in the DSM-5. The YFAS is a research screening tool, not a clinical diagnostic instrument. The book may present the evidence while noting the diagnostic status.

3. **"Dopamine D2 receptors are universally reduced in obese/overeating humans"** — The PET literature is mixed; some studies find reductions, others do not. Meta-analysis shows only a modest effect.

4. **"Fructose is a poison at any dose"** — The evidence supports dose-dependent toxicity at chronic high intake, not harm at all levels. Context (fed state, co-ingestion, exercise, individual metabolism) matters.

5. **"Sugar withdrawal is equivalent to heroin withdrawal"** — While animal models show opioid-mediated withdrawal, human evidence is limited to self-report. The book should not equate the severity.

6. **"Artificial sweeteners are the solution"** — The gut-brain axis evidence shows artificial sweeteners fail to activate the sugar-preference circuit; they may not satisfy the underlying drive. The book should not present them as a simple substitute solution.

7. **"Sugar CAUSES obesity"** — The relationship is correlational and complex; sugar contributes to excess calorie intake but the data correlating sugar to obesity account for only ~10% of observed effect. Sugar's metabolic harms are more strongly linked to disease independent of obesity.
