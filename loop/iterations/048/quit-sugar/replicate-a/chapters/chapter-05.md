Chapter 5
THE TREAT THAT NEVER LOVES BACK

IN THIS CHAPTER: the bakery doorway after school, the envelope on payday, the dark row with chocolate in hand, and the love that was there all along.

*The comfort never came from the sweet. The sweet only rode on the comfort and left you lower.*

THE BAKERY DOOR

You remember the door because you loved what stood behind it.

After school the air outside the bakery sat warm on the pavement. The glass shone. Inside, the trays stood in rows and the woman behind the counter knew your name. Your mother bought you something on Fridays. Your grandmother kept biscuits in a tin with a bright lid and lifted it down when you came through her door. The kitchen smelled of her soap and her cooking. You sat at her table with your legs swinging and she watched you eat as if your eating fed her too.

We all carry a doorway like that. I carry mine. I am not asking you to doubt the warmth. The warmth was real.

Ask what the warmth was made of.

Was it the sugar in the bun, or was it being fetched from school and walked home hand in hand? Was it the chemistry of the icing, or was it a mother turning her whole attention to you after a week of work? Was it the biscuit, or was it a grandmother who lit up when you arrived and made you the centre of her afternoon? If I had handed you the same bun from a stranger in a cold room with no one glad to see you, would it have tasted like love?

You know it would not.

Think of the winter evening when the power cut and the shop was shut and she still made the hour shine with candles and talk and a plain hot meal. No packet arrived to save that night, yet you remember it as rich. Think of the scraped knee held under cold water while she knelt to your height and stayed till the crying stopped. No icing did that mending. The child in you recorded people, voice, lap, unhurried time. The Treat Story later pasted a wrapper over the recording and taught you to replay the wrapper when you hungered for the people.

The love arrived from people. The safety arrived from being known. The relief arrived from the day ending and the house holding you. BAD SUGAR stood beside all of that and claimed the credit. We were small and we believed the claim because everyone around us believed it. The adult smiled while you ate and said what a treat. The advert showed bright families laughing. The whole street taught the same lesson. Sweets mean love. Sweets mean you are good. Sweets mean the day has been kind.

That lesson was the Treat Story, not love and not hunger.

I understand why this one holds hardest. Fuel felt practical. You could test it against the clock. Love feels sacred. You fear that if you question the sweet you question your mother. You fear that if you see through the reward you betray the grandmother who lifted down the tin. You tell yourself I grew up on it and it is love, and you hear rejecting the packet as rejecting the hand that gave it.

It is the other way around.

Your mother loved you. The bun did not. Your grandmother loved you. The tin did not. The love would have lived in any food she set before you, in the walk, in the lap, in the listening. The sweet only ever sneaked a ride on those hours and then taught you to call the ride by the driver's name. To free the sweet from the memory is not to lose the memory. It is to return the memory to its true owners.

Hold that doorway a moment longer because the whole reward story stands in it. You arrived hungry for attention after a long day of rules. You received attention, touch, rest, laughter. A sweet arrived at the same minute. The child mind tied them together. The adult mind never untied them. For the rest of your life you have paid yourself in the same coin whenever the old hunger returns, and you have called the coin love.

THE ENVELOPE ON PAYDAY

Friday comes and the week has taken its cut.

You sit at the desk late. The screen burns. Your shoulders ache. The pay has landed and the landlord has taken his share and the shop has taken hers, and you tell yourself something in the day should have been yours. You stop at the kiosk on the way home. You buy the bar you like, the one with the familiar wrapper, and you eat it on the stairs before you cook. You say I deserve it. You say I have managed to be good this week. You say treat when you feel you deserve one, and this week you earned it.

We all said it. I said it for years. I kept a private ledger in which hard hours earned sweet coins, and I paid out every Friday with interest.

Watch one payday whole, the way you watched one afternoon whole, and the reward story fails its own test.

You eat the bar. For minutes the shoulders drop. The head quiets. You feel seen, even if only by yourself. Then the minutes pass. The flat comes into view and the washing waits and the bill sits unopened on the table. The problem you bought the bar to soften stands exactly where it stood. The tiredness returns with a sweet edge. The mood dips lower than before the kiosk. By eight you are picturing a second sweet to finish the evening, though the first was meant to close the week.

Did the bar reward you? Or did it rent you ten minutes and bill you the evening?

A true reward leaves you richer. Money in the pocket leaves you richer. An evening walked with a friend leaves you richer. An hour of rest leaves you richer. This leaves you emptier, more restless, and already planning the next payment. We called it a genuine pleasure or treat and never asked what the pleasure was made of.

Ask of that Friday bar what any fair judge would ask of any reward.

Did it remove the tiredness, or did it pause the noticing of tiredness for minutes and then return it louder? Did it honour the work, or did it move the attention from the work you did to the packet you opened? Did you finish the bar and forget sweets until morning, or did you finish the bar and think of sweets sooner? Did the weeks with the biggest bars leave you proudest on Sunday, or most regretful?

There is only one honest answer. The pay was yours. The week was yours. The relief of Friday was yours. The bar rode alongside for minutes and claimed the week as its work.

I am warm to you in this because the need behind the bar is honourable. You do deserve payment. You do deserve to be seen after hard days. You were not foolish to want marking. You were conned into accepting a coin that spends itself before you reach home. The con is old. As children we learned sweets mean celebration. As workers we learned they mean function. Everyone at the desk does it. When the pinch came between hits, the old script was ready with the same translation. You are tired, it said. You earned this. We believed because everyone believed, and because the lift arrives just long enough to feel like proof before the bill arrives.

But a proof that needs re-proving every evening is no proof.

THE DARK ROW

The cinema tells the truth faster than any other room because the lights go down.

You queue with friends. The foyer smells of popcorn and warm air. You say celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate, the way we all learned to say it. You buy the large box though you meant the small. The lights fall. The screen opens. For the first twenty minutes the chocolate belongs to the film. The jokes land better. The hand moves without looking. You feel held by the dark and the story and the people beside you.

By the middle of the film the box is empty and the film goes on without it. Notice what happens then.

You do not sit back fuller and happier. You sit forward a little emptier. The seat feels harder. The mouth feels coated and wanting water. Attention snags. You think of the shop in the foyer though the film still plays. The pleasure of the first handful never returned for the last handful, yet the hand kept going. If the chocolate carried the evening, why did the evening thin as the box emptied? If it added to the film, why did the film feel best at the start when the box was full and worst after the box had done its work?

Stay in that row because it holds your objection whole. You say the sweet soothes when life presses. You say a hard day needs softening. You say comfort is real because the first bites feel kind.

I do not deny the first bites feel kind. I deny what you were told they mean.

The kindness of the first bites is brief relief from a low the last sweet brewed, brief return toward the normal you had before you ever opened the drawer, then a new low guaranteed. The dark, the rest, the pause, the laughter, the company, those did the soothing. The sweet rode alongside for minutes and took the bow for work done by everything else. It was only ever sneaking a ride.

Think of the evenings that prove it without argument. The film you watched with a friend and no sweets where you laughed till your sides hurt and walked home light. The hard day you talked through over plain food and sleep and woke clearer. The birthday cake at the family table you remember for the faces, not the icing. The wedding table where the cake is cut and the joy sits in the speeches and the dancing. You already own evenings where comfort arrived whole with no packet to thank. You lived them. The comfort did not need the sweet. The sweet needed the comfort to look like more than it is.

WHAT LOVE DID, WHAT THE SWEET DID

Put love and sweet side by side once and the tie breaks.

Love stays. The grandmother's kitchen stays in you now, decades on, though the biscuit is dust. The walk home holding a hand stays. The friend who waited through the hard month stays. Love leaves you steadier the next morning. Love never asks for a larger dose next time to feel the same. Love never leaves wrappers and regret and a vow to be good on Monday.

The sweet does the opposite. It fades within the hour. It asks for more next time to chase the first minutes. It leaves the mouth wanting and the mind busy. It leaves pain plus guilt where love leaves warmth plus rest. You know the aftertaste of a reward evening. The stomach heavy. The head foggy. The inner voice turning. The promise that tomorrow will be cleaner. That turning is not the mark of being loved. It is the mark of being billed.

Ask yourself three plain questions and answer as if plain speaking mattered more than defending a habit.

When did a sweet reward ever remove the problem it was meant to soften? When did a comfort eat ever leave you kinder to yourself an hour later than a walk, a bath, a call, plain food eaten slowly would have left you? When did celebrating with sweets ever leave the celebration brighter the next morning than celebrating with people and music and real food?

There is only one honest reading. Celebration and comfort never arrived from the sweet.

I know this is hard to accept if your best memories wear sweet wrappers. Keep an open mind a little longer. I am not asking you to throw away a single memory. I am asking you to return each memory to its true maker. The bakery door held a mother glad to see you. The Friday held a worker who survived the week. The dark row held friends and story and rest. Give the credit to its owners and the sweet stands alone, small and loud and empty, with nothing to pay you and everything to bill.

The fact is the sweet never loved you back. It used the rooms where love lived to look like love.

That is the inversion at the heart of reward. The rescuer arrived after the ache and offered help with the ache it keeps alive. We thanked the rescuer. We never checked who taught us to ache on schedule.

THE COMFORT THAT STAYS SORE

There is a sorer half to reward, and it must be spoken plainly because you have lived it in secret.

You pay yourself on Friday and by Sunday you punish yourself for the payment. You comfort yourself on Tuesday and by Wednesday you call yourself weak for needing comfort. The reward turns on its heel within hours and becomes the rod. You eat to feel good about surviving and end feeling bad about eating. The loop tightens because the bad feeling calls for a new reward, and the new reward guarantees a new bad feeling.

We called that loop food noise among ourselves before any book gave it a name. The mind busy through films and quiet talk. The cupboard pictured from the desk. The evening that should close staying open, pegged to small hits. Sleep coming later and lighter. Morning coming earlier and heavier. You were not weak to hear the noise. You were hearing the echo of fast hits stacked on fast hits.

Hear the limit inside that truth, because it protects you. This soreness is not proof you are broken. It does not mean your character failed or that you carry some special flaw. It means a healthy person answered a false reward the way healthy people answer false rewards, with disappointment. Being exact here matters. If I told you every low mood means you are ill, you would test it once against a good week and stop trusting me. I will not trade your trust for a bigger fright.

The fact is the daily treat predicts future mood, not relief. In a large long-followed group, men who took most sugar from sweet foods and drinks faced higher later odds of common low mood and worry, and the checking found the eating came first and the low mood followed. The sweet promised to lift the week and left the months heavier.

Do not try to change from fear here. I do not want you frightened of your past Fridays. The months frighten no free eater because the free eater stops stacking the wave that made them. The method takes care of it once the belief shifts. You are not facing a lifetime of soreness. You are facing days of seeing clearly, and then the noise thins because you stop feeding it.

See how different that is from the old advice. The old advice said the low mood proves you need comfort. The truth is the low mood follows the comfort that was no comfort. The sweet did not soften the hard week. It borrowed brightness from next week and spent it in minutes, then handed you the bill as fog and self-reproach. The queue grows longer with every ride because the ride itself shortens the legs you queue with. That is why the bar that once held till Saturday now barely holds till the stairs, and why one is too much because it is never enough among ourselves became a joke that stopped being funny.

Once you see the bill correctly, you stop signing it. You do not brace for the rest of your life against evenings. You simply stop buying the wave that made them. The evening stays yours without a counter on the wall or a rule in the pocket. It stays yours because nothing arrives to take it.

IF IT LOVED YOU

Stand the reward up for its last test and let it speak for itself.

If BAD SUGAR loved you, would love behave like this? Would love lift you for minutes and drop you for hours? Would love ask to be larger every month to feel the same? Would love leave you hiding wrappers and promising to be good? Would love turn within the hour from payment to punishment?

If it comforted you, why are you sorer after comfort than before? If it celebrated you, why does the celebration need forgiving the next morning? When did it ever stay and hold you through the night the way a kind word, a held hand, plain food shared slowly ever held you?

Answer plainly and the reward story fails its own test. What passed as love was billing. What passed as comfort was interruption. What passed as a genuine pleasure never pleased for longer than it took to re-arm the want.

You powered those good hours. Your people powered them. Sleep, food, music, laughter, rest, the second wind that comes when you stand and drink water and open the window. The sweet took the bow for work done by everything else, the way a man who jumps in front of a parade claims to lead it.

Keep the parade. Dismiss the man who jumped in front.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

SUMMARY
- The bakery warmth belonged to people and safety and attention, not to the bun that rode alongside them.
- The Friday bar never paid for the week, it rented minutes and billed the evening with more want.
- The cinema chocolate felt kind for handfuls because the dark and story and rest were kind, then left the film thinner as the box emptied.
- Love stays and steadies the next morning, the sweet fades within the hour and asks for more next time.
- Reward evenings end in pain plus guilt, the mind busy and sleep lighter, because the payment was a new low, not relief.
- Men who took most sugar from sweet foods and drinks faced higher later odds of low mood, the eating came first, the treat predicts soreness, not comfort.
- Celebration never needed the sweet to shine, the faces and music and real food carried every bright table you remember.
