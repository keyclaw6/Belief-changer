Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD AT ALL

IN THIS CHAPTER: the restaurant table after the plates are cleared, the spoon scraping an empty dish, the house that fills itself by evening, and the plain question that ends the old weighing.

*BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you, and once you see that you stop weighing harm and start asking for proof of good.*

THE PUDDING YOU FINISHED ANYWAY

Think of one dessert you finished though you stopped enjoying it after two bites.

Mine came at a table with people I love. The meal had been good. The talk had been good. Then the pudding came, tall and glossy. The first spoonful was loud. Sweet, cold, rich. The second was quieter. By the third I was no longer tasting pudding at all, only sweetness in the abstract, the same flat note again and again. Yet I finished it. I scraped the dish and felt fuller and flatter than before I started.

You know this dish. We all know it. The office cake dry at the edges. The ice cream eaten standing at the freezer. The bar gone before the traffic moved. The first bite promised something. The second withdrew it. The rest was finishing.

Look closely, because the whole matter sits in miniature in that dish. If BAD SUGAR gave what you say it gives, that dish would make no sense. A friend who brings joy does not bore you by the third minute while you ask him to stay. A comfort that comforts does not leave you flatter while you ask for more. Yet enjoyment ended early and eating continued to the end.

You will say I picked a poor example. You will say your favourites are different, the ones you love best, tied to celebration and tired evenings when only something sweet will do.

Good. Bring the best one to mind. The one you would keep if you could keep only one. I want no cheap victory. Hold your strongest case while we talk.

Now answer plainly. Did that favourite behave differently? Did it please all the way down, every time, to the last bite? Or did it follow the same curve, loud at first, then flat, then finished from habit while your mind was elsewhere? Did you stop when pleasure stopped? Or did the hand continue after the mouth had checked out?

There is only one honest answer. We finish what we no longer enjoy. That alone should make you suspicious.

THE WRONG ARITHMETIC

Until now we all did the same sum. We put harm on one side and good on the other and we weigh them. We say we know BAD SUGAR bosses us, but it helps, it soothes, it carries us through. We say it does more good than harm for me.

I understand that sum. We all learned it. It feels fair and adult. It lets us feel we faced the facts and chose.

The fact is the sum itself is the con.

I am not asking you to weigh harm against benefit. Harm can be argued. A friend can point to someone who eats sweets daily and looks well. A bad week can make any cost feel distant. If we stand on harm we can argue for the rest of your life and never move. The Treat Story loves the harm debate, because while we argue how bad BAD SUGAR is, we leave unexamined the claim that it is good for anything at all.

So put the scales away. The question is smaller and more dangerous. Not does it do more harm than good. But what good does it do at all?

Ask it of your best favourite, not your worst mistake. Name one real benefit that belongs to BAD SUGAR itself and to nothing else. One pleasure that cannot be explained without it. One comfort that arrived from the sweet and not from the moment around it.

You will reach for answers. We all do at first. Test each against the dish you finished. If the benefit were real, would it vanish after two bites while eating continued? If the comfort were real, would you feel emptier after the plate was empty? If the lift were real, would you need another so soon?

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Feel how different those are. TO is what lands on the body and the day. FOR is what is given back. We confused them for years because they arrive together. The stir arrives with the bite, so we call the stir a gift. But much that acts upon us gives nothing back. A cold acts upon you. A bill acts upon you. No one calls those generous because they happened to you.

BAD SUGAR has acted upon you thousands of times. That is a long list of TO. Now show the FOR. Show one thing it built, healed, or solved that would not have been better without it.

You cannot, because there is nothing to show.

PLENTY TO YOU

Let us speak the TO plainly.

BAD SUGAR arrives and makes a stir. The mouth lights for a moment. The mind narrows to the packet. The hand moves faster. Then the stir passes and leaves its bill. The mouth wants another to recover the first minute. The stomach feels full and unsatisfied at once. The mind grows busy with food noise through films and quiet evenings. The morning brings faint regret and a promise to be good today, which sets up the same stir by night.

We have all lived that day. Good until the tired hour. A little something to get through. For a few minutes the day feels patched. Then the patch thins. A little more. By night the pack is lighter than we meant and the mind louder than we meant.

Notice how much happened TO you. The stir arrived and narrowed your attention to the packet. The hurry took over and the relief thinned within minutes. The noise grew louder while no lasting good was given back. If doing a great deal were the same as doing good, the case would be closed in its favour.

But doing is not giving. A storm does a great deal to a roof. We do not thank the storm.

Ask of your own last week. How many times did a sweet leave you calm for the rest of the day, not merely quieter for ten minutes? How many times did it settle the tiredness or tension that called it, so the cause did not return? How many times did it leave no call behind for another? Answer as if plain speaking mattered more than defending a habit, and the column stays empty. The stir came. The gift did not.

This is no accusation. We were taught to read the stir as a gift. When the Nipper pinched and the sweet quieted it for a moment, the Treat Story said this proves the sweet helps. We believed because we had no other explanation and everyone around us said the same. A con needs no stupidity. It needs only trust and a low moment.

Once you see the difference between TO and FOR, you cannot unsee it. Plenty happened to you. Nothing was done for you.

NOTHING FOR YOU

Now we come to the phrase that held us longest.

We tell ourselves BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure or treat. Hear how solid it sounds. Genuine means real, not counterfeit. Pleasure means enjoyment that enriches the hour. Treat means something extra given in kindness to mark love or effort. Together the phrase claims there is real enjoyment here that makes life sweeter than it would be without it.

I believed that phrase for years. We all did. We used it to defend the pudding we finished without enjoying. We used it to defend the cinema chocolate and the tired-evening biscuit. Whenever the cost grew loud, we held up the phrase like a shield. It may cost, we said, but it is still a genuine pleasure or treat, and life without it would be grey.

Test the shield against the dish.

Was that finished pudding a genuine pleasure? A genuine pleasure does not fade by bite three while the hand continues. It does not need distraction to be endured. Think of pleasures you never doubt. You heard the laugh at that table before dessert arrived and it warmed the whole evening. You walked home in cool air and it cleared your head. You tasted the first mouthfuls of a meal eaten hungry, when taste was bright and your body said yes. Those complete and satisfy and leave no call behind. The pudding collapsed into finishing.

Was it a treat? A treat is given to delight you. Who gave this one? Not the people you love. They gave company and the evening itself. Not your body. Your body asked for nothing sweet after dinner and told you by bite three that novelty was gone. The only giver was the old script that says sweetness equals kindness. We confused the kindness of the occasion with the sweetness on the plate, and credited the plate.

The fact is the phrase reverses the truth. What we call a genuine pleasure or treat is a stir mistaken for delight and a habit mistaken for kindness. The delight belonged to the people, the rest, the laughter, the hunger met by real food. The sweet rode alongside and claimed the credit. It was only ever sneaking a ride.

You will feel a pinch hearing that. If the sweet never truly pleased, what were all those years? They were years of being conned by a story we did not write, not years of enjoying a friend we must mourn. There is nothing to mourn when the friend never brought the joy. The joy was yours. The company was yours. The appetite was yours.

Say the phrase once more and feel its fit fail. A genuine pleasure or treat would please to the end and leave you better. This finished without pleasing and left you flatter. When words stop fitting, we stop using them. That is clarity, not loss.

THE HOUSE THAT FILLED ITSELF

See how empty the FOR column is by seeing what fills it instead.

Picture a small gathering in your home early in the day. One guest arrives. A biscuit with morning tea. A sweetened drink at lunch. Nothing wild. He sits in the corner. He talks loudly for a few minutes, then goes dull. You think he will leave. He does not leave. He takes out his telephone and texts his friends.

By mid-afternoon another knocks. Then another. The cake in the break room. The something sweet to get through the slump. Each arrival is louder and duller than the last. By evening the house is full. The cupboard opens more often. The pack meant to last the week is thin. The mind pictures the float and the cookies and the sundae while still chewing. The guests have put their feet up on the couch and show no sign of leaving.

You did not invite the crowd. You invited one. The one invited the rest. That is how BAD SUGAR behaves in the day. One dose calls the next. The morning hit makes the afternoon dip louder. The afternoon hit makes the evening reach harder. Each hit does not settle the house. It fills the house with more hunger for itself.

Be warm to yourself here. You are not a bad host. You opened the door to what you were told was a small pleasure and the pleasure behaved like a gatecrasher. The guests are not your character. They are the trap doing what it was built to do. Fast mouthfuls arrive fast and leave fast, and fast leaving is itself a new invitation. Plain foods do not act like that. A proper meal closes. Fruit after hunger satisfies and quiets. Only the engineered carriers multiply, because only they pique without settling.

Watch one ordinary day and you will see it without any expert to tell you. Eat sweet early and count how often sweetness crosses your mind before night. Then remember a day of real food eaten hungry and stopped at full, and how rarely the thought came. The difference is not virtue on the second day. It is how many guests you let in at the start. One lets in many. None lets in none.

Ask what good the first guest did FOR you. Did he bring calm that lasted? Or only his friends and his noise and his feet on your couch? A benefit does not create the need for itself. Only a trap does that.

The house filled not because you are weak but because you opened the door to something that knows how to fill houses.

YOU WANTED WHAT YOU NO LONGER LIKED

There is one more proof inside your own mouth.

We all know the moment when liking ends and wanting continues. The cinema chocolate shows it clearest. You go in tired and flat. You tell yourself this is your reward. You say the line we all learned: treat when you feel you deserve one. The lights go down. The first squares are vivid. Then the film takes hold and the hand keeps going without you. You look down and half the bar is gone though you cannot recall tasting it. If asked in that dark minute whether you were enjoying it, you would pause. You were not enjoying. You were continuing.

Or take the evening pack. Bright for three bites. Mechanical by ten. By the end faintly sickly, yet finished to the corner. Why finish what stopped pleasing? Liking would have stopped at three. Something else carried you to the end.

That something is wanting, and wanting is not liking. Liking is what the mouth feels when taste is bright and new. Wanting is the pull to take another whether the mouth delights or not. In a free eater the two travel together. In our trap they split. Liking falls after a few bites, as it should when novelty fades. Wanting rises, because each bite re-arms the next. That is why the last bites are strangest. You did not even enjoy the last bite, but you still wanted it. You wanted the packet empty more than you wanted the taste.

Do you see how this empties the FOR column with no argument about harm? A genuine benefit pleases while used. It does not survive the death of enjoyment. A warm bath need not be endured after it cools to prove it warmed you. Only a loop asks you to keep taking after delight has left, and calls the taking proof of delight.

Many learned to call that loop a reward. After stress. After a small victory. After being good all week. Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate. The words sound like self-kindness. Look at what they describe. The day was hard, so we add a stir that makes the evening busier. The week was virtuous, so we pay ourselves with what makes next week harder. If a friend rewarded you that way, you would not call him kind.

You reached for a reward because you deserved kindness, and that instinct is good. The kindness never arrived from the packet. It arrived, when it arrived, from rest, from company, from the film, from knowing you had done well. The sweet stood beside those true givers and took the bow. Once you see who gave what, you stop thanking the one who gave nothing.

So ask of your best favourite: did you like it to the end, or did you want it past liking? If you wanted past liking, the thing you defend is not enjoyment. It is drive. Drive is not a gift. It is the pinch under a nicer name.

It never pleased the way you remember. It only kept you reaching after pleasure had gone.

ASK WHAT GOOD THERE IS AT ALL

Hold the pictures together. The finished pudding. The refused scales. The counted TO and the empty FOR. The house filled by one guest. The mouth wanting past liking.

They settle into one plain verdict. BAD SUGAR is not a flawed friend with pluses and minuses. It is a stir that calls itself a gift. Everything it did, it did TO you. Nothing it did, it did FOR you.

I know this is hard to accept, because a genuine pleasure or treat has lived in us since childhood. Keep an open mind a little longer. You need not argue harm. You need only demand one real benefit and watch the column stay white. An empty column is not opinion. It is a finding from your own dish, your own house, your own mouth.

So here is the next step, to carry where the last one led.

5. ASK WHAT GOOD THERE IS AT ALL
Refuse the harm debate and demand one real benefit.

Ask it of every sweet thought that comes. Not is this too harmful. But what good is there in this at all? Ask it at the table when pudding comes glossy and tall. Ask it at the desk when the tired hour whispers of a pick-me-up. Ask it in the cinema when the hand reaches before the mind has chosen. Ask kindly, without dread, the way you would ask about any claim on your purse. What will this do FOR me that the moment, the people, the meal would not do better without it?

You will hear old answers rise. Let them rise and be tested. One by one they thin, because they were echoes of a story installed before you could examine it. Behind the echoes no benefit waits. There is only the pinch and the script that misreads it, the Nipper complaining and the Treat Story translating the complaint into pleasure.

Watch that translation fail and the Sugar Trap loosens at its hinge. You cannot be conned once you demand the goods and find the box empty. Your body does much teaching from here. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

SUMMARY
- The harm debate keeps the benefit unexamined; the move is to ask what good BAD SUGAR does at all.
- A dessert finished after enjoyment ended proves eating outlived any pleasure it claimed to give.
- BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you in stir and pull and noise, and nothing FOR you in lasting good.
- What we called a genuine pleasure or treat was a stir mistaken for delight, with credit owed to people and moments and real food.
- One sweet invites the next, so a single hit fills the day with hunger for itself.
- Wanting continues after liking ends, which shows the drive is not enjoyment.
- Asking for one real benefit and finding none empties the old argument and leaves nothing to weigh.
