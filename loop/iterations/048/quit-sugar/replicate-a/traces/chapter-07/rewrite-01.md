Chapter 7
EATING AS IT IS MEANT TO BE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen at seven with a real hunger, one ordinary dinner eaten to full, the plate that closes without a sweet, and fruit that tastes brighter when the mouth has washed clean.

*Real food eaten hungry and left at full needs no sweet to finish it.*

THE FLATNESS YOU FEAR

You sit at six with the day behind you and supper ahead and a thought moves before hunger does.

It says eating without something sweet will be flat. It says the meal will lie there unfinished. It says Dessert is the best part of every meal and without it the table has no ending.

I hear you because we all heard that thought in the same kitchen. I heard it for years and answered it the same way, with a biscuit before supper and a pudding after and a bar later to close the evening that never closed.

We took sweets for the roof of the meal because every meal we remember had that roof. We called the flatness a fact about food. We called the sweet the part that makes eating worth sitting down for.

Ask what that flatness ever was.

Was it hunger still speaking after a meal that should have settled it? Or was it the mouth speaking after the stomach had already gone quiet? Did the flatness arrive before the first sweet of the day, when the mouth still tasted plainly? Or did it arrive after sweets, when the mouth had learned to compare everything to the last bar?

There is only one honest reading and you have already lived it a hundred times. The flatness is not the nature of food. It is the aftertaste of BAD SUGAR.

Plain food never promised to shout. It promised to satisfy. We mistook the shout for the satisfaction because we ate the shout daily and forgot what satisfaction feels like.

The fact is a meal that leaves you full and quiet is not flat. It is finished.

ONE ORDINARY DINNER

Do not take my word for it. Take one dinner and eat it whole.

Wait till you are hungry. Not peckish and passing the cupboard. Hungry. The hollow low down that comes slowly in the late afternoon and does not name a brand. It does not say chocolate. It does not say biscuits. It says food. You know that hollow from childhood before wrappers taught you other names. Let it arrive. Do not feed it early with sips and bites. Let the table find you hungry.

Cook plain food you like that satisfies. Cook meat with potatoes and greens that fills the hollow. Choose fish with bread and butter that holds hunger down. Eat beans with rice that settle the stomach. Cook eggs with vegetables and cheese that carry the evening. Make soup thick enough to stand the spoon in and stay with you. Choose food with weight and warmth and salt that answers hunger. Put it on a plate and sit down. Put nothing sweet on the table. Put no pudding behind it as a promise or a threat. Let the meal stand alone.

Eat slowly. Chew. Notice the first mouthfuls. Notice how hunger makes plain tastes loud. The bread tastes of more than bread when you are hungry. The soup tastes deeper. The meat tastes richer. Hunger is the sauce we spent years buying in packets.

Eat till the hollow fills. Not till the plate shines because the plate must shine. Till the body changes its signal. You will feel it low down first, a settling, a weight that is not heaviness. The bites slow on their own. The talk gets easier. The shoulders drop. The second helping looks less interesting than the talk. That is full. It is not stuffed. It is not the coated fullness after a pack. It is the quiet fullness after food.

Stop there. Put the fork down. Wait ten minutes with the plate before you decide anything about sweets.

Ask in that ten minutes what the body says.

Does the hollow still call? Or has the hollow gone and left the mind quiet for the first time since lunch? Do you picture a sweet because the stomach asks? Or do you picture a sweet because the hour says a sweet should follow? Are you reaching toward the cupboard from emptiness? Or from habit wearing hunger's coat?

You know the difference the moment you stop to watch it. Hunger gone quiet stays quiet for hours after real food. The mouth-pull talks fast and names wrappers and will not take bread. One is the body fed. The other is the loop talking through the mouth and borrowing the body's voice.

We ate sweets on top of full stomachs for years and called it finishing the meal. It never finished the meal. It restarted the mouth after the stomach had already closed the meal.

Let the meal close.

HUNGER IS THE BELL

Hunger feels like an enemy when you have fed it sweets for years. It arrives shaky and hollow and a little thin in the head and you read it as danger. You read it as proof you must carry something sweet to see you through.

Watch one hungry hour whole and hunger shows itself as something else.

Real hunger arrives slowly. It widens across the morning if breakfast was light. It deepens toward supper if lunch was hours back. It takes any plain meal gladly. Give it bread and it settles. Give it soup and it settles. Give it meat and potatoes and it settles for hours and leaves the mind off food. That is the bell ringing for food.

The sweet-hunger arrives sudden. It names a food by name. It will not take bread. It arrives an hour after a bun that was meant to see you through. It arrives at the desk at three when the morning was built from sips. It says now and says sweet and says quick. That is not the bell. That is the echo of the last hit wearing off and leaving the original hunger underneath plus a new pull on top.

You ask how you can trust hunger when hunger has lied to you so often. Hunger never lied to you. Sweets lied in hunger's name.

The fact is your body runs its own supply line from the food you eat across the day. You do not carry your day's strength in spoonfuls dropped from above. You eat meat and fish and bread and beans and vegetables and eggs, and the body keeps what it needs moving in the blood where it should be. The liver holds the store and spends it steady between meals. That is why a plain lunch holds you till supper and a sweet lunch drops you before the hour ends. One fed the store. The other shouted over the store and left it emptier than before.

You do not need to count that supply or manage it or learn its chemistry. You need only hear the bell and answer it with food.

When did a sweet taken for hunger ever keep you off food till the next meal the way a meal keeps you off food? When did the shaky hollow ever stay gone after BAD SUGAR and not return with interest before the hour ended? When did you finish a pack hungry and feel hunger done rather than hunger plus thirst plus motion?

You know the pattern because you timetable around it. The afternoons you built from meals held. The afternoons you built from grazes slid. The body did not change its rules between those days. The fuel changed.

Hunger is not a hole that sweets must fill. Hunger is the bell that food answers.

FULL IS THE ANSWER

Full feels like a mystery when you have eaten past it for years. You ask where the line is. You ask how you will know. You ask whether stopping is another rule to learn and fail.

Full is not a rule. Full is a reading.

Sit with one plate and you can read it plainly. The first third of the plate belongs to hunger. The bites come fast. The tastes sing. The hollow softens with every mouthful. The middle third belongs to satisfaction arriving. The bites slow. You start talking more. You notice the room. The last third belongs to the signal. Enough has arrived. The food tastes just as good but you want it less. The fork feels heavier. The breath comes easier. The stomach feels weighted and warm, not stretched, not coated, not thirsty.

Stop when wanting less arrives. Not when the plate is empty. Not when everyone else has finished. When you want it less.

We lost that reading because BAD SUGAR never lets it arrive. A pack has no last third. It opens loud and thins fast and never finishes till the container stands empty. The mouth goes dull by the middle but the hand keeps travelling. The stomach stays empty underneath while the mouth stays busy on top. There is no signal in that motion, only motion. No wonder you stopped trusting full. You practised for years on foods that have no full built into them.

Real food has full built in. The stomach receives weight and sends the word. The brain receives the word and quiets the thoughts. That quiet is not imagined and not learned from a book. Children live it before anyone teaches them portions. They run hungry to the table and eat with appetite and push the plate away and run again. They do not need a chart to tell them the meal is done. The body tells them and they obey because no one has yet taught them to distrust it.

You still own that reading. You used it this morning without thinking when you drank water and stopped when thirst ended. You used it last winter when you came in cold and stood by the fire till warm and stepped away without counting minutes. Hunger and fullness work the same way. They arrive, they are answered, they leave.

Ask of tonight's plate what any fair judge would ask of any instrument.

Did the plate eaten hungry settle the hollow for hours? Did the mind leave food alone after stopping at full? Did you rise from the table thinking of the evening ahead rather than the cupboard behind? If yes, the instruments work. They worked tonight without a sweet capstone, without a rule, without a count.

The fact is stopping at full is not control. It is hearing.

WHEN THE HAZE LIFTS

There is a taste story you carry without knowing you carry it.

You taste everything through a filter. The filter is the last bar. Every apple is measured against the bar. Every bowl of berries is measured against the bar. Every plain yoghurt pales beside that memory. Next to the bar, plain food tastes pale. Next to the bar, fruit tastes sour. Against that loud memory, supper tastes like the waiting room for pudding.

We called that pale taste the truth about plain food. It was the truth about the filter.

Think of a room painted white after years of yellow lamplight. At first the white looks blue and cold. The eyes complain. A week later the same white looks white. Nothing in the paint changed. The eyes washed clean.

The mouth washes clean the same way.

Eat ordinary dinners hungry and stopped at full for days on end, with no BAD SUGAR shouting over them, and plain tastes return to their own size. Soup tastes of more than salt. Bread tastes sweet in its own way without any sugar on it. An orange after supper tastes bright enough to make you pause. Berries taste almost too sweet, the way they tasted when you were small and a punnet was a feast.

I lived that return with plain fruit I had dismissed for years as rabbit food. I ate an apple after a week of ordinary meals and stopped halfway down the garden path because the apple stung sweet on the tongue. The same apple a month before had tasted like water and chewing. The apple had not changed its nature between those bites. My mouth had changed its comparison.

Watch the other side of the same washing and it tells you as much. Taste one shop treat after days of plain eating and it does not sing the way memory sang it. It hits too sweet, too flat, too chemical at the edges, like a cube of sugar with colour on it. You chew and reach for water. You wonder what the fuss ever was. The fuss was the filter. The bar tasted heavenly when every day was measured in bars. Against a clean mouth it tastes loud and small.

Do not take this as a timetable or a pledge. Mouths differ and days differ and no one can say which supper the brightness returns on. Take it as what eaters notice when they live on meals for a while. The haze thins. Plain food grows larger. Shop sweets grow smaller. Taste comes home.

Ask what that homecoming proves.

If plain food were truly flat, would it grow brighter the longer you eat it? If BAD SUGAR truly crowned taste, would it taste harsher the cleaner the mouth? What passed as proof of benefit was proof of comparison. The loudest taste set the scale and made every honest taste look pale beside it. Remove the loudest taste and the scale resets. Food tastes like food again.

That reset is not a prize for the future. It starts tonight, in one dinner eaten hungry, in fruit that already tastes a shade brighter when no bar stands beside it.

IT WAS NEVER THE STRAWBERRY

You say you fear life will narrow to boiled greens and duty. You say ordinary eating sounds like punishment with better lighting. You say the foods you love are the sweet ones and without them nothing sings.

Look at which foods ever sang and which only shouted.

When eaters are asked which foods they lose control over and rank them plainly, the same names crowd the top every time. Fizzy drinks. Chocolate. Biscuits. Pastries. Sweetened cereals. Flavoured yoghurts. Ice cream. Rolls that vanish to nothing in the mouth. The foods that rise are the processed ones built from sugar with fat, tuned sweet and soft to hit fast. At the bottom sit apples and strawberries and broccoli and plain staple foods eaten as food. No one writes that they emptied a bowl of apples till they felt sick and hid the cores. No one says the strawberry kept them up picturing more.

The fact is the trouble concentrates where sugar rides with fat and fast melt, the very foods the loop feeds on.

That ranking breaks the flatness fear open. If sweetness itself proved benefit, the strawberry would trap you hardest. It tastes bright and sweet and whole. Yet the strawberry never started the slide. The pack did. The difference is not deliciousness. The difference is construction. One was grown to be eaten and to finish. The other was tuned to pique without telling the mouth to stop, sweetness with melt that tells the mouth no weight arrived, so the mouth asks again.

You never needed to fear losing the strawberry. The strawberry stays. The orange stays. The roast stays. The bread stays. The soup stays. The cheese stays. The fish stays. The meal you ate hungry tonight stays, and stays satisfying, and stays yours every day you want it.

Think of the tables that prove it without argument. The dinner eaten hungry where bread and soup and meat satisfied and the talk carried the hour and no one missed a capstone. The lunch that held from noon till seven with no timetable of sips. The fruit eaten with appetite that needed no chocolate behind it. You already own hours where taste arrived whole with no pack to thank. You lived them. Taste did not need BAD SUGAR. BAD SUGAR needed taste to look like more than a fast hit that dulls as it pulls.

Put tonight beside those hours and the fear thins. Eating is not flat without sweets. Eating was flat with sweets, because sweets laid their filter over every honest taste and called the filter flavour.

SIXTH INSTRUCTION: EAT REAL FOOD WHEN YOU ARE HUNGRY AND STOP WHEN YOU ARE FULL
Hunger and satisfaction are the instruments, not rules.

Eat when the hollow calls. Stop when wanting less arrives. Let plain meals satisfy and let the quiet after them stand. Do not crown the meal. Do not close it with motion. Let it close itself.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

SUMMARY
- Eating without sweets is not flat, the flatness was the filter of the last bar laid over honest taste.
- One dinner eaten hungry and stopped at full settles the hollow for hours and leaves the mind quiet without a sweet to close it.
- Hunger arrives slow and takes any plain meal gladly, the sudden mouth-pull that names a wrapper is not hunger.
- The body keeps its own steady supply from the food you eat across the day, no spoonful carries the day.
- Full arrives as wanting less, real food carries that signal while packs carry only motion.
- Plain tastes grow brighter as the mouth washes clean, fruit tastes sweeter and shop treats taste loud and small.
- The trouble crowds into processed fast hits and leaves apples and strawberries alone, ordinary food stays and satisfies.
