Chapter 15

KEEP IT

IN THIS CHAPTER: one pocket page to carry, the list that brought you here, and the one joyful thought you keep for life.

*You are free now and you hold everything you need to stay free.*

THE PAGE YOU CARRY

You did the work and the work is done. You came as a smoker who believed cigarettes calmed you and pleased you and helped you belong. You leave as a non-smoker who sees the truth and feels glad to be out.

I do not want you to carry this whole book in your head. I want you to carry one page. What follows is that page. It holds every instruction I gave you in the order I gave it, in the words I gave it. Keep it where you keep what matters. If doubt ever knocks, you will not need to argue it out again. You will read the list and you will remember who you are now.

Keep the page close enough that you know where it is without thinking. Fold it into your wallet beside the cards you use every day. Keep it in the pocket you always reach for first. Keep a photograph of it in your phone beside the pictures of the people you love. Keep one copy where you sleep and one where you start the day. The place does not matter. The closeness matters. You carry it the way you carry what is settled and yours.

You will not read it every day. You will not need to read it every day. You carry it so that the keeping itself tells you the matter is closed. It sits beside you while you live. It waits while you work and while you eat and while you sleep. It asks nothing of you. It proves nothing again. It stays because you are free and free people keep what freed them.

When you do unfold it, you will unfold it slowly. You will see your own hands holding the same words you followed to walk out. You will read the first line and you will hear my voice saying it exactly as I said it to you. You will read the next line and the next, and each line will put you back in the room where you understood it. You do not study the page. You greet it. You look at what you hold and you know at once who holds it.

There will be a moment when you unfold it because a stray thought passed and you want the comfort of the familiar words. You will not open it to fight the thought. You will open it to remember that the fight is over. You will run your eyes down the list and you will feel the same calm you felt when each line landed the first time. The first line steadies you. The middle lines hold you. The last lines close the door again. You fold the page and you put it back, and the putting back is the whole point. The thought came and the page answered without argument, and you moved on.

There will be another moment when you unfold it for no reason at all. You find it while you change a coat. You see it while you tidy a drawer. You meet it in your phone while you look for something else. You smile because it is still there and still true. You read two lines and you put it away again. That small smile is the keeping doing its work. You do not need the page to convince you. You need it to remind you that you no longer need convincing.

Keep it for the days when memory softens and the old years try to look kinder than they were. Memory can smooth the edges of anything. It can make the slavery look like company and the hourly obedience look like choice. When that softening comes, you do not trust the soft picture. You trust the page. The page does not soften. It says the same words in the same order with the same plain meaning. It holds the shape of what you saw clearly on the day you walked out. You read it and the soft picture clears, and you are yourself again.

Keep it for the years ahead. The paper will wear at the folds. The photograph in your phone will move from one phone to the next. That wearing is gladness you can touch. It means you never needed another book and never needed another method. One page was enough because the truth on it was whole.

You will know you are still the free non-smoker who left because you know how you read the page. A smoker reads such a list as orders from outside and feels the weight of them. You read it as your own history and feel the lightness of it. You do not read it to force yourself to obey. You read it to recognise yourself. Each line sounds like something you already know and already live. That recognition is the proof of who you are, and it needs no further proof.

You will know it by the way you close the page. You do not close it with relief that you survived another reading. You close it with warmth that it is yours. You put it back where it lives and you turn your attention to the meal in front of you or the work in front of you or the person in front of you. The page goes quiet. Your life stays loud. That easy turning back to life is the sign that the keeping has done its job.

I ask you to keep the page in this spirit. Do not memorise it as a burden. Do not test yourself on it. Do not count how many lines you can recite. Let it sit where it sits and let it speak when you ask it to speak. The list does not guard you by being repeated. It guards you by being kept. While it is kept, you are the person who kept it, and that person is free.

THE THOUGHTS YOU KEEP

Keep one name for everything you left. The nicotine trap. When a stray thought comes about stress or pleasure or company or a hard day or a bright one, you give it that name and the thought has nowhere to go. It was never your voice. It was the voice of the trap you walked out of.

That naming is not a debate. You do not weigh the thought and answer its points. You name it and you smile at how clearly you see it. The name puts the thought where it belongs, outside you, behind you, finished. You learned the name on the day you saw the enclosure for what it was, and the name has kept its meaning ever since. One name holds the whole journey. You speak it silently and the journey stands whole in your mind.

Keep one thought for every time cigarettes cross your mind, on a good day or a bad day, among smokers or alone, for the rest of your life. Think it exactly as I taught you:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it with a smile when you wake and when you eat and when you drink and when you drive and when you wait. Think it when someone lights beside you. Think it when the evening runs late among friends. Each time you think it, the old cue becomes a small celebration. You do not debate the thought. You do not test it. You replace it with gladness and you get on with the moment, which was always yours.

This is the replacement I promised you at the start. One thought takes the place of every old reach. You do not need a different answer for each hour because the answer was always the same. You are free and you are glad, and the gladness fits every hour. Morning takes it. Night takes it. Work takes it. Rest takes it. The thought does not change its shape to fit the moment. The moment is met by the thought and the moment stays yours.

Think it the way you would greet a friend you are happy to see. Say it inside yourself with warmth and with force. Let the words carry the full weight of what they mean. Brilliant means your chest is clear and your mornings are yours and your money stays home and your hours are no longer rung by a bell you did not choose. Free means no dose stands between you and anything you love. You do not whisper it as a wish. You declare it as a fact, because it is a fact.

You will think it thousands of times and it will never wear out. It will not fade the way a slogan fades because it is not a slogan. It is the true description of your state spoken in joy. Each thinking keeps it bright. Each old cue that triggers it makes the next cue weaker and the joy stronger. The thought that once would have opened the door now locks it with gladness, and every locking makes the house more yours.

I ask nothing more of you. You changed nothing else in your life to win this. You keep nothing burning and you mourn nothing gone.

THE LIFE YOU CARRY IT INTO

You walk out of this book into your own life, and your life is waiting exactly as you left it, only larger.

Go forward with empty pockets in the best sense. No pack weighs the cloth. No timetable rings inside the day. What you carry now is paper and joy, a page and a shout, and both weigh nothing. You move lighter because nothing pulls at you, and you move gladder because you know why nothing pulls.

Let the days come as they come. You meet each day with the same hands and the same heart, and both are free. The page sits where you put it. The thought sits ready and bright. You do not walk carefully around life. You walk straight through the middle of it.

Carry the gladness outward. Let it show in the way you linger where you once hurried out. People will see that something is different before you say a word. They will see a person who is fully where he is. If they ask, you tell them simply that you escaped and that escape is gladness. Then you turn back to the day, because the day is the point.

Keep the joy plain and daily. It does not need a ceremony. It lives in the easy breath and the clean taste and the unbroken hour. These are not prizes you must earn again each day. They are what remains when the tax stops. You enjoy them the way you enjoy light through a window, without counting and without fearing the dark.

Let the years gather around the keeping. The page will grow soft. The shout will grow strong. You will find that you think the joyful thought less often as the old cues fire less, and when you do think it you think it with deeper warmth. That lessening is not forgetting. It is freedom settling into bone.

I want your last feeling in this book to be outward and bright. Do not look back at the enclosure. Do not keep one window open toward it. The door is closed and the ground behind it is not yours. Look at the road ahead and the people beside you and the hours in front of you. They are yours in a way they were never fully yours while the hourly obedience ran. Nothing stands between you and them now. Nothing burns. Nothing is mourned. One name holds what you left. One shout holds what you won. A page holds the road you walked. Your life holds everything else.

Hold that gladness all the way out. Hold it through the next morning and the next year and the year after that. Hold it when life is kind and when life is hard, because it belongs to both and it serves both. It does not leave when trouble comes. It stands beside you and reminds you who stands there. A free non-smoker stands there, holding a page and a shout and a whole life, needing nothing else.

SUMMARY

Keep this list. Photograph it if you wish and carry it with you.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND.
You cannot be conned twice if you check everything.
2. DO NOT STOP YET.
Let the belief change first, then stopping is easy.
3. BEGIN WITH GLADNESS.
There is no doom in what we do here.
4. NOTICE WHO IS IN CHARGE.
If it were free choice you would not need this book.
5. ASK WHAT GOOD IT DOES AT ALL.
Refuse the harm debate; demand one real gift.
6. WATCH ONE CIGARETTE CLOSELY.
See what it actually does, not what you credit it with.
7. REFUSE THE SPECIAL ONE.
If none is special, every exception story ends.
8. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD.
Mixed methods rebuild the trap.
9. IGNORE ANYONE WHO SAYS STOPPING MUST BE HARD.
They used the method we name, not the truth.
10. SMOKE YOUR LAST CIGARETTE WITH FULL ATTENTION.
Notice the ugliness, then close the door.
11. NEVER THINK ONE WILL NOT HURT.
The thought is the Con talking, not you.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! Go and enjoy life free.
