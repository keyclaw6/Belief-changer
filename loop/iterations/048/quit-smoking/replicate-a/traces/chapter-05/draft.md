Chapter 5

THE HEAVEN THAT LASTS A LIGHT

IN THIS CHAPTER: one evening meal finished at the table, one cigarette tasted from lighting to end beside a drink and beside doing nothing.

*The heaven you remember is real and it lasts seconds at the lighting, and everything after it you endure rather than enjoy.*

The Table After The Plates Are Cleared

Let me join you at the table where pleasure makes its strongest case, because this is where most of us would defend the cigarette if we had to defend it anywhere.

The meal is finished. The plates sit empty. The food was good and you ate it slowly and you feel that settled fullness that belongs to a meal closed clean. The drink stands beside your plate with a little left in it. The room holds the smell of the food and the warmth of having eaten enough. Nobody is hurrying you. This is the moment you love to light up.

You reach for the pack. You strike the lighter. You draw the first puff deep and you feel a rush lift through your chest and head. For a few seconds the world feels richer. The food feels finished in the right way. The evening feels rounded off. You lean back and you think this is heaven.

I believe you about those seconds. I lived for those seconds for years and I do not mock them for a moment.

You are not pretending when you call that moment good. You are not foolish for wanting to keep it. We all guarded that lit moment more closely than any other in the day because it felt like the one honest pleasure in the whole schedule. When the day had taken and taken, that first draw felt like something given back. Any person who felt that lift would want to name it pleasure. You named it pleasure because the lift and the lighting arrived together a thousand times.

Keep the honesty of that naming while we look closer, because the closeness of the look changes nothing about your taste and everything about where the pleasure came from.

You asked only for what any smoker asks for: A real pleasure, relief, or crutch.

If the cigarette is that pleasure, it should behave like pleasure behaves. True pleasure stays with you while you use it. It does not turn against you halfway through the use. It does not leave you feeling worse about yourself when it ends. It does not need you to be deprived beforehand to count as good. Hold the lit moment to that plain test, not to fear and not to habit, and watch what one cigarette does when you taste it all the way through.

Taste It All The Way Through

Smoke the next one in memory with full attention, the way you would taste anything you trusted with your evenings.

The lighting is sweet. The first draw rolls warm and full and the chest loosens and the head lightens for a few seconds. The second draw is still soft. By the third and fourth the sweetness thins. The smoke feels drier at the back of the throat. The taste turns papery and then bitter. The chest feels heavier rather than lighter. The nose fills with stale smell. The mouth feels coated. You keep drawing because the cigarette is lit, not because each draw improves on the last.

Tell me honestly whether you finish it for the taste. You finish it down to the filter while the taste worsens with every inch. You press it out and the air around you smells used. Your mouth tastes used. Your fingers smell used. The settled fullness from the meal feels covered over rather than completed. Within minutes a flat feeling arrives that was not there when the plates were cleared. You love smoking and yet you hate being a smoker, and both feelings live in that one small sequence.

That split tells the truth better than any argument. The heaven lives at the lighting and the dislike lives through the rest. If the whole cigarette were pleasure, the pleasure would deepen as you smoked. It does the opposite. It opens with a flash and then declines into endurance while you tell yourself you are still enjoying it.

You can test this against your own evenings without taking my word for it.

If you truly enjoyed the taste, you would smoke when your taste is cleanest and you would notice the taste improve. You do the reverse. You smoke after food and drink have already filled your mouth with flavour, when your mouth is least able to judge, and you let the smoke sit over the flavours you just enjoyed. You smoke when you have a cold and cannot taste at all, and you still call it enjoyment. You smoke the cigarette that follows a cigarette smoked ten minutes before, when the mouth already feels stale, and you still reach. A man who loves a flavour does not chase it when his tongue is dulled and does not keep using it after it turns harsh.

Ask yourself when you last smoked one slowly to the end for the flavour alone, with no tug beforehand and no pause to fill. You cannot find that cigarette, because the enjoyment never stood apart from the want before it. The moment you light up you are in heaven, but you feel poor afterwards, and the heaven and the poor feeling belong to the same object. The flash is real. The day-long gift is not.

Consider how quickly the flash passes even in your fondest memory. You remember the lighting and you forget the middle and the end. You remember the lean-back second and you forget the throat clearing and the window opened to let the air out and the glass of water to take the taste away. Memory keeps the seconds and discards the minutes because the seconds flatter the story. Attention keeps the whole cigarette and shows the proportion plain. Seconds of lift followed by minutes of dulling is not a pleasure worth its price. It is a flash sold as a feast.

Set It Beside A Meal, A Drink, And Doing Nothing

Now set the cigarette beside three honest pleasures and let each one show what it does without help.

Take the meal you just finished. You chose the food. You smelled it while it cooked. You tasted the first bite clean and you tasted the middle bites with the same interest and you finished with the taste still good in your mouth. The pleasure lasted through the using. It left you fed and satisfied. It did not ask you to be hungry for an hour beforehand to count as good. It did not turn bitter in the eating. It did not leave you wanting to wash its taste away the moment it ended.

Take a drink you like. You lift the glass. You sip. The first sip is good and the middle sips are good and the last sip is good. You can leave half the glass and return to it later and it is still good. You do not need to finish it to the dregs while the flavour worsens. You do not feel poorer as a person for having drunk it. The drink adds to the evening and then steps aside cleanly.

Take doing nothing. You sit after the meal with no task and no smoke. The chair holds you. The room stays warm. The fullness settles. Your mouth keeps the clean aftertaste of the food. Your breath moves easy. Your mind wanders and returns. Nothing needs renewing. The minute asks nothing of a lighter or an ashtray and gives back exactly what it promises, which is rest.

Now place the cigarette among those three. It is the only one that must be finished while it worsens. It is the only one that leaves its taste as something to remove. It is the only one that cannot be left half smoked without the mind returning to it. It is the only one that borrows its setting from the meal and the drink and the rest and then claims it made the setting. The meal gave the satisfaction. The drink gave the flavour. The pause gave the rest. The dose rode inside all three and collected the thanks they earned.

That borrowing is why the cigarette feels like it completes the meal. The meal truly completes something. Hunger ends. Flavour arrives. Company warms. The body settles. Then the lighter strikes inside that already complete minute and the flash of relief joins the completeness, and the mind joins them into one event. You credit the paper tube with finishing what the food had already finished. Lift the smoke out and leave everything else standing, and the meal still closes clean. The food still satisfies. The drink still tastes. The rest still rests. Nothing collapses except the trip to the ashtray and the harshness at the back of the throat.

Try the separation in memory before you try it in life. Replay last night's table beat by beat, then lift the smoke out and leave everything else standing. The plates still clear. The drink still stands with a little left in it. The room still holds warmth. Your breath still moves easy after eating. Your mouth still keeps the food taste instead of trading it for ash. The evening still feels rounded because a good meal rounds it. What remains is the hour itself, fully enjoyable, with its comforts intact. Your body already knows how to close a meal. It closed thousands of meals before the first cigarette ever taught it to doubt itself.

That is why non-smokers finish the same meal and lean back with the same satisfied sigh while wanting nothing from a pack. They are not missing a layer. They are tasting the layer whole. They keep the food taste clean to the end. They keep the evening clean to the end. You can keep yours the same way the moment the credit is corrected, because the finish never came from the paper tube. It came from eating enough and sitting warm and being done, all of which remain fully yours.

The same test holds for the drink you pair it with and for the idle minute you fill with it. The drink tastes better without smoke sitting over it. The idle minute rests deeper without a timetable inside it. The recliner and the ashtray waiting in their places on the patio taught you to read the pause as incomplete until lit, but the pause was whole before the lighting ever touched it. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

The Little Parties For Everything Finished

There is one more disguise pleasure wears through the day, and you know it well because it follows you from morning to night.

The cigarette poses as a small reward for anything finished. Teaching a class earns one. Finishing a story earns one. Finishing a paragraph earns one. Driving the long miles earns one. Driving the short road to the shops earns one. Anything completed becomes a reason, and anything endured becomes a reason, and the day fills with lit pauses that feel deserved.

I honour the work underneath those pauses. The class took your patience. The story and the paragraph took your care. The long drive and the short drive took your attention. Those acts deserve their glow. Your hands did them and your mind stayed with them, and the glow belongs to the doing.

The dose only attaches itself to the finished glow and claims the glow as its own work. It did not teach the class. It did not write the paragraph. It did not drive the road safe. It arrived after the work was already shining and stood beside the shining the way a stranger steps into a photograph. Then we thanked the stranger for the day.

Watch how widely those parties spread once you look for them. The morning holds one for getting out of bed. The mid-morning holds one for answering two messages. The noon holds one for eating. The afternoon holds one for surviving the call. The evening holds one for cooking and one for clearing and one for sitting down at last. The same small paper tube celebrates effort and rest and worry and relief without changing its nature once. Work keeps its full dignity in this account. The teaching held the room. The writing cost real thought. The driving kept you safe. None of that needs smoke to count. The parties add nothing to the acts they follow. They only interrupt the glow of finished work with a short errand, then return you with less time and heavier breath to enjoy what you did.

Notice how obedient those parties truly are. They arrive every hour, hard task or easy task, finished work or unfinished worry. A true reward follows the work. These follow the clock. The morning one comes before any work is done. The mid-morning one comes whether the morning went well or badly. The afternoon one comes because the hour has passed, not because the hour was survived. The ceremony looks private and chosen, but the timetable underneath is strict. The small tug names the hour. The small voice names the reason. Together they keep the schedule running while calling it celebration.

Concentration wears the same costume on the same schedule.

You tell yourself the cigarette helps you think. You light up before the letter and the paragraph comes easier. You light up before the drive and the road feels clearer. You treat the first puff as the engine of thought and you stare ahead while the smoke curls and you feel your mind settle enough to begin.

Look at what truly settled. Before you lit up you carried two loads: the task ahead and the faint edgy want underneath it. The want scatters attention the way any faint want scatters attention. You light up and the want quiets for minutes, and the quiet feels like sharpening. But the task received nothing. The mind received only the removal of an interruption the last dose had installed. A non-smoker begins the same letter with one load instead of two and keeps his attention whole without paying a dose for the privilege. He does not think better because he is gifted. He thinks with the distraction missing that you must briefly feed to miss for minutes.

Boredom wears it from the other side.

You tell yourself the cigarette fills the empty minute. The wait stretches. The room is quiet. Nothing asks for you. You light up to make the minute pass with flavour. Then watch what the minute becomes. It becomes a timetable minute rather than an empty minute. You check the pack. You check the time. You count to the end. You step out and step back. The empty minute that could have passed clean becomes a small errand with ash at the end. Doing nothing is already complete. It asks nothing and gives rest. The dose enters the rest and divides it into want and brief quiet and want again, then claims the brief quiet as entertainment.

Ask yourself three plain questions and let your own week answer them.

When the meal ended and you lit up, did the smoke improve the food taste through the whole cigarette or only lift the first seconds while the food taste faded underneath it.

When the task finished and you threw yourself a little party, did the party honour the work or interrupt the glow of the work with a harsh taste you then needed water to remove.

If the cigarette truly pleased you, why does the pleasure shrink with every draw while a meal and a drink and an idle minute please you from beginning to end.

There is only one answer that fits all three without calling you foolish. You were never foolish. That voice is The Con.

What Relief Was Hiding Inside Pleasure

That voice deserves a clear look, because it has spoken beside every lit moment you ever loved.

It told you the flash was the cigarette giving. It told you the meal needed a finish. It told you the paragraph needed a pause with smoke in it. It told you the idle minute was empty until filled. It spoke in your own tone so you took it for your own judgment. It is not your judgment. It is the belief system that feeds on the tug and calls the feeding pleasure.

Feel how the trick runs once you see both parts together. The tug grows quietly between doses as a faint empty edginess. It sharpens until the hour turns. Then the lighting quiets it in seconds, and the quiet feels enormous against the want before it. We mistake the size of the contrast for the size of the gift. The louder the want before, the sweeter the pause after. The cigarette makes the noise, then sells you the pause as pleasure. The heaven is seconds long and the price is the rest of the day carrying the timetable that demands the next heaven.

You do not need to fight that timetable. You do not need to brace against it or count your way past it. You only need to see whose quiet you felt in those seconds. It was the quiet of the last dose ending its own demand, not the quiet of a gift arriving. Once you see the owner clearly, the reach loses its glamour, because you no longer mistake feeding the last cigarette for enjoying the next minute.

Consider the isolation plainly, because it settles the whole pleasure claim without one word about illness.

Take two identical evenings after the same good meal. In one you sit with the drink and the fullness and no smoke. In the other you sit with the same drink and the same fullness and smoke. The food satisfies you in both. The drink flavours you in both. The rest restores you in both. The only difference is that in one evening a tug is quieted for seconds and then returns with its stale taste, while in the other the tug is left to fade on its own while the food taste stays clean. Everything that truly pleased was present in both evenings. Everything the smoke added was a brief quiet of its own noise followed by the promise of more noise.

That is why the pleasure argument ends the reward argument with it once it falls. The parties were never separate reasons. They were the same flash attached to every hour under a different name. Call it focus before the task and it is the tug quieted for minutes. Call it rest in the idle minute and it is the tug quieted for minutes. Call it reward after the work and it is the tug quieted for minutes. Remove the flash and every party in the day loses its music at once, because the music never came from the finished acts. It came from the brief quiet standing beside them.

So let the pleasure claim fall where your own watched cigarette dropped it.

You felt a real flash and you credited a real giver. The flash was real. The giver was not. The giver was the last dose pausing its own demand while the meal and the drink and the minute stood complete behind it. A flash that lasts a lighting and then turns harsh cannot be the pleasure of your evenings. It can only be relief mistaken for pleasure, sold again each hour by the want it feeds. You came to this chapter fearing life without that flash would taste grey. Look at what the full cigarette showed instead. The grey was never in the evenings without smoke. The grey sat in the middle and the end of every cigarette, in the coated mouth and the heavy chest and the flat feeling after, and you smoked through it while calling it colour.

This changes everything about the next lit moment you picture with fondness. You will still remember the lean-back second when the lighter struck. You will still feel tenderness toward the evenings it seemed to complete. But you will no longer read the tenderness as proof the cigarette gave. When the memory comes, you will know the food closed the meal and the drink gave the flavour and the pause gave the rest, and the dose only stood beside them and took the bow. You will take the meal and the drink and the idle minute whole, and you will leave the harsh middle and the stale end where they belong, outside your evenings for good.

Why The First One Of The Day Fools You Most

There is one cigarette that will argue back hardest while you read, so let me meet it directly.

You will tell me the first one of the day truly tastes wonderful. You wake with a dry mouth and a faint headache of want after the night without. You reach before your feet find the floor. You light up and the first draw feels deeper and fuller than any other in the day. You call that proof that at least one cigarette is pure pleasure.

I understand why that one feels convincing. The night has starved the tug longer than any hour of the day starves it. The hollow stands at its deepest when you wake. The first quiet after the longest noise always feels largest. You are not tasting a better cigarette. You are feeling a longer want go quiet for seconds. The cigarette did not improve overnight. The want deepened overnight, so the contrast sharpened.

Watch that morning cigarette past its opening the same way you watched the evening one. The first two draws feel full. The third feels ordinary. The fourth feels rough on an empty throat. By the end you cough and your mouth feels sour before food has touched it, and the clean taste of breakfast must push past the stale taste to reach you. A true pleasure does not greet the cleanest hour of the day by dulling it before it begins. It does not ask you to coat the tongue before tasting food. The morning reach proves only that the tug survived the night and asked first for its coin before the day could start.

Set that morning beside the same morning with the tug gone and the difference stands plain. You wake. Your mouth tastes only of sleep. Water tastes clean. Food tastes clean. Air on the step tastes clean. Your chest moves easy on the stairs without first paying for breath. Nothing rings to be answered before the day begins. The morning already knows how to open. It opened that way every day of your childhood before the first cigarette taught it to wait for lighting.

The Hands That Hold The Cup

Look once more at your own hands across any ordinary hour, because they testify without flattery.

A man enjoying a drink holds the glass loosely and forgets it between sips. A man enjoying food lingers over the plate and talks between bites. His hands rest. His shoulders rest. Nothing in him watches the clock.

Your hands with a cigarette behave nothing like enjoying hands. They check the pack. They tap the pack. They find the lighter. They shield the flame. They lift and lower on a fixed rhythm whether the talk is good or dull. They seek the ashtray. They press and twist at the end. They wash away the smell after. Those are the movements of tending, not of tasting. You do not tend a pleasure that way. You tend a small demand that will sour if left alone for an hour.

That tending follows you into every room and taxes every pleasure it touches. The meal pauses for the step outside. The drink waits while you finish. The talk pauses while you tend. The idle minute breaks in half around the lighting. Each tax feels small alone, and together they take the edge from the very evenings the cigarette claims to complete. A pleasure that taxes every other pleasure to pay for its seconds is not the crown of the evening. It is the toll upon it.

SUMMARY

- The heaven you remember lives at the lighting and lasts seconds, not through the cigarette.
- The rest of the cigarette turns harsh and stale while you finish it from habit rather than taste.
- A meal and a drink and an idle minute please you from beginning to end without needing prior hunger for smoke.
- The cigarette borrows its pleasure from the food and the drink and the pause and then takes the credit.
- The little rewards for everything finished follow the clock, not the work, and honour nothing they follow.
- Focus feels sharper only because the tug quieting removes an interruption the last dose installed.
- The idle minute is already complete and the dose only divides it into want and brief quiet.
- The flash was relief of the tug mistaken for a gift, and the price is carrying the timetable all day.
