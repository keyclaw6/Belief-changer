Chapter 3

WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY DO FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen table late at night, the two columns in your head, your reasons in your own words.

*Forget what it costs you for a moment and tell me what it ever gave you.*

The Accountant At The Table

Sit with me late at night at the kitchen table after everyone else has gone to bed. The ashtray is there. The pack is there. The lighter is there. Your chest is tight from the day. Your mouth is dry. You are doing the sums that every smoker does when the house is quiet.

On one side you put the price. You know the price without me listing it. The cough in the morning. The stairs that leave you short of breath. The money handed over at the till day after day. The smell on your coat and your hair and your breath. The time outside in the cold while life goes on inside without you. The small wash at the sink before you let anyone close. We have all paid that price and we have all known we were paying it.

On the other side you put the reasons to keep smoking. You tell yourself that the price must be buying something. No capable person pays every day for nothing. So you build the other column to balance the first. You say it relaxes you. You say it helps you cope. You say you enjoy it. You say it rewards you. You say it helps you belong. You say it helps you think and fills the empty minutes. The columns look even in the lamplight and you close the pack and tell yourself the benefits outweigh the harm.

I understand that balance sheet completely. I sat at that same table and did those same sums for years. I do not blame you for doing them. Every smoker does them because the trap teaches us to do them. We were taught that smoking is a bargain where harm is weighed against pleasure and a sensible person decides whether the pleasure is worth the price.

The fact is that the bargain itself is part of the trap.

While we argue about whether the harm beats the pleasure, we have already granted the cigarette something it has never earned. We have granted that there is a pleasure to weigh. We have placed a gift on the table that was never proved to be there. We argue about the size of the price and the size of the gift as if both sides were solid. The price is solid. You pay it in breath and money and time every day. The gift side is smoke. We filled it ourselves with words we were taught to say, and then we weighed our own words against our own breath and called the weighing honest.

Ask yourself who taught you to weigh it that way. Did you sit down before your first cigarette and list the benefits and decide the pleasure was worth the risk. You did not. We started because others smoked and it looked grown and sure and part of belonging. We coughed through the first ones and told ourselves we would learn to like it. We learned to say the words after we were already smoking on a timetable. The reasons arrived after the habit, not before it. They were fitted around the hourly reach the way excuses are fitted around a schedule that must be kept.

You hold this book because part of you already senses the columns are false. A man who owns a true pleasure does not need a book to stop enjoying it. He enjoys it and gets on with his life. You are doing sums late at night because the sums will not settle. The price keeps rising and the gift keeps thinning and you keep adding words to the gift side to keep the balance. That late night unease is not weakness. It is clear sight trying to get through. Something in you knows you are weighing smoke against breath.

So let us clear the table together. Push the price column aside for now. I am not asking you to stare at what smoking does to your body until you feel frightened into stopping. Fear never showed anyone that leaving costs nothing. The method takes care of it. I am asking you to look only at the other column. Empty it completely. Then make the cigarette earn its place back one true gift at a time.

If there is a real gift there, we will see it plainly and we will name it honestly. If there is none, the whole bargain falls without a single argument about harm. A bargain with nothing on one side is not a bargain at all.

Your Reasons In Your Own Words

Let me read your reasons back to you exactly as we say them to ourselves, so you hear how familiar they sound in your own voice.

"It relaxes me."

"It calms me down when anything stressful happens."

"I genuinely enjoy it."

"The moment I light up I am in heaven."

"It is my reward after I finish something."

"Cigarettes were my reward for almost everything."

"It goes with coffee and it finishes a meal."

"It helps me concentrate."

"It gives me something to do when I am bored."

"It helps me belong when I am out with friends."

"One will not hurt when I have been drinking."

Listen to that list the way you would listen to a friend reading his reasons for staying with someone who charges him every day. You know every line because you have spoken every line. I have spoken them too. We all speak them because the same voice taught them to all of us. The voice that turns a small hourly tug into a reason has a large vocabulary but only one job. It keeps the timetable running by giving each reach a respectable name.

Notice how complete the menu sounds. There is something for stress and something for pleasure and something for work finished and something for work waiting and something for people and something for being alone and something for thinking and something for not thinking. The cigarette claims to be a relaxant and a stimulant and a reward and a companion and a pause and a bridge between people. It claims to help you start the morning and close the evening and survive the hard hour and enjoy the easy hour. It claims opposite gifts at opposite moments without blushing.

A true gift does not behave that way. Food does not claim to both wake you and send you to sleep. Water does not claim to both excite you and calm you. A friend does not claim to be the reason you enjoy both noise and quiet and work and rest and company and solitude. When one small paper tube claims to improve every opposite state, we should stop and look at the claim itself. How does the same dose fix stress and boredom and focus and rest and hunger for company and hunger for quiet. What kind of gift fits every lock. A key that opens every door is usually a story we tell about the doors.

I want you to hold that thought without me pushing you further. I am not asking you to throw away a single reason yet. I am asking you to see the full menu laid out in your own words on the table between us. Every line you have ever used to defend the timetable is now where we can both see it. None of them has been mocked. None of them has been twisted. You said them and I heard them because I said them too when I smoked.

We lived inside those sentences for years. We said we smoked because we decided to in each separate moment for a separate reason. We said the coffee called for it and the meal called for it and the nerves called for it and the waiting called for it and the break called for it and the quiet called for it. The reasons changed every hour so the schedule could stay the same. Now the schedule has a name between us. The nicotine trap. The small tug has a name. The Nipper. The loud talk has a name. The Con. The names matter because they let us ask a cleaner question than the accountant asked.

The accountant asked whether the harm beats the pleasure. The free man asks whether there was ever a pleasure at all.

Why The Scales Cannot Settle It

You may feel loyal to the list. You may say to me that whatever I claim about timetables and traps, you know what you feel. You feel calmer after one when life shakes. You feel a flash of heaven at the light. You feel the reward land after work done. You feel the circle warmer with smoke in it. You tell me the benefits outweigh the harm because you have felt the benefits and I have only counted the harm.

I believe you about what you felt. I felt it too. The shoulders drop after the puff. The hands stop buzzing. The mind settles enough to go on. The first touch of smoke after an hour without one feels like heaven arriving through the lips. Afterwards you feel low about yourself and you hate being a smoker while you still love to smoke. Both halves are true and I will never mock either half. You are not lying about the heaven and you are not weak about the low. You are describing exactly what we all described when we smoked.

The question is not whether you felt it. The question is what you credited it to.

We were taught to credit every good minute that contained a cigarette to the cigarette itself. The meal ended and we lit up and felt full and settled, and we credited the smoke with closing the meal. The coffee arrived and we smoked between sips and felt warm and alert, and we credited the smoke with lifting the morning. The work finished and we stepped out and lit up and felt the glow of done, and we credited the smoke with rewarding the work. The friends laughed and the glasses clinked and the smoke curled over the circle, and we credited the smoke with warming the night. We credited the moment to the dose because the dose was in the moment.

But the moment was already there before the dose arrived. The food was already rich. The coffee was already warm and bitter and your own. The work was already done by your hands and your patience. The friends were already funny and the night air was already soft and the pause was already yours. The cigarette arrived inside pleasures it did not make and then claimed it made them. It rode along and took the credit the way a passenger takes credit for the car.

Consider how easily a claimed gift can be tested. If the cigarette truly relaxed you, you would grow calmer as you smoked more. Heavy smokers would be the calmest people alive. Look around at the heaviest smokers you know. Are they calm. Do they sit loose and easy through the day. Or are they the most restless people in the room, watching the clock and stepping out. The gift as advertised does not match the state of the people who use the most of it.

If the cigarette truly rewarded you, the reward would sit on top of the work and leave the work shining. The reward arrives with its own bill attached. You finish a paragraph and you pay with breath. You finish a drive to the shops and you pay with money and smell. You teach the class and you pay with time outside away from the people you taught. A true reward does not send you out into the rain to collect it. A true reward does not make you wash it off before you meet people you love. A true reward does not leave you saying you love to smoke and hate being a smoker in the same breath. That split sentence is the most honest sentence a smoker speaks. The heaven is named at the light. The heaviness is named after. Both are felt. Only one is credited correctly.

So which minute proves the gift. The seconds at the light when the tug quiets, or the rest of the day when the tug returns and the breath shortens and the money goes and the smell stays. If one flash at the light must be weighed against the hourly return of the want, is the weighing honest. If one lit second must excuse twenty smokes you did not even want and left burning while you did something else, is that a bargain or a story told to cover a timetable.

I am not asking you to answer with your fears about illness. Put illness aside completely while we talk. I do not want you to stop because you are frightened of what smoke does inside the chest. Frightened people still believe they are losing a friend. I want you to stop because you see there was never a friend there. What did it ever do for you that the morning and the meal and the work and the friends did not already do.

Ask it plainly against your own yesterday. When the phone rang badly and you reached, did the smoke move the bad news. When you waited for the bus and you lit up, did the smoke shorten the wait. When the evening was quiet and you smoked because the quiet called for it, did the smoke deepen the quiet. You know the honest answers because you lived those minutes. The smoke was present. The smoke was not the help.

We all believed the help was real because the timing hides the cause. The puff and the quiet arrive together, so we believe the puff fixed the day. The puff fixed the tug. The day waited exactly where we left it, with less breath and less time to meet it. Once you see that order, the scales look different. They are not balanced. They were never loaded. One side held breath and money and time. The other side held words.

What Would Count As A Gift

Let us be fair to the cigarette for once in a way smokers are rarely fair. Let us give it every chance to prove itself.

Forget harm entirely. Forget cough and stairs and money and smell. Forget what anyone told you about disease. Suppose a small paper tube told you it would take nothing from you and leave no mark at all. Suppose it were free and clean and invisible. Would you still want it if it did nothing for you. Of course you would not. Nobody collects an empty ritual for its own sake. Nobody pays attention to a habit that gives no pleasure and no relief and no help. The only reason the harm debate feels urgent is that we believe there is something on the other side worth debating.

So name what that something would have to be to count.

It would have to be a true gift, not a pause in a want the gift itself created. It would have to lift you above the calm of a man who never smoked, not merely lift you briefly out of the hollow the last dose dug. It would have to belong to the smoke itself, not to the coffee beside it or the meal before it or the friends around it or the work you finished before you lit up. It would have to last longer than the lighting of it. It would have to leave you better than it found you an hour later, not restless for another. It would have to be something you could not get cleaner and fuller without it.

Hold every line on your menu against that simple test. Not against fear. Against fairness.

Does it relax you in a way that leaves you calmer an hour later, or does it calm the tug for minutes and return you more restless. Does it please you through the whole smoke, or does the heaven live only at the light while the rest tastes harsh and the aftertaste brings shame. Does it reward the teaching and the story and the paragraph and the long drive and the short drive to the shops, or does it attach itself to anything finished and claim the finished glow as its own work. Does it warm the circle of friends, or does it send you out of the circle to feed apart and then make you wash before you return. Does it help you think, or does it interrupt thought every hour and then briefly quiet the interruption so you can think again. Does it fill the bored minute, or does it teach you to call every open minute empty unless it holds smoke.

These are not accusations. They are the questions any fair mind asks before it calls something a gift. A gift that must be excused every hour is not a gift. A pleasure that lasts only at the light is not a day of pleasure. A relief that guarantees its own return is not relief. A companion that sends you outside alone is not company. A reward that taxes every finished act is not a reward. The words are warm. The test is cold. Put the warm words beside the cold test and watch which survives.

I know this is hard to accept while the tug still talks. The voice that guards the timetable will shout that I am twisting your pleasure into nothing. Let it shout. Then answer it with your own evidence, not mine. Think of the cigarettes you did not even want yesterday. The one you left burning while you talked. The one you smoked in the rain while hating the rain. The one you smoked while ill in bed and hated the taste and smoked it anyway. Would a real pleasure behave that way. Would you leave a true gift burning unwatched. Would you enjoy a true gift while hating every puff of it. Would you call anything else in your life a pleasure that you use when you are too sick to taste it.

Would you call anything else a crutch that makes the limp worse. You would not. You call the cigarette those kind words only because the tug arrives on schedule and the talk arrives with it and the pair feel like a need.

Once you demand a true test, the menu changes shape. It stops looking like seven different gifts and starts looking like one claim said seven ways. Every line promises the same thing in different clothes. It promises that the dose fixes a state that life gave you.

There is only one honest name for what the cigarette claims to be. A real pleasure, relief, or crutch.

Say it slowly to yourself, not as my phrase but as the shape of your own demand. A real pleasure, relief, or crutch. If it is real, it will survive the test. It will prove itself above the calm of the man who never smoked. It will prove itself apart from coffee and food and friends and finished work. It will prove itself longer than the light. If it cannot survive that fair test, it was never any of those three. It was words around a tug. And words around a tug cannot outweigh breath, because breath is real and the words were rented.

To You Or For You

Now we can say the whole switch in one portable sentence you can carry into every old scene without carrying this chapter with you.

Every smoker knows two directions. Things done to you and things done for you. Food done for you leaves you fed. Rest done for you leaves you rested. A friend acting for you leaves you stronger. A blow done to you leaves a mark. A tax done to you leaves you lighter. A master acting on you leaves you smaller. The preposition tells the truth even when the mind is tired. To you means upon you. For you means on your side.

Smoking has lived by blurring that small difference. It acts upon you in every visible way. It dries your mouth. It tightens your chest. It dulls your smell and your taste. It shortens your breath on the stairs. It empties your pocket at the till. It stains your fingers and your coat and your breath. It sends you out of the warmth to feed apart. It rings its clock in meetings and films and meals and drives and wakes you hungry for it before your feet find the floor. All of that is done to you. You feel it daily. You pay it daily. No smoker doubts that side of the sentence.

The other direction is claimed but never shown. What is done for you. Where is the morning made clearer by smoke than the clear morning of the man who never needed a dose to start. Where is the meal closed cleaner by smoke than the meal closed clean with taste whole. Where is the drive made safer or the thought made sharper or the circle made kinder by the paper tube. You can name the tug quieted for minutes. You cannot name one hour left better than it was found. You cannot name one task done better because smoke touched it. You cannot name one friendship deepened by the need to step away from it. The for you column stays empty no matter how long you stare at it.

That is why the accountant could never settle the books. He was comparing marks upon you with gifts for you that never arrived. He weighed breath paid against heaven claimed at the light and called it close. Look again with the preposition held steady and the weighing ends. One side is crowded with daily evidence. The other side holds a flash at the light and a story about the flash.

Here is the sentence to keep where the menu used to argue. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

Speak it plainly when the old talk returns. When the coffee calls, answer that the coffee was always yours and the smoke only rode it. When the meal closes, answer that the food closed it and the smoke only stood beside the closing. When the finished paragraph glows, answer that your hands finished it and the smoke only taxed the glow. When the circle laughs, answer that people warmed it and the night air softened it and the pause rested you and the smoke only charged admission to what was already yours. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you. The sentence fits every room because every room already held its own pleasure before the dose arrived.

Do you see how this changes the fear of missing out. You are not facing a life with seven holes where seven gifts used to be. The clear morning does not lose its light when the cough goes. The meal does not lose its close when the aftertaste goes. Money staying home does not spend itself. Breath taken easy on the stairs does not miss the weight.

I speak to you now with the full weight of one who escaped the same sums. I believed for years that I faced a choice between health and pleasure. I was wrong about the choice, not weak in making it. We were all conned into the same weighing by the same talk.

Ask yourself three plain questions and let your own yesterday answer them honestly.

If the cigarette truly worked for you, why did the heaviest smokers look the most restless and chained to the pack. Why did more doses bring more want rather than more calm.

If each reason on your menu named a separate gift, why did every reason arrive on the same hourly ring. Why did stress and boredom and hunger for company all knock at the same fixed interval like one master wearing different masks.

If the gift were real, why did you need this chapter to defend it to yourself. Why does a true pleasure never need a late night accountant while this one cannot sleep without him.

There is only one answer that fits all three without calling you foolish. The gift was never there. The marks were always there. You were never foolish to misread one for the other. You were taught to credit the tug quieted as life helped. Once the credit moves back to where it belongs, the verdict lands by itself and it lands short.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

5. ASK WHAT GOOD IT DOES AT ALL

Refuse the harm debate and demand one real gift.

You came to this chapter weighing harm against pleasure like an accountant who fears the books will not balance. Leave it with the table cleared and the menu where you can see it. The price side needs no argument from me. The gift side must now prove itself or fall. When the talk offers stress or heaven or reward or belonging or focus or something for the empty minute, hold it to the single test we set together. Name the true gift apart from the moment that already held it. If it cannot stand apart and above and longer than the light, it is not a gift. It is the timetable talking. And a timetable has no claim on a free man.

SUMMARY

- The harm debate grants the cigarette a gift it never proved.
- Your reasons sound different but promise one thing in different words.
- The good minutes belonged to food and coffee and work and friends before smoke arrived.
- The heaven named at the light is brief while the want returns on schedule.
- The reward attaches to anything finished but the finished work did the shining.
- A true gift would leave you better an hour later and apart from the moment.
- The only fair question is what good exists at all.
