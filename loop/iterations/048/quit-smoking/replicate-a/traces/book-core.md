## 1. Book core

- Target behavior: compulsive cigarette smoking ended by total stop. Last cigarette, then none. Not cutting down, not vaping as a destination, not NRT as the method.
- Reader (planner-facing state): an adult daily smoker who lights up through stress, meals, coffee, drinks, breaks, and waiting; has failed before by resolve, patches, gum, or abrupt suffering; believes cigarettes calm, help, reward, and please; dreads life without them as empty and impossible.
- Load-bearing false belief (frozen): Cigarettes relax me, help me cope, and are my pleasure, and I would be deprived without them.
- Through-line: stopping is escape from a con, not sacrifice of a gift. Every chapter makes continuing feel like the ongoing burden and stopping feel like reclamation.
- Format: full-length belief-change book, fifteen chapters, about 60,000 words. One clear adult edition.
- Fork decisions:
  - Fork 1 (inner state): full Carr personification. The trivial physical tug is named the Nipper; the belief-system that feeds it is named the Con. The Nipper starves on its own once the Con dies. Never a mighty enemy demanding resolve.
  - Fork 2 (outcome): total cessation, commanded with warm certainty. Moderation foreclosed by the pincer and the cliff image. No autonomy hedge on the destination.
  - Fork 3 (science weight): hard facts stated plainly at full force where the truth frightens, then the reader is told not to change from fear but from escape-joy. Facts arrive as settled statements, never as literature review. Raw source detail stays out of the argument.
  - Fork 4 (villain): two villains, both named hard: the nicotine industry that designed the dose and recruited the reader, and the Willpower Method that sold impossibility and kept every failure personal.
  - Fork 5 (void): change nothing else in life. The non-smoker baseline returns on its own. No replacement ritual as the method, no environment-redesign programme.
- Redefinition: not needed. Smoking is quitable wholesale; no Good-X/Bad-X line, no CAPS subset name, no margin-for-error doctrine.
- Clinical perimeter: plan-wide advisory CA-A (defined in section 5). No medical advice, no instruction to ignore a doctor, crisis and illness pointers only. The last-cigarette ritual is ceremonial, not a dare to smoke more.
- Strongest pro-smoking scene: the night-out smoking area with drinks among friends, the circle that feels like belonging itself.
- Destination state: the reader thinks of a cigarette and feels relief and gladness at being free, never deprivation; the thought converts at once into celebration.
- Ending reframe (saved for the final movement, used nowhere earlier): you bury an enemy, not a friend — grief would be the only mistake left, and there is nothing to grieve.
