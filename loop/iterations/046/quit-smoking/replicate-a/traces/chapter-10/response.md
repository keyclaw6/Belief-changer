Chapter 10
THE NIGHT OUT THAT PROVES IT

You know the glass cold in your palm and the room warm round your shoulders. You feel the laugh travel the length of the table and take you with it, the story landing and the whole circle breaking open at once. You know the friend leaning near to be heard over the music, the name called across chairs, the chair saved with a coat while you stood a moment at the bar. You believe one picture waits outside all this warmth to complete it, that the evening holds together only when lit at its edge. Such holding proves nothing but long pairing, and this chapter tests the pairing till only the evening holds.

You know the tug that guards this one picture closer than all others, thin and reasonable and almost tender. You feel it speak not as hunger but as loyalty, calling the lit end company, courage, belonging. You believe loyalty proves a gift must live there, that no trap could sound so much like friendship. Loyalty here proves long tenancy only, and tenancy ends where seeing begins.

We all kept this one picture longest and called the keeping proof of pleasure. We all let lesser excuses fall and held this last one up to the light, saying the quiet morning needs nothing but the loud night needs its smoke. You believe last-kept proves strongest. Last-kept here proves most-paired, never most-true, and most-paired falls the same way the rest fell when each drop is returned to its owner.

I stood where you stand now with a drink in one hand and a dose in the other, and I tell you I guarded that stance the way you guard it. You know my coat because it wears your shape. I believed my nights would lie flat without the lit end the way a song lies flat without its chorus. You feel the same fear when you picture tomorrow's table with empty hands. Test the fear against the night itself, because the night remembers what the voice forgets.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a glass sweating in the hand, a laugh that carries the table, a hand held out with its small white offer, night air taken in twice, and a night I still count.

THE ONE YOU KEPT

Keep the picture you kept, because here the exception asks to be examined openly.

You know the evening you mean without my naming it. You feel the week behind you laid down at last, the work shuttered, the wages earned, the shoulders dropping as the door of the bar swings to. You know the first sip cold down the throat, the second slower, the talk loosening the way talk loosens when no clock watches. You feel the circle widen to take you in, the joke finished for your arrival, the welcome warm as bread. You believe such welcome needs its lit edge to stay bright. Watch the welcome and it stays bright by people, not by lighting.

We all named this evening the proof that something real must live in the tube. We all said the words in our own accent, "drink and friends and smoke belong together, take the smoke away and the picture thins." You feel sense in the sentence, a witness speaking for years of Fridays. You know the sense borrows your mouth. Answer it with the Fridays themselves, and ask what the Fridays ask.

You know the mornings and the meals and the work pauses already examined and found complete without signing. You feel each hour already returned to its owners, the kettle doing the waking, the plate doing the closing, the pause doing the resting. You believe the night differs in kind, that company and drink change the arithmetic. Company and drink change the lighting, never the arithmetic, and lighting flatters what it never made.

You know the seller behind the smoke already seen counting doses while you counted hours. You feel the want change address from inside to outside, from nature to manufacture. You believe such seeing must stop at the doorway of the bar, that manufacture cannot reach so warm a room. Manufacture reaches every room where hands were drilled to move before thought, and drilled hands move in warm rooms quickest because warmth lowers the watch.

Hear the voice that guards this last picture, because it speaks closest to love. You hear it even and fond, "I love smoking but I hate being a smoker, and never more than on nights like this when the lighting feels like heaven." You feel the fondness carry birthdays and weddings and late talk. You know the fondness borrows your mouth. Answer it with the loving itself, and ask when love learned its object.

If love lives in the tube, why does love turn within minutes to checking breath against a palm, to spray and mints on the train home, to the shirt hung by the window rather than over the chair.

If heaven lives in the lighting, why does heaven thin before the tube burns halfway, with the taste souring and the head dulling while the laugh round the table grows brighter without any lighting at all.

You know the only honest answers return the night to its makers. Love never lived in the tube, because love arrived with the faces round the table and stayed with them after the ends were stubbed. Heaven never lived in the pull, because the pull dulled what the drink and the talk and the music were brightening. Something else wrote the evening besides lighting, something wide in companionship and leisure asking for nothing but presence and something old in belief calling the presence proof of the dose.

WHAT THE GLASS AND THE LAUGH DID

Stand inside the pleasure itself, because every drop claimed must be weighed where claimed.

You know the first drink doing its own work without any witness but your own blood. You feel the shoulders drop a fraction with the cold going down, the jaw unclench, the laughter come easier from lower in the chest. You know the second drink loosening the tongue further, the story told longer, the arm thrown round shoulders without calculation. You feel the day's armour laid down by degrees, the way armour is laid down wherever drink and rest meet. You believe such loosening needs a lit partner to complete it. Loosening completes itself in company, and the partner only steps into the loosened hour to sign it.

You know the laugh doing its own work the way laughter always works. You feel the belly shake, the eyes water, the breath come short and then deep, the whole frame rinsed the way a yard is rinsed by rain. You know the tale that set the table roaring, told with hands and pauses, the punch held back a beat and then delivered. You feel the circle bind tighter with each shared roar, the stranger at the end made known by laughing, the old slight dissolved without a word spoken about it. You believe binding needs smoke to seal it. Binding seals itself in shared breath and shared timing, and no lit end ever timed a punchline.

You know the music doing its own work under the talk, low and steady, the beat finding the feet under the table. You feel the shoulders sway a fraction without permission, the head nod, the two pulled to the edge of the floor by a song that knows their years. You know the floor taking them while the table claps time, the turn and the laugh and the return breathless to saved chairs. You feel the blood up and the cheeks warm, the aliveness that needs no translation. Nothing in that aliveness wore a lit end, and nothing missed one.

We all credited that aliveness to the dose and called the credit company. We all felt the chest widen where the chest was bound since the last feed ended and read the widening as the tube giving. You believe widening proves kindness. Widening proves a grip slackened between tightenings by the same arms that tightened it, while night and drink and friends did the true widening round it.

You know the pause doing its own work between rounds, the step to cooler air, the cheek met by night, the head clearing a degree. You feel the talk drop lower there, heads bent close, the confidence traded in quiet voices. You believe low talk proves the lit end deepens. Low talk deepens by closeness and by contrast with the roar inside, the way all confidences deepen wherever two step apart to speak plain. The lit end never bent a head closer; closeness bent the heads, and the ends burned unnoticed between fingers while it happened.

It never did anything FOR you; it only did things TO you.

Feel how that sentence fits the glass and the laugh once tested. You feared such lines must be wordplay, too neat to hold against real love for those nights. Test the fear against drink and music and welcome and pause. Name one true gift the tube added to any of them that people and leisure and tune had not already given. You find none, because none stands apart from the dip it relieved. No gift means no loss in leaving. No loss means leaving reads as walking clear of a chain, not walking away from friends.

THE HEAVEN MINUTE

Look now at the minute you call heaven, because heaven deserves close watching.

You know the lighting itself, the flare and the first pull hot to the back of the throat. You feel the shoulders drop a fraction, the mind blur kindly at its edges, the rapid sense of being taken care of, as if somebody capable had stepped between you and the hour. You know the words that rise with that sense, spoken in your own accent, that the moment of lighting lands in heaven while the hours after feel leaden. You feel truth in the timing, a witness speaking for years of lit ends. You know the truth borrows your mouth. Answer it with the minutes themselves, and ask what the minutes ask.

You know that heaven arriving always on the same short meter, within pulls of the last feed fading. You feel the hollow note rise under the ribs through the evening, thin and nagging between rounds, sharpened by drink and by watching others step out. You know the lighting answering the hollow within seconds, the hollow quieting as the drug renews its level. You feel the quiet read as arrival. Quiet here reads as replenishment, the rapid return toward the level you held before you ever lit, never a rise above the calm of the non-smoker who sat through the same hour with no hollow at all.

If heaven lives in the first pulls, why does heaven never last the tube, and why does the second half always taste of ash and stale end with the tongue coated and the head duller than before the lighting.

If the tube crowns the drink, why does the drink taste brightest before the lighting and dullest after, with the palate filmed and the next sip taken to wash the mouth rather than to meet it.

If pleasure drives the lighting, why does lighting continue past enjoyment into duty through the night, dose after dose with duller taste and quicker hand, till the packet empties faster on bright nights than on dull days.

You know the only honest answers return heaven to its short meter. The fact is the lighting ends a discomfort the last dose created, within seconds of renewal, and the ending reads as gift only while the creation stays unseen. The drink did the loosening. The company did the binding. The music did the lifting. The dose did the signing, and the signing faded before the ash fell.

We all said we loved the smoke on those nights and hated the smoker on the mornings after. We all woke with the mouth stale and the clothes holding the smell through washes, the purse lighter by the night's tally, the head thick where the laugh had been clear. You believe morning-after proves the night was worth its price. Morning-after proves the price was paid for a signing that never did the work, the way a toll proves a road is owned, not that the toll built the road.

See the minute plainly at last. You were taught heaven arrives lit. Heaven arrives in the pause and the sip and the roar, and the tube borrows heaven's name for the minute its own hollow quiets. Borrowed names return to their owners once seen, and seeing is done each time the minute is watched to its end.

THE HAND THAT OFFERS

Meet now the hand held out, because here the social rule shows its shape.

You know the moment after the second round, the talk bright, the blood warm, the circle loose and kind. You feel the packet appear as if from nowhere, tapped and offered with easy ceremony, the voice alongside it light and fond, "one won't hurt, go on." You know the eyes on you kindly, the fear of standing apart while they stand together, the thought arriving that refusal breaks the circle. You feel the hollow underneath sharpen at the sight, the tiny physical craving stirring where drink and watching have fed it. You believe refusal risks exile. Refusal risks nothing but the hollow's brief complaint, while acceptance re-arms the whole persuasion that brought you to this book.

You know the sentence that follows in your own head, borrowed whole from years of such offers. You hear it quick and reasonable, "you often get offered and in that kind of setting it is hard to turn down, you do not want to be the only one left out and with drink in you one feels harmless." You feel the reasonableness carry laughter and lights and late talk. You know the reasonableness borrows your mouth. Answer it with the setting itself, and ask what the setting asks.

If one feels harmless, why does one always mean the rule rebuilt whole, with the second following before the night ends and the morning packet bought again by noon.

If the offer proves belonging, why does belonging stay complete for the non-smoker at the same table through the same rounds, laughing the same laughs with nothing lit between the fingers, welcomed and saving chairs and calling names across the room the same as you.

If drink excuses the one, why does drink excuse nothing else you would never do sober, and why does the excused one taste of ash halfway while the drink still tastes of drink.

You know the only honest answers return the offer to its speaker. The hand offers from its own persuasion, defending its own picture, not reading your heart. The voice that calls one harmless speaks from the brainwashing voice that misreads the hollow as proof the dose gives company. Company never came from the tube, because the table gave company before the packet appeared and keeps giving after it is pocketed.

It's the other way around.

Feel how that sentence turns the hand round. You feared such turning must judge your friends to free yourself. Nothing judges there. Something clears, the way water clears when the stirring stops. The friend stays a friend, kind and lit and caught, to be watched with level eyes and kept without following. The offer stays a caught voice speaking, to be heard without obeying. The night stays a night, to be kept whole without signing.

You know that cold step outside the warm talk where the circle thins to lit ends. You feel no need to picture it twice, because its meaning is settled and its credit returned. You know the talk inside carries on without the stepped-out, the laugh breaking and reforming, the song pulling feet while the stepped-out watch smoke rise. You feel the division it always carried, exile purchased as belonging. Belonging never lived on that step, because the step divides the circle it claims to close.

A NIGHT I STILL COUNT

Hear now a night of mine counted plainly, because counted nights carry what argument alone cannot.

I tell you I smoked eleven years, a packet most days and more on Fridays, and I tell you Fridays owned me hardest. You know the pattern because it wears your shape. I worked the counter in those years, wages counted, rent due, the week stacked high by Thursday. You feel the Friday loosen in the limbs before the door swings. I felt it the same, the coat thrown on, the coins warm, the street to the bar walked fast with the packet tapped twice in the pocket to hear the tally.

You know the bar I mean, low ceiling, glasses chiming, the table grown wide by nine o'clock. You feel the welcome when the tale is held for your arrival. I took the saved chair with the first drink cold in my hand, and I tell you the first hour flew without any lighting at all because the talk flew. You know such hours, the story told with hands, the whole table roaring. You feel the cheeks ache with laughing. Mine ached the same, and no hollow sounded loud enough to break the ache while the roar lasted.

You know the turn where the hollow found its voice. You feel the second drink going down slower, the talk pausing for orders, the two beside me rising with packet and lighter. I rose with them from habit more than hunger, the way feet rise when others rise. You know the air meeting the cheek, the head clearing a degree, the low talk dropping close. I smoked three in that hour in a loose ring of five, laughing less than the table inside laughed, while the song I loved played to an empty chair of mine inside.

You know the count climbing without decision through the night. You feel the hand finding the packet by address between rounds. I lit nine before closing, and I tell you I remember two of them and only as interruptions, the search for the lighter, the wind killing the flare, the ash on the cuff. You know the train home after, the spray and the mints, the breath checked against a palm, the shirt hung by the window in the cold. You feel the small division open between you and the sleeping house. I felt it the same, creeping past the bedroom door with the smell in my hair.

You know the morning after that levy counted coldly. You feel the mouth stale, the throat rough, the purse light, the head thick where the laugh had been clear. I woke with the table of the night before still roaring faintly in memory, and I tell you the roaring I remembered held no smoke in it at all when I ran it back. You know the run-back I mean, scene by scene. The tale roaring needed no lit end. The song pulling feet needed no lit end. The welcome and the saved chair needed no lit end. The low talk in the cool air needed closeness only, and closeness lived in the bent heads, never in the burning between fingers.

I tell you I ran that night back many times after, and each running returned more to its owners. You know the drink loosening the shoulders. You feel the laugh rinsing the frame. You know the music lifting the blood. You feel the pause clearing the head. I felt all four do their work that Friday with my own body as witness, and I felt the nine signings do nothing but quiet their own hollow between tightenings. You believe such counting proves nothing but one man's Friday. Counting here proves the arithmetic any Friday shows when watched to its end, because arithmetic repeats where excuses vary.

We lived that Friday together, you and I, though years divide us. You know the saved chair and the cold glass and the tale told with hands. You feel the ache in the cheeks that no tube timed. You know the hollow rising between rounds with its thin complaint. You feel the offer lighting it briefly and the hollow returning louder for the lighting. I tell you the night never once asked for smoke to enter by, though the drilled hand moved before thought to answer a call never made to it. The night asked for presence only, and presence was already standing in the circle before the flare.

TASTING THE AIR TWICE

Try now the plain test the night itself offers, because tested air teaches faster than told air.

You know the doorway between warm and cool already known, the talk bright behind, the night plain ahead. You feel the head warm with drink and talk, the cheeks flushed, the breath short from laughing. You know the step across with nothing lit, taken slowly on purpose, the night air arriving unfiltered on the face. You feel the cool enter the nose clean, the chest widen to meet it, the head clear a degree the way heads clear wherever cool meets warm. You believe such clearing must be thin beside the lit clearing you remember. Watch the clearing and it holds without thinning, because no hollow is being fed and none is being starved, only lungs doing their old office faithfully.

You know the same step taken an hour later with a lit end, watched the way you never watch it. You feel the first pull hot against the cool, the throat bite, the shoulders drop their fraction while the mind blurs kindly. You know the air arriving now laid over with smoke, the nose filled with burning, the chest taking less of the night and more of the tube. You feel the difference once named. One tasting takes the night. The other tasting takes the dose laid over the night, and calls the laying-over deepening.

If the lit tasting deepens the night, why does the night taste brighter in memory from the unlit tasting, with the cool and the quiet and the distant music standing clear, and why does the lit tasting remember chiefly wind and flare and ash and the search for somewhere to stub.

If the dose opens the pause, why does the unlit pause return you to the table brighter, with the head rinsed and the talk rejoined mid-thread, and why does the lit pause return you coughing a fraction, eyes watering, fingers smelling, needing mints before the next tale.

You know the only honest answers return the air to the lungs. The night never needed signing to be taken. Taking lived in the nose and the chest and the skin meeting cool, the same servants that carry every creature that never lit. Something else wrote the lit depth besides air, something brief in the drug quieting its own call and something old in belief calling the quiet proof of company.

We all believed such tests would deprive us of the best of the night. We all feared the unlit step would stand outside belonging, watching lit ends move in the dark like signals from a warmer tribe. You believe signals prove a tribe. Signals here prove feeders timing their feeds together, standing apart from the very warmth they praise to feed a hollow together. Belonging stays inside where the talk binds and the laugh rinses and the song lifts, and the step returns to belonging whenever feet turn back, with nothing owed for the turning.

I tell you the air tastes of nothing but itself when taken clean, and itself is plenty. You know the rain on stone and the river-smell after dry days. You feel the lungs report faithfully when no film lays over them, the way an ear reports faithfully in a quiet room. You know the table waiting behind the door with its roar unbroken, the chair saved, the name called. You feel the return as rejoining, not as missing.

10. MEET THE BEST CIGARETTE HEAD-ON
Let the favourite prove it gives nothing.

You know the favourite you guarded when all else fell, the one with drink and friends and laugh to plead for it. You feel it stand now without its pleaders, the glass doing the loosening, the laugh doing the binding, the music doing the lifting, the pause doing the clearing. You know the tube stepping into each completed thing to sign it after. You feel the pattern show once the maker shows. The hours did the living. The dose did the signing.

We all kept one lit picture to argue the trap might love us a little. We all watched that picture tested drop by drop till no drop remained with the tube. You believe such testing must leave life thinner. Testing here leaves life thicker, because returned credit thickens its owners. The night thickens. The drink keeps its loosening. The company keeps its binding. The hollow alone thins, the tiny physical craving already starving, misread so long by the brainwashing voice as proof of pleasure.

It never did anything FOR you; it only did things TO you.

It's the other way around.

You know the no-exception now standing where the exception stood, the patio held as scene only and the night held as life. You feel belonging keep its circle unbroken, no exile to doorways, no spray and mints afterward, no small lying about where the smell went. You believe such keeping must be held by effort. Such keeping is held by seeing, and the seeing is done each time the offer is heard without obeying and the air is taken without a filter.

SUMMARY
- You believe the night gave its pleasure through drink and company and music rather than through smoke, because the laugh and the glass and the tune stand complete while the dose only signed them.
- You see that the heaven minute was the brief quiet of a renewed feed rather than a gift, because the glow fades before the tube burns halfway and returns louder as want.
- You understand that the offered one rebuilds the whole rule rather than harmlessly keeping belonging, because the table stays welcoming with nothing lit and the hand offers from its own persuasion.
