Chapter 7
THEY CANNOT LEAD

You know the queue at the small shop on the corner. You feel the lino worn thin before the till, the glass shutter rattling down over the rows, the hand-written cards taped round the price lists. You know the coins counted out in the palm, warm from the pocket, the thumb pushing them across the scratched plastic while the throat already moves in expectation. You feel the packet drop heavy and square into the hand, cellophane crackling, the first seam split with the nail outside the door. You believe this small purchase proves a small freedom, that choosing the brand and paying the coins means a grown person directing a small pleasure.

You know the doorway beside the shop where the lads gather out of the wind. You feel the lighter held out before the question finishes, the flame cupped, the nod that says no words are needed between men who feed the same want. You know the stairwell behind the office with its sour air, the paint blistered where shoulders lean, the smell hanging low over the steps long after the last heel clicks away. You feel the tug when a lit end lifts near your face and the voice says friendly and easy what friends always say. You believe refusing would mark you cold, that taking what is offered keeps the hour kind.

We all stood in that queue and told ourselves the same story about choice. We all took the offered light on the steps and called it manners, called it company, called it nothing. You believe such moments sit outside the trap, that a dose handed by a friend carries a different meaning from a dose bought alone at midnight. Such moments sit at the centre of the trap, not outside it, and this chapter looks at them with clear eyes till they cannot lead you again.

I stood in that queue myself through years of so-called pleasure, and I tell you the coins never once bought what the hand claimed. You know my coat because it wears your shape. I counted the shutter prices like a connoisseur, I praised one brand over another, I took lights from mates on stairwells and told myself the gift made them sweeter. You feel the same pull when a friend holds out a packet now. Stay with what that pull really is, because here the last social rope falls away.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a shop queue behind the shutter, two flights of stairs climbed winded, a doorway with a handed cigarette and a cupped lighter, and a patio heard through an open window.

*When another smoker holds out a cigarette, you are not watching enjoyment you might miss; you are watching the same want speaking through a different mouth.*

THE QUEUE BEHIND THE SHUTTER

Stand again in that shop with clear eyes, because the room holds plain truth about who leads whom.

You know the shutter well. You feel the metal cool under the fingertips where the thumb lifts it to read the rows, the names in red and gold, the numbers promising mild and light. You know the money laid down without counting after a while, the hand knowing the price before the mind speaks it, the packet tapped twice against the wrist by habit on the step outside. You feel businesslike with such rite, a customer served, a need met cleanly. You believe businesslike proves control, that a regular purchase managed neatly cannot own the buyer.

You know what the queue teaches when watched closely. You feel the eyes slide to the clock above the till, the feet shift, the jaw tighten when the woman ahead counts change slow. You know the hollow note rising under the ribs, small and empty, asking to be fed, and the brainwashing voice reading the note as proof the packet holds calm. You feel the relief when the till drawer rings, sharp and total, before the first dose is even lit. You believe relief proves the packet helped. Relief proves the wait ended, the way a bell ends a wait, and the packet claims the bell as its own gift.

We all paid that till with pride in our brand knowledge. We all said the words about taste and strength as if choosing wine, "mine are the red ones, the smooth ones," as one of us put it in plain talk. You believe taste explains the loyalty, that leaf and blend decide the hand. Taste never decided anything, because the same hand praised whatever brand the queue supplied when the shelf ran bare, and the throat learned each new name within days.

You know the packet as a day's dose box when seen plainly. You feel the twenty tubes ranked neat, the foil keeping them soft, each one a timed squirt counted out till evening. You know the factory far away counting the same count, stacking boxes for shops, printing mild on blue and strong on red while the contents leave by the same door. You feel clever for reading the numbers. You believe numbers mean measure and care. Numbers mean theatre round the same feed, and the hand covers the small holes and pulls harder for its level without asking leave.

I worked that queue myself till the shopkeeper knew my step, and I tell you the familiarity never once made the purchase mine. You know the pattern. The price rose and the hand paid. The packs warned and the eyes slid past. The coins grew thin by Thursday and the packet still led the shopping list ahead of bread. You feel no pride reading this, only recognition. Take the recognition as a sign. The queue never proved a customer choosing. It proved a feeder being called, and the call came from a small craving in the body with a large voice laid over it.

If the queue proves free choice, why does the same hand buy through coughs and colds and empty purses, and why does the same mouth praise whatever brand the shutter offers when its own favourite runs out.

If the packet gives a genuine pleasure worth its coins, why does the pleasure vanish the moment the tiny physical craving sleeps, and why does a non-smoker stand in the same queue for milk and feel nothing missing in the air.

You know the only honest answers loosen the old story. Choice never stood behind the counter, because the hand moved before the mind weighed anything. Pleasure never sat inside the cellophane, because unwatched packets give no joy till the hollow note asks. Something else wrote the queue scene besides taste, something brief in the body asking for its level and something old in belief calling that asking proof of liking.

See the shape plainly before we climb the stairs. You were taught the queue proved you liked to smoke. The queue proved you were called to feed, dose by dose, on a meter that fades within minutes and must be fed all day. The hand did what starved hands do. The voice told its old story over them. The coins never measured joy. They measured the toll of a con that sells its own relief back as a gift.

TWO FLIGHTS WITH A LIT END

Follow the packet out of the shop and up the stairs, because the body tells truth the voice cannot cover.

You know those stairs well. You feel the handrail cool, the first flight taken fast with the fresh packet knocking the thigh, the second flight taken slower with breath coming short. You know the colleague climbing beside you who never smoked, the breath easy through the nose, the legs moving without drama to the landing. You feel the chest working to catch up, the cough held back as a joke, the laugh offered to cover the pause on the turn. You believe years and age explain the gap, that lungs tire by nature and smoke merely keeps company with the years.

You know the morning reach that starts such days. You feel the hand find the pack before the feet find the floor, the first drag harsh on empty lungs with watering eyes, the kettle still cold on the counter. You know the body coughing at what the mind calls pleasure, the throat closing where joy should open. You feel the clash and smooth it quickly. You believe clash proves the body waking slowly. Clash proves the body objecting plainly while the brainwashing voice talks over it.

We all climbed those stairs telling age stories. We all watched a younger smoker bound up while an older non-smoker breathed easy and still laid the difference on birthdays. You believe birthdays sort the winded from the clear. Birthdays never sorted anything here, because the stairs sorted by feed, not by years, and the clearest lungs on the landing often belonged to the oldest walker who never fed.

I climbed those stairs myself with a packet hammering my pocket, and I tell you the landing never lied. You know the pattern. The lift taken for two floors to hide the pant. The dose lit at the top to reward the climb that the doses made heavy. You feel the circle close without words. The feed makes the climb hard and then claims the pause at the top as its own gift.

So with the stairs. You know the dose blamed for nothing and credited with the pause. You feel the shoulders drop with the first pull at the landing window. You know the air outside untouched by any leaf, the legs resting, the view doing its quiet work. You feel calm return. You believe calm proves the leaf steadies. Calm proves rest steadies, air steadies, stopping steadies, and the leaf rode along to claim the credit.

Ask what the stairs never ask, and answer from your own landings.

If smoke gives breath and nerve, why do the clearest breaths on the landing belong to those who never lit, and why does the smoker need the pause the non-smoker walks through singing.

You know the honest answer ends the stairs as proof of pleasure. Breath never came from the tube. Breath left through the tube and returned in the pause the tube claimed. The stairs never tested age. They tested a feed, and the feed showed in the chest where no story could hide it.

THE HAND WITH THE LIGHTER

Turn now to the doorway where the offer comes, because here the trap wears its kindest face.

You know the doorway well. You feel the evening air cool on the face, the talk warm behind in the room, the small circle gathered where the light spills thin. You know the friend reaching with packet tipped, two white ends nudged forward, the lighter already thumbed alive. You feel the flame lean in the draught, the hand cupped round it for you, the eyes friendly and certain you will take. You believe refusal would wound, that manners demand acceptance and friendship lives in shared fire.

You know the words that ride with the flame. You hear them easy and light, "go on, one won't hurt," spoken with a grin that expects the grin back. You feel the old pull rise with the words, quick and practised, the fingers already loosening. You know the laugh through the patio heaters from the garden beyond, the glasses chiming, the night doing its work on everyone whether they hold a lit end or not. You feel the night call. You believe the call comes from the offered tube. The call comes from drink and company and break and air, and the tube sneaks a ride on them all.

We all took that hand and called it nothing. We all said the words "just being sociable" about ourselves, as if politeness explained the fingers. You believe politeness sorts the takers from the prudes. Politeness never sorted anything, because the same polite hand refuses a second dessert without fear, and only the feed makes refusal feel like insult.

I took that hand myself more times than I can count, and I tell you each taking taught the fingers, not the friendship. You know the pattern. The packet offered free tastes sweeter for being free, till free becomes the dearest price paid. The lighter kept ready teaches the pocket to keep its own ready. The nod among feeders teaches the eyes to read hunger as fellowship. You feel warmth in the circle. Test the warmth against the circle itself, and answer from what stays when the lit ends go out.

If the offered dose carries friendship, why does the friendship stand whole on nights when no one lights, and why do the deepest talks in that doorway happen in the pauses between pulls, not inside the pull itself.

If taking keeps you kind, why does the kindest person in the circle feel smallest after taking, slipping to the spray on the coat and the half-open door at home to hide the smell from love.

You know the only honest answers loosen the doorway story. Friendship never lived in the tube, because friendship stays when tubes stay pocketed. Kindness never demanded the feed, because true kindness never asks love to lie in a doorway. Something else wrote the doorway scene besides company, something small in the body glad of an excuse and something large in old belief glad to call the excuse proof of belonging.

See the shape plainly before we name the voice behind the hand. You were taught the offer proved smoking gives something social worth rejoining. The offer proved a feeder defending his own belief with a flame, asking your taking to steady his own story. The hand did what kind hands do when conned. The voice told its old story through kind mouths. The doorway never tested manners. It tested whether you can see a feed when it smiles.

WHY THE OFFER FEELS LIKE EVIDENCE

Hear the offer with clear ears now, because its power lies in seeming to come from outside you.

You know the thought that rises when three light together. You feel it form without words, that so many cannot be wrong, that pleasure shared must be real. You know the eyes watching the lit ends lift easy, the shoulders drop, the laugh come quicker. You feel the old brainwashing voice stir and whisper that freedom must be dull beside such ease. You believe numbers prove value, that a crowded doorway proves a full cup.

You know the trick inside the numbers. You feel it once seen. Three feeders easing the same hollow note together look like three friends sharing joy, the way three men scratching the same rash together might look like three friends sharing a pleasure if no one named the rash. You feel the picture shift. The ease you watch is not a gift arriving. It is a discomfort leaving for minutes, a meter fed, a small craving quieted while the talk does the real work.

We all mistook the scratch for the joy. We all watched the circle and felt our own throat move in answer, calling the move desire for company. You believe the move proves the company needs the tube. The move proves the body knows its feed time and the voice borrows company to excuse it.

You know the friend who presses hardest. You feel his grin widen when you hesitate, the packet shaken, the joke about willpower men and health cranks. You know the heat in his urging out of proportion to a small gift. You feel pressure where pleasure should need none. You believe pressure proves generosity. Pressure proves the opposite: a belief defending itself. Only a con presses its proof on a witness, because your refusal quietly questions his own story and your taking steadies it.

Ask the doorway its own question, and answer from your own evenings.

If other smokers prove a pleasure, why do they cough and spit and queue in rain for a pleasure, hide it from their children, count its cost with tight mouths, and call heaven only at the lighting moment before the taste turns stale.

You know the honest answer closes the doorway as evidence. Coughing pleasure-seekers, hiding pleasure-seekers, counting pleasure-seekers — the words do not hold together. What holds together is simpler and kinder: decent people caught by a small craving and a large story, mistaking relief for gift, pressing the gift to keep the story whole.

Hold the verdict that frees you in such circles. The offer is the trap speaking through kind mouths, not joy inviting you back.

THERE IS NO SUCH THING AS JUST ONE

Hear now the smallest sentence in the doorway story, because the tiniest offer carries the whole chain.

You know the line well. You hear it in your own voice before any friend speaks it, smooth and reasonable, "just one to be sociable, just one with drinks, just one because tonight is special." You feel sense in the smallness, a modest wish, almost humble. You know the evening that seems to ask for it, the drink warm, the talk loose, the old companion missing from the fingers. You feel the fingers empty and strange. You believe smallness makes the wish safe, that a single exception honours long sense.

You know what one does when watched without stories. You feel the first pull harsh after hours clear, the throat objecting, the head light in a way no pleasure explains. You know the second pull smoother, the third companionable, the fourth already counted as part of the night. You feel the old accounts reopen mid-sentence, crediting the tube with calm the drink and friends already gave. You know the morning after with its stale mouth and its quiet promise that last night was an exception. You feel the exception already planning its next occasion. You believe the morning proves a lapse. The morning proves a belief kept alive through one dose, calling the rest back by name.

We all nursed that smallness with clean intentions. We all told ourselves five months clear proved we could take or leave it, then took one to prove the mastery and learned the proof ran the other way. You know that account because it lives in house after house, "I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way, thinking after 5 months I could control myself and just have one." You feel the plain truth in those words when spoken by one who paid for them. One woke the tiny physical craving from its starving sleep and handed the brainwashing voice its microphone back, and the old story resumed as if never paused.

Name the protection plainly now, learned in your own body and not from any lecture. You know the line that keeps the chain boxed for good. There is no such thing as just one.

Feel how those words sit as kindness, not as threat. You feared such a line must be stern toward yourself. Test the sternness against your own mornings after single exceptions. One keeps the whole belief alive, and belief alive rebuilds the feed dose by dose. None honours how the trap works. One bargains with it.

PITY THEM; NEVER ENVY THEM

Learn now how to stand in any circle and watch lit ends without the old tug turning to loss.

You know the moment well. You feel the eyes follow the flame to lips, the chest remember the old rhythm, the throat rehearse the pull. You know the thought arrive dressed as fairness, that they have something in this cold hour while you stand empty-handed. You feel envy gather like weather. You believe envy proves they hold a pleasure. Envy proves only the old story still knows your address, mistaking relief for gift in other mouths.

You know what clear watching shows with open eyes. You feel the eyes stay open past the first pull. You know the smoker cough where the laugh should live, the fingers yellowed where the glass should shine clean, the feet already planning the next step out while the talk runs on inside. You feel the chain show through the manners. You know the small hollow note driving each lift, the brainwashing voice calling each quieting a pleasure, the meter fading within minutes and calling again. You feel understanding replace the tug. Such watching never needs hardness to be steady. What shows reads as slavery wearing company clothes, and no watcher calls watched chains a loss to miss.

Take the rule that steadies every future circle, stated plainly as protection and not as coldness. You know the line that keeps the eyes kind and the judgment your own. Pity them; never envy them.

Feel how that sentence sits warm in the chest, not hard. You feared pity must mean looking down. Test the fear against the doorway where you once stood pressing lights yourself. Pity looks level and sees a decent person paying a toll for relief from a want the toll maintains. Envy looks up at a gift. Pity looks plain at a feed. Only one of those readings is true, and truth steadies where envy shakes.

THE THREE SENTENCES THAT WILL COME

Know now the script that will arrive in your own voice, because forewarned thoughts arrive disarmed.

You know the first sentence well. You hear it light and social, "go on, just this once to be polite, everyone is lighting." You feel the smile ready with it. You know the second sentence following close, "it's a special occasion, the wedding, the drink, the night shift, these do not count." You feel comfort in the distinction. You know the third sentence saved for later, "one to prove I am in charge cannot undo months clear." You feel flattered by the logic, crowned by your own clean days. All three wear your voice and speak with your mouth. None comes from you. All three come from the voice that lived on doses and starves without them, auditioning for its old part.

We all heard those lines and mistook them for reasoning. We all answered them once with taking and called the taking our own. You believe answering proves autonomy. Answering proves the opposite: a script speaking through the listener. Name each line as it lands and it loses its power to sound like thought. The polite one is the trap asking manners to pay its toll. The special one is the trap asking occasions to except its rule. The mastery one is the trap asking pride to reopen its door.

You know how to meet each line where it lives, because you speak them in your own tongue and they deserve plain answers.

You feel the pull to be polite behind the offered flame. You know politeness never required feeding beside the feeder. True politeness stays in talk and laugh and glass raised, needing no lit end to prove warmth.

You feel the glow of the special night behind the exception. You know the night glows from people and music and break from labour, needing no tube to seal it. Sealing a bright hour with a feed stains what shone.

You feel mastery in clean days behind the test. You know clean days proved only the feed unfed, not the belief removed if one still tempts. Testing never proved mastery. Testing re-armed the voice that calls the feed a gift.

If politeness needs a lit end, why does the same politeness decline every other gift without fear, and why does the kindest refusal leave friendship warmer than taking ever did.

If special nights need an exception, why do the brightest nights in memory shine for faces and talk rather than ash, and why does the exception always ask for another special night soon after.

You know the answers close the script for good. Politeness never touched the want that sets the pace. Occasions never changed the meter that fades within minutes. Mastery never lived in tasting the chain to prove it loose. Each line was the trap rehearsing its return in your own accent, and heard plainly it cannot direct you.

THE CLEAR EYE

Gather the whole doorway now and see it with clear eyes, because here the social rope falls and readiness gathers.

You know your own count without my numbering it. You know the friend who lights with a sigh and calls the sigh pleasure, the mate who shakes the packet and calls the shake generosity, the circle that calls the feed fellowship. You feel them stand for your old evenings, tired, honest, baffled. You believe their number proves something real burns at the centre. Their number proves only one hard story was handed to many, and many smoke it faithfully in company.

Name what led you with its true name, learned among friends and not from any lecture. You know the nod, the flame cupped for you, the laugh that steadies the taker, the pressure that calls itself kindness. You feel the years such leading cost, the money, the stairs climbed short of breath, the love lied to in doorways. Give the whole bundle its honest sight at last, spoken plainly among us who followed it: other feeders cannot show you joy, only want. Feel how the words fit the queue and the stairs and the doorway in one breath. A plain sight for a plain scene that promised company and delivered a chain.

You know why that leading had to fail whoever followed it. You feel the logic settle without strain. Following a feeder to pleasure guarantees want, because the feeder mistakes his own relief for gift and teaches the mistake with a flame. The tiny physical craving adds only its brief hollow note, gone in days when unfed, while the brainwashing voice turns the note into fellowship and rite. You believe the fellowship is your own heart speaking. The fellowship is old installation speaking through kind mouths, installed by adverts and shelves and years of seeing lit ends mean pause and nerve and belonging.

I tell you with the full warmth of one who followed every offered light for a decade: the blame never belonged on your shoulders, and it never belonged on theirs. You feel the weight shift as you read, almost physical, like a pack set down. You know the sellers who built the want dose by dose far from any doorway, the colours named mild, the numbers printed low, the advisers who sold new glasses for the same drink with kind faces. Be harsh toward that drawing board and tender toward its customers, whether they offer or decline. The customer walked every hard mile ordered. The order omitted the cure.

See the gain already moving if you watch without flinching. You know the queue loses its power to call, the stairs lose their power to accuse, the doorway loses its power to shame. Such loosening never needs applause to be real.

The offer never proved a pleasure. The seeing does.

Hold those two sentences against your own doorways and feel how they hold. You feared standing apart must feel lonely, tallied in refusals, proved by holding out. Test the fear against queues and stairs and patios watched clearly. Apart never meant alone. Clear meant company without feed, talk without toll, nights whole without ash. Belief cleared is want cleared, and want cleared is the chain gone without a battle.

I ask one thing as you close this room, and I ask it with cheerful certainty, because one clear rule carries you faster than a crowd of leaders.

7. REFUSE TO BE LED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

SUMMARY
- You see that an offered cigarette never proved a pleasure because the hand offers to steady its own belief while the night and company do the real work.
- You understand that a single dose never stands alone because one keeps the belief alive and reliably calls the rest back in account after account.
- You believe that watching other smokers never needs envy because pity sees decent people paying for relief from a want the feed maintains.
