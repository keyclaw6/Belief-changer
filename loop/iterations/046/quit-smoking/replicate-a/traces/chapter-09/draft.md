Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

You know the kettle's song now and the plate cleared clean and the air on the face at the work door. You feel those three hours standing without signing, the feet finding the floor, the tongue reporting plain, the lungs widening unasked. You believe something has righted itself in those rooms, that the day holds where you feared it would lie flat. Hold that righted picture steady, because one question remains inside it and it asks to be answered plainly.

You know the tug that still sounds under the ribs from time to time, thin and small and oddly personal. You feel it arrive with your own accent, wearing your own memory, calling itself your taste, your break, your way of meeting sharp words. You believe that accent proves ownership, that no factory could sound so much like you. Such sounding proves nothing but long tenancy, and this chapter lives inside the tenancy till its landlord shows.

We all carried that landlord about in a shirt pocket and called the carrying nature. We all weighed the box a dozen times daily without noticing the weighing, thumbed its lid, counted its remainder against the hours left. You believe counting proves choosing. Counting proves dosing on a schedule somebody else wrote.

I stood at that same counter for years and paid for my own dosing with clean money and thanks. You know my coat because it wears your shape. I believed my want was born in me the way hunger is born. You feel the same privacy round your own want. Test the privacy against the box itself, because the box remembers what you were taught to forget.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a desk with the phone down, a pack weighed in the palm, a filter held to the light, and the office that counts its doses.

*Your want was never your nature; it was built elsewhere and posted to you as yourself.*

THE BOX IN YOUR POCKET

Take the pack from your pocket now and hold it the way you never hold it, because familiarity hides workmanship.

You know the weight of it before you look, the cellophane giving under the thumb, the lid flipping with its small practiced click. You feel the paper corners softened by the day's carrying, the foil innermouth torn to the same width you always tear. You know the printed colour chosen to speak calm or bold, the crest or the stripe, the name set in its confident type. You feel nothing strange in the handling, the way you feel nothing strange in turning a door knob. You believe smooth handling proves a simple thing. Smooth handling proves long practice at being handled.

You know the count you keep without keeping, the glance that reads nineteen or fourteen or nine and translates to hours. You feel the small arithmetic run under every plan, train times and meetings and evenings measured against remainder. You know the shop shutter lifting, the coins counted, the fresh box stepping out with you like a day's rations. You feel provided for when the box is full and exposed when it runs low. You believe provision proves a need met. Provision proves a dose-box topped up.

We all learned to read that box as a personal store, the way a walker reads a water bottle. We all tapped the end against the lid, watched one tube rise, drew it with lips and fingers in one practiced motion. You believe the ritual is yours because your hands know it. Your hands know it because they were drilled in it thousands of times by a product that needs hands to complete its office.

I weighed my own box the same way at garage tills and kiosks and late counters, and I tell you the weighing never once examined itself. You know the moment at the till where the shutter slides and the rows stand lit like a small lit chapel. You feel the eyes travel along the colours while the mouth names its usual without consulting. You know the packet slapped down, the change pocketed, the door pushed with the shoulder. You feel supplied. Nothing in that supplying speaks of leaf or soil or craft, and everything speaks of supply.

See the shape plainly before the desk calls. You were taught to see tobacco wrapped in paper. The fact is you hold a day's supply box counted in twenty measured feeds, priced to move daily, coloured to calm doubt. The box never hid its office. It counted you while you counted it.

THE HAND THAT MOVES FIRST

Sit now at the desk where the sharp words land, because here the want shows its authorship in motion.

You know the room with its afternoon light thinning across papers, the phone heavy in its cradle, the screen holding its unfinished line. You feel the shoulders gathered up round the ears, the jaw set, the breath shallow after the exchange that went wrong. You know the voice of the other still sounding in the ear, unfair and loud, leaving its heat in the chest. You feel the hollow note rise under the ribs, tight and nagging. You believe such tightening calls for your own remedy. Watch whose hand answers before you decide.

You know the hand moving across the blotter before any thought completes, the fingers finding the pack by address, the tube drawn and lit while the mind still replays the slight. You feel the first pull run hot to the back of the throat, the shoulders drop a fraction, the mind blur kindly at its edges. You know the rapid sense of being taken care of, as if somebody capable had stepped between you and the hour. You feel gratitude toward the tube for the loosening. You believe gratitude proves a friend arrived. Test the arrival against the desk itself, and feel what was tended and what was left.

You know the papers sitting exactly as you left them, the unsolved line still unsolved, the phone still holding its offence. You feel no paper moved by the pull, no word unsaid, no sum altered. You know the problem waiting with its mouth open while the chest enjoyed its minute of slack. You feel the slack already thinning as the tube burns down, the hollow note tuning up again underneath. You believe thinning proves the hour was hard. Thinning proves the feed fading on its own short meter.

We all credited that minute to the dose and called the credit calming. We all said the words in our own accent, "If anything stressful happens in my life, a dose takes care of it." You feel sense in the sentence, a witness speaking for years of desks. You know the sense borrows your mouth. Answer it with the desks themselves, and ask what the desks ask.

If the dose takes care of stress, why does the care leave the cause untouched on the blotter, and why does the taken-care-of feeling arrive in the chest while the work waits exactly as accused.

If the tube steadies the hand, why does the hand shake sooner toward the next feed, and why does the non-smoker at the next desk meet the same sharp words with the same heat and settle by breath and pause and answer, with nothing lit between the fingers.

You know the only honest answers return the desk to its worker. Calm never came from the tube, because the tube moved no paper and answered no voice. Quiet came from the pause and the breath and the stepping back a pace, the same servants that steady every creature that never lit. Something else wrote the minute besides lighting, something plain in the worked body asking for air and distance and something old in belief calling the asking proof of the dose.

Hear the voice that argues for the old tending, because it speaks quickest under pressure. You hear it low and reasonable, "I need this to cope, without it I cannot face the afternoon." You feel the reasonableness carry wage packets and rent and children fed. You know the reasonableness borrows your mouth. Ask it whose motion it describes, and watch the hand that moved before decision.

See the cigarette plainly in that reach at last, because here the authorship shows. You know the lit end as a brief slackening of a grip the last feed tightened. You feel no person in that tending now, only the drug creating the need it relieves, the trap selling its own relief back as care. The hand moved first because it was taught to move first, drilled by thousands of paired tightnesses and loosenings till the reach ran ahead of thought.

THE MILD THEATRE

Hold now one tube to the light, because the mildness you paid extra for deserves plain looking.

You know the filter between thumb and finger, its cork pattern printed to speak of craft, its white mouth neat as a laboratory. You feel the ring of tiny vents round its collar if you roll it slowly, pinpricks set in a perfect circle where the paper thins. You know the word mild printed on the box in soft silver, the numbers printed small along the side promising less. You feel reassured by neatness, the way neat instruments reassure. You believe neatness proves gentleness. Neatness proves stagecraft.

You know the lips finding their usual place across those vents without instruction, the fingers closing where they always close. You feel the draw run a fraction harder through the narrowed channel, the chest pulling deeper to fill the same hollow. You know the taste arriving thin and airy, almost clean, as if the danger had been laundered out. You feel clever for choosing the lighter road. You believe thin taste proves a smaller feed. Thin taste proves air mixed with the same feed.

We all played that theatre with straight faces and called the playing caution. We all covered the vent holes with lips and fingers and drew harder for the same quiet, then read the machine numbers as proof we had cut down. You believe numbers on a packet describe lungs. Numbers on a packet describe a machine that never covered a vent with a mouth, never pulled hard when the hollow called, never learned to dose itself through a working day.

I smoked those pale tubes myself when the cough thickened, and I tell you the day's count never fell. You know the count holding steady or creeping upward while the packet paled. You feel the feeds spaced closer because each feed satisfied less long, the meter fading faster on diluted draughts. You know the purse paying the same or more for the privilege of thinner smoke. You feel the bargain show once named.

Hear the voice that argues for the paler box, because it speaks in the accent of care. You hear it careful and hopeful, "At least I cut down, at least I smoke the light ones." You feel the hope carry love for those who breathe your air. You know the hope borrows your mouth. Answer it with your own fingers and lips, and ask what they do without asking you.

If mild means less, why do your lips and fingers close the vents the design left open, and why does your chest learn within days the harder pull that brings the same blood level the bold packet brought.

If cutting the numbers cut the want, why does the want count the same number of feeds by evening, and why do your own restless minutes arrive on the same schedule whatever colour you carry.

You know the only honest answers return the theatre to its builders. Less on the machine never meant less in the smoker, because the smoker is a living doser and the machine is a pump that never craves. Something else wrote the mildness besides mercy, something measured in the laboratory to print small numbers and something practiced in the body to take its accustomed feed through a narrower door.

See the shape plainly before the office counts. You were taught the filter refined the risk away. The filter refined the taste of the risk while the feed passed through, and the vents refined the numbers while the lungs did the compensating the design invited.

A TUBE FOR A DRUG

Count now what the box counts, because the office behind the shutter keeps cleaner books than we kept.

You know the pack as twenty, the carton as two hundred, the week as seven boxes marching. You feel the regularity faintly military, supply arriving on schedule, consumption marching to meet it. You know the factory floor you never saw, the long belts running, the tubes cut to identical length, the filters pressed, the packs sealed and stamped. You feel far from that floor at your desk and your sink. You believe distance proves no relation. Distance proves the relation works without your inspection.

You know the puff as a small event, the ember brightening, the paper ticking down a millimetre. You feel it as a taste or a pause or a reward. You believe the puff is the product. Watch the puff from the office side and it reads as a squirt, a measured shot of nicotine delivered through smoke to blood and brain within seconds, fading fast so the next is sought. You know the meter fading within minutes and calling again. You feel the feeds multiply through morning, meal, break, desk, road, evening, each one tender, each one needful. You believe many feedings prove many pleasures. Many feedings prove a short-acting drug needing renewal.

We all thought we bought leaf and paper and a moment's pleasure. We all read the packet as a small luxury, the way we read a chocolate bar. You believe luxury explains the price and the pleasure. Luxury cannot explain the schedule, the counting, the panic at nine remaining, the midnight walk for an open kiosk. Schedule explains schedule. The schedule is dosing.

I learned late what their own planners wrote when no smoker listened, and I tell you they never spoke of luxury in those rooms. You know the sentence they used among themselves, that the tube should be understood as a wrapper and the drug as the goods, the smoke as the carrier and the blood level as the sale. You feel the coldness of that bookkeeping. You believe coldness proves conspiracy thinking. Coldness proves a business describing its own business plainly where we cannot hear.

Hear the voice that calls this reading cynical, because it speaks in your own defence of pleasure. You hear it worldly and amused, "They just sell tobacco, I just like smoking." You feel the amusement protect something tender. You know the amusement borrows your mouth. Answer it with the schedule itself, and ask what schedule pleasure keeps.

If the goods are taste and moment, why does the want keep office hours from the bedside to midnight, and why does no dinner, however fine, ever close the account the way a non-smoker's dinner closes.

If the goods are leaf, why does the office measure its success in blood levels held through the day, and why does every improvement it ever offered narrow the door while keeping the drug's passage sure.

You know the only honest answers return the product to its makers. Taste never scheduled midnight walks, because taste is satisfied and sleeps. The drug schedules them, because the drug fades and calls. Something else wrote the regularity besides liking, something counted in the factory as doses per day and something counted in the body as levels falling and rising.

See the shape plainly before we name the teachers. You were taught you bought smoking. You bought measured nicotine in paper wrappers, twenty shots to hold the day's level, the smoke as the needle that carries it. The box is a day's dose-box. The tube is its wrapping.

WHO TAUGHT THE WANT

Ask now where so personal a want learned its accent, because accents are taught.

You know the first pictures before you ever lit, the kitchen table where the elders smoked after food, the car window cracked to let the weather in with the weather of smoke. You feel the grown world signalling what grown hours include, the laugh through the lit end, the pause that looked like thinking. You know the screens that raised you second, the hero stepping into rain with his collar up and his ember bright, the singer leaning to the microphone with smoke curling round the song. You feel admiration leak from the figure to the tube. You believe admiration proves nothing transferred. Admiration transfers everything it touches.

You know the corner shop growing taller as you grew, its shutter of colours at your eye level, its lights burning late. You feel the packets standing shoulder to shoulder like books that all tell one story. You know the friends at the school gate or the work gate, the offered tube with its small ceremony of belonging, the first cough laughed off as initiation. You feel the laugh bind tighter than any chain. You believe initiation proves choice. Initiation proves recruitment.

We all breathed that weather for years before we ever inhaled, and we called the weather normal. We all learned the gestures before we learned the need, the tap and the offer and the step to the doorway. You believe normal proves natural. Normal proves common, and common here was manufactured daily by offices that count doses the way bakers count loaves.

I believed my first want rose from inside me like hunger. You know that same belief in your own chest. I tell you my hunger for food arrived with no advertising, no shutter, no hero in rain to teach it. You feel the difference once named. Hunger needs no teacher. This want needed teachers in every street and every picture, and it found them.

Hear the voice that defends the want as character, because it speaks closest to shame. You hear it quiet and stubborn, "Maybe I am just the type, maybe my nerves need it." You feel the stubbornness protect dignity. You know the stubbornness borrows your mouth. Answer it with your own history, and ask when the type was typed.

At what stage did you decide you needed smoke to meet sharp words, and did you fear missing it before you ever fed it, or did the fear arrive after the feeding taught it.

At what stage did you choose the brand that tastes like you, and did the taste choose you before the vents and the silver and the numbers taught your fingers where to close.

You know the only honest answers return the want to its school. No child decides at leisure that smoke steadies nerves, because no child knows the pairing till the pairing is drilled. No palate is born mild or bold, because palates learn the packet they are given. Something else wrote the personality besides nature, something broadcast and shelved and offered till the brainwashing voice could speak in your accent and pass for you.

You were conned, not weak.

Feel how that sentence sits as fact, not as comfort. You feared such lines must excuse you softly to keep you reading. Test the fear against offices and shutters and screens counted coldly. Nothing softens there. Something straightens, the way a back straightens when a heavy pack is seen to belong to somebody else. The want stops reading as your flaw and starts reading as their work.

WHAT IT DID WHILE CLAIMING TO DO FOR

Stand now where the credit belongs, because every drop claimed must be returned to its owner.

You know the claim in its plainest form, spoken over desks and tables and yard walls for years. You feel it warm in the mouth, almost grateful, that the dose helps, steadies, rewards, accompanies. You know the moments lined up as witnesses, the sharp words survived, the meal closed, the break carried. You feel the witnesses crowd. You believe crowding proves a case. Crowding proves one witness moved from room to room to sign each scene.

You know the desk with its papers untouched, the plate whose tastes arrived louder when clean, the yard air that widened the lungs unasked. You feel each hour already examined and found complete without signing. You know the tube stepping into each completed thing to sign it after. You feel the pattern show once the maker shows. The hours did the living. The dose did the signing.

We all let the signing pass for making and called the confusion pleasure. We all felt the chest loosen where the chest was bound since the last feed ended and read the loosening as a gift. You believe loosening proves kindness. Loosening proves a grip slackened between tightenings by the same arms that tightened it.

It never did anything FOR you; it only did things TO you.

Feel how that sentence sits as arithmetic, not as slogan. You feared such lines must be argued long to hold. Test the fear against the desk with its work undone, the vents with their theatre opened, the box with its count counted. Nothing argues there. Something adds, the way columns add when the figures are set straight. The doing-TO stands in cough and cost and hiding and chain, the doing-FOR empty as a signed name where the work was done by others.

I make no threat here and I paint no doom, because fear never freed a smoker and I do not ask fear of you. You know the offices counted coldly and the theatres printed softly. You feel anger rise cleanly toward the counting, the way anger rises toward any hand found in the pocket. You believe anger proves bitterness. Anger here proves sight returning, and sight is already escape beginning, held without strain.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

You know the seller behind the smoke now, the office that wrapped a drug as a pleasure and taught your hand to move before thought. You feel the want change address from inside to outside, from nature to manufacture. You believe such change must be held by effort. Such change is held by seeing, and the seeing is done each time the box is weighed and known for what it is.

SUMMARY
- You see that your want was manufactured and dosed by a seller rather than born in you, because the pack counts doses while you count hours.
- You understand that mild filters and machine numbers change the theatre but not the feed, because lips and lungs take what the design keeps delivering.
- You believe that the dose did nothing for you while doing plenty to you, because desks and meals and breaks stand complete while the tube only signed them.
