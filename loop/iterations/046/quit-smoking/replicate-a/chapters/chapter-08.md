Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

The doorway where the hand held out its light is behind you now. You know how that offer reads when seen plainly, and you know the three sentences that arrive in your own voice and speak for the want, not for you. You feel the circles you feared grow quieter already, watched with level eyes. You believe the hardest watching is done, that the private hours will test you where no friend watches. Such hours test nothing but the same story in softer clothes, and this chapter lives them through till they stand complete.

You know the hours I mean. You feel them arrive three times daily without announcement, wearing the face of nature itself. You know the first minutes after sleep, the feet meeting the cold floor, the kettle waiting on its stand. You know the plate pushed back after the midday meal, the taste still moving on the tongue, the chair leaned back a breath. You know the mid-morning pause at work, the door pushed open, the air on the face, the hands suddenly with nothing to hold. You believe these three moments belong to the dose the way dawn belongs to birds, that morning and meal-end and break arrive unfinished till lit.

We all dressed those moments in smoke and called the dressing their nature. We all woke and reached, ate and reached, paused and reached, till the reach felt like the hinge the hour turned on. You believe the hinge proves the hour needs it. Stay inside each hour with me, lived slowly and to the end, and feel where the living really happens.

I lived these same three hours as a feeder for years, and I live them now with nothing lit, and I tell you the hours did not shrink when the smoke left them. You know my coat because it wears your shape. I believed my mornings would lie flat without the first dose the way a room lies flat with its lamp unlit. You feel the same fear when you picture tomorrow's kettle with empty hands. Test the fear against the hours themselves, because here the private rope falls away.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a kitchen at first light with the kettle coming to song, a plate cleared clean with taste lingering, a work break with air on the face, and arms that held too tight.

*Your morning was never lit by the dose; the kettle, the plate and the pause did the living, and you are about to feel them do it.*

FEET TO THE KETTLE

Stand in the kitchen at first light, because the day tells its truth before the voice wakes fully.

You know the floor cold under the soles, the boards or the tiles taking the feet the way they take them every winter. You feel the spine lengthen after the night's fold, the shoulders roll once, the jaw open wide round a yawn that shakes the whole frame. You know the window over the sink holding the pale sky, the street still quiet, a bird testing one note and then another. You feel the body arrive in the day before any thought arrives, breathing the cool air of the room deep without instruction. You believe such arrival needs a push, that the frame cannot wake itself. Watch the frame and it wakes itself before your eyes.

You know the kettle's weight in the hand, the tap running cold, the click of the switch and the small lamp glowing orange. You feel the wait of ninety seconds stretch kind, the low talk of the water rising toward its song. You know the cupboard opened half-asleep, the cup chosen by feel, the spoon chiming once against the rim. You feel the hands busy and certain, doing their old dance without asking. You believe busy hands prove nothing, that they fidget toward the missing tube. Busy hands prove the morning already holds them, pouring and stirring and warming round the cup.

You know the old reach that used to cut across this dance before the feet found the floor, that fumbling toward the bedside packet with the eyes still gummed. You feel no need to rebuild that reach here, because its shape is known and its meaning settled. You know the cough that used to answer the first pull on empty lungs, the watering eyes, the kettle still cold on the counter while the throat took its scolding. You feel the clash it always carried, joy claimed where the body objected. You believe clash proves the body waking slowly. Clash proves the body objecting plainly on the year's first evidence, while the brainwashing voice talked over it with its waking story.

We all let that first dose sign its name across the dawn and called the signature sunrise. We all felt the head swim a fraction and read the swim as starting, felt the throat bite and read the bite as clearing. You believe the swim starts the engine. The swim tilts the room a degree, and the kettle rights it while claiming none of the honour.

I take my tea to the same window now, and I tell you the light never once asked for smoke to enter by. You know the first sip scalding the lip, blown once, taken short, the heat running down the centre of the chest like a small sun rising inside. You feel the eyes clear with the warmth, the shoulders drop their night's guard, the breath come easy through the nose with the steam. You know the second sip deeper, the throat open, the taste of the leaf or the coffee's dark ground arriving full on the tongue. You feel the morning gather itself round the cup. Nothing in that gathering wears a lit end, and nothing misses one.

Hear the voice that argues for the old signing, because it speaks in your own accent before the kettle sings. You hear it smooth and almost tender, "my morning dose gets me going, without it I sit half-asleep till noon." You feel sense in the tenderness, a witness speaking for years of dawns. You know the tenderness borrows your mouth. Answer it with the dawns themselves, and ask what the dawns ask.

If the first dose wakes you, why does the non-smoker at the next window wake fully on kettle steam alone, and why do your own clearest wakings happen in the shower's water and the cold air at the door, minutes before any dose is lit.

If the dose clears the head, why does the head feel thickest in the very minutes after it, with the eyes watering and the chest working, and why does the clearness arrive with the wash and the drink and the moving about, the same servants that wake every creature that never lit.

You know the only honest answers return the dawn to its owners. Waking never came from the tube, because waking was already walking the floorboards before the hand reached. Clearness never came from the pull, because the pull clouded what the water and the warmth and the light were clearing. Something else wrote the morning besides lighting, something wide in the body rising to meet the day and something old in belief calling the rise proof of the dose.

See the shape plainly before the plate arrives. You were taught the morning needed signing to begin. The morning begins in the feet and the lungs and the kettle's song, and the dose only steps into the begun thing to sign it. Feet first, breath next, warmth after, and the day stands up without asking leave of any packet.

THE MEAL THAT ENDS CLEAN

Sit now at the table's end, because the plate tells the same truth in a warmer voice.

You know the meal arriving with its proper noise, the steam off the potatoes, the fork's weight, the first mouthful taken while still too hot. You feel the hunger meet its answer, the belly's hollow filling by degrees, the shoulders loosening with each plate emptied a fraction. You know the talk round the table doing its own feeding, the laugh that makes the food taste brighter, the second helping taken because the company lingers. You feel the whole animal settle toward sufficiency, the way fed creatures settle everywhere. You believe settlement needs sealing, that the full stop at the meal's end must be lit. Watch the settlement and it seals itself before your eyes.

You know the taste lingering after the last forkful, the gravy or the oil still speaking on the tongue, the bread's crust still crackling faint in memory. You feel the mouth alive with aftermath, the palate reporting course by course what passed. You know the cup pushed forward or the fruit bowl drawn near, the small sweet close of apple or tea carrying the meal downhill gently. You feel the hour round itself the way good hours round, complete as a poured cup. The hour holds together fully, with no part of it asking for smoke.

You know the old pellet that used to close such meals, the chair pushed back, the step to the door or the window, the meal's company traded for a lit end and an ash. You feel no need to rebuild that pellet day here, because its shape is known from every finished paragraph and finished errand ever paid the same way. You know the party was always the dose, never the work, never the meal, the same pellet moved from hour to hour to claim each closing. You feel the pattern show once named. The meal closed itself, and the pellet signed the closing.

We all stood from laden tables and told ourselves the food asked for the finish. We all felt the belly's full signal rise and read it as the dose calling, felt the tongue's rich aftermath and read it as ash asking. You believe fullness calls the tube. Fullness calls rest, and the tube answers a call never made to it.

I eat my evening meal at the same board now, and I tell you the tastes arrive louder with nothing laid over them. You know the gravy's salt speaking plain, the greens still bitter at their edges, the tea afterward arriving as its own small ceremony. You feel the tongue report faithfully when no stale end films it, the way an ear reports faithfully in a quiet room. You know the chair leaned back a breath with the belly warm, the hands resting empty on the belly's rise, the talk continuing without division. You feel the company keep its circle unbroken, no exile to doorways, no spray and mints afterward, no small lying about where the smell went. The meal keeps its eaters, and none is sent out.

Hear the voice that argues for the old finish, because it speaks creamiest round food. You hear it rich and certain, "I need something after a meal, it settles everything and rounds it off." You feel the richness carry years of tables. You know the richness borrows your mouth. Answer it with the tables themselves, and ask what the tables ask.

If the dose settles the meal, why does the settling arrive in the chewing and the filling and the sitting still, and why does the non-smoker at the same board sigh the same full sigh and rest the same rested hands with nothing lit between the fingers.

If the dose rounds the taste, why does the taste report duller through its film, with the tongue coated and the palate blunted, and why do the brightest tastings of your life live in meals where the dose waited hours away and the mouth stayed clean to receive them.

You know the only honest answers return the table to its eaters. Settlement never came from the tube, because the belly settles by filling, not by lighting. Taste never came from the pull, because the pull lays its film across the very tongue it claims to crown. Something else wrote the meal's end besides lighting, something deep in the fed body sighing toward rest and something old in belief calling the sigh proof of the dose.

See the shape plainly before the break bell rings. You were taught the plate needed its lit full stop. The plate writes its own ending in warmth and sufficiency and lingering taste, and the dose only steps into the written thing to sign it. Hunger met, palate speaking, company kept, and the hour closes clean because eating has done its work.

AIR ON YOUR FACE

Step now through the work door for the pause, because the break tells the truth in open air.

You know the chair pushed back after the long sitting, the spine unfolding joint by joint, the legs finding their stride down the corridor. You feel the blood move again after stillness, the neck roll loose, the eyes lift from the near page to the far distance. You know the door's weight and the step over the threshold, the outside air arriving all at once on the face like a cool hand laid kindly. You feel the lungs take the larger draught unasked, the chest widen, the breath slow of its own accord. You believe such widening needs a lit companion, that the pause cannot open without it. Watch the pause and it opens itself before your eyes.

You know the sounds of the yard or the street at mid-morning, the traffic's wash, a hammer somewhere keeping time, gulls or pigeons turning over the roofs. You feel the mind unhook from its task the way a boat unhooks from its mooring, drifting a blessed minute with no cargo. You know the sun or the clouds doing their theatre overhead, the light changing on the brick, the wind finding the warm neck. You feel the minute restore out of proportion to its length, the way a sip restores the thirsty beyond its measure. You come back restored, and no absence sits beside you.

You know the hands hanging free at the sides, the fingers flexing after the keyboard or the tools, the palms cooling in the air. You feel their emptiness strange for a breath, the old itch for something to hold. You know the strangeness passes like a cloud shadow, there and then gone, while the air keeps doing its work on the face. You feel the fingers learn their older trade, swinging loose, brushing the pocket's seam, resting easy in the jacket's mouth. You believe empty hands prove a missing thing. Empty hands prove hands restored to themselves, holding nothing because nothing needs holding.

We all stood in such breaks and lit the minute we claimed to love, then credited the minute to the lighting. We all felt the shoulders drop with the first pull at the yard wall and read the drop as the leaf steadying, felt the mind clear with the step outside and read the clearing as the tube's gift. You believe the drop proves the leaf steadies. The drop proves stopping steadies, air steadies, distance steadies, and the leaf rode along to claim the credit, as it claimed the stairs and the landing before.

I take my breaks at the same wall now, and I tell you the air never once asked for company of smoke to do its office. You know the ten minutes stretching kind, the legs stamping warm in winter or the sleeves rolled in summer, the talk with the mate who stands alongside with nothing lit between. You feel the return to the bench or the desk with the head rinsed clear, the task resumed at a run, the hands quick and certain. You know the afternoon owes that quickness to the pause, not to any packet. The pause paid, and none collected but you.

Hear the voice that argues for the old companion, because it speaks briskest at work. You hear it practical and clipped, "I need my break, it gets me through the shift, without it the morning drags." You feel the practicality carry wage packets and deadlines. You know the practicality borrows your mouth. Answer it with the shifts themselves, and ask what the shifts ask.

If the dose carries you through the work, why do the steadiest hands on the floor belong to those who never stepped out to light, and why do your own fastest hours follow the walk and the sandwich and the laugh, with the dose nowhere inside them.

If the break needs the tube to be a break, why does the breather taken without one leave you quicker to the mark, with the chest easy and the mouth clean, while the breather taken with one leaves you coughing at the restart and counting the minutes till the next.

You know the only honest answers return the pause to its air. Stamina never came from the tube, because work is carried by rest and breath and bread, not by lighting. Rest never came from the pull, because the pull shortens the very breath the break came to lengthen. Something else wrote the break besides lighting, something plain in the worked body asking for air and distance and something old in belief calling the asking proof of the dose.

See the shape plainly before we name the arms. You were taught the pause needed its lit hinge. The pause hinges on stepping out and breathing deep and letting the mind drift, and the dose only steps into the hinged thing to sign it. Legs moving, air arriving, mind unhooking, and the break stands whole by itself.

THE ARMS THAT WOULD NOT LET GO

Know now why those three hours felt hinged at all, because here the feeling itself is explained.

You know the embrace that met you in every one of those hours, warm and prompt as a friend's arm round the shoulders. You feel it in memory's body, there at the kettle before the day began, there at the plate's end before the taste faded, there at the yard wall before the air finished its work. You know its manner, loosening something tight in the chest for a minute, easing the shoulders, quieting the small hollow note under the ribs. You feel gratitude rise toward it the way gratitude rises toward any arms that loosen a grip. You believe gratitude proves a friend. Test the arms the way you tested the hours, and feel whose grip loosens and whose tightens.

You know the trick of those arms once watched across a whole day. You feel the loosening arrive each time precisely where a tightening went before, the chest eased where the chest was bound since the last embrace ended. You know the meter fading within minutes and calling again, the coin dropping with every pull to buy back briefly what the last pull spent. You feel the embraces multiply through the morning, the meal, the break, each one tender, each one needful, each one arriving exactly on the schedule the embraces themselves wrote. You believe many embracings prove much love. Many embracings prove a grip retightened between holdings, the way a hand that squeezes and releases and squeezes again teaches the wrist to bless each release.

We all blessed those arms with clean hearts and mistook the blessing's object. We all felt the hug at dawn and called it waking, the hug after food and called it settling, the hug at work and called it carrying, never noticing the same arms brought the tightness they loosened. You believe three witnesses prove three gifts. Three witnesses describe one grip felt in three rooms, a single hold moving with you from kitchen to table to yard, loosening to be blessed and tightening to be needed.

See the cigarette plainly in those arms at last, because the rescuer wears no other face. You know the lit end between the fingers as the arm's hand, the pull as the arm's squeeze, the brief quiet as the squeeze slackened. You feel no person in that embrace now, only the tube doing its office, the drug creating the need it relieves, the trap selling its own relief back as a gift. Here stands only the embrace and its arithmetic: each loosening borrowed against the next tightening, each gratitude harvested from a discomfort its own arms planted.

I stood inside those arms a decade and called them company through mornings I now know were company already. You know the kettle, the plate, the yard air standing round every embrace unregarded, doing the true holding while the arms took the thanks. You feel the picture right itself once seen, the way a tilted frame rights on its nail. The arms never held you up. They held you, the way a grip holds, and called the holding love.

Hold the verdict that frees the three hours in one breath. The hug never gave the morning, the meal or the break. It took them in its arms and signed them.

HANDS FREE

Live now the same three hours with the arms seen, because seen arms cannot sign again.

You know tomorrow's kettle before it sings, the feet finding the floor, the window holding its pale sky. You feel the tea's heat run down the centre beforehand in mind's rehearsal, the shoulders dropping with the first sip, the breath easy through the steam. You know the old tug may sound once under the ribs, small and empty, asking in its thin voice. You feel it for what it is now, brief body pharmacology asking to be fed, the kind most bodies know for days and then know no longer. You believe the tug proves a missing pleasure. The tug proves a small craving emptying as it starves, and here is its true name spoken kindly in passing. The empty feeling is the cure starting, not something to cure.

You know the midday plate before it steams, the hunger arriving honest, the first mouthful taken hot. You feel the tongue report course by course with nothing filmed over it, the tastes arriving louder for the clean mouth. You know the chair leaned back with the belly warm, the hands resting empty and easy on the belly's rise. You feel the company keep its circle, no exile, no doorway, no small arithmetic of spray and hiding. You believe clean tongues and kept circles sound too plain to satisfy. You know the work break before the door opens, the corridor's length, the threshold, the air's cool hand. You feel the lungs widen unasked, the mind unhook and drift its blessed minute, the fingers swinging loose and learning their older trade. You know the return to the task with the head rinsed, the hands quick, the afternoon owing its quickness to the pause. You feel the break stand in memory afterward whole and signed by none. Such wholeness never needs applause to be real.

We all feared these hours would lie flat as rooms with their lamps unlit. We all pictured the kettle joyless, the plate pointless, the break barren, and braced for a life of withstandings. You believe picturing proves knowing. Picturing proved only the brainwashing voice describing its own absence as your loss, painting the rooms grey so you would keep its lamp burning. Step into the rooms and the paint washes off in the living.

THE MORNING WAS THE GIFT

Gather the three hours now and feel them stand together, because together they end the question.

You know the kettle's song and the cup's warmth, the plate's steam and the lingering taste, the door's weight and the air's cool hand. You feel each hour whole from foot to crown, needing no signature beyond its own living. You know the tug small where it sounds, the voice known where it speaks, the arms seen where they reach. You feel the seeing do what striving never did, the rooms brightening not by effort but by plain sight. You believe brightness must be earned by holding out. Brightness arrives by seeing true, and the seeing is done.

The morning never needed the dose. The morning was the gift.

Feel how those two sentences sit as kindness, not as threat. You feared such lines must bind you sternly to missing. Test the fear against kitchens and tables and yards lived clearly. Nothing binds there. Something unbinds, the way dawn unbinds the sleeper without asking his strength. The hours you feared empty arrive fuller, breathed deeper, tasted cleaner, held in hands that hold nothing because nothing needs holding.

I make you one promise with the full warmth of one who feared these same rooms, and I make it with cheerful certainty, because one clear rule carries you faster than a hundred reasonings.
