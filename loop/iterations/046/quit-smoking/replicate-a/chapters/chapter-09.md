Chapter 9
WHO BUILT THIS WANT

You know the kettle's song now and the plate cleared clean and the air on the face at the work door. You feel those three hours standing without signing, the feet finding the floor, the tongue reporting plain, the lungs widening unasked. You believe something has righted itself in those rooms, that the day holds where you feared it would lie flat. Hold that righted picture steady, because one question remains inside it and it asks to be answered plainly.

You know the tug that still sounds under the ribs from time to time, thin and small and oddly personal. You feel it arrive with your own accent, wearing your own memory, calling itself your taste, your break, your way of meeting sharp words. You believe that accent proves ownership, that no factory could sound so much like you. Such sounding proves nothing but long tenancy, and this chapter lives inside the tenancy till its landlord shows.

We all carried that landlord about in a shirt pocket and called the carrying nature. We all weighed the box a dozen times daily without noticing the weighing, thumbed its lid, counted its remainder against the hours left. You believe counting proves choosing. Counting proves dosing on a schedule somebody else wrote.

I stood at that same counter for years and paid for my own dosing with clean money and thanks. You know my coat because it wears your shape. I believed my want was born in me the way hunger is born. You feel the same privacy round your own want. Test the privacy against the box itself, because the box remembers what you were taught to forget.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a desk with the phone down, a pack weighed in the palm, the office that counts its doses, and the school that taught the want.

*Your want was never your nature; it was built elsewhere and posted to you as yourself.*

THE WEIGHT IN THE PALM

Take the pack from your pocket now and hold it the way you never hold it, because familiarity hides workmanship.

You know the weight of it before you look, the smooth board under the thumb, the lid flipping with its small practiced click. You feel the corners softened by the day's carrying, the colour chosen to speak calm or bold, the crest or the stripe set in its confident type. You know nothing strange in the handling, the way you feel nothing strange in turning a door knob. You believe smooth handling proves a simple thing. Smooth handling proves long practice at being handled.

You know that box already seen as a ration, counted in twenties to hold the day, and the ration already proves an office counted you while you counted hours.

We all learned to read that box as a personal store, the way a walker reads a water bottle. We all tapped the end against the lid, watched one tube rise, drew it with lips and fingers in one practiced motion. You believe the ritual is yours because your hands know it. Your hands know it because they were drilled in it thousands of times by a product that needs hands to complete its office.

You know those vents already seen sealed by your own lips and fingers, and the sealing already proves the mildness was printed to keep you dosing, not to lighten anything.

I weighed my own box the same way at garage tills and kiosks and late counters, and I tell you the weighing never once examined itself. You know the moment at the till where the shutter slides and the rows stand lit like a small lit chapel. You feel the eyes travel along the colours while the mouth names its usual without consulting. You know the packet slapped down, the change pocketed, the door pushed with the shoulder. You feel supplied. Nothing in that supplying speaks of leaf or soil or craft, and everything speaks of supply.

See whose hand chose the colour before your hand chose the packet. You were taught to see tobacco wrapped in paper. The fact is a seller chose the silver to calm you, the bold to flatter you, the crest to steady you, and shelved them at your eye level to be read daily. The packet never hid its maker. It schooled you while you carried it.

THE HAND THAT MOVES FIRST

Sit now at the desk where the sharp words land, because here the want shows its authorship in motion.

You know the room with its afternoon light thinning across papers, the phone heavy in its cradle, the screen holding its unfinished line. You feel the shoulders gathered up round the ears, the jaw set, the breath shallow after the exchange that went wrong. You know the voice of the other still sounding in the ear, unfair and loud, leaving its heat in the chest. You feel the hollow note rise under the ribs, tight and nagging. You believe such tightening calls for your own remedy. Watch whose hand answers before you decide.

You know the hand moving across the blotter before any thought completes, the fingers finding the pack by address, the tube drawn and lit while the mind still replays the slight. You feel the first pull run hot to the back of the throat, the shoulders drop a fraction, the mind blur kindly at its edges. You know the rapid sense of being taken care of, as if somebody capable had stepped between you and the hour. You feel gratitude toward the tube for the loosening. You believe gratitude proves a friend arrived. Test the arrival against the desk itself, and feel what was tended and what was left.

You know the papers sitting exactly as you left them, the unsolved line still unsolved, the phone still holding its offence. You feel no paper moved by the pull, no word unsaid, no sum altered. You know the problem waiting with its mouth open while the chest enjoyed its minute of slack. You feel the slack already thinning as the tube burns down, the hollow note tuning up again underneath. You believe thinning proves the hour was hard. Thinning proves the feed fading on its own short meter.

We all credited that minute to the dose and called the credit calming. We all said the words in our own accent, "If anything stressful happens in my life, a dose takes care of it." You feel sense in the sentence, a witness speaking for years of desks. You know the sense borrows your mouth. Answer it with the desks themselves, and ask what the desks ask.

If the dose takes care of stress, why does the care leave the cause untouched on the blotter, and why does the taken-care-of feeling arrive in the chest while the work waits exactly as accused.

If the tube steadies the hand, why does the hand shake sooner toward the next feed, and why does the non-smoker at the next desk meet the same sharp words with the same heat and settle by breath and pause and answer, with nothing lit between the fingers.

You know the only honest answers return the desk to its worker. Calm never came from the tube, because the tube moved no paper and answered no voice. Quiet came from the pause and the breath and the stepping back a pace, the same servants that steady every creature that never lit. Something else wrote the minute besides lighting, something plain in the worked body asking for air and distance and something old in belief calling the asking proof of the dose.

Hear the voice that argues for the old tending, because it speaks quickest under pressure. You hear it low and reasonable, "I need this to cope, without it I cannot face the afternoon." You feel the reasonableness carry wage packets and rent and children fed. You know the reasonableness borrows your mouth. Ask it whose motion it describes, and watch the hand that moved before decision.

See the cigarette plainly in that reach at last, because here the authorship shows. You know the lit end as a brief slackening of a grip the last feed tightened. You feel no person in that tending now, only the drug creating the need it relieves, the trap selling its own relief back as care. The hand moved first because it was taught to move first, drilled by thousands of paired tightnesses and loosenings till the reach ran ahead of thought.

THE OFFICE THAT COUNTS

Count now who counts with you, because the office behind the shutter keeps cleaner books than we kept.

You know the week as seven boxes marching, supply arriving on schedule, consumption marching to meet it. You feel the regularity faintly military, the fresh box stepping out with you like a day's rations, the panic at nine remaining, the midnight walk for an open kiosk. You believe many feedings prove many pleasures. Many feedings prove a short-acting drug needing renewal, and renewal on schedule proves a seller learned the meter before you felt it.

You know that tube already seen as a wrapper for a fast-fading drug, and the wrapper already proves a maker chose smoke to carry it to blood in seconds.

We all thought we bought leaf and paper and a moment's pleasure. We all read the packet as a small luxury, the way we read a chocolate bar. You believe luxury explains the price and the pleasure. Luxury cannot explain the shutter of colours at your eye level, the lights burning late, the packets standing shoulder to shoulder like books that all tell one story. Stories shelved together are published together, and publishing on that scale is never an accident of taste.

I learned late what their own planners wrote when no smoker listened, and I tell you they never spoke of luxury in those rooms. You know the sentence they used among themselves, that the tube should be understood as a wrapper and the drug as the goods, the smoke as the carrier and the blood level as the sale. You feel the coldness of that bookkeeping. You believe coldness proves cynical reading. Coldness proves a business describing its own business plainly where we cannot hear.

See the counting plainly before we name the teachers. You were taught you bought smoking. You bought measured nicotine in paper wrappers, twenty shots to hold the day's level, the smoke as the needle that carries it. The box is a day's dose-box only because an office counted doses the way bakers count loaves, and taught your pocket to keep their tally.

Hold that tally in the palm a moment longer. You know the thumb finding the lid without looking, the fingers reading remainder by weight, the mind translating remainder to hours. You feel the translation run under every plan, train times and meetings and evenings measured against remainder. You believe translation proves prudence. Translation proves a dose-clock installed where a clock already hung, ticking faster than hunger, faster than thirst, always asking to be fed on time. No hunger ever needed a shutter to stay hungry. This hunger needed shutters and colours and late lights, and it found them on every street you walk.

WHO TAUGHT THE WANT

Ask now where so personal a want learned its accent, because accents are taught.

You know the first pictures before you ever lit, the kitchen table where the elders smoked after food, the car window cracked to let the weather in with the weather of smoke. You feel the grown world signalling what grown hours include, the laugh through the lit end, the pause that looked like thinking. You know the car rides where the packet lived on the dashboard, tapped and offered with the ease of long habit, the ash tipped through the cracked glass. You feel small in the back seat breathing that weather, learning that grown worry ends in lighting. You believe watching proves nothing transferred. Watching transfers everything it touches when the watching lasts years.

You know the screens that raised you second, the hero stepping into rain with his collar up and his ember bright, the singer leaning to the microphone with smoke curling round the song. You feel admiration leak from the figure to the tube. You know the late films where the hard talk happens through smoke, the pause before the answer filled with a pull, the room made serious by the lit end. You feel the story teach without teaching, the way manners teach. You believe stories prove nothing about smoke. Stories prove everything about schooling, because no child lights from instinct while watching heroes light from script.

You know the corner shop growing taller as you grew, its shutter of colours at your eye level, its lights burning late. You feel the packets standing shoulder to shoulder like books that all tell one story. You know the till where the shutter slides and the rows stand lit, the hand learning its brand the way a tongue learns a name. You feel the naming feel like choosing. Naming learned at a lit wall is reciting, not choosing, and reciting daily makes the mouth believe it speaks for itself.

You know the friends at the school gate or the work gate, the offered tube with its small ceremony of belonging, the first cough laughed off as initiation. You feel the laugh bind tighter than any chain. You know the work doorway where the crowd gathers at break, the lighter passed hand to hand, the talk made easy by shared lighting. You feel exile threaten if you stand inside while they stand out. You believe initiation proves choice. Initiation proves recruitment, and recruitment works best when the recruit pays for his own uniform.

We all breathed that weather for years before we ever inhaled, and we called the weather normal. We all learned the gestures before we learned the need, the tap and the offer and the step to the doorway. You believe normal proves natural. Normal proves common, and common here was manufactured daily by offices that count doses the way bakers count loaves.

I believed my first want rose from inside me like hunger. You know that same belief in your own chest. I tell you my hunger for food arrived with no advertising, no shutter, no hero in rain to teach it. You feel the difference once named. Hunger needs no teacher. This want needed teachers in every street and every picture, and it found them.

You know the school kept no register you signed, yet every lesson left its mark. You feel the mark in the hand that finds the pack by address, in the mouth that names its brand without consulting, in the chest that reads its hollow as a call for lighting. You believe marks so deep must be nature. Marks so deep prove long schooling, the way a well-drilled step feels natural on parade. Parade ground ease never proves the march was born in the feet.

Hear the voice that defends the want as character, because it speaks closest to shame. You hear it quiet and stubborn, "Maybe I am just the type, maybe my nerves need it." You feel the stubbornness protect dignity. You know the stubbornness borrows your mouth. Answer it with your own history, and ask when the type was typed.

At what stage did you decide you needed smoke to meet sharp words, and did you fear missing it before you ever fed it, or did the fear arrive after the feeding taught it.

At what stage did you choose the brand that tastes like you, and did the taste choose you before the silver and the numbers taught your fingers where to close.

You know the only honest answers return the want to its school. No child decides at leisure that smoke steadies nerves, because no child knows the pairing till the pairing is drilled. No palate is born mild or bold, because palates learn the packet they are given. Something else wrote the personality besides nature, something broadcast and shelved and offered till the brainwashing voice could speak in your accent and pass for you.

You were conned, not weak.

Feel how that sentence sits as fact, not as comfort. You feared such lines must excuse you softly to keep you reading. Test the fear against offices and shutters and screens counted coldly. Nothing softens there. Something straightens, the way a back straightens when a heavy pack is seen to belong to somebody else. The want stops reading as your flaw and starts reading as their work.

WHAT IT DID WHILE CLAIMING TO DO FOR

Stand now where the credit belongs, because every drop claimed must be returned to its owner.

You know the claim in its plainest form, spoken over desks and tables and yard walls for years. You feel it warm in the mouth, almost grateful, that the dose helps, steadies, rewards, accompanies. You know the moments lined up as witnesses, the sharp words survived, the meal closed, the break carried. You feel the witnesses crowd. You believe crowding proves a case. Crowding proves one witness moved from room to room to sign each scene.

You know the desk with its papers untouched, the plate whose tastes arrived louder when clean, the yard air that widened the lungs unasked. You feel each hour already examined and found complete without signing. You know the tube stepping into each completed thing to sign it after. You feel the pattern show once the maker shows. The hours did the living. The dose did the signing.

We all let the signing pass for making and called the confusion pleasure. We all felt the chest loosen where the chest was bound since the last feed ended and read the loosening as a gift. You believe loosening proves kindness. Loosening proves a grip slackened between tightenings by the same arms that tightened it.

You know the meal that closed clean without lighting, the gravy speaking plain, the tea arriving as its own small ceremony. You feel the tongue report faithfully when no stale end films it. You know the kettle that rights the morning while claiming none of the honour, the feet finding the floor, the breath easy through the steam. You feel the morning gather itself round the cup. You know the yard wall where the pause opened itself, legs moving, air arriving, mind unhooking, the break standing whole by itself. You feel the return to the task with the head rinsed clear. Nothing in that gathering wore a lit end, and nothing missed one.

It never did anything FOR you; it only did things TO you.

Feel how that sentence sits as arithmetic, not as slogan. You feared such lines must be argued long to hold. Test the fear against the desk with its work undone, the box with its count counted, the school with its lessons listed. Nothing argues there. Something adds, the way columns add when the figures are set straight. The doing-TO stands in cough and cost and hiding and chain, the doing-FOR empty as a signed name where the work was done by others.

You know the cost written daily where the credit was claimed. You feel the purse paying for its own dosing, the clothes holding the smell through washes, the stairs taken winded while a non-smoking colleague breathes easy beside. You believe cost proves a price worth paying for pleasure. Cost proves a levy collected for a signing that never did the work, the way a toll proves a road is owned, not that the toll built the road.

I make no threat here and I paint no doom, because fear never freed a smoker and I do not ask fear of you. You know the offices counted coldly and the shutters burned late. You feel anger rise cleanly toward the counting, the way anger rises toward any hand found in the pocket. You believe anger proves bitterness. Anger here proves sight returning, and sight is already escape beginning, held without strain.

9. SEE THE SELLER BEHIND THE SMOKE
Remember who built the trap and why.

You know the seller behind the smoke now, the office that wrapped a drug as a pleasure and taught your hand to move before thought. You feel the want change address from inside to outside, from nature to manufacture. You believe such change must be held by effort. Such change is held by seeing, and the seeing is done each time the box is weighed and known for what it is.

SUMMARY
- You see that your want was schooled and dosed by sellers rather than born in you, because shutters and screens and offered tubes taught your hand to move before thought.
- You understand that the office counts doses while you count hours, because the pack keeps its tally in twenties and your day marches to meet it.
- You believe that the dose did nothing for you while doing plenty to you, because desks and meals and breaks stand complete while the tube only signed them.
