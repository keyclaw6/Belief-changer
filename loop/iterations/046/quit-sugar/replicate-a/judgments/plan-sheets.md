## 3. Redefinition decision

**Boxed definition (placed in Chapter 1, invoked thereafter by the CAPS name):**

> BAD SUGAR means refined sugar and all caloric added sugars plus the ultra-processed sweet-fat foods built around them — sweets, biscuits, cakes, pastries, ice cream, chocolate confectionery, sugary drinks, sweetened cereals, and the lookalike honey-syrup-agave swaps that keep the same loop alive. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Whole fruit, vegetables and plain whole foods are not the target and are part of freedom.

**Decree line on card C1:** when I say sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

**CAPS carriage:** the CAPS name carries the definition in every sentence thereafter; naming is the repetition.

**Total-abstinence logic inside the line:** there is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero as a habit; the good subset stays fully available so leaving the bad subset is not deprivation.

**Margin for error:** the body can cope with an accidental blip, but the mind must not be allowed to reopen the decision. A slip revives nothing unless the old belief is invited back. Guard the belief, not the mouthful. The seatbelt image carries it: it is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Gray-zone and accidental consumption is non-catastrophic; deliberate re-admission as a treat is the trap talking.

**Conditional extensions:** none required. Adjacent indulgences are not the book's business; the door is left open without recruiting them.

## 4. Clinical advisory

**ID: SAFE-1**

> This book is about changing what you believe sugar does for you. It is not medical advice, not diabetes care, not eating-disorder treatment and not a diet plan. If you live with diabetes, take glucose-affecting medication, are pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, have a history of disordered eating, or feel faint, shaky or unwell in ways that worry you, speak to a qualified professional about your own situation and use this book only for its belief argument. If eating feels out of control and frightening, or you think you may harm yourself, contact a trusted professional or local support service now.

Routing: cited in guardrails on cards where appetite, crash, withdrawal-hump or body-trust language appears; routed on non-argument safety-adjacent passages; reflected in evidence safety limits. Never fused into instruction frozen wording. Never boxed mid-chapter by the writer.

## 5. Compact evidence ledger

Each entry carries finding, research-unit link, source ID, grade, scope, permitted inference, prohibited inference, empirical limit, safety limit. Exact quotations match the synthesis; interpretations are unquoted.

- **L-01 — the fuel misread.** Reader wording: "I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop." Units: LEU-001. Source: bank-02 B-003. Grade: lived. Scope: afternoon dip answered with a sweet top-up. Permitted: the lift-crash cycle is self-described by recovered writers; the fuel misreads a spike-then-drop as genuine energy. Prohibited: that every reader has clinically low blood sugar, or that sugar is ever real fuel. Empirical limit: lived self-report, not glucose measurement. Safety limit: no medical diagnosis claims from lived quotes. See SAFE-1.
- **L-02 — one bite becomes the box.** Reader wording: "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop." Units: LEU-002. Source: bank-02 BM-3. Grade: lived. Scope: evening moderation allowance that empties the box. Permitted: the craving once triggered carries the binge; loss of control is the trap signature. Prohibited: that anyone is condemned to helplessness permanently. Empirical limit: self-report of loss of control. Safety limit: no pathological labeling of the reader.
- **L-03 — the deserved reward.** Reader wording: "celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate." Units: LEU-003. Source: bank-09 LX-158. Grade: lived. Scope: stress, victory or ceremony paid in sweets. Permitted: dessert as self-payment for virtue is a community-named frame; the reward frame keeps the loop alive. Prohibited: that no one may ever enjoy sweet food. Empirical limit: community language sample. Safety limit: do not moralize sweetness as universally forbidden.
- **L-04 — binge, withdrawal, craving and cross-sensitization demonstrated with sugar as reinforcer.** Units: S-1. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: rats, intermittent 12-hour access, 10 percent sucrose. Permitted: a well-known scientific model shows sugar can be consumed in a binge and deprivation pattern that produces classic behavioral signs of dependence in animals. Prohibited: treating rat-model findings as proof of human sugar addiction; claiming universal not conditional effects. Empirical limit: translates only cautiously to humans. Safety limit: do not diagnose the reader.
- **L-05 — each binge re-triggers the reward signal.** Units: S-2. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: animal microdialysis, nucleus accumbens. Permitted: each hit in binge-like consumption re-triggers the reward circuit dopamine signal. Prohibited: implying measured human evidence of same magnitude; stating sugar equals stimulants or opiates. Empirical limit: magnitude smaller than drugs of abuse. Safety limit: magnitude language must stay small.
- **L-06 — the dip after the sugar is measured.** Units: S-3. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: naloxone-precipitated or deprivation-induced state in rats. Permitted: the deficit chemistry after sugar-binge cycles has been measured; the dip after the sugar is not imaginary. Prohibited: claiming humans undergo same measured neurochemical withdrawal; describing sugar withdrawal as a diagnosable human condition. Empirical limit: opiate-like, precipitated model. Safety limit: human withdrawal not a diagnosis.
- **L-07 — mild but well-defined.** Finding: the overall effect is described by the pro-addiction lab as mild but well-defined dependency. Units: S-4. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: rat model summary conclusion. Permitted: even the pro-addiction laboratory calls its measured effect mild; used to defuse fear without denying a grip. Prohibited: quoting as evidence of severe human dependence; overstating magnitude. Empirical limit: explicitly mild. Safety limit: defuse, do not frighten with magnitude.
- **L-08 — the schedule is the trap form.** Finding: ad-lib access does not produce dependency signs; intermittent schedule is critical. Units: S-9. Source: PMC4361030. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: control-group comparison, intermittent versus ad-lib. Permitted: intermittent restraint-then-release feeding is the schedule that manufactures the loop, echoing the human diet-binge yo-yo. Prohibited: claiming sugar eaten steadily ad-lib produces dependence in rodents; translating as moderation is safe for everyone. Empirical limit: animal schedule-driven effects. Safety limit: no personal prescription from schedule data.
- **L-09 — open question, honestly held.** Finding: little evidence to support sugar addiction in humans; animal bingeing occurs only with intermittent access and likely reflects access pattern not sugar neurochemistry. Units: S-11. Source: PMC5174153. Grade: CONTESTED. Scope: critical review perspective, not new data. Permitted: present whether sugar is addictive as an open scientific question; human evidence thin; both camps grant binge behaviors occur under intermittent access. Prohibited: asserting settled science either way; quoting either side as consensus. Empirical limit: perspective review. Safety limit: do not use contest to shame or to license the loop.
- **L-10 — not uniquely weak.** Finding: pooled prevalence of food-addiction-like criteria 14 percent adults and 12 percent children, similar to legal substances. Units: S-12. Source: PMC10561019. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: self-report scale across 281 studies, 36 countries. Permitted: measurable populations meet addiction-like criteria on validated tools; population-scale phenomenon. Prohibited: calling the reader diagnosed or addicted; implying formal diagnosis. Empirical limit: not a DSM diagnosis; self-report. Safety limit: no self-diagnosis language. Prevalence claim appears once, in Chapter 2.
- **L-11 — post-meal dip mechanism with honest boundary.** Finding: reactive pattern 2 to 5 hours after intake via late excessive second-phase insulin; symptoms can occur without glucose at or below 55. Units: S-21. Source: PMC7192270. Grade: MIXED. Scope: clinical review of post-meal glucose versus insulin physiology. Permitted: the mechanism for a slump after sweet loads is biologically real; the lift is insulin working and the same surge can produce a real after-dip. Prohibited: promising everyone measurable hypoglycemia after sugar; pathologizing ordinary readers. Empirical limit: everyday slumps often without measured low glucose; frequency in healthy non-diabetics contested. Safety limit: see SAFE-1; everyday swing is not clinical hypoglycemia.
- **L-12 — clinical threshold.** Finding: for people without diabetes, hypoglycemia is typically below 55, rare, tied to identified medical causes or medicines. Units: S-43. Source: MedlinePlus hypoglycemia. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: federal consumer-health definition. Permitted: the honest crash line is regulatory swing and postprandial experience, not clinical hypoglycemia. Prohibited: claiming ordinary sweet eaters reach clinically low glucose; telling a reader they are hypoglycemic. Empirical limit: definition, not a study; does not cover reactive window. Safety limit: see SAFE-1.
- **L-13 — clean baseline.** Finding: average body needs 60 to 65 grams glucose per day, manufactured from protein and glycerol; glycemia stays in physiological range without dietary carbohydrate. Units: S-22. Source: PMC3826507. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: physiology review grounded in classic starvation biochemistry. Permitted: glucose is not a nutrient to import spoon by spoon; the body runs its own supply line. Prohibited: claiming carbohydrates are poisonous; prescribing ketogenic diets; saying the brain needs no glucose. Empirical limit: supports baseline, not a diet prescription. Safety limit: no diet prescription.
- **L-14 — days-long hump, not lifelong fight.** Finding: animal experimental withdrawal converges; human preliminary support; scale-measured symptoms peak 2 to 5 days after cutting down then pass. Units: S-23. Source: PMC9786266. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: systematic review; human side preliminary and retrospective. Permitted: the first-days headache, irritability and grouchiness has a real model and scale; time-limited hump. Prohibited: telling a reader they withdraw medically like an opiate user; implying the hump never ends. Empirical limit: no human physiologic markers; self-report. Safety limit: promise small and brief; see SAFE-1.
- **L-15 — wanting, not liking, drives the loop.** Finding: wanting is generated by large robust dopamine systems; liking is mediated by smaller fragile systems and is not dopamine dependent. Units: S-32. Source: PMC5171207. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: founder synthesis of 25 years animal plus human work. Permitted: the loop runs on wanting manufactured by dopamine, not liking; explains finishing without enjoying. Prohibited: dopamine equals pleasure; equating craving with happiness. Empirical limit: psychological distinction, not proof sugar causes addiction. Safety limit: no brain-damage language.
- **L-16 — cues fire before the bite.** Finding: enhanced responding for sugar-associated cues after abstinence; human cue hyper-reactivity to pictures of high-calorie food in reward circuits. Units: S-7 plus S-27. Kept as two single-unit applications on cards: S-7 for lingering cue strength animal analogue; S-27 for human imaging cue reactivity. Grades: SUPPORTED each. Scope: rodents weeks after bingeing; human imaging largely obesity and binge-eating samples. Permitted S-7: pull back after time off and renewed cue strength has a demonstrated neural analogue. Permitted S-27: smell, wrapper and corner-shop trigger more than eating does. Prohibited: claiming abstinence makes humans drink more; asserting cue incubation proven in humans; claiming permanent receptor damage; generalizing obesity findings to every sweet eater. Empirical limit: interpretive labels kept in quotes by authors; cause versus consequence open. Safety limit: no fatalism.
- **L-17 — liver load, pressure and inflammation.** Finding: higher added sugars linked with higher heart and stroke risk; overload converts to fat, raises pressure and chronic inflammation; liquid sugar bypasses appetite control. Units: S-17. Source: Harvard Health summary of 110,000-person cohort plus guidelines. Grade: SUPPORTED as institutional summary. Scope: population-level. Permitted: lift is deferred load, not free energy. Prohibited: panic claims; presenting 17-teaspoon average as universal. Empirical limit: population causal language; teaspoon conversions approximate. Safety limit: sober stakes, no personal fate prediction.
- **L-18 — teeth as daily stake.** Finding: free sugars to plaque acid to decay; caries most common noncommunicable condition worldwide. Units: S-20. Source: WHO caries fact sheet. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: mechanism claim; fluoride and brushing mediate. Permitted: same treat feeds the loop and feeds acid against teeth. Prohibited: claiming caries inevitable from any exposure; claiming diet cures cavities. Empirical limit: mechanism, not systemic disease proof. Safety limit: concrete, non-moralized.
- **L-19 — one drink spends the day.** Finding: one 12-ounce cola holds 39 grams, almost 10 teaspoons. Units: S-46. Source: Harvard Chan Nutrition Source. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: single product example, label math. Permitted: one treated drink nearly spends the whole WHO daily budget at once. Prohibited: claiming one soda harms everyone medically. Empirical limit: conversion arithmetic. Safety limit: tangible dose, not scare arithmetic.
- **L-20 — bliss point engineering.** Finding: middle sweetness liked most; biggest hits avoid a distinct overriding flavor that tells the brain to stop; conscious effort in labs and meetings to get people hooked on convenient inexpensive foods. Units: V-01 plus V-03 plus V-06 applied singly by card. Source: Moskowitz and Moss via Cornucopia reprint. Grade: sourced industry reporting. Scope: processed-food design practice. Permitted: desire is manufactured by formula and mouthfeel, not discovered in the eater. Prohibited: attributing a single quote beyond its speaker. Empirical limit: journalistic account of industry sources. Safety limit: anger at trap, never at reader.
- **L-21 — vanishing density and hyperpalatable supply.** Finding: fast-melting foods read as nearly calorie-free so eating continues; 68 percent of supply hyperpalatable; combinations rarely in nature trigger reward and disrupt fullness. Units: V-26 plus V-57 applied singly. Source: Witherly via Moss; Fazzino via KU News. Grade: sourced. Scope: engineering principle; US supply estimate. Permitted: the food is built to be hard to stop and to trick fullness. Prohibited: claiming personal choice plays no part; naming individual brands as targets. Empirical limit: supply share is a study estimate. Safety limit: absolve and dignify the reader.
- **L-22 — suppression receipts.** Finding: 1965 industry-funded review discounted sucrose and heart links; Project 259 terminated without publishing after linking sucrose to triglycerides and possible bladder signals; dental strategy deflected from intake restriction. Units: V-32 plus V-35 plus V-43 applied singly. Source: PLOS Biology and PLOS Medicine analyses of internal documents. Grade: sourced peer-reviewed history. Scope: historical document analysis. Permitted: the reader was kept in the dark by design; confusion was profitable. Prohibited: inventing motives beyond what documents show; presenting allegations as court findings. Empirical limit: historical, not a claim about any current product. Safety limit: channel anger away from self-shame.
- **L-23 — hidden carriers and label history.** Finding: added sugars hide under many names; ride inside biscuits, chocolate, flavoured yoghurts, cereals and fizzy drinks; added-versus-total disclosure is recent. Units: S-48 plus S-62 plus S-75 applied singly. Source: Harvard Chan; NHS; Harvard label history. Grade: SUPPORTED each. Scope: labeling and carrier inventory. Permitted: counting or careful shopping fails because the fuel is not where the reader thinks it is. Prohibited: claiming all non-sucrose sweeteners harmful in every way. Empirical limit: illustrative lists, not exhaustive. Safety limit: no brand shaming.
- **L-24 — substitution does not exit the loop.** Finding: reducing free sugars should be done without non-sugar sweeteners as the route; gut-brain calorie sensing rewards sucrose beyond sweetness alone. Units: S-69 plus S-40 applied singly. Source: WHO healthy-diet fact sheet; Westwater review of Domingos animal work. Grade: SUPPORTED each. Scope: guidance strategy; rodent optogenetics. Permitted: diet-fare swaps keep the sweetness relation alive; sweetness replacement alone leaves the engine running. Prohibited: claiming sweeteners toxic or addictive; claiming human neuron proof. Empirical limit: guidance conditional; animal circuits untested in addiction paradigms. Safety limit: no sweetener panic.
