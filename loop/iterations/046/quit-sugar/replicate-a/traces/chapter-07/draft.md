Chapter 7
THE LOOP

Night finds you in the kitchen with the light off and the fridge door open, and you believe the dark covers you, because dark rooms seem to keep secrets. Cold air sits on your ankles. The bulb throws a white square on the lino. The packet stands where you left it for morning, torn at the corner, folded over to look untouched. You told yourself one square. The hand takes three. The mouth fills before the mind arrives. The foil whispers. The smell rises before the bite lands on the tongue, sweet and warm and certain, and you believe the smell proves hunger, because hunger is supposed to smell like this. The smell proves nothing about hunger. It proves the call knows its hour.
You believe no one must know about this hour, because morning eyes will count, and counting shames. You push the wrappers deep under peelings so the bin looks innocent. You fold the empty packet small and press it flat. You rinse the fingers and drink water to clear the coating. You stand in the dark chewing faster than tasting, finishing past enjoyment to finish the packet because an open packet rings louder than a finished one. You believe finishing proves the craving was strong, because strength explains speed. Finishing proves the craving outlived the liking by a long road.
You believe this night proves you need BAD SUGAR, because need talks loudest at midnight, and midnight feels like truth. I have stood in that exact cold square with the same folded packet and the same hidden wrappers and the same mouth moving after pleasure left, and you believe your midnight runs harder than mine, because shame keeps private accounts. Mine ran the same. We all learned the same raid. We all woke the same house without waking it.
You have felt a plain meal close quiet and leave the hand forgetting the kitchen, and you believe that quiet belongs to other days, because midnight shouts louder than noon in memory. Hold both hours side by side with me and let them judge each other. Noon closed with warmth under the ribs and attention turning outward. Midnight closed with coating on the tongue and attention turning inward toward hiding. You believe both closes satisfy in their way, because both stop the hand for a night. Only one stops the calling. The other feeds it.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a dark kitchen, a drawer that rings, a whisper turned to shouting, and an actor paid to rescue you.

The Packet Finished Past Taste

You know the raid by heart, and you believe its second half still holds pleasure, because the hand would not move without pay. Watch the second half with cooler eyes and see where pleasure left the room. The first bite lands loud on the tongue. Sweetness floods. The head lifts for a minute on noise. The second bite lands less loud. The third lands duller. By the middle of the bar the tongue reports coating more than taste. The jaw works from habit. The belly sends fullness without rest. The hand keeps moving. You believe the hand keeps moving toward enjoyment, because movement looks like purpose. The hand keeps moving away from the low the first bites re-lit.
You tell me, "the craving proves I need it," and I take the sentence with care, because craving feels like need in the body, insistent and personal and true. You believe need explains why you finish what you no longer enjoy, because need does not ask about enjoyment. Look at what need truly does when the body asks. Hunger accepts bread and soup and apples gladly and closes quiet after supper. Hunger never demands one wrapper be finished past tasting in the dark. Hunger never hides its wrappers for morning. What finishes past enjoyment is not hunger. It is want outliving liking by hours.
Ask your own midnight as witness, and answer inwardly with the candour you already showed me. If the last third of the packet gave pleasure, why did you chew faster as pleasure fell. If the craving measured bodily need, why did need grow sharper with every fed bite through the week instead of settling. If BAD SUGAR truly comforted, why did comfort never arrive, only a brief muting of the hollow followed by a louder hollow. There is one plain answer that fits all three, and you feel its weight without any scolding from me. Each hit re-lights a small signal in the wanting part of the mind, brief and chemical and real, then leaves a small dip behind it. You never rose above the calm of a person who never started. You only climbed briefly toward it, then paid for the climb.
You know the excuses the hand whispers to keep moving, and you believe waste forbids leaving squares behind, because good children clear plates. Listen to what waste truly wastes in that hour. The tongue already reports nothing. The belly already holds too much. The bin already waits for hiding. Leaving two squares saves nothing and leaving nothing saves no money already spent. Finishing past enjoyment wastes comfort, sleep and a clean waking, to save a wrapper already bought. You believe leaving would nag all night, because open packets nagged before. Open packets nagged because the bell was fed daily. Unfed bells thin. Full bellies sleep.
You know the morning count that follows the raid, and you believe the count proves lack of character, because numbers feel like judgment. Count differently with me and see what the numbers truly count. First squares pleased briefly. Middle squares pleased less. Last squares pleased nothing and left coating and a jaw aching from habit. The packet did not give ten equal pleasures. It gave one loud minute followed by duller minutes that re-lit want while killing liking. You believe ten squares must hold ten pleasures, because ten bought squares look like ten gifts. They hold one brief muting followed by nine payments toward the next hollow.
You know tight shoes at evening when the laces cut all day, and you believe the taking off proves the shoes give pleasure, because relief floods warm. The evening raid works the same way in the mouth. The day of small doses tightens the want. The night dose loosens it for minutes. Relief floods and calls itself pleasure. The shoes never gave. They only lent quiet from the ache they kept.
The fact is the tongue stops reporting long before the packet ends, but the want keeps ordering. That is the sugar trap. Liking dies early in the packet. Wanting walks on to the end and washes its hands of taste.
You believe seeing this clearly must make the night shameful, because clear seeing often shames. Let clear seeing do the opposite here. You were never weak in that kitchen. You were answering a manufactured want with a tired body at midnight, schooled for years to read want as need. Shame belongs to the schooling, never to you.

The Whisper Turned To A Shout

Morning food speaks low, and you believe low speech means weak pleasure, because loud things win attention in this house. Watch how loudness is made and what it means. An apple speaks in chew and juice and round sweetness that builds slow and leaves quiet. Bread speaks in warmth and substance. Soup speaks in salt and depth. The body hears these low voices plainly when no louder voice talks over them. They whisper, and the whisper is enough, because the ear was built for whispers.
BAD SUGAR does not whisper. It shouts into the same ear with a fast concentrated dose, sweetness plus fat plus melt arriving together at speed, and you believe shouting proves greater pleasure, because the ear turns fastest toward shouting. Shouting proves volume and speed, never depth. The shout hits the wanting part hard and fast, re-triggering its small signal every time, then drops fast and leaves the ear ringing for more. Laboratories still argue what to call all this, and you do not need them to agree to hear the difference in your own mouth across one week.
You believe the shout must mean the body prefers it, because bodies chase what serves them. Bodies chase what is loudest only while loudness trains them. See the child who never learned the shout and is offered both tables. The child takes the apple with noise and running feet and juice on the chin and asks for another apple. No schooling yet tells the child that joy must arrive in foil. The shout had to be taught, packet by packet, ad by ad, bakery by bakery, until the ear mistook loud for good.
You know speed changes everything in the mouth, and you believe sweetness is sweetness wherever it sits, because the tongue names one word for all sweet. Watch the same sweetness arrive at two speeds and hear the different answer. Sweetness chewed slow inside whole fruit arrives with fibre and water and pause, building round and leaving quiet that holds for hours. The same weight of sweetness drunk fast or melted fast arrives as one loud rush, spiking want hard, then dropping hard toward hollow and pictures of more. You believe faster must mean better served, because fast service pleases in shops. In the mouth fast means shouted, and shouted means want re-lit harder while liking fades faster. The dose makes the shout. The rate makes the trap sing.
We lived with shouting so long we called shouting taste, and you believe long training must reflect nature, because nature feels fixed. Training reflects repetition, and repetition can be undone by leaving. Eat plain through steady days in thought and hear the volume change without any lecture. The whisper returns first as thin, then as clear, then as sufficient. The shout, remembered from the drawer, sounds thinner each time it is not answered with steps. The ear re-learns its first language. The mouth remembers whispers carry meals home.
You believe one loud evening must reset the ear back to shouting for good, because past evenings seemed to undo weeks. One evening rings loud for a night, then fades when not fed again, because the small signal is small and the dip is small and both pass on their own account. What kept the ear tuned to shouting was never one evening. It was the daily answering, the morning top-up and the afternoon top-up and the evening raid feeding the next morning hollow. Stop answering daily and the shouting loses its schooling. The whisper holds the table.
You know the music of ordinary meals returning, and you believe such music must be talked into liking, because past liking needed talk all day. This music talks in the mouth without talk. Soup tastes salt and deep again. Bread tastes wheaten and warm. Water tastes cold and clean after supper. Hunger seasons all of it the way light seasons a room, present without comment, making colour true. You believe flatness proved plain food lacks flavour, because the tongue seemed to report so across bright years. The tongue reported comparison with the last bar, not flavour. Lift the last bar and flavour stands where it always stood.
It never was a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift. It was a loud minute that borrowed quiet from the hour after and called the borrowing a gift.

The Doorbell In The Drawer

You know the drawer by heart, and you believe its pull proves hunger lives in the drawer, because the hand moves toward it before thought finishes. Watch the pull with cooler eyes and see where it truly starts. Three o'clock thins the light. Work thins. The drawer sits shut with chocolate inside, bought for others, kept for later, stored for safety. No smell escapes. No sound escapes. Yet passing the desk rings something in the chest. The hand slows. The mind pictures crack and melt in exact colour. The mouth waters before any bite. You believe watering proves the body asks, because bodies water for food. Bodies water for pictures schooling painted.
Think of a doorbell kept in a drawer, wired to your chest, and you believe the ringing means a visitor waits with news, because bells mean visitors. The stored bar rings every time you pass, not because the drawer calls, but because years wired the bell to the place and the hour and the paper twist. The hand answers because hands answer bells. Hunger never rang like that. Hunger sits low and wordless under the ribs, patient across minutes, naming no brand and no shelf, eased by many foods. The bell names exactly. It says the corner shop. It says the machine on the second floor. It says the dark cookies call me. Schooling names. Hunger does not.
Try the observation in lived detail, enacted, not described as a task. You walk the shop aisle in thought with supper already eaten to satisfaction. The belly holds quiet. The shelves stand bright. Then the wrapper catches the eye, gold at the end cap, and the smell from the bakery corner reaches before any tasting, warm and sweet and thick. Notice what fires first and strongest. Not the chew. Not the swallow. The seeing and the smelling fire before the bite, pictures and words arriving with urgency and bargaining about just one and starting again tomorrow. You lift the packet and turn it in the hand. The bell rings loud. You set the packet back and walk on toward bread and fish and fruit. Notice what thins behind you with steps. The bell thins with distance. Hunger walks on unchanged, still quiet from supper. The bell was never hunger. It was the call riding on sight and smell.
You know the strange return after steady days, and you believe it proves the bell grows stronger without feeding, because the ring sounds loud on the fourth day. Hear the return plainly and it proves the opposite of what fear says. After days without loud doses the ear hears whispers again, so one passing smell stands out sharp against quiet, the way one bell stands out in a silent street. You believe sharpness means strength returning, because sharp things cut. Sharpness here means contrast, not power. The bell rings bright for a minute against quiet, then thins fast when not answered, because no daily feeding stands behind it. The street stays silent. The bell learns silence.
You believe the bell must be answered or it will ring forever louder, because unanswered bells in houses ring on. This bell rings different. Answered daily, it learns the house answers and rings harder at its hours, wiring deeper with every feeding. Unanswered daily, it rings thin and less often, because the small signal behind it is small and the schooling behind it fades without feeding. We all feared the ringing would grow past bearing. It grows past bearing only when fed.
You know the evening raid already taught this, and you believe the raid taught lack of control, because control broke nightly. The raid taught cue power, not character. Wrapper and smell and hour and hiding place fired before tasting began, and tasting mattered less with every square while firing mattered more. Once you see firing as wiring, not wanting as nature, the drawer loses its office. It holds paper and twist. It holds no orders.
You believe safety lies in keeping no sweet in reach while keeping the thought warm, because distance looks like care. Distance without seeing only moves the bell to another drawer. Kept sweets ring where they sit, and avoided streets ring when remembered. Safety lies in seeing the bell as wiring wherever it rings, in drawer or aisle or celebration air, and hearing it thin with every unanswered passing. The hand learns new orders from seeing, not from distance.
The bell rings in the drawer. You do not live in the drawer.

The Paid Actor

You know the rescue by heart, and you believe rescue proves friendship, because rescue arrives exactly when the hollow aches. Mid-afternoon drags. Head thickens. Temper shortens. Hands slow on the keys. The bar breaks with its clean crack. Sweetness floods. The hollow mutes for minutes. Shoulders drop. Breath comes easier. The mind says friend. You believe the friend deserves thanks, because thanks follow rescue. Watch the rescue from the wings and see who hired the actor.
Think of a paid actor hired to play the hero in your afternoon, and you believe the performance proves the man, because tears come easily at good acting. The hit arrives like relief because it mutes for minutes the very dip the last hits laid down. It pushes you in, then pulls you out for a minute, then bows while you clap. The script brings you back daily for another take, because the dip returns on its own account and wears the face of need. The rescuer and the perpetrator share one dressing room. Once seen, the performance cannot be unseen.
We paid this actor daily with coin and health and midnight hours, and you believe payment proves value received, because purses open only for worth. Purses open for schooling too, and schooling billed us for our own muting. See the accounts plain across one week in thought. Monday pays for Friday hollow. Tuesday pays for Monday hollow. Each day rescues the last and creates the next. The non-eater pays nothing and holds no hollow to rescue. Which of the two stands richer at Sunday night. The one who never bought the rescue because no dip stood to be muted.
You tell me BAD SUGAR steadies your nerves and lifts your mood and helps you face people, and I hear real nerves and real low mood in the words, because afternoons press hard on a working adult. You believe lifting proves giving, because lifting feels like gift. Notice what lifting truly lifts across months. After bright years the nerves sit shorter between doses, the mood dips lower between packets, the patience thins toward evening, and each rescue lifts less high and for fewer minutes while demanding sooner return. A giver who leaves you thinner each visit is not giving. A lender at hard interest is collecting. The fact is the lift borrows from the next hour and charges the next hour for the loan, and the loan book grows while the relief shrinks.
You know the actor plays two houses, noon and midnight, and you believe the two show different faces, because noon wears hurry and midnight wears secrecy. Watch both shows from the wings and see one script. At noon the actor plays pick-me-up against the desk hollow, muting the dip from the morning dose. At midnight the actor plays comfort against the day hollow, muting the dip from the noon doses. Both houses clap for minutes. Both houses empty into a deeper hollow. You believe two different needs must live here, because hours feel different in the body. One dip wears two clocks. One actor takes two bows.
You believe such talk must mean your brain is damaged for good, because damage talk fills the air around sweets. Nothing here is broken beyond mending. The effect the most worried laboratories can measure, even when they feed sweetness in the most trapping schedule they can devise, stays small and brief and well defined, a mild dependency beside the hard drugs that frighten rightly. I tell you this not to keep you in, but to shrink the fight you fear. The physical part is a small hollow and a few days of grouch, passing on its own account. The loud part is the schooled want, and schooling can be left the way any taught lesson is left, by seeing through it once and not feeding it after.
You believe the actor must be mourned when the run ends, because long runs earn affection even from clear-eyed watchers. Mourn nothing here. The afternoons you loved held sun and rest and talk and music and a paused hour that pleased on its own account. The actor only rode along and billed the hour for his riding. Keep the sun. Keep the pause. Keep the talk. Let the rider walk. The hour plays better without his shouting from the wings.
Do not change from fear of what sweets do to the body. Change from relief that you never need another rescue. Fear would only hire another actor. Relief closes the theatre.
The actor bows. You leave before the next take.

The Small Dip And The Loud Want

You came to this chapter braced for a hard physical fight, and you believe bracing proves the fight runs hard, because adults brace only for real blows. Lay down the bracing with me and measure the true sizes. The body part sits small. A low hollow after the sweet hour. A headache and short temper across a few days when daily doses stop, passing without ceremony while real meals hold the line. The body keeps its own supply running from ordinary food across the day, holding its range without spooned refills, which is why hunger quiets after supper and stays quiet for hours. Small dips pass. Bodies steady themselves the way water clears after stirring.
The loud part sits elsewhere and runs the show. The brainwashing voice that calls the dip need and the bell need and the actor friend. The voice that says just one will prove control and starting again tomorrow will settle accounts. The voice that turns a smell into orders and a wrapper into a bell. You believe the voice sounds like yourself, because it speaks in your first person. It speaks in schooling first person, installed bite by bite and ad by ad and bakery by bakery, and its loudness proves repetition, never truth.
You believe loudness must mean strength that only strain can meet, because loud enemies in life need muscle. This loudness needs only seeing. See the dip as small and passing. See the want as taught and fading when unfed. See the rescue as the loan shark dressed as friend. Once seen, nothing must be fought with clenched hands. The small starves on its own when the big is understood. Understanding does the work that effort never did.
You are the analytical reader who wants the mechanism straight, and you believe straight talk must bring a hard sentence, because straight talk in clinics often does. Here straight talk brings a light sentence. The dip after the sugar is measured and real, not imaginary, and it stays mild and brief beside the noise schooling built on top of it. Wanting runs on a large loud system built to chase. Liking runs on a smaller quieter system that tires fast in a packet. That mismatch explains every midnight finish without joy. I lay this plainly not to frighten you into moving, because frightened movers hire new actors. The method takes care of the small while you take care of the seeing.
We fell into this loop together across years, and you will walk out of it the same way understanding walks, not marching, simply seeing and not answering. You have already felt plain meals close quiet and fruit taste round after loudness leaves. Hold those hours as your own evidence that appetite tells truth and the call repeats a lesson. The lesson ends where seeing begins.
The loop fed on belief. Belief now starves the loop.
