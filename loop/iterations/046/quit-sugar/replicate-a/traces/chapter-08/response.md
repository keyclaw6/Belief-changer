Chapter 8
THE WRONG METHOD

Your phone still keeps the count. Three apps with three different colours, all opened with hope in January, all left open in the background by March. One counts grams. One counts days without. One counts days with, as if numbers would scold you into sense. The kitchen drawer still keeps the paper version. A folded diet sheet with the sweet column circled in red. A curfew note in your own hand, taped inside the cupboard door where only you would see it. No sweets after seven. No sweets on weekdays. No sweets from the machine on the second floor. The calendar on the fridge still shows the white-knuckle weeks, stars for clean days, crosses for bad evenings, arrows pointing to Monday with the words start again written so hard the pen cut the paper.
You believe that drawer proves you lack character, because honest people do not need curfews for food, and you needed four. I have sat with that same drawer and that same phone and that same Monday handwriting, and you believe my drawer looked tidier than yours, because shame always assumes tidier drawers elsewhere. Mine looked the same. We all kept the same drawer. We all wrote the same Monday promise in the same hard pen.
You know the week by heart, and you believe its shape proves you cannot be trusted, because the shape never changed no matter how hard you meant it. Monday holds clean and bright, the fridge full of plain food, the jaw set, the voice loud in the head about this time. Tuesday holds, thinner. Wednesday thins toward evening, the pictures of the drawer growing louder with every hour unfed. Thursday breaks at three o'clock beside the same desk and the same drawer and the same sentence about just one to get through. Night finishes the packet past enjoyment, exactly the midnight raid you already know. Friday wakes heavy with the count restarted. You believe breaking on Thursday proves Thursday reveals your true self, because failure feels like confession. Thursday reveals the method, not the self.
You have felt plain meals close quiet and leave the hand forgetting the kitchen, and you have felt the midnight packet close loud and leave the hand listening for more, and you believe both closes live side by side because you are two people, one good on Monday, one weak on Thursday. You are one person under two methods. Monday used understanding. Thursday used strain. Strain lost. Strain always loses, because strain feeds what it claims to starve.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a drawer of counts and curfews, a white-knuckle week, a fear with two ropes, and the will that proves you can walk out.

*You never failed to stop; the strain method failed you, and the will you showed in all that effort is exactly what walks you out.*

The Drawer Full Of Defeats

Open the drawer with me and read it differently, and you believe reading differently excuses failure, because failure in this house has always meant blame. Reading differently assigns blame where it belongs. Look at the first sheet. A diet that named BAD SUGAR poison on Monday and paid it as reward on Saturday. Look at the second. A curfew that forbade sweets after seven and moved the raid to half past six, then to the car, then to the shop on the way home. Look at the phone. A counter that turned each clean day into a precious streak and each broken evening into a smashed streak, so one evening meant the whole stair fell and the packet might as well be finished. You believe the smashed streak proves one slip proves ruin, because numbers feel like law. Numbers were the method talking, not law.
Ask what every sheet in that drawer asked you to do, and answer plainly without scolding yourself. Every sheet asked you to want BAD SUGAR just as much and have it just as rarely, to hold the same belief that it gives you a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift and then to clench against it with a timetable. No sheet touched the belief. Every sheet built its whole house on clenching. That is the Willpower Method.
You tell me, "I failed, so I am weak, and I will fail again," and I take the sentence with both hands, because it carries years of Thursday nights and morning counts and hidden wrappers. You believe weak explains why the curfew broke, because breaking looks like weakness from the outside. Watch breaking from the inside and weakness explains nothing. You held Monday through Wednesday on nothing but clench while the wanting part shouted its small signal louder every hour. You walked past the shop twice without buying. You left squares on Thursday afternoon while the mouth watered. You lasted longer than any uninterested person would last around something they truly loved. Weak people do not do that. Wilful people do that, and wilful people fail on strain for one reason only. Strain cannot change want.
You believe strain should work because strain works on heavy doors and long hills, because muscle answers pushing in honest work. This was never honest pushing work. This was holding the breath around a bell that rings harder the longer it is held against. The drawer proves you pushed. The drawer proves pushing cannot unring a bell. The fact is the method asked for clench and called clench the cure, and clench kept the belief alive underneath while making each dose more precious by forbidding it.
You know the braggers and the moaners from those same weeks, and you believe their voices prove the method works for others, because other voices always sound surer than your own at midnight. One voice at work said cutting down was simple and counted his two squares aloud like medals. One voice at home said nothing works and listed every diet as proof the hole has no bottom. Listen to both voices with cooler ears and hear the same strain underneath. The bragger white-knuckled through his week one and called his sore hands success. The moaner white-knuckled through hers and called her sore hands failure. Both kept the same thought warm, that BAD SUGAR gives something real and must be held off by force. Both woke listening for more. Neither walked out. Their hardship proves their method, never your nature.
We kept those drawers together across years, and you believe keeping them together proves we all share a flaw, because shared failure looks like shared nature. We share a schooled belief, never a flaw. We all learned that sweets lift and soothe and pay, and we all learned that the way to leave them is to forbid and count and hold the breath. Schooling can be left in an hour. Nature cannot. That the drawer fills in every house proves the schooling, not the house.

Strain Builds What It Claims To Break

You believe forbidding thins want, because forbidding thins hands around hot plates and feet near cliff edges. Forbidding heats want around BAD SUGAR because the heat is re-lit want, not wisdom about burns. Watch the schedule your curfews built and see the loop being manufactured week by week. Monday to Friday clean and counted, jaw set, pictures growing brighter with every unfed hour. Saturday freed by permission, the first dose landing loud after five days of pictures, re-lighting the small wanting signal hard, muting the hollow for minutes, then dropping toward a louder hollow that asks for Sunday to finish the job. Monday opens clean again with shame added to want. You believe Saturday proves you cannot hold freedom, because Saturday undoes Monday in hours. Saturday proves the schedule undoes Monday by design.
The laboratories found the same shape when they fed sweetness in the most trapping schedule they could devise, and you do not need their words to trust your own Thursdays. Fed steadily without break, without curfew, without release day, the animals showed no such chasing signs. Fed in bursts with hungry gaps between, restraint then release, curfew then permission, they chased and binged and paced the cage. The schedule made the chasing. I tell you this not as human proof from rats, because rats are not readers and cages are not kitchens. I tell you this because your drawer ran the same schedule on purpose for years, restraint then release, Monday then Saturday, and your chasing followed the same curve. That is the sugar trap.
Ask your own calendar as witness, and answer inwardly with the candour you already showed me. If clean weeks proved control, why did control collapse fastest after the longest clean run. If release days proved kindness to yourself, why did kindness always end past enjoyment with coating on the tongue and hiding for morning. If counting proved care, why did care always end in smashing the stair and finishing the packet because the stair already fell. There is one plain answer that fits all three without any scolding from me. Intermittent forbidding makes each hit louder by making each gap emptier, re-triggering want harder while liking dies earlier in the packet. Steady forbidding would be hard. Burst forbidding is harder, because every release re-teaches the bell that ringing buys steps after waiting.
You believe this means steady eating of BAD SUGAR would be safer than curfews, because steady sounds calmer than bursting. Steady would keep the same belief alive by another door, and I do not point you to that door. The point is never which schedule of doses harms less. The point is the schedule of forbidding manufactures the very chasing it claims to cure, while leaving the belief untouched that the dose gives something real. Change the belief and the schedule loses its office. Keep the belief and every schedule, strict or loose, feeds the loop by feeding pictures between doses.
It is like telling yourself you can jump off the cliff as long as you do not fall more than a few metres.
You know the app counts already taught this, and you believe counts must help because pilots use counts and nurses use counts, and counts guide honest work. Counts guide honest work where the work is doing. Here the work is seeing, and counts kept the eyes on streaks while the belief ran the house underneath. A clean day counted as virtue. A broken evening counted as ruin. Ruin licensed finishing. Finishing licensed hiding. Hiding licensed Monday shame that needed soothing by Saturday. The counter never counted the belief once. It counted days while the loop counted doses.
The fact is restraint then release built your Thursdays as surely as hunger builds supper. Remove the belief that the dose gives, and restraint has nothing to hold against. Keep the belief and restraint only fattens release.

The Fear That Holds Both Ends

You believe fear proves you see clearly, because fear arrives with pictures of the future that feel like foresight. Fear here proves the trap holds both ropes and pulls whichever you touch. Name both ropes with me and watch one hand holding both.
The first rope is fear of failing again. You picture Thursday returning, the packet finished past taste, the morning count restarted, the drawer filling with one more sheet. You picture colleagues seeing. You picture the voice saying there goes the Monday promiser again. You believe this fear must keep you sharp, because fear keeps hands sharp near saws. Here fear keeps hands shaking around bells. The sharper the fear of breaking, the more precious each clean hour feels, the louder the pictures grow between doses, the harder the small signal rings when three o'clock thins the light. Fear of failing fattens failing, because strain grows with fear and strain builds release.
The second rope is fear of succeeding. You picture a birthday table with no sweet in your hand while others lift theirs. You picture evenings with no packet to close the day. You picture a life where nothing ever lifts or soothes or pays again, flat and grey and dutiful, lived by someone thinner but duller than you. You believe this fear proves sweets truly give, because loss would not ache if nothing were lost. Loss aches here because the belief still says something real would be lost. The belief aches, not the future. Every sweet you ever loved rode on sun and rest and talk and music and pause, and the sweet billed the hour for its riding. Take the rider off the bill and the hour stands. Fear of succeeding mourns a friend that was never a friend. It mourns the paid actor after the run ends.
Ask which fear to obey, and hear the trap answer both ways with one mouth. If you lean toward walking out, the first rope pulls, remember Thursday, you will fail again, better not start. If you lean toward staying, the second rope pulls, remember the birthday, life will be empty, better not stop. You believe two opposite fears must prove two real dangers, because opposite pulls in life usually mean a middle path between cliffs. Here both pulls come from one hand. Both say the same sentence underneath, that BAD SUGAR gives something real and leaving it means loss, whether by failing to leave or by leaving into grey. One hand. One sentence. One lie.
You tell me, "most people handle it fine, so the danger must be me," and I hear how lonely Thursdays made that sentence feel true. You believe normal tables prove normal handling, because supermarket aisles look calm and office tables look easy. Calm aisles hide the same drawer in other houses. Easy tables hide the same night kitchens after the plates are cleared. You see handling where you see public hours. You do not see the private hours that match yours square for square. Even where handling looks real, it proves nothing about you. If others walk near the edge without falling, their footing does not prove you are broken for falling where the ground was greased for you from childhood by bakery smiles and payday candy and ads that taught the ear to mistake loud for good.
You were told you would carry this tug for every day from now on.
You believe a tug felt daily must be obeyed daily, because daily pains in life mean daily care. This tug means daily schooling, and schooling ends where seeing begins. The tug is not a life sentence. It is a taught bell thinning with every unanswered passing, a small dip passing on its own account, a loud want fading when unfed. Both ropes loosen the hour the belief loosens. Both ropes were always held by the same hand, and the hand opens when the belief opens.
The fact is fear of failing and fear of succeeding are one fear wearing two masks, the fear that loss is real. Loss is not real. There is nothing to lose and everything to see.

Wilful, Not Weak

You believe your history sorts you into a type, the sweet-toothed, the hooked, the one with no stop button, the one who finishes past enjoyment while others leave squares. You say it almost with tenderness now, as if naming the type explains Thursdays and softens them. I hear the tenderness and I answer it with care, because types feel like shelter after years of blame. Watch the type with cooler eyes and see which came first, the type or the loop.
Did you finish packets before you learned packets, or after years of curfews and releases taught the bell to ring louder between doses. Did you hide wrappers before schooling taught hiding as the price of breaking, or after the counter turned one evening into ruin that might as well be finished. Did you call yourself hooked before the midnight raid taught the word, or after the raid needed a name that was not your name. You believe the type explains the loop, because types explain feathers and fur. Here the loop explains the type. The traits you call nature are the results of years inside, not the cause of entering. Chasing follows burst schedules. Hiding follows counting. Finishing follows forbidding that made each packet more precious. Take the schedule away and the traits thin with it, because they were never bone. They were wiring.
You tell me you have no stop button, and I ask you to look at supper eaten to satisfaction, where the hand forgets the kitchen for hours without orders. You tell me sweets call you by name, the dark cookies call me in the drawer at three, my body was saying eat cake at midnight, and I ask you to hear schooling naming shelves and hours while hunger sits low and wordless and names nothing. You tell me foggy and irritable and emotional proves need, thick head on clean mornings proves the body cannot run without, and I ask you to mark when those states arrive and when they leave. They arrive after the last dose wears off and leave when plain meals hold the line across steady days. Need that is fed and grows sharper with feeding is not need. It is want re-lit.
Even if you were the most hooked reader who ever held this book, the method still frees you the same way, and you believe such grace must have a catch, because past methods always charged extra for hard cases. There is no catch here and no extra stair for hard cases. The belief is one belief in every house, that BAD SUGAR gives a genuine lift and comfort, and the seeing is one seeing. Hard cases only prove more strain behind them, longer drawers, louder bells, more Thursdays survived on clench. More strain proves more will, never less worth. I make you this promise with the same certainty I bring to every page. What kept you in was never weakness. What walks you out is seeing, and seeing asks no type and pays no regard to labels.
You believe the first days without loud doses must be dreadful, because past first days felt dreadful under strain, headaches behind the eyes, short temper at small noises, grouch across evenings that seemed to prove the body demands. Hear those days plainly and they prove the opposite of what strain said. The headache and the short temper and the grouch have a real small shape, brief and well defined, rising across two to five days after daily hits stop, then thinning and passing while real food holds the line. I do not call this a grand withdrawal and I do not dress it as danger. It stays small and brief beside the noise schooling built on top of it. You walk out easily, at once, and for good.
You believe small must mean imaginary, because small things in life are often waved away. Small here means passable, not imaginary. The dip after the sweet hour is real. The few days of edge are real. Real and small can be walked through without ceremony, the way water clears after stirring, the way a bell thins with distance. The method takes care of the small while you take care of the seeing. Fear would only hire another actor. Seeing closes the theatre.
The fact is it takes a strong will to persist for years in something that goes against all your instincts, that leaves coating and hiding and heavy mornings. Weakness quits early. You stayed. You counted. You restarted. You clenched through white-knuckle week one more times than any uninterested person would. That will is not your enemy. That will is your evidence.

If This Is So Easy

You have followed me this far with the drawer open, and now the strongest objection rises, and you believe voicing it breaks trust, because good readers are not supposed to doubt a kind guide. Doubt here builds trust when voiced plain. Speak it plainly with me.
"If leaving is seeing, why did no one say so before, and why do I still feel the pull while reading. If this is so easy, why did my Thursdays run so hard. Easy is for light cases. I am not a light case."
I welcome the objection because it carries the whole brainwashing in one mouthful, and you believe weight proves truth, because heavy doubts feel truer than light answers. Weight here proves repetition, never truth. No one said so before because every voice around you spoke from inside the same schooling, the apps that count streaks, the sheets that forbid and release, the talk that calls Thursdays character. They never touched the belief because they never saw the belief. They saw hands and mouths and blamed hands and mouths. I show you the belief because I stood where you stand and left by seeing, and I have watched others leave the same way without ceremony.
You believe feeling the pull while reading disproves seeing, because cures in life remove pain on contact. Seeing removes belief on contact, and the bell thins after, not before. The drawer still rings while you read because years wired it to ring at its hours, and one hour of seeing does not unwire years in a minute. Unanswered ringing thins. Answered ringing wires deeper. You have already felt plain meals close quiet and fruit taste round after loudness leaves. Hold those hours as your own evidence that appetite tells truth and the call repeats a lesson. The lesson ends where seeing holds.
You believe hard Thursdays must mean a hard exit, because hard illnesses need hard cures in honest medicine. This was never hard illness. It was a small dip plus a loud lesson, a mild hollow that passes and a schooled want that fades when unfed. Hard Thursdays came from hard strain against loud pictures, not from hard need in the body. Remove strain and keep seeing, and Thursdays lose their office. The exit matches the need, not the history. History was loud because strain was loud. Need stays small because need was always small.
You tell me, "I need something sweet to get me through, a pick-me-up at three, a treat when you deserve one after I have been good this week," and I hear real tiredness and real desert in the words, because afternoons press hard and weeks run long. You believe tired proves the need for a lift, because lifting feels like help. Notice what lifting truly lifted across months. It muted the hollow from the last dose for minutes and charged the next hour for the muting, leaving nerves shorter between doses and patience thinner toward evening. A helper who leaves you thinner each visit is not helping. The afternoon you loved held pause and air and talk. The hit only rode along and billed the hour.
The fact is easy does not mean light. Easy means the work is in the right place. The work was never in the hands. The work was always in the belief, and belief changes the hour it is seen.

Three Locks For The Walk Out

You stand now where seeing turns into walking, and you believe walking needs one more push of strain, because every past walk began with the jaw set and the breath held. This walk begins with locks, not pushes. Locks keep the old voice out while the bell thins. Set all three with me now, numbered the way the whole method numbers its promises, spoken plain, kept whole.
The old voice will come as a wobble, a passing picture at three o'clock, a sentence about just one, a morning after a thin night saying start again tomorrow. You will hear it in your first person because schooling speaks first person. Hear it as schooling and it loses its orders. Doubt after seeing is not new evidence. It is the bell ringing in an empty street because the street went quiet.

6. NEVER DOUBT THE DECISION ONCE IT IS MADE.
A wobble is the old voice, not new evidence.

While you read, other voices will press their sheets and counts and tips through every gap, well meant and wholly wrong for this exit. One will send a diet. One will send a curfew. One will send a story of strain survived and call it proof. You cannot walk out by two maps at once. One map says clench against want. This map says see through want. Two methods cannot drive one exit. Keep this map whole until the bell thins and your own steady days prove it.

7. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD WHILE YOU READ.
Two methods cannot drive one exit.

You will meet the voices of strain survived, the white-knuckle week one told as medal, the count told as virtue, the sore hands held up as wisdom. Pity those hands and do not borrow them. Their hardship proves the strain they used, never the ease you use. They left by clench and still listen for more. You leave by seeing and hear less daily. Let their medals stay theirs.

8. IGNORE ANYONE WHO STOPPED BY STRAIN AND STRUGGLE.
Their hardship proves their method, not yours.

Set the three and the drawer loses its office. The phone counts nothing because days are not streaks. The curfew curls on the cupboard door because hours are not crimes. The calendar star fades because steady days need no stars. What remains is one belief seen through and three locks kept, while the small dip passes and the loud want thins with every unanswered passing.

SUMMARY
- I now believe my past failures proved the strain method, not my character, and I see my strong will as evidence I can walk out.
- I now believe forbidding in bursts built my chasing, and I see fear of failing and fear of succeeding as one pull from the trap.
- I now believe the first-days edge is small and brief, and I see the loud want as schooling that fades when unfed.
