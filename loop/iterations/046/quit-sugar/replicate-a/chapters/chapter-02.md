Chapter 2
ALREADY CAUGHT

Morning light lies thin across the worktop and the kettle has just clicked off. The mug stands where it always stands. The cereal box leans open. You believe this is simply breakfast in a busy house, and I ask you to stay beside me while we look at it closely, without blame and without haste, because blame never showed anyone a door.

IN THIS CHAPTER: an ordinary Tuesday, a bright supermarket, and the body you stopped hearing.

*You did not choose the sweet day; the sweet day was laid around you.*

A Normal Tuesday

You rise and you know the way to the sweet tea before thought has started, and you believe the mug only wakes you. Two spoonfuls swirl and vanish. The children's hoops leave sweet milk and you drink the last inch from the small bowl while wiping the table, telling yourself a sip is tidiness. You believe sips taken on your feet leave no trace, and I understand the feeling, because seated meals feel real and footed moments feel free of record. Notice how the day takes its first sweet before appetite has lifted its head. The stomach is quiet. The routine is loud.

By ten the office kitchen fills with talk and steam. A box lies open near the microwave. A slab sits where yesterday's leaving cake still waits. You lift a square while answering a question about invoices, then a finger of biscuit while the printer warms, because the boxes sit at elbow height and the morning drags. You believe this is being easy company, and you are easy company, warm and unforced. Watch where ease points. Coffee without sweet gets nursed and judged. Cake gets taken while eyes stay on the screen. The hand learns the short path across the counter though the mind never voted for the trip.

Dinner at noon is proper food eaten with relish. I have no doubt you eat proper food, because nearly every sweet eater does. Thick soup. A filled roll. Leftovers reheated with care. Laughter with the people at the table. You believe a solid dinner should see you through, and any honest eater would expect the same. Then comes the small bright finish. A pudding pot from the canteen fridge. A square kept in the coat pocket. A stroll to the kiosk for something to round things off. Hunger is absent. A sense of incompleteness is present. You believe meals arrive with a sweet tail, and the belief feels like memory, because memory holds hundreds of tails and hardly one plain close.

Early afternoon thickens. The room warms. Email stacks. The throat feels dry and the eyes feel sandy. Someone says brew and chairs push back as one. The corner table holds the morning's remainder and a fresh packet someone fetched. You take a biscuit for the walk back, then another at the desk while the file loads, then a fizzy drink when the tin runs bare and the clock still shows hours. You say you eat sensibly apart from sugar and you carry a sweet tooth, and I take you at your word on both counts. You believe these fragments are too slight to shape a life, and singly they are slight. Together they form a sweet shadow that walks beside your real meals, asking for coins and minutes and attention at each corner. Seeing the shadow whole brings no charge. It brings proportion, and proportion begins to lift a weight you thought was character.

Homeward you stop at the corner shop for milk and leave with milk plus a bar. The bus crawls. The bar opens with one hand. Street lights come on early. You tell yourself travel bites are too small to matter, and the thought feels fair, because travel feels like pause rather than life. Yet pause repeats daily, and the pause has its own menu now, learned so well that the sight of the stop starts the saliva before thought. Hunger never rang. The hour rang.

Supper is cooked from scratch and eaten seated, and you take pride in it rightly. Chopped vegetables soften in the pan while rice or pasta boils beside them, and chicken or beans brings body you chose yourself with seasoning you measured. While the pan simmers you lift a biscuit from the tin to keep you going, then a spoon of ice cream while portioning, then a chocolate from the high shelf while calling everyone in. You believe a cook may taste, and a good cook does. Observe the direction of tasting. The stew gets a taste and a pinch of salt. The sweet gets taste without measure and without verdict. The table meal satisfies, plates empty, talk turns, and still the cupboard calls for a pudding though bellies feel full. Fullness closed one door. Habit knocks on another.

Later the lamp glows low and the remote rests near your knee. The packet opens with a soft tear. The first handful pleases. The fourth still pleases less. The packet empties because it stands open and the programme still runs. A second packet follows because the evening still stretches. You promise yourself a cleaner run next time, and the promise is sincere, because you are sincere after dark. Cups and papers gather quietly. Morning will find them pressed flat to seem fewer. I speak of this without a frown. We have all smoothed the signs of an evening that gave less than it pledged.

The school gate adds its own sweet tax, and you know it gate by gate though you never budgeted it. The small kiosk by the crossing sells iced buns and sour ropes at child's eye height. You buy one for the child and one for the wait, telling yourself waiting deserves company. You believe waiting bites are kindness, and kindness lives in you, because you walk extra miles for those you love. Notice how kindness always adds one extra for your own pocket. The playground bench teaches the same lesson on Saturdays. Flasks of sweet tea. Shared bags passed round. Crumbs brushed from coats before the drive home. You believe weekend treats stand apart from weekday routine, and the thought comforts, because weekends feel chosen. Count the weekends across a year and the apartness thins. The same hand opens the same bright wrap, weekday or Sunday alike, by the same bench, at the same hour, for the same unhungry mouth.

Night work adds a further shift you rarely mention, and you believe it hardly belongs in the tally because nights feel exceptional. The vending corner hums through the small hours. The drawer in the locker holds coins and a spare bar for the low stretch near three. You eat with eyes on monitors, tasting little, staying upright by the chew. You believe the chew keeps you watchful, and staying watchful matters, because people count on you awake. Observe what watchfulness receives. The chew passes. Drowsiness returns wearing the same coat. Another coin drops. Another wrap tears. By dawn the bin holds a small hill of foil that no hunger ordered. Exceptional nights repeat weekly, and weekly exception is routine wearing a mask.

You call the whole of it a sweet tooth. The phrase is gentle and I hear no lie in it. You mean you are otherwise steady, fond of real meals, able in most rooms of life. You believe steadiness elsewhere proves the sweet part is chosen lightly. I want you to hold the Tuesday whole for a moment, from tea to lamp light, and feel how little hunger led it. Hours led it. Sights led it. Places led it. Tins and drawers and kiosks and tables led it. A life led this way is not a set of free picks. It is a route walked daily while believing you ramble.

The Bright Supermarket

Come with me on Friday after work, because you believe the trolley obeys the list and the list proves freedom. Fluorescent light hums even. Wheels rattle. The smell of warm loaves drifts from the back. Your list holds milk, bread, eggs, soap. Your trolley already leans toward full though you have passed only six aisles. You believe plenty means choice, and plenty is kindly meant, because a full shelf feels generous.

Feel how pace changes near the bright rows. Biscuit towers rise at the corner and cake boxes with windows line the next stretch, while buns dusted white wait beside tubs stacked high behind misted doors. Bars hang where queues twist and bottles glow green and orange along the chiller. Morning flakes promise honeyed suns from tall boxes and small pots the colour of orchards crowd the low shelf. You believe the hand picks favourites freely, and favourites are real, shaped by years. Observe what freedom meets. Favourites sit at eye level. New packets jut at the turn. Small sizes whisper trial. Large sizes whisper saving. One for the work drawer. One for the car door. One for Friday film. One because the second costs little and thrift lives deep in you. The list stays short. The metal basket grows heavy.

Around you every trolley tells the same quiet story. A student weighs two tubs and takes both. A father lets a small hand add a roll of sweets and nods with tired love. An older couple compare two cakes and keep the larger. You believe mutual habit proves harmlessness, and the thought brings comfort, because shared ways feel tested. Notice the exact comfort at the belt. Goods glide. Beeps answer. No brow lifts. Payment taps. Bags swell with the same bright weight. Outside the doors dusk falls on car parks full of identical boots closing. When every load matches, no load feels strange. Sameness stops being evidence and becomes anaesthetic.

Trace one bag to its rooms, because you believe purchase and habit live apart. Biscuits settle in the tin. Bars slide beneath folded tea towels. Tubs find the cold drawer. Bottles line the inner door. Flakes take the high shelf. By eight the flat holds fresh posts at every wait. You believe stored food waits quietly, and tins do look quiet, because lids close. Listen for what quiet does. The closed tin knocks in thought during the kettle's minute. The cold drawer glows in thought during the late programme. The coat pocket weighs in thought on the bus. What looked like provision was preparation for three evenings of grazing that hunger never ordered. The shop does not sell one tea. It seeds a run.

Mark how smooth the seeding feels. Doors slide open unaided. Prices end in small change. Packets tear without tools. Sweetness arrives in seconds. Cost arrives in months. You believe smoothness means lightness, and rough things do warn louder. Here warning never sounds, so the slope feels like floor. A snare that hurt would teach caution. A snare that soothes teaches return, and return repeated feels like preference. That is why the bright aisles matter more than any single bar. They school the feet before the stomach speaks, then praise the schooling as normal.

Linger a breath at the packet fronts, because you believe bright claims guide wisely. Suns and barns and orchards smile from cardboard. Words speak of oven warmth and meadow milk and grandmother care. You believe kindly pictures mean kindly contents, and the hope is human, because humans trust faces. Turn the packet slowly in thought. Small print lists sweet under many soft names, tucked among starch and oils and syrups that keep the loop fed. You need no chemistry to feel the drift. The front sings plenty. The back whispers dose. Both live in one wrap, and the hand that trusted the front carries both home. The till kiosk near the exit adds a last nudge you know well. Small bars wait at child's reach beside the belt where queues crawl. You lift one for the road because the line drags and tempers fray. You believe a tiny last addition cannot sway a laden trolley, and arithmetic seems on your side. Notice how the laden trolley plus the tiny addition becomes the Tuesday you described, week upon week. Drops fill the pail precisely because each drop felt too small to count.

You fear this seeing might sour shopping for good. Let me set that fear down. Bread stays bread. Milk stays milk. Neighbours stay neighbours. Only the slope gets a name. When baskets match everywhere, the question moves from what flaw lives in you to what shape lives in the aisles. The second question can be answered without lowering your head. Warmth stays with you. Hard words stay for the slope and for the old teaching that fined you for sliding.

The Quiet Count

You believe your Tuesday is a private failing, and I want you to hear its echoes without taking a label and without accepting shame. The fact is about 14 percent of adults and about 12 percent of children tick enough boxes on food questionnaires to look like addiction on paper, at rates much like legal substances, and I say this not to label you. Hear the care in that sentence. I call you no name. I place no mark. I tell you only that the hidden drawer and the flattened papers live in crowds, though crowds eat them silently and name them lightly. In a queue of seven, one trolley will mirror yours. In a room of eight, one desk will hide your drawer. Widespread never meant willed. It meant many were schooled alike, and schooling alike never meant you are broken.

You believe widespread means harmless, and the thought is natural, because humans trust well trodden paths. Ask whether feet make a path safe. Crowds once lit cigarettes with coffee and called it poise. Crowds now steal sleep for glowing feeds and call it keeping up. Numbers tell you many walk together. They never tell you the ground is firm, and they never tell you who you are. You are an able adult raised inside a sweetened routine that never asked consent. The count removes oddness. It adds no brand to your forehead, and I will never add one.

In laboratories, animals offered sweet in brief binges broken by waits show gorging and fretting that calms when access steadies, and I cite animals only to show the rhythm is known, never to say you live in a cage. Keep that border firm. Caged work never proves a human heart. It hints that timing tutors appetite, that pause then flood teaches another flood. You know the timetable without bars. Careful days then loose nights. Empty tin then humming machine. Skipped pudding then double packet. You believe the careful stretch proves command, and the belief flatters, because care feels like helm. Observe what care precedes. The strict stretch sharpens the spree. The spree answers the stretch. The wheel turns because pushes alternate, not because will is thin.

The office canteen shows the same wheel at noon, and you believe its glass case merely serves choice. Trays of pale sandwiches wait beside tall chillers of bright bottles. Pudding cups stand in rows like small lanterns. You lift a tray for hunger and add a cup for close, because all tables around you close the same way. You believe copying the room is manners, and manners matter to you, because you respect shared tables. Watch what manners cost across months. The room never asks whether hollowness closed. The room asks whether the tray looks complete. Completeness has been taught to wear foil, so good manners rehearse the loop while meaning only kindness. No blame lives in that kindness. Schooling lives there, and schooling can be unschooled without losing kindness.

There is a tiny physical craving, brief and light, and there is a brainwashing voice that translates it as need, and I set both down here only as passing shapes you will recognise later. The first arrives as a thin thought of sweet, a dry wish, gone within minutes when left unwatered. The second arrives as the sentence riding it, that something sweet will see you through, that one square will right the hour. You believe the sentence rises from the gut, and the mistake is easy, because it borrows the gut's tone. In fact the tug is slight and the sentence is schooled, gathered from tables and tins and screens and kind rewards across years. A memory flashes of a small hand in yours before a glass case, patience rewarded with a chosen bun, winter tins saved and shared with pride, and warmth floods before judgment. Hold the warmth. It belongs to love. What rode in with it may still be weighed without dimming love.

The fact is your body brews its own steady glucose and holds it even without spooned sweet, which is why a sufficient dinner can leave you finished. You believe flesh calls for BAD SUGAR by name, and the belief feels bodily, because the wish tugs low in the day. Watch what flesh does when fed plain. Broth and bread and meat taken slowly to enough let the spoon rest of itself. Hunger lifts like mist. The mouth freshens. The belly settles. The hands forget the drawer without command. Plain produce and garden food and orchard fruit belong to that sufficiency and remain yours. I write BAD SUGAR for the boxed and bottled dose alone, leaving produce and plain cooking fully yours, so talk of apples never tangles with talk of bars. The frame you mistrust still signals truly. It was only shouted down.

The Con That Removed Choice

You tell me you choose this and remain well. I take your words exactly as you mean them, because candour deserves answer. You tell me pleasure is real, cost is distant, no hand compels yours. You tell me meals are sound and only the sweet edge strays. Let us weigh those claims as fair minds weigh any claim, gently and without mercy for the loop. I will put plain questions. You will answer inwardly. The honest answer will keep standing after we leave.

If you freely choose, why do you eat on your feet what you would never plate seated? If all is well, why do you press papers flat to seem fewer by morning? If pleasure leads, why does the hand return once pleasure has thinned? There is one plain answer that fits all three, and you already feel it. Pleasure does not lead and hunger does not lead. Hours lead and sights lead and stored nearness leads. Tins lead. Drawers lead. Bright rows lead. That is not picking. That is a con that lifted choice while polishing the feel of choosing, so reckoning would fall on you.

Follow the schooling without needing villains in capes. No one threatened. All taught. Sweet waits at each pause, so pause learns to crave. Sweet seals each kindness, so kindness learns to wrap in foil. Sweet crowns each weary spell, so weariness learns to ask by brand. Then every hoarding and screen repeats that all live this way and all fare well. You were not dull to credit it. You were tutored before consent was possible, the way we all were tutored, on partial tales. A small child praised with coins for courage links courage to coins. A clerk praised with cake for late hours links late hours to cake. By the age of weighing, the road already runs. A routine that needs a book to stop steers itself more than you steer it, and the holding of these pages answers more truthfully than any defence.

Your long trying testifies against your self charge. You drew lines round weekdays. You kept tidy spells. You cleared drawers on Sundays. You wrote lists and meant them. A heedless soul would not have laboured so. A frail soul would not have risen so often to try again. That labour ran inside the Willpower Method. I lay no fault on you in that sentence. I name the schooling that preached freedom through strain and painted wishes as commands to be fought, a schooling that gilds the barred thing and keeps tallies until tallies keep the thing enthroned in thought all day. No marvel labour never closed it. Any traveller fails with a chart that circles home. Once the chart bears fault, the traveller walks free of fault. That turn is not solace. It is the hinge on which this book swings.

Gather the whole view into one pocketed shape you can carry past bright rows. Tea and office box and noon finish and thick afternoon and corner shop bar and simmer tasting and seated supper and pudding beyond fullness and lamplight packets gather into one shape, bought in one humming aisle where every trolley matched, moved by places and hours rather than hollowness, tallied in crowds yet pressed flat at night, schooled by kind hands before consent. That gathered shape owns a name you may hold without heat. That is the sugar trap.

Keep its calendar tucked in the name, because you believe one bar spends one moment and the till tells longer. One packet tutors the next. One evening wires the next wait. One week of care whets the next spree. Coins slip in handfuls that mount to wages. Minutes slip in waits that mount to weeks. Mused wishes slip in tugs that mount to a constant hum. You meet the cost not once but sweet upon sweet for every day from now on. Read that span two ways. Left unwatched, it levies afternoons and evenings for years. Seen clearly and quitted, those hours revert to your purse. The span never menaces. It measures. A levy raised daily is no trifle. It is a tenancy.

You were caught, not careless.

What Your Body Already Knows

Join me at a plain board for supper, because you believe appetite plays false and I want you to observe it behave truly. No tallies lie on the cloth and no bans hang over it. A bowl of stew steams beside a heel of bread, with greens in oil alongside and an apple waiting in the dish for after. You sit, because sitting lets signals sound. You eat, because hollowness called, not because the clock struck. You believe hollowness is crisis that only swift sweet can meet, and the belief grips, because the thin wish has always yelled. Notice what hollowness does when heeded plainly. It climbs like tide. It holds. It ebbs when fed. It never howls a brand. It asks for dinner and then stills when dinner lands.

Watch appetite the way you would watch gulls ride and settle. At times it runs keen before noon. At times it stays slight till dusk. At times it closes early after a few mouthfuls. You believe its shifts convict you of disorder, and attention to them is right, because attention is care. In truth shifting is its order. Sleep moves it. Work moves it. Cold moves it. Company moves it. The frame trims itself without consulting wishes. Fed with sufficient real food to enough, the fork pauses unaided. Breath deepens. Talk leaves victuals for news. The mouth rests. Stillness here is never forced. It remains when clamour ceases.

You mistrust gut lore because sweet schooled you to hear clamour as lore. True lore speaks low. It draws toward dinner when empty and turns away when filled. It finds bread savoury and broth deep when daily sweet no longer coats the tongue. It parts mouth wish from belly need, the bright idea that a square would be pleasant from the hollow that asks for supper. You believe the two feel alike, and confusion is pardonable, because the loud one long mimicked the soft. Attend for a spell without steering. The bright idea keeps office hours, half past two, the queue, the sofa, the kettle minute, fed or unfed alike. The hollow keeps body hours, after labour, after walking, after a spare lunch, and departs when fed. One keeps place time. One keeps your time. Telling them apart asks for notice, not force, and notice here stays feather light.

Take a sufficient dinner to enough and mark how spare the close feels. You chew. You rest the fork. You take more because relish asks. You cease because enough feels plain. No parley. No sweet seal needed where hollowness already sealed. You believe closure wears foil, and the belief feels like recall, because recall holds hundreds of foiled closes and few plain ones. Observe what closure truly is. The spoon lies laid, breath eases, and speech turns from dishes to plans. A finished board leaves thought quiet though the sink stands full. That quiet never means lack. It means done. Plain cooking grants it daily when permitted. BAD SUGAR only mimicked it while restarting the hand.

I speak of flesh with regard, because regard is earned. The frame brews its own current, steadies between dinners, asks without clamour and ceases without drama, clears the tongue and settles sleep when left to its office. Bright packets bawled over it for seasons and still it whispers true. You believe seasons of sweet have spoiled its dials, and the fear is understandable. The fact is dials answer swiftly once no longer drowned. You have felt it on plain weeks away, when flavours rang higher and nights sat deeper, though you thanked the sea wind. Thank the frame. It bore you through every line and every restart without desertion. Trust here is no leap. It is greeting of a servant that never departed.

Mornings teach the same lesson in miniature, and you believe the early mug must be sweet to open the eyes. Plain porridge with milk and a sliced apple waits on the same table. You lift the spoon with doubt, expecting blandness, because memory paints plain as punishment. Notice what the mouth reports when given a chance. Warmth spreads. Chewing slows. The apple tastes bright against the grain. The belly accepts without clamour. Thirst often masquerades as hunger in these hours, and you believe every dry signal asks for sweet, because sweet once answered every signal. A glass of water followed by ten quiet minutes often stills what a bar would only restart. The frame distinguishes thirst from hollowness and hollowness from custom when heard without haste. That discernment never needed training. It needed quieter air.

Hold this ground while we proceed, because further pages will press what benefit sweet truly lends, and you will need firm footing for the press. The snare owns a name now. The Tuesday stands seen. The bright aisles stand walked with open eyes. What stays is to test the vaunted gain on scales the frame itself holds. You bring no gloom to that test. You bring lively curiosity and a quiet lift that a door unmarked may stand near. The frame you deemed foe will serve as witness that sets you loose.
