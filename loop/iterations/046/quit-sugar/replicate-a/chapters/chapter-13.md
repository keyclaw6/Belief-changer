WHAT YOU HOLD

We once stood inside the sugar trap and mistook its noise for our own voice, you and I, and called the hurry hunger and the coating comfort.

What you once called a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift has no office left in your day. The company paid the warmth at tables and rest paid the ease after strain and hunger met with food paid the satisfaction, while the sweet only billed them all and left its hollow for the evening.

When did the sweet ever add anything the hour did not already hold. When did leaving it untouched ever cool a single evening you sat through with appetite. There was never anything to lose.

I stood where you stand, with the same drawer of rules behind me and the same doubt ahead, and I know the difference between policing a mouth and seeing through a con. You left it easily, at once, and for good, not by effort but by seeing straight.

Eat as much of the real food you love as you want, whenever you want it, and never count a bite again.

You know that sentence in your body now, at table after table where appetite decided the ending. This was never about BAD SUGAR. It was about appetite and attention and trust in yourself returning, and the sweet was only where their absence showed.

THE PAGE TO KEEP

What follows holds only because the seeing behind it already stands. A reader who opens here first belongs at the beginning, because these lines carry weight only after the belief behind each one has changed.

Keep this page where a hand finds it without searching. Photograph it into the telephone you carry everywhere. Fold the paper into the wallet beside the money. WHEN LIFE GETS LOUD

You tell me, “This holds now, in a quiet house with a full belly, but life gets loud and I will forget all this,” and I take the sentence kindly, because it sounds like experience talking.

We all know that loud. The telephone ringing against the kettle boiling. The deadline moved forward and the money moved backward. The child ill and the train late and the inbox full before nine. The bright celebration with everybody drinking and laughing and pressing plates toward you. The dull tired evening when the day has taken everything and the night asks for something back. You believe loud must mean lost, because loud once meant reaching without thinking.

Loud changes the volume. It puts nothing back into the sweet.

Ask the loud itself as witness, because you already know how to ask. When did noise ever put a benefit back into a mouthful that held none in the quiet. When did a hard week ever need the evening hollow to meet it. When did forgetting the exact words ever mean the seeing left your eyes. You know the answer without reaching, because you lived the quiet proof and the loud cannot unlive it. Noise makes the old tug louder for an hour. It does not make it true for a minute.

This is how the page is held when loud comes. You do not argue with the tug. Argument has done its work. You do not weigh whether one would help, because there is no benefit left to weigh. You glance at the lines you carry and you remember the seeing behind them stands whole. One line is enough. A wobble is the old voice, not new evidence. One exception keeps the whole belief alive. You read the line you already know, you smile where you stand, and the hour moves on with you inside it.

You believe use must mean reading and rereading to keep belief warm, because old plans needed constant reminding. This needs no warming. The list is not a set of orders to obey against want. It is a photograph of a door you already walked through. You look at the photograph to remember the door stays shut behind you, not to push it shut again. The hand that holds the telephone with your page inside holds the same truth the hand held at the table. The drawer that holds the paper holds dinner and bills and keys beside it, and none of them asks you to become someone else to meet the day.

Loud will bring the old tug across the mind wearing your own voice. “You have earned a small comfort.” “One cannot hurt after all this seeing.” “Today has been hard and you need something to get you through.” You hear the taught bell and you know whose hand pulls the rope. The tug is the brainwashing voice asking for rent on a house you have left. You do not obey a voice you have already answered. You think your shout of relief and the talk ends.

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

You think it glad where you stand, dishes stacked or bags in hand or keys half out of the pocket, and the thought came and the truth answered. The body stays with its plain day while the pictures thin for lack of belief behind them. The loud goes on around you and you go on inside it, present where your feet stand.

The fact is loud proves nothing except that life is loud, and the list proves the seeing holds inside it.

LIFE FROM HERE

The days ahead hold mornings and shops and shared tables, and you meet each one as its owner. When the old tug crosses, it answers itself with the truth you already hold.

What returns to you now shows in what the days can carry. Appetite came back first, and you know it by the way a meal closes itself. Hunger asks for food and gets food. The hand slows on its own. The belly tells the truth and the mind believes it. You believe a returned appetite must only change meals, because meals were where the loss showed. A returned appetite changes every hour that asks to be met, because every hour is met by the same body. Work taken up without side-talk underneath. Talk listened to without counting underneath. An evening sat through without bargaining underneath. The mouth that knows when it has had enough knows when the day has been enough, and both knowings come from the same trust.

Attention came back with it, and you know it by where you stand. We once lived half inside pictures of the next sweet while the hour went past unheard, you and I, present nowhere. You attend now where your feet stand. You hear the sentence put to you and you answer it. You see the plate and the face beside the plate in the same look. You believe attention must only help at tables, because tables were where pictures interrupted. Attention kept where you stand helps everywhere pictures once stood between you and the hour. The meeting heard whole. The walk home seen whole. The child’s story caught whole. None of this is added to you. It is what stood there all along once the noise stopped billing it.

Trust in yourself came back last and holds the other two, and you know it by the quiet after a promise kept. You said you would walk out and you walked out. You said the tug is not orders and you do not obey it. You believe self-trust must only concern BAD SUGAR, because BAD SUGAR was where trust broke. A trust mended where it broke holds where the break never showed. The word you give yourself about money and work and rest carries more weight now, because the same mouth that stopped answering pictures answers plainly everywhere. You do not second-guess the hand that sets the plate down finished. You do not second-guess the voice that says not for me, thanks. The owner of the mouth has returned, and the owner tends the whole house.

You tell me, “But what if the loud lasts weeks,” and I ask you to look at what lasting means now. When did a long loud spell ever return a benefit to the sweet. When did weeks of noise ever make the hollow smaller the morning after. When did standing through loud plain ever leave you with less than loud dosed left you. There is one plain answer that fits without blame. The sweet never met the loud. It only postponed meeting it and charged the postponement to the evening.

You answer with the shout you already own until the answer arrives before the question, and each crossing leaves the days fuller and the pictures emptier. The thought came. The truth answered. The body stays with its day while the loud passes the way weather passes over a house that stands.

Go and enjoy your life.
