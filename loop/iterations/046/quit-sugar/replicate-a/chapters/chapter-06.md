Chapter 6
EATING

Morning finds you with the tongue coated and the head thick and the eyes gummed at the corners. You believe the night explains the morning, because mornings keep the score that nights write. The bin holds bright twists from hours you barely tasted. The mouth remembers sweetness gone dull toward the end. The belly remembers fullness without rest. Light comes grey through the blind gap and lands on the plate left from supper and the glass left half drunk.
You believe such mornings prove you need a sweet start to lift the fog, because fog asks for light from somewhere. I have woken in that exact heaviness with the same crust in the eyes and the same slow limbs and the same short temper over small noises, and you believe your case runs heavier than mine, because shame keeps private accounts. Mine ran the same. We all paid the same evening price and met the same morning bill.
You know the kitchen as it waits in that light, and you believe its stillness accuses you, because still rooms seem to stare. The kettle stands cold. The bread sits in its bag. The fruit bowl holds apples gone soft at one edge from days untouched while foil won. The chair stands where you left it after midnight. The air smells of last night sugar and morning dust. You believe no appetite can wake kindly in such a room, because the room holds yesterday breath. Watch what changes when the evening changes and the room keeps its place.

IN THIS CHAPTER: a heavy morning, a plain table, an empty hour watched, a filter lifting and a piece of fruit.

*Hunger is honest, real food answers it, and appetite can be trusted again.*

The Morning After

You know this waking by heart, and you believe it shows what the years cost, because the body keeps no secrets in the first minute. The head feels packed with cotton wool. The eyes open sticky and close again against the light. The mouth tastes stale and flat. The limbs feel heavy under the blanket. The temper sits short, ready to snap at the alarm and the kettle and the news voice. You believe the temper proves character frayed by life, because tired people blame tired nerves. Look with cooler eyes at what the evening poured in.
We ate past tasting in the lamplight, and you believe past tasting still counts as pleasure, because the hand kept moving. The hand kept moving after the tongue stopped reporting. The first squares pleased the tongue for a minute. The middle squares pleased less. The last squares pleased nothing and left coating and a jaw working from habit. You believe the coating proves nothing, because mornings blur detail. Mornings blur nothing. They report the night without flattery.
You tell me in your own true words, "foggy and irritable and emotional," and "thick head," and I hear no fault in the phrases, because they describe what many live mornings carry. You believe those words describe a self that needs a sweet lift, because schooled talk points that way. The words describe an evening of loud doses followed by a night of broken rest. The fact is the heaviness lifts as the day steadies without BAD SUGAR, and mornings grow lighter on their own account.
See the noon hour that follows such a morning, and you believe the day can only be saved by a bright packet at eleven, because the head still drags. The drawer opens. The bar breaks with its clean crack. Sweetness floods loud for minutes. The head lifts on noise. Within the hour the lift thins and the hollow returns with its picture attached, asking for more to hold the lift in place. You believe the return proves the morning needed fuel, because hunger talk covers all dips. The return proves the lift borrowed from the afternoon and charged the afternoon for the loan.
See the same morning some weeks later, lived without the evening foil, and you believe such pictures are promised to others, because hope feels risky to hold. Hold it with me as lived report, not as pledge. Waking with the mouth clean. Eyes opening without paste at the corners. Head present behind the eyes. Shoulders willing to lift the blanket. Hunger arriving as an open hollow toward eight, clean and simple, asking for breakfast. The pillowy lure that used to call toward three in the afternoon, soft and pale and sweet on the tongue in thought, gone quiet, not mourned, barely remembered until the old hour passes and you notice you did not look toward the drawer. You believe quiet of that kind must be bought with effort, because past quiet always broke by noon. This quiet is bought with nothing. It arrives when loudness leaves.
You know the difference between the two kinds of tired, and you believe all tired feels the same, because tired talk lumps all heaviness together. The old tired sat behind the eyes with coating on the tongue and short breath after stairs and a head that hummed toward evening. The new tired after a full working day sits in the muscles and asks for supper and sleep and clears by morning. One tired deepens with sweets. The other clears with meals and rest. Bodies know which is which once loudness stops shouting over the signal.
Ask plainly what changed between the two mornings. The bed stayed the same. The job stayed the same. The house stayed the same. Only the evening changed. The evening lost its noise and kept its rest, its chair, its lamplight, its talk, its warm drink held in both hands. The morning kept the difference. Bodies tell truth before opinions wake.

The Plain Table

Midday lays a plain table, and you believe plain tables cannot hold you, because bright packets taught the eye to look for shine. See the table with me as it stands. A bowl of soup steaming, thick with vegetables and barley. Bread torn, not sliced thin for show. Cheese cut in honest wedges. An apple with cold skin from the sill. Water poured tall. Steam rising in the light from the window. The chair pulled close. No wrapper crackle. No bright twist. No bell ringing from a drawer.
You believe such food leaves the eater watching the clock toward the next sweet, because past meals ended with listening for more. Sit through this one in thought at an unhurried pace and watch what fullness does when no loud dose rides on top. The first spoonfuls warm the mouth and throat. Hunger, which arrived as an open hollow, begins to narrow. The bread chews with substance and asks for swallowing and water. The cheese holds the belly. The apple crunches loud and leaves juice on the chin. Breath slows. Shoulders drop without any bar to thank. Talk can run or rest can hold, and the plate empties at its own speed.
You know the old supper pattern by heart, and you believe a meal without a sweet close stays open, because closes were always iced. Plates cleared. Bellies full of meat and potatoes. Talk still running. Hands still reaching toward the cupboard for the close that truly finishes. Watch the plain close in thought and see what finishes when foil stays shut. The belly holds warm and full. The mouth tastes salt and butter and bread. Water drunk cold clears the palate. The body sends its plain signal of enough, heaviness lifting toward comfort, attention turning outward. You believe the turning outward must be forced, because past turnings needed talk all evening. The turning comes on its own when no loud minute calls the hand back.
You tell me, "I will never feel satisfied without something sweet," and "eating will be dull," and I take both sentences with care, because dullness frightens anyone who loves food. You believe dullness lives in plain food, because comparison taught the tongue to judge. Notice what dull really described across the years. After bright twists the tongue reported less with each square and the hand moved faster. After the plain meal the tongue reports steady and the hand slows on its own. Which of the two sounds dull in honest language. The meal that keeps taste to the end. The packet that loses taste by the middle.
We learned to call the loud minute on the tongue satisfaction, and you believe loudness proves depth, because loud things win attention. Loudness proves volume, not depth. Depth shows in the hour after. After the loud minute the hand listens for the drawer and the mind counts what stays in the box and the belly asks again within the hour. After the plain meal eaten to satisfaction the hand forgets the kitchen and the mind turns to work and talk and the street outside and the belly holds quiet for hours. Forgetting proves answered need. Listening proves renewed call.
You believe eating with others will expose you, because eyes seem to watch the refusing hand, and watching frightens. See the shared table with plain food at its centre and watch where eyes truly look. Hands pass bread. Voices lift over small news. Laughter comes when it comes. No one weighs your plate. No one mourns your missing wrap. The talk holds all faces together. What stood out on sweet nights was leaving the table to hunt, foil in the pocket, steps quick toward the shop. What blends on plain nights is staying, seated and fed, part of the hour. Belonging never lived in glaze.
Eat the plain meal to satisfaction in thought and mark the signs that tell you hunger has been met. The hollow fills. Warmth spreads under the ribs. Food loses its urgent shine and becomes simply present. A last spoonful sits willingly uneaten because enough has arrived. Water tastes good cold. Breath comes full. That is the body saying enough in its first language, before words. You believe that language was lost years ago, because foil spoke louder. The language was only covered. It speaks again the moment loudness leaves the table.
The fact is real food meets hunger the way water meets thirst. It fills and quiets and leaves the drinker free to turn toward the day. BAD SUGAR never meets hunger. It covers hunger with noise, then leaves hunger plus noise. Once you feel the difference in the belly across one unhurried hour, the claim that plain eating cannot satisfy cannot stand beside the clock.

The Empty Hour Watched

Late afternoon brings an empty feeling, and you believe every empty feeling is hunger calling for sweets, because the years trained one answer for all hollows. Watch one such hour with me without hurry and learn its two shapes. Four o'clock pales the hall. Work thins. Light shifts on the wall. The belly holds a small hollow ache. The head holds a bright picture of chocolate, exact in colour and crack and melt, arriving with words attached.
You believe the picture proves the hollow asks for chocolate, because picture and hollow arrive together. Pictures lie when schooling lends them. Sit with the hour in thought and separate what the body says from what the voice says. Hunger arrives wordless. It sits low under the ribs, open and dull, patient across minutes, eased by food of many kinds, growing slowly toward evening, never naming a brand or a wrapper or a shop. The other call arrives loud and narrow and naming. It says the dark cookies call me. It says my body was saying eat cake. It points to the drawer, the machine, the corner shop, the exact shelf. It carries urgency and pictures and bargaining. Bodies do not bargain in brand names. Schooling bargains.
You tell me, "I'm too tired to function without it," and "shaky and light-headed unless I eat," and I hear real tiredness and real tremble in the words, because afternoons can shake a hard-working adult. You believe shaking proves the sweet is needed as fuel, because tremble frightens. Notice when tremble truly comes and what truly steadies it. After a morning of little food and many loud doses, the midday hour swings and hands shake and temper shortens. After a plain breakfast and a plain midday plate eaten to satisfaction, the same work hour passes with hunger arriving clean and leaving clean after supper. The meal steadied. The doses swung. The fact is the body keeps its own supply line running across the day, manufacturing what the blood needs from ordinary food, holding its range without spooned sweetness. You do not need to import the supply mouthful by mouthful. Real meals keep the line fed.
You know the ride with no platform from your own afternoons, and you believe its dips prove you must keep riding, because stepping off feels like falling. Watch the ride from the ground in thought. The car climbs loud on sweetness, hangs bright for minutes, then drops fast toward hollow and shake, then climbs again only with another ticket. Riders call the climb relief and the drop need. The ground never moved. Plain meals leave the car in the shed. Hunger walks the road at its own pace, rising open toward supper, closing quiet after, no climb, no drop, no ticket. You believe the road sounds flat beside the ride, because rides dazzle. Flat roads carry people home.
Try the sitting in lived detail, enacted, not described as a task. You sit at the table with water at hand and the hour open before you. The hollow sits low. The bright picture comes with its sentence about needing something sweet. You drink water slowly. You breathe and wait five minutes by the clock. You notice the picture thins when not fed with steps toward the drawer, while the hollow stays honest and open, waiting for supper. You eat supper later, meat and potatoes and greens and bread and water, at an unhurried pace, to satisfaction. The hollow closes. The picture loses its colour. Hunger was the hollow. The picture was the schooled call riding on top.
You know how evenings used to run after such hours, and you believe one watched hour changes nothing, because nights hold their own pull. Follow the watched hour into its evening in thought and see what follows what. The unwatched hour ran toward foil, then toward coating, then toward the packet finished past enjoyment, then toward heavy sleep and a thick morning. The watched hour runs toward supper eaten sitting, then toward talk and lamplight, then toward sleep that holds, then toward a mouth that wakes clean. One hour turns the tap. The evening carries the water.
Ask your own afternoon as witness, and answer inwardly with the candour you already showed me. If the bright picture truly signalled hunger, why does hunger accept bread and soup and apples gladly while the picture accepts only foil. If the shaking truly asked for sweets, why do steady meals leave less shaking across the week than sweet top-ups leave. There is one plain answer that fits both, and you feel its weight without any scolding from me. Hunger takes real food. The call takes only the loop feeding itself.
Hunger tells the truth. The call repeats a lesson. You believe the lesson still owns you, because its voice sounds like your own. Hear it once as schooling and its ownership ends.

The Filter Lifts

Taste itself was covered, and you believe your tongue simply prefers strong sweetness, because strong sweetness won every contest for years. See how contests were fixed. Each bar set the measure for the next mouthful. Food after the bar tasted flat beside memory of melt. An apple tasted sour. Bread tasted dry. Soup tasted thin. Morning tea needed two spoonfuls to register at all. You believe flatness proves plain food lacks flavour, because the tongue seemed to report so. The tongue reported comparison, not flavour.
Think of looking at noon light through smoked glass, and you believe the day runs dim, because the eye trusts the glass. Wipe the glass and noon stands bright without any change in the sun. So it runs with the palate after years of loud doses. The last bar hangs like smoked glass over the next meal. Everything mild reads as nothing. Everything subtle reads as missing. The glass, not the meal, made the dimness.
We lived behind that glass so long we called it taste, and you believe long habits must reflect nature, because nature feels fixed. Habits reflect training, and training lifts. Eat plain through several steady days in thought and watch flavour return without any lesson or chart. Soup tastes salt and deep. Bread tastes wheaten and warm. Cheese tastes sharp to the edge. Water tastes cold and clean. Hunger seasons all of it the way light seasons a room, present without comment, making colour true.
You know morning tea by heart, and you believe its sweetness must come from the spoon, because the spoon always stood beside the cup. Taste the cup across the change in thought. The old cup arrived pale and needed spoonfuls to register, then left the tongue coated and asking. The new cup after plain days arrives dark and fragrant, bitter at the edge, warming through the chest, needing nothing on top to stand as pleasure. You believe the difference must be talked into liking bitter, because bitter talk sounds like settling. Nothing is settled. The tongue simply meets the cup without glass between, and the cup gives what it always held.
Then take the apple, because the apple carries the whole proof in one hand. On the first day the apple tastes small beside remembered icing, sweet but thin, gone in two bites, followed by listening for more. Some days later, after plain tables eaten to satisfaction and evenings without foil and mornings waking clean, the same apple cracks loud between the teeth. Juice runs. Sweetness spreads real and round, not loud, lasting through the chew, followed by quiet, not by listening. You believe such change must be talked into being, because past changes needed talk all day. This change talks in the mouth. The mouth knows when glass leaves.
You tell me food tastes better only for people who never loved sweets, and your love runs deeper than theirs, because love flatters the lover. I honour the love and answer it plainly. Those who report food tasting better loved sweets hard and daily for years, then found the apple louder after the glass lifted. Their love was loud habit plus bright memory. Your love can meet the same lifting. The filter was laid over every tongue that ate loud daily. Lifting follows the same leaving.
Once the apple proves the point, the old boast that BAD SUGAR gives flavour cannot stand. BAD SUGAR lent loudness and took keenness. Real food returns keenness and keeps sweetness where sweetness belongs, in fruit and meals, bright without noise. The palate was never dull. It was filtered.

What Stays On The Table

Look at what stays when loudness leaves, and you believe leaving must mean counting and measuring and a thin life, because past leavings were ledgers. This leaving keeps the table wide. Meat and fish. Potatoes and rice. Bread and butter. Milk and cheese. Eggs. Beans and lentils. Vegetables from the ground and the vine. Fruit whole from the tree, skin and flesh together. Water. Meals eaten sitting, chewed, tasted, finished when enough arrives. Nothing weighed. Nothing tallied. Nothing eaten standing over the sink while thinking about the next sweet.
You know the market walk by heart, and you believe its aisles must be navigated like tests, because past walks ended with a basket of old favourites. Walk it in thought with appetite trusted and see what the shelves truly hold. Vegetables piled bright. Bread warm in its paper. Fish laid on ice. Cheese in wheels. Fruit heaped with smell rising sweet in the air. The bright packets stand in their rows, loud in colour, calling from every end. You believe the call proves hunger, because the eye moves fast toward shine. The eye moves toward training. Hunger moves toward meals. Push the basket past the loud rows toward food that quiets, and the call thins behind you like noise from another street.
You believe the body asks for spooned sweetness to keep its line, because packets say energy sits in bright twists. The fact is the body holds its own supply across the day from ordinary food, keeping its range without any need for spooned refills. Glucose is not goods to import packet by packet. The kitchen already delivers what the blood needs through meals that satisfy. That is why hunger quiets after supper and stays quiet for hours, and why mornings can wake clean without a sweet start. The line runs from food, not from foil.
Keep whole fruit fully on your table, because you believe all sweet taste must leave together, and loss gathers there. Nothing loved leaves with BAD SUGAR except the loop. An orange peeled in sunlight with spray on the fingers. Berries in a bowl with cream. A pear eaten over the sink with juice on the wrist. These stay, whole and chewed and savoured, part of meals and part of freedom. What leaves is the carton juice that drinks like fruit while acting like a dose, sweet water without chew or pause or satisfaction, and the pale sweet powders and syrups with kind names that keep the same bright relation alive, honey and agave and cane dressed as nature. The tongue meets them as loudness. The hand meets them as listening for more. Whole fruit meets hunger and leaves quiet. The difference sits in the belly an hour later, not in the name on the jar.
You tell me a lighter sweet or a juice habit could hold the middle ground while you eat well otherwise, and middle grounds tempt good planners, because they look like care. Watch the middle in lived weeks, not in labels. The morning juice raises the same bright memory as the bar and wakes the same listening toward ten. The pale packet after supper keeps the evening picture bright and the drawer interesting. Sweetness kept as a daily answer keeps the old relation warm, even when the wrapper changes. The relation, not the wrapper, drives the reaching. Let sweetness live where hunger lives, in whole food chewed and finished, and the relation cools for want of feeding.
Eat as much of the real food you love as you want, whenever you want it, and never count a bite again.
You believe counting must guard appetite, because appetite ran wild in bright years. Counting guarded nothing while the loop ran, and the night kitchen with its fridge light proves it, the door open on cold ankles, the packet finished past enjoyment, the count abandoned by nine. Appetite runs true when loudness leaves. Hunger arrives open. Satisfaction arrives plain. The hand stops because enough arrived, not because a number said stop. Trust sits in that stopping. It grows each time a plain meal closes and the hour after holds quiet.
You know the old fear that hunger cannot be trusted, and you believe trust must be earned through months of proof, because past trust broke fast. Trust earns itself across days you can already feel. Breakfast eaten sitting holds till midday. Midday eaten to satisfaction holds till supper. Supper eaten unhurried holds through talk into sleep. Each closing teaches the next opening. The belly learns it will be answered, so it asks cleanly. The hand learns food will satisfy, so it reaches calmly. Schooling fades where answering repeats.
You believe one plain week proves nothing against years of foil, because years weigh heavy in memory. Weigh the week by its own signs. Mornings waking lighter with the mouth clean. Midday plates emptied without hurry. Afternoons passing without the drawer bell. Apples tasting round. Suppers closing with water tasting good. Evenings kept with talk and rest and lamplight, not with coating. No single sign shouts. Together they draw a new favourite, not by argument but by appetite. The body votes with quiet, and quiet repeats.
You know the bowl on the sill by heart, and you believe its quiet presence changes nothing, because loud packets once ruled the shelf. See the bowl across plain days in thought and watch what it teaches. Apples waiting cool. Oranges bright against the wood. A banana freckling toward perfect ripeness. Hands reaching without bargaining, peeling without counting, tasting without listening for more. You believe such reaching must be policed, because past reaching ran past enough. Past reaching ran on loudness. This reaching runs on hunger and closes on satisfaction. The bowl empties and fills again with the week shop, ordinary as bread, faithful as morning light.

The favourite was never the bar. The favourite was the meal that answers and the fruit that sings after glass leaves and the morning that wakes willing. Once appetite has shown it can be trusted across ordinary days, dullness talk ends. Eating stands revealed as the pleasure the loop only imitated. The table holds. Hunger speaks. Food answers.
