# Master Plan — Quit Sugar

SLUG: quit-sugar / ITER 045 / REPLICATE a. Full-length calibration book. Target 60,000 words.

## 1. Book core

Target behavior: compulsive consumption of refined and added sugar and junk carbs — the craving-snacking loop and its grip, not nutrition pedantry.

Reader (planner-facing state): an adult who grazes daily and binges some evenings, hides wrappers, runs 3pm top-ups to get through, pays for treats with self-reproach, has failed diets, moderation rules and willpower, and suspects the whole approach is wrong. General adult edition, one reader.

Load-bearing false belief: bad sugar is a genuine treat and lift that makes life sweeter, and life without it would be deprivation.

Through-line: it does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you — see that, and escape is immediate.

Format: full-length Carr book, 15 chapters, last ordinary meal as ritual, one ordinary-life chapter, short photographable recap. Budgets sum to 60,000 words.

Fork decisions (style-guide Part A §4, Carr defaults):
- Fork 1, inner state: full Carr personification. The trivial physical loop is THE SUGAR MITE; the belief-system that feeds it is THE SWEET TALKER. Both frozen on the mantra sheet. The mite whimpers; the Talker translates the whimper as treat, fuel, love. Starve the mite by refusing BAD SUGAR; kill the Talker by argument. Never a mighty enemy demanding willpower — a starving parasite already dying.
- Fork 2, outcome: total cessation of BAD SUGAR, commanded with cheerful certainty. Moderation foreclosed with the pincer and the cliff-jump. No autonomy-to-moderate override. Conditional-bonus neighbours (alcohol, dairy) are not required to stop.
- Fork 3, science weight: hard facts at full Carr force, flat and frightening where true, then relief in the same chapter — change from escape-joy, never from fear. Facts arrive as settled fact. Raw citations quarantined to back matter. The scare stays in the argument, never in an appendix.
- Fork 4, villain: two villains, both hit hard — the engineered food industry that built the trap, and THE WILLPOWER METHOD that kept the reader in it. Warm to the person, vicious to both.
- Fork 5, void: change nothing else in your life. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own; the reader inhabits every old context confidently. No replacement system, no transmutation programme. Root-plus-direction is not taken.

Redefinition and margin-for-error doctrine: the category eating cannot be quit, so the target is redrawn early and carried by a CAPS name.
- Boxed definition in CH-01: BAD SUGAR means added and free sugars plus the junk carbs that behave like them in the loop — sweets, pastries, biscuits, ice cream, chocolate confectionery, sugary drinks including juices and sweetened mixes, sweetened yoghurts and cereals, white-flour snacks that vanish without satiety. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Whole fruit, vegetables, plain unrefined staples and the glucose your own liver makes are not BAD SUGAR; they belong to the positive authority.
- Full total-abstinence trap logic runs inside the line: there is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero.
- Margin for error: your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip, but your mind cannot afford to re-admit the belief. A slip revives nothing unless the Talker is let back in. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. Seatbelt logic: there for accidents, not for driving erratically.

Clinical and eating-disorder safety perimeter: this book covers the everyday sugar loop for a general adult. It gives no medical nutrition therapy, no diabetes management advice, no eating-disorder treatment, no weight-loss-program mechanics, no prescription. Readers with diabetes, pregnancy, medication, an eating-disorder history, or purging/compensatory behaviour follow their clinician first. Crisis pointers live in front matter. All clinical limits live in the single plan-wide advisory AD-A and in ledger safety limits, never inside instruction wording.

Strongest pro-behaviour scene: the family birthday table — warm light, the rich chocolate mousse or holiday cake everyone eats, you ordering mousse-all-of-it rather than sit out, the joke that skipping means you are no fun. Sugar claims here to be love, belonging and celebration at once. Met head-on in CH-10; every drop of pleasure reassigned to company, occasion and real food.

Destination state: whenever the reader thinks of BAD SUGAR they feel relief and freedom that they no longer feed it — happy to be free, free from day one, never counting days.

Saved ending reframe (appears only in the final movement): you did not bury a friend; you buried an enemy that posed as one — grieve nothing.

## 2. Compact evidence ledger

Each row is a single research unit. No multi-unit binding, so no union paraphrase arises. Quotations match the synthesis exactly; interpretations stay unquoted.

E-01 — lift misread as fuel. Reader line: "I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop." Unit: LEU-001. Source: bank-02 B-003. Tier: lived recovered-writer self-description. Scope: mid-afternoon dip answered with a sweet top-up. Permitted: the lift–crash cycle is self-described by recovered writers; the "fuel" misreads a spike-then-drop as genuine energy. Prohibited: that every reader has clinically low blood sugar, or that sugar is ever real "fuel." Empirical limit: self-report, no glucose measurement. Safety: no medical diagnosis claims from these lived-experience quotes; route clinical crash to AD-A.

E-02 — one bite becomes the box. Reader line: "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop." Unit: LEU-002. Source: bank-02 BM-3. Tier: lived loss-of-control account. Scope: evening moderation allowance collapsing into the box. Permitted: the craving, once triggered, carries the binge; the loss-of-control is the trap's signature. Prohibited: that sugar condemns anyone to helplessness permanently. Empirical limit: self-report of trigger, not dose measurement. Safety: no pathological labeling of the reader.

E-03 — dessert as self-payment. Reader line: "celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate." Unit: LEU-003. Source: bank-09 LX-158. Tier: lived reward-frame account. Scope: stress, victory or cinema paid with a sweet. Permitted: dessert as self-payment for virtue is a community-named frame; the reward-frame keeps the loop alive. Prohibited: that no one may ever enjoy sweet food. Empirical limit: community slang, not outcome data. Safety: do not moralize sweetness as universally forbidden.

E-04 — four-part animal model. Finding: "Four components of addiction are analyzed. 'Bingeing', 'withdrawal', 'craving' and cross-sensitization are each given operational definitions and demonstrated behaviorally with sugar bingeing as the reinforcer." Unit: S-1. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: rats, intermittent 12-h access model, 10% sucrose. Permitted: A book may state that a well-known scientific model shows sugar can be consumed in a binge/deprivation pattern that produces the classic behavioral signs of dependence in animals. Prohibited: Treating rat-model findings as if they proved human sugar addiction; claiming universal, not conditional (intermittent-binge) effects. Empirical limit: rats, intermittent 12-h access model (10% sucrose ≈ soft drink); findings translate only cautiously to humans. Safety: withhold diagnosis from the reader.

E-05 — each binge re-fires reward. Finding: "We find that rats with intermittent access to sugar will drink in a binge-like manner that releases DA in the NAc each time, like the classic effect of most substances of abuse." Unit: S-2. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: animal microdialysis, nucleus accumbens. Permitted: The addiction-loop chemistry — each hit of sugar, in binge-like consumption, re-triggers the reward circuit's dopamine signal — is a real mechanism that has been directly measured (in animals). Prohibited: Implying measured human brain evidence of the same magnitude; stating sugar equals psychostimulants/opiates. Empirical limit: animal data; magnitude smaller than drugs of abuse. Safety: grip, not equivalence.

E-06 — dip after the sugar. Finding: "This withdrawal state involves at least two neurochemical manifestations. First is a decrease in extracellular DA in the accumbens, and second is the release of acetylcholine (ACh) from accumbens interneurons." Unit: S-3. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: naloxone-precipitated or deprivation-induced withdrawal after intermittent bingeing, rats. Permitted: The deficit chemistry of withdrawal — dopamine flatlines while an opposing transmitter rises — has been measured after sugar-binge cycles; the brain's dip after the sugar is not imaginary. Prohibited: Claiming humans undergo the same measured neurochemical withdrawal; describing "sugar withdrawal" as a diagnosable human condition. Empirical limit: opiate-like, naloxone-precipitated; human syndrome not established. Safety: no human withdrawal diagnosis.

E-07 — mild but well-defined. Finding: "The overall effect of these neurochemical adaptations is mild, but well-defined, dependency." Unit: S-4. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: authors' conservative conclusion, rat model. Permitted: Even the pro-addiction laboratory still calls its measured sugar effect mild — a book may use this to defuse fear ("small magnitude, still a grip"). Prohibited: Quoting this as evidence of severe human dependence; overstating magnitude. Empirical limit: rat model; explicitly mild. Safety: defuses dread without denying grip.

E-08 — escalation belongs to the schedule. Finding: "Rats fed daily intermittent sugar and chow escalate their sugar intake and increase their intake during the first hour of daily access, which we define as a 'binge'… the limited-access animals consume as much sugar in 12 h as ad libitum-fed animals do in 24 h." Unit: S-5. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: intermittent versus ad-lib, animals; echoes diet-binge yo-yo. Permitted: The "one bite becomes the box" escalation pattern maps onto the same animal model's binge escalation; escalation over time is the signature of the loop. Prohibited: Claiming any human consumer will necessarily escalate exactly this way; generalizing to all sugar intake patterns. Empirical limit: animal meal-pattern data. Safety: no fate claim about individuals.

E-09 — runs on the body's own opioid handle. Finding: "Signs of withdrawal seem to be largely due to the opioid modifications since withdrawal can be obtained with the opioid antagonist naloxone. Food deprivation is also sufficient to precipitate opiate-like withdrawal signs." Unit: S-6. Source: PMC2235907. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: rodent somatic signs plus anxiety/depression analogues. Permitted: The sugar loop runs on the body's own opioid system, not merely "weak will"; when the reward stops, the body reacts. Prohibited: Saying sugar physically causes opiate addiction in people; medicalizing everyday readers. Empirical limit: rodent; antagonist inference. Safety: no opiate-addiction label.

E-10 — cues linger and strengthen. Finding: "Other long-lasting effects of sugar bingeing include a) enhanced lever pressing for sugar after 2 weeks of abstinence, b) enhanced voluntary alcohol intake in rats with a history of sugar-bingeing, and c) enhanced responding for sugar-associated cues." Unit: S-7. Source: PMC4361030. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: days to weeks after bingeing stopped, animals. Permitted: The pull back to sugar after a period off it, and the renewed strength of sugar-associated cues (smell, wrapper, time of day), has a demonstrated neural analogue — craving that lingers and grows with abstinence is a real modeled phenomenon. Prohibited: Claiming sugar abstinence makes humans drink more alcohol; asserting cue-"incubation" as proven in humans. Empirical limit: animal; craving/gateway in scare-quotes by authors. Safety: no cross-addiction prediction.

E-11 — honest disagreement. Finding: "We find little evidence to support sugar addiction in humans, and findings from the animal literature suggest that addiction-like behaviours, such as bingeing, occur only in the context of intermittent access to sugar. These behaviours likely arise from intermittent access to sweet tasting or highly palatable foods, not the neurochemical effects of sugar." Unit: S-11. Source: PMC5174153. Grade: CONTESTED. Scope: Cambridge critical review; perspective, not new data. Permitted: The book must present "is sugar addictive?" honestly as an open scientific question: the animal bingeing evidence is real, but leading scientists argue the behavior is the product of the availability pattern, not a cocaine-like substance chemistry, and human evidence is thin. Prohibited: Asserting settled science that sugar is or is not addictive; quoting either side as if it were consensus. Empirical limit: grants binge behaviours under intermittent access. Safety: no consensus claim.

E-12 — not uniquely weak. Finding: "A recent analysis of two systematic reviews including 281 studies from 36 different countries found the overall pooled prevalence of food addiction using YFAS was 14% in adults and 12% in children. This reported prevalence is similar to the levels of addiction seen for other legal substances in adults (eg, 14% for alcohol and 18% for tobacco)." Unit: S-12. Source: PMC10561019. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: self-reported scale across countries. Permitted: A book may say that measurable populations meet addiction-like criteria for food, with tools validated across hundreds of studies and countries — the "you are not uniquely weak, this is a recognized population-scale phenomenon" frame. Prohibited: Calling the reader "diagnosed"/"addicted"; implying food addiction is a formal DSM-5 diagnosis. Empirical limit: self-report; not DSM-5. Safety: withhold diagnosis; use once only.

E-13 — same system, not same strength. Finding: "Refined carbohydrates or fats evoke similar levels of extracellular dopamine in the brain striatum to those seen with addictive substances such as nicotine and alcohol… The combination of refined carbohydrates and fats often found in UPFs seems to have a supra-additive effect on brain reward systems." Unit: S-13. Source: PMC10561019. Grade: MIXED. Scope: cross-study, cross-species comparisons. Permitted: A book may say brain-reward physiology shows sugar-rich foods engage the same dopamine system that addictive substances engage — grip, not equivalence. Prohibited: Claiming food "is like nicotine" as a proven equivalence; using mono-nutrient "sugar alone" findings to stand in for whole foods. Empirical limit: "seems to" is interpretation. Safety: grip language only.

E-14 — daily treat predicts future mood. Finding: "In prospective analyses, men in the highest tertile of sugar intake from sweet food/beverages had a 23% increased odds of incident CMD after 5 years… independent of health behaviours, socio-demographic and diet-related factors, adiposity and other diseases." and "Neither CMD nor depression predicted intake changes." Unit: S-15. Source: PMC5532289. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: Whitehall II, 23,245 person-observations, men significant, women no association. Permitted: A book may say large long-running cohort studies (and a checked-for-reverse-direction analysis) link higher sugar intake from sweet foods/drinks with higher later odds of common mental disorder in men — i.e., "the daily treat predicts future mood, not relief." Prohibited: Claiming sugar causes depression; generalizing the effect to all; implying mood made people eat (the reverse was not found). Empirical limit: observational; men only for incident CMD. Safety: association, not personal fate.

E-15 — world ceiling. Finding: "Consumption of free sugars should be limited to less than 10% of total daily energy intake, which is equivalent to 50 g (or about 12 level teaspoons) for a person of healthy body weight consuming about 2000 calories per day. Limiting further to 5% or less of total daily energy intake may provide additional health benefits." Unit: S-16. Source: WHO healthy-diet fact sheet. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: population recommendation. Permitted: The world's public-health authority sets an upper ceiling (10%) and a better-target (5%) because current consumption exceeds both. Prohibited: Framing any free sugar as toxic at any dose; inventing figures not in the guideline. Empirical limit: population ceiling, conditional 5% tier. Safety: ceiling, not taboo.

E-16 — deferred load, not free energy. Finding: "On average, US adults take in 17 teaspoons of added sugar per day. Sweetened beverages and bakery products are the top culprits." and "Eating higher amounts of added sugars… was linked with higher risks of heart disease and stroke. The higher the added sugar intake, the higher the risk." Unit: S-17. Source: Harvard Health summary of BMC Medicine 110,000+, 9 years. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: population review. Permitted: The "energy-food that quietly taxes the liver, blood pressure, and appetite-control" consequence set is institutional mainstream — the lift is not free energy, it is deferred load. Prohibited: Panic-level claims of sugar killing people; presenting the 17-tsp figure as universal. Empirical limit: population causal language; teaspoon conversions approximate. Safety: sober stakes, no personal doom.

E-17 — tooth stake. Finding: "Dental caries (also known as tooth decay or dental cavities) is the most common noncommunicable disease (NCD) worldwide, affecting 2.5 billion people… Dental caries results when plaque forms on the surface of a tooth and converts the free sugars… contained in foods and beverages into acids that destroy the tooth over time." Unit: S-20. Source: WHO sugars-and-caries fact sheet. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: mechanism claim. Permitted: "The same sugary treat is simultaneously feeding your loop and feeding acid against your teeth — the tooth doesn't get a vote" — an honest, concrete bodily stake. Prohibited: Claiming caries is inevitable from any sugar exposure; claiming sugar-free-diet cures existing cavities. Empirical limit: fluoride and brushing mediate. Safety: concrete stake, no inevitability.

E-18 — slump has a mechanism. Finding: "Reactive hypoglycemia (RH) is the condition of postprandially hypoglycemia occurring 2-5 hours after food intake." Mechanism: after high-carb loads, "first-phase insulin response decreases, blood glucose starts to rise after the meal. This leads to late but excessive secretion of the second-phase insulin secretion. Thus, late reactive hypoglycemia occurs." Unit: S-21. Source: PMC7192270. Grade: MIXED. Scope: post-meal physiology review. Permitted: The mechanism for a "3pm slump after the elevator-sugar" is biologically real — the lift IS insulin working, and the same surge can produce a real after-dip. Prohibited: Promise that everyone experiences measurable hypoglycemia after sugar; pathologizing ordinary readers; diagnosis claims. Empirical limit: symptoms often without measured 55 mg/dL hypoglycemia; RH in healthy non-diabetics contested. Safety: mechanism, not diagnosis; route to AD-A.

E-19 — everyday slump is not clinical hypoglycaemia. Finding: "For people who don't have diabetes, hypoglycemia is typically a blood glucose level lower than 55 mg/dL." And "Although it's rare, you can still get low blood glucose without having diabetes." Unit: S-43. Source: MedlinePlus hypoglycemia. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: federal definition. Permitted: The honest "crash" line for a general-audience book: the everyday slump after sweets is the body's insulin-and-glucose swing and postprandial-syndrome experience, NOT clinical hypoglycemia. Prohibited: Claiming ordinary sugar-eaters experience clinically low blood glucose; telling a reader they are hypoglycemic. Empirical limit: definition, not study; fasting-type threshold. Safety: protects credibility; route medical cases to AD-A.

E-20 — clean baseline. Finding: "The average human body requires 60-65 g of glucose per day, and during the first phase of a diet very low in carbohydrates this is partially (16%) obtained from glycerol, with the major part derived via gluconeogenesis from proteins… glycaemia, even though reduced, remains within physiological levels because of the fact that glucose is formed from two sources: from glucogenic amino acids and from glycerol liberated via lysis from triglycerides." Unit: S-22. Source: PMC3826507. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: gluconeogenesis physiology. Permitted: The book's clean-baseline claim: "Glucose is not a nutrient you must import one spoon at a time; your body runs its own glucose supply line from the food you already eat" — sugar is not our energy supplier, our liver is. Prohibited: Claiming carbohydrates are poisonous; prescribing ketogenic diets; saying the brain needs no sugar at all. Empirical limit: supports manufacture, not a diet prescription. Safety: no diet prescription; route metabolic disease to AD-A.

E-21 — days-long hump. Finding: "Controlled experimental evidence indicates animals experience HP food withdrawal marked by neural reward changes and behaviors consistent with withdrawal from other addictive substances. In humans, preliminary evidence supports subjective withdrawal-like experiences. However, most current human research is limited to retrospective recall." Unit: S-23. Source: PMC9786266. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: systematic review; human ProWS peak 2–5 days. Permitted: The "I get headaches/irritable/grouchy for the first days after I stop" experience has a real model and scale: symptoms peak at 2–5 days, then pass — the book can promise a time-limited hump, not a lifelong fight. Prohibited: Telling a reader they are medically withdrawing like an opiate addict; implying the hump never ends or needs replacement therapy. Empirical limit: human evidence preliminary plus retrospective. Safety: hump language only; no medical withdrawal label.

E-22 — teen evidence split. Finding: Two adolescent sugar-sweetened-beverage abstinence experiments conflict directly: one (n=25) reported increased craving and decreased motivation after 3 days; the other (n=149) found symptoms improved overall after 3 days. Unit: S-24. Source: PMC9786266. Grade: CONTESTED. Scope: adolescents, SSB only, 3-day windows. Permitted: The book must neither deny nor certify the sugar-withdrawal hump in teens; the honest line is "the evidence is genuinely split, and both camps agree it's a days-long, small-scale phenomenon." Prohibited: Choosing one study to "prove" withdrawal; claiming the hump doesn't exist or that it is severe. Empirical limit: no randomized control, no blinding. Safety: no teen prescription.

E-23 — wanting, not liking. Finding: "Incentive salience or 'wanting', a form of motivation, is generated by large and robust neural systems that include mesolimbic dopamine. By comparison, 'liking', or the actual pleasurable impact of reward consumption, is mediated by smaller and fragile neural systems, and is not dependent on dopamine." Unit: S-32. Source: PMC5171207. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: 25-year synthesis, animal plus human. Permitted: The mechanism that drives the loop is wanting, manufactured by dopamine, not liking — which is why "you didn't even enjoy the last bite, but you still wanted it" is exactly the mechanism, and why the loop outlives diminishing pleasure. Prohibited: "Dopamine = pleasure"; equating craving with happiness. Empirical limit: distinction is psychological/neural, not sugar-causes-addiction. Safety: no brain-damage claim.

E-24 — which foods. Finding: "The current study provides preliminary evidence that not all foods are equally implicated in addictive-like eating behavior, and highly processed foods, which may share characteristics with drugs of abuse (e.g. high dose, rapid rate of absorption) appear to be particularly associated with 'food addiction.'" Unit: S-31. Source: PMC4334652. Grade: SUPPORTED. Scope: n=120 plus n=384 rankings across 35 foods; chocolate, ice cream, fries, pizza, cookies, cake top; unprocessed bottom. Permitted: "Which foods?" is answered by data: the problems concentrate in the processed, fat+sugar, fast-absorbing foods — the exact ones the loop feeds on — while apples, strawberries, broccoli rank at the bottom. The book can promise "it was never the strawberry." Prohibited: Claiming these foods cause addiction in individuals; implying unprocessed sweet fruit is equally implicated. Empirical limit: cross-sectional rankings, preliminary. Safety: food-group clarity supports redefinition.

E-25 — villain engineering, bliss. Finding: "More is not necessarily better... As the sensory intensity (say, of sweetness) increases, consumers first say that they like the product more, but eventually, with a middle level of sweetness, consumers like the product the most (this is their optimum, or 'bliss,' point)." Unit: V-01. Source: Moskowitz via Moss/Cornucopia. Tier: industry insider account. Scope: lab optimization practice. Permitted: may state the industry tunes sweetness to a measured optimum to maximize craving. Prohibited: presenting the quote as the book's own science; naming individual retail products as proof. Empirical limit: trade-press interview, not trial. Safety: anger at trap, never at reader.

E-26 — villain intent, stomach share. Finding: "Rivals any other day, the C.E.O.'s and company presidents had come together for a rare, private meeting. On the agenda was one item: the emerging obesity epidemic and how to deal with it. ... Their stature was defined by their skill in fighting one another for what they called 'stomach share' — the amount of digestive space that any one company's brand can grab from the competition." Unit: V-12. Source: Moss on 1999 Pillsbury meeting. Tier: investigative report. Scope: business-model framing. Permitted: may state executives named the prize as stomach share. Prohibited: inventing conspiracy beyond the reported meeting. Empirical limit: journalistic reconstruction. Safety: industry as villain, reader as conned party.

E-27 — villain suppression. Finding: "In 1965, the Sugar Research Foundation (SRF) secretly funded a review in the New England Journal of Medicine that discounted evidence linking sucrose consumption to blood lipid levels and hence coronary heart disease (CHD)." Unit: V-32. Source: PLOS Biology 2017. Tier: peer-reviewed historical analysis. Scope: funding non-disclosure. Permitted: may state the review was secretly industry-funded and discounted sucrose-CHD evidence. Prohibited: extending to unproven carcinogen claims as settled. Empirical limit: historical documents. Safety: sober, no litigant language.

E-28 — night raid. Reader line: "We are like theives in the night, waiting for our families to go to sleep so we can raid the cupboard in private. For what, though? It doesn't even taste good!" Unit: lived bank-03 E-17. Source: Stop Being Sweet commenter Amy. Tier: lived secret-eating account. Scope: late-night private raid past pleasure. Permitted: may illustrate compulsion operating past enjoyment. Prohibited: generalizing to every reader nightly. Empirical limit: single comment. Safety: warm to person; shame described as trap product.

E-29 — cold turkey easier. Reader line: "It's been WAY easier to go cold-turkey on sugar like this than it ever has been when I've just tried to cut down on it a little in the past." Unit: lived bank-06 A-15. Source: StyleyGeek quit diary. Tier: lived moderation-failure contrast. Scope: first two weeks off versus cutting down. Permitted: may use as lived proof abstinence felt easier than control for this writer. Prohibited: promising the same ease timeline to every reader. Empirical limit: single blog. Safety: no willpower moral.

E-30 — three-week quiet. Reader line: "About three weeks into my experiment, something curious started to happen. I was no longer regularly craving sweet treats." Unit: lived bank-10 F-27. Source: BBC Future Hogenboom. Tier: lived craving-quieted. Scope: six-week no-added-sugar experiment, moderate baseline. Permitted: may illustrate cravings can stop coming within weeks and snacking can shift to olives, nuts, fruit. Prohibited: promising three weeks to all; presenting as trial efficacy. ## 3. Mantra and frozen-token sheet

Mantras M-A to M-I are the repeated incantations. Tokens M-J to M-M are settled verdicts invoked verbatim. Each debuts once, then echoes only where it is the natural next sentence. Hand-over gives the line to the reader for life.

M-A — "You have nothing to lose and everything to gain." Archetype: entry promise. Job: buys compliance with the reading contract at the outrageous promise. Debut: CH-01. Echo: CH-08, CH-14. Hand-over: final list carries it as the reader's reason to have read on.

M-B — "easily, immediately and permanently." Archetype: promise triad. Job: states the impossible-sounding contract with total confidence. Debut: CH-01. Echo: CH-07, CH-14. Hand-over: CH-15 returns it inside the congratulation, then stops arguing ease.

M-C — "the Sugar Trap." Archetype: trap metaphor. Job: makes stopping an escape, not a sacrifice. Debut: CH-02. Echo: CH-07, CH-08, CH-09, CH-12, CH-14. Hand-over: CH-15 hands it as the name for what was left behind.

M-D — "the Sugar Mite." Archetype: little creature. Job: externalizes the trivial physical whimper as a small starving parasite to be starved, whose throes are revelled in. Debut: CH-02 in passing. Echo: CH-07, CH-14, CH-15. Hand-over: whenever the faint whimper comes, the reader names the mite and rejoices it is dying.

M-E — "the Sweet Talker." Archetype: big creature. Job: names the belief-voice that mistranslates the mite's whimper as treat, fuel, love. The real target. Debut: CH-02 in passing. Echo: CH-05, CH-07, CH-12, CH-14. Hand-over: whenever a pro-sugar sentence arrives pre-labelled as the Talker's script.

M-F — "a genuine treat or lift." Archetype: illusion phrase. Job: fixes the perceived benefit as one demolishable object; thereafter the benefit is only ever called by this token. Debut: CH-03. Echo: CH-04, CH-05, CH-10. Hand-over: CH-15 keeps it only to name what never existed.

M-G — "a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling." Archetype: sensory phrase. Job: relabels withdrawal/craving in the book's words so the reader recognizes the mite, not hunger or need. Debut: CH-02. Echo: CH-07, CH-14. Hand-over: CH-15 gives it as the body signal that means the mite is dying.

M-H — "for the rest of your life." Archetype: stakes phrase, dual valence. Job: carries both threat and reward across the horizon. Debut: CH-01. Echo: CH-09, CH-12, CH-14. Hand-over: CH-15 closes with the free valence only.

M-I — "FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!" Archetype: terminal mantra. Job: the thought script kept forever; joyful response to any future thought of sugar. Debut: CH-14. Echo: CH-15. Hand-over: the book's final word on the subject; whenever sugar crosses the mind, think this.

M-J — "the Willpower Method." Archetype: anti-method token. Job: reattributes every past failure to the wrong method, never to the reader. Debut: CH-01. Echo: CH-08, CH-12, CH-13. Hand-over: CH-15 lists it once as what never to consult again.
