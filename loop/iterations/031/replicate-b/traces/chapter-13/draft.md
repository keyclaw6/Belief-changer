Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The photographed list. The closed book. The meal ahead, eaten hungry.

*This was never about losing sweetness. Sweetness has been handed back to you.*

IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

Put this page down and begin at the first page.

I mean that with love. These are saved reminders, not the method. They will hold you only when the sight behind them is yours. That sight is built page by page, easily, while you live normally and eat normally, until freedom feels obvious.

If you are holding this book at the end, free and clear, read on. Photograph the list. Close the book. Go and eat hungry with joy.

You came here trapped in the Sugar Trap, loving sweet hits and fearing life without them. You leave knowing they did nothing for you at all. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. You have followed them. Now let them keep you in one bright sentence and let life do the rest.

Do you need ongoing teaching to stay free? No. You need memory and permission. Memory lives in the list. Permission lives in the truth. You are free. Live as a free eater lives.

YOU HAVE ARRIVED

Take a breath and feel where you stand.

We lived years inside the hunt. The 3pm drawer. The midnight box. The bright packet bought for the children that never reached the children. The mouth filmed. The head foggy. The belly calling again an hour after the dose that promised to satisfy. We called that pleasure. We called that fuel. We called that love and reward and comfort.

You see it now. Loud, sickly, cloying, and empty. That verdict was proved on a plain plate under a plain light, and attention does not lie.

Ask yourself. When did one sweet hit ever close hunger the way a real meal closes it? When did the buzz ever carry you clean through the afternoon? When did the box ever say enough and mean it?

There is only one honest answer, and your body gave it.

This morning you woke clear. You ate hungry. You stopped satisfied. You walked past bright rows with a quiet head. You sat on the same sofa with tea and people you love and felt warmth whole. That ease is not luck. That ease is baseline returning on its own because you stopped knocking it sideways three times a day.

I stood where you stand. I remember the filmed mornings and the tally and the bargain all the way down the aisle. Just one for Friday. Just one because the week was hard. I remember how mighty that pull felt and how small it looks now. Yours looks small now because the belief that fed it is dead.

Rejoice. Celebrate. Feel relief flood the chest. You are not watching yourself. You are not guarding yourself. You are happy to be free, and each ordinary hour proves there was never anything to miss.

If you take medicine that affects blood sugar or live with diabetes or an eating disorder past or present, follow your clinician first and let this book do the belief work only.

WHAT WAS STOLEN IS RETURNED

Hear the fresh truth, because this is the part I saved for the end.

Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

We were born eaters. Hunger spoke. Food answered. Satisfaction closed. A peach eaten hungry was marvellous. Bread eaten hungry was wonderful. Fruit that spurts and perfumes, soup that warms the hands, market vegetables with colour still in them, a meal noticed and savoured and left finished — all of it exciting to a clean tongue. Then the Trap taught the tongue to shout for bright doses, dulled the power to taste real food, and sold the dullness back as need.

Do you see? BAD SUGAR never added sweetness to life. It borrowed sweetness from life, filmed the tongue, fogged the head, dug the low that called for the next hit, and left you flat, foggy and hunting while real food waited unheard.

Now the film is gone. Hunger leads again. Satisfaction closes again. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That is not a rule. That is nature remembered. Like that small child with food, stopping mid-bite to run off outside, you stop because the body says enough, and you run to your life with no counting and no guilt.

Think what it means to stay in that hunt for the rest of your life, paying every day for something you do not even need, mornings filmed, afternoons broken, evenings restless, teeth and mood and wallet paying rent to a con.

Now think what it means to walk clear and light for the rest of your life, happy to be free, mornings clear, shops quiet, meals that close, evenings whole.

Which rest of life do you want? You have chosen. The choice holds because sight holds it.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing back into the eater you were before dreaming covered you. A child does not quit bright packets to become dull. A child grows out of dreaming and tastes again. You have grown. Keep growing. There is nothing more to learn and nothing to unlearn.

Ask yourself. When real food tastes bright again, what was lost? When hunger closes clean, what is missed? When joy arrives without a wrapper, what need remains?

There is only one verdict that fits your mouth. Nothing was taken. Everything was returned.

GO ON AND LIVE

So close the book and get on with enjoying your life.

Eat breakfast hungry. Enjoy lunch to the close. Cook plainly or richly, as you love. Laugh loudly. Take seconds if hunger asks. Leave half if satisfaction closes early. Wake clear. Shop with a quiet head. Close evenings clean. Love people. Do work you care about. Rest when tired. Walk in noon light with bags in the hand and a plan for supper forming without fear.

Change nothing else to earn this. Same kitchen. Same shop. Same sofa. Same love. Freedom did not ask for a new life. It handed your old life back with the film wiped off. That is why ordinary days feel marvellous, wonderful, exciting. Not because days changed, but because you meet them present.

When a sweet thought crosses, let it come. Do not chase it. Do not hold it. See it as dead script walking past the window and answer with joy. Each answer makes the next thought fainter, until faint becomes gone.

Pity trapped hands, never envy them. Warm to the person, harsh to the con. Wish them your sight, silently. Let visible ease do the talking. Live the change, and the change speaks louder than argument.

I promise you as one who walks these days. The thoughts thin. The head stays light. The belly stays honest. The marvellous variety of real food keeps giving without taking. You will not need to study this. You will not need to guard this with effort. The vow protects itself because sight holds it.

Look ahead with excitement, not caution. Tomorrow breakfast hungry. Tomorrow shop clear. Tomorrow lunch that closes. Tomorrow sofa easy. Birthday bright with company. Tired evening kind with rest. No menu to police. No Fridays to worship. No tins to watch. Only hunger, satisfaction, real food, and life.

Ask yourself. Does that future sound like vigilance? Does a quiet head sound like effort? Does joy sound like loss?

There is only one word for it, and you own it. Freedom.
