Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The desk cleared after the fog. The grey plate you fear. The morning market with wet crates and warm bread. A plain favourite meal eaten hungry. The quiet belly hours after.

*Eating itself is joy, and hunger met with real food is the favourite you thought lived in bright paper.*

You Walk In With Clear Eyes

You walk into this room cleared, and I want you to mark what clearing means.

We lifted blame from your shoulders. We watched the drawer open at three o'clock and the fog roll in at four. We held the tight shoes in our hands and saw relief mistaken for power. You know now the lift makes the low. You know now a BAD SUGAR dose borrows and never feeds. You stand here as an investigator who has seen the afternoon read backwards.

Good. Keep that clear head. We need it for a kinder job.

There is one fear left standing that keeps many good people hovering at the edge. It is quiet and reasonable and it speaks in your own kind voice. It says, very well, the lift was a con, the drawer was a trick, the fog was rent paid for brightness. But if bright paper leaves my life, what will eating be? Will meals turn grey? Will joy go with the wrapper? Will I sit before plain food while others glow?

I hear that fear because I carried it for years. We all carried it. We called dessert love. We called the evening dose comfort. We called chocolate celebration. We told ourselves honest hunger was dull and only a sweet hit made food sing. We were not foolish. We were conned into reading our own body backwards.

The fact is eating was never grey. Hunger was never the enemy. Satisfaction was never found in bright paper. The joy you thought lived in the dose lived in hunger met, in food tasted, in enough reached. Once seen, it cannot be unseen.

Bring your mouth with you this morning. Bring your belly. Bring an ordinary day with no wrapper in it. Let us eat together and watch what happens without flinching.

The Grey Plate You Fear

Let me speak your fear out loud so you see I know your mind.

You say, "I want to get off the roller coaster, yes. I see the lift carries its own drop. But food without sweets sounds like punishment. A plate of vegetables. A slice of bread. An apple. That is fuel for a monk, not joy for a living person. Favourite food? You mean plain meals will feel like chocolate? That sounds like a play on words."

I welcome that doubt. Of course you doubt. You were taught for years that pleasure lives at the sweet end of the meal and everything before is duty. You were taught that children peck at dinner to earn pudding, that adults earn dessert after hard days, that love arrives with "treat when you feel you deserve one" and "celebrating with chocolate." You lived inside that reading so long the plate itself looks pale without the ending.

So let us test the reading with your own mouth as witness. No theory. Your tongue. Your belly. Your hours after.

When you were ravenous after a long walk and sat to soup and bread, did the first spoonful need a wrapper to taste marvellous? When you bit warm bread with butter and salt, crust cracking, crumb soft, did joy wait for permission from bright paper? When you ate ripe fruit with juice running down the chin, eyes closed, did you think, this needs improvement? Where did that brightness come from, if brightness must be bought in a dose?

If plain food is grey, why do hungry people glow over plain food? Why does a tired worker sigh with pleasure over stew? Why does a child laugh over peaches? Why did you yourself, on holidays when meals were real and clocks did not rule, linger at the table long after the plates were empty, talking, laughing, picking at crumbs, in no hurry for a hit? What held you there, if holding must come from sugar?

If the dose made meals joyful, why did dosed meals end flat? You know that ending. Sweet course eaten fast, barely tasted, mouth coated, thirst underneath, belly both full and hollow at once, mind already planning a stricter rule for tomorrow. That is not joy deepening. That is wanting re-lit by its own act. Joy settles. This recruits.

There is only one reading that fits your table without twisting. The meal gave. The dose borrowed.

It was the meal all along.

Morning Market

Come with me to the market, because I want you to feel this in your hands before we argue another word.

Early morning. Cold air. Cots rattling on stone. Wet crates stacked high. Greens dark with water, carrots with tops still on, roots with earth still clinging. Apples in boxes, blush and green, smelling of orchard. Bread from the corner oven, loaves split along the top, steam rising when torn. Cheese with rind. Eggs brown and speckled. Hands move fast. Voices call. A woman holds a tomato to your nose and says, smell. A man breaks bread and presses the warm heel into your palm.

Take it. Eat nothing yet. Only look and smell. Notice what your body does without instruction.

The mouth waters. The belly stirs. The eyes widen a fraction. Hunger comes clean, open, honest, with no twitch and no urgency for a named brand. It does not know drawer or clock. It knows smell and colour and emptiness ready to be met. Your body was built for this hour. It reads food the way eyes read light.

I speak as one who walked that market for years with dulled senses and called it duty. I bought bright packets at the till while real food waited untouched. I told myself vegetables were virtue and virtue was dull. We all told ourselves that. We walked past living smell to reach dead brightness and called the dead thing treat.

Buy plain things. Carry them home in paper. Lay them out. Nothing counted. Nothing forbidden. I lay down no eating plan here. I ask you to weigh nothing and forbid nothing. If you live with diabetes or take medicine that touches blood sugar, let your clinician lead and use this book for the belief alone.

Cook simply. Wash. Cut. Heat. Salt. Oil. The kitchen fills with honest steam. The bread cracks. The greens shine. Fruit waits in a bowl, unhidden, asking nothing.

Now wait until hunger comes. Do not eat by the clock. Do not eat by rule. Wait until the belly speaks cleanly, empty, ready, a little hollow, a little eager. Then sit. Then eat your favourite plain meal hungry, noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied.

Watch what happens bite by bite.

First bite after true hunger is thunder. Bread torn, chewed slowly, salt and wheat flooding the mouth. Soup hot, settling the belly with warmth that spreads to the hands. Vegetables with bite, sweet from the earth in their own way, not the bright flood that shouts, the quiet sweetness that feeds. Fruit after, cold and loud with juice, eaten slowly, licked from fingers. Company around you if company is there. Laughter if laughter comes. Quiet if quiet comes. The room holds you.

Notice. Savour. Stop when the body sighs.

That sigh is the whole book in one breath.

Hunger Comes Clean

Hunger has been slandered, and I will clear its name flat because you are ready for plain truth.

We learned to read every stirring in the belly as emergency. The old reading said hunger is pain to be killed fast with quick brightness, that a hollow belly means danger, that shaking hands mean need-something-sweet-now. We answered clean hunger with flood and called the flood feeding.

The fact is hunger comes in two voices and your body knows the difference the way ears know music from noise.

Clean hunger comes open and patient. A hollow ease. Attention turning to food. Mouth watering at smell. Belly asking, ready to wait while food is made. It grows slowly, eases the moment eating starts, goes silent for hours after enough. It carries no twitch. It names no brand. It does not know drawer or wrapper. It knows meal.

Pinch comes tight and loud. Urgency with edge. Mouth dry under sweetness. Mind fixed on one bright taste. Clock-bound, place-bound, wrapper-bound. It shouts louder an hour after feeding. It never goes silent for hours. It asks for more brightness to finish brightness.

You felt both. You called both hunger. They are not the same.

Watch a small child with a peach who stops mid-bite and runs to play.

You have seen her. No counting. No guilt. No rule. She eats because hunger calls, stops because satisfaction calls, runs because life calls. The half peach sits browning on the table and she does not mourn it. She does not carry it in a pocket for later. She does not dream of it at night. Hunger came. Food met it. Quiet followed. That is the machine working as built.

We were that child once. We ate, stopped, ran. No one taught us to graze from boredom or to pay ourselves in doses for virtue. The reading was installed later, bite by bite, ad by ad, till by till, until we mistook pinch for hunger and flood for feeding.

Your body still owns that child rhythm. It makes its own steady fuel from plain meals, from bread, from vegetables, from fruit, from protein, from rest, from sleep. Cut bright doses out entirely and the body does not go empty. The clean baseline stands on its own. I claim no diet over you. I claim only what the machine proves daily: the machine was complete before bright paper arrived and stays complete when bright paper leaves.

So when hollow comes, hear it cleanly. Do not kill it fast. Meet it. Chew. Taste. Linger. Let quiet come. Hunger met with real food settles for hours because hunger was built to be met, not to be tricked.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Enough Arrives

Satisfaction has been slandered worse than hunger, and I will clear its name with your own belly as witness.

We learned to read satisfaction as a full packet and an empty box. The old reading said enough means the box is finished, the plate licked, the mouth tired. We chased the end of the pack and called the chase appetite. We ate past sigh, past ease, past pleasure, into coating and thirst and fog, and blamed ourselves for lack of control.

The fact is satisfaction arrives long before the box ends, and your body announces it plainly if bright flood does not drown the signal.

True enough comes as ease. The edge leaves hunger. The mouth slows. Flavours grow less loud and more soft. The belly feels warm and settled, neither hollow nor stuffed. The eyes lift from the plate to the room. Conversation sounds more interesting than the next bite. The hand rests. A small sigh comes unasked. Food has done its job.

You know that sigh. You felt it over real meals in childhood, over holiday tables, over soup when ill, over bread after swimming, when no one counted and no one earned. The sigh said, enough, and you stopped without effort and without sense of loss. No one praised your will. No will was needed where glow had faded naturally.

A BAD SUGAR dose never lets that sigh arrive. It floods the mouth with brightness that shouts to the system to handle it fast, then drops you below where you started and calls the drop hunger. The mouth asks for more brightness to finish brightness. The hand reaches. The box empties. Regret arrives before swallow ends. You called that evening proof of weak will. It was proof the signal was drowned.

Ask yourself with the empty box on your knee. Did stopping feel hard because food was marvellous, or because the dose kept re-lighting want? Did real meals ever drive you to eat past pain while barely tasting? Did bread ever make you whisper, I feel like i cant stop, with crumbs on your chest at midnight? Did an apple ever make you hide wrappers? Which food lets the sigh arrive, and which food buries it?

If the dose satisfied, why did satisfaction never last? Real satisfaction lasts hours. Dosed brightness lasts minutes and leaves fog. If more was better, why did the third square taste duller than the first and the sixth barely tasted at all? Real pleasure deepens with attention. This dulled with repetition because dulled want is not pleasure deepening. It is want re-lit.

There is only one verdict that fits your evenings without twisting. Enough was never in the box. Enough was in hunger met and left in peace.

Learn that sigh again. Eat hungry. Eat slowly. Taste to the middle of each bite. Put hands down between bites. Talk. Breathe. When ease comes and flavours soften and the belly feels warm and settled, stop. Leave food on the plate without mourning. Left food is not wasted joy. It is proof joy arrived. The child leaves half a peach and runs to play. You can leave half a loaf and get on with enjoying your life.

Is Favourite A Trick

Now I will meet your sharpest suspicion head-on, because I want no whisper left alive under this roof.

You say, "Favourite food sounds like a trick. You tell me plain meals will be favourite. I hear wordplay. You mean I must learn to love dullness and call the lesson freedom. Where is the catch?"

I answer flat, with warmth and without hedging. There is no catch. The pleasure is real.

Favourite was never a property of bright paper. Favourite is what happens when hunger meets food that feeds and stops at enough. That meeting is built into your nerves. Chewing, smell, warmth, salt, juice, crunch, fullness easing hollow, company laughing, hunger going silent for hours. That is not philosophy. That is the body rejoicing at its own completion.

Think of the meals you already call favourite without thinking of doses. The stew after cold rain. The bread torn on a train. The fruit eaten on a wall in sun. The breakfast after long sleep, eggs and toast and coffee steam, eaten slowly while the day opens. Did those need bright endings to be marvellous? Did you leave those tables mourning? Or did you leave them fed, clear and settled, carrying hours of quiet?

Think of love you called dessert love. Evening sofa, film on, shared bowl, hand finding hand in the dark. "Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate." Those are your own old words for reward. I honour the love in them. I do not mock it. But where did the warmth live? In the paper or in the room? In the square or in the laughter, the rest after hard day, the permission to soften, the company, the story on screen?

Take the dose out of that room and play the evening again. Same sofa. Same film. Same hand. Same laughter. Same tired body easing. Same hunger met with real food before the film, belly quiet, mouth clean. Does warmth vanish? Does laughter thin? Does love leave with the wrapper? Or does the evening stand whole, warmer because no fog rolls in at the middle and no box empties with regret before the end?

You know the answer because you lived both evenings. Dosed evenings ended with coating and thirst and a second reach. Undosed evenings ended with ease and sleep. Which one loved you? Which one used you?

If favourite lived in bright paper, why did favourite need ever larger family packs to feel the same? Real favourites do not need escalation. The bread you loved at ten still moves you at forty with one bite. The peach you loved as a child still stops you mid-sentence. Dosed favourites need more, louder, newer, because crowding dulls and cue firing lights before the bite. More for the same effect is not love deepening. It is the trap tightening.

If plain food is dull, why does hunger make plain food sing? Dull things stay dull when needed most. Water stays dull to a thirsty man? No. Water is marvellous to thirst. Bread is marvellous to hunger. Hunger is the sauce no packet can bottle. You never needed to add brightness to hunger. Hunger already seasoned the meal.

There is no trick here. No hidden rule. No earning. No paying yourself for virtue. No treating yourself for surviving the day. The day does not need to be survived once fog lifts. The meal does not need to be earned once hunger is heard. Pleasure is not a prize at the sweet end. Pleasure is the whole meeting, from hollow to sigh.

You are not learning to love dullness. You are remembering that real food was always favourite before the reading told you otherwise.

Eat And Live

I know part of you still whispers, but my evenings are real, my comfort is real, my dessert love is real. Of course they are real. Your evenings are real. Your hunger is real. Your love of company and rest is real. What was never real was the gift inside the wrapper.

Look at your life without the old reading for a moment. Morning hunger met with breakfast eaten slowly, belly quiet till noon. Midday meal chewed and tasted, head clear for work. Evening meal shared, savoured, stopped at sigh, night free of rustle and regret. Fruit in a bowl, bright, unhidden, eaten when wanted, left when enough. No drawer ruling the clock. No box testifying against you. No Monday restart. That is not a monk life. That is an eater life. That is marvellous, wonderful, free.

Do you want that life? Of course you do. Every tired grazer does. You get it not by holding your hands behind your back beside bright packets, but by seeing the packets hold no light worth crossing the room for. When seeing is clear, the hands rest on their own. No clench is needed where no glow remains.

Cast aside gloom about grey plates ahead. There is no long mountain of dreary meals. There is a brief echo while rhythm steadies, then steady hours you already know from childhood, from absorbed days, from holidays when food was real. You are escaping fog, never losing flavour. Hunger returns as friend. Satisfaction returns as signal. Real food returns as favourite.

So hear me now as instruction, never as suggestion, issued with warm total certainty because the argument is won and the table is laid.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy lives in hunger met with a real meal, not in bright paper at the end.
- Clean hunger comes patient and open, goes silent for hours after enough, names no brand.
- Satisfaction arrives as ease and a small sigh, long before any box ends, and stopping there feels natural.
- Plain market food, bread, vegetables, fruit, tasted slowly, settles the belly and leaves the head clear.
- Evenings of company, laughter and rest stay warm and whole with the meal as the pleasure.
- The body makes steady fuel from plain meals, and no counting or forbidding is needed to eat well.
- Hunger and satisfaction can be trusted as guides, and eating can be lived as joy.
