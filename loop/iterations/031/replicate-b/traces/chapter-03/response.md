Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER
The morning table with yesterday's wrappers. The notebook with two columns. The desk drawer at half past three. The dentist's mirror. The till receipt. The quiet sofa at night.

*Forget what it does to you for a moment and ask what it ever did for you, and you will find you cannot name one true thing.*

You Walk In Cleared

You walk into this chapter cleared of one charge, and I want you to feel that clearance in your shoulders as you read.

Last night we stood together in that bright kitchen at ten o'clock. We watched the hand move before thought had finished. We watched one permitted biscuit turn into a blur and an empty box. We watched Monday virtue turn into Friday night. That was not free choice. That was not weak character. That was the Sugar Trap closing quietly round a decent person.

Keep that acquittal with you, because I am going to ask you to look with colder eyes than before, and I want no shame in the room while you look.

We all arrived here with the same fair-sounding defence. I know it harms me a little. Teeth, weight, money, the fog after lunch. But I get something from it. Life would be greyer without it. On balance the good outweighs the bad.

That sounds adult. It sounds honest. It is the last kind wall holding the trap up, and I am going to take that wall down gently, brick by brick, with your own ordinary day as the witness.

I speak as one who built that same wall for years. I told myself I knew the costs and accepted them. I told myself a man who works hard has earned his pleasures. I told myself the glow in the mouth was worth the heaviness an hour later. I believed it because I needed to believe it. We all do that when we feel caught. We call the cage a home so the bars hurt less.

We will not do that any longer. Not because I say so. Because your own day says so when we put it on paper.

The Fair Defence

Let me give your defence its strongest voice before I touch it, because I respect you too much to argue with a weak version of what you feel.

You say, I am not blind. I see the fillings. I see the coins going. I see the afternoons when my eyes go heavy. I am not a child. I have read the warnings. But there is another side. It lifts me. It rewards me. It makes the dull hours bearable. It makes birthdays birthdays. Take it away entirely and what is left? Plain days. Grey food. No lift. No treat. A life a little smaller.

Is that close to your inner voice? Of course it is. I have heard it in my own mouth a thousand times. We say it warmly, reasonably, almost proudly. We are weighing harm against benefit like sensible adults.

And that weighing is the trick.

For years we allowed the argument to be harm against benefit. Is the harm big enough to force me to change? Are the teeth bad enough? Is the weight bad enough? Is the tiredness bad enough? While we argue about the size of the harm, the benefit sits in the corner unexamined, safe, glowing, assumed.

What if the benefit were never there at all? Then what are we weighing?

I am not asking you whether BAD SUGAR harms a little or a lot. I am asking a colder, simpler question. What good is there at all? Name it. Point to it. Show it on yesterday's paper in plain ink.

If there is no benefit, the size of the harm stops mattering. A man does not negotiate a safe number of times to be pickpocketed once he sees the hand in his pocket.

That is the turn of this chapter. We leave the harm debate and we demand the benefit. You will find, as I found, as we all find when we look straight, that the column where the benefit should stand is empty.

The Day On Paper

Let us do something plain together. No lecture. No scolding. A plain audit of one ordinary day.

Take yesterday. Not a bad day. Not a secret day you hide from. An ordinary, respectable, moderate day. The kind you would defend to a friend.

Morning. Tea with two sugars, or that bright sweetened cereal that sings for three minutes. Mid-morning biscuit with the second cup, eaten standing because the phone rang, barely tasted. Lunch that was decent enough, finished with something sweet because the meal felt unfinished without it. Afternoon. That low, flat hour when the light goes thin and the eyes go heavy. Chocolate from the drawer. Cola or sweet tea. Ten bright minutes, then the fog again and the eyes rubbing. Evening. Pudding because it was there. Biscuits while the programme played, hand to mouth without looking. Night. The small sweet to close the day, to round it, to be kind to yourself.

Write it if you like on a scrap of paper. Time. What you took. What happened next. Mood. Mouth. Wallet. Minutes. Keep it ordinary. The trap loves to hide behind extremes. It says, ah, that was a bad day, that does not count. So take a good moderate day. The defence looks strongest there. That is where we will test it.

Now draw two columns beside that list. On the left, what did it do TO you. On the right, what did it do FOR you.

Be honest as a friend would be honest at midnight. No showing off. No self-attack. What is true? We are investigators tonight, not judges. A judge punishes. An investigator looks.

You already know the left column will fill itself. Let it fill. Then look long at the right.

What It Did TO You

Start with the left, because it writes itself from your own hand.

Mood first. Did yesterday feel steady and clear from morning to night? Or did it swing? Bright for minutes after each sweet hit, then flat, then twitchy, then calling for the next? Did the late morning dip arrive with that restless mouth? Did the afternoon bring heavy lids and thin patience? Did the evening bring that full-but-empty feeling where the belly is loaded and the mouth still calls for something? We rode that roller coaster so long we called it level ground. Short lift, fast drop, reach again. I have heard our own truth spoken back by those who walked out: I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop. That was not poetry. That was Tuesday.

Teeth next. You know this without me frightening you, and I will not leave you in fear about it. Free sugars feed the acid in plaque that eats at teeth. That is plain mechanism, not fate and not a verdict on you. Brushing and fluoride and a good dentist matter, of course they do, and no single sweet writes your future in the chair. The fact is frequent sweet doses through the day keep that acid working overtime. Look in your own mouth. The fillings. The bills. The careful smile in photographs. That column has entries, written by many small doses, not by one great sin.

Money and time next, because they add quietly and the trap counts on you never adding them. A bar here. A packet there. A drink at the station. A pudding no one truly wanted but everyone paid for. Crumbs on the coat. Wrapper in the bin. Add the week. Add the month. Add the minutes spent thinking about it, fetching it, queuing for it, hiding it, regretting it, promising about it tomorrow. Small coins make a heavy bag when carried daily. Small minutes make a lost week when lived daily.

And body load. I will say this flat, because honesty outranks force, and then I will lift the weight at once so you do not carry fear out of this room. Across whole populations, high added-sugar intake walks hand in hand with heavier hearts, higher pressure, tired livers, low-grade inflammation, appetite knocked off its hinge. Guidance now says keep free sugars below a tenth of daily energy, under fifty grams for most adults, with lower better, because ordinary eating sits far above it. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low. That is guidance for crowds, not a sentence on you. If you live with diabetes, take medicine that touches blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry worry around food, let your clinician lead on eating and use this book for belief alone.

Do you see? The left column writes itself from your own day. Mood swung. Mouth paid. Purse leaked. Hours leaked. Body carried.

I do not want you to change from fear of that column. Fear never freed anyone, and I will not leave you in dread. I want you to see it clearly once, then set it aside, because the real question is not there at all. Even if every entry on the left were small, the question on the right would still stand. What did you buy with all that?

And What Did It Do FOR You?

Now the right column. The benefit column. The column the whole defence rests on.

I ask you as an investigator, not as a judge. What did yesterday's sugar do FOR you?

Name one solid thing. Not what it did TO your mood for ten minutes. What it gave you that you did not already own before you took it.

Was it pleasure? Take that bright minute with the chocolate at half past three. Was the pleasure in the chocolate, or in stopping for a minute, sitting down, breathing out, being kind to yourself after hours of strain? Would a pause, a stretch, a laugh by the window, a warm drink held in both hands, have held the same pleasure without the fog after? When you strip the moment bare, what part needed BAD SUGAR?

Was it company? That shared dessert, that laugh round the table. Who made the warmth, the sweet or the people? Did the love live in the bowl or in the faces round it? Would the room have been cold without the sugar in it?

Was it hunger met? Did the sweet settle you cleanly till the next meal, the way soup settles, bread settles, fruit settles? Or did it wake hunger an hour later, louder, more nagging, dressed up as need? Did it satisfy, or did it recruit?

You feel the pinch because the column stays empty. Everything we write on the right, when looked at straight, slides to the left. Pleasure that was really a pause. Company that was really people. Hunger met that was really hunger woken.

Tell me in your own dialect, the words we all used when we defended the corner. It was sugary somethings to get me through. It was a treat when you feel you deserve one. It was celebrating with chocolate. I say them with warmth, because the person who says them is honest and tired and doing their best. The reading is false, and a false reading can be corrected without struggle.

Ask three plain questions and answer with your day in front of you.

First, if it truly gave, why did you need it again so soon? A true gift lasts. Bread lasts. A meal lasts. A kind word lasts. A walk in air lasts. Why did this gift evaporate in minutes and leave you lower than before? What kind of gift leaves the receiver emptier?

Second, if it truly helped, why did the helped hours get worse, not better? Why did the afternoons that were topped up fog hardest? Why did the evenings that were rewarded feel flattest? Why did the days you were good all week end with I just crave sweets and when i eat them, I feel like i cant stop?

Third, if it truly loved you, why did it take while it gave? Money, teeth, clarity, calm. Why does every small glow send a bill? What kind of friend charges you for the smile and keeps the change?

There is only one honest answer, and it lands like a key turning in a lock you have rattled for years. Nothing was given. Something was taken and a fraction was handed back as relief, and we called the handing back a gift.

Do you want to get off the roller coaster? Of course you do. Every rider does once they see the ride is going nowhere. You do not get off by riding better. You get off by seeing there was never a view, only motion.

A Genuine Treat Or Fuel

Here is the phrase that has run your life without ever being examined. I want to lift it into the light and hold it there until you see through it.

You believed BAD SUGAR was a genuine treat or fuel.

Say it slowly to yourself. A genuine treat or fuel. That is the whole case for the defence in five words. A treat says it gives joy. Fuel says it gives energy. Treat for the heart. Fuel for the body. Who would refuse both? Who would not pay a small price for joy and energy in one bright packet?

We breathed that phrase in before we could spell. Sweet means love. Sweet means reward. Sweet means go. Teachers gave it for good work. Grandmothers pressed it into tears with kind hands. Advertisements poured laughing families round it as if laughter lived inside the packet. Tired parents bought peace with it at the till. Shops placed it where tired hands wait. We learned to read every dip as a call for it and every triumph as a reason for it. Brainwashed? Yes, but warmly, by people who were themselves conned. There is no blame in it. There is only a story to see through.

Look at what those five words really cover when tested by yesterday.

Treat? A true treat can be taken singly, noticed, finished, forgotten. A peach can. A meal can. A square of good cheese can. Yesterday's sweets could not. They rushed. They blurred. They called for more before tasting ended. That is not treat behaviour. That is dose behaviour. A treat releases you. A dose recruits you. What releases after one and what recruits after one cannot be the same thing, whatever label the packet wears.

Fuel? True fuel builds and steadies. Real food eaten hungry gives hours of clear, quiet energy you can work and laugh on. Yesterday's top-ups gave minutes of buzz and hours of fog. That is not fuel behaviour. That is a bright loan with heavy repayment. A lender who hands you ten and takes twenty is not a helper, however kindly he smiles at the handover.

So the phrase fails at both ends. Not genuine. Not treat. Not fuel. A learned label stuck on a tiny body echo after the last hit, misread as proof of a gift. The low you felt was real. The reading of the low was false. The low did not prove you needed sugar. The low proved the last sugar was wearing off. We will open that echo fully later when your eyes are wider. For now take only this. A feeling can be real while its meaning is backwards.

Do you see the trick now? The trap taught you to read every dip as need-sugar and every triumph as deserve-sugar, so sugar stood at both doors, in and out. Tired? Take it. Happy? Take it. Bored? Take it. Kind to yourself? Take it. There was no hour it did not claim. That is not a treat. That is not fuel. That is a story that made itself necessary by naming itself as the answer to every feeling.

Once seen, those five words can never rule you again. Whenever the old glow whispers, test it against that exact phrase. Was that a genuine treat or fuel? Or was it a dose that took more than it handed back? You already know yesterday's answer. The right column stands empty. The left stands full. No honest investigator keeps paying for an empty column.

Who Rescued Whom?

Let me put one picture before you, not as a full story for tonight, only as a question to carry in your pocket.

What if a man pushed you into a ditch every morning, then came at noon with a clean hand and lifted you out, and you thanked him with all your heart for the rescue? Would you call him kind? Would you say you needed him? Would you pay him for the lift and tell friends how lost you would be without him?

Or would you see, with a flash that warmed you and angered you at once, that rescuer and pusher were the same man, and the thanks belonged nowhere?

Ask that of yesterday. Who made the four o'clock fog that the four o'clock bar then lifted for ten minutes? Who made the evening flatness that the evening packet then soothed? Who dug the hole that the next dose was thanked for filling? Who sold you the discomfort it then relieved?

We were warm, grateful friends to our rescuer. We never looked at his other hand, the hand that dug. That is not foolishness. That is how a good confidence trick works. It shows you the rescue and hides the push.

Keep that question. Let it do its quiet work behind your eyes. Every time relief arrives after a sweet, ask who sold you the discomfort it relieves. You need not answer fully yet. Later chapters will open the pusher's hand and the rescuer's hand side by side until no honest eye can confuse them. For now the question is enough. A question, once truly asked, goes on working while you sleep.

And notice what the question does to gratitude. We felt grateful to the packet. Grateful for the lift. Grateful for the break. Grateful for the kindness to ourselves. What if gratitude was the most conned part of all? What if we were thanking the hand that emptied us for handing a spoonful back?

For The Rest Of Your Life

Now widen the lens, because a day is a year in miniature and a year is a life in miniature.

Multiply yesterday by weeks. By years. By decades. Do not flinch. Look steadily, the way an investigator looks at a photograph, not the way a judge looks at a prisoner.

Do you want to ride that lift-drop-reach loop for the rest of your life for something that never gave? Do you want to pay the mood bill, the teeth bill, the money bill, the time bill, year after year, decade after decade, for an empty right column? Do you want to stand at midnight kitchens at fifty, sixty, seventy, telling the same kind lie, I've managed to be good this week, while the box stands empty again? Do you want your calendar marked by promises and collapses, Mondays and Fridays, birthdays used as permissions and holidays used as delays, all to keep a benefit that yesterday could not produce on paper?

Look at that picture steadily. Not to frighten you. To bore you with its sameness. The trap has no new act. Only repetition. The same bright minute. The same dim hour. The same regret. The same vow. That is what continuing means. Not drama. Dreariness. A treadmill painted like a treat cupboard, running for the rest of your life.

Now turn the picture over and look at the other rest of your life.

Picture mornings, shops, meals, evenings lived clear for the rest of your life with nothing to miss and nothing to count. Hunger arriving clean like a bell. Food tasting bright because hunger is there to meet it. Stop arriving quiet like a hand on the shoulder. No drawer calling at three. No till ambush when you are tired. No midnight rustle. No Monday vow because Friday never collapsed. That is not fantasy. That is the natural baseline waiting beneath the noise, ready to stand up the moment the false benefit is seen through. Your body remembers it. It never left. It waits.

Which rest of life do you want? The loop that takes and thanks you for the taking? Or the clear days that ask for no thanks because they take nothing?

You do not have to earn the second picture by effort. You have to stop paying for the first by seeing. That is the whole difference between a trap and a fate. A fate must be endured. A trap must be seen.

The Verdict On Yesterday

Read your paper again before we close, because a plain paper beats a loud lecture.

Left column full. Right column empty. Mood swung. Mouth paid. Purse leaked. Hours leaked. Nothing handed that you did not already own in the pause, the people, the meal, the rest.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Say it in your own mouth until it sounds ordinary, because ordinary truth is what frees. No drama. No struggle. A plain seen fact. The benefit you weighed against the harm never existed. There is nothing to weigh. The scales were a picture painted on the wall. Behind the picture, only the trap.

Once that lands, the whole harm debate falls away and we need never pick it up again. We need not argue whether one sweet harms a little or a lot. We need not count grams or trade horrors. If there is no benefit, the amount of harm stops mattering. You do not negotiate a safe number of ditches to be pushed into. You stop walking with the pusher and get on with your morning.

So here is your rule for the chapters ahead, and I give it as a command because I know it is true and I know it keeps you safe while the rest of the unravelling completes.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

SUMMARY
- An ordinary moderate day fills the harm column from your own life and leaves the benefit column empty.
- Frequent sweet doses keep mood swinging and keep plaque acid working, while money and minutes leak daily.
- Population guidance sits far above ordinary intake, and that guidance describes crowds, not a verdict on you.
- The glow that felt like help was relief handed back after taking, not a gift.
- Bad sugar fails its own defence because it is neither a genuine treat nor fuel that lasts and settles.
- The rescue you thanked points to the same hand that dug the hole.
- Continuing means repeating the same loop for years for nothing given, while clear days wait beneath the noise.
- The audit lands in one line that ends the weighing for good.
