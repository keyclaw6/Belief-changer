Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

IN THIS CHAPTER
- The kitchen table after supper
- The ordinary biscuit tin with the lid off
- The cloying mouthful tasted awake at last
- The spoken vow and the grin that follows

*Delay never made it safer — take one ordinary sweet with eyes open tonight and let the want end tonight.*

## THE TOMORROW THAT NEVER ARRIVES

We know that voice by heart because we all obeyed it for years.

"I'll start after the holidays. After the birthday. When work calms down. When I feel ready someday."

It sounds kind. It sounds sensible. It lets tonight pass without argument. It lets the tin empty with permission and pins the change on a cleaner morning that always sits one week ahead.

Watch that tomorrow at work across a real year, with investigator eyes, not with blame.

The birthday passes and Christmas takes its place. Christmas passes and the January sales arrive with bright packets at hand height. January passes and a hard week supplies a new reason. The box empties and refills. A new tomorrow gets stapled to each new lid. Each tonight borrows glow from a morning that never arrives to pay. Each today gets divided by rustle and hunt and second reach, while the mind rehearses a start date like a prayer.

Ask cold, without defending the date.

If the dose were harmless, why would you need a start date at all?

What does sunrise change in the nature of a dose that calls the next within the hour?

Has any tomorrow in your life ever arrived cleaner than today, or has it always arrived filmed, tired, with fresh reasons for next tomorrow?

Read your own calendars and the verdict shuts the haggle. Tomorrow is not a date. It is a lock.

I tell you this flat because flat is kind here. Waiting is trap-talk wearing wisdom clothes. Wisdom watches one evening whole and acts on what eyes report. Trap-talk begs for delay because delay keeps belief unexamined one more night. Examined belief dies fast. Delay keeps it breathing.

Think of all those Mondays we stacked behind us. We woke dull, mouth coated, head tipped toward fog, and told the mirror next Monday would be different. Next Monday woke the same because the same belief ordered the same timetable. The calendar did not fail us. The belief used the calendar.

You have already done the hard part. You have watched the Friday rule light the week louder. You have watched the house without packets still obey packets. You have watched the pale swap keep sweet o'clock ticking. You have watched the cinema row stand whole with hands empty. What is left to wait for? One more filmed morning to prove what twelve filmed years proved?

I state what I have watched a thousand times: I tell you plain that you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain, so test this night against your own mouth and hours and let eyes decide.

That divided night-talk they called the tug-of-war of craving and fear was never wisdom talking. It was two ropes pulled by the same hands. One rope pulls toward the packet with talk of deserve and hard day and one cannot hurt. The other rope pulls back with talk of teeth and waist and shame. Both ropes burn the hands. Both keep eyes on packets. Cut both at once and hands are empty for living.

The dose did not close the hollow. The hollow was the dose.

## TASTE IT AWAKE FOR ONCE

Tonight you take your last ordinary sweet. Not a laboratory dose. Not an experiment. Not a farewell staged with candles.

Take what you would have taken on any dull evening. The ordinary bar from the corner shop. The ordinary biscuit from the tin you know. The ordinary dessert spoon you lift on the sofa while the programme talks. Take it normally, as you always did, with one difference that changes all: pay attention all the way through.

We spent years dosing half-asleep. Hand moved before decision. Wrapper opened during talk. Chew ran under talk. Swallow ended before taste began. Mind supplied the glow while tongue reported almost nothing. That sleep was the con. Awake, the con cannot stand.

So sit down after supper, hungry no longer, quiet room, no rush. Unwrap slowly. Look at it. Smell it. Put the first bite in and do not talk over it. Follow it with full attention.

Notice the blast in the first three seconds, loud and thin. Notice how fast blast turns to film across teeth. Notice thirst stepping in behind film. Notice chew turning to churn, sweet turning to sickly, edges turning cloying at the back of the tongue. Notice the throat asking for water to wash away what mind called pleasure. Notice the second call rising inside the hour, thin and needy, asking for more chew to cover the film left by the last chew.

Stay with the whole hour. Do not get up for the second. Watch it.

Does the mouth feel clean or coated?

Does the head feel bright or tipped toward fog?

Does the belly feel met or drowned and then roaring back louder?

Ask from the chair, not from loyalty.

When did that coating ever feel like love?

When did that fog ever feel like fuel?

When did that churn ever feel like a treat earned?

Your own tongue settles it faster than labels ever could. Bread smells like bread within days when no hourly blast films it. Apples crack. Soup holds. Water tastes like something, not like lack. Tonight you catch the blast lying in real time. What mind gilded from memory, tongue reports as cloying when watched awake.

That is why this last instance matters. Not to punish. Not to memorize pain. To see. Seen once awake, it cannot be unseen. The glow was memory and timetable and talk laid over a coated mouth. The mouth told truth all along.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Take it ordinary. Taste it awake. Leave the second where it stands.

## READY MEANS CHAMPING, NOT PERFECT

Now the gate every honest reader reaches.

"Am I ready? What if doubt remains? What if I still hear packet-talk?"

Hear me with both ears. Ready does not mean calm without a ripple. Ready does not mean you will never think of sweets again. Ready means you see the con and you are champing to step out of it.

Ask yourself plain in the chair.

Do you see that no kept exception ever stayed kept?

Do you see that one permitted bite opens the hunt rather than closes the night?

Do you see that waiting never delivered a cleaner morning?

If yes rings back, even quiet yes under nerves, you are ready. Nerves are old timetable firing, not orders. Doubt is the last talk of a belief that knows it is beaten. You do not need to silence every ripple to act on what eyes report.

If no answers back, if some glow still feels like proof, do not summon grit. Close the tin, put this book down for an hour, and re-read the evenings you marked — the Friday week, the cinema row, the awake mouthful — until yes comes. What happens tonight happens easily, immediately and permanently, not by stages and not by strain. Strain would mean belief still half alive. Belief seen whole dies whole.

I stood where you stand, tin open, programme low, night humming with old hours. I did not feel heroic. I felt a small flutter under the ribs and a loud talk about tomorrow. I tasted awake, felt film and thirst and the thin call for more, and knew the talk was lying. I spoke the vow with a dry mouth and grinned before the grin felt full. The grin filled in within the hour and has widened since. I tell you that not to boast but to lend you my eyes for the next ten minutes.

You do not need a perfect mood. You need an honest yes. Honest yes is enough to carry the vow. The vow carries the nights after.

Do not wait for a holiday to clear. Holidays bank flags. Do not wait for stress to clear. Stress was always the excuse that proved the trap. Do not wait for the cupboard to empty. Empty cupboards never emptied minds. Tonight with a full shop outside and packets bright at every till is the cleanest night, because tonight proves shops can shout while you walk past without commentary from the mouth pulling at talk.

Like the lion pacing by the open cage from habit, not need, old feet may hover a step toward the old hour. Habit paces. Need is gone.

## WHAT THE NEXT FEW DAYS HOLD

Let me tell you true what body may report, so no surprise gets misread as loss.

For a few days there may be a faint hollow grumble in the evening, that thin mouth-noise asking for a hit at old hours. It peaks around the second or third day, then it fades to nothing. It is small. It is brief. It asks for attention, not for obedience.

That grumble is not hunger. Hunger knocks clean from the belly, asks for food, meets food, and goes quiet till next knock with a clear bell. This other noise scratches thin at the tongue and lips, asks for blast, meets blast, and calls again louder within the hour. Different knock. Different end. Once seen, you cannot mistake one for the other.

Do not weigh it. Do not time it. Do not narrate it to the room. Note it the way you note rain on glass — there, passing, nothing to obey — and keep hands on the next living thing. Wash up. Fold. Hold a cup with both palms. Talk whole through the programme to the end. Joke lands fuller because no rustle talks over it. Ten o'clock teeth brush over a clean mouth. Morning wakes with tongue clean and head light.

Ask those mornings plain.

Did the grumble grow or did it thin when unfed?

Did work hold or did work stop for lack of packets?

Did hunger meet meals and close, or did hunger vanish?

Your own days answer. Unfed, the small echo starves in days. Fed, the same echo orders weeks. Chemistry did not decide those two paths. Belief did.

If a clinician guides what you eat, follow them first — this book changes belief alone.

No dread belongs here. Dread was the old method talking, the one that told you nights would be a battle and mornings a sentence. You watched that method fail for years because it fought the call while feeding the belief. This night feeds nothing. The call thins because the line is cut. Small and brief is the whole story, and small and brief ends.

## TWO DOORS THAT MUST STAY SHUT

From tonight two thoughts will knock at different hours wearing your voice. Hear them now pre-labeled, so when they arrive they arrive as scripts, not as reasoning.

The first knocks on a hard day. Feet ache. Head hums. A kind voice offers rescue.

"You've been good. One kind dose will soften tonight. Hard day earns sweet."

We all heard it. We all obeyed it. Watch what that kind dose ever did on a hard day, hour by hour. Blast, film, thirst, second call, debate, dull head through the programme, broken sleep, duller morning carrying the same hard life plus coating. Did the day soften or did the evening divide? Did talk with the people you love lengthen or did rustle talk over it? The rescuer was the perpetrator wearing a kind coat. Pity that coat. Never wear it.

A hard evening asks for food if hungry, rest if tired, company if lonely, air if stale, sleep if late. Packet blast answers none and bills all. Ask the chair when the knock comes: did blast ever answer tired, or did it only add filmed to tired? Honest answer closes the door.

The second knocks on a bright day. Sun out. Work holds. Mouth quiet for a week. A light voice suggests a test.

"One cannot hurt. You've proved control. Just taste to prove it."

We all heard that one too, often with a smile and offered plate. Watch what one ever did after quiet days. First dose wakes mouth-noise. Mouth-noise wakes old hours. Old hours wake maps — shop-maps, smell-maps, excuse-maps. Maps wake hunt. Hunt wakes debate. Within days the timetable stands whole again, not because you are weak but because one kept flag orders the whole building. Pull every cord and the light goes out. Leave one cord and the same film plays.

Ask the bright day plain: if one is nothing, why does the thought need proving? Who asks to prove quiet by breaking quiet? Honest answer closes that door too.

From tonight, when any sweet memory crosses the mind — wrapper shine, birthday candles, cinema row — do not argue it away and do not push it down. Pushing keeps it alive as something missed. Answer it with one joyful thought that replaces the old talk whole.

Think, on purpose, with a grin, from the belly:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the shop when packets shout. Say it at the table when plates pass. Say it on the sofa when old hours ping. Not as a shield held with effort. As a report of fact that delights you each time it fires. The thought was a dead enemy knocking from habit. The answer turns each knock into a hit of delight that the knock holds no office.

Speak it aloud the first nights if walls allow. Hear your own voice owning it. Each firing wires delight where dread once wired. Within days the ping itself triggers the grin before memory finishes its sentence. That is not technique held. That is belief guarded, and guarded belief steadies behaviour on its own.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

Speak it solemn now, tin open, eyes open, last ordinary mouthful tasted awake. "I leave packet doses where they stand. I eat when hungry, stop when satisfied. I never keep a special sweet." Then grin, stand, clear the things, and live the evening whole. You walk into morning with tongue clean and head light, and morning confirms what vow conferred.

**SUMMARY**
- Tomorrow never arrived cleaner than today, and delay kept belief breathing one more night.
- One ordinary sweet tasted awake showed blast turning fast to film, thirst and call again.
- Ready meant a champing yes to what eyes reported, not a perfect mood without ripples.
- A faint grumble for a few days thinned when unfed and ended, while hunger met meals and closed.
- A hard-day rescue and a bright-day test both rebuilt the hunt, so both stay shut.
- A sweet memory gets answered with one joyful thought that turns each knock into delight.
