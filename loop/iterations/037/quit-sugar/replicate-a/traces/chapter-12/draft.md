Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The bright kitchen and kettle, the supermarket aisle and checkout queue, the shared table at noon, the evening sofa and lamp, the thin old-hour ping.

*Nothing now needs watching — breakfast, shops and evenings run on hunger, not on timetable.*

## THE FIRST MORNING

You wake with a clean tongue and a light head.

No film. No thirst hammering for wash-away. No dull weight behind the eyes. Feet to cold floor, wash, dress, kettle on. Cup down. Bread out. Window cracked for air. Hands move through small tasks without stopping to debate packets.

We lived years of divided mornings. We woke coated, mouth sour, head tipped toward fog, and told the mirror the day would need sweet o'clock to start. We reached before hunger knocked. We called the reach breakfast. We called the fog hunger. The clock ordered the hand.

Watch this morning with investigator eyes.

Hunger knocks clean from the belly in its own time. It asks for food. It meets food. It goes quiet till next knock with a clear bell. You meet it seated. You eat porridge, eggs, bread, fruit — plain favourite food, noticed, chewed, savoured. You stop when the belly says enough. Mouth clean after. Head level through the first hour.

Like a child who drops a half-finished peach and runs off to play, you stop mid-plate when satisfied and turn to the day.

Ask cold from the table.

Did the plate need a blast to hold you till noon?

Did the head need a hit to clear, or did hunger met and closed clear it?

When did a coated mouth ever start a day better than a clean one?

Your own hours settle it. The blast never built morning. Morning stood whole once timetable talk fell silent. What mind gilded from memory, tongue reports plain in daylight.

I stood where you stand on my first morning after the vow. Kettle loud, street grey, hands a little quick from old hours. I ate seated, washed the bowl, stood at the window with the cup warm in both palms. Bins out. Lights going off house by house. Shops dark at the end of the road. Nothing in the mouth pulled toward them. Nothing in the hands reached. That quiet in the hands told more than talk ever told.

Keep hands on the next living thing. Make the bed. Rinse the cup. Fold the cloth. Tie shoes. Small tasks done whole wire the day where debate once wired. No clock pings for sweet o'clock. No hunt runs underneath about one more. Talk runs whole. Work holds because hunger holds, not because packets hold.

This is the hour to notice. Not to test. Not to count. To notice. Tongue pink. Water tasting like something, clean and cool. Breath running longer to the bottom. Shoulders dropping half an inch as you watch. The day does not ask for blast. The day asks for food when hungry, water when dry, air when stale, movement when stiff. Packet blast answered none and billed all.

## THE AISLE HOLDS NO ORDERS

Mid-morning the street wakes fully and you walk it as you always walked it.

Tills bright. Packets at hand height. Cartoon faces. Price flags shouting two for one. Smell pumped from the bakery corner. Checkout ambush stacked low for small hands and tired hands. We walked this aisle for years with mouth-noise leading the trolley. Hand lifted before decision. Eye snagged on shine. Mind supplied glow while tongue remembered almost nothing. That sleep was the con.

Walk it awake today.

Push the trolley steady. Buy what the household eats — vegetables from the market stall, fresh bread, milk, eggs, fruit, soup for the cold shelf, coffee for guests who drink it. Read no labels with fear. Hunt no packets with virtue. Let packets shout while you pass. Shout holds no office now.

You stepped out of the Sugar Trap, and the aisle proves it hour by hour.

Ask from the trolley, not from loyalty.

If those packs gave love, why did the basket always leave you hunted rather than held?

If those doses gave fuel, why did the walk home always sag toward the next?

When did a full bag ever quiet the mouth, and when did a met belly ever roar?

Honest answer closes the haggle. The packs never fed hunger. Hunger meets meals and closes. The packs fed timetable. Timetable never closes. It only resets the clock for the next reach.

Watch other trolleys without commentary from the mouth. A tired mother piles bars for the evening. A young clerk grabs a bottle for the desk. A grey man counts coins for the small cake at the till. We were them. We know the rustle, the hunt, the second reach, the debate underneath. We know the divided night that follows the bright packet.

"They look settled with their bars. They look happy. Do I miss that?"

Hear the thought as habit pacing past, then answer it plain.

Does that really look like enjoyment — hand moving before decision, wrapper talk over talk, box empty before swallow ends?

Does that look like a treat, or like a timetable wearing treat clothes?

You are not deprived while they indulge. They are hunted while you eat. Pity that hunt. Never envy it. Envy would mean the dose still held a gift. Pity means eyes report true. Eyes report a mouth filmed, a head fogged, a night divided by rustle and hunt. Pity keeps hands steady on the trolley bar. Envy would turn eyes back to packets.

Say nothing at the till. No speech. No sideways look. No counsel offered to strangers. Pay, pack, lift the bags. The woman ahead fumbles with coins and a bar for the child. The child whines for blast. The mother sighs and pays. You hold your bags and wait your turn. No superiority. No mission. Only quiet knowledge that blast never quieted a child longer than minutes, and hunger met with a meal quiets for hours.

## THE SHARED TABLE AT NOON

Noon asks for food, and food answers.

Sit down. Plate warm. Soup steaming. Bread torn, not counted. Apples cracking. Cheese plain. Water cool in a tall glass. Company talking through the meal to the end. Joke lands fuller because no rustle talks over it. You eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, and let the plate stand.

We spent years dosing through meals. Sweet drink beside the plate. Dessert glow laid over hunger before hunger spoke. Chew running under talk. Swallow ending before taste began. Mind supplied the glow while tongue reported film. That sleep thinned every meal to a hunt for the next.

Taste awake today.

Notice bread smelling like bread. Notice soup holding salt and heat. Notice apple juice running sharp and clean. Notice water tasting like something, not like lack. Notice the belly meeting, holding, closing with a clear bell. Notice the mouth staying clean after, teeth smooth, tongue pink, head level for the afternoon.

"Is this all eating will ever be now? Plain food and water?"

We all hear that thin question at the first shared table. It sounds like loss. It is timetable talk wearing plain clothes. Answer it from the plate.

When did blast ever make a meal more shared?

When did film across teeth ever make company warmer?

When did thirst behind film ever make water less welcome?

Your own table answers. Love was meal, company, hunger met. Blast only talked over all three. Removed, all three stand louder. The table holds without commentary from packets.

Eat what the household eats. Cook as you cooked. Serve as you served. Change nothing else in the day. Morning tasks, noon tasks, walk, calls, washing, mending — same hours, same hands, same rooms. The body returns to its own knock without a programme. Hunger leads. Satisfaction closes. Instinct, met daily, steadies within days. No new rules. No new system. No weighing. No timing. No narrating to the room.

If guests bring dessert, let dessert sit where it sits. Offer tea. Offer fruit. Offer cheese. Take what hunger asks among real food and leave the packet doses where they stand. No speech about method. No refusal staged with effort. A quiet "No thank you, I had plenty" ends it. Plenty is true. Plenty is report, not shield. Hands stay on cups and plates. Talk stays on work and weather and children and plans. The table does not divide around you. The table continues whole.

## THE EVENING SOFA

Evening comes with the lamp and the programme, the blanket over the chair arm, the cup held with both palms.

This was the hard hour for years. Feet ache. Head hums. Old hours ping at eight, at nine, at ten. Hand remembering the tin. Eye remembering the shop-map. Mind supplying glow from birthdays and dimmed lights and passed chocolate. That memory-talk was the last talk of a beaten belief. Hear it as talk, not as orders.

Sit down in the same dent on the sofa. Programme talking to the end. Blanket folded. Lamp straightened. Hands doing the next living thing while the mouth stays quiet. Wash up. Fold. Hold the cup. Talk whole through to the credits. No hand lifts at old hours. No debate runs underneath about earning, about proving, about one cannot hurt. Talk runs whole.

When any sweet memory crosses the mind — wrapper shine, birthday candles, cinema row — do not argue it away and do not push it down. Pushing keeps it alive as something missed. Answer it with one joyful thought that replaces the old talk whole.

Say to yourself with a grin — BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the shop when packets shout. Say it at the table when plates pass. Say it on the sofa when old hours ping. Not as a shield held with effort. As a report of fact that delights you each time it fires. The thought was a dead enemy knocking from habit. The answer turns each knock into a hit of delight that the knock holds no office.

Ask from the chair.

Did the evening need blast to soften, or did rest soften, company soften, sleep waiting at ten soften?

Did the programme need rustle, or did whole attention make joke and story fuller?

Did the mouth need coating to close the day, or did clean teeth and smooth tongue close it?

Your own sofa settles it. Blast never closed evenings. Blast divided them — blast, film, thirst, second call, debate, dull head through the programme, broken sleep, duller morning carrying the same life plus coating. Whole attention closes them. Clean mouth closes them. Ten o'clock teeth brush over a mouth that feels smooth and rested. Bed cool, room dark, head on pillow. Sleep comes the way it comes to a child who has run all day — because the day closed.

That is the proof to collect, night by night. Not a streak to guard. Not a count to keep. A lived evening, whole and yours, and whole was what you wanted from sweets all along.

## WHEN THE OLD HOUR PINGS

For a few evenings there may be a faint hollow grumble, thin mouth-noise at old hours asking for a hit. It peaks, then it fades to nothing. It is small. It is brief. It asks for attention, not for obedience.

That thin ping is the Nibbler dying.

Do not weigh it. Do not time it. Do not narrate it to the room. Note it the way you note rain on glass — there, passing, nothing to obey — and keep hands on the next living thing. Wash up. Fold. Hold a cup with both palms. Talk whole through the programme to the end. Joke lands fuller because no rustle talks over it. Ten o'clock teeth brush over a mouth that feels smooth and rested. Morning wakes bright and steady.

Hear the difference plain, because difference ends confusion.

Hunger knocks clean from the belly, asks for food, meets food, and goes quiet till next knock with a clear bell. This other noise scratches thin at the tongue and lips, asks for blast, meets blast, and calls again louder within the hour. Different knock. Different end. Once seen, you cannot mistake one for the other.

"What if I hear packet-talk louder on a hard day? What if the voice sounds like mine?"

We all hear those two knocks wearing our voice. Hear them now pre-labeled, so when they arrive they arrive as scripts, not as reasoning.

The first knocks when feet ache. "You've been good. One kind dose will soften tonight. Hard day earns sweet." Watch what that kind dose ever did, hour by hour. Blast, film, thirst, second call, debate, dull head through the programme, broken sleep, duller morning carrying the same hard life plus coating. Did the day soften or did the evening divide? A hard evening asks for food if hungry, rest if tired, company if lonely, air if stale, sleep if late. Packet blast answers none and bills all.

The second knocks on a bright day. "One cannot hurt. You've proved control. Just taste to prove it." Watch what one ever did after quiet days. First dose wakes mouth-noise. Mouth-noise wakes old hours. Old hours wake maps — shop-maps, smell-maps, excuse-maps. Maps wake hunt. Hunt wakes debate. Within days the timetable stands whole again, not because you are weak but because one kept flag orders the whole building.

If a clinician guides what you eat, follow their advice first — this book changes belief alone.

If a dose ever passes the lips by accident or by old hand — a bite taken half-asleep at a party, a spoon lifted to be polite — do not turn it into a verdict on yourself. One blip never rebuilt a timetable. Belief rebuilt timetables. Note the rumble the way you note a rumble strip under tyres — there to warn, not to punish — learn the bend, steer back to the middle of the road, and drive on. Spit if you wish. Drink water. Eat when hungry next knocks. Sleep will still come clean. Morning will still wake bright. The body copes with an accidental crumb. The mind must not steer toward it on purpose. A slip forgiven as lesson steadies hands. A slip mourned as failure loosens them. Let lesson steady.

Ask the morning after plain.

Did the grumble grow or did it thin when unfed?

Did work hold or did work stop for lack of packets?

Did hunger meet meals and close, or did hunger vanish?

Your own days answer. Unfed, the small echo starves in days. Fed, the same echo orders weeks. Belief decided those two paths, not chemistry, not character.

## NOTHING ELSE TO CHANGE, NOTHING TO PREACH

Live the same life with quiet hands.

Same shop. Same street. Same table. Same sofa. Same work. Same people. The method asks for no new programme, no trigger maps, no avoidance routes, no announcements to the household. Change nothing else in the day and let hunger and satisfaction steady the rest. Hunger met daily teaches faster than rules. Satisfaction noticed daily holds longer than vows rehearsed as strain.

I tell you flat what I watched in my own kitchen and in a thousand kitchens lent to me in letters. Those who rearranged the whole house to avoid packets kept packets alive as something powerful and missed. Those who walked the same aisle, sat at the same table, kept the same sofa dent, and let old hours ping once or twice without obedience watched the ping thin to nothing within days. Habit paces a night or two by the open door, then lies down. Need left with the vow. Only habit paces.

Do not preach.

Fresh delight makes the tongue quick to counsel. Hold it. The tired mother at the till, the clerk with the bottle, the grey man with coins — they hear counsel as judgment and judgment hardens timetable talk. Let visible quiet do what argument cannot do. Eat seated. Walk steady past packets. Laugh whole through the programme. Brush teeth over a smooth mouth. Wake bright. When asked, answer brief and warm.

"I stopped feeding timetable talk. Mornings run plainer now."

One sentence. No lecture. No warning. No mission. If they scoff, smile and pour tea. If they argue, do not argue back. Argument reopens debate in your own head that vow closed. Leave the door open for their own eyes. Eyes change minds. Talk rarely does.

Pity, never envy, those still dosing. That is the script to keep.

When a colleague unwraps at three and the blast smell crosses the desk, hear the old thought — "sugary somethings to get me through" — as a recording, not as hunger. Watch the ten-minute buzz tip toward fog by four and the second reach follow. Watch the roller coaster climb, drop, climb. You know that ride from the inside. You know the short term lift then fast drop, the I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling at dusk, the I feel like i cant stop after the first. You know celebrating with chocolate never celebrated — the scene celebrated while the dose talked over it.

Does that desk ride look like fuel or like timetable?

Does that evening box look like love or like hunt?

Does that checkout queue look like choice or like con?

Honest seeing turns each exposure into confirmation. Each packet shout confirms the aisle holds no orders for you. Each wrapper rustle confirms your sofa stays whole without rustle. Each birthday table confirms the room makes the warmth while doses film the mouth. Confirmation wires delight where dread once wired. Within days the ping itself triggers the grin before memory finishes its sentence.

Keep the line clean. One kept exception keeps the whole building lit. No Friday allowance. No house-rule sliver. No tomorrow promise. No pale swap with sweeteners to keep sweet o'clock ticking. Swap the product and you remain a user, still hunted. Pull every cord and the light goes out. Leave one cord and the same film plays. Totality holds because totality tells truth — inside BAD SUGAR there is no kept sweet that stays kept. A permitted sliver carries the binge within the hour. Evening boxes emptied after one prove it. Friday weeks creeping to Thursday and Saturday prove it.

That is not a rule to carry with effort. It is a description of what eyes watched. Watched once awake, it cannot be unseen.

13. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

**SUMMARY**
- Mornings run on hunger met and closed, not on timetable and blast.
- The aisle shouts while the trolley holds steady, and other baskets call for pity, not envy.
- Shared meals stand whole on real food, company and satisfaction noticed.
- Evenings close clean on the sofa with whole attention and smooth teeth at ten.
- A thin ping notes a dying echo to smile at, and any blip serves as rumble warning, never as verdict.
- The same life continues with quiet hands, no preaching, and no kept exception.
