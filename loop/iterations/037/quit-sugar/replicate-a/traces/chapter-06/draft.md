Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- Grey plate fear at the kitchen table
- Market morning, warm bread, garden vegetables, orchard fruit
- Hunger arriving slow and the clear stop
- Favourite meal eaten hungry to satisfaction

*Plain hunger met with plain food is the favourite you feared only sweets could give.*

## THE GREY PLATE FEAR

You have watched the drawer hammer for what it is.

You have seen the ten bright minutes borrow from the hours that follow and call the borrowing power. You have seen the bell ring louder as taste grows thinner. That seeing stays. I do not ask you to re-argue it. I ask you to look at the next fear that rises the moment the hammer is seen, because this fear keeps many good eaters hovering.

It speaks in your own voice, quiet and reasonable:

"Very well. The lift was a loan. But what is left? If BAD SUGAR goes, what will eating be? A grey plate. Chewing because I must. Swallow and duty. No lift, no glow, no love in it. I will sit at meals that taste of cardboard while others laugh over dessert."

I know that voice because I carried it for years. We all did. We who lived in the loop learned to call dessert love and sweet hits joy, and we cannot picture joy without them. The mouth has been loud so long that quiet sounds like nothing. The tongue has been shouted at so long that plain sounds like punishment.

Let me answer you flat, as one eater to another, with no play on words and no catch hidden behind the back.

There is no trick in the word favourite here. I do not mean you must train yourself to call dull things delightful. I do not mean you must lecture yourself into liking what you do not like. I mean the pleasure itself was never in the packet. The pleasure was in hunger met, in food tasted, in company kept, in a body that closes a meal with a clear bell and turns gladly to living.

We confused loudness with taste. That confusion is natural. When every meal ends with a shout, the shout is remembered as the point. When every afternoon is punctuated by rustle and chew, the rustle is mistaken for appetite. When every celebration is photographed with chocolate in hand, the chocolate steals the credit for laughter that belonged to people.

Ask your own table memory, without blame and without theory:

When did plain bread last taste almost sweet on the tongue — was it after a day of grazing on biscuits and sips, or after hours without food when hunger stood clean and open?

When did an apple last crack loud and flood the mouth — was it perched on top of a full sweet stomach, or eaten hungry on a bench with juice running?

When did a meal last leave you quiet and content and ready to move — was it the meal chased by dessert and followed by picking, or the meal that closed itself?

You know the answer from the inside. We all do when we stop defending the packet and watch the mouth.

Hunger is not an enemy to be dosed into silence. Hunger is the cook. It seasons before salt seasons. It makes simple things vivid. It makes stopping clear. We were taught to fear it, to read its first stir as emergency, to cover it fast with loudness. The loop taught that reading because a frightened eater reaches fast and reaches for one thing.

I stood at my own kitchen for a decade and misread that stir nightly. Six o'clock. Stir in the belly. I called it sweet need. I took biscuits while the supper cooked and called it taking the edge off. Supper then arrived muted. I ate fast. I finished full and unmet. I took dessert to close what never opened. I finished fuller and still looking toward the cupboard. That night I told myself food without sweets would be grey. The next noon, busy and late, I ate the same supper hungry — no biscuits before, no dessert after — standing for a moment, then sitting, chewing slow. It tasted bright, deep, complete. Same cook. Same pot. Different mouth. Hunger had done the seasoning I had tried to buy.

That difference is not poetry. It is mechanics you can watch tonight.

A mouth deafened by loudness all day cannot hear plain food. A tongue filmed by films cannot report true flavour. Nerves rung hourly cannot settle to taste. Remove the hourly ringing and the instrument tunes itself. No effort tunes it. No holding tunes it. Only hunger and pause and chewing tune it, the way night tunes eyes to see stars.

You fear grey. Grey is what the loop leaves, not what hunger brings. Grey is the filmed tongue, the furry teeth-edge at half past three, the muted supper that needs more salt and more sauce to pierce through. Bright is what hunger brings to plain food. Bright is not buzz. Bright is flavour noticed, chewed, enough.

## MARKET MORNING

Come with me on an ordinary errand, eaten hungry, with nothing to prove.

Saturday morning. Market air cool. Canvas shades flapping. Crates stacked high. Greens still damp. Carrots with tops on. Tomatoes warm from the van. Apples piled red and green. Figs split with ripeness. Grapes dusty blue. Melon heavy in the hand. Bread from the corner stall, loaves split at the top, crust dark, steam still leaving the cut when broken. Cheese plain. Soup from yesterday waiting at home. Water.

You have not grazed since supper. The belly stirs, dull and open, not sharp, not pointed at one packet, glad of food in general. That openness is the sign to trust. You buy what looks alive to you. No list from me. No portions from me. No windows, no weighing, no counting. Your hunger leads. Your nose leads. Your hand weighs a melon and puts it back and takes another.

Home. Kitchen light. Bread torn, not sliced neat. Crust cracks. Crumb pulls soft with holes. Butter melts if you use it, oil shines if you use it. Greens washed, shaken, dressed with salt and oil and lemon. Vegetables roasted or boiled or eaten raw — as you like them, as your people cook them. Fruit washed and set in a bowl for colour and smell. Nothing staged for a photograph. Nothing performed as virtue.

You sit because hunger called, not because the clock bullied. First bite hungry.

Notice what happens when the mouth is not deafened.

Bread crust sounds in the ears. Crumb chews with resistance then gives. Salt lifts. Oil rounds. Greens bite then yield, bitter then sweet in the same chew. Tomato floods, acid then mellow. Apple cracks, juice sharp then mellow, skin tart. Water tastes of something — cold, mineral, alive — not thin. Chewing slows by itself. Talk comes easy between bites. Shoulders drop. Breath deepens.

Halfway through, pleasure does not thin the way packet pleasure thins. Packet pleasure peaks at bite one and slides by bite four into chase. Meal pleasure deepens through the middle. Bite ten tastes more than bite one because hunger has opened further and flavours have layered. The body reports as you go: still open, still glad, then easing, then fulling, then done.

Done arrives as a clear bell. Not stuffed. Not sick. Not regretful. A plain sense: enough. The hand stops without order. The mind turns to other things — dishes, walk, paper, child, work — without commentary from the mouth. No hand hovers. No tongue looks. No wrapper calls from the bin because there is no bin full of witnesses.

That closing is satisfaction. Real satisfaction, bodily, untaught, older than any packet. It needs no praise. It is known by what follows: quiet mouth, level head, hands ready for the next living thing.

I ate such a meal last autumn and understood in the fingers what argument had not yet reached. Hungry after a walk, I sat to bread and soup and apples with no plan to learn anything. I ate. I stopped. I sat a minute with the bowl empty and the mouth clean and thought, this is what closing feels like. No tug to chase. No film to clear. No sour talk to begin. Only fed and ready. I had chased that readiness for years with hits that promised it and always billed it away.

Watch the difference in rhythm, because rhythm tells truth when talk deceives.

Hungry hands at a real meal: slow to start, open to many foods, glad of chew, pausing, sipping, sighing, done. Packet hands at the drawer: quick, narrow, pointed at one thing, tear, bite, chew fast, reach, rustle, look, reach again. Same hands. Two masters. One closes. One loops. Once felt in the fingers, the two cannot be confused again.

You do not need a new palate to taste this. You need hunger and pause. The palate you own under the noise is the palate you owned as a small eater before bright packets taught timetable. It has not left. It has waited under film.

## HUNGER HAS ITS OWN BELL

Let me set hunger beside the tug so you can tell them apart in the body, not in theory.

Hunger arrives slow. It builds dull and wide low in the belly. It grows over an hour, not a minute. It is glad of food in general — bread, soup, vegetables, fruit, meat, rice, whatever your table holds. It welcomes chewing. It deepens flavour. It closes with enough and lets the mind move on. It does not point, does not shout, does not fix on one packet. It does not return ten minutes after eating. It returns hours later, honest, open again.

The tug arrives fast. It jumps. It points at one thing, sweet and loud, now. It does not welcome food in general. Offer it bread and it sulks. Offer it soup and it waits. Offer it the packet and it quiets for minutes then returns louder. It never closes with enough. It closes with full-and-still-looking. That contradiction — tired of it and asking for it — is its signature. Hunger never does that. Hunger tires of waiting and welcomes food. The tug tires of the thing and demands more of the thing.

Learn the two by watching one afternoon with eyes, not by whipping yourself.

Two o'clock. Lunch behind you. Mouth clean. Work level. Four o'clock approaches. Stir low. Ask: is it wide or pointed? Would bread gladly do? Would soup gladly do? Would water and pause and air gladly do for now? If yes across foods, that is hunger early, or honest tiredness asking for pause. Answer in kind: pause, water, air, movement, then meal when open.

If no — if only the packet will do, if bread sounds dull and only loudness sounds right, if the thought is narrow and urgent and fixed — that is echo, not appetite. Note it. Smile at the narrowness. Let the narrowness show itself as narrow. Hunger is broad. Echo is narrow. Broad feeds. Narrow loops.

The fact is, the body makes what the brain needs from ordinary meals — from protein and slow starch and vegetables and fruit and water — hour by hour without drama. If you carry diabetes, use medication that shifts appetite or glucose, are pregnant, live with past or present eating trouble, or face medical risk in changing meals, speak with your clinician and follow their guidance, holding this book to belief change alone. For most of us the everyday dip is not a rare clinical low. It is spike-then-drop misread, or honest tiredness misread, or hunger misread as order for one packet.

I speak carefully here because I prescribe nothing. No menu from me. No good-food list and bad-food list from me. No portions, no times, no forbidden tables. I am not your clinician and this is not a diet. I speak of what hunger is and what echo is, so you can trust the one and watch the other pass.

Ask the body questions and let memory answer clean:

Does hunger ever demand one brand in one wrapper at one hour, or does it welcome many foods at many tables?

Does hunger ever quiet for ten minutes then return louder for the same thing, or does it quiet for hours after eating and return open?

Does hunger ever leave the mouth furry and the head foggy and the hands restless, or does it leave the mouth clean and the head level and the hands ready?

There is only one honest answer, and you feel it in the belly. Hunger feeds. Echo rents.

That distinction carries its own quiet relief in the chest when seen, not as thrill, but as daylight after confusion.

Hunger was never the enemy. Hunger was the guide the loop taught you to fear so you would reach fast and reach narrow. Trust the guide and the guide provides. Note the tug and the tug loses its authority, because authority belonged to hunger all along.

## THE MEAL THAT CLOSES ITSELF

Now inhabit the close itself, because stopping is the part the loop stole.

We who grazed forgot what enough feels like in the body. We knew stuffed and still-looking. We knew full-and-hollow. We knew regret before swallow ends. We forgot the clear bell that says done and means it.

Sit hungry to a favourite plain meal — not a test meal, not a punishment meal, your own favourite among real foods. Market vegetables cooked as you like them. Fresh bread. Fruit for colour. Soup, rice, potatoes, meat, beans — whatever your table calls food when no packet leads. Eat noticed.

First mouthfuls: deep gladness. Hunger meets flavour and both rise. Chew. Put the spoon down between bites if you like. Talk. Breathe. Taste salt, oil, acid, sweet that lives inside vegetables and fruit without shout.

Middle: steady pleasure. Not louder, deeper. The body reports still open. The mind stays at table, not drifting to cupboard. Hands slow by themselves. No order slows them. Hunger eases at the edges.

Last third: the bell. Easing becomes fulling becomes done. Flavour still good, but want thins. A bite left on the plate looks fine left. The hand rests. The mouth feels clean, not filmed. The belly feels fed, not churned. The head feels level, not wired. You stop because stopping is what the body asked. No counting stopped you. No rule stopped you. Hunger started, satisfaction stopped.

Sit a minute after. Notice what does not happen. No hand moves to look. No tongue searches for more loudness. No sour talk begins. The meal is closed. The mind turns — to dishes, to walk, to work, to rest — without a tail of picking.

That turning is joy. Eating itself is joy when hunger leads and satisfaction stops. Not thrill. Not buzz. Joy as function fulfilled, as thirst quenched, as sleep after true tiredness. Plain and owned.

Compare it with the packet close you know by heart, to feel the difference in the mouth.

Packet close: mouth loud then furry, tongue tired and still asking, belly full and hollow together, head wired and dull together, hands restless, bin loud with witnesses, talk sour, evening spoiled. Hunger close: mouth clean, tongue content, belly fed, head level, hands quiet, table calm, evening owned.

Which would a body choose if choice were clear? Which did your body choose before bright packets taught otherwise?

I watched my own table change without my ordering it. No new rules on the fridge. No announcements. Only hunger trusted and stopping noticed. Meals grew brighter month by month as film left the tongue. Salt needed less because flavour needed less piercing. Portions settled by themselves — sometimes larger after a long walk, sometimes smaller after a large lunch — led by openness, not by arithmetic. Weight settled where living settled it, without my chasing it. Sleep deepened because evenings closed instead of looping. Mornings woke cleaner because nights closed instead of grazing.

I claim no numbers here and promise no outcomes. I report rhythm. Rhythm is enough to trust.

Hunger opens. Food satisfies. Satisfaction stops. Mouth quiets. Life continues. That ring is older than any wrapper and stronger than any timetable once seen.

## FAVOURITE WITHOUT A TRICK

Now the suspicion itself, given full air, because I want you to feel answered, not manoeuvred.

"You say favourite. You mean I must call plain food my favourite and pretend sweets were never liked. That is words. I liked what I liked. Dessert was love. Cinema chocolate was warmth. Birthday cake was the point. Calling bread my favourite will not make it so."

I welcome that plainness. I said it myself at the table for years. I believed love lived in dessert and warmth lived in the shared bar and the point of the evening lived in the bowl with candles. Take those away, I thought, and love thins, warmth cools, the point blurs.

Look with investigator eyes at where the warmth actually lived.

Think of a meal that held you — people round, talk low, dishes passing, hunger met bite by bite. Laughter came. Stories came. Hands reached. Faces softened. Was the softness in the sugar bowl, or in faces, voices, hunger eased together? Remove the bowl in memory and replay the same faces, same voices, same hunger eased. Does warmth vanish? Or does warmth stay and only loudness leave?

Think of fruit eaten hungry outdoors — apple cracked on a wall, grapes pulled walking, melon cold at noon. Sweetness floods, clean, with chew and water and fibre around it, closing with enough. Did you ever call that grey? Did you ever need to be taught to like it? No teaching was needed because hunger taught it. That sweetness belongs to meals, to orchards, to tables. It was never the target here. Enjoyment of sweet food in its living place is not forbidden and never was. The reward-frame that says love must arrive as a packet after virtue is the frame that kept the loop alive, not the fruit that grows sweet on its own.

The packet sneaked a ride on living warmth and claimed the ride as its own gift. Isolate the variable and the credit returns.

When lights dim and laughter rises and a hand finds yours, warmth is people, story, leisure, touch, laughter. The bar in the other hand adds rustle, chew, film, and a bill in fog an hour later. It adds nothing to laughter that laughter did not own. When birthday candles blow and faces shine, joy is faces, singing, wish, belonging. Cake in hand adds loudness and chase and the tired-and-asking contradiction after. It adds nothing to belonging that belonging did not own.

Ask it of your own brightest table:

If dessert made love, why do the most dessert-heavy evenings often end flattest, with picking and sour talk and heavy sleep?

If chocolate made warmth, why does warmth stay bright in memory when you picture faces and voices, even with the sweet removed in mind?

If cake was the point, why does the point hold steady at tables where hunger is met with favourite real food and the bell closes clear?

There is only one honest answer. Love was meal, company, hunger met. The packet rode along and took the praise.

And here is the flat sentence with no catch, lived before it is spoken:

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read it as permission, not as rule. What you want means what your hunger answers to gladly among real foods — bread you like, vegetables you like, fruit you like, dishes your people cook — not what narrow echo orders. When you are hungry means when openness calls, dull and wide, not when the clock or the tug bullies. Stop when you are satisfied means when the bell rings clear, enough, mind turning. Never punish yourself again means no sour talk after, no penance meals, no arithmetic of worth. Hunger guides. Satisfaction stops. No whip walks beside the plate.

That sentence will sound too simple to carry freedom. Simplicity is its strength. Bodies are precision instruments. They report hunger clean and satisfaction clear when loudness does not shout over them. Trust the report.

Picture the small eater we all were, who stops mid-bite and runs off to play. No counting taught that stop. No will taught it. Hunger opened, food gladly met it, enough closed it, play called louder than more bites. That stop lives in you still under timetable. Hunger has not forgotten it. You have only to hear it again over quieter days.

Hunger was the flavour all along.

That is the verdict to carry to the table: flavour lives in hunger met, not in loudness added. Loudness deafened. Hunger seasoned. Seasoning returns the moment ringing quiets.

You do not face grey plates. You face bread that cracks, greens that bite then sweeten, fruit that floods then closes, meals that open with gladness and close with quiet, evenings that stay owned. You face eating as joy, not as management.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food joy was never placed by packets; openness of hunger seasons plain meals and makes them bright.
- Hunger builds slow and welcomes many foods, while the narrow tug points at one loud thing and never closes.
- The body answers ordinary meals hour by hour without drama, and clean baseline without BAD SUGAR holds steady.
- Sweetness living inside fruit and meals keeps its place at the table; love at celebrations belonged to people and hunger met together.
- Satisfaction arrives as a clear stop — fed, quiet-mouthed, ready to live — and needs no penance after.
- Grey belonged to filmed tongue and looping evenings; brightness returns as ringing quiets and hunger leads.
