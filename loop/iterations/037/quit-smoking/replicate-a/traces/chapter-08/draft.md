Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

IN THIS CHAPTER
Cold lino under bare feet. Steam off the kettle. The cleared plate. The yard door and wind on the cheek.

*The morning, the meal and the pause were whole before the dose touched them — live them plain and you taste what the dose took.*

### THE FEET THAT FOUND THE PACK FIRST

I know that reach because I lived it for years.

Alarm. Half dark. Feet have not touched the floor before the hand finds the pack on the chair. Thumb on the wheel. First pull on empty lungs while the eyes are still half shut. Throat bites. Head turns. You stand at the window in your shirt and call that waking.

We all did that reach. We told ourselves the day could not start without it. We read the bite as a key turning in a lock.

Look closer with me at one plain morning lived without the tube in it.

Feet touch cold lino. That cold is real and it wakes better than smoke ever did. You stand. Legs take weight. Spine lengthens. Kettle on. Click. Cup out. Milk. Spoon. The small clinks line up in order while water climbs to boil. Steam lifts. You wrap hands around the hot cup and drink standing at the window.

What is missing.

You hear the old voice at once, and I want you to hear it in its own words so it cannot whisper later:

“But I need one to get going. Mornings will be flat without it.”

Keep that voice up in daylight and test it against the cup in your hand.

When the steam warms your face, does the face need paper on top of warmth to feel warm. When the hot tea loosens the throat, does the throat need bite on top of loosening to feel loose. When the head clears by the second sip, was it the night leaving you, as it leaves every sleeper, or was it a dose giving you something a sleeper without doses does not have.

Ask from the lino, not from dread.

If the first dose woke you, why did you need a second before the post came. If the bite cleared you, why was the mouth papery and the chest a touch tighter after the clearing than during it. If the window moment belonged to smoke, why do you remember the light on the roofs and the kettle song and the neighbour door — none of which came out of paper.

You know. The waking was getting up, water, heat, light, movement. The dose only rode along and charged a fee.

We were taught to read a thin morning hum as proof we cannot start. What hums then is only an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

That tug is not you. It is not hunger. It is not character. It is the echo of yesterday’s topping asking to be topped again. Drink the tea. Move. Dress. Answer the kettle. Within minutes the hum thins because attention has somewhere true to go. The morning does not stall without a light. The morning runs as mornings run when nothing cuts it into slices.

I stood where you stand. I feared blank mornings more than I feared coughing on those stairs where I hauled while a non-smoker climbed light beside me. Then I lived three mornings plain and saw the trick. Feet, floor, water, cup, light. Nothing in that line asked for smoke. Smoke only stood in the line and took credit for the line moving.

The morning is not a hole to fill. It is a start that starts itself.

### THE PLATE THAT KEPT ITS TASTE

Now take the meal-end, the second place we swore needed it.

Plate cleared. Crumbs. Warmth in the belly. Talk still going around the table or quiet humming from the cooker. Hand moves by habit. We used to say the meal was not closed until the light. We said the taste needed a full stop. We said that pellet after the paragraph of food made the food more.

I do not mock that memory. We all laid that pellet down like a seal.

Live one meal-end plain and watch what taste actually does.

You finish. You put the fork down. The mouth is full of what you ate — salt, butter, sharp green, sweet end. You sit a minute. You breathe through the nose. The flavour stays and rounds and slowly settles. You sip water and the sip lifts the last of it clean. The belly warms. Shoulders drop because eating done is pleasant in itself.

Light a dose on top of that and see what changes. First pull wipes the mouth to ash. Tongue goes papery. Nose fills with stale. The lingering food goes. What you called closing the meal was painting over the meal.

Whose pleasure was that.

Ventriloquize the defence fully:

“I love the taste with coffee. Food just tastes better finished with a stick.”

Then ask the questions whose only honest answer ends it.

When the flavour lingered after you did not light, was the lingering weaker or clearer. When you lit after Sunday roast and the roast vanished under ash in two pulls, did the dose keep the taste or take it. When a non-smoker at your table closes his eyes at the sweet and smiles with nothing in his hand, is his meal unclosed or is yours the one that was interrupted.

You know because your tongue knows. The food did the pleasing. The dose did the wiping.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

Read that in the mouth, not on the page. The itch for a seal after eating arrived after dosing began. Before the trap you ate and were done and the done was good. Inside the trap the end of eating calls for topping because topping taught the mouth to expect wiping. Take the wiping away and the end returns to what it was: taste staying, belly warm, talk continuing, hands quiet.

We gave the work of the cook and the good of the grain to paper. Put the credit back. The cook did it. The grain did it. Your tongue did it. The dose only sneaked a ride on a finished thing and made you pay with the next want.

A finished thing needs no seal but its own finish.

### THE STEP OUTSIDE AND THE AIR

Third room. The break. The door step. The yard air on the face.

Mid-morning the eyes blur. Shoulders knot. The screen swims. The brain offers its old translation at once: step out, light, clear the head, then work again.

We obeyed for years. Out. Flick. Pull. Stare at brick and sky. Back in feeling steadied for minutes. We called that air.

Live one break plain and see where the steadiness lives.

You stand. You push the chair back. You walk to the door. That standing and walking already loosens the knot — blood moves, eyes lift from close print to far roof. You push the door. Cold air hits the cheek. You breathe deep through the nose and the chest widens. You hear traffic, gull, hammer down the street. You roll the neck. You stretch the hands wide with nothing to hold. You stand five minutes and look at sky.

Go back in. Sit. The line you stumbled on reads clean now.

What cleared it. The paper or the standing, the far looking, the cold, the moving, the pausing.

Ask from the step, not from the story.

If the dose cleared the head, why did the head cloud again before the hour turned with no new work done. If the yard needed smoke to be a break, why does the dog-walker, the postman, the mate who never lit take the same step and come back bright with nothing lit. If five minutes of air steadies, what did ash add to air that air did not already carry.

You know. The pause did it. The legs did it. The eyes changing distance did it. The cold did it.

We cut our own pauses into coins and then praised the coin for the pause. Take the coin out and the pause remains — longer, cleaner, uncut. Hands empty around a hot mug warm as well as any hand with paper in it. In fact warmer, because no fingers chill holding a burning tip into wind, no eyes water against drift, no mind counts pulls against time.

Think of that pellet after the paragraph you once paid for each finished chunk. The paragraph pleased because thought landed. The landing was yours. The pellet only rented the minute after landing and sent a bill for the next landing. So with the break. The air pleases because lungs pull and blood moves. Leave paper out and lungs still pull, blood still moves, head still clears. The break is not emptied by missing smoke. The break is returned whole.

Do not borrow the yard smoker’s eyes for this one scene. Watch what he does. He leaves the laugh, feeds, returns, fades, leaves again. His break is hired out to timing. Yours, lived plain, is yours from door to chair.

A pause that pauses you is a break. A pause that bills you is a lease.

### THE BODY THAT ALREADY KNOWS

There remains the fear under all three rooms.

“But my body will not play without it. Taste will go dull. Breath will stay short. Breaks will drag.”

I take that fear seriously because I carried it. Let us answer it with the body itself, not with talk.

Your tongue already proved itself at the plate. Given one meal without wiping, it held flavour longer. Your nose already proved itself at the kettle, catching steam and toast and rain on the step that ash used to cover. Your chest already proved itself on the walk to the door, widening on cold without bite in it. If you feel ill or worried about stopping, speak to a clinician; I give no medical orders here. My point is about credit, not medicine: the tasting and breathing you just lived were done by you, not lent by paper.

We were taught that dullness without topping means topping gives brightness. It is the other way around.

Dosing dulls between toppings and then mutes the dullness for minutes. Undosed, the dullness thins because nothing re-dulls it. That is why the second plain morning tastes sharper than the first, and the third break walks lighter than the first. Not by effort. By absence of covering. The body does not need teaching to do this. It does it as water clears when mud is no longer stirred in.

Consider the hum that visits between. It comes. Thin pull under the ribs. Eyes to the pack from habit. It passes when you drink, move, answer, chew. For many it sharpens for a short spell when topping stops and then thins into wider clear gaps, though not to the same hour for every chest. That short swell is not proof the dose was you. It is proof topping taught an echo. Unfed, the echo asks, gets no answer, asks weaker, forgets. Refed, it learns again to ask loud.

So put each room back where it belongs.

Morning starts with feet, floor, water, heat, light. Meal ends with flavour staying, belly warm, talk continuing. Break works with standing, far looking, cold, moving. None asked for smoke. Smoke asked to stand in each and take the bow.

Your body knows this without lesson. Trust what your tongue and lungs just told you in plain minutes.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

**SUMMARY**
- Morning starts with feet, floor, kettle and light, and runs complete when no dose cuts it.
- Meal-end flavour stays longer when ash does not wipe it, and the finish belongs to food and company.
- A break steadies through standing, moving, looking far and cold air, not through lighting.
- The thin hum between is an echo of topping, not the self, and it thins when left unfed.
- Work pleased because thought landed, food pleased because taste lingered, pause pleased because breath and blood moved.
