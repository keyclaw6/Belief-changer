Chapter 2
ARE YOU REALLY CHOOSING THIS

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The garage door half-open to the night, the spray can and the mint tin on the shelf, the hand reaching before thought, the bright minute of lighting and the stale shame after

*You never chose to need cigarettes; you were tricked into needing them, and that need now chooses for you.*

### YOU SAY YOU CHOOSE IT

"I like smoking. Nobody forces me. I could stop if I wanted to."

I hear you. I said the same for years, and I meant it. We all say it. It sounds like common sense. No one holds a cigarette to your head. You buy the pack. You take out the stick. You light it. Whose hand is that if not yours?

Let me ask you plainly, as one smoker to another, and answer honestly in your own head.

If you freely choose it, why do you need this book in your hand to go on choosing it? If you like it, why do you do it in hiding? If no one forces you, why does your hand move before you have decided?

Think of any small pleasure you truly choose. A second piece of cake. A longer walk because the evening is fine. Do you hide that from your partner? Do you stand in the cold to do it? Do you spray the curtains after, chew mints, wave your shirt in the doorway, and hope the kids will not smell it on you? Choice does not behave like that.

We tell ourselves we choose because the alternative frightens us. If we choose, we are in charge. If we are in charge, there is nothing to face. The fact is, we were conned out of charge a long time ago.

Did you choose the first ones? Coughing, dizzy, sick on the back step, pushing through nausea because the older lads did it and you wanted to look grown. No one chooses that for pleasure. We persisted because we believed there was something on the other side once we learned. That belief was fed to us by friends, by films, by adults stepping out for a moment that looked like rest. We breathed that air. It was not foolishness. It was phoney information.

And once we learned to inhale without choking, something else took over. We stopped choosing to smoke and started needing to smoke in order to feel normal. The hand learned the path to the pocket. The mind learned the story to cover it. That story is what I mean when I speak of the nicotine trap.

The nicotine trap is not a phrase to frighten you. It is a plain description of what happened. You walked in on false promises, the door closed quietly behind you, and now you call the inside your choice.

### THE GARAGE DOOR AT MIDNIGHT

Let me stand with you where choice would never need to go.

It is near midnight. The house is quiet. Your partner is asleep. The kids are asleep. You tell yourself you want some air. You slip to the garage, half-close the door against the cold, and light quickly, cupping the glow as if it might be seen.

You do not savour it. You hurry it. You listen for a footstep on the stairs. You blow toward the crack in the door. When it is done you grind it hard under your shoe, tuck the end deep in an old tin so no smell leaks, spray hard around your jacket, work a mint, wash your hands, wave your shirt cold at the door. You Febreeze the hell out of yourself and walk back in holding your breath a little, hoping.

Warm reader, listen to me. I am not scolding you. I have done every inch of that dance. So have thousands of decent, honest smokers I have sat with — teachers and drivers and nurses and fathers and grandmothers. There is nothing wrong with you. There is everything wrong with the need that makes a grown adult hide in his own garage like a schoolboy.

Ask yourself while you stand there with the spray still in the air.

Would a free pleasure make you lie to people you love? Would enjoyment need mints and excuses? Would choice need a locked door?

I know a man who kept a separate coat for smoking, hung in the garage, never brought inside. I know a woman who brushed her teeth three times before bed, not for hygiene but for concealment, then lay awake listening to her own chest. I know closet smokers who smoke only at work, only in the car, only when no one is looking, and tell everyone including themselves that they have it under control.

Control does not hide. Hiding confesses the trap.

Notice the divided self in that moment. One part of you lights. The other part cleans up after the lighting as if after someone else. You love the lighting minute and you hate being the person who needs it. Both feelings are honest. The split is not proof you are weak. It is proof you are caught — one voice speaking for the need, one voice speaking for you.

A free man does not need to deceive his own household to enjoy himself for five minutes. A chained man does.

### THE HAND THAT MOVES FIRST

Watch your own day while you read, with the pack beside you as it always is. Do not change anything. Only watch.

The phone goes down after a sharp exchange. The meal ends. The car door shuts. The kettle clicks. Before thought has finished, the hand is already moving — pocket, pack, tap, light. You notice you are smoking after you are smoking.

That is not decision. That is a pull acting faster than decision.

We flatter ourselves that we weigh and choose each dose. We do not. We light to quiet a restlessness and call the quiet enjoyment. We mistake the speed of the reach for desire, and desire for choice.

If this were choice, you could take it or leave it with equal ease. Try the plain test in your mind, without doing anything yet. Picture the morning you cannot find the pack. Feel that small flare in the stomach. Is that the calm of a man missing a pleasure, or the edge of a man missing a fix? A pleasure missed leaves you a little disappointed. A fix missed makes you search drawers, borrow from a neighbour, step out in rain in slippers. Which man have you been?

You do not search drawers for something you merely like. You search because something in the body calls and the mind obeys before pride can object.

I am not asking you to fight that hand. Fighting is not the way out and I will never ask it of you. I am asking you to see it clearly for what it is. A choosing hand pauses. A hooked hand reaches.

When did your hand learn to move before you? Not when you were born. Not when you were a child breathing clear on the stairs. It learned after the first hundred doses taught it the circuit. That learning was not your fault. But it is not your freedom either.

Free choice does not hurry. Hiding hurries. Reaching hurries.

### HEAVEN FOR A SECOND, THEN SHAME

Now let us be fair to your own experience, because I will never lie to you about what you feel.

There is a minute, the lighting minute, that feels like heaven. The match flares, the first drag bites, the shoulders drop a fraction. For a few seconds something in you says, ah, that is better. Then the stick burns down, the taste turns stale, the aftertaste sits sour, and you feel shitty afterwards. You love smoking and you hate being a smoker in the same hour.

I believe both halves. I am not here to mock what you feel or to tell you that you never enjoyed anything. That heaven second is real as a feeling. It arrives fast and it fades fast. It does not last the day. It does not even last the cigarette.

Notice what that tells you about choice.

A genuine pleasure leaves you glad you did it. Food you love leaves you satisfied. Company you love leaves you warm. This leaves you washing, spraying, chewing, regretting, and planning the next one. Why would a pleasure need to be cleaned off you?

And notice this too. If anything stressful happens in your life, a cigarette takes care of it — that is what you feel in the moment, a rapid taken-care-of feeling that you credit to the dose. I do not deny the feeling. You feel what you feel. Feeling it does not prove the dose fixed anything. It proves only how fast the credit is given, while the phone, the bill, the argument sit exactly where they were.

We do not need to settle yet what that heaven second is or why it fades. We will look at each credit in its turn, with your own day as evidence. For now hold only the split that choice cannot explain. Choice would not call heaven what leaves shame. Choice would not pay for a second of bright with an hour of stale.

You are not lying when you say the lighting feels good. You are misreading what that good proves. A caged bird that paces and then sits for a second has not chosen the cage because sitting felt better than pacing.

### TWO THINGS HOLD YOU, NOT ONE

So what holds you, if not choice and not pleasure?

Only two things, and both are smaller than your fear makes them. I name them here in passing so we have words for what we see. They will be our vocabulary from here on, not a lesson to learn.

The first is tiny and physical. A little emptiness between doses, hardly pain at all. Not flu. Not agony. It is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug that comes when the last dose fades and nags to be fed. Feed it and it quiets for minutes. Leave it and it nags a while, then passes on its own like a distant horn. I mention it only as the Nipper, a small starving creature you carry without knowing, already dying whenever you stop feeding it.

The second is larger and louder, and it is the real holder. The voices, pictures and excuses that interpret that little tug as proof you need a cigarette to cope, to pause, to belong. Everyone does it. One won't hurt. My work needs it. My nerves need it. That noise was installed early and topped up daily by the world around you. I mention it only as the Smokescreen, the belief-system that feeds the Nipper by telling you the dose gives what it only seems to give.

That is the whole shape of the nicotine trap as you live it. A small tug plus a loud story. The tug moves the hand. The story calls the moving choice.

See how this reframes your past. You did not fail twenty times because you lack muscle. You failed because you fought the tug while believing the story. No one told you the story was the holder. Once the story is seen, the tug is revealed as trivial — a passing edginess, not a command.

You do not need to fight either of them now. You need only to keep watching with open eyes while you go on smoking as normal. Seeing is how you walk out through a door that was never locked, and that walking out is an escape into clear seeing, not a loss of anything real.

Free choice does not need a story to excuse it. The trap does.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN YET
Smoke as normal until your last cigarette.

**SUMMARY**
- Smoking stopped being a free choice when the first false promises closed behind you.
- Hiding, spraying and hurrying prove a need is acting, not a pleasure being chosen.
- Your hand often lights before you decide, which choice would never need to do.
- The bright minute of lighting is real as a moment, but it fades fast and leaves shame and a stale mouth, not day-long good.
- A tiny physical tug and a loud learned story together keep the lighting going, not lack of strength or sense.
