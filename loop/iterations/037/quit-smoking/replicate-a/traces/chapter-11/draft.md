Chapter 11
THERE IS NO JUST ONE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
- The bargain after the lit circle
- A dusty lamp on a shelf
- A cliff edge in fog and a diary creeping back up
- Five months clear and an outstretched hand
- Quick answers for the last bargaining voices

*Cutting down, keeping one for later and waiting till tomorrow all mean the same thing: staying inside.*

### THE BARGAIN AFTER THE BEST ONE

You have met the best one head-on and watched it give nothing.

That late patio with drinks and laughter did the giving. The paper only hired your hands while the giving happened elsewhere. You know that now because you tasted the same hour with and without, and the hour stood taller without.

So why does a new voice start up, quieter, almost reasonable?

“All right. The night-out dose is empty. But surely I can keep just one for Christmas? Surely I can cut to three a day till work calms down? Surely tomorrow is a cleaner day to finish?”

I know that voice. We all know it. We stood over sinks and swore the next pack would be the last controlled one. We counted, we delayed, we saved special ones in tins as if a dose in a tin were not a dose.

Hear it for what it is. It is not you thinking. That bargaining is the nicotine trap talking, not you.

Ask from behaviour, not from hope.

When you ever cut down, did you smoke less and think less about smoking — or smoke less and think about smoking all day? When you saved one for a special night, did the night stay special — or did the whole week lean toward that night? When you set tomorrow, did tomorrow ever arrive cleaner — or did it arrive with the same tug and a fresh excuse?

You know. Every bargain keeps attention on paper. And attention on paper is the trap fed.

### THE LAMP YOU MUST NOT RUB

Let me show you the special one plain, because this is where years are lost.

Picture a brass lamp on a high shelf, dusty, almost forgotten. You tell yourself you live without it now. Then a voice says, “Rub it once, just to prove you are master of it. One puff cannot rebuild anything after months.”

You rub. Smoke pours out. The walls rebuild. You are back inside the bottle arguing about how to get out again.

I have watched this happen after five months clear. A man I knew — nineteen years dosing, then five months with nothing lit, breathing clear, mornings owned — takes one offered dose at a barbecue because drink is flowing and he wants to prove control. He coughs, feels dizzy, tells himself it was horrible. By nightfall he is bumming a second to “cover the taste.” By Friday he is buying a pack. By month’s end he is counting again as if the five months never happened.

Did one dose prove control? Or did one dose rebuild the prison?

Ask from that barbecue, not from theory.

If one were proof of freedom, why did freedom need proving with paper? If one were nothing, why did nothing demand a second by nightfall? If five months made one safe, why did the safe man leave the barbecue counting?

You know because you have lived the smaller version a hundred times. The first dose after a gap never satisfies. It re-creates the itch it pretends to scratch. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

This is why I speak of one with such finality. Not to frighten you. If you feel ill or worry about stopping, speak to your clinician — I deal only in what the dose gives you, which is nothing. I speak of one flatly because one is not one. One is the whole chain re-threaded.

A slip, if it ever happens, is a warning rumble, not a crash. Do not torture yourself. Do not say, “I have blown it, so I may as well dose.” Notice what happened — the old script spoke, you answered for a second — close the door and walk on. But never plan a slip to test yourself. The lamp is not for testing. Leave it dusty.

When that future thought arrives — and it will arrive dressed as your own reason — hear it pre-labelled:

“I’ll just have one to prove I’m free.” That is the script, not you.

“I’ll have one because this day is terrible.” That is the script, not you.

“I’ll have one because this day is wonderful.” That is the script, not you.

Freedom needs no proof with paper. A non-smoker does not prove clear lungs by inhaling.

One is not an exception to the trap. One is the trap, rebuilt.

### THE CLIFF YOU CANNOT FALL HALF OF

Cutting down wears kinder clothes, but it walks the same cliff.

Imagine standing at a cliff edge in fog. Someone says, “Jump, but only fall a little way. Control the fall. Three metres, not thirty.” You would laugh. Falling is falling. The ground does not negotiate.

Cutting down is trying to fall a little way.

We tell ourselves fewer doses means less slavery. Watch what fewer doses do in a real day.

You set ten, then six, then three. Each permitted dose becomes precious. You wait for it. You watch the clock for it. You inhale harder, hold longer, time the day around it. The diary starts neat — Monday: 6, Tuesday: 5 — then creeps: 5, 7, 9, a pack on Friday because “the week was hard.” The waiting makes each hit feel bigger, because the tug has grown bigger between hits. The preciousness proves nothing except that forbidden fruit tastes larger in the mind.

Ask from the diary, not from intention.

Did cutting down ever make you think less about doses? Or did it make every hour a negotiation? Did the small number hold for a month? Or did the number creep while the tug stayed exactly where it was, ordering the same exits?

You know. We never cut down to freedom. We cut down to longer craving with better maths.

And lights, milds, smaller sticks? Same cliff, different coat. You cover tiny vent holes with lips and fingers without knowing and pull harder to get the dose you were always seeking. The machine counts less. The mouth does not. The throat knows. The tug knows.

If fewer gave freedom, the fewest would give most freedom, and none would give all of it. Why pay for the cliff in instalments when you can walk away from the edge?

### TOMORROW IS THE FAVOURITE DAY OF THE TRAP

Then there is the softest bargain, the one that sounds almost sensible.

“I will finish tomorrow. After the wedding. After the deadline. When stress lifts.”

I understand why tomorrow shines. Today feels full. Tomorrow looks clean and empty, a fresh page with no tug on it.

But whose clean page is it?

Today you dose twenty times to quiet twenty tugs. Tomorrow you will dose twenty times to quiet twenty tugs. What changes in the night except the date? The tug does not attend weddings. The trap does not respect deadlines.

Ask from the calendar, not from comfort.

If doses helped with stress, would the most stressed weeks not be the easiest weeks to stop, full of help? If tomorrow were truly cleaner, why has every past tomorrow arrived dirty with the same excuse? If waiting saved anything, what has a year of waiting saved except money spent and breath shortened?

That pull both ways — fear of staying and fear of leaving — is the tug-of-war of fear, and both ropes belong to the trap.

Fear of staying says, “You must stop, look at the cost.” Fear of leaving says, “You cannot stop, how will you cope, who will you be at parties?” Both sound like you. Neither is you. The trap holds one rope with threat and the other with false comfort, and you stand in the middle burning paper to bear the pull the paper creates.

Cut the ropes by seeing them. There is no coping to lose, because the dose never coped. There is no self to lose, because the doser was never the self — it was the self plus a tug. Tomorrow does not clean the tug. Seeing cleans it now.

Every tomorrow you set extends the bill for something you already know gives nothing. Continuing is the painful ongoing payment, not stopping. Stopping is walking off the field where the ropes pull.

### QUICK ANSWERS FOR THE LAST VOICES

Now let me answer rapid-fire, in your own words, so no whisper is left for later. When these sentences rise next month, you will hear them already answered.

“I’ll just cut to five a day. That’s sensible.”
Sensible for what? Five precious doses with all-day waiting, harder pulls, and the same tug ordering exits. You tried fewer. Fewer made paper bigger in the mind. Totality makes it nothing.

“I’ll keep only the wedding one, the Christmas one, the holiday one.”
A special one keeps the belief alive intact. If the best patio dose gave nothing, a December dose with tinsel gives nothing with tinsel. Special means the trap survives on anniversaries.

“I went months once without, so one now can’t hurt.”
That is exactly the story that hurts. Months do not make one safe; one makes months irrelevant by replanting the tug. The man at the barbecue proved control for five months. He proved the opposite with one.

“What if I slip? Does that mean I’m back to zero?”
No. A slip is a rumble strip, not a verdict. If a tyre touches it, you steer back, you do not drive into the ditch to prove you slipped. Guard the seeing, not the streak. Never plan a slip to test the seeing.

“Don’t trials show props help, and most tries fail alone? Don’t I need one?”
A trial counting months against a dummy and a survey counting how most tries happen answer two different questions. Neither answers you, here, once you see there is nothing to prop. I prescribe nothing and forbid nothing medical — I speak only of credit. The belief decides, not the aid.

“Isn’t this just brainwashing? You keep telling me it’s easy.”
Yes — in reverse. You were taught for years that paper relaxes, rewards, belongs with drink and friends. That teaching was never examined. I am asking you to examine it against your own Tuesdays and patios and diaries and find it false. If it is false, easy is not a trick. Easy is what is left when there is nothing to miss.

Hear the shape of every bargain now. It always offers a smaller trap as if smaller were outside. Smaller is inside with better lighting.

11. NEVER ALLOW JUST ONE OR A SPECIAL ONE
One keeps the trap alive.

**SUMMARY**
- Bargains after the best dose keep attention on paper, and attention on paper feeds the trap.
- One dose after months rebuilds the tug instead of proving control.
- Cutting down makes each permitted dose more precious and lets numbers creep back up.
- Tomorrow never arrives cleaner because the tug does not keep calendars.
- A slip is a warning to steer back, never a licence to plan another.
