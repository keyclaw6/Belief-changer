Chapter 7
THE ITCH IT PRETENDS TO SCRATCH

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — The kerb meter with its fading needle. A day of twenty lights watched from waking to midnight. The itch under the ribs named for what it is.

*That lift you feel is not a lift at all — it is the noise going quiet for a few minutes because you fed the noise itself.*

### THE FEAR LEFT AFTER THE DRAWER

You have shut the drawer without blaming the hand that opened it.

We left the foil and the squares and the peppery cheek-burn where they belong — with the orders that told you to want and to hold at the same time. I stand by that verdict. It was never weak backbone. It was wrong orders.

And still, behind the cleared shame, one fear sits up in most men at this point. I hear it because I carried it.

"All right. The method was wrong. But my body needs it. Take the paper away and something in me will scream."

Say it plainly. That is honest. You know the pellet took no credit for work, you know the pause steadied without smoke in it, you know the mild was holes covered by your own lips — and yet the chest says: there is a physical need here that argument cannot touch.

I do not mock that voice. We all heard it. We mistook a small tug for a large master because we had been taught to read it that way.

Watch one ordinary day with me, dose by dose, and see the size of what you fear.

### THE METER AT THE KERB

Picture a parking meter at the kerb outside your house.

You drop a coin in. The needle jumps. You walk away feeling paid up. Within minutes the needle is sliding. In less than an hour it is flashing red. You must feed it again. And again. All day. Every day you have smoked, you have stood on that pavement feeding that meter.

That is what a day of doses looks like when you stop telling its story and start timing it.

First light on empty lungs. The head turns for a moment. The shoulders drop half an inch. By the time the kettle boils the edge is back. Second dose to face the post. Third to face the drive. Mid-morning two more because the morning would not run without topping. Lunch. Afternoon. Tea. Step outside. Step back in. Evening chair. Late call. Last one at the door before bed. Twenty coins. Twenty brief jumps of the needle. Twenty slides back to red.

Ask from your own day, not from me.

If each dose truly lifted you, why did the lift never hold past the washing of the cup. If each dose truly calmed, why did the calm need re-calming before the hour was out. If each dose gave something, why did the man who had twenty gifts in a day wake the next morning empty-handed and reaching before his feet touched the floor.

You know. The gift never stayed because there was never a gift. There was a coin and a fade.

The fact is simple, and I state it flat because your own clock already proved it. When smoke enters the lungs the drug rides the blood to the brain within seconds, and the acute turn quiets almost as fast as it came. That quick up and quick down is why the hand watches the clock all day. Not because the day is hard. Because the coin fades fast and the meter is built to fade.

Do not read timing as a promise that every smoker fades to the minute alike. Bodies differ. Days differ. The shape is the same: jump, slide, red, repeat. This is the nicotine trap in daylight.

And here is the question that breaks the kindest lie about that jump.

Did the coin put money in your pocket, or did it only stop the meter screaming for a few minutes. Did you rise above the man who never smoked, or did you climb for ten minutes back toward him and then sink below him again to pay for the climb.

You were never lifted. You were muted, then billed.

There is a small, starving creature behind that muting. That is the Nipper asking for its feed, nothing more.

### THE ITCH THAT MAKES ITSELF

We were conned into reading the fade backwards.

We said: I am tense, I light, I feel less tense, therefore the tube calms tension. We said: I am dull, I light, I feel less dull, therefore the tube sharpens. We said: I cannot face the pause, I light, the pause passes, therefore the tube helps pauses.

It is the other way around.

Think of a man who wears tight shoes all day for the pleasure of taking them off at night. Would you say the taking-off gives his feet something. Or would you say the shoes caused the ache the evening only pauses.

We wore the shoes and praised the taking-off. We called the pause in pain a gift.

Scratch an itch and watch what happens. For a second the scratch feels necessary, almost sweet. Then the skin burns hotter. You must scratch again. Soon the skin is raw and the hand cannot leave it alone. Would you say the scratching heals. Or would you say the scratching keeps the itch alive and sells itself as cure.

Smoking is scratching that first made the skin itch.

The first tubes taught the body to expect a top. The body answered with a low signal between tops — not grand pain, not collapse, just a thin unease under the ribs. What you feel between doses is an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

That tug is tiny. I speak as a man who feared it for years and then watched it closely. It is not hunger. It is not grief. It is not illness. It is a whisper that says something is missing, and the whisper borrows a loudspeaker from belief.

That story is the Smokescreen talking.

The Smokescreen says: this tug means you need a friend, you are frayed, life is too much without help, one light will set you right. The Nipper itself cannot talk. It only tugs. The voice that turns tug into need is the old brainwashing laid over years — films, breaks, mates, packets that promised calm and party and pause.

Strip the voice off and look at the tug naked.

It wakes. You feed it. It sleeps for forty minutes. It wakes again. That is the whole animal. It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

Read that again in your own chest, not on the page. The first half of your smoking life you did not have that hourly tug at all. A non-smoker reading beside you does not have it now. The tug arrived after the dosing began, grew with the dosing, and calls for dosing to quiet it for minutes. What creates the need it then briefly quiets. What sells the muting as pleasure.

Ask the two questions whose only honest answer ends the debate.

If the tube were a true helper, why does the man who never touched one not miss its help all day long. If the tug were proof of true need, why did you not feel that tug before you were taught to feed it.

You know because you lived both sides. Before the trap you were complete without topping. Inside the trap you are incomplete every hour unless topped. The tube did not fill a hole in you. It dug the hole and rented back the ladder for ten minutes at a time.

That ladder never reached higher than the ground you stood on before. It only pulled you out of the pit it put you in, then dropped you back while you thanked it for the climb.

### SMALL TUG, BIG STORY

"But when I go without, it feels enormous. It feels like panic, like I will fly apart."

I take that fear seriously. Let us measure it without the loudspeaker.

When the dosing stops, the whisper gets louder for a short while because it is no longer being muted every hour. For most of us the restlessness sharpens over the first days, crests, then thins. If you feel ill or worried about stopping, speak to your clinician; I give no medical orders here. My point is about belief, not medicine: the empty tug is a brief echo of feeding, not proof the tube was your nature.

Not every reader feels it to the same hour or the same pitch. Some feel little more than a light emptiness. Some feel edgy for a few evenings. No one I ever freed met the weeks of torture the Smokescreen promised on the packet of fear. The promise of torture is part of the con. It keeps you feeding to avoid meeting what is, when met, small and already dying.

Starve it and it starves. Feed it and it lives to ask again.

Think of a puppy whining at the door the first night in a new house. If you go down and feed it, it learns whining brings food and whines louder the second night. If you leave it warm and unfed in that habit, it whines, sleeps, forgets. The Nipper is that puppy — blind, tiny, noisy for a night or two, dead by neglect, not by battle.

You step out of it easily, immediately and permanently.

Ask from the meter, not from dread.

When the needle slid to red between doses, did the world end, or did you simply feed and watch it slide again. When you slept eight hours without a top, did the tug wake you screaming, or did you wake reaching from habit and call habit need. When flu kept you in bed two days with no smoke, did you die of missing, or did you cough, sleep, drink tea, and find the missing thinner than fear said because the mind was busy elsewhere.

You know. The hours you already went without prove the tug cannot hold you. The fear of the tug holds you. And fear belongs to the story, not to the skin.

So put the credit back where it belongs. The calm after a light was not the tube calming life. It was the tug quieting because it was fed. The problem on the desk sat untouched while the shoulders dropped half an inch. The night, the food, the mate, the pause — they gave what was good. The tube only sneaked a ride on the minutes and charged you the next low as fare.

### WATCH THEM WITHOUT BORROWING THEIR EYES

There remains one loudspeaker outside your own ribs.

Other smokers.

You will stand near them today. At work. At the door. In the yard. A hand will extend a tube with the old grin. The air will smell the old smell. The brain will offer the old translation: they are enjoying, you are missing, one with them would be company.

Look with clear eyes and keep your own judgment for this one scene.

Watch what the smoker actually does, not what the Smokescreen says he does.

He does not light once and shine all evening. He lights, talks, fades, grows fidgety, checks, leaves the laugh to feed the meter, returns, lights again. His evening is cut into coins. His laugh is interrupted by need. His hand is hired out. Does that look like a man holding a pleasure you lack. Or a man pausing his pleasure every hour to quiet a tug you will soon never feel.

Ventriloquize the grin fully so it cannot whisper later.

"Go on. One won't hurt. You don't want to be the odd one out."

Hear what it asks you to buy. It asks you to believe that belonging lives in paper, that drink needs smoke to be night, that refusing a coin means losing the company. You know from every patio you ever stood on that the company was talk and drink and air and late light — and the tube only rode along. Take the tube out of that picture and nothing of the night is lost. Put it back in and the night is taxed every forty minutes.

Ask the questions that leave no honest room for envy.

If their tubes give joy, why do they cough, hide, spray, count, and wish in quiet moments they had never started. If one with them is company, why does the company continue after the tube is stubbed, and why do non-smokers in the same circle laugh as loud without stepping out.

You know. You are not watching enjoyment. You are watching men feed a meter and call the feeding ritual.

Do not borrow their eyes. Keep your own. Their tug is speaking through their grin. Your seeing can hear both and still walk past.

7. REFUSE TO BE INFLUENCED BY OTHER SMOKERS
Watch them with clear eyes and keep your own judgment.

**SUMMARY**
- Each dose jumps the needle for minutes then slides back to red, so the day becomes feeding, fading, feeding again.
- The lift never rose above the non-smoker baseline, it only muted for minutes a low the dosing itself created.
- The tug between doses is small and brief, a whisper made loud by belief, not proof the tube was needed or was you.
- Other smokers interrupt their own evenings to feed the same fade, so their lighting proves the need, not the pleasure.
