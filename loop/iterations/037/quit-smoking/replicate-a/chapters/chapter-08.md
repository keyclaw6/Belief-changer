Chapter 8
MORNINGS, MEALS AND BREAKS WITHOUT IT

IN THIS CHAPTER — cold lino underfoot, a plate holding flavour, a doorway step in winter air with a hot mug in both hands

*The morning, the meal and the pause were whole before the dose touched them — live them plain and you taste what the dose took.*

### THE FEET THAT FOUND THE PACK FIRST

I know that reach because I lived it for years.

Alarm. Half dark. Feet have not touched the floor before the hand finds the pack on the chair. Thumb on the wheel. First pull on empty lungs while the eyes are still half shut. Throat bites. Head turns. You stand at the window in your shirt and call that waking.

We all did that reach. We told ourselves the day could not start without it. We read the bite as a key turning in a lock.

Look closer with me at one plain morning lived without the tube in it.

Feet touch cold lino. That cold is real and it wakes better than smoke ever did. You stand. Legs take weight. Spine lengthens. The floor holds you without argument. You pad to the kitchen in bare feet. Kettle on. Click. Cup out. Milk. Spoon. The small clinks line up in order while water climbs to boil. Steam lifts. You wrap both hands around the hot cup and drink standing at the window.

Light on the roofs. Kettle song behind you. Neighbour door downstairs. Sparrows on the wire. Your throat loosens with heat. Your shoulders come down a notch with the first swallow. Your head clears by the second sip as sleep thins out of it.

What is missing.

You hear the old voice at once, and I want you to hear it in its own words so it cannot whisper later:

“But I need one to get going. Mornings will be flat without it.”

Keep that voice up in daylight and test it against the cup in your hand.

When steam warms your face, does the face need paper on top of warmth to feel warm. When hot tea loosens the throat, does the throat need bite on top of loosening to feel loose. When the head clears by the second sip, was it the night leaving you, as it leaves every sleeper, or was it a dose giving you something a sleeper without doses does not have.

Ask from the lino, not from dread.

If the first dose woke you, why did you need a second before the post came. If the bite cleared you, why was the mouth papery and the chest a touch tighter after the clearing than during it. If the window moment belonged to smoke, why do you remember the light on the roofs and the kettle song and the neighbour door — none of which came out of paper.

You know. The waking was getting up, water, heat, light, movement. The dose only rode along and charged a fee.

Dress plain and watch the same truth from the wardrobe side. Shirt over head. Cold arms in sleeves. Belt. Socks. Shoes. Laces pulled. Each small act pulls you a fraction more awake because blood moves and skin meets air and hands do their work. No act asks for topping. The body strings itself together by doing, not by lighting. When we lit between sock and shoe we did not speed the stringing. We cut it, stepped out to the window, stood in shirt-tails watching ash, and came back to cold tea.

We were taught to read a thin morning hum as proof we cannot start. What hums then is only an empty, slightly restless, slightly edgy little tug.

That tug is not you. It is not hunger. It is not character. It is the echo of yesterday’s topping asking to be topped again. Drink the tea. Move. Dress. Answer the kettle. Rinse the cup. Put milk back. Within minutes the hum thins because attention has somewhere true to go. The morning does not stall without a light. The morning runs as mornings run when nothing cuts it into slices.

I stood where you stand. I feared blank mornings more than I feared hauling on those stairs while a non-smoker climbed light beside me. Then I lived three mornings plain and saw the trick. Feet, floor, water, cup, light. Nothing in that line asked for smoke. Smoke only stood in the line and took credit for the line moving.

Stay with the kettle to the post and see the line hold. You carry the cup to the table. You sort bills, circulars, a letter worth opening. You read standing, then sitting, then standing again to pour a second half cup. The mouth tastes tea and toast. The nose catches toast and rain at the cracked window. The hands turn envelopes without shaking and without looking for a pack. Ten minutes pass. Fifteen. The thin pull visits once under the ribs and passes when you lift the cup and answer the letter. That passing without a coin is the morning proving itself. Not by argument. By minutes that did not need feeding to keep moving.

The morning is not a hole to fill. It is a start that starts itself.

### THE PLATE THAT KEPT ITS TASTE

Now take the meal-end, the second place we swore needed it.

Plate cleared. Crumbs. Warmth in the belly. Talk still going around the table or quiet humming from the cooker. The chair pushed back half an inch. The body settling into that fed heaviness that is pleasant in itself. Hand moves by habit. We used to say the meal was not closed until the light. We said the taste needed a full stop. We said that pellet after the paragraph of food made the food more.

I do not mock that memory. We all laid that pellet down like a seal.

Live one meal-end plain and watch what taste actually does.

You finish. You put the fork down. The mouth is full of what you ate — salt, butter, sharp green, sweet end. You sit a minute. You breathe through the nose. The flavour stays and rounds and slowly settles. You sip water and the sip lifts the last of it clear. The belly warms. Shoulders drop because eating done is pleasant in itself. Talk continues without a gap. Hands lie quiet on the table or around a warm cup.

Light a dose on top of that and see what changes. First pull wipes the mouth to ash. Tongue goes papery. Nose fills with stale. The lingering food goes. What you called closing the meal was painting over the meal.

Whose pleasure was that.

Ventriloquize the defence fully so it cannot return as a whisper:

“I love the taste with coffee. Food just tastes better finished with a stick.”

Then ask the questions whose only honest answer ends it.

When the flavour lingered after you did not light, was the lingering weaker or clearer. When you lit after Sunday roast and the roast vanished under ash in two pulls, did the dose keep the taste or take it. When a non-smoker at your table closes his eyes at the sweet and smiles with nothing in his hand, is his meal unclosed or is yours the one that was interrupted.

You know because your tongue knows. The food did the pleasing. The dose did the wiping.

Stay at that table a little longer and let the tongue finish its evidence. Five minutes after the last bite the salt still sits at the sides of the tongue. Ten minutes and the sweet still comes back when you sip. The coffee, taken black and hot, tastes of coffee — bitter, deep, uncut — not of paper laid over it. The talk finds its second wind because no one has left to stand in a doorway. The plate sits empty and does not call for anything.

You stay. You pour a second half cup and keep both palms around it. The heat comes through the china into the fingers and holds there. No hand leaves the table. No chair scrapes to the step. The coffee sits black and hot and tastes of itself from first sip to last — bitter at the edge, deep underneath, the same cup getting cooler and sweeter as you go. You drink it slow because there is nowhere else to be to finish it.

The talk goes on without a gap. Plans for the afternoon. A joke about the burnt edge of the roast. A second telling of a story from the morning. You are there for all of it, not half out of your seat. Your hands stay quiet — one around the cup, one flat on the table, then both around the cup again when you lift it. No tapping. No shielding from draught. No watching a tip burn down to time your answer.

Eight minutes past the plate. You take another sip and the sweet from the pudding comes back again with it, fainter now, mixed with coffee. Twelve minutes. Someone refills the water jug and you hear the glug and the clink. Someone else pushes crumbs into a pile with a thumb. You laugh at the right place because you heard the whole sentence. Fifteen minutes. The cup is half gone. The belly is settled and warm. The mouth still tastes of what you ate, thinned now to a clean trace, and the coffee laid over that trace without covering it.

Look at what those fifteen minutes held when nothing cut them. Hands warm all the way through. Cup emptied by sips, not by pauses to step out. Talk kept whole, not split by an absence everyone politely ignores. Shoulders down the whole time, not up and down with lighting and stubbing. That is a meal-end lived to its end. No seal put on it. No toll taken out of it. Just plate, cup, talk, hands quiet, taste staying as long as taste stays.

That quiet after eating is not emptiness asking to be filled. It is fullness asking to be left alone.

We used to cut that quiet with a light and call the cut a seal. Watch what the cut cost. The tongue that could have carried flavour for half an hour carried ash in two minutes. The nose that could have carried steam and peel and fresh air carried stale for an hour. The hands that could have stayed warm around the cup went cold holding a tip into draught. The table that could have stayed whole lost its smoker to the step for seven minutes. The seal did not keep the meal. It broke it.

It never fixed the itch. It caused it.

Read that in the mouth, not on the page. The itch for a seal after eating arrived after dosing began. Before the trap you ate and were done and the done was good. Inside the trap the end of eating calls for topping because topping taught the mouth to expect wiping. Take the wiping away and the end returns to what it was: taste staying, belly warm, talk continuing, hands quiet.

Think of that pellet after the paragraph you once paid for each finished chunk. The paragraph pleased because thought landed. So with food. The bite pleased because hunger answered and flavour landed. The landing was yours and the cook’s and the grain’s. The pellet only rented the minute after landing and sent a bill for the next landing.

We gave the work of the cook and the good of the grain to paper. Put the credit back. The cook did it. The grain did it. Your tongue did it. The dose only sneaked a ride on a finished thing and made you pay with the next want.

A finished thing needs no seal but its own finish.

### THE STEP OUTSIDE AND THE AIR

Third room. The break. The door step. The yard air on the cheek.

Mid-morning the eyes blur. Shoulders knot. The screen swims. The neck aches from looking down. The brain offers its old translation at once: step out, light, clear the head, then work again.

We obeyed for years. Out. Flick. Pull. Stare at brick and sky. Back in feeling steadied for minutes. We called that air.

Live one break plain and see where the steadiness lives.

You stand. You push the chair back. That standing and walking already loosens the knot — blood moves, eyes lift from close print to far roof. You walk the passage. You push the door. Cold air hits the cheek. You breathe deep through the nose and the chest widens. You hear traffic, gull, hammer down the street. Hot mug in both palms. You roll the neck. You stretch the hands wide with nothing to hold. You stand five minutes and look at sky.

No counting of pulls. No watching of tip. No shifting to keep drift out of the eyes. No fingers chilling around paper while palms want heat. Just standing, looking far, swallowing warm tea, letting the eyes change distance from near print to far cloud.

Go back in. Sit. The line you stumbled on reads clean now.

What cleared it. The paper or the standing, the far looking, the cold, the moving, the pausing.

Ask from the step, not from the story.

If the dose cleared the head, why did the head cloud again before the hour turned with no new work done. If the yard needed smoke to be a break, why does the dog-walker, the postman, the mate who never lit take the same step and come back bright with nothing lit. If five minutes of air steadies, what did ash add to air that air did not already carry.

You know. The pause did it. The legs did it. The eyes changing distance did it. The cold did it.

Now live what happens next, because this is where the break proves itself whole.

You are back at the desk. Chair pulled in. Mug set down to the right where steam can rise without crossing the papers. Both palms warm around heat for a moment, then one hand to the mug, one hand to the pen. Nothing held between fingers that must be kept alight and kept clear of sleeves. Nothing to tap, to stub, to shield from draught. The hands do what hands do at work: lift, turn, type, underline, carry tea to mouth and set it down again.

Read the next paragraph through. The line that swam before the door now holds still. You underline a phrase. You write a note in the margin. You turn a page. Attention stays on the page without splitting to watch a level. No part of the mind hired out to count minutes since the last light or minutes to the next. No glance to the pack. No pat of the pocket. No thin listening under the work for when the hum will return to order you out again.

Take the phone when it rings. You lift with the writing hand, cradle, talk, jot with the other, hang up, return to the paragraph without losing the thread. Before, the call would have been timed against topping — can I fit a light before I ring back, shall I light while he talks, shall I hurry him so I can step out. Now the call is only the call. The voice on the line, the note on the pad, the decision made and written. Work moves because you stay with it, not because you keep leaving it to feed it.

Work the next stretch to the hour and watch the hands stay willing. Keys under fingers. The clack runs even. Shoulders stay down because you rolled the neck at the door and have not re-knotted them by hunching over ash. Eyes stay wet and plain because wind and far looking washed them and no drift dried them. Mug emptied to half, refilled at the kettle without ceremony, carried back warm. The page count grows. Two pages read and marked. A short reply drafted. Figures checked twice without re-reading the same line three times.

That even run is the point the old story never let you see. We praised the light for the steadiness of the first minutes back inside and never entered what followed: the fidget rebuilding under the steadiness, the eyes drifting to the clock, the paragraph re-read because half the mind was already stepping out again. Take paper out and there is no rebuilding under the floor. The floor holds. The steadiness does not thin by the quarter hour because nothing was laid over it to fade. It carries because standing, cold, movement and far looking carry, and because no fade was booked inside them.

Ventriloquize the yard smoker you just left so his picture cannot borrow your eyes later:

“Come on, you work better with one. You think better out there with us.”

Hear what it asks you to buy. It asks you to believe thought lives in paper and company lives in lighting together. Look at what he does while you live what you do. He leaves the laugh, feeds, returns, fades, leaves again. His hands are never empty long enough to settle to keys. His break is hired out to timing — lit, talked through smoke, cut short by stub, taxed by the next. Yours, lived plain, ran from door to chair to hour without a toll. Which hands did the work. Which head kept the thread.

Ask from that kept thread, not from habit.

When you read two pages straight without losing the line, was something missing from the hands or was something missing from the watching. When the phone call held without hurrying, did the steadiness come from topping or from staying. When the hour closed with pages marked and mug washed and no eye to the clock, did the break fail for want of paper or did the work continue because the break was allowed to be a break.

You know because you just lived it. The hands on mug and keys, the eyes on page and far roof in turn, the attention undivided — that is working with a body uninterrupted. No tug-monitoring. No pocket check. No listening under. The thin pull may visit once like a gull crying far off and pass while you turn a page. You do not answer it because there is nothing to answer. You drink, you move the shoulders, you read on, and it thins because life is louder.

Do not borrow the yard smoker’s eyes for this one scene. Watch what he does. He leaves the laugh, feeds, returns, fades, leaves again. His break is hired out to timing. Yours, lived plain, is yours from door to chair.

A pause that pauses you is a break. A pause that bills you is a lease.

### THE BODY THAT ALREADY KNOWS

There remains the fear under all three rooms.

“But my body will not play without it. Taste will go dull. Breath will stay short. Breaks will drag.”

I take that fear seriously because I carried it. Let us answer it with the body itself, not with talk.

Your tongue already proved itself at the plate. Given one meal without wiping, it held flavour longer. Your nose already proved itself at the kettle, catching steam and toast and rain on the step that ash used to cover. Your chest already proved itself on the walk to the door, widening on cold without bite in it. If you feel ill or worried about stopping, speak to your clinician; I give no medical orders here. My point is about credit, not medicine: the tasting and breathing you just lived were done by you, not lent by paper.

We were taught that dullness without topping means topping gives brightness. It is the other way around.

Dosing dulls between toppings and then mutes the dullness for minutes. Undosed, the dullness thins because nothing re-dulls it. That is why the second plain morning tastes sharper than the first, and the third break walks lighter than the first. Not by effort. By absence of covering. The body does not need teaching to do this. It does it as water clears when mud is no longer stirred in.

Consider the hum that visits between. It comes. Thin pull under the ribs. Eyes to the pack from habit. It passes when you drink, move, answer, chew. For many it sharpens for a short spell when topping stops and then thins into wider clear gaps, though not to the same hour for every chest. That short swell is not proof the dose was you. It is proof topping taught an echo. Unfed, the echo asks, gets no answer, asks weaker, forgets. Refed, it learns again to ask loud.

So put each room back where it belongs.

Morning starts with feet, floor, water, heat, light. Meal ends with flavour staying, belly warm, talk continuing. Break works with standing, far looking, cold, moving. None asked for smoke. Smoke asked to stand in each and take the bow.

Your body knows this without lesson. Trust what your tongue and lungs just told you in plain minutes.

8. TRUST YOUR BODY TO BREATHE AND TASTE
Your body knows freedom without being taught.

**SUMMARY**
- Morning starts with feet, floor, kettle and light, and runs complete when no dose cuts it.
- Meal-end flavour stays longer when ash does not wipe it, and the finish belongs to food and company.
- A break steadies through standing, moving, looking far and cold air, not through lighting.
- Hands on mug and keys keep working with attention undivided, with no watching of the clock once the pause has done its work.
- The thin hum between is an echo of topping, not the self, and it thins when left unfed.
- Work pleased because thought landed, food pleased because taste lingered, pause pleased because breath and blood moved.
