## 4. Scene and Analogy Bank

Define once; chapter card references ID. Full staging debuts once; later cites are token-echo only (one phrase). No two scenes share the same demolition job.

**A-01 — Tight Shoes (inversion image)**
- Description: A person wears tight shoes all day for the pleasure of taking them off — the relief is real but manufactured; the shoes caused the pain.
- Job: Makes the rescuer-as-perpetrator inversion felt in one image.
- Constraint: Original wording; do not import Burgeon/Carr tight-shoe prose verbatim.

**A-02 — The Confidence Trick / Fraudulent Investment**
- Description: A con artist sells phoney information about a worthless investment; the victim is not foolish but conned; the trap removes choice after the first exposure.
- Job: Dissolves free-choice defense without shame; channels blame to trap.
- Constraint: Never implies reader stupidity.

**A-03 — The Bliss-Point Laboratory**
- Description: White-coated food engineers in a lab tuning fat-sugar-salt, crunch, melt, and bliss-point to bypass satiety and engineer craveability — armies of scientists weaponizing abundance.
- Job: Externalizes villain as predatory recruitment; reframes craving as engineered, not personal.
- Constraint: Vivid but non-conspiratorial; no brand-specific accusations.

**A-04 — The Cliff Jump (moderation absurdity)**
- Description: Telling a trapped person to cut down is like saying you may jump off the cliff as long as you don't fall more than a few metres.
- Job: Forecloses moderation/cut-down escape route.
- Constraint: Use once at full force; thereafter token.

**A-05 — The Pink Elephant (thought suppression)**
- Description: Telling someone not to think of a pink elephant guarantees they will — you cannot suppress thought, only reframe how it feels.
- Job: Justifies reframe-don't-suppress relapse-proofing.
- Constraint: Do not turn into a willpower suppression exercise.

**A-06 — Mourning an Enemy vs Mourning a Friend**
- Description: Same factual loss — the person is gone — but mourning an enemy is relief and celebration, mourning a friend is grief; the emotion is chosen by the belief about what was lost.
- Job: Makes post-quit celebration vs deprivation a choice of frame; saves ending reframe.
- Constraint: Reserved for final movement; do not preview earlier.

**A-07 — The Caged Lion / Open Cage**
- Description: A lion freed when the cage door opens but hovers by the bars out of habit — anxiety after release is cage-habit, not proof it needs the cage.
- Job: Normalizes post-vow unease as habit, not need.
- Constraint: Warm, not frightening.

**A-08 — The Birthday Cinema (strongest-case scene)**
- Description: Sunlit kitchen where birthday cake is brought out to applause; cinema row where chocolate is passed hand to hand; 3pm office where the drawer opens — the moments that feel most like love.
- Job: Strongest-case head-on demolition; reassigns all warmth to people/moment/body, exposing sugar as sneaking a ride. Owns the social/belonging justification that celebration requires BAD SUGAR.
- Constraint: Must not shame celebration; must honour the moment while stripping the substance.

**A-09 — The Optical-Illusion Tables**
- Description: Two table tops that look different sizes but are identical — a hands-on proof the reader's own perception accepts falsehood as true.
- Job: Primes reader to distrust felt benefit as illusion; enables perception-enacted exercise.
- Constraint: Interactive, not lecture; one enactment.

**A-10 — The Limbic Switchboard (100 cords)**
- Description: A dumb appliance with 100 power cords — unplug 99 and it still runs; one surviving belief keeps trap powered.
- Job: Justifies totality — why one exception preserves the whole trap.
- Constraint: Use only to land totality, not to complicate mechanism.

**A-11 — The Boiling Frog**
- Description: A frog in water heated so gradually it never jumps — harm so gradual the reader felt fine while being harmed.
- Job: Explains why reader felt normal while costs accumulated; supports hard-facts-then-relief.
- Constraint: Deliver flat, not gory.

**A-12 — The Prison Door (fear of failure)**
- Description: A prisoner whose cell door is already unlocked but who stays seated, convinced they will fail if they try to walk out — the fear of failing keeps them inside.
- Job: Demolishes fear-of-failure as a rope held by the trap.
- Constraint: Validate fear before disarming; warm, not shaming.

**A-13 — The Released Convict (fear of success / identity)**
- Description: A person released after years inside who fears life outside has no shape and tells themselves they are a convict at heart — the fear of succeeding and the identity story that sugar is who they are.
- Job: Demolishes fear-of-success and the "that's just how I am" identity excuse.
- Constraint: Original wording; identity is shown as result of captivity, not cause.
