Chapter 1
THE INVITATION

IN THIS CHAPTER

*You don't have to force yourself to stop wanting sugar — you only have to see what it was never giving you, and wanting stops on its own.*

### THE REASON YOU PICKED THIS UP

You are reading this for one of two reasons.

Either you know you are caught and you want out. Or you suspect you are caught and you want to know if that is true.

We have all been there. We tell ourselves we like sugar. We tell ourselves we choose it. We tell ourselves we could stop whenever we wanted, we just don't want to. And then we find ourselves opening the cupboard we swore we would not open, finishing the packet we swore we would make last until Friday, feeling that faint, tired irritation afterwards and promising ourselves we will be better tomorrow.

If you wanted to stop doing something you truly enjoy, would you need a book to show you how to stop? Would you need to bargain with yourself about rules and limits? Would you need to hide wrappers, or negotiate one more, or feel a little less steady than you wanted to feel?

That question answers itself. You are not freely choosing. You are doing something you wish you did not have to do. I say that with no judgement at all. Warmly. As one who did the same for years. The whole purpose of this book is to take that uneasy feeling you already have — that sense that something has taken hold — and to show you, plainly and kindly, that it is real, that it is not your fault, and that you can be free. Not later. Not after months of effort. Now.

I know how that sounds.

This sounds too good to be true. This sounds like one more promise that will fall flat. This sounds like it cannot be for you, because you have already tried, and because you still half-believe that sugar gives you something wonderful you will miss.

That was my reaction too.

### THE NIGHT IT ENDED FOR ME

I did not come to this as someone who was good at stopping. I was someone who was good at starting again.

For years I lived on the roller coaster. I grazed through the morning telling myself I was being good, I reached hard at three o'clock for sugary somethings to get me through, I told myself that once a week is fine and that just a treat after dinner was earned and reasonable. In the evening I would say I just crave sweets and when I eat them I feel like I can't stop — and then I would prove it true. One bite becomes the box. Always.

I tried every deal you have tried. I don't keep it in the house. I've managed to be good this week so tonight is allowed. I will cut down. I will only have it at weekends. I will only have the good kind. Every deal worked until the moment I needed it to work. Then the hit did what it always does. It promised lift and left me lower.

I believed what you may still believe: that sugar is a genuine pleasure, a little lift, a friend that makes a hard day softer and a happy day sweeter, and that life without it would be flatter, greyer, somehow less kind.

And then, on an ordinary night, it ended. Not after a heroic struggle. Not after weeks of white knuckles. In one evening.

I had not planned to stop that night. I had a packet open on the counter. I was reading something a friend had pressed into my hands about why we really want these hits — not nutrition, not facts about calories, but the simple trick underneath. I remember laughing at a line because it named my own voice back to me so exactly I felt caught. I remember putting the packet down halfway through, not because I was resisting it, but because I had lost interest in it. The wanting had gone.

The next morning I woke up and waited for the punishment I had been taught to expect. It did not come. I ate breakfast because I was hungry. I enjoyed it. The day went on as normal, except lighter. I walked past the shop where I would usually detour. I did not detour. Not through effort. Through absence of desire. I felt what every free person feels on day one and never wants to lose: relief. Clean, light, steady relief that I no longer had to feed that loop.

I am not telling you this to impress you. I am telling you so you know where we are going. I was not stronger than you. I was not more prepared. I was more thoroughly caught, and more thoroughly convinced there was nothing to be done — until I saw the trap for what it was.

When you see it, you cannot unsee it. That is the whole method.

### SHE DID NOT BELIEVE IT EITHER

She will not mind me telling you about her, because she is not one person. She is many people folded into one, so you can see yourself without feeling singled out.

Let us call her Maya.

Maya had done moderation perfectly on paper. She could explain the difference between good sugar and bad sugar, she knew which biscuits were worse than others, she had an app that counted and a rule that allowed a treat when you feel you deserve one after a hard day. She was articulate, disciplined in the rest of her life, and privately exhausted by the negotiation that ran from morning until night: can I have it, should I have it, I have been good, I have been bad.

When a friend suggested this book she said exactly what you may be thinking: "That is not for me. I don't have a real problem, I just like sweet things. And even if I did, I don't want to live without treats."

She opened the book on a Tuesday evening because she had promised she would, not because she believed it. She read with a mug beside her and a bar broken into squares on a saucer. She kept eating as normal, because the book told her to, which she found both suspicious and reassuring.

What changed for Maya was not willpower. She did not use any. What changed was the way she heard her own sentences. That line — just a treat — began to sound different once she had seen who profits from it. The I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling, which she had taken as proof she needed fuel, began to feel like precisely what it is: the tail end of the last hit, not the call for the next.

She finished the book on Thursday. She did not feel deprived. She felt indignant that she had spent so many years paying for the dip with the next dose. She ate her dinner that night, pushed away the bowl where she would usually hunt for something sweet, and said out loud to an empty kitchen a simple vow that made her laugh. The next day she went to work. Three o'clock came. The drawer opened. She saw the old script — treat yourself, you deserve it — and felt, instead of longing, a bright, almost funny freedom that she no longer had to obey it.

She did not become a different person. She became more herself. She still loves food. She still celebrates. Nothing in her life had to be rearranged to make room for freedom. Her body did what bodies do when you stop feeding the trick: it returned to steady.

I tell you Maya's story for one reason. She succeeded against her own expectation. She did not believe until she did. That is the order it happens in.

You do not have to believe me now. You only have to keep reading.

### WHAT THIS BOOK WILL DO

I am going to show you the Sugar Trap.

Not as a metaphor for weak will. As a real, engineered trap with a simple design. It works because we misread what it does. We think it lifts us. The fact is it only ever relieves a low it created, and then guarantees the next low. You never soar above the normal of a person who never fed it. You only borrow back a little of the steadiness you just spent.

Once you see that, three things that look like pleasures reveal themselves as one trick with three faces: the idea that it tastes better, the idea that it powers you, the idea that it rewards you. Each will get its own honest look, on ground we have already laid together, so nothing is taken on faith.

I will also show you who built the trap and why your past attempts did not work. Past failures were not your fault. They were the fault of the wrong method — the Willpower Method — which tells you to endure loss while still believing you are losing something valuable. No wonder it failed. No wonder you felt torn. This time we kill the belief. The wanting goes. The behaviour follows easily, without struggle, without shame.

This is important so you hear it plainly: we are not going to make you miserable in order to make you free. There is no need to be miserable. I want you to begin with excitement, not doom. Think how marvellous it will be to go through a whole day and never once have to negotiate that packet. Think how wonderful to sit at a table and want food, enjoy food, stop when you are satisfied, and get on with enjoying your life.

And I want to remove the risk you feel right now. That scared, guarded feeling that says, "What if this doesn't work for me?" I promise you this: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain. Keep the promise in your pocket. When a line in this book feels outrageous, hold it up. Ask yourself if you have been harmed by reading on. You will see you have not. You will see you have gained clarity with every page.

There is a second promise and I want you to hear it with the same total confidence with which I will repeat it throughout: this can happen easily, immediately and permanently. Not easily if you are strong enough. Not immediately after weeks. Easily, immediately and permanently, because freedom is a change of mind, not a climb. The moment the belief changes, you are free. Not in a month. Now.

### THE LINE WE DRAW TOGETHER

Because we cannot stop eating, I must be precise about what we are stopping. Otherwise the trap hides in vagueness.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

BAD SUGAR is added and refined sugar, and foods where sugar has been engineered in, and ultra-processed junk carbs that behave as sugar in the body — sweets, biscuits, pastries, desserts, sugary drinks, confectionery, and the white and brown refined carb products that spike the same loop and drive the same craving. That is the target.

Whole fruit, vegetables, and unrefined foods eaten as food are GOOD SUGAR. They are not the target. They are not counted. They are not restricted. Your body knows what to do with food. It has always known.

Inside the line we have just drawn, the trap logic runs cleanly. There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero as a voluntary habit. This is not about haggling over how much harm is acceptable. The question is not how much you can get away with. The question is what good is there at all. When we have answered that — and we will — moderation shows itself as the trick's favourite hiding place. If there is no benefit in having it often, there is no point having it occasionally either.

One more word before we make our contract, because it will save you worry later. Your body can cope with an accidental blip — a hidden sugar in a sauce, a set menu you did not choose. Your mind will not cope if you let that blip rebuild the belief. A blip revives nothing physically. It only becomes a relapse if you re-invite the story that BAD SUGAR gave you something. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. Use the seatbelt for the rare bump in the road, not for driving erratically.

### OUR CONTRACT

I am not asking you to stop now. On the contrary. I am asking you to change nothing yet except how you read.

You are an adult who has kept rules and watched them collapse. You are half-convinced this costs you, and half-afraid life will be duller without it. That is the perfect place to start. You do not need to be sure. You only need to be willing to investigate as if this might be true, and to let your own experience answer.

If you will meet me there — open, curious, not yet deciding — I will do the rest. I will show you the trap, I will show you how to walk out, and I will have you rejoice that you did.

We face this together. Falling into the trap was something we did together, all of us, in the same ordinary, sunlit way. Walking out is something you will do, with my hand on your shoulder, on a normal day, after a normal meal, with a clear and steady feeling that surprises you with how light it is.

To make that possible, I need four agreements from you. They are small. They matter.

#### 1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND

You have believed for years that sugar is a genuine pleasure and a lift. That belief is the trap's floor. If you keep it, you stay inside. If you question it, you are already stepping out. I am not asking you to believe the opposite on command. I am asking you to keep an open mind while we examine it together. When doubt appears — and it will — note it warmly and read on. Your own experience will settle it.

#### 2. CONTINUE TO EAT AS NORMAL UNTIL THE FINAL VOW

Do not stop, do not cut down, do not pre-quit while you read. Carry on exactly as normal until you finish this book and make the solemn vow at your last ordinary meal. This is not permission to binge. It is protection. If you try to stop while the belief is still intact, you will feel deprived, and deprivation looks like proof. We want belief to change first. Then stopping is not deprivation. It is relief. We will tell you exactly when. Until then, eat as normal and observe with new eyes.

#### 3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF EXCITEMENT NOT DOOM

Cast aside all feelings of doom and gloom. There is no need to be miserable and there is no virtue in it. Begin with a feeling of excitement. This is the start of an escape. Every chapter removes one more bar. You are not giving something up; you are getting away from something that was doing something to you. Let yourself feel the lift that belongs to that thought. Excitement keeps the mind open; dread slams it shut.

#### 4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS

This method works because it is whole. It is like a combination lock: miss one number and the door stays shut; enter them in order and it swings open. So follow all the instructions, exactly as written. Do not pick among them. Do not add willpower on top. All you have to do is follow the instructions and let the belief do its work. I will never ask you to be strong. I will only ask you to be clear.

Those are the four. They are easy to keep, and they make everything else easy too. If you keep them, I promise you — you have nothing to lose and everything to gain, and you can be free easily, immediately and permanently. In a day or two you will be telling a friend, with a little wonder in your voice, that it was the most marvellous thing to stop wanting what you once thought you loved.

One last thing before we step into the trap together.

You do not need to become someone else to be free. You do not need a new diet, a new personality, a new life. Change nothing else. Eat when you are hungry, enjoy real food, live the same days in the same streets and shops. Freedom does not require a rearranged life. It reveals the steadiness that was there all along, once the noise stops.

Are you ready to see it?

Read on with that open mind, with that excitement, with your meals as normal, following all the instructions. I am with you.

You have guessed the direction. Now let us look squarely at the trap you already live in, so you can walk out of it forever.

SUMMARY
* The goal is to feel relief and freedom whenever sugar crosses your mind, not deprivation or willpower.
* You do not need to believe yet; you only need to keep an open mind and keep reading with excitement.
* Do not change what you eat until the final vow — we change belief first, then behaviour follows easily.
* When this book says sugar, it means BAD SUGAR — added and refined sugar and the ultra-processed carbs that act like it; whole fruit and real food are not the target.
* Freedom is available now, once the belief that sugar gives you something genuine is seen clearly for what it is.
