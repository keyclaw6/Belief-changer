Chapter 1
THE INVITATION

IN THIS CHAPTER — the kitchen at half past eleven at night, the bin and what was fished from it, the sceptic in the third row with folded arms, the shop doorway you walk through without buying, the last ordinary table before the vow

*You don't need willpower to escape; you need to see clearly that there is nothing to lose and the gain starts at once.*

### THE NIGHT I STOPPED FIGHTING

I did not ease my way out. I escaped overnight, easily, and I have not looked back with longing for a single day since.

For years I lived exactly as you live now. I did not call it a problem. I called it a sweet tooth. I called it liking biscuits. I kept a drawer, a tin, a packet in the car for the long drive. I made rules that sounded sensible on Monday and sounded absurd by Wednesday. One after dinner. Only at weekends. Only the good stuff. Only when I have earned it. Every rule collapsed into the same hand-to-mouth shuffle, and every collapse I filed as proof that I lacked discipline.

One evening I sat on the kitchen floor. That is not a metaphor. The house was quiet. On the worktop was an empty family bag that twenty minutes earlier had been full. I had told myself I would have a handful. I had not. I had eaten standing up, fast, hardly tasting, already promising myself I would be good tomorrow. I felt tired and a little sick and ashamed in a vague, familiar way, and I opened the bin to hide the wrapper under the peelings so no one would see it in the morning. I remember the exact sound the wrapper made when I pushed it down. I remember thinking, with complete sincerity, I do not want to do this and I do not know how not to.

I had not read a book about sugar. I was not trying to quit that night. I was simply exhausted by the negotiation. We all know that negotiation. We do the arithmetic of the day. We borrow against tomorrow. We haggle with ourselves as if we were the shopkeeper and the customer at once. We had been doing it for years without ever deciding to start.

What happened next was not discipline. I did not white-knuckle anything. I saw something I had never allowed myself to see plainly: the thing I reached for to make the evening better was the reason the evening needed making better. The lift and the dip were not two separate events. They were one loop with a delay. Once I saw the loop, pity for myself was replaced by something much warmer. I felt absurdly relieved, as if someone had told me the door was open after I had been leaning on it for years.

I ate supper normally that night. I slept. I woke and I had no desire to bargain. Not because I was being good. Because the bargain had been exposed as a trick. From that morning, I have lived free. Sweets sit on tables at birthdays and in shops. I notice them. I feel no tug to haggle. I feel light and steady. I eat when I am hungry. I stop when I am satisfied. I enjoy food far more now because taste is not being shouted over by craving.

I tell you this at once, and I tell it plainly, so you know where I stand and what I claim. I am not a person with extraordinary control. I am not living on permanent guard. I smoked out that little bargain-loop and it lost its power over me in one clear view. That is what this book does. It gives you that view.

A friend of mine sat in the third row when I first talked about this. She had her arms folded before I had said a word. She is clever, kind, and entirely done with advice about eating. She had done the plans. She had done the apps and the points and the powders and the promises. She told me afterwards, I came to be polite and to prove this would not work for me. She told me also that she ate sweets in secret, not because she loved secrecy, but because she did not want to argue about it. Just a treat. Once a week is fine. I don't keep it in the house — except when I do. She knew every line because we have all spoken it.

She read the book as a favour. She did not cut down while she read it, because I had asked her not to. She turned the last page, made one ordinary meal her last ordinary moment with BAD SUGAR, and she walked free. I watched her do it. She did not struggle. She laughed. She kept saying, why did nobody tell me it was like this? She is free still. She does not count days.

I give you her story not to impress you but to reassure you. She was not a believer. She was a reluctant investigator. She succeeded against her own expectation. That matters, because perhaps you opened this book with the same folded arms. Too good to be true? Not for you? You have tried before? I know. I know that tiredness exactly. I also know this, and I will say it plainly because it is true and because you need to hear it from someone who has been on both sides of the trap: the method that failed you last time is not a verdict on you. It was the wrong method.

### WHY THIS SOUNDS TOO GOOD

Of course you are suspicious when a book tells you that you can be free from craving without sacrifice and without a fight. You should be. We have all been taught that freedom from a habit must be earned by suffering, that you must pay in grit what you once paid in habit. That is the brainwashing talking. That is the part the trap installed to keep you inside it.

Let me ask you a simple question and answer it with you.

Do you want to stop feeling nagged by sweets and junk carbs? Of course you do. That is why you are reading. If you truly enjoyed being hooked, if it were a genuine pleasure or treat that added to your life without a downside, why would you ever need a book about how to stop? The fact that you are here, holding this, proves the tug-of-war you live with. You want to stop and you fear you will miss something if you do.

That fear is the only thing we need to correct. The desire does not come from the sweetness. The desire comes from the fear that life without it will be less warm, less easy, less yours.

For a moment, set aside whether you believe me. Try this instead. Read as an investigator. Test what I say against your own day, your own hand reaching, your own drawer, your own 3pm. See if my description of the trap matches your experience more closely than the old explanation that you love sugar and lack discipline. If it does not, you will have lost nothing. If it does, you will have gained everything you came looking for, and more you had forgotten to hope for.

That is the risk-reversal I offer you, and I offer it with complete confidence. You are not being asked to believe in advance. You are not being asked to stop today. You are not being asked to be miserable for a week to see if it works. You are being asked to read, as you are, with curiosity, eating as you normally eat until we arrive together at the last ordinary instance. Is that fair?

Because here is the truth stripped of decoration: you have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Read that again as a lived fact, not a slogan. On one side, nothing leaves your life that was ever doing anything for you. On the other side, you regain the steady appetite, the clean taste, the freedom to walk past the aisle without a negotiation, the pleasure of food as food, the relief of not hiding a wrapper. To say you have nothing to lose and everything to gain is not a pep talk. It is the accounting. The trap has made the accounting look reversed. We will turn it the right way up.

And the nature of that gaining is not slow, not piecemeal, not earned over months of endurance. The method in this book, when followed as written, frees you easily, immediately and permanently.

I choose those three words carefully, and I mean each one. Easily, because there is no contest of will to win once you see the trick; you do not fight a con, you leave it. Immediately, because freedom is conferred at the moment you make the vow at your last ordinary meal, not after a probationary sentence; you do not serve time to earn what is already yours. Permanently, because the only thing that ever pulled you back was a belief, and once that belief is gone there is nothing left to drag you; easily, immediately and permanently is not a boast, it is the correct description of leaving a trap that never gave you anything in the first place.

If that still sounds outrageous, good. Keep your scepticism in the room and let it watch. Keep reading. The only thing that would make this difficult is closing your mind before the evidence has been heard.

### WHAT WE MEAN WHEN WE SAY SUGAR

Before we go another page, let us draw the line cleanly, so you are never confused about what you are escaping and what you are keeping.

When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

> **BAD SUGAR = added and refined sugar + foods where sugar has been engineered in + ultra-processed junk carbs that behave as sugar — sweets, biscuits, pastries, desserts, sugary drinks, confectionery, and the white and brown refined carb products that spike the same craving loop.**

Whole fruit, vegetables, and unrefined foods eaten as food are GOOD SUGAR — not the target, not counted, not restricted. You are not giving up food. You are not giving up sweetness as nature made it. Your body knows what to do with an apple. It does not need a dose engineered in a factory to taste sweet.

That CAPS name will carry the definition for the rest of the book, every time you see it. Naming does the remembering for you.

Inside that line, the logic is total. There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero as a voluntary habit. We are not haggling over how much harm is acceptable. We are asking what good it ever did at all. The answer, when seen clearly, is none. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you. That sentence will become your compass.

Your body is generous about accidents. A hidden sugar in a sauce, an unchosen dessert on a set menu, the sugared bread at someone else's table — your body copes with an occasional blip. Your mind is less forgiving if you let the old belief back in. A blip revives nothing physically. It only becomes a relapse if you decide it gave you something and re-invite the belief that feeds the trap. So guard the belief, not the behaviour. The margin is for accidents, not for erratic driving. You will not need to be perfect. You will need to be clear.

Nothing else in your life needs to change for BAD SUGAR freedom to be complete. You do not need to become a different person, move house, avoid friends, or fill the void with projects. The body's natural baseline returns on its own when you stop feeding the loop. Old contexts are where you prove you are free, not where you fall. You will walk the same streets and shop the same aisles and feel the difference in how you walk them — light, unhooked, unhurried.

That is all the preparation this chapter needs to do on definition. From here on, BAD SUGAR means that boxed target, and freedom means life without that voluntary habit, happily.

### THE CONTRACT THAT MAKES IT EASY

I promised you a method that works without willpower, and a promise is only good if the contract for using it is clear. So here is the contract, in plain language, exactly as you will live it.

You do not stop while you read. You read with an open mind. You read with excitement not dread. And you follow all the instructions when they come. That is the whole method. The instructions are small and explicit. They do not ask you to suffer. They ask you to let a different view settle and then to act on it once, joyfully, at a single ordinary moment.

Some of you will be tempted to skim, to jump ahead, to get the trick without the viewing. Do not. The point is not to collect tips. The point is to see the trap from inside your own day until desire collapses on its own. Skimming keeps the belief intact and makes stopping feel like you are sacrificing something. Reading as an investigator, beginning to end, removes the belief and makes stopping feel like you are leaving something that was doing something to you.

Some of you will be tempted to cut down while you read, to get a head start, to prove you are serious. Do not. We want your reading self to be unchanged, un-deprived, un-irritable, seeing the mechanism in real time. If you cut down, you create extra wanting and you blur the view. Eat as normal. Watch. Notice. Let the book do the arguing. Your only job while you read is to watch your own hand and your own thoughts with kindly honesty.

Some of you will be tempted to read with a heavy heart, braced for the loss of a friend. I ask you directly to set that weight down. Feel a flicker of excitement, even if you must put it on like a coat at first. Excitement is not naive. Dread closes the mind we need open. Excitement opens it. You are not about to lose a pleasure. You are about to discover that what you called pleasure never was, and that discovery carries its own lift. Begin with a feeling of excitement not doom, and the book can do its work.

And some of you will be tempted to cherry-pick — to take what you like and leave what jars. That also keeps the trap powered. The trap is clever because it needs only one cord left in the socket. This method works because it unplugs every cord. Follow all the instructions, even the ones that sound odd until they are explained. I do not ask this lightly, and I do not ask it without reason. Each instruction guards the next. Together they are the combination that opens the lock.

You have guessed, perhaps, that I speak with very settled certainty. I do. I speak as someone who escaped and then helped many others escape, and who has watched the same sequence repeat: scepticism, clear seeing, relief, a single vow, an ordinary life lived lightly. I do not hedge that sequence. I promise it. I promise you this: if you keep the contract, the last page will find you champing at the bit, not white-knuckled but eager, ready to make a simple, solemn vow at your last ordinary meal and to be free from that moment on, happy to be free, not merely free on paper while secretly mourning. You will close the book and get on with enjoying your life.

There is a final kindness I want to name before we seal the contract. We have all failed before. We blamed ourselves. That blame is part of the trap. The method you used before — the careful counting, the rules, the moderation deals, the starting again on Monday — that method was bound to fail because it left the belief intact and asked your will to do what only clear seeing can do. Your will is not weak. You are wilful enough to have persisted in something that goes against all your instincts for years. The failure was not in you. It was in the tool. This time we use the right tool.

We fell into the same con. We were not foolish. We were conned. The con told us BAD SUGAR was a treat, a reward, a fuel, a glue of celebration, a harmless pleasure. It was none of those things. It was a dose that fed the next want. We can be warm to ourselves for falling for that, and harsh to the con that sold it. That warmth is not sentiment. It is strategy. Shame drives secrecy and secrecy drives the loop. Warm clarity ends it.

So here we are, you and I, at the first threshold. You have not yet seen the trap drawn. You have not yet met its two characters. You have not yet watched the 3pm desk or the birthday table without the dose sneaking a ride. All of that comes, in order, in short rooms you can enter one at a time and leave lighter than you entered. For now, only this is asked:

There will be four instructions that govern how you read this book. They are not suggestions. They are the way we keep the mind open and the desire in view so the belief can change. Each will return at its appointed moment, and each will be spoken once at its climax. I state them now as the contract you keep while you read.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND**
If something jars, do not push it away and do not swallow it whole. Hold it beside your own experience and see which fits. Borrow my eyes for a few hours and test the view.

**2. CONTINUE TO EAT AS NORMAL UNTIL THE FINAL VOW**
Do not quit, cut down, or switch to a lighter sweet while you read. Keep eating exactly as you do now. Watch what happens before, during, and after each dose. The book needs your honest, unedited day.

**3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF EXCITEMENT NOT DOOM**
However you feel now, deliberately begin with excitement. Refuse the heavy heart. You are not facing deprivation. You are facing escape.

**4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS**
Not most. All. When an instruction is spoken, do as it says, as it is said. The freedom this book promises is the sum of every small agreement kept.

That is the contract. Keep it and the rest is easy.

I will leave you with the accounting again, because it bears repeating until it feels simple. Read with that open, excited, ordinary-mouth attention, follow each instruction as it comes, and you will not be asked to give up anything real. You will see that what looked like a friend was never a friend. You will make one ordinary meal your last with BAD SUGAR, make a solemn vow without drama, and walk free — you have nothing to lose and everything to gain, and that gain arrives easily, immediately and permanently. That is not a slogan bought from hope. That is the description of what happens when the belief changes and the behaviour follows.

You have everything you need to begin. I am glad you are here.

**SUMMARY**

- Freedom from BAD SUGAR does not require willpower; it requires seeing that the treat you fear losing never gave you anything real.
- The book defines its target clearly — BAD SUGAR — and leaves GOOD SUGAR alone; whole food and real sweetness remain yours.
- You do not quit while you read; you read as an investigator, watching your own day with an open, excited curiosity.
- The method works because you keep a small, clear contract — open mind, normal eating until the vow, excitement not doom, and following every instruction — so the belief can change and the desire can fall away on its own.
