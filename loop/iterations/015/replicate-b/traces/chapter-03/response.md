Chapter 3
WHAT IS IT DOING TO YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER — the kitchen table at noon, the small print on the back of a packet, the teaspoon row by the sink, the teeth in the mirror, the heart and the vessels working out of sight

*The question is not how much harm sugar does. The question is what good it ever did for you at all.*

### THE BARGAIN IN YOUR HEAD

You saw the trap.

That was Chapter 2's job and it is done. You watched the film of your own day — the drawer, the shop, the wrapper folded small — and you felt the click. Not, I like sweets. But, I was conned into a wanting that learned to call itself liking. We fell in by the same confidence trick, we got used to the warm water, we hovered by the open cage out of habit. Call it by its name and it stays visible: the Sugar Trap.

Now a new thought arrives, quick and reasonable. It sounds like common sense. It says: all right, perhaps there is a trap. But it can't be that bad. A little sweet never hurt anyone. The harm is overstated.

I know that thought well. I held it for years. It feels balanced. It feels adult. And it keeps you inside.

It keeps you inside because it puts the wrong question on the table. It asks you to weigh harm against benefit — how bad versus how nice — and then to haggle over the middle. That haggle has no end. There is always a study that says a little less, a slightly smaller dose. There is always a day when you felt fine after a sweet. The trap loves that haggle. While you argue about how much harm is acceptable, you have already conceded that there is some good to weigh against it.

I want to move the scales.

We will not weigh harm against benefit. We will ask a simpler, sterner question: what good is there at all? Not how much does it cost, but what does it pay you? That is the axis switch that ends the bargaining. Once you demand that BAD SUGAR justify itself by a real, irreplaceable benefit, the case collapses. The rest of this book will demolish each claimed benefit one by one. This chapter does one job only: it turns your head from FOR to TO, and shows you, flatly, what BAD SUGAR is already doing TO you while you were counting what you thought it did FOR you.

When we say sugar here, we mean BAD SUGAR — added and refined sugar, foods where sugar has been engineered in, ultra-processed junk carbs that behave as sugar, sweets, biscuits, pastries, desserts, sugary drinks, confectionery, the white and brown refined products that spike the same loop. Whole fruit and vegetables eaten as food are not the target and are not counted. That line was drawn and frozen in Chapter 1 so you can think clearly. Keep it.

### THE FIVE LINES WE TELL OURSELVES

Before we look plainly at the body, let us print the menu that the Big Confection hands us — your own lines, in your own dialect. You have said at least two of these this month:

"It's a genuine pleasure/treat,"

"It gives me fuel/energy to function,"

"It's my reward/comfort — I deserve it,"

"It's sociable — celebration needs it,"

"It's harmless in moderation"

I print them now because each will be met head-on where it belongs and not before. The harmless-in-moderation line is settled in this chapter, on the body. The idea that BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure or treat is settled in the next chapter. The fuel lie has its own afternoon. The private reward has its own kitchen and sofa. The sociable story — the birthday that feels most like love — will be lived without BAD SUGAR and found still warm. I do not work ahead. I name the menu now so you see the whole trap at once, and then we switch the question for good.

Call that first line by its proper name from this page on: a genuine pleasure or treat. That is the illusion phrase. It is one object now. We will not chase five separate pleasures. We will see that the same trick creates them all.

You half-believe that at least one of those lines must be true. Otherwise why would so many of us do it? That feeling is brainwashing, not evidence. Keep an open mind for twenty minutes while we look TO instead of FOR.

### KEEP YOUR BODY ON THE TABLE

Sit with me at the kitchen table. Not a lecture theatre. A table where you actually eat.

Turn a packet over. Read the small print you have trained yourself not to read. On a bottle, a bar, a yoghurt sold as light, a loaf that looks like bread — added sugar under a dozen names. You do not need to be a chemist. You need one plain number to anchor you.

The fact is, the World Health Organization sets a ceiling: less than ten percent of your daily energy from free sugars, and less than five percent for additional benefit. On a 2,000-calorie day that ten percent is about fifty grams — roughly twelve teaspoons — and five percent is half that. That is the population-level ceiling, not a personal prescription. The fact is, in our streets we live far above it without ever feeling we are living above anything. Teaspoons hide in plain sight: a sweetened drink can carry eight or nine alone; a pastry can carry six; a spoonful here and there across the day pushes you over without a single moment feeling like excess. We were not exceeding a limit on purpose. The limit was engineered out of view.

What does living above it do TO you? I will tell you flatly, as settled fact, without hedges, and then we will put fear back where it belongs — out of the decision.

To your teeth: free sugars drive plaque acid. Bacteria feed on the sugars left on the teeth and produce acid that softens enamel. Brushing and fluoride mediate, but the driver is the free sugar itself. That is not a scare. It is a mechanism you can taste after a sweet and feel as roughness later. Dentists see it every day because the driver is common.

To your appetite: engineered sweetness bypasses appetite control. It is designed to be craveable — to go down easily, to not satisfy, to call the next bite. You have lived this. You eat something sweet and shortly after you want more, not less. That is not your will. That is the product doing what it was tuned to do.

To your vessels and heart, liver and pressure: at population level, high added-sugar intake is linked with higher risk to heart and vessels via liver overload, raised blood pressure, inflammation, and the same bypass of satiety that keeps energy coming in. That is not a sentence on you. Bodies differ, lives differ, and risk at this level lives in populations across years, not in a single biscuit on a single afternoon — and if you live with diabetes or take medication where sugar intake is medically directed, speak to your clinician before changing what you eat; this book is not medical advice. I say it plainly once so you do not take a population pattern as a personal prognosis.

Do you feel how quickly the mind wants to bargain here? It says, but not much, but not me, but moderation. That is the old axis: how much harm is acceptable? I do not accept the axis. I ask: what did that dose do FOR you that required any harm at all? If a thing does nothing FOR you, the debate about how much harm you can accept is moot. The only honest ceiling for a voluntary habit that does nothing for you is zero.

That is why I said we settle harmless-in-moderation here. Not by proving a sweet is poison. By proving there is no good on the other pan to warrant even a small cost. Moderation is not a virtue when the thing itself is a con. You would not ask for a moderate amount of a trick.

Put the packet down. Look at your hands. They are the same hands that pushed wrappers to the bottom of the bin. We are warm to those hands. We are harsh to what trained them.

### THE BODY YOU ALREADY HAVE

There is a second flat fact that ends the FOR story from the other side. It is simple and it should make you feel lighter the moment you hear it.

The fact is, glucose is a nutrient your body manufactures itself from protein and glycerol when it needs it. You do not need to import sugar to run your blood sugar. You were not designed around a daily delivery of BAD SUGAR. I am not prescribing any diet, not ketogenic, not anything. I am not telling you to avoid fruit; fruit eaten as food is not BAD SUGAR and is not restricted here. I am telling you that eliminating BAD SUGAR cannot deprive you of an essential import, because the essential import does not exist.

That lands hard if you have been told sugar is fuel. You will see in the next two chapters that the fuel story is backwards and the treat story is stolen. For now keep the clean implication: your body's baseline knows how to be steady. Hunger as an honest signal; satisfaction that arrives and holds; sweetness that tastes sweet and stops. You saw that baseline in a child eating on the floor — hunger, eating, stopping, running to play. You have not lost it. You buried it under a louder, fake signal that called every restless moment hunger and every celebration need sugar.

When you remove BAD SUGAR you are not removing fuel. You are removing the thing that made the fuel signal noisy. The body returns toward clean, not toward lack. That is why stopping is not a sacrifice of nutrition. There is nothing real to sacrifice.

Do you want to stop for that reason? I hope not. I did not show you teeth and vessels and teaspoons so you would act from fear. Fear makes a resolute quitter for a week and a deprived quitter forever — precisely the willpower method that kept the Big Confection alive. Fear still believes the thing was valuable and you are losing it. Freedom believes the thing was never valuable and you are escaping it.

So take the hard facts as they are — flat, settled, population-level — and then put them where they belong: behind you. They did their job if they killed the sentence, It's harmless. They do not need to haunt you. You do not need to be frightened to leave the Sugar Trap, any more than you need to be frightened to step out of tight shoes. You leave because the shoes hurt and you feel better without them, immediately, not because someone listed the calluses they caused.

If fear rises while you read, tell it: noted — I am not quitting because I am scared; I am escaping because I see. That reframe is important. We will keep it until it is automatic.

### TURN THE LIGHTS ON

We fell for the FOR story because the hit feels like something FOR you. A few minutes of lift, a smoothing of mood, a sense that you can cope. That feeling is real. Its explanation is false.

You have already described it without my help: a short-term lift inevitably followed by a fast drop. You thought the lift was fuel added. The fact is the lift was relief from a low the previous dose created. You never rose above the normal of a person who never started; you climbed briefly back toward normal, paid the teaspoon price, and fell again. The rescue was the perpetrator in different clothes.

That inversion will be proved in detail over the next chapters — with your own afternoons and evenings as evidence, without a literature review, without grading a study in front of you. I keep the empirical limits silently and do not overclaim: bodies vary, slumps are not always measured low glucose, patterns live at population scale, and no single afternoon proves a diagnosis. The lived proof is enough: one becomes the box; the treat predicts later low mood rather than relieving it; the crave arrives on cue before the first bite.

You do not need to learn those now. You need only switch the axis and hold it.

There are two columns and only two columns that matter from this page on. Call them TO and FOR. Every time you think of BAD SUGAR, run that crisp test. Not — does it do more harm than good? But —

**It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.**

Say it aloud at the table. It should feel like a verdict, not a slogan. On the TO side: more teaspoons than any person needs, teeth acid, appetite bypassed, liver load, pressure and inflammation risk across populations, a restless half-hour after every lift. On the FOR side: nothing you cannot get without BAD SUGAR — pleasure, energy, reward, belonging all belong to the moment, the people, and the clean body, not to the dose.

Feel how that sentence reassigns the whole bargain. The bargain assumed BAD SUGAR paid you. It does not. The cost was real and the payment was the temporary ending of a discomfort BAD SUGAR itself created. You were paying yourself with your own virtue and calling it a treat. You were borrowing steady and paying back tired, wired and craving — but that triple cost is not for this chapter to land. Here we only need the axis. TO versus FOR. Once that axis is installed, the later demolitions are not arguments. They are recognitions.

Will you still think, but it is a genuine pleasure or treat? Of course a flicker will come — the Big Confection has rehearsed that line since childhood. Meet the flicker with the axis, not with a fight. Think: there it is, the old line — a genuine pleasure or treat — and then, flat: It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you. No suppression. No pink-elephant command. A reframe you can feel.

And feel, even now, the tiny lift that comes with seeing, not from fear but from clarity. You have not yet killed the treat story at its root, nor inverted the fuel lie, nor reassigned the private reward to its true owner. You do not need to. You have only to refuse the old question. The moment you do, quitting stops feeling like sacrifice and starts feeling like escape. Escape is immediate. Escape is gain.

We fell into the Sugar Trap together by accepting a beautiful lie about what sugar did FOR us. We leave it together by telling an even simpler truth about what it does TO us. That truth is on the table now, with the teaspoons, the small print, the quiet teeth, the steady body underneath. You do not need to white-knuckle past the packets. You do not need to count days. You need to guard the axis. Every thought of BAD SUGAR becomes a reminder of the same freedom: you no longer need to feed anything to feel steady, and you never did.

When you think of sugar next — at the till, at the drawer, at the bright shelf — think trap, think TO not FOR, and feel the relief that the bargain is over.

**SUMMARY**

- The right question is not how much harm is acceptable but what good there is at all; with no real benefit, any cost moots the bargain.
- At population level, free sugars above WHO ceilings — less than ten percent of energy, about fifty grams on a 2,000-calorie day, ideally less than five percent — drive tooth acid and are linked with appetite bypass, liver overload, raised pressure and inflammation; single doses do not write personal fate.
- Glucose is not an essential import — your body manufactures it when needed — so removing BAD SUGAR removes a con, not fuel; whole fruit eaten as food remains on the menu.
- The five familiar justifications are on the table and will be settled where each belongs; harmless-in-moderation is settled here by the TO column.
- The new lens is fixed: It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you — and with that lens, stopping is escape, not sacrifice.
