Chapter 15
ORDINARY LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — the morning kitchen in ordinary light, the shop aisle with its bright wrappers, the office drawer at 3pm, the friend's outstretched plate, the evening sofa that used to ask for a dose

*Your ordinary days do not need BAD SUGAR to work — they work better without it, and you will prove it by living them exactly as they are.*

### THE MORNING YOU ALREADY KNOW HOW TO DO

You wake up. That is the whole assignment.

No new routine to learn. No special shopping list. No replacement ritual. You get up, you wash, you eat when you are hungry, you get on with enjoying your life. This is what freedom looks like on a Tuesday. It is not dramatic. It is ordinary. That is the point.

For years we were taught that mornings needed a hit to start. “I need something sweet to get going. I need that lift.” Do you hear that line now? Listen to what it really says:

“I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop.”

That is not my phrase. That is a person describing their own yo-yo, in their own words, and you have lived it. The quick sweet never gave you energy. It gave you a short lift — minutes of quiet from a low the last dose created — and then the fast drop that called for the next dose. The fuel story was backwards.

So this morning, you eat food. Real food. Not a science project. Hunger arrives honestly — a clean, light signal, not an emergency. You answer it with something that satisfies. You sit, you chew, you notice. Taste is already steadier than yesterday. The signal of enough, which BAD SUGAR was engineered to bypass, arrives on time and you honour it. No counting, no virtue, no announcement. Just eating as sensing, not as negotiating.

And what do you feel an hour later? Steady. Not buzzing, not crashing. The marvellous, wonderful steadiness of a body that is not being spiked and dropped. We spent so long believing that flat feeling was fatigue that needed fixing. It was freedom. You never rose above the normal of a person who never fed the trap — you only ever scrambled back toward it for a moment, then fell again.

You may notice a thought flicker this morning. A faint, empty, slightly edgy, slightly restless feeling that says “something missing.” Do not turn it into a problem. That is not need. That is just the Nibbler dying. I want you to smile when you feel it — not to fight it, but to recognize it. The Big Confection spent a lifetime teaching you to read that faint tug as proof BAD SUGAR gave you something. Now you read it correctly: a small, starving creature that loses a little more strength every hour you do not feed it. It was real but it was always tiny, and it passes.

You do not change anything else about your morning. You inhabit it. The same kitchen, the same light, the same walk to work. The courage here is not to avoid your life. It is to live it confidently and prove it is complete without a dose.

And the fact is, it is. You are not missing a treat this morning. You escaped a trap that made every morning tired, wired and craving. You are out of the Sugar Trap, and your body knows it before your old reflexes do.

### THE SHOP AISLE WITH THE BRIGHT WRAPPERS

You will go to the shop. Of course you will. You live in this world. I do not want you to tiptoe around shops as if they were enemy territory. That is the Willpower Method talking — “avoid triggers, stay strong, use discipline.” We do not do that. You walk in with open eyes and a light heart.

Watch what you see when you are free.

The aisle is beautiful on purpose. Colours tuned to call. Crunch engineered, melt engineered, bliss-point tuned so one bite becomes the box. You see it now the way a free person sees a confidence trick after the con has been explained: not with longing, but with clear sight. Armies of scientists weaponized abundance so that your hand would move before your mind could object. That was never your lack of character. That was craveability by design.

You may hear the old script try once, politely: “Go on, just look. Just one, you’re free now, you can have a little if you want.”

You know the answer because you already own it. Try not thinking of a pink elephant — and you think of it instantly. You cannot suppress a thought by ordering it away. So you do not suppress it. You reframe how it feels when it arrives.

The wrapper shimmers. The thought arrives: BAD SUGAR. You do not argue. You do not debate. You think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and you feel the relief that you no longer have to take that hit. The pink elephant is not wrestled out of the room. It is painted joyful and it walks out on its own.

Walk the aisle and choose food. Good food. Not “healthy” as punishment, but food you like, food that satisfies, food that leaves you light and steady. Whole fruit if you want it — that is GOOD SUGAR, not the target, not counted, not restricted. Your body knows the difference. The trap needs you to be confused about that so you will keep negotiating. You are not confused any more.

Someone with a basket next to you is choosing doses. Two bars, a bottle the colour of medicine. Watch them without commentary for a second — not with superiority, but with clarity. For years you would have envied that basket. You thought those hands were indulging. Now you see: those hands are feeding a low. Does that really look like pleasure? One bite and the box is open — “I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop.” That is not enjoyment. That is the loop running. They are not being spoiled. They are being kept tired, wired and craving.

You may feel a strange flutter in that aisle — not desire, but habit. Like a lion that has been freed but hovers by the open cage for a moment because it stood there so long. That flutter is not proof you need the cage. It is proof you lived in it. You smile, you keep walking, you discover you are already past it. Nothing was taken from you in that aisle. Something was given back.

### THE 3PM DRAWER

Then it comes — the moment that used to own you. The 3pm desk. The drawer. The air in the office goes flat, your eyes sting a little, the screen blurs, and the old faithful thought slides in like a friend: “I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling. Just a little something to get through.”

Let us watch that feeling together, without dramatics.

It is not your body asking for fuel. It is the same surge that felt like fuel earlier, now dropping. When you ate BAD SUGAR before, insulin worked, the lift happened, and the same surge guaranteed the dip. Not a rare disease. Not every slump a clinical low. Just the trap’s own curve: up quick, down quick, want again. The lift was the cause of the dip. It was always the other way around.

And the feeling itself — what is it, actually, when you sit with it for ten seconds and stop calling it hunger?

A faint, empty, slightly edgy, slightly restless feeling. That is all. Not agony. Not danger. Just a whisper that says “feed me.” The Nibbler complaining because the Big Confection has finally stopped answering its calls. Yesterday you would have translated that whisper as “I need sugar to concentrate.” Today you translate it correctly: the little creature is dying, and your concentration is returning as the noise fades.

So what do you do at 3pm as a free person?

You do what a free person would do if that drawer had never been a shrine. You drink water if you are thirsty. You stretch. You eat if you are truly hungry — food that satisfies, not a fix that spikes. Or you simply notice: “There it is, that faint tug. Good. That is freedom happening.” And you go back to your work, and you find — as thousands have found before you — that your focus comes back cleaner without the hit, not duller.

You do not tell yourself “I can’t have it.” There is no “instead.” There is only the steady you, choosing what is actually FOR you. And because you are not fighting anything, there is no fight to lose. You did not resist a craving. You saw it for what it was and it lost its power. That is what easy means.

By four o’clock you will have had a quiet revelation no lecture could give you: you got through the hour the old method told you would be impossible, and you got through it lighter, not harder. That memory is now yours. It will make tomorrow even easier.

### THE OFFERED PLATE

And then — the warmest test — someone offers you sweets.

A friend at work with a box. A child’s birthday with cake lit and everyone singing. A weekend cinema where the chocolate is passed hand to hand. A family kitchen where love has always been shown with “just a treat, you deserve it.”

You feel that well, I know. That tug that says “celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate.” For years that line kept the loop alive because it made BAD SUGAR sound like love. If love lives in the sweet, how can you refuse without refusing love?

Watch the scene again as a free person, with everything reassigned.

The warmth is real. The laughter is real. The applause when the cake comes out is real. The pleasure of being together is real. Who made that warmth? The people made it. The moment made it. Your body, tasting and alive, made it. BAD SUGAR was only ever sneaking a ride on that warmth and claiming the credit.

Run it without the dose and see what remains. You did earlier — the birthday replayed without BAD SUGAR, joy undiminished — and you felt it still warm, still belonging. A plate offered to you does not contain belonging. Belonging is in the eyes that offer it. When you understand that, you do not need to defend the sweet to protect the love. You can keep all the love and leave the confection.

So what do you say when the plate comes?

You say what a free person says, lightly, without a speech: “No thank you. I don’t feed that any more — I’m happy to be free.” Or simply “Not for me, thank you” with a smile. No apology, no lecture, no confession of a struggle you do not have. You are not being deprived. They are. And you do not preach. Visible ease recruits more than any sermon. Let them see you light and steady while they are reaching for the next hit.

And inside, as the plate passes, you do one thing only:

9. NEVER ENVY SOMEONE EATING BAD SUGAR — PITY THEM

Not with contempt. With warm, clear pity. Watch the hand that takes the dose. Watch how quickly pleasure becomes reaching. Watch how one bite becomes the box, how the treat that promised comfort leaves the person tired, wired and craving a little more. Does that really look like freedom? You know what it does TO them. It does nothing FOR them. You felt that for years. Now you feel relief that you no longer do.

When that thought comes — “they get to enjoy it and I don’t” — you answer it with the truth you already own. You are not missing out. They are enduring. Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind in that moment, think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and mean it. That is the replacement thought doing its work: converting an old envy into a hit of freedom every single time.

If the offer came with a little pressure — “Go on, just one can’t hurt, you’ve been good, celebrate with us” — hear it as the Big Confection speaking through a kind mouth. Kind mouth, trap script. You do not debate whether one would hurt. One keeps the belief alive, and the belief is the trap. There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR as a habit other than zero, not because any single crumb is poison, but because there was never anything good there at all to ration. You do not need to be convinced again. You are convinced.

### THE EVENING SOFA THAT USED TO ASK FOR A DOSE

Night. The kitchen quiet. The day’s virtue done — and here came the most private script: “I deserve it. Just a little treat. I’ve managed to be good.” The sofa, the screen, the late sweet that bought five minutes of softness and left you heavier.

Let us re-see that sofa too, not as a place of sacrifice but as a place of return.

You sit. You breathe. The house settles. You may want comfort — of course you do. You have earned ease today. But ask plainly: did that dose ever give comfort, or did it give relief from the low it had been maintaining all day?

You know the answer now. The softness it gave was the brief ending of a discomfort it had created. Remove the discomfort and the need for softness-from-a-hit vanishes. What you want at night is not a hit. You want rest, warmth, a little kindness to yourself, food that leaves you steady so sleep comes clean.

So you have it. Not “instead.” Not “I can’t have the other thing so I’ll have this.” Just: “Great, I’ll have what I actually want.” A bowl of real food if you are hungry, a drink you enjoy, a walk, an early night — whatever leaves you light. No rules to follow, no points to count. Change nothing else in your life except this one belief. That is the deal and it is a marvellous one.

And if a faint thought knocks — the bad-day rescue offer, the good-day just-one whisper — you already know them. They are predictable. They are pre-refuted. You do not reopen the decision. You rejoice that the enemy is dead and you do not need to keep checking the grave.

You are free on an ordinary evening. That is not a lesser freedom. That is the whole thing. Freedom that only works on holidays is not freedom. Freedom that works on a plain Tuesday, in a shop aisle, at a lit birthday, on a sofa at nine o’clock — that is freedom for the rest of your life, and you can feel tonight that it is true.

Guard the belief, not the behavior. If an accidental blip happens tomorrow — sugar hidden in a sauce you did not expect — your body can cope, but your mind must not re-invite the Big Confection. Treat it as a rumble strip, not a crash. Correct course, smile that you saw the trick quickly, and keep going. The margin is for accidents, not for erratic driving. You never use “I got away with it” to license a deliberate repeat.

You have already rehearsed tomorrow by living today. Mornings, shops, desks, plates, sofas — each re-seen and re-scripted with thoughts you already own. Nothing new to learn. Nothing to carry except the joy that you are out.

Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind tonight — and it will, gently — think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and let the thought turn to relief. That is the Sugar Trap losing its last voice in your head, and the sound of it leaving is freedom.

- Warm social moments keep every drop of their warmth when BAD SUGAR is absent because the people and the moment create the joy, not the dose.
- Seeing someone else eat BAD SUGAR triggers pity, not envy, because you see it doing plenty TO them and nothing FOR them.
- You guard the belief by never debating just one, by not suppressing thoughts but answering them with joy, and by treating any accidental blip as feedback, not failure.
