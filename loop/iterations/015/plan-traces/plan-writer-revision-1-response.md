# Master Plan — Quit Sugar
`production-books/quit-sugar/master-plan.md`

## 1. Book Core

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of refined/added sugar and junk carbs — the craving–snacking loop of sweets, biscuits, desserts, sugary drinks, and ultra-processed carbs. Not nutrition pedantry, not whole-food carbohydrate.

**One reader (planner-facing state):** An adult who grazes, binges, or negotiates daily — who has kept rules, diets, and moderation deals, watched each collapse into the same box or bag, and quietly suspects the problem is not their character but the approach itself — still half-convinced sugar gives something valuable they will miss.

**Load-bearing false belief:** Sugar is a genuine pleasure, treat, and energy lift that makes life sweeter and I would be deprived without it.

**Through-line:** The thing you reach for as relief is the cause. Every lift is relief from a low the last dose created; you never rise above the normal of a person who never started. Expose that inversion, dissolve each borrowed pleasure, and stopping becomes escaping a trap, not sacrificing a friend.

**Format:** Full-length, 16 chapters, ~61,500 words. Front matter authority dossier + 5 reading instructions; single-purpose short chapters with titled sections; body argues one correction and lands on an ALL-CAPS verdict; IN THIS CHAPTER / italic thesis / SUMMARY per chapter; last ordinary meal as vow; one ordinary-life chapter; photographable short recap. No mid-book recap, no teaching-manual tail.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene (met head-on):** The warm, sunlit celebration — birthday cake brought out while everyone laughs, the cinema hand reaching for chocolate, the 3pm desk where a sweet feels earned — the exact moment that feels most like love and reward. This book replays it without the sugar and reassigns every drop of warmth to the people, the moment, and the body.

**Destination state:** The reader thinks of sugar and feels instant relief and freedom that they no longer feed the trap — not willpower, not mourning. Free from day one, happy to be free, guarding only the belief.

**Fresh ending reframe (saved for close):** You did not give something up. You escaped something that was doing something to you. Mourn an enemy's death and you rejoice — and the sweets left on the table are not being enjoyed by those still reaching for them; they are being endured.

### Fork Decisions (all five, locked)

**Fork 1 — Inner monster:** Full Carr personification (default). Two original mechanism characters are named and made load-bearing: the trivial physical creature to starve and the belief-system that feeds it. No Burgeon no-monster override.

- Little creature: **the Nibbler**
- Big creature: **the Big Confection**

The Nibbler is small, starving, already dying, its death throes to be revelled in — never a mighty enemy requiring willpower. The Big Confection is the lifelong brainwashing that misreads the Nibbler's faint complaint as proof sugar gives you something.

**Fork 2 — Abstinence vs autonomy:** Total cessation of BAD SUGAR, commanded with Carr's cheerful certainty (default). Numbered instructions are expected to be followed. Moderation is foreclosed as the trap's logic. No radical-autonomy override.

**Fork 3 — Science weight:** Deliver hard facts at full Carr force — vivid, flat, unhedged, frightening where true — then disown fear as the reason to change (default). Hard facts arrive as "The fact is..." not as literature review. Raw citations quarantined to appendix if needed. No hedge, no omission of a scare Carr would deliver.

**Fork 4 — Villain:** Two villains, both hit hard (default): the substance/industry that engineered the trap and the named anti-method that kept the reader in it — **the Willpower Method**. Past failures are reattributed to method, never to the reader. No expanded anti-recovery crusade.

**Fork 5 — Void:** Change nothing else in your life (default). The body's natural baseline returns on its own; old contexts are inhabited confidently to prove freedom. No elaborate replacement system. Burgeon transmutation is not needed for this consumptive behavior.

### Redefinition and Margin-for-Error Doctrine

Because eating cannot be quit wholesale, the book redraws the target in Chapter 1, boxes it, and freezes it.

**Definitional decree:** When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.

**BAD SUGAR = added/refined sugar + foods where sugar has been engineered in + ultra-processed junk carbs that behave as sugar (sweets, biscuits, pastries, desserts, sugary drinks, confectionery, and the white/brown refined carb products that spike the same loop).** Whole fruit, vegetables, and unrefined foods eaten as food are GOOD SUGAR — not the target, not counted, not restricted. The CAPS name carries the definition thereafter (naming IS repetition).

**Total-abstinence trap logic runs inside the line:** There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero as a voluntary habit. The question is not how much harm is acceptable. The question is what good is there at all.

**Margin for error (seatbelt doctrine):** Your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip — a hidden sugar in a sauce, an unchosen dessert on a set menu — but your mind will not if you let the belief back in. A blip revives nothing physically; it only becomes a relapse if you re-invite the Big Confection and decide BAD SUGAR gave you something. The margin is for accidents, not for erratic driving. Guard the belief, not the behavior.

**Conditional-bonus extensions:** Alcohol and dairy are not required to quit to be free of BAD SUGAR. The book leaves those doors open, notes the same trap logic applies, and points to where help lives if the reader wants the further upgrade — never as a condition of this freedom.

### Clinical / Eating-Disorder Safety Perimeter

This book is for the everyday sugar/junk-carb trap in a general adult. It is not medical nutrition therapy, not diabetes management, not an eating-disorder treatment.

A plan-wide boxed advisory **CA-SAFE** is defined once and routed on non-argument safety cards and in ledger safety limits — never fused into instruction wording, never pasted mid-chapter as a boxed title. Chapter prose honours it silently or in one plain spoken clause where relevant. Any reader with diabetes, a diagnosed eating disorder, history of restrictive eating, or medication where sugar intake is medically directed must speak to their clinician before changing intake and must not use this book as medical advice. Crisis pointers are provided where the topic touches shame/binge distress; no diagnosis, no prescription, no therapeutic mechanics are offered as method.

**E-01 — Intermittent-access binge model (behavioral trap form)**
- Finding: Intermittent 12-h sugar access produces bingeing, withdrawal signs, craving, and cross-sensitization; ad-lib access does not.
- Units: SEU-S01, SEU-S05, SEU-S09
- Source IDs: S-1, S-5, S-9
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope/context: Rat intermittent-binge model; control-group comparison ad-lib vs intermittent.
- Permitted inference: The schedule (restrict–binge yo-yo) is the trap's form; intermittent access drives escalation.
- Prohibited inference: That any human use instantiates a diagnosed addiction or that schedule alone proves human sugar addiction.
- Empirical limit: Animal model; translates cautiously to humans; magnitude smaller than drugs of abuse.
- Safety limit: Do not label reader as addicted; describe behaviors, not diagnosis.

**E-02 — Dopamine release on each binge (wanting, not liking)**

- Grade: SUPPORTED

- Prohibited inference: That dopamine equals pleasure or that the effect size equals drugs of abuse.
- Empirical limit: Animal data; magnitude smaller than drugs; wanting/liking distinction applies.
- Safety limit: No superpower/pleasure overclaim.
