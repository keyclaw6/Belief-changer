Chapter 1
THE EASY CONTRACT

You can stop smoking, and it can be easy and permanent, with no willpower and no suffering, and I know you find that hard to believe — read on.

IN THIS CHAPTER: the night corridor, your own long list of attempts, and the contract for reading on.

*You did not fail because you are weak; you were given the wrong method, and this time you will not need force at all.*

### THE NIGHT THE CEILING LISTENED

I want to start with a corridor, not a lecture.

It is half past two in the morning. The strip light hums. A man I know well — a smoker of twenty years, twenty a day, cough first thing, last dose last thing — sits on a plastic chair outside a ward where his father has just been taken in. He is not allowed to smoke inside, so he has the pack in his pocket and his hands shake around it. He goes down the stairs, out into the cold, lights up with the others in dressing gowns, and hates himself while he does it. He finishes, comes back up, washes his hands so his mother will not smell it, and sits down again to wait. He looks at the ceiling tiles and makes the same promise every smoker has made at three in the morning: if I get out of this scare, I will stop.

You know that corridor. You may have sat in one like it. If not that corridor, then the dentist chair, or the breathless climb, or the morning you coughed so hard your ribs ached and you lit another within the hour to steady yourself. We all know the moment when the body speaks plainly and we hear it.

The fact is, tobacco kills more than 8 million people each year, including more than 1.3 million non-smokers from second-hand smoke.

Read that again slowly, because I will not soften it. More than 8 million dead every year. More than a million people who never lit the dose themselves. That is not a slogan. That is what it costs to keep feeding the same small fire, year after year, for the rest of your life if you stay inside it.

Does that frighten you? Of course it frightens you. It frightened me.

And here is what I need you to understand before you turn another page: I do not want you to stop because you are frightened.

Fear never freed a smoker. Fear makes you light up. You know this from your own life. The worse the news, the heavier the smoking. The doctor warns you, your chest tightens, and where do your hands go? To the pack. If fear worked, every smoker who ever sat in that night corridor would have walked out free the next morning. They did not. They walked out and lit one on the steps of the hospital.

So let the fear do its honest job and then let it go. The danger is real. You are not stupid for seeing it. But you will not quit out of dread, and I am not asking you to. You will quit because you see there is nothing to keep, and when you see that, the fear has no work left to do. There is relief waiting on the other side of this page, and it belongs to you.

Ask yourself one plain question. If cigarettes truly helped you, if they truly steadied you and completed you, why did that man in the corridor feel worse with every puff? Why do you feel worse after the very dose you said would help? The answer is simple, and we will look at it together, without panic and without force.

You have no reason to feel doomed. You have every reason to feel curious.

### TWENTY TIMES IS NOT WEAKNESS

Let me tell you about failure, because you carry a heavy list of it.

You have tried before. Most long-term smokers I meet have a list that embarrasses them. Patches on the arm that itched and slipped. Gum chewed until the jaw ached. Cutting down and counting and bargaining. White-knuckle Mondays that collapsed by Friday. Promises made on birthdays and broken on ordinary Tuesdays.

One smoker put it exactly like this:

"I had probably attempted at least twenty times before and used most of the replacements and meds on the market with no success."

Does that sound like you? Have you stood in the chemist and bought the next box with less hope than the last? Have you told your wife or your husband or your best friend, this time is different, and watched their eyes try to believe you? Have you woken on day three without cigarettes feeling empty and edgy and told yourself, this is just who I am, I cannot do this?

Listen to me carefully. That record does not prove you are weak.

It proves you were using the wrong method.

There is a method almost every smoker is taught, and I will give it its true name so you can see it clearly from now on. It is the Willpower Method. It tells you smoking gives you something real — calm, pleasure, help — and then tells you to fight yourself for the rest of your life to keep away from the thing you still want. It tells you to be miserable and brave at the same time. It tells you substitutes will set you free while they keep the same want alive in a different shape.

No wonder you failed. Anyone would fail on those terms.

Think about it. If I told you there is a locked door and the only way through is to push it with your forehead for the rest of your life, would your failure to get through prove you have a weak forehead? Or would it prove I gave you the wrong instructions? You did not fail twenty times because you lack character. You failed because you were told to fight a want that was never removed.

I know strong people who run businesses, raise children alone, work nights, bury parents and keep going — and who could not stop smoking by force. Were they weak? Of course not. They were persistent. They kept trying. That persistence is your best asset in this book. We will simply point it in the right direction, where no fight is needed.

So put the list down. You will not need to be tougher this time. You will not need to suffer to prove you mean it. The thing that made every past attempt miserable was not you. It was the method that left the belief intact and then blamed you for still wanting the dose.

Would you agree to this much: that if someone showed you cigarettes do nothing for you at all — no calm, no help, no completion — you would find it easy to walk away? Keep that question with you. You do not have to answer it yet.

### WHY I CAN PROMISE YOU EASE

You do not know me yet, so let me tell you who is speaking to you.

I smoked heavily for years. I lit without deciding, the way you do. First thing before my feet found the floor properly. After meals. With coffee. When work jammed. When I was bored. When I was happy and wanted to mark it. I believed, as you believe now, that the dose steadied my nerves and finished my pleasures. I believed life without it would be grey and restless.

I tried to stop the way you tried. I counted. I delayed. I swapped one product for another. I lasted hours, days, once months — and every time the same small voice brought me back: just one, one won't hurt, you have earned it, you cannot cope without it.

Then I saw something I had never seen, and it changed everything. I saw that the relief I credited to the cigarette arrived only because the previous cigarette had created the want. I was not rising above normal. I was climbing out of a hole I had just dug, and calling the climb a lift. Once I saw that clearly, there was nothing left to miss. I smoked my last cigarette, and I have never wanted another. It was easy. It was immediate. It has lasted.

I know how that sounds. Too good to be true? That was my reaction too when I first heard it. How can something you have failed at twenty times be easy? How can a habit of years end without pain? You believe quitting means suffering. You believe freedom, if it comes at all, comes after months of misery.

I understand that belief, because I held it. And I am asking you to do something very small with it for now. Do not throw it away. Do not accept what I say yet. Simply allow that you might be wrong about how hard this has to be.

Since then I have watched the same change happen in smokers heavier than you, longer in the trap than you, more certain than you that they could never stop. Men who smoked forty a day in all weathers. Women who hid it from their children and smoked in the garage with the door shut. Social smokers who swore they only smoked on nights out and then smoked the whole pack. They walked out the same way I did — not braver, not stronger, simply clearer. Easily, immediately and permanently.

Read those three words again, because they are my contract with you. Easily, immediately and permanently.

Easily means no fight with yourself. Immediately means you do not wait months to feel free; you feel free the moment you see the truth. Permanently means you do not spend life looking over your shoulder. That is what this book does. It does not teach you to cope without something precious. It shows you there was never anything precious there, and then stopping takes care of itself.

You think: that cannot be true for me. My case is different. I enjoy it too much. I need it too much. I have failed too often.

Ask yourself: how do you know? Have you ever examined the belief, or have you only obeyed it? If a friend told you he pays a man every hour to make him feel normal for ten minutes, would you call that pleasure? Would you call that help? Or would you tell him it is the other way around — the man creates the unease and then sells back a brief pause from it?

You do not need to answer now. I only ask you to keep reading with the door ajar.

And consider the bargain I am offering you. You do not have to stop today. You do not have to cut down. You do not have to feel bad while you read. You only have to read with an open mind and let the argument do its work. If I am wrong, what have you lost? An hour or two of reading. If I am right, what have you gained? Everything. Your breath, your money, your mornings, your self-respect, your freedom. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

Let that sentence sit as a plain fact about reading on. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. No one asks you to suffer, to decide, or to believe yet. You only keep turning pages while you still smoke as you did before, and you watch what happens to your belief.

Is that fair? Can you allow that much?

### THE SENTENCES YOU KNOW BY HEART

I want to show you that I know your mind, because smokers tell me the same truths in the same words, year after year.

See if you hear yourself here:

"If anything stressful happens in my life, a cigarette takes care of it. Even though I know that's false, I still feel the relief neurologically and psychologically."

"Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything."

"the moment I light up I'm in heaven, but feel shitty afterwards. So whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker"

"Like taking a cup of coffee. The two just combine so well. As do cigarettes and beer."

"I remember thinking I could never do it because it was part of who I was."

"Nothing to do? Fill the time with a cigarette, right?"

"It makes my day to indulge myself even though I know it's terrible for my health. Life is short so why not enjoy yourself."

"I'm an on and off smoker, I can go months without smoking yet when I'm on a night out and I get a few drinks in me I could smoke a whole 20 pack which is just crazy!"

"I know I can never have just one. I learned that the hard way, thinking after 5 months I could control myself and just have one. Nope."

"I told myself it wasn't a big deal since I didn't smoke that much."

Do those sentences feel familiar? They should. We have all used them. The stressed reach. The reward after the finished job, the drive, the meal. The heaven at lighting and the shame an hour later. The coffee that seems to call for it. The beer garden that seems to require it. The uniform we thought was our personality. The boredom we filled with smoke.

I am not mocking a single one of those thoughts. Each felt true when you thought it. Each felt like your own reasoning. I believed every one of them myself.

Notice something, though. They cannot all be true at once. Does the dose calm you or excite you? Does it help you think or help you stop thinking? Is it a reward or a relief? Is it part of who you are or something you hate being? A thing that does opposite jobs on demand is not doing any of them. It is doing something else, and we will see plainly what that is.

For now I only want you to feel known. I know what you say to yourself at midnight. I know the little rituals — the patted pocket, the offered light, the first deep pull that seems to say, now I can think. I know the shame of hiding it and the pride of saying you choose it. I do not look down on you for any of it. We were all taught the same lines.

You do not need to defend those sentences to me, and you do not need to abandon them yet. Let them sit on the page. Read on, and watch whether they still sound like truth when you understand where they came from.

The fact is, every one of those sentences was installed, not born. You did not smoke your first cigarette because you needed calm or reward or company. Your first cigarettes tasted foul and made you dizzy. You persisted because everyone seemed to do it, because it looked grown, because you did not want to be left out. Then the doses taught your body to want, and your mind learned to call that want by grand names. That is not weakness. That is how the trap works on anyone who steps inside it.

Does that ring even slightly true? If you wanted to stop a hobby you truly enjoyed, would you need a book on how to stop? The mere fact you hold this book tells you something your excuses cannot cover: you do not feel free. And if you do not feel free, the question is not how to be stronger. The question is what has taken your freedom, and how to take it back without pain.

### THE CONTRACT

So here is my part, stated flatly, and your part, which is very small.

My part: I will show you, step by step, that cigarettes do nothing for you at all. Not calm. Not pleasure. Not help. I will not lecture you. I will not frighten you into changing. I will not ask you to be miserable. I will take each reason you think you smoke and show you where the pleasure really came from — the break, the drink, the company, the finished job — and you will see the dose was only ever sneaking a ride on moments that were good without it.

Your part: three simple agreements for reading. They are not hard. They are the whole method of reading this book, and they make everything else easy.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
Nothing here asks for belief yet, only a fair hearing.

2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH THE BOOK
Let the belief change first, so stopping costs you nothing.

3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF EXCITEMENT, NOT DOOM
You are starting an escape, not serving a sentence.

Read those again. They matter more than they look.

Keep an open mind means you do not argue ahead. When something sounds strange — and it will — you do not close the book. You say, let me see where this goes. You have believed for years that quitting must be hard. Allow, just for the hours of reading, that the opposite might be true.

Do not stop or cut down until you finish means exactly that. This will surprise you. Every other method told you to stop at once, to cut down, to show commitment by suffering early. I tell you the opposite, because suffering blinds you. If you try to prove yourself while you read, you will read through panic and craving, and you will miss the argument. Smoke as before while you read. Let your mind stay clear to judge. When belief changes, stopping will not feel like an act of courage. It will feel obvious.

Begin with excitement, not doom, means you change your expectation of what happens next. You are not starting a sentence. You are starting an escape. Think how you feel when a long bill is paid off, when a bad tooth stops aching, when you step out of tight shoes at night. That lift — the weight gone — is the feeling to bring to page one. You are about to be shown a door you thought was a wall.

Can you feel even a flicker of that? You hoped, when you picked up this book as a last resort, that you might be wrong about suffering. Hold that hope. It is enough.

Some smokers ask me: should I believe you now? No. Belief now would be cheap. I want something better from you. I want you to test me. Read as an investigator, not a pupil. Notice your own smoking while you read. Notice when you light, what you tell yourself, how the dose tastes when you pay full attention instead of dreaming through it. Notice whether the calm follows the cigarette or the cigarette follows the unease. You are gathering your own evidence, and your own evidence will free you.

And if doubt speaks — and it will — let it speak. I know this is hard to accept. A habit of years, ended easily? Freedom without missing? It sounds like a trick. Good. Bring that doubt with you. Doubt examined is more useful than faith borrowed. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain by hearing me out to the end.

Ask yourself: what do you risk by reading on exactly as you are? No pain. No decision tonight. No one watching. And what do you stand to win if this is true? Your mornings without coughing and reaching. Your money staying in your pocket. Your clothes and breath clean. Your children never seeing you slip to the garage again. Your evenings yours again. Easily, immediately and permanently.

Those three words are not a slogan. They are what happens when the want is removed instead of fought. You do not need to be strong enough. You need to see clearly enough. And clarity comes from reading, not from struggling.

So make the small choice. Stay open. Keep smoking while you read. Come to the next page expecting to be shown something good, not punished for something bad.

Let me be plain about what keeping an open mind looks like in practice, because you have been disappointed before and caution is natural. It does not mean you force yourself to agree with me. It means you let each page finish speaking before you judge it. When you catch yourself thinking, this cannot apply to me, you notice the thought and read the next paragraph anyway. That is all. Smokers who arrived certain they were the exception have told me that this one habit of reading — finishing the page before deciding — was what finally let the argument reach them. You can allow the same.

And understand why I ask you to bring excitement rather than dread. Dread narrows the mind. When you expect pain, every sentence sounds like a threat and every craving sounds like proof you cannot succeed. Expectation shapes what you notice. If you sit down expecting another scolding, you will hear one even where none exists. If you sit down expecting an escape to be explained, you will hear clearly. You know from your own working life how differently you learn when you are curious rather than braced. Bring the curious mind, not the braced one. Tell yourself the truth: I am about to learn why I really smoke, and learning is never punishment.

You may wonder whether you have the right to feel hopeful after so many failures. You do. Hope here is not wishful thinking. It is logic. Your past attempts left the want in place and then asked you to live with it by effort. This book removes the want by showing it has no gift inside it. Those are two entirely different tasks. Failing at the first tells you nothing about the second. Would you call yourself hopeless with maps because a faulty compass once led you wrong? You would change the compass. Let this book be the corrected compass, and let yourself feel the lift that comes with it.

### YOU ARE NOT THE ENEMY

Before we go further, let me leave you where you need to be tonight.

You are not weak. You are not foolish. You are not a lost cause because you failed twenty times. We fell into the same hole, for the same reasons, saying the same sentences. The hole was dug before we arrived, and it is kept dug for profit while we blame ourselves for falling in.

Warmth belongs to you. Contempt belongs to the trap that rents your breath and your money and calls it pleasure. I will be harsh about that trap in this book, because it deserves harshness. I will never be harsh about you.

Take the corridor picture with you. That man on the plastic chair, hands around the pack, promising the ceiling he will stop. He meant it. You have meant it. Meaning it was never the problem. The method was the problem. This time we remove the want instead of fighting it, and you will find there is nothing to fight.

Do you feel even slightly lighter at the thought that your failures might not have been your fault? Hold that lightness. It will grow.

You came to this book braced for another lecture and another deprivation. Leave this first chapter willing to investigate instead. Bring your doubt, bring your pack, bring your list of reasons. I ask for nothing else yet except the three agreements you have already read.

Read on — not with dread, but with curiosity. The door you thought was a wall has a handle on your side.

- I now know the danger of staying is real, yet I understand I will not quit from fear but from seeing there is nothing to miss.
- I now see stopping here can be easy and I am willing to keep reading with an open mind.
