Chapter 15
THE LIST AND THE OPEN DOOR

IN THIS CHAPTER: the folded list kept by the kettle, the shut door behind you, and the open one ahead into your own life.

*You were never built to smoke — you have only come home to yourself.*

You are standing now where I promised you would stand.

The packet is gone. The counting is gone. The little tug under the ribs has starved out. Mornings begin without a reach. Coffee stands on its own. Evenings unfold without an interval. You have lived ordinary days and found them complete, and each one has proved you a little more quietly than the last.

I want you to stop for a moment and feel what that means.

We fell into this together, you and I. We believed the same lie, that a roll of paper and leaf calmed us and completed our pleasures and helped us cope. We paid for that lie with our breath and our money and our freedom to come and go. We stood outside in the rain for it. We hid for it. We timed our lives by it.

Look at you now. You breathe without bargaining. You come and go without asking leave. There is nothing left to miss because there was never anything in it.

That is done. This short closing chapter does only three things. It puts the whole method on one picture you can keep. It tells you the one truth I have saved until now. And then it sends you out.

### KEEP THIS PICTURE

If you came here first, flicking through, stop now and start at the beginning. This list will mean nothing without the seeing that goes before it, and the seeing takes a whole book. You have nothing to prove by hurrying. Begin on the first page with an open mind and read straight through, and this page will be waiting for you when you are free.

For the rest of you, who have read and seen and smoked your final cigarette consciously and shut the door, here is everything I asked of you, gathered in one place. Tear it out in your mind. Keep a photograph of it by the kettle if you like. There is nothing new in it. That is the point.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
2. DON'T STOP OR CUT DOWN UNTIL YOU FINISH THE BOOK
3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF EXCITEMENT, NOT DOOM
4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
6. TAKE NO NOTICE OF OTHER SMOKERS' QUITTING TALK
7. NEVER HAVE JUST ONE
8. NOTICE WHAT THE MOMENT ITSELF GIVES YOU
9. USE NO SUBSTITUTES
10. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE CONSCIOUSLY, THEN CLOSE THE DOOR
11. WHEN THE THOUGHT OF A CIGARETTE COMES, ANSWER IT WITH JOY

You followed them, and they have done their work. You need no other rules. You need no timetable and no counting of days. You are not serving anything. You are free as of now, and free tomorrow on the same terms, by the same seeing.

When the old thought flickers, thin and far off, you know what to do. You do not debate it. You do not feed it. You answer it once with joy and you move on with your day. Think it loudly inside yourself the way a free person speaks of an escape.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Each time that answer fires it becomes a small burst of relief. The reminder that once would have pulled you back now tells you the door behind you is shut.

### THE PROPERTY RETURNS TO ITS OWNER

Here is the truth I saved for this last room.

You were never a smoker by nature.

I want you to feel the weight of that. Before the first one you were complete. You laughed and worked and rested and faced trouble and enjoyed good nights without any of this. Then you were taken, slowly, by a con that told you a dose was pleasure and support, and every dose after it tightened the hold by creating the very emptiness it claimed to fill. What smoked for years was not your nature. It was something kept.

You were property the trap kept, and stopping has returned what was always yours.

Does that change how you see these last days? You did not lose a companion. You did not surrender a pleasure. You recovered stolen goods. Your breath. Your mornings. Your money staying in your pocket. Your freedom to stay inside when it rains and to step outside when you choose. Your coffee tasting of itself. Your own company, without a timetable ringing in it.

I felt this myself on the other side of my own last cigarette. I waited for grief and felt instead a strange familiarity, as if I had met an old self coming toward me down the street. I had not become someone new. I had come back. You will feel that familiarity deepen in the weeks ahead until you no longer remember why you ever believed otherwise.

There is nothing to mourn here. A kept thing set free does not mourn its keeping. It runs.

So take your life back in both hands. I say this to you as one escaped prisoner to another, with all the certainty I own. The door behind you is shut. The one ahead stands open onto ordinary life, which you now know is the prize, not the price.

Go and enjoy the rest of your life, free.

### SUMMARY

- I now know I was never a smoker by nature, and I feel relief that what was always mine has been returned to me.
- I now know ordinary life was always complete in itself, and I live it free with nothing to miss and nothing to resist.
- I now feel joy whenever smoking crosses my mind, and I answer the thought with relief that I no longer smoke.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!
