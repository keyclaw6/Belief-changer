Chapter 14
MORNINGS, DRIVES, SHOPS, EVENINGS

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kettle on the first free morning, the car without the window down, the shop queue, coffee that tastes of coffee, the patio in evening air, and the pub garden where the old circle still lights up.

*You are not waiting for an ambush now — you are living ordinary days as a non-smoker.*

You wake and the hand does not move.

The light is coming through the curtain the way it always did. The kettle clicks. The house makes its small morning sounds. For years this was the hour when you coughed yourself awake and reached for the pack before your feet reached the floor, and the day began with a small empty tug under the ribs that told you when to stand and when to sit. This morning the tug is not giving orders. You notice the quiet because you know what used to fill it.

That knowing is the whole difference.

Get up slowly and feel how ordinary this is. Wash. Dress. Drink water. The chest feels clear in a way you had forgotten was normal, and you know the clearness is yours again, not borrowed for twenty minutes. We used to pay for that first small lift with the rest of the day on the hourly bell. You see now there was never anything in it to miss.

### THREE QUIET MORNINGS

The first morning feels strange because you expect it to feel strange, and then you feel almost surprised by how little there is to fight.

The second morning you wake and for a second the old thought flickers, have one with coffee, before you are even fully awake. Do not be startled by that flicker. It is only a memory firing while the little physical itch starves out within days. You answer it with joy and you get on with your morning, and you feel relief that you no longer smoke.

The third morning you forget to remember. You make coffee. You find your keys. You open the door and the air outside smells of rain and bins and someone's toast, and you take a deep breath for nothing and you know freedom feels ordinary, which is exactly why it lasts.

Nothing here needs new rules. You do not need a different morning. You need the same morning, lived free.

### THE DRIVE AND THE SHOP QUEUE

Take the car you always took.

For years a long drive meant five hours of smoke and the window cracked open whatever the weather, and then the dance at the petrol station with the ashtray and the shirt and the hands, trying to leave no trace. You know that dance was never enjoyment. It was housekeeping for a habit.

Drive it now with the window shut. Breathe the clean air in the car and feel how good it is to arrive without needing to air yourself out. When the old thought comes at the red light, that a drive goes better with one, ask yourself what better ever meant. Did the road change? Did the traffic ease? Or did a want quiet for a few minutes because you fed it? You know the answer, and the lights change, and you drive on free.

Stand in the shop queue where you used to count the packs behind the glass and work out whether you had enough for the evening. Stand there with empty pockets and feel the lightness. You do not need to avoid the shop. You do not need to look away. Walk in for milk and walk out with milk. Inhabit every old place confidently and let each one prove you. Changing nothing else in your life is how you prove the trap took nothing with it when it went.

### COFFEE THAT TASTES OF COFFEE

Sit down with morning coffee and a finished dinner the way you always did, and notice what the moment itself gives you.

We used to believe the cigarette completed them. The coffee. The meal. The pause with company when the plates are cleared and nobody wants the evening to end. You believed the taste was richer and the company warmer because of the smoke beside it.

Taste it now and see where the pleasure always lived. The coffee tastes of coffee. The food tastes of food. The talk is still talk. The completion you feel when the cup is empty is complete without a dose on top of it. The cigarette was only ever sneaking a ride on a moment that was already whole.

Sit a little longer than you need to, not because you must prove anything, but because you enjoy sitting there. That ease is what a non-smoker feels for the rest of your life, and it is yours from now on without rent.

### THE PATIO IN EVENING AIR

Go out to the same back patio in the evening.

You know this place. The chair. The rail. The sound of the street thinning out. For years you stood here to feed a want on a timetable and called it company. Now stand here with nothing in your hand and no clock in your head. Take one concrete breath of the cool air and feel it reach all the way down.

This patio belongs to its owner again.

You do not need to fill the pause. The pause is not empty. It is free time, and you feel glad the timetable no longer rings in it.

When a faint pang comes here, thin and far off, do not turn it into a debate. There are no two sides in you now to debate with. There is only a dying echo and your answer. Think it loudly inside yourself, the way a free person speaks of an escape.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Each time that thought fires, it becomes a small burst of relief. The reminder that once would have pulled you back now tells you the door is shut and the room beyond is yours.

### THE PUB GARDEN

You will stand in the pub garden among friends while the old circle lights up, and you will feel something you did not expect.

You will not feel envy. You will feel distance and warmth at once. Watch them step out into the cold to stand around a small fire of their own making, watch the talk pause for the lighting and the searching for lighters, watch one go to buy more because the pack is short for the night. Ask yourself honestly what you are seeing.

Does that really look like enjoyment?

You know it does not. It looks like the hourly bell ringing in good company. You feel glad you no longer answer it, and you feel kind toward the people who still do, because you stood exactly where they stand. We all stood there once.

Say nothing about it unless they ask you plainly. I tell you this firmly because I want to protect your freedom. Preaching breeds argument, and argument drags you back into defending what you no longer need to defend. Let your ease do the talking. If someone asks how you stopped, tell them it was easy and you feel wonderful, and leave it there. A happy non-smoker in the circle does more good than a sermon.

Do not avoid your friends and do not avoid the evening. Keep your friendships and your drink and your good nights exactly as they are. The night does not need a dose to be complete, and you prove that by staying in it, free.

There is one piece of language I want you to drop for good. Never say instead. Never say I can't have one so I'll have this. A sweet, a vape, a chew, a deep-breathing ritual used as a replacement all whisper the same lie, that something was taken and something must fill the gap. Nothing was taken. You escaped. Say what you truly want in positive words. Great, I'll have coffee. Lovely, I'll take a walk. You do not substitute for a dead enemy. You rejoice that the enemy is dead.

You feel a small sadness sometimes when you think of it as an old companion, and I understand that sadness. For years it stood beside work and breaks and talk. See it clearly now and let the sadness turn. You are not mourning a friend. You are celebrating the death of a captor that posed as one. A dead captor deserves no tears. It deserves relief.

11. WHEN THE THOUGHT OF A CIGARETTE COMES, ANSWER IT WITH JOY
Let every reminder fire the replacement thought.

You own that answer now, and you use it once each time, and you move on. You never reopen the decision. The deciding was finished over the ashtray. A free person does not hold a meeting about every memory.

### IF YOU LIGHT ONE

Let me tell you what to do if you ever light one, so one mistake never becomes a chain.

Do not tell yourself you have failed. Do not tell yourself you have got away with it either. Both sentences are the same lie wearing different coats.

If it happens, see it for what it is. A warning rumble, never a failure and never a license to repeat. It tastes foul because it is foul. It gives you nothing because there was never anything in it. What matters is never letting the belief back in. Remind yourself at once that you are a non-smoker who made one mistaken puff, not a smoker again, and think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! and go on free from that minute.

Do not smoke another to punish yourself or to test yourself. There is no test. There is only the door, and you keep it shut.

You came out of the last chapter free. You walk through these ordinary days and you find them ordinary, which delights you more each time. Mornings without the reach. Drives without the window down. Shops without the count. Coffee that tastes of itself. Evenings on a patio that is yours. Company you keep without stepping out into the cold.

Live them exactly as you would have lived them, only free.

### SUMMARY

- I now know ordinary moments were always complete in themselves, and I live them free without needing new rules.
- I now feel joy whenever smoking crosses my mind, and I answer the thought with relief that I no longer smoke.
- I now see a lit cigarette among friends as bondage rather than pleasure, and I pity the circle without envying it.
