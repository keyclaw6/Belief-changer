Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD IS THERE AT ALL

IN THIS CHAPTER: tonight's pack, one lighting watched all the way through, and the end of the weighing.

*If it does nothing for you, there is nothing to weigh against what it does to you.*

### THE BARGAIN YOU THINK YOU MADE

You see the loop now. The hand moving before thought. The clock ringing through the day. You know you were conned into it rather than choosing it freely.

And still a voice says, all right, it harms me, but I like it. The pleasure is worth the risk.

I hear that voice with complete respect, because it was my voice for years. Let me speak it plainly so you know I know your mind.

"It makes my day to indulge myself even though I know it's terrible for my health. Life is short so why not enjoy yourself."

"I love smoking even while I hate being a smoker. The moment I light up I'm in heaven, but I feel shitty afterwards."

I do not mock either sentence. The heaven is real as a feeling. The indulgence feels real in the second you permit it. If I told you that you never felt anything, you would close this book, and you would be right to close it. I believe you felt what you felt.

I only ask you to look at where you felt it, and how long it stayed.

We all make bargains in life. We pay money for food that feeds us. We pay tired legs for a walk that clears the head. We pay a late night for talk with friends that we remember for years. In each true bargain something leaves our hands and something real arrives in return and stays.

With cigarettes we tell ourselves we made that kind of bargain. Health and money and breath on one side. Pleasure and help on the other. We picture old scales, harm piled here, enjoyment piled there, and we tell ourselves the scales roughly balance, or that enjoyment just tips it.

I want to take those scales away from you, not to rebalance them.

A bargain needs two sides. Harm on one side is certain. You feel it in the morning throat and on the stairs and in the pocket. What is on the other side? Not the price of the harm. The proof of a good. Show me one good it does, one solid good that stands on its own and does not vanish when you look closely, and then we can talk about worth and risk. Until then there is no bargain to weigh. There is only harm paid for nothing.

Does that sound hard to accept? Good. Keep reading with that hardness in your chest. You do not need to believe me yet. You only need to demand, as an investigator would, that the cigarette show its benefit the way any honest seller must.

### WATCH ONE LIGHTING ALL THE WAY THROUGH

Tonight, watch one of your own lightings from start to finish. Not from memory. Live, while you smoke.

You tap the pack. You place the tube between your lips. You bring the flame. The first pull burns hot at the back of the throat. Under the burn there is a flavour, menthol or tobacco or whatever brand you buy, and the flavour softens the burn just enough to let you pull again.

Then for a puff, perhaps two, comes the minute you call heaven. The shoulders drop. The noise dips. A warmth spreads behind the eyes. The hand stops reaching because it holds. For that minute the want goes quiet and you call the quiet pleasure.

Stay watching. Do not stop the observation where memory usually stops it.

See what happens by the third or fourth pull. The warmth thins. The taste turns stale and bitter at the edges. The throat that welcomed the first pull now coughs at the smoke. You look down and half the stick is ash, and you are not enjoying it any more, you are finishing it. The heaven lasted a puff. The finishing lasts the rest of the cigarette.

Then see what follows after you stub it out. The stale mouth. The smell on the fingers that soap does not quite lift. The faint heaviness behind the eyes rather than lightness. The small sink inside, the one you named so honestly: I feel shitty afterwards, I hate being a smoker while I love smoking. That sink is not an extra. It belongs to the same lighting as the heaven. The heaven and the sink are one event, priced as one.

Now let me ask you three plain questions, and answer them with your own tonight in your hand, not with slogans.

If that first minute is a pleasure worth the price, why does it last only a puff before it turns into finishing? A true pleasure can be lingered over. A meal you love stays good to the last bite. Music you love does not turn foul halfway through. Why does this heaven need to be chased with a whole stick that you stop tasting?

If the indulgence makes your day, why does the day need it again an hour later, and again after that? A true indulgence satisfies. You take the afternoon off and you feel restored for days. You eat the good dinner and you are done eating. Why does this indulgence leave you exactly as hungry as before, on exactly the same schedule as every other day?

And if you love smoking, why do you hate being a smoker? Love does not come with shame attached. You love your coffee and you say so freely. You love your walk and you invite others to join. Why does this love hide in the garage, stand alone in the rain while the party goes on inside, wash its hands before hugging its children?

There is only one honest answer that fits all three. What you love in that minute is not a gift arriving. It is a want going quiet for a minute. The quiet feels like heaven because the noise before it was hellish, and both the noise and the quiet belong to the same loop.

I know the objection that rises here, because I raised it myself. You will say, but the quiet is still pleasant. Even if it is only relief, relief feels good. Why dismiss a pleasant minute?

I do not dismiss that the minute feels pleasant. I ask what it is pleasant against. Scratch an itch and the scratching feels pleasant. Would you call the itch a pleasure? Would you pay thousands a year to keep an itch alive so you can enjoy scratching it? Would you stand winded on stairs for the privilege? You would say I am mad to suggest it. Yet that is the shape of the minute you call heaven. A discomfort created by the previous dose, paused by this dose, guaranteed to return before the hour is out.

We were taught to photograph only the scratch and to cut the itch out of the picture. Put the whole strip back together, itch and scratch and itch again, and the word pleasure no longer fits. What fits is interruption. A brief pause in a disturbance that the pauses themselves keep alive.

### WHAT IT DOES TO YOU, AND WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU

Let me put this as settled fact, without a worksheet and without a debate.

Look at what smoking does TO you. It burns the throat on the way in. It leaves the mouth stale and the hair and coat smelling. It shortens the breath on stairs you once climbed without thought. It installs a clock in your day that rings every hour and sends the hand to the pack. It takes money in a steady stream for ash. It makes you hide and plan and deodorize. It leaves that sink after each lighting, the knowing that you are doing what you wish you did not do.

Now look for what it does FOR you. Not what it pauses. Not what it interrupts. What it gives that you did not have before you ever started.

Before the first pack, you faced hard hours and good hours with your own nerves and your own senses intact. Food tasted of food. Coffee tasted of coffee. A finished job felt finished. A break felt like rest. You slept through the night without waking to feed anything. You climbed stairs without calculation. You did not need a minute of heaven to feel normal, because normal was yours without paying.

What did the years of doses add to that person? Did they give steadier nerves? You smoke when nervous and remain a nervous smoker. Did they give better company? You step out alone to feed the want while company continues inside. Did they give richer taste? You taste mostly burn and then stale mouth. Did they give a fuller day? Your day is the same day cut into hourly intervals between feeds.

I say this as one who searched hard for the exception. I turned each lighting over looking for the one that truly added. The slow one after dinner. The funny one outside the pub. The quiet one on the patio with music on. Each time the setting gave and the dose took credit. The food was good. The friends were funny. The evening was warm. The dose stood beside good things the way a man who walks beside you in the sun claims he carries the light.

Strip the setting away and what remains in the hand? Paper and leaf and smoke that bites. Inhale without the coffee, without the friends, without the finished job beside it, on a rainy kerb with nobody watching, and ask honestly what it gives. The same burn. The same brief quiet. The same stale finish. The setting was doing the giving all along.

This is why the scales must go. Harm against benefit assumes a benefit exists to pile on. Once you look for the benefit directly, with tonight's lighting still warm in the ashtray, the pile on that side is empty. There is plenty it does TO you. There is nothing it does FOR you.

That sentence is the whole chapter. Hold it beside every lighting this week and watch whether your own observation confirms or denies it.

### WHAT WOULD COUNT AS GOOD

To be fair to the cigarette, let me say what I would accept as a good.

A genuine pleasure leaves you better than it found you, or at least no worse. You eat the good meal and you are fed. You hear the good talk and you carry it with you. You rest and you rise clearer. It does not need to be repeated hourly to keep you normal. It does not punish you between encounters. It does not make you hide it from those you love. You can take it or leave it and still feel yourself.

A genuine crutch does the same in harder shape. It holds you up where you would fall without it. Glasses let you see letters you cannot see. A splint holds a bone that cannot hold itself. Take the crutch away and the lack is obvious and specific. Keep it and life widens. It does not create the limp it then supports. It does not shorten the leg a little more each year while calling the support help.

Now set the cigarette beside those measures.

Does it leave you better than it found you? After each lighting you are breathless sooner, poorer by the price, stale in mouth and hair, bound tighter to the next hour. Does it hold you up where you would fall? Before you started you stood without it through every kind of hour, difficult and joyful, and children and non-smokers stand through those same hours today without its help. Does it widen life? Your life narrowed around exits and shelters and packs counted for the morning.

You call it a genuine pleasure or crutch because those are the only two names a good can carry. Either it delights you or it supports you. I have turned it under both lights and found neither. Not a smaller delight worth a smaller price. Not a rough support worth its splinters. Nothing that stands when the setting is lifted away. The delight was the setting's. The support was the pause of a want the doses themselves maintain.

Do you feel how final that is? If there were one real delight in it, one real support nothing else gives, the trap would have a leg to stand on. I would tell you to weigh it carefully against the cost. I find no leg. I find a burn masked with flavour, a minute of quiet mistaken for a gift, and a long chain of hours paying for the mistake.

Concede nothing here out of kindness to old habit. Kindness to yourself is to demand the proof and to accept its absence. The cigarette has had years to show you its good. Tonight, watched closely, it showed a burn, a mask, a puff of quiet, and a sink. That is not a pleasure to balance. That is not a crutch to lean on. That is a dose feeding an itch and calling the feeding love.

Once that is seen, the old question falls away. Worth the risk assumes worth. Where there is no worth, there is no weighing. There is only a thing that does much TO you and nothing FOR you, asking to be paid as if it were your friend.

That is life inside the nicotine trap. You pay the friend that robs you and thank the robber for the pause between robberies.

### KEEP THE PICTURE CLEAR

You hold the verdict now. It will be attacked, and I want you protected while the rest of the proof arrives.

In the coming days you will hear other voices about how to stop. A friend will press a piece of gum into your hand and tell you to taper. A packet will promise a milder smoke. A programme will tell you to count days and fight cravings and avoid every corner where you ever lit up. A well-meaning voice will tell you this book asks too much belief and you should mix a little of everything.

Do not mix. I say this not from pride but from care for the picture you just won.

Each of those well-meaning offers carries the old belief hidden inside it: that there is something real to forfeit, something to wean, something to replace, something to brace against. Follow them while you read and you will smuggle back in the very scales we just removed. You will read my sentences through their belief and wonder why the words do not land. The confusion will feel like doubt about yourself. It will be confusion of methods.

Let your smoking stay as it is while you read, steady and observed, and let every other method wait outside the door until you have finished. Watch each lighting with the verdict beside it. Does this one show the good that was promised? Does this one do anything FOR me that the hour did not already hold? The observation itself is the work now. No inner argument needs winning beyond the next honest look.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Mixed methods mix beliefs, and mixed beliefs keep the trap alive.

Carry that one agreement with the verdict and the next pieces will land cleanly. You see the loop. You demanded the benefit and found the plate empty. The named pleasures still stand in line to be examined one by one on their own ground, each in its own hour, each returned to its rightful owner. You do not need to rush to meet them. Tonight it is enough to know there is no weight on that side of the scales, and therefore no bargain to mourn.

### SUMMARY

- I now refuse the harm-against-pleasure bargain because I see there is no benefit to weigh.
- I now understand the heaven of one puff was only the brief quiet of a want the doses keep alive.
- I now demand proof of anything it does for me before I let talk of price confuse me again.
