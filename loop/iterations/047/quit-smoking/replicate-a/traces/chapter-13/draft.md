Chapter 13
THE LAST ONE, ON PURPOSE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the chair by the window, the ashtray and the pack, one ordinary cigarette watched to its end, the vow spoken aloud, the answering thought for every later thought, and the two doors kept shut.

*This last cigarette proves there is nothing to wait for and nothing to miss.*

You have waited the whole book for this hour. You can feel it in your hands.

Sit where you usually smoke. Not in a special place. Not on a holiday. At the kitchen table, on the step, in the chair by the window where the light falls in the afternoon. Put the pack down. Put the lighter down. Put the ashtray where you always put it. Nothing about this hour is grand. That plainness is the whole point. Freedom does not need a stage. It needs clear sight and one deliberate act.

You are impatient now, and I am glad. Impatience here is not nerves. It is sight pressing to act.

Before we act, let me check one thing with you, because I take this hour seriously.

### ARE YOU IMPATIENT TO FINISH

Tell me true. Do you feel ready to finish?

Not ready the way a man feels ready to lift something heavy. Ready the way a man feels when he has packed and checked his pockets three times and keeps looking at the door. A lightness. An eagerness to be done. A wish to smoke this last one with your eyes open and then walk out and live.

If you feel that, we go on. You have done the looking. You know the calm was the ending of a want the last dose had lit. You know the work kept the credit and the moment kept the credit. You know the uniform was rented. You know the best night was sun and talk and company with a dose sneaking a ride. You know whose hands wound the timetable and whose method farmed the failures. There is nothing left to argue. There is only to finish.

If you do not feel that, do not force this hour. Put this chapter down and read the book again from the start. I mean that kindly and I mean it firmly. A reader who still believes there is a pleasure or a help in the dose should not yet smoke a final one. The belief must change first, so that stopping costs you nothing. When the belief has changed, the impatience comes on its own. You will know it because you will be almost annoyed with me for talking instead of letting you finish.

Which is it for you? Are you holding one excuse back for after this pack, this week, this hard patch at work? What would another pack teach you that the last ten thousand did not? What would another week prove except that waiting keeps the hourly bell ringing? If waiting gave answers, you would have them by now.

You are ready. Let us finish it, Easily, immediately and permanently.

### SMOKE IT THE WAY IT REALLY IS

Take one cigarette. Only one. This is not extra smoking. This is not permission to enjoy a farewell. This is attention paid once, fully, so you never need to pay it again.

Light it the way you always do. Then watch what you are doing as if you had never seen it before.

Taste the first pull. Is it sweet? Is it rich? Or is it hot paper and chemical bite at the back of the throat, with a flavouring that has to mask the smoke to make it bearable? Notice the smell on your fingers already. Notice the thin grey curl rising and the ash forming. That ash is what you have been drawing into your chest for years. Look at it while it is outside you.

Smoke it to the middle. Feel the chest fill and the head give its small shift. That shift is what you once called help. Ask yourself plainly now, with your eyes open, what help has arrived. Has the room changed? Has the worry changed? Has the work changed? Or has a small empty tug under the ribs quieted for a few minutes because you fed it? You know the answer because you have watched this mechanism across the whole book. The quiet is replenishment speed. The dose ends a want the last dose created, then guarantees the want returns.

Smoke it to the last third. This is where no smoker looks by choice. It turns sour and hot. The taste turns bitter. The throat scratches. The stomach gives its faint protest under the supposed relief. The fingers smell. The air around your head thickens. Pay full attention here especially. This is not a pleasure cooling. This is the thing showing itself.

What part of this would you invite back for the rest of your life? The burn? The sour end? The smell in the curtains an hour later? The hourly check that starts again twenty minutes after you stub it out? Which single pull would improve a morning, a meal, a drive, a talk with someone you love? Name it. Touch it. Is there one?

There is none. That is not my verdict laid on you. It is your own sight, from your own mouth and nose and chest, in this ordinary chair.

Stub it out. Look at the crushed end in the ashtray. That is what you were told you could not live without.

Do you feel a small sadness as you look at it? I understand that sadness, and I do not mock it. For years this stick stood beside coffee and breaks and talk and hard hours. It feels like a companion is about to leave the room. Let me tell you what that sadness really is, because this matters for everything after.

You are not about to lose a friend. You are about to bury an enemy that posed as one. The grief is real, and the friend was never real. The thing that stood beside you in every picture took your breath, your money, and your peace in return for ending a want it had planted itself. When you see that clearly, the sadness turns. What you mourned as a farewell you now celebrate as an escape. A dead captor deserves no tears. It deserves relief.

You have smoked your last one on purpose. Now close the door.

10. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE CONSCIOUSLY, THEN CLOSE THE DOOR
You have seen it true, and you are done.

If you take prescribed medication, live with a medical condition, are pregnant, or feel seriously unwell, keep following your doctor's advice and ask them about stopping smoking.

Now make the vow. Say it aloud where you sit, with the ashtray in front of you. Mean every word, because every word is true.

I will never smoke again, not one puff, for the rest of my life. I am free.

Say it again, slower. Let it land. You have not started a sentence. You have ended one.

You are a non-smoker as of now. Not from tomorrow. Not after the weekend. Now. Congratulations. I mean that with my whole heart. You have walked out.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions, and you have followed them.

### THE THOUGHT THAT ANSWERS EVERY THOUGHT

You will think of cigarettes again. Of course you will. The street will show you a lit doorway. A meal will end. A hard hour will come. A good hour will come. The old bell will give a faint ring now and then, thin and far off, while the little itch starves out within days.

Do not be startled when it happens. Do not count it. Do not turn it into the tug-of-war where you pull against yourself with both ends held by the same old hands. There are no two sides in you now. There is only a memory firing, and your answer.

Answer every such thought at once with joy, not with effort. Think it deliberately, loudly inside yourself, the way a free man speaks of an escape.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Think it when coffee comes and the hand expects its old partner. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! Think it when work presses and the mind offers its old off-switch. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! Think it when drink flows and the night swells with talk. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! Each reminder becomes a small burst of relief. The thought that once would have been a tug becomes a bell that tells you the door is shut and the room beyond is yours.

This is how you live, for the rest of your life, as a non-smoker who enjoys being one.

There are two doors to keep shut, and I want them shut now, before life tests them.

The first door is the bad day. A bad day will come. Work will turn. Someone will sicken. Money will pinch. Grief will come as it comes to all of us. In that hour the old voice will speak softly and sound almost kind. Just one to get through this. You know that voice. It is not kindness. It is the old translation offering the cause as the cure. Ask yourself on that day what one dose ever fixed outside the ashtray. Did it mend the worry? Did it lift the load? Or did it add a fresh chain to a heavy hour and restart the clock? You do not need its help because there is no help in it. Let the hour be what it is, and let it pass while you stay free.

The second door is the just-one thought in a good hour. Months from now, when you have forgotten the ashtray smell and the sour last third, the voice will try a different tone. Light. Almost amused. You have beaten it. One will not hurt. You can control it now. Do you remember the smoker who learned after five months that one is never one? He speaks truth. One dose re-lights the want, wakes the bell, and rebuilds in weeks what you dismantled in this book. There is no such thing as one. There is only the door open or the door shut. Keep it shut and you keep everything.

Shut both now in your mind while you sit in this quiet. See the bad-day offer and answer it. See the just-one offer and answer it. Both get the same answer. I am free and I stay free.

You came to this chapter impatient. You leave it free. The waiting is over. The last ordinary instance is behind you. The vow is spoken. The answering thought is yours. The two doors are named and shut.

Stand up from the chair. Empty the ashtray. Throw the pack away where you cannot neatly retrieve it, bin it, wet it, break it. Wash your hands. Drink a glass of water. Take a deep breath for nothing and notice the cold air reaching further than it reached this morning. Go on with your evening exactly as you would have lived it, only free.

### SUMMARY

- I now know my last cigarette was foul and empty, and I smoked it with open eyes so I never need another.
- I now feel free as of my vow, and I answer every thought of smoking with joy that I no longer smoke.
- I now see the bad-day offer and the just-one thought as the same lie, and I keep both doors shut.
