Chapter 7
THE LOOP

IN THIS CHAPTER: the dark kitchen at midnight, the cold floor and the hid wrappers, the shout and the whisper, the drawer that rings, the paid actor, the want that outlives the like, the small dip.

*The crave never proves sweet helps you — it proves the last sweet left you low and taught you to want what you no longer even like.*

## THE NIGHT KITCHEN

You know this hour though you never speak of it at noon.

Midnight. House still. Cold lino under bare feet. Fridge light spills white across the floor and hums. You stand in a shirt and pants with the door half shut to keep the light low. You told yourself at tea you were done for the day. You ate well. Meat and bread and veg. Then the bar called from the drawer and you had a bite to close. That bite called a second. The second called the packet. Now you stand here with the packet dead in the bin and a fresh one in your hand though your belly aches from bite six and your mouth feels pasted and sick.

I know that floor because I stood on it for years. We all stood on it and called it hunger though we knew it was not hunger.

Mark what your own hands do though shame begs you to look away. First two bites sing. Third bite sings less. By bite six joy dies though the hand marches on. You finish the packet not to taste more but to end the pull by killing it. Then you fold the wrapper small and push it deep under peelings so morning will not see.

Ask straight, as a man asks about his own house though the old voice hates plain asks. If that midnight raid fed hunger, why did hunger grow though the belly filled. If those bites gave joy, why did joy die at bite three while the hand worked to bite twelve. If craving proves need, why did need shout loudest after you fed it full.

You know the answer though it shames the old talk. Hunger never eats past ache to finish a packet. Hunger shuts and leaves though food stays. Only want finishes though joy died and plans the next while it chews. That forward lean while chewing is not appetite. Appetite stops and thanks.

See the secrecy for what it proves though I blame you not one grain, for you know shame never lived in plain eating.

You never hid an apple core for morning. You never folded a bread crust small under peelings. You never ate soup standing in the dark with the door half shut. Plain food leaves no trail to hide because plain food shuts. Midnight sweet leaves wrappers to hide because you know while you hide them that you ate past want. The hide is not proof you are weak. The hide is the mark of the loop. Night eaters hide the way drinkers hide bottles, not from hunger but from a pull they cannot square with their own eyes.

I hid too. Bar in the desk drawer under bills. I told myself I liked it. I did not like bite six. Paste. Ache. Sour mouth. I liked bite one and I chased bite one through eleven dead bites though bite one never came back that night.

That chase names the whole night though we leave its workings for the next rooms. The mouth stops liking long before the hand stops taking. Liking dies early. Want marches on. You finish past joy because joy never drove the finish. Want drove it.

Think of your own last raid though you change nothing yet and you buy nothing new. When did you last stop at joy. Never. Joy stops at two. The packet stops at empty. That gap between joy stopping and the hand stopping is the loop made flesh on your own floor. You lived it. I lived it. We all lived it and called it a sweet tooth though teeth never drove it.

So hold this floor in mind while we name its parts, for this room must hold though later rooms shake. Night sweet did not feed. It pulled. Night hands did not enjoy. They obeyed. Night wrappers did not hide food. They hid want.

The packet empties. Joy died first.

## THE SHOUT AND THE WHISPER

Now hear why sweet shouts so loud though plain feeds more, for you know the difference in your own ears though shop talk never marked it.

Stand with me in a summer garden though the year runs cold, for you know this garden from your own life though sweet sat nowhere near it that day.

Bread torn warm. Cheese that smells of the farm. Apple cold from the bag. Talk runs easy round the table. Sun on the arms. Kids laugh by the fence. You eat slow and you taste each bite though no bite screams. Salt. Crumb. Juice. Your head feels fed though no flash hit. That feed whispers. It does not seize. It fills low and wide and leaves you free to laugh without checking the drawer.

Now stand the same man at half three at a desk with a bar though the belly holds lunch and you know this desk too though you call it need.

Wrapper cracks. Smell lifts. First bite floods. Head jerks up a notch. Blood sings for ten minutes. Hands type fast. Then the sing thins. Mouth turns dry. Head sinks lower than before the bar. Hands slow. Eyes check the drawer though the belly holds food. That hit shouts. It seizes the ear for ten minutes then leaves the ear ringing.

You know both hours though the old talk calls only the second one pleasure. Mark the trick though it wears plain dress.

True rewards whisper. Bread whispers. Soup whispers. Sun whispers. Rest whispers. A good meal whispers shut and frees the head for hours. Engineered fast hits shout into the same ear. They flood the mouth in seconds, spike the want signal hard, then drop you lower than they found you. Shout, drop, shout, drop. That round is not stronger pleasure. It is louder pull. Loud proves nothing but loud.

Ask with me, for your own tongue holds the proof though the old talk buries it. Why does water taste of nothing after a bar but taste sweet after weeks plain though the tap never changed. Same tap. Same mouth. Flat then sweet. If plain meant dull by build, water would stay dull. Water sweetens because the shout stops drowning the whisper. Why does fruit taste sour to a mouth fresh off flood but taste sharp and kind to a mouth left plain for days though the fruit never changed. Same apple. Same mouth. Sour then sweet. If taste lived in sweet alone, fruit would never rise. Fruit rises because the ear clears once the shout stops.

I learned the shout through tea though I laugh to tell it. Strong tea with two sugars for twenty years. One plain week, tea tasted like dishwater. Second plain week, tea tasted round and dark and kind. No leaf changed. Only the shout died. That hour taught me my tongue was never dead. It was shouted down.

See the build behind the shout though I blame no eater, for you know desire never grew wild like grass. Flood food hits fast by build. Sugar milled fine melts fast on the tongue. Chew never gets time to say enough. Fullness never gets time to stand up. So the hand lifts again though the belly holds food. That is fast build doing fast work.

Whispers feed. Shouts pull.

You say to me, but I feel the lift, it must mean fuel. I hear the ache under that line, for lift feels true in the blood. Only ask, what lifts. Does bread lift by feeding or does BAD SUGAR lift by ending a dip its last dose laid. You know the answer from your own afternoons though the old voice begs you to blink. Plain at one holds you even to five though no flood arrived. Sweet at two flashes to half past then drops you dull by three though the belly holds food. If spooned sweet fuelled, more spoon would mean more fire. With true food that holds. With BAD SUGAR the reverse holds. Flood borrows from later to flash now.

So mark the shout for what it is though the old talk calls it taste. Loud never meant good. Loud meant fast. Fast never meant fed. Fast meant pulled.

The shout drowns. The whisper feeds.

## THE DOORBELL IN THE DRAWER

Now see why the pull fires before the bite though you swore hunger starts in the belly, for you know this ring though you call it thought.

Keep a bar in the drawer for a week though you change nothing yet and you eat as usual, for you know this drawer though you never watched it work.

Morning. You pass the desk to fetch a pen. Hand stills half a breath over the drawer. Mouth waters though the belly holds breakfast. Picture of the bar stands up behind the eyes though you meant to write mail. You shake it off and work. Half ten. You pass again for paper. Ring louder. Smell seems to rise through wood though the bar sits sealed. Jaw aches faint. Hands feel empty though they hold work. Lunch plain behind you. One o'clock plain meal eaten to shut. Two o'clock steady. Half three tired head, dry mouth, edgy nerves. You pass a third time and the hand answers before the head votes. Wrapper cracks before you chose. First bite floods though the belly said shut at one.

I know that drawer because I kept it for years. Top right. Under bills. Bars stacked neat like letters. Each pass rang though I called myself strong at dawn. By Friday the drawer stood empty though I swore on Monday this week would run plain. No belly drove that week. A bell drove it.

Think of a doorbell wired inside a drawer though you never wired it on purpose, for you know this bell though no iron hangs there.

The stored sweet rings each time you pass. The hand answers though the head meant work. Morning soft ring. Noon louder ring. Tired hour hard ring that will not stop till the drawer opens. You call that ring thought. You call it memory. You call it a hard day. It is none though each wears its coat. It is learned pull firing at sight, smell, hour, place, mood. Wrapper. Till. Corner shop glow. Tea break clatter. Box on the breakroom table. Each rings though the belly holds food. Each answers with feet though the mouth holds no hunger.

Ask straight, for your own week holds the proof though the old talk paints it need. Does plain bread ring from the bin in that hard hour. Never. Does soup call from the pan by name at half three. Never. Does the apple cry out from the bowl though it sits in plain sight. Never. Only one shape rings. Soft. Sweet. Quick. Biscuit. Bar. Cola. Cake. If that ring lived in the belly, bread would ring too. Bread never rings. One wrapper rings because one wrapper taught the bell.

See how the bell learns though I dress it in no white coat and no hard words past your own eyes, for you know learning faster than any talk.

Each flood lays its wire. Sweet on sweet, day on day, the wire thickens though you brush twice and mean well. Bite pairs with drawer. Flood pairs with three o'clock. Wrapper pairs with tea. Till pairs with pay. Soon drawer alone fires the mouth though no sweet touched the tongue. Smell alone fires the hand though the belly says shut. Picture alone fires the feet though the head says work. You know the feet moving before the vote. I knew them too. We all joked about feet with minds of their own though the joke told straight. Feet learn. Mouths water on cue. Hands answer bells.

That is why time off alone never kills the bell though the old talk swears it should, for you know men clean for weeks then fall at one smell though the blood runs plain.

The wire is not doom. Bells thin once seen though they ring loud while fed. Each time you hear the ring and mark it as ring, not hunger, the wire loosens. Each time you eat plain to shut and let the ring die unfed, the bell learns new silence. Hunger waits and takes bread. Want nags and takes one wrapper. Three tells never lie. Doubt the wrapper now.

You hand me your fear though you speak it low. If bells fire so strong, I must fight them for life. I tell you flat you fight nothing. Bells need belief the way fire needs air. See the bell as bell and the air thins. That is sight.

So when the thought comes at half three though the belly holds food, hear it for what it is though the old voice lends it your name. Not hunger. Not need. Not proof. A bell in a drawer wired by old floods and rung by hour and smell. Mark it. Let it pace. Watch it die unfed while you drink water and finish the line you write. It dies faster than fear swore though fear swore hours. Minutes, often less, once eyes stay open.

The drawer rings. The belly tells straight.

## THE PAID ACTOR

Now meet the man who saved you though he pushed you in, for you know him though you clapped him for years.

Picture a stage though the boards stand in your own kitchen, for you know this play though you never bought a ticket.

Each afternoon the lights dim. Head thickens. Mood thins. Hands slow. Enter the hero through the drawer door, cloak bright, smile wide. Bar in hand. He lifts you in ten minutes. Head clears a notch. Hands fly. You clap. Great, you say, sweet saved the day. Curtain. Next day same dim, same entry, same lift, same clap. You pay in coin and in thirst and in thick mornings though the bill never shows on stage. You call him friend though he writes the dim he saves you from.

I clapped that actor for twenty years. Three o'clock low, bar high, work saved. Breakroom cake, dull noon, smile back. Night ice cream, edgy nerves, calm for an hour. Each rescue felt true in the blood though each rescue billed later. Mood paid. Sleep paid. Morning paid. Belly paid. I never joined the bills to the hero though the hero stood by each bill smiling. We all do that. We thank the hand that lifts though it dug the hole.

See the script work in plain hours though the greasepaint blinds, for you know each line though you thought them yours.

BAD SUGAR floods. Blood answers hard. Body over-answers to hold the line. The over-answer drops you lower than before the flood though the belly holds food. That lower is not tired day. That lower is payback. Mouth dry. Head thick. Nerves edgy. Want sharp. Then the next flood ends the payback for twenty minutes and you call the end pleasure. It never was a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift. It was payback paused. The actor caused the dim then sold you the lamp.

Think of tight shoes kicked off at the door though we keep the picture to one breath, for you know that minute though I claim nothing past it.

All day pinch. All day ache. Door shut, laces loose, shoes off, ah. That ah feels sweet though shoes gave nothing. Shoes took ease then sold its return. Sweet runs that minute by the hour though labels paint it gift. Flood pinches. Flood lifts the pinch it laid. Pinch, lift, pinch, lift. That round feels like help though help never left the stage. Only the pinch left and returned.

Ask with me, and answer from your own desk hours though the old voice hates plain asks. When did sweet lift you above men who never dosed. Never. It lifts you toward their plain though it never reaches it, then drops you below though their heads hold even. When did sweet give what plain hours lack. Never. Plain hours hold even head, shut belly, clean mouth though no flood arrived. Sweet hours hold flash then payback though the mouth sang. If the actor healed, healed would hold. Healed never holds past the hour. Only the bill holds to morning.

You say to me, but the lift feels real, I type faster, I laugh louder. I hear the ache under that line, for false friends feel true while they hold your arm. Only see who set the low they lift. Tired at three after sweet noon though plain noon held even to five. Edgy at nine after evening packet though plain evenings sat kind. Foggy at dawn after midnight raid though plain nights woke plain. Each low wears sweet fingerprints though the actor bows as stranger. Lift the fingerprints and the need for lift dies with them.

Here you must see the pay split though shop talk merges it to keep its wage, for you know true pay when I name it though sweet claimed it for years.

Rest pays tired though sweet took the bow. Pause pays edgy though sweet took the bow. Sun pays flat though sweet took the bow. Laugh pays dull though sweet took the bow. Food pays hollow though sweet took the bow. Sweet stood near each true gift like a small loud guest and took the bow for the whole room. It brewed nothing. It borrowed. The body brewed. The meal fed. The pause rested. That round is the sugar trap. A guest taking bows for gifts he never brought while billing the house for flowers.

So when the old voice says the hit helps you cope, answer flat, for you know the script now. Tired asks for rest or air. Edgy asks for pause. Flat asks for light or company. Cake answers none though want swears it answers all.

The hero bills. The victim pays.

## WANTING WITHOUT LIKING

Now hold the split that ends the quarrel though your head fought it for years, for you know both sides in your own mouth though no talk joined them.

Wanting is not liking though shop talk merges them to keep its pay. You know the split though you never named it.

Liking is small and frail and honest. It sings at bite one and thins by bite three and dies by bite six though the bar never changed. It knows when enough stood up. It shuts. Wanting is big and loud and blind. It shouts at sight, at smell, at hour, at mood though the belly holds food. It knows no enough. It marches past joy to empty. You lived both in one packet though you called both love. Bite one liked and wanted. Bite six wanted though liking lay dead. That gap proves want never measured joy. Want measured wire.

Think of your own packet though you change nothing yet and you buy nothing new, for you know each bite though shame blurs the count.

Bite one: crack, flood, sing. Head lifts. Aye, there it is, you think. Bite two: sweet still, sing thinner. Bite three: paste starts, chew slows, joy thins though hand lifts. Bite four: teeth coat, thirst stirs, belly says full though eyes stay on screen. Bite five: no sing, only chew, only finish. Bite six to twelve: ache, paste, sour, still chewing though joy died four bites back while the head plans tomorrow's plain though the hand buys tomorrow's flood. If want were liking, liking would grow with bites. Liking dies while want grows. That cross proves they never were one though one name hid both.

I learned the split through finishing though I laugh sour to tell it. Family tin at Christmas. Biscuits bright under the tree. I ate one and liked it. I ate three and liked less. I ate nine standing by the sink though the sink held wash and my belly ached and my wife called from the room. No bite past three gave joy though each bite promised joy would return if I ate just one more. Joy never returned that night. Want returned each bite though joy lay dead. I washed crumbs from the board with a sour mouth and knew though I blinked for months. The mouth quit liking hours before the hand quit taking.

You say to me, but I love chocolate, I love cake, take all else but leave me this. I hear the ache under that line, for love frightens leavers more than hunger. Only ask, what loves. Does the mouth love bite six paste and morning sour and packet ache, or does the wire love its hourly ticket though the mouth pays. You know true love when I name it though sweet claimed the name for years. Love eats slow and thanks and leaves though food stays. Love shuts. Wire finishes though joy died and hides the trail. That finish never was love though it wore love dress. Love never hides wrappers.

Ask again, for your own best meals hold the proof though the old talk discounts them. When did plain ever drive you past ache to empty though the pan held more. Never. Roast eaten to shut leaves though meat stays. Soup eaten to shut leaves though bread stays. Apple eaten to shut leaves though bowl stays. Plain liking shuts because plain liking tells straight. Only midnight sweet finishes though ache says stop. That finish gives the game away though the tongue talks on.

See the engine behind the split though I keep it to one plain line and no hard words past your need, for you know enough to walk though doctors still argue the word for it.

Each flood spikes the want signal though the sing thins. Each spike teaches the wire to shout louder next hour though liking stays small. Want grows with feeding though liking dies with feeding. That is why old eaters want most though they like least. Years in, bite one sings less than new mouths sing, yet pull shouts louder than new mouths hear. More want. Less like. Same bar. That cross is the loop aging in your own blood though no talk marked it. You do not enjoy more with years. You obey more with less joy.

Whether men in coats call this habit or hunger or need or its harder name changes nothing on your floor, for you know the floor though names shift above it. Call it what they will after long debate, you binge, you dip, you want again. Names never altered wrappers hid for morning. Sight alters them.

So test the split on your own skin though you believe nothing yet on my word, for you know the test is honest though fear paints it hard.

Next sharp call, mark which side speaks though the old voice hates plain marks. Offer bread honest. Offer soup honest. Offer apple honest. Hunger says yes to bread. Want says no to all but one. Hunger sits low and wide and hollow and waits ten minutes while you finish the line. Want sits high and tight and edgy and nags and names the drawer and the shop and tomorrow. Hunger follows empty hours and work. Want follows a sweet flood and a dip though the belly holds food. Three tells never lie though sweet taught you to doubt your own belly more than any wrapper. Mark them and the coat slips.

The mouth tells straight. The wire shouts past it.

You fear life greys if want dies though I know that fear and I do not mock it. Only see what dies and what stays. Want dies. Liking stays. Shout dies. Whisper stays. Flood dies. Food stays. You lose nothing the mouth liked. You lose only the nag that ate past liking.

Liking ends. Want marches on.

## THE SMALL DIP AND THE LOUD WANT

Now shrink the fight you feared though you braced for war, for you know giants shrink once seen though fear painted this one tall.

You fear the crave proves deep need though the old talk arms that fear with hard words. You picture days of shake and nights of torment though the belly holds food. You picture a mighty foe that demands strain to beat though strain never beat it yet. I tell you flat as one who stood where you stand though I soften nothing true. The foe stands small. The talk stands tall.

Hear the plain chemistry though I dress it in no white coat and no numbers past what a man can hold, for you know dips though no talk measured them.

Each binge spikes the want signal anew though the sing thins bite by bite. Each spike spends. After the spend comes the dip. Mouth dry. Head thick. Mood thin. Hands edgy. Belly packed yet calling though food sits in it. That dip is true though old talk calls it need. It is not need for sweet the way lungs need air. It is payback after flood the way ears ring after shout. True and small. Small and brief. A hollow pang. A dry thought of sweet. Gone by the time the kettle boils if old belief stops feeding it loud.

Even the keenest labs that feed sweet by the hour to caged bellies call what they find mild though they hunted hard for strong, for you know strong would have shown if strong stood there. Mild but marked. A grip, not a chain. Enough to feel. Never enough to hold a man who sees. I speak blunt here because blunt frees you though I frighten none. If the physical stood tall, plain weeks would climb hard. Plain weeks climb low. Nights run plain. Mornings wake plainer. Tongue clears. Bell shouts at first then thins. That curve proves the body lets go fast though the talk holds on long.

You know this curve from your own short plain runs though you read them back to front, for you know days you ate plain though no book told you then.

Two plain days. Head aches faint. Mood thins. Sweet thoughts pace though the belly holds food. Third plain day lighter though the bell rings still. Week plain, fruit runs sweet, drawer rings soft, sleep sits deeper though nights felt long at first. You called that torment proving need. It proved the reverse though no one marked it. Torment thinned while unfed though true need grows while unfed. Hunger unfed grows. Thirst unfed grows. Air unfed kills. Sweet want unfed thins though it swears it grows. That thin proves want never lived in the blood the way food lives. It lived in wire fed by flood and talk. Pull the flood and the wire starves though it kicks at first like a small thing dying.

Ask one last time, and answer from your own floor though the old voice begs you to blink. If the dip proved need, why does plain bread never cure it though the belly takes food. Bread cures hunger. Bread never cures three o'clock sharp though the plate stands full. If the dip proved lack, why does more sweet deepen tomorrow's dip though tonight's fades. More food cures lack. More sweet feeds tomorrow's low though tonight lifts. That reverse proves the dip never was lack. It was payback. Payback ends when floods end though bells ring at first to keep their wage.

So see the fight for what it stands though fear armed you wrong, for you know small from large once I set them side by side.

Small part: brief dip after flood, dry mouth, thick head, edgy hour, gone fast unfed. Loud part: learned want shouting in your own voice, one more, just this once, start again tomorrow, you deserve, special day, drawer, smell, hour, picture, bell. Small part lives in the blood and dies unfed in hours. Loud part lives in talk installed by years of flood and shop smiles and family tins and breakroom tables. Small needs no battle. Loud needs sight. Eyes beat bells though hands never could.

I warn you plain as one who watched both for years. Do not mistake the loud three o'clock nag for hollow though it swears it starves. Hollow waits and takes bread. Sharp nags and takes one wrapper. Hollow sits low and wide. Sharp sits high and tight. Hollow follows empty hours. Sharp follows a flood. You will hear straight within days though years of flood dulled the ear. Ears clear fast once the flood stops shouting.

Hold these two in one hand while we walk on to blame the right foe elsewhere, for this hand must hold though later rooms shake. The body dips small and steadies. The mind wants loud and learns. Feed the small and you feed the loud though the mouth hopes to still it. See the loud and the small starves though it kicks at first. That is the loop: small dip lit by loud talk, loud talk paid by small dip, round and round though no joy rides it.

The dip is small. The talk is loud.

## SUMMARY

- You now see midnight finishing proves wanting outlives liking, for joy dies at bite three while the hand marches to empty though the belly aches.
- You now know each hit only pauses a low the last flood laid, with bells ringing before the bite and a small brief dip behind it though old talk called it need.
- You now believe crave shouts loudest where liking died fastest, so you mark the bell as wire not hunger though it wears your voice.
