## THE WEEKEND COUNT

You know this count though you never wrote it straight, for you live it each week though you call it sense.

Monday to Friday plain. No sweet in the mouth though bells ring. Oats plain. Tea plain. Bread plain though hands shake at three. You hold the line though the line cuts. You count good days like coins though the purse stays shut. Then Friday night comes with its warm glow and you tell yourself you earned a break though the mouth waters before the thought lands.

Just this weekend. Just Friday to Sunday. Just one a day though the day runs long. Just a few bites though bites never stayed few. Monday we start clean though clean never held past noon.

We all ran that count. I ran it for years.

See how the count reads though the old talk paints it fair, for you know sums when I lay them though sweet blurred them.

Five days starve though want lives. Two days flood though want swears this time will hold. Starve sharpens want though want hopes to still. Flood feeds want though want hopes to die. Monday head thin though belly holds food. Tuesday bells loud though drawer stands shut. Wednesday mouth dry though soup came hot. Thursday hands empty though work ran full. Friday bite meant as pay for five good days. That bite floods. Want spikes. Hand marches to empty though joy dies at bite three. Night ends past ache. Morning ends in shame and a new vow to hold harder though harder broke you last time.

You know this round though the old voice calls it treat. It never was treat. It was wind.

Ask straight, as a man asks about his own house though the old voice hates plain asks. If five plain days cured want, why did Friday bite always call the box. If weekend breaks freed you, why did Monday bells ring louder after each break though mouths hoped for soft. If a few bites stayed few, why do you hide wrappers by Sunday though you swore one though plain weeks with no count never drove you there.

You know the answer though it shames the old talk. The break made the binge. The count made the flood. The free days made the chained weeks.

Think of your own last free weekend though you change nothing yet and you buy nothing new. Friday one bar meant as pay. That bar wakes the old wire though wire slept through plain days. Saturday morning head thick, mouth calls for more though belly holds food. Saturday noon second bar to still the call though call grows. Saturday night box emptied past joy though ache says stop. Sunday sick, new rule writ for Monday though Monday starve sharpens next flood. That round never proved you lack grip. It proved free days wind the wire though free days swear to loose it.

I lived that count till my eyes opened. Friday tin opened though I called it joy. Sunday hid though I called it love.

We did that. We all did that and called it balance though balance never lived there.

See the count for what it holds though I blame you not one grain, for you know blame never freed a man.

It holds proof you can hold plain for days. It holds proof you want out enough to pay. It holds proof the flood comes not from weak blood but from loose breaks though breaks swear to save. Same mouth. Same bar. Break decides. Pull the break-flood round and the pull thins. Keep the round and the pull grows though the sweet stays same.

The weekend never saved the week. The weekend sank it.

You hand me your doubt though you speak it low. But I ate less in the week, the rule must help some. I hear the ache under that line, for good weeks feel true while they hold. Only ask, what paid for those good weeks. Did good weeks end in shut or did good weeks end in flood though the mouth hoped this time would hold. You know the bill though shame hides it.

So mark free days for what they stand though the old talk calls them mercy. Starve by week brews flood by weekend. Flood by weekend brews starve by week. Round and round though no joy rides it.

Free days chain. Plain sight frees.
