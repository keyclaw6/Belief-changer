Chapter 4
THE LIFT THAT BORROWS

IN THIS CHAPTER: the two o'clock desk, the breakroom table with its leftover cake, the coaster with no platform, the house that fills after one knock, the same hour weeks on.

*The lift you feel from sweet is not fuel put in, it is the last dip briefly covered while the next dip gets laid.*

## THE BREAKROOM AT TWO

You know this hour. It sits in the gut of the work day, half past one sliding toward three, when the noon meal lies behind you and the close of day still hides round the bend. You sit at your desk and you feel the light in your head go down a notch. Your eyes drag on the page. Your jaw sets. Your hand finds its way to your pocket or your drawer though you did not bid it move. You think you know what this hour means. You think it means you run on empty and need fuel, and you think you know where fuel lives.

I know that hour too. I lived it for years, and I read it wrong for years. I sat where you sit, with the same dull weight behind the eyes, and I told myself the same tale. Work took it out of me. My brain burned through its stock. A sweet would put back what work took out. That tale felt like plain horse sense. Every tired clerk round me told it. Every ad told it. My own mouth told it loudest of all. You believe it now because I believed it then, and we both learned it before we ever thought to test it.

Walk with me to the breakroom and see it as it is. The strip light hums. The kettle sulks in the corner with its white crust. The bin brims with torn sachets. And there on the table, on a chipped plate with a paper napkin stuck to its base, sits the leftover cake from someone's send-off. No one chose it fresh. No one hungered for it by name. It came in a box at eleven, got cut with a plastic knife, got praised out loud, and now it sits drying at the edges while folk pass and palm a slab on the way back to their chairs. You know the slab I mean. Sponge gone tacky. Icing gone crusty. Not loved. Not wanted. Taken.

Mark how you take it. You do not sit. You do not plate it with care the way you plate food you love. You take it standing, half turned toward the door, one eye on the clock, and you eat it in three fast bites while you think about the next sweet waiting at your desk. You tell yourself you take it for fuel, yet you do not taste it the way a man tastes fuel he trusts. You chew fast, you swill tea after it to shift the clag, and your mind runs ahead to the drawer, the machine, the shop round the bend. That rush gives the game away, and you can see it once you look straight. Hunger does not act like that. Hunger asks for food, takes food, and shuts. This asks, takes, and asks more.

See too who you blame and who you spare. You never blame the cake. You blame your weak self for wanting it, or your hard day for forcing it on you. I did the same, and I tell you flat we both blamed the wrong thing. The table laid the snare, not your heart. Some kind hand left the plate where tired eyes must pass. The smell hung in the small room with no window. The talk round it said take, all take, why not you. The hour drooped as it always droops after sweet mornings. You walked in hungry for air and pause, and the trap taught you to read that honest droop as need for a fix. That misread is the whole catch at this hour, and once you see it you cannot unsee it.

You say to me, but I needed it, I could not think till I ate. Hold that line up with me, for I do not brush off what you feel. I felt it too. The fog at two is real. The drag is real. The snappish turn when the phone rings twice is real. I do not deny your afternoon. I deny what the trap told you it means. The droop does not prove you run on empty. It proves the last dose wore off, and you can test this on your own day the way I tested it on mine.

Think of your morning with a cold eye. Toast with sweet spread, cereal that crunches sweet, tea with two spoons, juice sold as health though it hits like syrup, a biscuit with the eleven o'clock cup. You think you ate for strength, yet by one your head already thins. By two you droop. By half past you hunt. If sweet truly fuelled a man, why does the best-fuelled morning breed the worst droop. Lorry fuel does not work like that. Coal does not work like that. Bread and eggs and fish do not work like that. Only the fix works like that, bright for an hour then gone with interest, and you know it because you live it each day.

That is the sugar trap at its slyest in daylight. Not the night binge you hide. Not the feast you fear to refuse. The plain work hour dressed up as need, where a tired clerk eats standing what no one chose, thinking of the next dose while he chews, and calls it fuel. That is the sugar trap.

## WHAT THE LIFT IS MADE OF

Let me show you the shape of the lift itself, for you have felt it a thousand times yet never held it still long enough to see it. You take the slab at two. For ten minutes the fog lifts a notch. Your shoulders drop. The page comes back into focus. You think, there, that helped, I was right, sweet gives me a lift. You believe the lift proves the fuel tale true, and I understand why, for I read it the same way for years.

Now watch the next hour with the same eyes and you see what I saw. By three the lift thins. By half past three your head feels thicker than before you ate. Your eyes sting. Your temper shortens. Your hand starts its old stroll toward drawer or pocket. You tell yourself the work got harder, the room got hotter, age creeps on, sleep last night fell short. You lay the blame on all things but the one thing that changed, which is the dose you took at two. That blind spot is not stupidity. That blind spot is brainwashing, laid drip by drip till it sounds like your own thought.

"I need it to get through the afternoon." Say it out loud and hear how firm it sounds. "The sugar I need to see me through my daily slump." Firm too. "I will crave sugar all day." Firm as stone. Each line feels like a report from your own flesh. Yet set them beside your own week and watch them wobble. On days you take more sweet, do you crave less or more. You know the answer though you flinch to say it. More sweet, more crave. Less sweet mornings, less three o'clock hunt. If sweet filled the tank, more would mean less want. With petrol that holds true. With food that holds true. With BAD SUGAR the reverse holds true, and the reverse proves the tale false.

"I need something sweet." "To get me through." "I need a pick-me-up." You have said all three, I said all three, clerks in every town say all three before the same chipped plate. Mark what the lines share. Each one points at an hour, not at a food. Through the afternoon. Through the slump. Up from the dip. None of them praise taste. None of them name hunger. Each confesses the hour droops first and the sweet comes second. That order damns the fuel tale, and you can see it if you hold the lines still. Fuel does not wait for a dip to prove itself. Fuel feeds whether you droop or not. This so-called fuel only shines against the dim it just laid.

Here is the plain shape, said short the way true things say themselves. The sweet gives a short loud lift which dies fast and leaves you lower than it found you. You know this shape in your own flesh. Quick up, fast down, hand out for more. That is not my phrase laid on you. That is your afternoon speaking, the same afternoon you have lived a thousand times. Quick up. Fast down. Hand out. Once you see that three-step you see why the day runs the way it runs. The lift is real the way a splash is real. The thirst it leaves is realer.

You ask me, but why does the down come so sure. Hear it in plain words with no white-coat talk, for you need sight not jargon. Sweet loads hit fast and loud. Your frame answers fast to meet the flood, it overanswers the way a man hauls too hard on a rope, and a couple of hours on you slide into the dip. The dip feels like empty, like fog, like cross talk in the head. That dip is not low blood the way a doctor means low blood, it is the swing after the surge, the payback built into the hit. You do not need my word for this. You have timed it yourself without knowing you timed it. Sweet at ten, droop at twelve. Sweet at two, droop at four. Sweet at nine at night, grey wake at dawn. Same gap. Same payback. Same teacher.

"It helps me concentrate." Hold that one up next. When does your mind sharpen after sweet. For ten minutes, while the flash lasts and the crave shuts for a breath. What dulls it again. The fade of that same flash. So what did the sweet teach your mind across a month. That thought comes with a wrapper. That focus must be bought per hour. That a dull head means eat, never pause, drink water, stand, breathe, eat a proper meal. You think sweet lends you mind, yet month on month your mind at three grows duller, not brighter, though the dose grows. A lender who leaves you poorer each visit is no lender. He is a loan shark in a kind mask, and you know his walk.

"Fresh energy for my body." Same test. Does your body feel fresh an hour after the bar, or does it feel heavy, thirsty, thick-tongued, keen for a nap it cannot take at a desk. You know the thick tongue. You know the thirst sweet leaves though it posed as lift. You know the yawns that come crowding at four though you fed the yawns at two. Fresh things do not breed stale hours. Food that feeds does not ask to be fed again within the hour. Only the loop eats like that, loud at first lick then dull, then hungry for itself.

You called it a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift.

## THE COASTER WITH NO PLATFORM

You call these ups and downs your "roller coaster" and your "sugar crash," and I keep your words, for they paint true. Only see the whole ride, not the first climb. A fair ride lifts you high, thrills you through, and sets you down where it found you, laughing, legs shaky, keen to ride again. You queue again because the ride gave more than it took. That is a ride worth the coin.

Now picture a coaster with no platform to step off. You get hauled up fast with a lurch that feels like thrill. Then you get dropped fast with your gut in your throat. Then hauled again before you catch breath. Then dropped again. The first haul felt grand against the grey queue. By the sixth haul you feel sick. By the tenth you would pay to get off, only there is no platform, there is just the next haul sold as the cure for the last drop. That is your afternoon on sweet, and you know it once the picture stands still.

Fun belongs to those who can get off. The rider who cannot get off is just thrown about. Read that twice and set it beside your day. The clerk who takes one square at Christmas and leaves it at that rides once and steps off laughing. You and I never rode once. We rode round and round, haul and drop, haul and drop, from first sweet tea at dawn to last spoon at night, calling each haul fuel and each drop need. The ride did not carry us to work. The ride kept us from work while we paid per turn.

Ask the two asks that split this open, and answer straight for you have the proof in your own diary. First, when do you feel the lift most. Is it not when you feel worst, an hour or two after the last dose, when head and temper droop lowest. Second, who feels no need of this lift at all. Is it not the man who took no sweet for weeks and eats plain food with appetite, whose three o'clock passes like two and four, no haul, no drop, no hunt. If the lift were fuel, the unfuelled man would droop worst. He droops least. That single fact kills the fuel tale stone dead, and no talk can raise it.

Think of lads at play before they learn sweet. They run all afternoon on bread and meat and fruit and water, they think hard at school, they laugh loud, they sleep deep. No one tells them they lack fuel. No one hands them a bar to finish sums. Their lift comes from sleep, food, air, movement, rest. Then we teach them the wrapper tale and by twenty they swear they cannot think past three without a fix. Did their frames change, or did the talk change. You know it was the talk. Brains do not forget how to think at three. They learn to ask for a ticket to ride.

I speak blunt here because blunt serves you. Sweet does not top you up. It drops you then rents you the ladder out. Up a notch, down two. Up a notch, down two. Each rent feels like help. Each help digs the pit the next rent fills. That is the borrow in the title I gave this turn. A borrow you repay with interest out of your own head. And the lender smiles each time like a friend.

It borrows. It pays back less.

## ONE INVITES THE HOUSE

Now see why one at two never stays one, for this part of the trap bites hardest at work where sweet sits out and none counts it. You take the slab standing and you tell yourself that ends it. An hour on you take a biscuit with tea. An hour on you take two squares from the drawer. At half past four you buy a bottle to see you to the train. At night you take more because the day took much. Each take felt single at the time. Together they make a stack you never meant to lift.

Picture a lone guest at your door at dusk. He knocks soft. He looks small. He says he only wants a chair for ten minutes. You think, one small guest, what harm. You let him in. Before your kettle boils he has texted his mates. By eight the hall fills. By nine the couches sag with loud guests who eat your food, drink your drink, and will not leave though you yawn in their faces. You blame your weak door. I tell you the fault lay in the first knock, for that guest never comes alone. Sweet comes the same way. One small carb at two texts the whole house by four.

You know this pull in your own day though you never named it. Sweet begets sweet. The noon sandwich with sweet drink leaves you hungrier at two than plain food ever did. The two o'clock slab leaves you hungrier at four. The four o'clock bottle leaves you ravenous at six though your belly holds plenty. Second meals run bigger after sweet first meals. Night runs hungriest after sweet days. That is not weak will. That is the loop feeding itself, dose waking want, want waking talk, talk waking leave, leave waking dose. Round and round till shoes wear thin.

Mark the grazing this breeds, for grazing without hunger is the fingerprint of the trap at work. True hunger growls low, asks for a meal, takes it with thanks, and shuts for hours. You know that shut gate well from days built on plain food. Grazing never shuts. It picks, it nibbles, it sips sweet through the hour, it thinks of the next bite while it chews this one. That thinking-of-next-while-chewing gives it away. Hungry men think of the bite in the mouth. Trapped men think of the bite to come. You stood in the breakroom chewing slab one while planning drawer two. I stood there too. We all stood there. That forward lean is not appetite. That forward lean is the house filling after one knock.

Set this beside the fuel tale and watch the tale starve. Fuel satisfies. Petrol fills the tank and the hunt ends. Bread fills the belly and the mind turns to work. Sweet at two breeds hunt at three. Sweet at three breeds hunt at four. What sort of fuel makes a man hungrier. What sort of strength makes a man weaker each hour he takes it. You know no such fuel exists outside the trap. Food ends hunger. Only the fix extends it.

"I'm too tired to function without it." I hear the ache under that line, for tired is real and work is long and sleep falls short with kids and bills and noise. I do not mock tired. I was tired too. Only ask with me, does the slab cut tired at its root or paint it for ten minutes. Tired at root asks for rest, sleep, food, air, pause. The slab gives none of these. It gives flash while tired waits underneath, then adds a drop to tired so the night feels heavier. Month on month the tired grows though the dose grows. If the cure worked, the sick would mend. The sick worsen. That worsening proves the cure false.

See how the house fills in talk as well as in bites. One dose needs a tale to excuse it. That tale needs a second dose to prove it. That dose needs a fresh rule to wipe it. I start fresh Monday. Just this once today. All take it so why not I. Each line sounds firm alone. Together they keep the door ajar for the next knock. You do not need grit to shut that door. You need sight to see no lone guest ever knocked. Each knock brings the house.

One lets in all.

## FOCUS, TIRES AND THE SHAKY HOUR

We must meet the hardest lines head on, for they sound like flesh itself and they hold many good folk in the trap years past sense. "I'm too tired to function without it." "Shaky and light-headed unless I eat." "I will crave sugar all day." I take each in both hands. I press no shame on you for saying them. I said them myself when the trap spoke through my mouth.

Take tired first, for tired rules the work day. You wake grey after short sleep. The dawn meal runs sweet. By ten your head thins. By two your lids weigh more than your pay slip. You read this droop as proof your frame makes no strength of its own. I read it so too, till I learned how strength truly comes. Your frame makes its own steady supply all day from plain food and its own stores, drip fed, no lurch, no bell. It does not need spoon-by-spoon top-ups to think or stand. Babes think and run without bars. Old folk who eat plain think straight to dusk. Your thinker did not break. Your fuel gauge got rigged by years of flood and overanswer, so the needle swings though the tank stands full.

Test it on your own week. Which days bring the worst two o'clock. Is it not the days that start sweetest, juice and frosted crunch and spooned tea, days you thought best fuelled. Which days pass mildest. Is it not the days built on eggs, fish, meat, bread, soup, greens, plain food eaten to full, days the trap calls dull. If tired came from lack of sweet, sweet mornings would fly. They crawl. If strength came from plain food, plain mornings would drag. They hold. You have run this test a hundred times without marking the result. Mark it now and let it stand.

Take focus next. "It helps me concentrate." When did sweet first help you think. Was it before school taught you the wrapper, or after years of fixes trained your head to ask per hour. Did farm lads of old need a bar to learn sums. Did your gran need a bottle to sew straight seams to dusk. Thought runs on rest, air, pause, water, food. Sweet adds flash while thought was dimming from the last flash, then dims thought again. That is not help. That is rent. True help leaves the helped stronger after. This leaves thought duller after, hungrier after, less able after. Call things by their right names and the help tale dies.

Now the shaky hour, and here I speak most careful, for shakes frighten and fright must never drive you. Some afternoons your hands tremble a touch. Your head feels light when you stand fast. Your temper frays and your gut gnaws though lunch lies short behind. The trap shouts at once, you see, you need sweet, your blood sinks, eat now. I know that shout. I obeyed it for years. Hear me flat as one who lived it. That sway an hour or two after sweet is the swing after the surge I spoke of, your frame overanswering the flood then righting itself. It passes as your frame rights itself. It is not proof you run on empty. It is proof the flood came too fast and loud.

Do not read this as scorn for your shakes. Do not read it as leave to fear your frame. Your frame rights itself a thousand times a day without your hand on the wheel. The sway feels bad, I grant it, yet it is small beside the tale the trap builds on it. The trap turns an hour of sway into a life sentence of hourly fixes. That trade is false. An hour of sway after flood does not mean a life of need. It means floods bring sways, so stop the floods and the sways lose their maker. Plain food brings no such flood. Plain days bring no such sway. You know this from your own mild days.

"I will crave sugar all day." Of course you crave all day while you feed all day. Crave is not a hole in your frame. Crave is the echo of the last hit plus the voice that reads echo as need. Feed hourly and echo never dies. Let me tell you what the first days without flood truly hold, small and plain, for the trap paints them huge to keep you riding. Head may ache a day or two. Temper may fray. The hand may stroll to the drawer out of wont. The mind may pipe for its hourly ticket. That hump is days long, not life long, and it passes while you eat plain and drink water and sleep. I passed it. Folk in every town pass it. It is a hill, not a wall. Do not change from fear of it. The method takes care of it while you read on and eat as before.

That strain-and-count grind you blame for past fails is the Willpower Method.

## THE SAME HOUR WEEKS ON

Now replay the hour with new eyes, for here the whole turn proves itself in one room. Same desk. Same strip light. Same two o'clock slant through the blinds. Same chipped plate in the breakroom down the hall, for the office will not change its ways though you change yours. Only one thing stands different, and that thing is sight.

You eat a plain noon meal to full, bread, soup, eggs, fish, meat, greens, fruit with its own true taste, water cold from the tap. No flood. No flash. No vow tied to it. You work through one. At two the light still dips a notch, for work tires honest heads and honest tired asks for pause. Only mark the kind of tired. It sits light, not thick. It asks for stand and stretch and air at the window, not for a fix. You stand. You roll your shoulders. You drink water. You look out at roofs and sky for a minute. The minute answers. Thought returns without ticket. You know this return well from days before sweet taught its hourly rent.

Half past two passes and the drawer never opens in your head. No forward lean to the next bite. No chew-and-plan. The hand rests. The gut stays shut the way a fed gut shuts. Three comes with its small tasks and goes. Four comes and finds you still at work, head thinner than dawn yet still yours, temper even, mouth plain, no clag, no thirst that water will not meet. You think of the plate down the hall and you see it as it is, dry sponge, crust icing, bait for droops it helped lay. Pity brushes you for the queue at the table, good folk reading droop as need the way you once did. No scorn in it. You stood there too.

You may ask, is this a tale I spin to please you. It is not. It is the shape plain days take once floods stop, lived by folk in every trade. The hour that once owned you becomes an hour like other hours. Work still tires. Life still frets. Sleep still falls short some nights. Only the hourly hunt drops out, and with it the haul-and-drop that posed as fuel. What stays is yours by right, thought that holds to four, a gut that asks for meals not tickets, an afternoon that runs on one even keel. Mark that even keel well. That is your frame as built, before talk rigged the gauge.

See what this proves about the lift you chased. The lift never carried you. Pause carried you. Air carried you. Water carried you. Food carried you. Rest carried you. The sweet rode each of these like a thief on a cart and claimed the ride as its gift. Peel the hour off the hit and naught stays in the palm but flash then flat. That is why the same hour passes well without the fix. The fix never drove the hour. The hour drove itself while the fix took the praise.

So set the two afternoons side by side and judge like a grown man judging a tool. Afternoon with fix, bright ten minutes, thick head by three, hunt by four, stack by night, grey wake. Afternoon plain, even keel, pause answering tired, hand at rest, mouth plain, night still, dawn fair. Which one fuelled. Which one drained. You know though the trap begs you to blink. The tool that leaves the work weaker each hour is no tool. The food that leaves hunger louder each hour is no food.

The lift borrows. The plain hour owns.

## SUMMARY

- You now see the afternoon lift was never fuel put in but the last dip briefly covered while the next dip got laid.
- You now know one sweet at two wakes the whole house by four because sweet begets sweet and grazing without hunger marks the loop.
- You now believe plain food and pause carry thought to dusk better than any fix because your frame makes its own supply without floods.
