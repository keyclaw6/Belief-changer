Chapter 6
EATING

IN THIS CHAPTER: the cold bedroom at half six, the thick head and shut mouth, the plain table at noon, the hollow belly and the sharp call, the apple against the carton, the filter lifting.

*Hunger tells straight and plain food fills you once sweet stops shouting over both.*

## THE MORNING AFTER

You know this waking. I know it too. We all lived it and called it normal.

The alarm drags you up at half six in a cold room. Your mouth feels dry and pasted. Your tongue sits thick. Your eyes open with crust at the corners and you rub them clean with a thumb. Your head feels foggy and irritable and slow to start. Your belly feels blown and heavy though you ate hours back. You swing your feet to the floor and you feel older than your years for ten minutes. You tell yourself you slept wrong. You tell yourself you need something sweet to get moving.

Mark that hour with a cold eye, for you know it from your own bed though you never joined the parts.

Last night you ate well at tea. Meat and bread and veg and enough. Then the packet came out in front of the box. Biscuits first, then chocolate, then a bowl of ice cream to close though you felt full at the second biscuit. First two bites sang. The rest pasted and clagged. You finished past want because the hand kept going while the eyes watched the screen. You woke in the night thirsty with a sour mouth and drank water standing in the dark. You told yourself it was nothing. Morning tells you it was something.

Ask straight, as a man asks about his own house. If those night bites fed you, why do you wake emptier than men who ate plain and shut. If those night bites rested you, why do you wake heavier and foggier than nights you ate and left it. You know the answer though the old voice begs you to blink. Night sweet did not feed the day. It borrowed from the morning.

I lived those mornings for years and I read them wrong the way you read them wrong. I blamed the mattress. I blamed the weather. I blamed work and age and the kids crying in the night. I never blamed the packet by the chair though the packet sat there empty each dawn accusing me. We all do that. We blame life for a pinch the loop laid in the night.

See the pinch work in plain hours. You eat the evening dose. It floods the mouth and lifts the head a notch for twenty minutes. Then the lift thins. Thirst wakes. Mouth turns. Belly blows. Sleep sits light and broken. You wake dry and thick and edgy. You call that waking tiredness and you answer it with a sweet tea and a biscuit to get moving. That morning fix lifts the haze a notch for an hour and you say, there, I needed that, sweet steadies me. I said it too. We both read it back to front.

The fix did not steady a straight hunger. It lifted a haze the last fix laid. Pinch, lift, pinch, lift, through night into day. That round keeps the mouth primed and the head humming low and calls itself appetite. You know it is not appetite because true appetite sleeps through night and wakes clean. This wakes dirty.

Now stand with me a few weeks ahead, for I want you to feel what plain nights pay though I keep the picture low on purpose.

You eat at tea and you shut. No packet by the chair. No bar in the drawer. You drink water. You sleep. You wake at half six in the same cold room to the same alarm. Your mouth feels plain. Your eyes open without paste. Your head feels quieter behind the eyes. Your belly feels flat and hollow in an honest way that asks for breakfast. You sit up and you feel your years, not your grandfather's. You walk downstairs and the kettle boils and bread toasts and you taste it. That is a clear morning with eyes that open clean and a mouth ready for food, and you know the difference the first time it comes because no talk can fake it.

I do not paint heaven here. I paint Tuesday. Cold lino. Kettle steam. Toast brown at the edges. You eat and you go to work. Nothing shines. Nothing sings. Only the thick head stays away and the sour mouth stays away and the blown belly stays away. You know those three absences at once the way a man knows noise stopping. The night stopped charging the morning.

So mark the morning for what it proves though we leave deeper workings for later. The heavy foggy waking was never proof you need sweet to start. It was proof sweet had taken the start. The pillowy lure that calls mid-afternoon for something soft and sweet thins once nights run plain, and you will see it thin in your own week though I make no timetable and no promise past your own eyes. Plain nights, plain mornings. That order holds.

The bed told on the packet. The packet paid for no sleep.

## THE PLAIN MEAL

Come with me to noon, for here your fear sits hardest though you speak it soft.

You fear plain eating will punish you. No shine. No lift. Plate after plate of dull chew while other folk eat bright food and laugh. Belly never shutting. Mind forever drifting to the drawer. A grey life measured in mouthfuls. I know that fear because I carried it for years. Generous eaters carry it most, for we love food and we think love of food means love of sweet. Hear me as one who stood where you stand. You love food. Sweet taught you to misname what food means.

Set a plain table with me and eat without hurry, for you know this table though the old talk paints it poor.

Midday. Kitchen warm. Plate full. You know this fullness though old talk calls it poor. I name no grams and no rules and I count nothing. A proper plate of the real food you love. Say roast meat and potatoes browned in fat and greens with butter. Say thick soup with bread torn and cheese that smells of the farm. Choose what your house loves when no one watches the clock. Sit down. No screen. Sit like a man meant to eat.

First mouthfuls talk loud. Salt. Fat. Heat. Chew. You taste more than you expect because no loud hit shouted first, and you see at once the mouth was never dead. Second mouthfuls settle. Belly warms. Shoulders drop a notch. Talk, if folk sit with you, runs easy. If you sit alone, your head runs easy. You chew and you swallow and you breathe between bites without thinking it. Half the plate gone and the edge leaves hunger. Three parts gone and fullness stands up plain and says enough. You push the plate an inch. You sit. You feel shut.

Know that shut feeling, for it is the feeling sweet stole and sold back.

True fullness shuts without noise. No pull for more though more sits in the pan. No eye on the drawer though the drawer holds bars. No jaw ache though you ate well. Mouth clean. Belly warm and still. Head steady. You could rise and work. You could rise and walk. You could sit a while longer with tea because the hour feels kind. That still hour is appetite doing its old job though you had not trusted it for years. You thought fullness came from sweet weight. It comes from food eaten and noticed to its end.

I ate that meal for the first time after years of grazing and I nearly laughed at the table. I waited for the call to come. It did not come. I waited for the hollow to stay though the plate stood empty. It did not stay. My belly had shut the way a door shuts, firm and without slam. I drank tea. I washed the pan. I went back to work and forgot food for three hours. Forgetting food shocked me most, for I had thought of food each hour for a decade. You will know that forgetting too, not as a triumph but as a quiet absence, food leaving the head because the belly spoke plain.

Ask with me, and answer from your own noon hours. When did a sweet lunch ever shut like that. A bun and a cola at the desk eaten while mail piled. Sweet floods. Head lifts. Belly feels packed yet hollow an hour later. Hand back in the drawer by two. Cold pizza left from night eaten standing though the cheese felt tired. No shut. Only pause. If BAD SUGAR fed, packed would mean shut. With true food packed means shut. With BAD SUGAR packed means soon again. That soon again gives the game away though the tongue talks on.

You say to me, but I like sweet taste, plain food bores me. I hear the ache under that line, for boredom frightens eaters more than hunger. Only ask, what bores. Is it the roast that bores, with its brown edges and running juices, or is it a mouth still ringing from last night's flood that finds roast faint. You know a trumpet deafens the ear for talk after. Sweet trumpets the mouth for food after. Plain tastes faint only while the ring lasts. Let the ring die and plain speaks loud.

Think of your own hungriest best meals in memory though sweet sat nowhere near. After a long walk on cold air when bread and soup nearly burned the tongue and you ate too fast and laughed at yourself. After work that ran late when eggs and toast at nine at night tasted better than any feast. After illness when plain mash stayed down and you nearly wept at kindness of it. No sweet touched those hours yet joy stood in them. That joy proves taste never lived in sweet alone. Hunger seasoned plain and plain answered. You know those meals though you discount them. Count them now.

The mouth was never broken. It was shouted down.

Mark too how plain eating pays beyond the plate in ways you can check by Thursday though I claim no marvels. Afternoon runs steadier without the hourly dip and lift. Hands shake less over keys. Temper holds longer with kids. Tea tastes of tea. You do not float. You do not glow. You work and you eat and you work again. That even run is appetite trusted, and trust grows each time you eat and shut without a chaser. One shut meal teaches more than ten talks, for the belly learns faster than the ear.

This is the room I wanted you to stand in before we name deeper parts. Not a lecture on hunger. A meal eaten to its end. Sit. Chew. Notice the door shut. That shut is yours by build though the loop taught you to doubt it. You ate. You filled. You stopped with thanks though no one taught you how this time. The body knew though the talk forgot.

Food fills. Sweet interrupts.

## HOLLOW OR HAUNTED

Now learn to tell two calls apart, for here most good folk get fooled though their own belly tells straight.

Two pulls wear one name in our talk. Both say eat. Only one means eat. You know both though you call both hunger. I did too. We all paid the wrong one for years.

Catch yourself tomorrow at half eleven though you change nothing yet and you buy nothing new. Morning plain behind you. Work half done. Your belly speaks. Mark where it speaks and how. Low and hollow under the ribs. Wide and empty, not sharp. A slow gnaw that comes in waves and waits. Water does not scare it. Time does not spike it. Thought of plain food answers it. Bread sounds good. Soup sounds good. Apple sounds good. Cheese sounds good. The call points at food, not at one wrapper. That is hunger. You know it from childhood though sweet buried it. It asks and it can wait ten minutes while you finish the line you write.

Now catch yourself at half three after a sweet noon though the plate stood small and the drawer stood near. Tired head. Dry mouth. Edgy nerves. A sharp narrow call high in the chest and throat, not low in the belly. Restless hands. Eyes on the clock. Thought of plain food answers nothing. Bread sounds dull. Soup sounds too much. Apple sounds like a joke. Only one shape answers, soft and sweet and quick, biscuit, bar, cola, cake. The call points at one wrapper, not at food. It cannot wait ten minutes without growing loud. That is not hunger though it wears hunger's coat. That is the old want calling for its hourly ticket.

Ask the asks that split them though the old voice hates plain asks. First, does plain food answer it. Hunger says yes to bread. Want says no to all but one. Second, where does it sit. Hunger sits low and wide and hollow. Want sits high and tight and edgy. Third, what did you eat two hours back. Hunger follows empty hours and work. Want follows a sweet flood and a dip though the belly holds food. You know these three tells from your own afternoons though no one taught you to mark them. Mark them now and the coat slips.

I sat this sitting myself on a wet Tuesday though I thought I knew hunger cold. Half eleven hollow, bread welcome. Half three sharp, bread dull, bar loud. Same man. Same day. Same mouth. Two different calls. I drank water and waited ten minutes and watched. The eleven call stayed honest and waited with me. The three call paced and nagged and bargained and named the drawer and the shop and tomorrow. Hunger waits. Want nags. That difference taught me more than all my years of labels. Bodies wait. Loops nag.

See too how want dresses as body talk to keep its pay though you can see through the dress once you look straight. My body was saying eat cake. The dark cookies call me. I need something sweet. You said all three. I said all three. Each line points at a feeling, not at an empty belly. Tired. Edgy. Flat. Bored. Cross. Each true feeling asks for its true answer. Tired asks for rest or air. Edgy asks for pause. Flat asks for light or company. Bored asks for work worth doing. Cake answers none though want swears it answers all. A call that claims to cure all hours cures none. Hunger cures hunger. Want feeds want.

Test it on your own skin though you believe nothing yet on my word. Next sharp call, eat plain if hunger also stirs, bread or soup or what your house loves, eat to shut and mark what happens to the sharp call. It thins for twenty minutes under fullness then pokes back though the belly holds food. True hunger never pokes back through fullness. Full shuts hunger. Full never shuts want. That poke-back proves the call never lived in the belly. It lived in the learned pull that fires when the last flood thins.

Think of night hours to see the split work in the dark though we keep this glimpse short for its full room waits elsewhere. Fridge light. Cold floor on feet. Wrapper hid for morning. Eaten past joy to finish the packet though the belly ached at bite six. Hunger never eats past ache to finish a packet. Hunger shuts and leaves though food stays. Want finishes though joy died at bite three and plans the next while it chews. You stood there. I stood there. That forward lean while chewing is not appetite. Appetite stops and thanks. Only the loop chews ahead.

You fear you cannot trust yourself to tell them apart in a loud hour. I tell you flat you can though you learned not to. The low hollow never lies though it growls. The high sharp never tells straight though it swears. Water, ten minutes, plain food offered honest. Hunger says yes and waits. Want says no to all but one and nags. Three tells. One belly. You will hear straight within days though years of flood dulled the ear. Ears clear fast once the flood stops shouting.

The belly asks. The loop orders.

## THE HAZE LIFTS

Now I show you why plain tasted faint though you swore your tongue told straight.

Think of a window with a thin film smeared across it though you never smeared it on purpose. Light comes through but dulled. Green looks grey-green. Red looks brown-red. You wipe one corner clean with a thumb and that corner shocks you. Bright grass. Bright paint. You see at once the glass lied, not the garden. You thought the garden dimmed. The film dimmed the glass.

That filmed glass is your mouth after years of BAD SUGAR though I speak plain and claim no marvel beyond your own tongue. Each flood lays its film. Sweet on sweet, day on day, the film thickens though you brush twice and chew gum and drink water. Real tastes arrive through film and read faint. Bread reads dry. Meat reads flat. Apple reads sour. You blame the food and you blame age and you blame your cook. I blamed all three. We all blamed all but the film.

Ask straight, for your own meals hold the proof though the old talk buries it. Why does the first bite of a bar sing while the sixth bite clags though the bar never changed. Same bar. Same mouth. Song then paste. If taste lived in the bar, song would hold. Song dies because the film thickens bite by bite while want marches on. Why does water taste sweet after weeks plain though water never changed. Same tap. Same mouth. Flat then sweet. If plain meant dull by build, water would stay dull. Water sweetens because the film thins. You know both turns though you never joined them. Join them now.

I learned the film through tea though I laugh to tell it. Strong tea with two sugars for twenty years, stirred till the spoon stood. One plain week, tea tasted like dishwater and I nearly spat. Second plain week, tea tasted round and dark and kind and I drank it slow. No leaf changed. No hand changed. Only the film thinned. Then an apple surprised me more though I thought apples poor man's food. Cold from the shelf. Skin tight. First bite cracked loud. Juice ran sharp and sweet though no sugar sat in it. I ate to the core and licked my thumb though no one watched. That hour taught me my tongue was never broken. It was filmed.

That is the haze lifting though I keep the picture to your own mouth and claim nothing past it. Sweet shouts and plain whispers while the shout lasts. Let the shout stop and the whisper carries the room. Fruit tastes sweeter on a mouth left plain for days, not as a phrase but as a crack and run and sharp juice you can check at your own table, and you know the check is honest because no talk can fake juice running down a chin.

Here you must see a line the old talk blurs though plain eyes split it clean.

Fruit and juice wear one name in shop talk and they are not one thing though both taste sweet. Hold an orange in one hand and a carton in the other and mark what each does in your own belly though I lay down no diet and no rule. The orange asks to be peeled. It takes time. It spits pips. It chews. Fibre holds the flood. Belly warms and shuts for an hour. Hunger answers and leaves. The carton asks nothing. Twist, pour, swallow. No chew. No pause. Flood runs straight through though the label shows fruit on its face. Belly feels packed yet calls again by the hour. Same word on the front. Opposite work inside.

Know that split cold, for it frees your table without chaining it. Whole fruit stands with plain food though sweet talk tried to draft it. It chews. It fills. It shuts. Juice, though squeezed from fruit, works as a sweet drink in the mouth and the belly though the carton smiles. Fizzy drinks and sweetened cartons and honey swirls in tea all ride the same flood though names change. Swaps that keep the flood keep the film though labels promise light. A sweet mouth stays a filmed mouth whatever the bottle calls itself. I name no poison and I frighten none. I mark work. Chew shuts. Flood calls again. That work you can test by noon.

You ask me, does this mean all sweet taste leaves life. I tell you flat it means the reverse though the old voice paints grey. Life keeps its sweet though BAD SUGAR leaves it. Berries cold from the punnet. Pears that drip down the wrist. Baked apple with skin crisped. Those sweets arrive with chew and pause and shut though no wrapper calls. Your own mouth will relearn them once film thins though you doubt it today. Taste never left. It waited under film.

Think of music heard through a bad speaker for years though you blamed the band. Bass booms. Words drown. Then you hear the same song played plain in a quiet room and you hear strings you never knew stood there. You do not blame the song for past dullness. You blame the speaker. Blame your speaker here with the same fairness. Bread, broth, fruit, meat, greens, these played true all while you heard them dull through flood. Wipe the glass. The garden never dimmed.

The film thins. The garden never moved.

## THE BODY KEEPS ITS OWN SHOP

Now hear the plain fact that steadies all you felt though I dress it in no white coat and no numbers beyond what a man can hold.

Your body keeps its own shop for the fuel it burns though shop talk tells you to spoon it in. Day and night, fed or fasting, it brews what the blood needs from plain food and from its own stores and holds the line steady without your hand on the tap. You never watched it brew. You never ordered the brew. It brewed while you slept and while you worked and while you forgot food for hours on a walk. That steady brewing is why men wake and work and think before breakfast though no sweet touched the tongue. That steadiness is build, not luck.

I speak blunt here because blunt serves you though I claim no cure and no plan. You were told sweet fuels work the way coal fuels fire, spoon more, burn brighter. Your own afternoons prove the tale false though you still speak it. Spoon more at two and you burn duller at three, head thick, eyes heavy, hands slow, want loud again by four. Eat plain at one and you burn even to five though no flood arrived. If spooned sweet fuelled, more spoon would mean more fire. With true food that holds. With BAD SUGAR the reverse holds. Flood borrows from later to flash now. That borrow names the whole fuel tale false.

Ask with me, for three asks kill this tale though it wears a white coat in shop talk. First, do babes need bars to grow though they double weight on milk and plain mash. They do not. They grow fast on plain though no wrapper feeds them. Second, do men on long walks fail for want of bars though they carry bread and cheese and water and walk all day. They do not. Legs hold though pockets hold no sweet. Third, did your own steadiest weeks run on most sweet or least. Least, always least, though you swore the reverse while the flood rang. Full belly, plain food, even head. That curve proves fuel never lived in the flood. It lived in food and in the shop that brews though you never thanked it.

See how the shop talk conned us though I blame no eater and I scorn no mother. Sweet arrived always with true pay in its hands, rest, praise, pause, sun, laugh, and claimed it brewed the pay. It brewed nothing. It borrowed. The body brewed. The meal fed. The pause rested. Sweet stood near each true gift like a small loud guest and took the bow for the whole room. You know that guest now from pay weeks and love hours already seen through. Here the guest wears fuel dress. Same guest. Same bow. New mask.

You fear dull days without the flash though I know that fear and I do not mock it. Flash feels like life to a mouth long filmed. Even feels like nothing at first to a head long thrown. Only mark what even pays once you live it a week though I keep the picture low. You wake plainer. You eat and shut. You work though the clock without hourly drawer trips. Your tongue finds tea and bread again. You meet night without blown belly though supper satisfied. No flash blinds. No dip binds. That even run is not grey though the old voice paints it grey to keep its pay. Grey is waking thick and chewing past joy. Even is eating and forgetting till hunger calls honest again.

Trust the hollow though sweet taught you to fear it. Hollow means the shop asks for food, not for one wrapper. Answer hollow with real food eaten sitting to shut. Eat as much of the real food you love as you want, whenever you want it, and never count a bite again. I lay that line as your table rule though I count nothing and I weigh nothing and I forbid no dish your house loves. Hunger knows amounts though heads forgot. Shut knows the end though wrappers hid it. Eat till shut. Leave. Work. That is the whole table art though shop talk sells a thousand arts. Mouths shut. Bellies thank. Heads clear though no one taught this week's fashion.

Think of the tiny physical craving though we leave its full face for later hours when your eyes stand stronger. It is small. It complains brief when unfed though you were told it roars. A hollow pang, a dry thought of sweet, gone by the time the kettle boils if the old belief stops feeding it. The brainwashing voice feeds it loud though it names itself you. One more, it says. Just this once. Start again tomorrow. You know those lines though they wear your voice. They are not your voice though they borrow it. They are old talk installed by years of flood and shop smiles. Mark them as talk and they thin. Feed them as truth and they shout.

I warn you plain as one who watched both calls for years. Do not mistake the sharp three o'clock nag for hollow though it swears it starves. Hollow waits and takes bread. Sharp nags and takes one wrapper. Hollow sits low and wide. Sharp sits high and tight. Hollow follows empty hours. Sharp follows a flood. Three tells never lie though sweet taught you to doubt your own belly more than any wrapper. Doubt the wrapper now. Trust the belly though trust returns slow like a dog long shouted at. Feed it plain and kind and it comes back to hand.

You hand me your last doubt though you speak it low. I will never feel full without something sweet to close. I hear the ache under that line, for close matters to eaters. Only ask, what closes. Is it sweet that closes, or is it pause that closes. Mark your best closes in memory. Tea nursed while plates soak. Fruit peeled slow while talk runs. Chair pushed back an inch while belly settles. Those closes shut without flood though you call them small. Sweet never closed though you call it close. It opened. True close shuts and frees the head for hours. That shut you felt at noon with plain food to its end.

So stand in this room a while before we walk on to name the pull deeper, for this room must hold though later rooms shake. You woke thick from flood and woke plain without it. You ate plain to shut and forgot food. You told hollow from sharp by three tells. You watched film thin till fruit ran sweet. You learned the shop brews steady without spooned flash. Five plain proofs though no one weighed you and no one counted you. Proofs the belly gave though talk denied. Hold them though the old voice talks loud on Friday and at tables elsewhere. Bellies do not argue. They shut or they call again. Yours will shut on plain though you feared it never would.

Plain shuts. Flood reopens.

## SUMMARY

- You now know hunger tells straight as a low hollow that takes plain food and shuts, while the sharp high call takes one wrapper and nags though the belly holds food.
- You now see plain meals satisfy to forgetting because film thins and real tastes return, with whole fruit standing as food while sweet drinks flood though labels share a name.
- You now believe your body brews steady without spooned flash, so you eat real food to shut and trust the belly though sweet taught doubt for years.
