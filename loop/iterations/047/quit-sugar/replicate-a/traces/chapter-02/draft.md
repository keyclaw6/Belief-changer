Chapter 2
ALREADY CAUGHT

IN THIS CHAPTER: the supermarket at six, a plain day of mouthfuls, the body that knows hunger.

*You never chose the sweet day. The sweet day chose you.*

## THE SHOP WHERE EVERYONE DOES IT

Walk with me down the bright aisle at six in the evening. We all know this walk. We push the basket, tired from work, and we scan the shelves without any real thought. The packets glow. The biscuits sit beside the cakes. The chocolate bars wait by the till. The fizzy drinks stand in tall rows, cold and sweet. The sweet cereals show a sunrise on the box. The jam, the syrup, the honey that claims to be different, the buns, the pastries, the ice cream behind glass. No one tells us to take them. No one needs to. Our hands reach on their own.

Look at the other baskets as we walk. Every basket holds the same sweet load. A mother with two small children drops in the same box we drop in. An old man who walks slow takes the same biscuits. A young clerk off shift takes the same cola. A fit runner with good shoes takes the same bar for after. No one looks odd. No one hides it. The till beeps and we all pay and we all wheel out into the dusk with the same bags. That calm is the most artful part of the whole business. When all do it, none ask why.

I stood in that aisle for years and never saw it. I saw food. I saw treats. I saw small joys I had earned by working hard. I did not see design. I did not see that the shelf had been built to catch my eye at my weakest hour, when my blood runs low from a missed lunch and my will runs low from a long day. I did not see that the packets had been shaped to melt fast, to taste strong in the first bite and fade by the third, so the hand goes back for a fourth. I did not see that the prices, the lights, the smell from the bakery corner, the sweets at a child's eye height, all pulled one way. I saw my own hand move and I called that move choice.

We call it choice because it feels quick. The hand shoots out. The mind says yes before we have weighed anything. Quick feels free. Quick feels like want. But quick here is not free. Quick is trained. We have walked this aisle ten thousand times. We have learned where each packet lives the way we learn where the stairs are at home. The feet slow at the same spot. The eyes land on the same red box. The tiny craving tugs, brief as a tug on a sleeve, and the brainwashing voice we all carry from years of ads and tables and kind offers says take it, you have earned it, all take it. We take it. Then we tell ourselves we chose it because we like it. The fact is we chose it because we were taught to, and the teaching was paid for by men who gain when we reach.

Ask yourself in that aisle, with the basket half full. Did you plan this morning to buy those biscuits, or did the shelf plan it for you. If you left the list at home, why does the same sweet still land in the basket. If it is free choice, why does free choice look the same in every basket down the line.

You know the answer when you look straight. The sameness gives it away. Free men choose in all directions. Conn men choose as one.

I want you to feel no shame in that sight. Shame belongs to the cheat, not to the cheated. We were not fools. We were fed false news from birth that sweet packets mean joy, that a full basket means a good home, that to turn down the sweet is to turn down life. How could we have known the news was bought. A child who takes a smile-face cookie from his mother's hand at the bakery learns love with sugar on it before he learns his letters. I still see that small shop on the corner, the warm glass, my mother's hand on my back. That flash lives in me still, kind and bright. It taught me well, and it taught me wrong. Not from malice. From the same false news she had been fed. We all pass on what we were told until one of us looks twice.

Look twice now. The aisle is not a market. It is a snare set in the open, where the bait is priced low and the catch walks in smiling. When I say sugar in this book, take it to mean BAD SUGAR, the sweetened packets and bottles and bars built to pull you back within the hour, not the real food that feeds you. The aisle runs on BAD SUGAR. The calm runs on BAD SUGAR. The sameness in every basket is BAD SUGAR doing its work in the open while we call it choice.

That is the sugar trap.

Say those words slowly in your head and feel how they fit. Not a weak will. Not a sweet tooth. Not a flaw in you. That is the sugar trap. A set of packets and cues and taught beliefs that lifts your hand before your mind has voted. Once you name it, you will see it each time you shop, and each sight will loosen its hold a notch more.

## A PLAIN DAY OF MOUTHFULS

Now watch a plain day with me, your day, not a feast day, not a party. No one binges at dawn. The trap does not need binges. It needs drips.

We wake and we drink something sweet because we always have. We tell ourselves it wakes us. At ten the drawer opens. Not from hunger. From the sight of the tin, from the smell down the hall, from the clock that says break. We take a biscuit. It tastes strong for half a bite, then papery, then gone. We take a second to find the first taste again. We do not find it. We go back to the screen with a sticky mouth and a vague wish we had not bothered, and an hour later the wish has faded and the hand is back in the drawer.

At lunch we eat, then we crown it with something sweet because a meal feels bare without a sweet close. The afternoon drags. The tin in the next room calls louder than any memo. We fetch a bar and eat it standing, half reading mail, half thinking of the next bite while we chew this one. We do not savor. We scan. At four we drink something sweet to lift the drag, and for ten minutes the drag lifts, then it sinks lower than before, and we blame the day, the boss, the weather, the bad sleep. At night, when the house goes still, we take the true load. The packet opens with a soft pop. One square to close the day. Then the square breaks uneven and the edge calls, then the row calls, then the whole bar is gone and the wrapper stares up empty. We sit with a full belly and an empty head and we say the words we always say.

"I choose this and I am fine."

"I eat very well except for sugar."

"I have a sweet tooth, that is just how I am."

Do you hear how soft those words are. They are not lies. They are lullabies. They keep us calm while the same film runs each day. I sang them myself for years. I am a grown man who runs his work and pays his bills and keeps his word, and I stood in a dark kitchen eating jam with a spoon, wiping the jar rim so no one would know, and I called that habit. Habit sounds small. Habit sounds like a quirk, like biting nails. Habit lets us keep our pride while we lose our say.

But look at the film with cold eyes. Where in that day did hunger speak. Real hunger growls low, builds slow, waits well, loves plain food, stops glad when full. Did your belly growl at ten. Did it plead for biscuits. Did it wake at eleven at night crying for chocolate. Or did the eyes lead and the belly follow. Did the clock lead. Did the tin lead. Did the ad you saw at six lead. Did the dull mood lead, the same dull mood the last sweet left behind.

Here is the test I give you, and I give it with love because I know you will pass it if you answer straight.

Think of yesterday, bite by bite. Name the mouthfuls you ate from true hunger, the ones your belly asked for before your eyes saw food. Now name the mouthfuls that came from sight, smell, hour, mood, or the mere fact that food sat near. Which list holds the BAD SUGAR. Which list is longer. You will find the same answer I found, and the same answer millions find when they watch straight. The real meals sit in the first list. The BAD SUGAR sits in the second list, every time, without fail.

Think of the night packet of BAD SUGAR. Did you taste bite nine. Be honest. Did bite nine give you even half the joy of bite one. If the sweet truly pleased you, why does pleasure fall as the dose grows. Why do we finish past joy to chase a taste that died at bite three.

If you chose freely, why do you eat the same way on days you vowed not to. Why does the vow made in the bright morning die in the dim night without any new reason. A free choice bends with reasons. A caught choice runs on rails.

It runs on rails. That is the verdict. Short and blunt. You did not choose the day. The day used you.

We ate by cues, not by need. The cues rang and we marched. The bell rang at ten and we drooled. The wrapper shone and we reached. The clock struck and we chewed. That is not choice. That is drill. To see the drill is not to feel low. It is to feel the first lift of the lid, the first draught of cold air from outside. You are not weak. You were drilled.

## WHAT THE BODY ASKS FOR

Let us set the packets down for a minute and listen to the body you live in. Your body is no fool. It has kept you alive through fevers and late nights and hard years. It knows how to mend a cut without your help. It knows how to sleep you when you need sleep. It knows hunger too, plain and true, and it speaks in a tongue you knew as a child and forgot as a grown sweet eater.

True hunger comes slow. It does not scream for one food. It does not cry for chocolate by name. It growls, it empties, it makes plain bread taste good, makes soup taste deep, makes an apple crunch loud and sweet enough. It takes food gladly and then it shuts the gate. Full is full. The plate can sit half done and you walk away without a backward look. That is the body's own rule, old as bone.

Now set beside it the sweet want. Sweet want comes fast. It names names. It says chocolate, cola, cake, now. It strikes when the belly is full. It fades if you look away for twenty minutes, then roars back when you see the packet again. It never shuts the gate. Full men still reach. Sick men still reach. Sad men still reach. Men who just vowed never again still reach within the hour. Does that sound like hunger to you. Does hunger act like that. Hunger feeds you. This bleeds you.

I tell you flat what your body already knows. The body makes its own steady sugar in the blood from the real food you eat, day and night, without any spoonfuls tipped in from above. You do not need to pour sugar in to keep the fire lit. The fire keeps itself when you feed it meals. That is why a man can sleep eight hours with no bite and wake and walk and talk. That is why a child can play all morning on breakfast. The sweet packet is not fuel for the engine. It is a splash of loud taste on a tongue that was built for quieter fare.

Watch this in your own flesh today. Eat a plain meal when true hunger calls. Meat, fish, eggs, potatoes, rice, greens, bread that chews, fruit that snaps. Eat till full. Then mark the hours. Note how long you walk clear without thought of food. Note how the belly rests. Now set beside it a sweet day. Note how soon the hand stirs again, how the mouth wants more within the hour, how the belly never seems to close. Which day shows a body fed. Which day shows a body teased.

Your body has not failed you. We failed to hear it through years of loud taste. The din trained us to read each dip as need for more of the same din. The fact is the dip follows the din as night follows day. But I will not press that proof here. That proof has its own room further on, and it will land clean when its hour comes. For now hold only this ground, for it is firm enough to stand on. Your hunger is sound. Your taste is sound. Real food still feeds and fills you the way it fed the boy and girl you were before the packets took the chair at your table.

Trust the belly over the packet. Trust the growl over the jingle. When in doubt, ask which voice spoke first, the eye or the gut. The eye serves the trap. The gut serves you. That is why BAD SUGAR loses its pull once seen straight. Real food answers hunger and shuts the gate. BAD SUGAR answers cues and leaves the gate wide, so the hand stirs again within the hour and the mind reads the stir as need.

A reader told me once, with tears not far off, that she feared she had lost her grip for good because she ate at night in secret. She was a nurse, steady handed, kind eyed, the one all rang when trouble hit. She hid wrappers in her coat pocket so her husband would not see. She called herself weak. I asked her one thing. Do the weak hold dying hands all night and still turn up for dawn shift. She laughed through the tears. No, she said. Then hear me, I said. The night eating is not your weak self. It is your drilled self. The drill runs strongest at night when the day's drips have worn you thin and the house is still and the packet knows where you live. Strong women march to that drill each night, not from lust for joy, but from cues laid down by day. Take the shame off your back and lay it where it fits, on the drill, on the con, on the years of false news. You were never weak. You were caught.

## CAUGHT, NOT WEAK

Let me speak plain now, as one who was caught and got out, to one who is caught and will get out.

You believe you choose this and you are fine. I believed it too. We all believe it until we watch the day. How could a free man need a book to leave a mere habit he loves. If you hold this book, some part of you already knows the hand moves without leave. That is the logic trap with steel teeth. No free sweet lover seeks a way out. The seek proves the snare.

Who is in charge here. You, who vow at dawn, or the packet, that wins by dusk. You, who buy for the kids, or the basket, that fills with sweets you swore to skip. You, who eat to live, or the tin, that rings like a bell and brings you from the next room. Answer straight and the brag of choice dies in your throat. A choice that always votes one way is no choice. It is a leash.

And you are far from alone in that leash. When teams of researchers ask large crowds plain questions about loss of grip, binges that run past joy, cravings that crowd the day, hidden eating and failed vows, about fourteen in a hundred grown-ups tick enough boxes to sit in the food-addiction-like group, with a like share in children, at rates much the same as for drink and smoke. Those are self-reports from checklists, not a doctor's stamp on you, and I lay no stamp on any reader. I cite the count once for one reason only, to break the lie that you are odd or weak. Millions march the same drill. Normal does not mean free. Normal here means caught together.

Even the labs catch a glimpse of how the drill is laid. Rats fed sweet sips by fits and starts gorge and fret and chase in ways steady-fed rats do not. I do not claim a rat in a box proves your case. A man is not a rat, and a box is not a home. I cite it as a hint only, and a straight one. Fits and starts breed binges. Feast then fast then feast makes the chase hot. Does that ring a bell in your own life. The strict Monday, the clean week, the good spell broken by Friday night, the whole box gone by ten. The schedule feeds the snare. The snare loves your rules. That is why the strain-based path you tried never cut you loose, and why that path has a name you already know.

That shame you carry from past tries is the work of the Willpower Method.

Speak that name with scorn, for it earned scorn. It told you the sweet was joy and bade you fight joy with grit. It weighed you, counted you, praised your streak, then watched you fall and called you weak. It never showed you the drill. It never named the con. It left you to wrestle your own cues with bare hands while the aisle kept ringing its bells. Strong men and women fail at that method each day, not from want of spine, but from want of truth. You did not fail. The tool failed.

Watch how BAD SUGAR rides through one plain workday without once asking leave. We wake and pour the sweet drink out of sheer rote. We drive past the same shop and the same sign lifts the same want. We sit near a drawer that holds BAD SUGAR and the drawer pulls at us each time we shift in our chair. We queue at the till and the low shelf by our knees shows small bars at a low price, placed there because tired hands grab. None of this asks hunger. All of it feeds the loop. Each small hit lays the cue for the next hit, and each cue passed unmarked grows roots a notch deeper. By dusk the roots hold the feet, and we walk home the same path to the same shelf and lift the same packet and tell the same soft tale.

Ask the only three questions that matter here, and answer them without flinch.

Did the packet please you past bite three, or did you chase a taste already dead. Did the day run on hunger, or did hunger run unfed while cues fed. If this is choice, why does choice never once choose less.

You know what those answers say. We all know once we stop turning away. The pleasure died early. The cues ran the day. The choice chose the same each time because it was never choice at all. It was drill. Drill is not fate. Drill seen is drill doomed. That is blunt and true. You were drilled. You are not weak.

See how the parts lock now. The shop teaches the hand. The day drills the hand. The body cries out under the drill but is misread. The old voice calls the drill choice. The failed tries prove the method false, not the man false. Bit by bit the free tale falls apart, and what stands in its place is not a flaw in you but a device around you, built by other hands, oiled by false news, wound each dawn and sprung each night.

That device is the sugar trap.

There it is again, in plain speech, because plain speech is how lifers learn. You walked in through no fault. You stayed because each dose laid the next cue. You will walk out the same door you walked in, with eyes open this time, and the door will shut behind you for good.

Mark the time scale with me, for time is the part we hide from. This is not one bar. This is a drip that steals from you for every day from now on. Count it cold. A bar a day, a cola a day, a tin shared yet emptied, year piled on year. Money paid to feel worse. Teeth bared to acid. Moods sunk. Nights spent in secret munching while the house sleeps. Mornings woke thick. All for tastes that die by bite three. Look down that road to its end and ask if you chose that road or if the road chose you while you slept. No free man signs such a lease with open eyes. A caught man signs it daily without reading, for every day from now on.

Now turn the glass over, for the same stretch of days holds the gain when the snare lifts. The same mornings yours. The same shops yours. The same tables yours, with real food that fills and lets you rise and forget food till hunger calls true. Think of waking and owning each hour, of shopping and passing the bright packets the way you pass goods meant for another house, of closing each meal and walking off free, and holding that stead for every day from now on. The days do not shrink. They widen. The trap took days. Freedom returns them with interest. Hold both sights together and choose with open eyes which lease you will sign. A caught man signs blind each dawn. A wise man reads first, then signs once, then never signs that lease again. You stand at that desk now, with the pen in your hand and the small print laid bare at last.

You do not need to fight this sight. You need only to watch it work in your own hours. That is why I lay on you now the one rule of this chapter, at the peak where it fits, with no more words than it needs.

4. NOTICE HOW YOU EAT TODAY.
Watch appetite the way you would watch weather.

Take that rule as a command, not as counsel. Today, mark each bite. Was it gut or eye that called. Did hunger growl first, or did the packet shine first. Did bite one please more than bite five. Did full stop the hand, or did the hand outrun full. Did BAD SUGAR close hunger, or did it wake more want within the hour. Write nothing down. Count nothing. Strain after nothing. Only watch. You will catch the drill in the act within hours, and what you catch you can no longer call choice. The watch itself is the wedge that splits choice from con.

I know this is hard to take in one gulp. You have called this choice for years. To hear it was drill can sting. Let the sting pass. The sting is pride waking, not joy dying. No joy dies here. Only a false tale dies, and false tales deserve death. What lives is you, the watcher, the grown man or woman who can now spot the strings on the hands and cut them with a look.

So do not wail. Do not wade in gloom. Lift your head. The con read from the aisle to the night drawer is now out in the light. You see the strings. You know the bells. You hold the body's true voice against the packet's loud voice. You hold the name for the device and the scorn for the false method that kept you marching. That is much ground won in one short march. The next ground waits, and it asks what the sweet ever gave you that was real. Keep that test in mind as you watch today, for your watch will fill your hands with proof.

The choice tale is dead. The con stands bare.

## SUMMARY

- You now see your plain sweet day was not free choice but a drilled round of cues that moved your hand without hunger.
- You now believe you were conned, not weak, and you trust your body's hunger over the packet's call.
- You now know watching each bite splits the drill from choice because you mark if gut or eye called first.
