Chapter 3
WHAT GOOD AT ALL

IN THIS CHAPTER: the drawer of rules, the list that talks in your voice, a bite tasted straight.

*If it does nothing good for you, there is nothing to weigh.*

## THE DRAWER OF RULES

Open the drawer in your kitchen and look with me. We all know this drawer. It holds the small white bags tied too tight, the half bar folded back in foil, the loose sweets in a chipped mug, the biscuits kept for guests who never come at the right hour. Beside it sits the rule you made last Sunday. No sweets before six. No sweets at work. No second help. Small rules, neat rules, rules written in a firm hand. You know this drawer and you know these rules because you have lived with both for years.

I know them too. I kept the same drawer. I hid the same bar behind the rice where my own eyes would find it by night. I told myself the drawer proved I was in charge. A man in charge keeps order, I thought. A man in charge sets bounds. I did not see that the drawer itself was the mark of the catch. Free men need no rules for bread. Free men need no vows for soup. No one locks plain food in a drawer and counts it by day. We count only what owns us.

Look at your day through this drawer. We wake and we swear this day will be clean. By ten the hand shakes a bit and the mind starts its old talk. By three the talk gets loud. By nine the rule lies torn and we eat standing by the cold light with the door half shut. Then we make a fresh rule to calm the shame. Start Monday. Keep none in the house. Take just one. Take only the fine kind. Take only at feasts. You know each of these turns because you have turned them all. I turned them all and each turn led back to the same drawer.

We call this weighing good against bad. We say the sweet must bring some good or we would not chase it so hard. We line up the harm on one side, the teeth, the weight, the slump, the money paid out year on year, and we line up the claimed good on the other side, the lift, the calm, the prize, the love in it. Then we haggle with ourselves. A little harm for a little good. A small dose for a small joy. That haggle feels wise. It feels grown. The fact is the haggle is the con talking through your mouth.

Think straight with me here. A haggle makes sense only when both sides hold real goods. You weigh a coin against bread because both are real. You weigh an hour of work against an hour of rest because both are real. But what if one side holds air. What if the sweet brings no true good at all and the whole weigh-off was taught to you by the men who gain from each dose. Then you are not wise to haggle. You are conned to haggle. The trap needs you weighing harm against gain because while you weigh, you never ask if there is any gain to weigh.

That is the sugar trap. At its slyest it does not roar. Not the bright aisle. Not the night binge. The quiet voice that says be fair, count both sides, all things in small share do no harm. Fair sounds honest. Small sounds safe. But fair to a cheat is cheat. Small of a lie is still lie. When I say sugar in this book, take it to mean BAD SUGAR, the sweet packets and bottles and bars built to pull you back within the hour, not the plain food that feeds you true. The drawer holds BAD SUGAR. The rules guard BAD SUGAR. The haggle serves BAD SUGAR. None of it serves you.

Ask yourself with a cold eye, with the drawer open before you. When did you first decide the sweet did you good. Did you test it like a grown man tests a tool, or did you take the word of ads and kind hands and bright tables before you could spell your name. Did you choose the weigh-off, or did the weigh-off get put in your head the way the packets got put in your drawer. You know the answer when you stop blinking. No child weighs. The child takes what shines. The grown man then learns to call the shine good so he need not feel fooled. That is brainwashing, plain and low, fed drip by drip till it sounds like your own thought.

I want no gloom on you here. Feel no shame for the drawer. Shame is the trap's rent and I will not let you pay it. We were hooked young, each of us, by taste laid on love, by prize laid on work, by rest laid on sugar till the wires crossed. We fed the loop because the loop was fed to us. To see the drawer now is not to feel low. It is to feel the lid lift. You stand at the sink in the late light, you see the tied bags and the torn rules, and you know for the first time which side put them there. Not your weak self. Your drilled self. And what was drilled can be undrilled by sight alone.

## THE LIST THAT KNOWS YOU

Let me lay out the talk in your head so you hear how well I know it. I do not guess. I heard it in my own head for years and I hear it now from sweet eaters in every town. Read slow and mark each line you have said or thought. You will know your own voice here.

"I need it to get through the afternoon."

"the sugar I need to see me through my daily slump."

"I will crave sugar all day."

"It helps me concentrate."

"fresh energy for my body."

"I need a pick-me-up."

"I'm too tired to function without it."

"celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate."

"Sugar is the reward for a hard earned paycheck."

"I deserve a little treat now and then."

"The sweetness of chocolate can hint at love."

"Dessert is the best part of every meal."

"chocolate makes me feel like I am in heaven."

"Just one a day."

"only on the weekend."

"a few bites."

"I stay away except for special occasions."

"I don't keep it in the house."

"I don't usually keep candy."

"Everything in moderation."

"only fine confections, quality not quantity."

"I eat very well except for sugar."

"I typically eat a healthy diet, I also have a sweet tooth."

"It's natural honey."

"fruit juice instead."

"sugar-free cookies."

"diet soda instead."

"I'll start again tomorrow."

"restart Monday."

"one glass after, then a little before bed."

"Other people handle it fine."

"most people can eat in moderation."

"I need something sweet."

"to get me through."

"treat when you deserve one."

"I have been good this week."

"special occasions."

"just one."

"start again tomorrow."

"my body was saying eat cake."

"a pick-me-up."

That leftover cake in the breakroom at two, nobody chose it yet all take it, that too sits in the list though none speak it plain.

Do you hear yourself. Of course you do. That is the point. I speak in your voice because the trap speaks in your voice. It learned your hours, your tired spots, your kind heart, your pay day, your feast days, your low moods, and it hung a sweet on each peg. Tired, take a fix. Sad, take a dose. Glad, take a prize. Kind, share a bar. Good all week, pay with a hit. Bad all week, soothe with a hit. Each line sounds like sense. Heard together they show their true shape. There is no hour with no line. There is no mood with no dose. A need that fits all hours fits none. A cure for all ills cures none.

Take note of how the lines slide. In the bright morning you say just one. By dusk you say start again tomorrow. At the shop you say only the fine kind. At night you eat the cheap kind and hide the wrap. With friends you say special occasions. Alone you make each night special. You believe each line as you say it. I believed each line as I said it. The belief felt firm at the time. An hour later a fresh line took its place and felt just as firm. That slide gives the game away. True needs do not change shape by the hour. Hunger does not say just one then say start Monday then say all take it so why not I. Hunger growls low and asks for food and shuts when fed. Only a con talks in ten voices to sell one dose.

Now mark the other trick in the list. Each line talks of one sweet, one prize, one small treat. Just one a day. A few bites. One glass after. The talk keeps your eye on a single bright bite while your week holds scores of dim ones you never count. You count the bar you chose. You do not count the spoon in tea, the cola with lunch, the sweet drink sold as juice, the cereal that crunches sweet at dawn, the yoghurt that tastes of sweets though it claims milk, the sauce that hides sugar, the bread that hides sugar, the honey you trust because bees made it though it pulls the same loop. You believe you take a little because you look at a little. The trap lives on the much you never look at.

I lay this out not to scold your count. I lay it out because you cannot weigh what you will not see. Men who gain from your hand to mouth love your small bright count. They stock the dim corners while you watch the bright bar. Biscuits for the tin. Chocolate for the prize. Flavoured yoghurts for the health claim. Sweet cereals for the dawn rush. Fizzy drinks for the thirst that sweet made worse. Each sits in plain sight yet out of mind. You pass them daily and you do not tag them as dose. Then you tell yourself you take just one. Just one of what. Just one of scores. That is why your rules fail though your will stands firm. You lock the front door while the back stands wide.

See how the list works as one beast now. The sweet talk gives you leave to take a dose. The dim supply makes sure the dose never ends at one. The fresh rule wipes the slate so you take the next dose with a clean heart. Round and round. Dose wakes want. Want wakes talk. Talk wakes leave. Leave wakes dose. That round is the loop, and we have all run it till our shoes wore thin. You are not weak for running it. You were taught the talk, blinded to the supply, and handed the rules as a leash you mistook for a lifeline.

## WHAT IT DOES TO YOU, WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU

Now we come to the turn this whole book stands on, and I ask you to give me your full mind for five minutes.

Stop weighing harm against good. That weigh-off is a false game and I will have no more of it in your head. Harm talk never freed a man. Show a sweet eater his teeth and he nods and eats. Show him his weight and he sighs and eats. Show him his slump and he blames the day and eats. Fear never cut the tie because fear leaves the claimed good standing. While the good stands, the heart clings. While the heart clings, the hand reaches. I do not want you scared. I want you clear. There is a straight test and it cuts truer than all the fright in the world.

BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you. Read that line twice and let it bite.

TO you means what lands on your flesh and hours. The rush then the drag. The full belly that still wants. The night raid past joy. The wrap hid by dawn. The cash paid out for tastes dead by bite three. The teeth bared to acid with each sip. The dull head at dusk. The fresh rule each Monday. That TO side is long and you know each inch because you paid each inch in your own coin.

FOR you means what true good it puts in your hand that naught else could put there. Not what rides with it. Not the rest, the laugh, the sun, the dear face across the table. What the sweet itself does for you, alone, stripped of hour and place and folk. Name one such good and hold it up to the light. That is the test. Not less harm. Not small share. One real good, yours to keep, that you could not have had without the dose.

You will say at once, but it lifts me. Hold that claim up with me and look it in the face. When does the lift come. It comes when you droop. When do you droop. An hour after the last dose. Who taught you to read that droop as need for more of what laid it. The same voice that sold you the first bar. Keep only this grip for now. Hold the claim loose and test it against your own tongue.

You will say, but it calms me, it pays me, it loves me. I hear you and I honour the ache under the words. You work hard. You give much. You want some sweet pay at close of day. Who would not. But ask with me, does the calm live in the bite or in the pause. Does the pay live in the chocolate or in the thought I did well. Does the love live in the cake or in the dear hands that cut it. Keep those asks warm in your chest. Each claimed good arrives glued to a good hour. Peel the hour off and weigh what stays in your palm.

For the talk of one small treat hides the dim stack. You say a few bites yet you drink and spoon and crunch sweet from dawn to dusk without once saying dose. Biscuits with tea. Chocolate from the drawer. Flavoured yoghurts sold as light. Sweet cereals sold as morn fuel. Fizzy drinks sold as thirst cure. Honey and syrup sold as kind though they tug the same wire. None of these stand on your bright count. All stand on your blood and teeth. How can a weigh-off stand when one pan holds one bar and the other pan hides the shop. It cannot. That is why your count never squares with your girth, your teeth, your dusk droop. You count the prize. The trap counts the stack.

So drop the scales. Scales lie when one side is hid. Take the straight test instead. Do not ask does it harm more than help. Ask what good it does at all. That one ask moves the whole fight from To and Fro to Show Me. Show me one true good, alone, with hour and folk peeled off, that BAD SUGAR puts in my hand. Show it bare. Show it such that plain food and rest and folk could not show it better. Till that show stands, there is naught to weigh. Air weighs naught against stone.

I know this turn can feel stark at first. The haggle felt like care. It blinded you. What falls now is not your joy. What falls now is the false frame that kept joy tied to dose.

## TASTE IT STRAIGHT

Words can fog. Taste cuts fog. Let me put a bite in your mouth, here, now, in plain day, with no fuss and no vow tied to it.

Fetch your usual fix. The bar, the biscuit, the cola, what you take when the hour droops. Take it as you always take it, no change, no fast, no brave face. I bid you eat while you read, for I want truth from your tongue not from my pen. Take one bite. Chew slow. Mark it well. Sweet hits loud at first, strong, bright, full. The head lifts a notch. Good. Note that. That first flash is what you chase all day.

Now take a second bite straight on its heels. Mark it true. Less bright. Less full. The edge dulled. Take a third. Mark it cold. Papery. Thin. The joy thins though the sweet stays same. Take a fourth and fifth if your wont runs that far. Mark them flat. By now you chew more from push than from joy. The mouth moves. The want moves. The joy does not move. It died at bite two or three and left the hand working alone.

Sit with that plain fact and answer me straight. If the good lived in the sweet, why does the same sweet give less with each bite. Same bar. Same mouth. Same you. Less joy. What changed. Not the sugar. The hour changed. The crave eased a notch, the tongue dulled, the brain ceased to hail the hit. That slide proves with your own tongue what all my lines could only point at. Joy does not sit in the bite. Joy sits in the meet of bite and crave. Kill the crave and the bite stands bare, loud at first lick then dull as paper.

Now push the test one notch more, for this notch splits talk from truth. Call to mind plain food eaten from true hunger. Bread, soup, eggs, fish, meat, rice, greens, fruit that snaps and runs sweet in its own clean way. Mark how bite five tastes fuller than bite one because hunger still asks and food still answers. Mark how the belly shuts glad at close and the hand rests without a backward look. Now set beside it the sweet eaten off crave. Bite one shouts. Bite five whispers. Belly full yet hand still stirs. Which feeds. Which teases. You know with your tongue now what your head long hid. Real food answers hunger and shuts the gate. BAD SUGAR answers crave and leaves the gate wide so the next dose finds you ajar.

You may have felt, in weeks when you ate less sweet, that plain food tasted sweeter and fuller, fruit like a feast, bread deep, water cold and good. That soft note tells true. Sweet blunts the tongue for plain fare while it lasts, then the tongue wakes when the din dies. Keep it as a small bell in the back of your mind each time plain food sings louder after sweet-free days.

Now gather the loose lines from the list into one fist. Lift. Calm. Prize. Love. Heaven in a bar. Pay for hard work. Friend at dusk. Each claims its own gain, each wears its own dress, yet each dies the same death at bite three. That one death proves one root. Diverse dresses, one thin body. What you called many goods is one flash that fades on contact. Name it now in one breath the way you felt it in your mouth just now, not in my words but in yours made plain. You called it lift when tired. You called it calm when tense. You called it prize when good. You called it love when dear folk near. Yet each time the tongue told the same tale of flash then flat. Strip the hour off each name and one bare shape stands out, the same shape under each dress. What you chased was never joy stacked on joy. What you chased was a brief loud flash that dies as you chase, sold to you as a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift.

Say those words slow in your head and feel how they fit your mouth's own proof. Not my phrase. Your tongue's phrase. A flash you took for a genuine pleasure, comfort or lift. Hold it there. That shape will do much work for us. Each time a line from the list pipes up, set this shape beside it and ask does the line survive bite three. None survive. That is not scorn. That is sight. The dress was hour and folk and rest. The body was flash and fade. Once seen, the dress never quite fools again.

## THE ONLY TEST THAT COUNTS

I hear your last stand and I meet it with love because it sounds like sense till you hold it to the light.

"It cannot be that bad if all do it."

All buy it. All serve it. Kind mums. Fit lads. Wise docs. Bright clerks. The shop glows and none hide. How could scores of millions be wrong. How could what sits on each table be a cheat. That doubt sits deep and I will not brush it off. I will take it in both hands and crack it like a nut.

Tell me, when all did smoke in rooms and planes, did the smoke turn kind. When all fed strong drink at lunch, did the drink turn safe. Did the count of hands make the dose true, or did it prove how wide a net the gain-hunters cast. You know though you flinch to say it. Wide use proves wide sell, not true good. The aisle glows because glow pays, not because glow feeds.

Tell me more. If all are free in this, why do all hide wraps, break vows, eat past joy, swear Monday, count bites, lock drawers. Did you ever hide bread. Did you ever vow Monday off soup. Did you ever eat plain meals past sick and weep. Free joys need no rules, no hides, no fresh starts. The fact that all share the same hides and vows proves not freedom but a shared catch. All in the same snare does not make snare home. It makes escape more glad when eyes open.

Tell me last. Who taught all to do it. Did your gut teach you before your eyes saw sweet, or did kind hands, bright screens, school fetes, feast tables, pay-day treats teach you year on year till the taught taste felt born. All do it because all were taught it by the same loud teachers who gain from each taught hand. That is not nature speaking. That is brainwashing speaking with many mouths. Many mouths can shout a lie. Shout never made lie true.

There is the nut cracked. Wide does not mean wise. Wide means well sold. That is blunt and short. All caught proves catch, not good.

So stand now where the drawer meets the list meets the tongue. You hold three plain sights. The drawer proved rules mark catch not choice. The list proved talk shifts shape by hour and hides the dim stack. The tongue proved joy dies at bite three though sweet stays same. Three sights, one point. No real good has shown its face yet. Much TO you. Naught FOR you yet proved. That is not my verdict laid on you. That is your own mouth, drawer, and day speaking as one.

You do not need to fight that sight. You need only to hold it and ask the one ask that fits it. That is why I lay on you now the one rule of this turn, at the peak where it fits, with no more words than it needs.

5. ASK WHAT GOOD IT DOES AT ALL.
Refuse the harm trade and demand one real benefit.

Take that rule as a command, not as counsel. When the talk pipes up, lift, calm, prize, love, meet it with that one ask and make it show clean. What good, alone, stripped of hour and folk, that naught else gives better. Let the line stammer. Let it dress and redress. Hold for the bare shape. Flash then flat. Much TO you. Naught FOR you. You will find the talk dies faster each time you ask, for talk lives on haze and this ask is a cold draught. No strain in it. No grit in it. Only sight. Sight does the work while you eat as before and read on.

I know some will read this and feel a pinch. If no good stands, was my life with sweet all waste. Hear me flat as one who paid the same coin. No years were waste. You loved folk true. You rested true. You laughed true. The sweet rode those goods like a thief on a cart and claimed the ride as its gift. Take the cart back and the goods stay yours. The thief stole naught but coin and hours, and hours from now run free when sight holds. Lift your head. The pinch is pride healing, not joy dying. No true joy dies when a false gain dies. Only the leash dies.

Keep this grip. Bring your list with you, every line. Bring too the bare shape your tongue just learned and set it beside each line when it calls. Bring the cold ask. You no longer haggle harm against air. You ask for good and wait for show.

The weigh-off is dead. The ask stands.

## SUMMARY

- You now see the harm weigh-off was a false game because no real gain has yet shown itself to weigh.
- You now believe BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you and naught FOR you yet proved.
- You now know to ask what good it does at all each time the old talk pipes up.
