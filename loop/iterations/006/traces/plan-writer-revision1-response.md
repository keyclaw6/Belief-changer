## 1. BOOK CORE

**Target behavior:** Compulsive consumption of refined/added sugar and junk-processed carbohydrates (BAD SUGAR) — the craving-snacking loop, the secret binge, the daily graze and the 3pm lift-crash cycle. Not nutrition pedantry, not whole-food eating.

**Reader:** One general-adult reader who feels trapped in the sugar loop, has tried diets, moderation rules, sugar swaps and willpower and watched them all fail, suspects the whole approach is wrong, and is reading with skepticism and quiet shame.

**Functional personas (3–6):**
- P-01 The loss-of-control binger — night/weekend binges, one-bite-becomes-the-box, hiding, shame cycle, failed diets
- P-02 The in-denial moderate — "just a treat," daily grazing that never reads as a problem, negotiation and carve-outs
- P-03 The comfort/identity eater — sugar as reward, love, coping, nostalgia, "I deserve it"
- P-04 The energy-crash yo-yoer — sugar as fuel for focus/energy, 3pm slumps, lift-crash as productivity strategy

**Load-bearing false belief (one sentence):**
> Sugar — as refined sweets and junk carbs — is a genuine treat, lift and reward that makes life sweeter, steadier and more bearable, and life without it would be deprivation.

**Through-line:** Sugar isn't treating you — it's tricking you. The lift is the low it created.

**Format:** Full-length Carr — 20 chapters, ~59,800 words, pocket-book spine expanded with B10 full-length apparatus (chapter anatomy, instruction spine, redefinition, firewall, homework, ritual, recap). Front matter carries authority dossier + first instructions; close carries verbatim instruction recap + terminal mantra.

**Fork decisions (§4 — Carr defaults, executed at Carr amplitude):**

- **Fork 1 — Inner monster:** DEFAULT CARR. Full personification. Two mechanism characters with original behavior-fitted names, frozen in mantra sheet: the trivial physical creature to starve (Little-Monster equivalent) and the belief-system that feeds it (Big-Monster equivalent). Craving is external, small, already dying. No Burgeon no-monster blend.
- **Fork 2 — Abstinence vs autonomy:** DEFAULT CARR — total cessation, commanded with cheerful certainty, inside the redefinition line. BAD SUGAR is quit wholly; moderation pincer and cliff-jump foreclose "just a little." Autonomy is preserved via the Good/Bad line and conditional-bonus extensions, not via moderation of BAD SUGAR.
- **Fork 3 — Science weight:** DEFAULT CARR — hard facts at full flat frightening force, then explicitly disowned as motivator (scare-then-disown). Facts arrive as "The fact is…" never as hedged review. Raw citations quarantined to appendix.
- **Fork 4 — Villain:** DEFAULT CARR — two villains hit hard: (1) the substance/industry that engineered the trap (food-industry bliss-point engineering), (2) the named anti-method — the Willpower Method — that kept you in it. Past failures reattributed to method, never to reader. Warm to person, vicious to trap.
- **Fork 5 — Void:** DEFAULT CARR — "Change nothing else in your life." Natural baseline returns on its own; inhabit old contexts confidently to prove freedom. No Burgeon elaborate replacement system. Positive authority (Nature's Guide) supplies operational tools for distinguishing Good/Bad, not a life-rebuild.

**Redefinition and margin-for-error doctrine (B10):**

- **Definitional decree (boxed, Ch1, repeated 300+ ×):** When this book says **SUGAR**, take it to mean **BAD SUGAR**.
- **BAD SUGAR [CAPS name]:** All refined/added free sugars (sucrose, glucose, high-fructose corn syrup, honey/syrups added) + junk-processed carbs where fibre/structure has been stripped (white bread, pastries, sweets, biscuits, cakes, ice cream, sugary drinks, confectionery, ultra-processed snacks). The CAPS name carries the definition in every sentence thereafter.
- **GOOD SUGAR / NOT-SUGAR:** Intrinsic sugars in whole fruit, vegetables, and unrefined whole foods eaten as whole foods, and plain natural foods generally. These are part of Nature's Guide menu and are not restricted. You are not quitting food, sweetness as taste, or eating itself.
- **Total-abstinence logic runs inside the line:** For BAD SUGAR there is no healthy level other than zero — not "less," not "occasional." If there's no benefit to doing it often, there's no point doing it occasionally either.
- **Margin for error (seatbelt doctrine):** Your body can cope with an occasional, accidental blip of BAD SUGAR — trace sugars, a sauce, a mouthful at a dinner — without harm. Your mind cannot cope with entertaining the belief again. A blip revives nothing physically; it only becomes a trap again if you re-enshrine the Big Monster belief ("that was nice / I need this"). Guard the belief, not the behaviour. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically. Do not test the margin.
- **Conditional-bonus extensions (autonomy open door):** Alcohol and other adjacent indulgences are NOT required to quit BAD SUGAR. This book is not a stop-alcohol/stop-dairy manual. If you want the upgrade, the pointer lives in the appendix — an exciting option, not a condition.

**Clinical / eating-disorder safety perimeter:**
This book is not medical nutrition therapy, diabetes management, or eating-disorder treatment. It does not diagnose, prescribe medications, or set clinical thresholds. Any reader with diabetes, hypoglycemia, an eating disorder, history of disordered eating, pregnancy, or clinician-prescribed therapeutic diet must follow qualified clinical guidance; nothing in these instructions overrides professional advice. Instructions that could conflict with care carry that exception verbatim. A boxed practical-safety advisory appears in Ch15/Ch20 routing medical specifics to a professional, kept outside the belief argument. Crisis/ED pointers are in front and back matter, never as a persuasion device. No pathological labeling of the reader.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene (to meet head-on, Ch11):**
The warm, deserved, sociable reward moment — Friday-night cinema / sofa with chocolate, or the shared birthday cake / family dessert where love, celebration and sweetness seem inseparable. All pleasure will be reassigned to its true sources (company, warmth, rest, the film, the occasion, your own body) — BAD SUGAR was only sneaking a ride.

**Destination state:** Whenever the reader thinks about BAD SUGAR, they feel *relief and freedom that they no longer do it* — free, and happy to be free — so that not having it feels like escape and reclamation, not sacrifice, from Day One.

**Fresh ending reframe (saved for final movement, Ch20):**
You didn't give up sweetness — you gave up being tricked about sweetness. This was never about quitting; it was about growing. *Burgeon* = to grow: you outgrew the need for the con.

## 2. COMPACT EVIDENCE LEDGER

Each row is the only permitted source for that claim. Chapter cards resolve to these IDs. `SI` = permitted inference, `PI` = prohibited inference, `EL` = empirical limit, `SL` = safety limit.

**E-01 — Intermittent-binge model shows bingeing, withdrawal, craving, cross-sensitization with sugar as reinforcer**
- Finding: under intermittent 12-h sugar access, rats binge, show withdrawal signs, craving and cross-sensitization.
- Units/Source: SEU S-1 / S-1
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope: rat model; intermittent-access paradigm
- SI: the schedule (restrict-binge) drives dependency-like behaviour; echoes human diet-binge yo-yo
- PI: sugar is pharmacologically addictive in humans at everyday doses
- EL: translates cautiously; intermittent vs ad-lib distinction essential
- SL: do not diagnose human sugar withdrawal syndrome

**E-02 — Each sugar binge releases dopamine in nucleus accumbens, re-triggering reward circuit**
- Finding: microdialysis shows accumbens dopamine on sugar binge.
- Units/Source: SEU S-2, S-8 / S-2, S-8
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope: animal microdialysis; magnitude smaller than drugs of abuse
- SI: each hit re-primes the loop; fuels cue-driven wanting
- PI: dopamine = pleasure; dose = drug-like high
- EL: wanting/liking distinction; smaller magnitude
- SL: no claim that human dopamine proves addiction to sugar

**E-03 — Withdrawal after sugar bingeing: low accumbens dopamine with rise in opposing transmitter**
- Finding: after sugar bingeing, accumbens dopamine dips with opposing rise; naloxone-precipitated signs observed.
- Units/Source: SEU S-3 / S-3
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope: rodent; opiate-like withdrawal model
- SI: there is a real, measured dip after the hit — the low the next hit relieves
- PI: humans experience opiate-like physiologic withdrawal from everyday sugar
- EL: precipitated in animals; human sugar withdrawal not a diagnosis
- SL: do not frighten with disease labeling

**E-04 — Dependency characterized by pro-addiction lab as "mild but well-defined"**
- Finding: neurochemical dependency described as mild but well-defined.
- Units/Source: SEU S-4 / S-4
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope: rat model; defuses fear frame
- SI: grip is real but small and starvable; Big Monster is the problem
- PI: grip is severe, lifelong, or needs medical detox
- EL: small magnitude; rat model
- SL: use to de-escalate, not to minimize lived difficulty

**E-05 — Schedule is the trap: intermittent drives escalation; ad-lib does not produce dependency signs**
- Finding: intermittent access escalates binge volume; ad-lib access shows no dependency signs.
- Units/Source: SEU S-5, S-9 / S-5, S-9
- Grade: SUPPORTED
- Scope: animal control-group comparison
- SI: restriction->binge rhythm powers the trap; "cutting down" can intensify
- PI: any sugar exposure is addictive regardless of pattern
- EL: animal; human parallel is echo, not proof
- SL: do not prescribe eating schedules as clinical therapy

**E-06 — Opioid-system mediation (naloxone signs) + cue/reactivity circuits as neural analogue for lingering craving**
