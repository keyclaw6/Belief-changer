### THE WRONG QUESTION THAT KEEPS YOU BARGAINING

We have been asking the wrong question for years.

We ask, "Does the harm outweigh the benefit?" We picture a set of scales. On one side we pile the worries — weight, teeth, slumps, money, the tired, foggy and drained feeling between hits. On the other side we place a single shining object. We call it a genuine treat or lift.

Do you recognise that weighing? Do you hear yourself do it? "I know it's not great for me, but it helps me get through." "I know I shouldn't, but I deserve something." That is the whole trap in one sentence. Harm on one side. A supposed help on the other.

Why have we agreed to argue on those terms? Who taught you to weigh it that way?

I will tell you. The trap did. The industry did. The well-meaning advice did. Every conversation about BAD SUGAR starts with harm versus benefit and therefore smuggles in the assumption that there *is* a benefit. You never notice the smuggling. You defend the benefit before you have proven it exists.

Do you see how that rigs the game? If I tell you a lump of glass is a diamond, you will argue about whether the sparkle is worth the price. You will never ask whether it sparkles at all.

So we will refuse that game. Completely.

We will not argue harm versus benefit. We will ask a simpler, sterner question. What does BAD SUGAR actually do FOR you? Not what you fear it does TO you. What does it genuinely give you that you could not have without it?

That is the switch. From weighing to auditing. From "is it worth it?" to "does it do anything at all?"

Do you feel the defence rise as I say that? Do you think, "But of course it does something — it tastes good, it lifts me, it comforts me"? Good. Hold that thought. We will not dismiss it. We will test it. And we will test it properly.

The fact is… you have been sold a benefit that has never had to prove itself. Today it will have to prove itself. And you will be the judge.

### THE TWO-COLUMN TEST THAT ENDS THE BARGAIN

I want you to do a small exercise with me right now. Not later. Now. You need a scrap of paper or your phone notes. Draw a line down the middle.

On the left, write TO. On the right, write FOR.

On the left you will list what BAD SUGAR does TO your body, your mood, your freedom. On the right you will list what BAD SUGAR genuinely does FOR you. Not what it is *said* to do. What you can isolate and prove it does for you that is truly good and could not be had without it.

Do it with me as we go. Be honest. Be literal.

Think of yesterday. What did BAD SUGAR do TO you? Write it plainly.

It spiked you, then dropped you. It made you chase the next hit. It made you negotiate all day. "Just a treat." "To get me through." "I've been good this week." It took your steady hunger and turned it into craving. It took your calm between meals and made it restless.

It took money. Every day. It took attention. Every day. It took time spent planning, getting, eating, regretting. For the rest of your life if you stay in the Sugar Trap — that is the real cost: a daily tax, not a dramatic event.

Keep writing. What else does it do TO you?

The fact is… free sugars feed the bacteria in plaque, the plaque makes acid, the acid dissolves enamel, and cavities follow. That is not a scare story. That is a mechanism. You brush. You use fluoride. Those help. The mechanism remains. Does that belong on the TO side? Yes. Put it there.

The fact is… the world health guidance for free sugars is less than ten percent of your energy, with additional benefit below five percent — about twenty-five to fifty grams a day for many adults — and current population intake sits far above both. Do you know what that tells you? Not how many grams you personally should have — your clinician guides your specifics — but that normalised intake is far above the population ceiling for health. That is not a diagnosis of you. It is a measure of how far the water has warmed for all of us. Put it on the TO side too. The scale of the overshoot belongs there.

Now look at the right column. The FOR side.

What will you write there that survives a fair test?

You might write "taste." You might write "energy." You might write "comfort." You might write "reward." We will test each of those in the next four chapters, one by one, with the variable isolated. For now, I ask you to leave the FOR side almost blank. Not because I tell you to. Because you cannot yet prove a single entry belongs there.

Do you feel the shift? From defending a treat to auditing an illusion?

I know this is hard to accept. You have called BAD SUGAR a genuine treat or lift for years — perhaps for your whole adult life. Those words feel true in your mouth. I use them deliberately. a genuine treat or lift is the name I give to that single shining object on the scales. I compress every claimed benefit — taste, lift, love, normal — into those five words so we can hold it up to the light and see it whole.

Hear how strange the claim sounds once compressed. One substance, in tiny white crystals and stripped white carbs, is said to be simultaneously your pleasure, your fuel, your comfort, your reward and your social glue. Does any other food make that many promises? Does water claim to be love? Does an apple claim to be your personality? No. The very breadth of the promise is a clue. A trap must promise everything to keep you.

So I ask you directly. Can you name one thing BAD SUGAR does FOR you that is real, irreplaceable, and stands up when isolated from the moment you eat it?

Take your time. What is your answer?

If you are honest, you hear a pause. The pause is important. The pause is the beginning of freedom.

You see, the debate you were trained to have — harm versus benefit — collapses if there is no benefit. You do not need to prove harm outweighs benefit if there is nothing on the benefit side to weigh.

Do we need to settle whether sugar is "addictive" in some laboratory sense to pass this test? No. Some researchers show bingeing, craving and withdrawal-like patterns under intermittent access; other careful reviews argue that the pattern, not the molecule alone, drives the behaviour, and that human evidence remains thin and contested. The fact is… both sides in that honest scientific split still observe the same human behaviour under a restrict-then-binge schedule: the loop, the craving, the chase. We do not need the label to see the trap. We see it in your day. So we will not plant our flag on a contested claim. We plant it on your own two-column audit. The trap runs on belief, not on a verdict about a molecule.

And that is why the burden now moves. You do not have to prove BAD SUGAR harms you more than it helps you. BAD SUGAR has to prove it does anything FOR you at all.

Have you ever put it to that test before? Have you ever demanded proof instead of offering excuses?

We will. Together. Benefit by benefit.

### WHAT BAD SUGAR DOES TO YOU — THE LEDGER YOU NEVER TOTALLED

We never totalled the TO side. We felt it in flashes, then explained it away.

Let's total it now. Slowly. Without fear as your motive. Understand it, then put fear behind you. I don't want you to escape because you are frightened. Fear fades. Understanding stays. I want you free because you see clearly and because freedom is wonderful.

What does BAD SUGAR do TO you?

Your body warns you. That warning is not moral. It is mechanical. The mechanism is there whether you brush perfectly or not, though brushing and fluoride mediate it. Why would a genuine treat or lift need to etch the body that tastes it? Put it on the ledger.

It does plenty TO your steady state. Remember yesterday's rollercoaster? "I need something RIGHT NOW." The quick and sweet. The brief lift. The fast drop. We call it the rollercoaster for a reason. Your colleagues say it. You say it. "The roller coaster." You climb, you plunge, you queue again. That is not energy. That is the cost of the last hit, relabelled as your personality. When everyone's afternoon collapses at three, no one calls it the trap. They call it life. Put it on the ledger.

It does plenty TO your freedom. Remember the Sugar Trap we named in the last chapter? The closed loop: phoney belief plus more-ishness plus repetition plus the low the last hit guarantees. Once that loop closed — when you were a child being told sweets were love and reward — it began to run itself. You did not choose the loop. You fell into it. The fact is… the loop then removes choice dose by dose, while telling you that you are choosing. Put that theft of choice on the ledger. It is the largest item. It does plenty TO your peace. The negotiation. The justification menu you carry in your head. "Once a week is fine." "I don't keep it in the house." "It's fuel not a treat." Hear your own lines? Those are not thoughts. Those are scripts the trap fed you so you would keep feeding it. Each script powers the next dose. Put the mental noise on the ledger.

Do you see how long the TO side grows when you stop hiding it?

And notice what we have not done. We have not tried to frighten you into stopping. We have not listed diseases as threats. We have named mechanisms you can verify in your own mouth, your own afternoon, your own day of ten doses. If you have diabetes, low blood sugar, an eating disorder, or a clinician-prescribed eating plan, you follow your clinician for any numbers and thresholds — this book never overrides medical guidance. For everyone else, the population guidance is simply a signpost: your day sits far above what bodies were nudged to need, and your body tells you so between hits.

Is that ledger small? Is it vague? No. It's daily. It's hourly. It's yours.

Now look across the page at the FOR side.

Still almost empty.

How can something that does so much TO you do so little FOR you? How did we ever call that a fair trade?

We called it a fair trade because we never laid the two columns side by side. We have now. Keep the paper. We will need it again.
