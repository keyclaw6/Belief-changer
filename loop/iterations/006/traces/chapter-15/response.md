### WHAT DO I EAT NOW? — THE LINE THAT SETS YOU FREE

You have just unfolded your wings. The last chapter cleared the false identity — you are not a sweet-toothed person, you were simply taught to eat like one. Does that leave a question hanging?

"What do I eat now?"

We were all taught to fear that question. Will you have to count? Measure? Live on joyless "health food" while the world keeps dessert? Will you miss sweetness itself?

No. The answer is wonderfully simple. And it starts by drawing the line cleanly, once and for all.

Remember the boxed decree from Chapter 1. When this book says **SUGAR**, take it to mean **BAD SUGAR**.

**BAD SUGAR** is all refined and added free sugars — sucrose, glucose, high-fructose corn syrup, honey and syrups added — plus junk-processed carbs where fibre and structure have been stripped. White bread. Pastries. Sweets. Biscuits. Cakes. Ice cream. Sugary drinks. Confectionery. Ultra-processed snacks. The CAPS name carries the definition in every sentence. This is what you quit. Wholly. For this, there is no healthy level other than zero.

**GOOD SUGAR / NOT-SUGAR** is not what you quit. The intrinsic sugars in whole fruit and vegetables and unrefined whole foods eaten as whole foods, and plain natural foods generally — meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, whole fruit, nuts, plain whole foods — these are not restricted. You are not quitting food. You are not quitting sweetness as a taste. You are not quitting eating itself.

Do you see? The Sweet Con wants you to hear "quit sugar" and think "quit joy, quit flavour, quit sweetness." Is that honest? Or is it the con blurring the line on purpose?

The fact is your body can make all the glucose it needs from protein and other parts of food. You do not need to import BAD SUGAR to have energy. Does that surprise you? It should reassure you. Eliminating BAD SUGAR is not deprivation. It is returning to baseline. Nature already handles sweetness gently — slowly, with fibre, with chewing, with satisfaction built in. BAD SUGAR is the crude flood that vandalises that quiet system.

The fact is population guidance has said for years to keep free sugars below 10% of energy — about fifty grams for many adults — with additional benefit below 5%. Where are we? Far above it. Is that guidance a diet to follow gram by gram? No. It is background proof that your current intake — normal a few decades ago — is far from natural. Zero BAD SUGAR is coherent with that guidance. It is not extreme. Normal is extreme.

And the fact is the sugars that feed plaque acid and drive decay come from free sugars. Your teeth know the difference. A whole apple rinses, fibrous, self-cleaning in its way. A sticky bun coats, clings, feeds acid for hours. Which one do you think your body designed teeth for? The cavity is not moral. It is a bodily stake — a warning that something is hanging around too long, too often. I do not want you to quit out of fear of the dentist. See it, then put it behind you. The body warns for a reason.

So keep the line bright. BAD SUGAR — out. Fresh whole foods — free. Sweet taste stays, via fruit and real food. Have you lost sweetness? Or have you reclaimed it?

Quite simple. Quite freeing.

### THE BODY THAT ALREADY KNOWS — AWE AT NATURE'S GUIDE

Let me install reverence where brainwashing lived. Inside the Sugar Trap we learned to distrust our bodies. We learned we need willpower, numbers, apps, rules, points. We learned our appetite is the enemy.

The fact is the opposite is true. Your body is staggeringly sophisticated.

Think of what it does without your instruction. Ten thousand taste buds that can pick a whisper of bitter poison from a mouthful. Saliva that begins breaking food before you swallow. A gut that senses fibre, fat, protein and sends quiet signals to your brain. A liver that steadies blood sugar hour by hour, making glucose as needed. Hormones that rise and fall like a tide to say "now" and "enough." Did you design any of that? Do you count it?

I call this quiet intelligence **Nature's Guide**.

We need that name. Not as a diet. Not as a guru. As an alternate compass after brainwashing goes. The Sweet Con shouted. Nature's Guide whispers. Have you been listening?

Look at how Nature handles sweetness. A peach. Fibre slows it. Chewing slows it. Sugars arrive with water, vitamins, bulk. You feel satisfaction rise and then level. You stop. No crash. No queue for more. No wrapper calling from the kitchen. Is that not elegant?

Now picture the crude flood. A glass of sugary drink or a bag of sweets. No fibre. No chewing. No signal until you have taken far too much. A spike. A surge to clear it. A dip that feels like hunger again. Is that elegant? Or is it vandalism of an elegant system?

Do you see why **Nature's Guide** feels like relief? You do not have to become a nutritionist. You do not have to count. You have to trust the compass you already own.

That compass is not a new belief I ask you to swallow blindly. Test it against your own memory. Did you ever eat fresh foods when genuinely hungry and feel comfortably satisfied, light, energetic? Did you ever eat BAD SUGAR when not hungry and feel briefly stuffed, then restless and wanting again? Which felt natural? Which felt engineered?

We were the ones who overrode nature. We flooded a subtle system with weaponised sweetness. We called the flood normal. No wonder we felt tired, foggy and drained.

Nature's Guide has been there all along. Patient. Quiet. Ready.

Will you let her lead?

### WHY FRESH FOOD NEEDS NO COMPANY — THE GATECRASHER GOES HOME

Have you noticed lonely carbs?

Take white bread. Plain crackers. A dry biscuit. A naked pastry base. Eat them alone, without spread, jam, butter, cheese, chocolate, frosting. Are they delicious? Or do they sit dry, bland, needing company to be palatable?

These are the house-party gatecrashers. They arrive uninvited — stripped of fibre and structure — and they cannot entertain on their own. They need to invite company over. They need jam. They need butter. They need chocolate spread. They need a sugary drink alongside.

Fresh foods do not behave like that. A ripe mango needs no jam. A handful of roasted nuts needs no frosting. Steamed fish with vegetables needs no sugary sauce to be satisfying. A crisp apple is a complete pleasure on its own. Do you see the test? Genuine pleasures stand alone. Gatecrashers need co-conspirators.

We proved this to ourselves in a different way. Do you remember the freshness you felt after a simple meal eaten because you were hungry — light, clear, content? And the stickiness, the cloy, the thirst after a BAD SUGAR hit eaten because the packet was there? One needs nothing added. The other needs water, needs a walk, needs a promise to be good tomorrow.

Ask the isolation question. Its only honest answer ends the argument.

If BAD SUGAR were truly delicious, why does the base it rides on taste so lonely without it?

If fresh food were deprivation, why does it satisfy without needing a fix added?

If you love taste, which taste loves you back?

This is why the promise of the next chapter lands so cleanly. You will **eat as much of your favourite fresh foods as you want, whenever you want, without counting, without willpower and without feeling deprived**. That is not a slogan. It is a description of eating with Nature's Guide instead of against her. Fresh food needs no company. It is company.

Do you want food that needs a costume to be liked? Or food that is liked as itself?

Let the gatecrasher go home. Keep the feast.

### TWO SIMPLE INSTRUMENTS — THE RAW TEST AND THE HUNGER GAUGE

Freedom needs tools, not rules. I promised you an operating manual without willpower. Here it is. Two simple instruments. No counting. No diet. Just listening.

I will give you both now. They are simple enough to use today, and you will use them for the rest of your life.

**Instrument One: The Raw Test — RT-01**

Whenever you are not sure whether you are hungry or just hearing a whisper, ask this one question:

*"Would I eat a plain raw apple, a carrot, a handful of nuts, or plain steamed veg/fish right now?"*

Listen for the honest answer.

If **yes** — that is genuine hunger. Eat. Eat your favourite fresh foods — fruit, vegetables, nuts, plain whole foods, a proper meal — as much as you want until you feel comfortably satisfied. No clock. No limit. No guilt. Your body is asking for nourishment. Give it freely.

If **no** — and only BAD SUGAR appeals — that is not hunger. That is the Nibbler whispering — that faint, empty, slightly restless, slightly insecure craving — plus the Sweet Con selling it as "a genuine treat or lift" or "to get me through." You are not hungry. You are hearing the trap. Do not count calories; let the answer guide you.

Do you see how kind this is? It does not forbid. It distinguishes. It sorts true appetite from conditioned wanting without willpower. Have you ever used a test so gentle?

Short. True. Use it.

The test works because fresh foods satisfy hunger and BAD SUGAR does not. The gatecrasher needs company. Hunger wants food.

**Instrument Two: The Hunger Gauge — HG-01**

Think of hunger as a dial from 0 to 20.

0 = stuffed, uncomfortable, overfull. 5 = not hungry. 10 = neutral, comfortable, neither hungry nor full. 12–15 = clearly hungry and food would be satisfying. 20 = ravenous, shaky, urgent.

Use it like this: eat when you reach about **12–15** — you feel clearly hungry and food sounds good. Stop when you return to about **8–10** — satisfied, light, energetic, comfortable. Not stuffed. Not empty. Content.

This is not numbers to track day-to-day. It is a listening aid, not a prescription. No diary. No points. You glance inward. You ask, "Where am I?" You eat or wait accordingly. Do you need an app to know when you need the toilet? No. You learned to notice. Hunger is the same. Nature's Guide already calibrated the gauge.

And a vital safety boundary, spoken plainly and warmly: if you have diabetes, hypoglycemia, an eating disorder, or a clinician-prescribed eating pattern, follow your clinician's guidance, not this gauge. This compass never overrides medical advice. Your clinician knows your specifics. I never will. Honour their plan. Keep this book's compass inside their boundaries.

Do you feel the difference? No external rule shouting "you must." Just a quiet question: "Are you hungry?" And a quiet answer. **All you have to do is follow all the instructions** — and this is one of them.

Picture your first fresh-food meal with this guide. You wait until you feel that clear 12–15. You choose something you truly like — crisp, bright, real. You eat until that light 8–10. You stop. No calculation. No negotiation. You get on with your life. Does that feel like a diet? Or does it feel like remembering?

Two instruments. One compass.

Will you try them and see?

### THE SEATBELT AND THE OPEN DOOR — THE BLIP AND THE BONUS

Two worries arrive at this exact point. Both are the Sweet Con trying to keep a foothold. Let me remove both now, cleanly, so freedom is not a void and the mind is guarded.

**Worry One: "What if I slip and have a trace?"**

Here is the margin-for-error doctrine. Your body can cope with an occasional, accidental blip of BAD SUGAR — trace sugars in a sauce, a mouthful at a dinner, a bite you did not expect — without harm. Your mind cannot cope with entertaining the belief again.

Do you understand the split? A blip revives nothing physically. It only becomes a trap again if you re-enshrine the Big Monster belief — the Sweet Con whispering "that was nice / I needed that / just one is fine." Guard the belief, not the behaviour. Do not test the margin. The seatbelt is there for accidents, not for driving erratically.

Think of a seatbelt. Does its existence mean you should drive into walls to test it? No. You drive well. You rejoice it is there if you hit a bump. You do not use it as permission to crash. So with BAD SUGAR: live freely, eat fresh foods, use the Raw Test. If a little finds you by accident, smile at how little it matters — and do not deliberately repeat it. Never deliberately test the margin. Testing is the trap knocking in the costume of curiosity.

Have you ever worried that one accidental crumb would ruin everything? It does not. What ruins is the decision to call the crumb wonderful. Keep the decision dead and you stay free.

**Worry Two: "Do I have to quit alcohol or dairy or other indulgences too?"**

No. This book is not a stop-alcohol manual. It is not a stop-dairy manual. You are here to get free from BAD SUGAR. Alcohol and other adjacent indulgences are NOT required to quit BAD SUGAR.

Does that not honour your autonomy? I am not taking your choices away. I am returning one.

If you want the upgrade — if, once free, you feel curious about widening that freedom — the pointer lives in the appendix. It is an exciting option, not a condition. You can read it later, or never. Your freedom from BAD SUGAR stands complete without it. The door is open. You do not have to walk through it.

Do you see how light this is? Nothing to count. Nothing extra to give up. No new identity to build. Just the line, the compass, two instruments, and a seatbelt you hope never to use.

The Sweet Con has told you freedom is a void — "what will I eat? what will I do? who will I be at birthdays?" Is that true? Or is freedom the return of appetite, taste, and choice — with tools that fit in one pocket?

Let me ask you trap questions. Their only honest answer is relief.

If BAD SUGAR were needed for energy, why does your body make glucose without it?

If quitting it were deprivation, why does eating fresh foods until satisfied feel comfortable and satisfying?

If this were a diet, where is the counting?

Where?

We have reached the climax of this chapter. Every demolition before this cleared the false benefits. The identity excuse is gone. Now the positive authority stands in its place.

I told you earlier you would receive one instruction per chapter at its peak. Here is yours. It carries the compass and the instruments inside it.

> **9. LET NATURE'S GUIDE BE YOUR COMPASS. Eat as much of your favourite fresh foods as you want, whenever you want, using the Raw Test and your body's signals — not a diet.**

Read that again slowly. It is a promise, not a threat.

Eat as much of your favourite fresh foods as you want, whenever you want, without counting, without willpower and without feeling deprived. Let the Raw Test distinguish hunger from the Nibbler's whisper plus the Sweet Con's sales pitch. Let the Hunger Gauge remind you to notice 12–15 and to pause at 8–10. Change nothing else in your life. Inhabit old contexts confidently to prove freedom. Do not avoid meals or people. Live.

Feel the rationale warm and firm. Why this instruction? Because willpower needs rules to break. Freedom needs a guide to follow. The Willpower Method gave you limits, forbidden fruit, and a finish line that never arrived. Nature's Guide gives you abundance, distinction, and immediate satisfaction. Which one feels like freedom? Which one is?

Sweetness remains — in fruit, in roasted vegetables, in a ripe, fragrant piece of food eaten when you are truly hungry. What leaves is BAD SUGAR — the con that sold you engineered intensity as pleasure.

Do you remember the gatecrasher? Fresh food needs no company. Nature's Guide brings the only company you need: appetite.

Eat if you are hungry — freely, joyfully. Wait if you are not.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions. This one included. Will you?
