Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

*You need no more teaching to stay out — the sweetness is yours again.*

## IF YOU OPENED HERE FIRST

Put this page down and start on the first page.

These lines are saved reminders for someone already out. They are not the method. Read alone, they sound like orders. Read after the journey, they are photographs of ground you have walked.

We all wanted a shortcut once. We turned to the last page hoping for the trick that would spare us the looking. There is no trick on the last page. The looking was the way out. The list below only holds its meaning because you have seen each point prove itself in your own mouth, your own belly, your own day.

If your cupboard is still full and your mind still glows at the thought of a packet blast, you have lost nothing by closing this now. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Start at the beginning. Read with an open mind and a full cupboard. Let each page do its work. I will meet you back here when the table is set.

Did you ever learn to swim from a list pinned to a fence?

Did one swallowed lesson ever quiet a hunt that lived in the belly?

When did reading the end ever do the walking?

Your own life answers. Go to the start and walk it.

## WHAT YOU CARRY

You carry no rules for hard days and no armour for birthdays. You carry sight.

We lived years inside timetable talk. We woke coated and called it hunger. We sagged at three and called it need. We rustled at ten and called it love. We called the film pleasure and the hunt choice. Mind supplied the glow while tongue reported almost nothing.

Ask cold from this table.

What did one more dose ever add to a laugh that was already warm?

What did one more blast ever build in a day that was already whole?

When did a coated mouth ever taste more than a clean one?

Your own hours settled it. Blast never built. Blast billed. Film, thirst, second call, debate, dull head, broken sleep. That was the wage, every time, for the rest of your life hooked.

See what stands now, plain in daylight. Bread smelling like bread. Soup holding salt and heat. Apple juice running sharp and clean. Water tasting like something. Hunger knocking clean from the belly, meeting food, closing with a clear bell till next knock. Company talking through to the end with no rustle talking over it. Teeth smooth at ten. Head level at morning. That is the wage now, every day, for the rest of your life free.

If a clinician guides what you eat, follow their advice first — this book changes belief alone.

I stood where you stand on the night after my vow, hands a little quick from old hours, eyes catching shine out of habit. I ate seated. I washed the bowl. I stood at the window with the cup warm in both palms. Nothing in the mouth pulled. Nothing in the hands reached. That quiet told more than talk ever told.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That sentence is not a rule to hold with effort. It is a report of how the body already works when timetable talk falls silent. Hunger leads. Satisfaction closes. Instinct, met daily, steadies within days.

When any sweet memory crosses the mind — wrapper shine, candles, cinema row — answer it with one joyful thought that replaces the old talk whole. Say to yourself with a grin — BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it in the shop when packets shout. Say it at the table when plates pass. Say it on the sofa when old hours ping. Not as a shield. As a report that delights you each time it fires.

## THE EATER YOU WERE

Here is the last sight, and it is the sweetest.

You have lost no sweetness. Sweetness was taken from you, dose by dose, and has now been handed back.

Think of a child with a peach. Juice down the chin. Eyes elsewhere. Half the peach eaten, half forgotten, dropped to run off to play. No counting. No debate. No glow laid over hunger before hunger spoke. Stop when enough. Run when running calls.

We were that eater before packets taught timetable. We learned to eat blast before hunger, to drink sweet beside meals, to lay dessert glow over love before love spoke. We learned to call film taste and hunt need. The Trap taught us to mistrust knock and bell and to obey clock and packet. That schooling thinned every meal to a hunt for the next.

This was never about quitting. It was about growing back into the eater you were.

The peach still lives in the bowl. The bread still smells like bread. The belly still knocks clean and closes clear. You did not learn a new way to eat. You shed a loud way that never belonged. What remains is yours by birth, noticed again, savoured again, stopped at satisfied again.

So close the book. Put it on the shelf. Photograph the list if you wish. Then turn to the next living thing.

Hear the cover shut. That soft thump is not an ending to mourn. Slide the spine in between the cookbooks where it belongs, title out, work done. Lift the phone, take the photograph of the list, square and plain, and slip the phone into your pocket. You do not need to study it. You do not need to rehearse it. You do not need to keep this book open on the counter while you live. The list rides along the way a pocket photograph rides along — looked at when you want the grin, forgotten while you get on with the hour.

Walk into the kitchen. The room is as you left it. Cloth folded. Board wiped. Light on. Put the kettle on its stand. Lay out what hunger asks for when it knocks. Bread. Soup. Apples. Cheese. Water cool in a tall glass. Nothing measured. Nothing timed. Nothing consulted.

When hunger knocks you sit down. You eat seated, chewing through to the end of each mouthful. Talk goes on around you. You answer, you laugh, you pass the plate along. When the belly closes with its clear bell you stop. You do not open the book to check if you stopped right. You do not count back through chapters to give yourself permission. You push the plate back half an inch, drink the water, stand.

That is what carrying sight means. No second lesson runs underneath. No voice whispers to re-read before you swallow. The mouth stays quiet because the seeing stays whole, not because a page holds it quiet. You rinse the bowl. You wipe the board. You shake the cloth out of the window. Hands do the next living thing while the day ticks on — post to sort, bed to make, shoes to tie, door to open for air.

If a sweet thought drifts past in that hour — shine, rustle, old eight o'clock — you do not reach for the shelf. You grin in the kitchen with wet hands and think — BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! — and the thought passes the way weather passes. The list in your pocket stays in your pocket. Freedom does not leak out between readings. It holds because you see, and seeing does not need re-teaching.

Make the bed. Rinse the cup. Slice the bread thick. Pour the soup hot. Sit down among your people. Talk whole through to the end.

Get on with enjoying your life.
