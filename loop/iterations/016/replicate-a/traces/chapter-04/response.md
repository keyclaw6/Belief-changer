Chapter 4
THE ENERGY LIE

IN THIS CHAPTER — The 3pm desk under strip light, The humming vending machine, The sticky mouth and heavy eyelids, The rollercoaster you pay to ride twice

*That lift is not fuel — it is a loan that creates the crash.*

### THE VOICE AT THREE O'CLOCK

You know the voice. It arrives at the same time, with the same reason, in the same tired words.

"I need sugar to get me through."

"To get me through the afternoon."

"It gives me energy. It helps me concentrate. It's fuel."

We have all said it. We say it in the same dialect because we were taught it. You sit at the desk at three o'clock, the screen still bright, the air still warm, your eyelids starting to feel heavy, and the voice says, I just need something quick and sweet to get me through. Not a feast. Just a fix. Just enough to function.

I want you to hear that voice for what it is.

It is not you reasoning. It is the Sweet Con speaking in your voice. It says fuel, and you believe it, because you remember the twenty-second lift after the biscuit, the chocolate, the cola. You remember the buzz. That memory feels like proof. Of course it was fuel, you think. I felt it work.

Did you?

Let me ask you plainly. If BAD SUGAR were fuel, real fuel, why would you be more tired an hour later than you were before you took it? If it gave you energy, why would you need another dose to get through the same afternoon tomorrow, and the next day, and every day for the rest of the afternoon you can remember? Real fuel does not work like that. Petrol does not make the car go slower after the first mile so you need more petrol to get home.

The fact is, you are not describing fuel. You are describing relief.

That distinction changes everything.

### SOMETHING QUICK AND SWEET

Listen to how you describe it when you are honest.

"I would have something quick and sweet but now know that will only give a short term lift which will inevitably be followed by a fast drop."

That is not my sentence. It is yours. I have heard it in offices, in kitchens, in queues, from people exactly like you who live the same yo-yo. Quick and sweet. Short term lift. Fast drop. The roller coaster in your own words.

We call the lift energy. We are wrong. What you feel is the Imp being quietened for a few minutes. The Sugar Imp has been whining since the last dose wore off — a mild, empty, slightly insecure feeling you have learned to call hunger or tiredness or lack of concentration. You feed it BAD SUGAR and the whining stops. For a few minutes the noise stops and you call the silence energy.

Then what happens?

Your body does what a healthy body does. It deals with the surge. Insulin does its work, the sugar moves, the blood chemistry shifts, and the lift falls away. The sticky mouth comes. The heavy eyelids come. The faint irritability comes, the sense that you cannot quite focus unless you have another. We blame the afternoon. We blame the meeting. We blame age or sleep or character. We say, I hit a slump, I need sugar.

It is the other way around.

You did not have a slump and then need sugar. You had sugar and then got the slump. The dose created the dip it now claims to cure.

Ask yourself a simple isolation. Do small children need BAD SUGAR to concentrate at three o'clock? Do you need BAD SUGAR to concentrate after a good night's sleep and a satisfying meal of real food, on a morning when you have not had any? No. You concentrate beautifully when the Imp is not complaining. The afternoon concentration you credit to the biscuit was your own concentration returning for a few minutes because you silenced the Imp that BAD SUGAR itself had wound up.

That lift was never a genuine pleasure or treat. It was relief from discomfort BAD SUGAR put there.

### THE ROLLERCOASTER YOU PAY TO RIDE

I want you to ride it once more, but this time with your eyes open.

It is 3:02. Strip light. The vending machine hums at the end of the corridor like an insect. You have been sitting still and your legs are a little restless, your mouth a little dry. Someone opens a packet. The smell reaches you before thought does. Want fires — not hunger, want — a quick, bright tug behind the breastbone that says, quick and sweet, get me through.

You tell yourself, just one. You get up. The machine glows. You press. The packet drops. The first bite is sweet and loud, sugar on the tongue, then the jaw moves, then the swallow, then for a breath you feel brighter, lighter, as if someone turned the light up a fraction.

Notice what else arrives with it. The sweetness coats. The mouth turns sticky. The throat asks for water. Ten minutes later the brightness is gone and the eyelids are heavier than before. Fifteen minutes later you are staring at the same email and rereading the same sentence. Twenty minutes later the same tug returns, a little stronger, a little more insistent, saying again, quick and sweet.

That is the ride. We get on at the dip, we pay a dose to go up a few feet, we are dropped lower than where we started, we pay again to be lifted again. The machine is not giving you height. It is selling you back the ground you lost on the last drop.

You were taught to call that energy. Real energy does not spike and crash. Real energy is steady, quiet, available. You know real energy — you have felt it on days when you ate a proper meal when hungry, drank water, moved, and simply got on with things without thinking about food at all. That steady hum is body's own. The rollercoaster is not more energy. It is borrowed light with interest, and the interest is tiredness.

The Sweet Con tells you, you need sugar to function. Look again. Who is functioning before the dose? You, dulled by the last dose. Who is functioning after the dose? You, briefly relieved of that dullness, then dulled again, forever paying to get back to the normal a person who never ate BAD SUGAR lives on for free.

You never rise above normal. You only pay to get back to it for a few minutes.

### WHAT THE LIFT COSTS

The fact is, at population level, a high intake of added sugar is linked with a higher load on heart and vessels — liver overload, raised pressure, chronic inflammation, appetite control bypassed so you take more without feeling satisfied. That is a flat, frightening truth about the tissue tax of what you call a lift.

I do not want you to stop because you are frightened. Fear keeps the trap alive by making the trap feel like a master you must obey. I want you to stop because you see there is nothing to lose and because the relief of escape is marvellous. This is not a sentence on you, and if you have diabetes, take prescribed medicine, have a history of eating distress, or any medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before you change what you eat.

And the crash itself?

The fact is, after a sugar surge your body can over-correct and you can feel a real dip — shaky, flat, foggy, needing something. That is real physiology. But in healthy people who are not diabetic, everyday slumps very often happen without any measured low blood sugar at all, and true clinical low blood sugar is rare. You do not need to diagnose yourself. You do not need to chase a number. What you can trust is your own lived pattern: quick and sweet, short term lift, fast drop. That sentence you wrote yourself tells the truth without a blood test. The lift IS the mechanism that produces the crash you blame on needing more. The rollercoaster is not proof you need fuel. It is proof the fuel was false.

You have been paying the body with the very thing that tires the body, and calling the brief pause in the tire a gift.

Once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

### IT'S THE OTHER WAY AROUND

You say, it helps me concentrate.

Look. You concentrate despite BAD SUGAR, not because of it. When you give the body real food when you are truly hungry, when you drink water, when you move and breathe, concentration returns on its own. The biscuit did not give it. It quietened the Imp that was stealing it.

You say, I need it to get me through.

Through what? Through the dip that last dose made. Without that dose you would not be in the dip. We say to get me through as if the afternoon were a mountain. The afternoon is not a mountain. The Sugar Trap built the mountain.

So let us land the verdict plainly.

BAD SUGAR does not give you energy. It takes your natural steady energy and sells a spoonful of it back to you for twenty minutes, then takes more.

It does not lift you above normal. It drops you below normal and rents you normal back.

It is the other way around.

If that is true, what is there to want? Who wants to keep paying to be returned to where they already were, with interest charged in tiredness? Who wants to keep riding a ride that ends lower every time and call that fuel?

You are not deprived of energy without BAD SUGAR. You are deprived of energy by it. Your body's own steady hum is waiting, already there, marvellous in its quiet competence, ready to carry you through the afternoon without a single quick and sweet being needed. We will meet that hum properly in the next chapter, as a lived meal, not a lecture. For now it is enough to know: the energy story was the Sweet Con's cleverest line, and it is false.

You do not need a fix to function. You need to stop being fixed.

NEVER THINK I CAN'T HAVE BAD SUGAR — THINK GREAT, I'M FREE
There is nothing to give up.

Say it the moment the old thought flickers: I can't have BAD SUGAR. Catch it and replace it instantly with the truth: great, I'm free. Not I must not. Not I wish I could. Great. I'm. Free. The thought you rehearse is the belief you keep. The Imp whispers feed me; the Sweet Con translates it as I need fuel; you answer with freedom and the whisper starves.

Do you feel the switch? From I need it to get me through to I was made to feel I needed it so I would pay with tiredness for a lift that was never energy at all. From deprivation to relief. That is the whole move of this chapter, won now and not later. We will not fight the afternoon. We will see it plain.

SUMMARY

- The afternoon craving for something quick and sweet is not proof BAD SUGAR gives energy; it is proof the last dose created the slump it claims to cure.
- The short term lift followed by a fast drop is the lived pattern of a loan with interest, not of real fuel.
- Real energy is steady and bodily; the sweet hit only briefly relieves a low BAD SUGAR made, never raising you above the normal of someone who never feeds the trap.
- At population level the lift carries a bodily load, and everyday slumps are not clinical low blood sugar; the trap is exposed without fear and without diagnosis.
- Freedom is thinking GREAT, I'M FREE the moment the old thought appears, because there is nothing to give up.
