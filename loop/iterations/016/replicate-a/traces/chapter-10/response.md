Chapter 10
FEAR AND THE WILLPOWER METHOD

IN THIS CHAPTER — The prison door standing open, The released prisoner hovering in the corridor, The braggers and the whingers at the same party, The method with no finish line

*Fear of failing and fear of succeeding are the same rope, and the trap is holding both ends.*

### THE TWO FEARS THAT HOLD YOU STILL

You can feel it as a tug-of-war of fear.

On one side, the fear of failure. I will try and fail again, so why humiliate myself. You remember the diets, the rules, the Monday restarts, the box emptied after the promise of one biscuit. The shame afterwards was worse than the craving. So part of you says do not try.

On the other side, the fear of success. If I do escape, who am I without this. What will evenings be, what will comfort be, what will I do with my hands, with stress, with celebration, what if life is bleaker without my treat. Part of you says do not succeed.

We were taught to think these are two different fears. They are not. They are the same trap pulling both ropes.

Failure fear says you are too weak to get free. Success fear says you are someone who needs BAD SUGAR to be yourself. Both tell you that freedom is unsafe and staying trapped is safer. The fact is both stories were installed. We all learned young that sweet food is love and reward and fuel, and we then proved it to ourselves by feeling low and reaching and feeling briefly better — never noticing that the low was made by the last reach.

You are not afraid of freedom. You are afraid of what the Sweet Con told you freedom would be.

And I want to answer the voice that pipes up right here, because it will, and it should, and I welcome it.

It says, this sounds too easy. This is just talk. You are trying to brainwash me out of something I enjoy, and when I finish this chapter I will be exactly the same and then I will have failed again.

I hear that. I felt it too. If a book promised you could walk out of a prison you have lived in for years, easily, immediately and permanently, you would be right to raise an eyebrow. The fact is you are not being asked to believe me. You are being asked to look. Look at the film-night bowl we just stripped. Look at the 3pm roller coaster. Look at the hands reaching for a pleasure that kept fading as they reached. If that was a genuine pleasure or treat, why did the pleasure get thinner with each bite and leave a sticky mouth and a dip? That is not opinion. That is your own lived evidence, once you watch it plain.

And if you have a medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before changing what you eat — I want you free, not frightened, and your body looked after while your mind gets free.

So we will not argue about belief. We will look at the mechanism that makes fear feel true, and then watch it collapse.

### THE PRISON DOOR STANDING OPEN

Picture a prison you have not seen from the outside.

You wake in a cell. The door is closed. For years you have been told the door is locked, that you need a key called willpower, that you must push and sweat and earn the right to be free. Every past attempt was pushing that door with your shoulder while someone on the other side quietly held it shut — and that someone was not you.

That is the Willpower Method.

The Willpower Method tells you BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure you are sacrificing, then asks you to resist it with grit while feeling deprived. It counts days. It calls it one day at a time. It tells you to be strong and white-knuckle past triggers and to mourn the treat you lost. With the Willpower Method there is no finish line. You are never free, you are only abstaining today, hoping you will have enough discipline tomorrow. If you fail, it says you were weak. If you suffer, it says that is normal, quitting is meant to be hard.

No wonder you failed. No wonder we all failed on it. That method keeps the belief intact and then demands you overpower the belief with character. No one can do that for the rest of your life. The failure was never yours. It was the method's.

Now see what is true. The door was never locked. The Sugar Imp behind it is tiny, a faint, empty gnawing that is mild but well-defined and already starving. The lab work that watched that gnawing found it peaks in the first two to five days if you stop feeding it and then passes. Not weeks. Not months. Days. A brief hump, mild and short-lived, that dies if you do not refed it.

You are not going to feel nothing at all — I will not promise zero sensation, because your body is real and honesty matters. You may feel a flat, slightly restless dip for a short while, the Imp's death throes as it misses its fix. That feeling is not a command. It is not you needing BAD SUGAR. It is a little parasite going quiet. And you do not need to be brave to outlast a few days of a mild hump. You only need to stop feeding it and to stop believing its advert.

Look again at the door. In this picture you are already standing, the cell empty behind you, the corridor quiet, sunlight out beyond. The door is open a hand's width. You can see the light on the floor. You are afraid to walk out because you have been told you will fail in the corridor or that you will miss the cell if you leave it. That tug-of-war of fear is not inside you. It belongs to the trap. Both ropes are being pulled by the same story.

When you see that, fear does not need to be fought. It drops. The fact is fear-of-failure and fear-of-success are not two verdicts on you. They are two threats from the same con, keeping you in the cell one day longer. For the rest of your life is not a sentence in that cell. For the rest of your life free beyond that door is the other way that same phrase can feel, and it is wonderful.

### THE RELEASED PRISONER HOVERING

Now picture the released prisoner.

The gate has swung wide. He steps out, free. And then he hovers by the wall. He paces a little circle by the gate. He looks back at the cell not because he loves it but because habit lives in the legs. His body still walks the old path. His mind, for a day or two, still listens for the bell that called him to the bowl in the middle, the vending machine hum at three.

If you watch him you might think, look, he still needs prison. But he does not. He needs a moment for habit to catch up with freedom. That hovering is not proof he belongs inside. It is proof he just left.

That is what you may feel after you stop. Not a roaring withdrawal, not a lifelong void. A mild, brief hump of flatness that comes and goes while the belief is already dead. If you feed the Imp again, you re-create the low you are trying to escape. If you do not, it starves. In either case the feeling is small. The story about the feeling is huge. The Sweet Con says, this unease means you must have BAD SUGAR, you cannot cope, you are deprived. The truth is the unease is the Imp dying and the old cue firing at the sight of wrappers and smells before hunger has any say.

I know the fear that says, what if I am the exception, what if my case is stronger. You are not. I was the one who ate the cake from the bin and called it a treat. I was the one who said I don't keep it in the house so I am in control, then bought it on the way home. If the trap could keep anyone, it could keep me. It kept us because we believed its script, not because we were broken. When the belief changed, the want collapsed and the small physical hump was easy to smile at.

You do not need to test this by suffering. You do not need to change from fear. The fact is the bodily load BAD SUGAR puts on a heart and on teeth is real, and you already sense it, and that is not why I am asking you to leave. I am asking you to leave because there is nothing to keep. The scare is true and then it is set down. Freedom is the gain. Relief is now.

### BRAGGERS AND WHINGERS

While you were in the Willpower Method's prison, you met its two spokespeople. They both keep you trapped.

The bragger says, I stopped with sheer willpower, you can too, just be disciplined. He makes a virtue of suffering, counts days, polishes the sacrifice story. He is proud of not feeding the Imp while still believing the Imp is a pleasure. He envies you while you still dose, and wants you to envy him.

The whinger says, I tried to stop and it was agony, cravings were terrible, I white-knuckled for months and went back. He makes suffering proof that freedom is impossible. He wants company back in the cell.

Both are on the Willpower Method. Both kept the Sweet Con alive. One brags about resisting a pleasure that does not exist; the other whinges about the pain of depriving himself of that same fiction. Both miscount the problem as physical grit. Both erase the fact that the physical part is small and the trap is belief.

You do not need their story. Their struggle is not your guide.

See them with pity, not envy. The bragger is still pulling the rope, calling the strain virtue. The whinger was pulled back in and calls that proof. You are about to put the rope down entirely.

And this is where your best objection does its work for you. If you think, I have strong willpower, I should have been able to stop before — hear what that proves. It takes a strong will to persist in feeding a trap that goes against all your instincts, your body, your real food and your pride, day after day, year after year, while hiding packets and managing shame. The willful person is not weak-willed. Your persistence proves strength. That strength, applied to the wrong method, kept you trapped. Applied to truth, it makes escape immediate.

You do not need more willpower. You need a truer picture. In that picture there is nothing to resist.

At the climax of this chapter I give you two firewalls so the picture stays clear. These are not tips. They are orders because the method works only if you follow all of them.

7. IGNORE ANYONE WHO STOPPED BY THE WILLPOWER METHOD
Their struggle is not your guide.

8. AVOID THE INFLUENCE OF OTHER USERS WHILE YOU READ
Brainwashing is contagious.

Keep your mind sealed while you finish this book. Let no other user's craving, no brag, no whinge, no offer of one more fix, rewrite the belief that is already changing. You would not ask a prisoner still inside to guide you out. You would not sit in the canteen while reading the map out. Give yourself these last pages of quiet and you give the truth time to settle as your own.

When you see the Willpower Method for what it is — a second trap with no finish line that keeps the Sweet Con alive and calls the strain noble — fear loses its foothold. Fear of failing was fear of the method failing you again. Fear of succeeding was fear of losing a pleasure you never had. Both ropes fall from your hands the moment you stop pulling.

You are not hovering because you need the cell. You are hovering because you just stepped free and your feet remember the old pacing. Smile at that pacing. Rejoice that the enemy is dying, not that a friend is lost. Pity the queue at the bright pharmacy aisle, do not envy it. You are not about to be deprived. You are about to be relieved.

SUMMARY

- Fear of failure and fear of success are not two truths about you; they are the same trap pulling both ends of the same rope.
- The Willpower Method is a second trap with no finish line; it keeps the belief that BAD SUGAR is a genuine pleasure and then asks you to resist it with grit.
- The physical part of the trap is mild but well-defined and brief — a short hump of a few days that starves if not fed, not a lifelong sentence.
- Hovering after release is habit, not need; it is the old path echoing, not proof you belong inside.
- Strong will that kept you persisting in the trap proves strength, not weakness, and makes escape immediate once the belief changes.
