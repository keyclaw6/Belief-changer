Chapter 1
THE INVITATION

IN THIS CHAPTER — The kitchen at night, The shelf of failed diets, What BAD SUGAR means in this book, A quiet confidence trick on a bright street, The contract for how you will read

*You do not need willpower to be free of BAD SUGAR — you need to see it clearly for what it is.*

### YOU EXPECTED ANOTHER LECTURE

I know what you are thinking as you open this book.

Another diet. Another set of rules. Another person about to tell you that you eat too much sugar, that you should know better, that if you were just a little stronger you would have sorted this by now.

I understand why you think that. We have all tried everything.

We have counted calories and points. We have done the thirty days with no sweets, the January with no biscuits, the week where we were very good and then the evening where we were not. We have hidden the packet, thrown the packet away, bought the smaller packet, promised we would only have it at weekends. We have stood in the kitchen at ten at night telling ourselves this is the last one, and then had another.

And each time we told ourselves the same thing: next time I will be stronger.

I am not here to tell you to be stronger. I am not here to give you another set of rules to fail at. The fact is, strength was never the problem.

I was where you are. I grazed and binged most days. I told myself it was a treat, that I needed it to get through the afternoon, that without it life would be a little greyer and a little harder. I knew it was doing me no good and I still wanted it. I tried to cut down. I tried to be moderate. I tried to be good. Nothing lasted, and each failure made me feel more ashamed and more certain that the fault was in me.

Then something changed. Not through willpower. Through understanding. I saw the trap I was in, and once I saw it I walked out. It was not a struggle. It was relief. It happened easily, immediately and permanently, and it has stayed that way because the belief that kept me trapped is gone.

I tell you that at the very start for one reason: so you know what is possible. Not for someone else. For you.

Too good to be true? That was my reaction too when I first heard it. I thought, sugar is not like that, and even if it is, nothing is easy. I thought the person telling me had not understood my life — my stress, my family, my evenings, my cravings.

I hear that doubt, and I welcome it. Keep it with you for a few chapters. Do not push it away. Bring your scepticism to the page and test what I say against your own experience. That is how you will know it is true.

This book asks only one thing that is different from every diet you have tried: it does not ask you to stop yet.

### WHAT I MEAN WHEN I SAY SUGAR

Before we go any further, let me make one thing absolutely clear, because this confusion keeps people trapped for years.

> When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR = added and refined sugar, processed junk carbs and starchy carbs engineered to spike and crash. GOOD CARBS and natural sweetness in whole foods, fruit and vegetables are not BAD SUGAR and stay on the menu.

That box matters. Hold it.

BAD SUGAR is not the sweetness in an apple. It is not the carbs that nature put into vegetables, potatoes in their whole form, fruit, plain food you cook. Those stay. You do not need to weigh them, fear them, or count them. Your body knows what to do with them.

BAD SUGAR is the added stuff. The white sugar in biscuits, chocolate, sweets, cakes, pastries, ice cream. The sugar hidden in bread, sauces, cereals, low-fat yoghurts, fizzy drinks and juices that are made to taste innocent. The white starches engineered to hit you fast — the crisps, the white baguettes, the junk carbs that dissolve to sugar almost before you swallow. They were designed to spike you and then drop you.

There is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero. That sounds strict only until you see what it really is. We will look at that together, without panic and without lectures. For now, just keep the line clean: when I write BAD SUGAR in this book, I mean that engineered, refined, added hit. When I write real food, I mean the food your body actually recognises.

You do not need to decide today what you will eat tomorrow. You only need to keep the definition clear while you read. The rest will sort itself once the belief changes.

And a quiet word, because it matters: this book is about escaping the BAD SUGAR trap for the general adult reader. It is not medical advice and it does not replace your clinician. If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, have a history of disordered eating, or any medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before changing what you eat. If you need help with eating distress, contact a qualified professional or support in your country. I say that once, plainly, so we can get on with the real job — changing how you see BAD SUGAR.

### THE PROMISE I MAKE YOU

Let me tell you what this book will do, and what it will not do.

It will not demand willpower. It will not ask you to fight yourself, to white-knuckle your way past the shop, to use grit to get through the evening. Every past attempt that asked you to do that failed for the same reason: the method was wrong, not you. We will give that method a name later. For now, just know it is not here.

It will not ask you to feel deprived. It will not tell you to mourn a pleasure and be brave about the loss. There is no loss. That is the whole point you will come to feel.

What it will do is change the belief that makes BAD SUGAR look like a treat, a reward, a lift. At the moment you believe it gives you something wonderful and without it you would be left wanting. That belief is the trap. While you hold it, of course you crave it. Of course you feel torn. Anyone would.

This book is counter-brainwashing. It simply shows you — plainly, kindly, and with total certainty — that the belief is false. Once you see it, the desire collapses on its own. You do not have to resist a desire that is not there.

That is why I can promise you this with a clear conscience: you can get free easily, immediately and permanently.

Read that again. Not with effort over months. Not one day at a time forever counting how long since your last dose. Easily, immediately and permanently — because the change happens in how you see BAD SUGAR, and that change happens the moment you see it.

You may think: easy sounds impossible. I thought so too. I had failed too many times to believe in easy. But think for a moment what easy actually means here. It does not mean you are flippant or that you have not suffered. It means you stop doing something you no longer want to do. It takes no willpower whatsoever to avoid eating something you do not want.

That feeling — relief that you no longer do it — is the destination. Whenever you think about BAD SUGAR you will feel wonderful to be free, not miserable that you cannot have it. Behaviour then changes on its own, because you are not forcing it. You are not sacrificing. You are escaping.

And because I know you have bought books before that promised a lot and measured progress in shame, let me put the risk the right way round. If what I say in these pages is wrong, what have you lost? A few hours of reading. If it is right, what have you gained? Your freedom, your energy, your peace around food, your evenings back. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. There is no downside to reading with an open mind and seeing for yourself.

I do not want you to believe me because I say so. I want you to test everything I say against your own life, your own kitchen, your own 3 p.m. slump, your own film night on the sofa. We will do that chapter by chapter. You will be the judge, and you will find you have already lived the proof.

### THE CONFIDENCE TRICK

Let me show you how you got here without ever deciding to get here.

Imagine you are walking down a bright street. A polite, well-dressed person stops you with a smile and offers you something. They tell you it will make life sweeter. That it is a treat. That everyone enjoys it. That it will pick you up when you are tired and reward you when you have been good. They give you a taste for free. It is very sweet. Then they tell you you can have more whenever you want — from every shop, on every shelf, in every advertisement with happy families and laughing friends.

You did not ask to be hooked. You just accepted a gift on false information.

A confidence trick works exactly like that. The trickster does not need to force you. He needs to misinform you. He sells you phoney information — that the thing is a pleasure, a support, a friend — and you, being trusting and normal, believe him. You choose the first taste because you think it is a treat. After that, the trap itself starts to do the choosing.

This is important: you were not foolish. You were conned. Warmly and deliberately conned by an industry that employs armies of people to make BAD SUGAR look like love and to make it available everywhere at the perfect sweetness. And you were conned by all of us who believed the same story — parents, friends, magazines, the whole culture that says you deserve a treat and sugar is that treat.

If that sounds harsh on the trap, good. I am warm to you and I am harsh to the trap. You deserve that.

Think how this trick removes choice without you noticing. If BAD SUGAR really were a genuine pleasure you freely chose, would you need a book to help you stop? Would you feel uneasy after you have had it? Would you hide the packet, or tell yourself you will not buy it tomorrow, or promise you will only have one and then find the box empty?

We tell ourselves we choose this. We say, I could stop if I wanted to. But the test is simple: if you truly wanted to stop, would you need to use willpower to stop wanting it?

The confidence trick answers that. You chose to try it because you were told it was a pleasure. You keep going because the trick made you believe you would be deprived without it. That belief is the only thing that keeps your hand moving to the bowl.

You have never been weak. You have been misled. That matters, because weak people need willpower. Misled people need clear sight. Once you see the trick, you do not need to fight it — you simply stop being fooled by it.

We went into this trap together — you, me, all of us who grew up with sweets as love on birthdays and puddings as reward for eating the rest. We will walk out of it together, with our eyes open.

### HOW TO READ THIS BOOK SO IT WORKS

I will keep this simple. There are three instructions for how to read. They are not decorations. They are the method. All you have to do is follow them, and the rest takes care of itself.

If you follow them, the promise holds. If you do not, you will make the book argue against your own resistance instead of for your freedom. That is not hard — it just needs you to agree at the start.

Here they are:

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
You cannot judge until you have finished.

2. CARRY ON EATING AS NORMAL UNTIL YOU FINISH THIS BOOK
Do not try to cut down yet; the belief must change first.

3. BEGIN WITH A FEELING OF ELATION
You are about to escape, not suffer.

Let me explain each so you can feel why they matter.

Keep an open mind. I am going to tell you things that contradict what you have believed for years — that BAD SUGAR is a treat, that it gives you energy, that it comforts you. Your first reflex will be to argue. That is the brainwashing talking, not you. Just notice the reflex and keep reading. You can test everything after you have heard it whole. You cannot weigh evidence you have not yet seen. Give the whole argument a fair hearing. That is all I ask.

Carry on eating as normal until you finish this book. This is essential. Do not try to cut down while you read, do not throw food away, do not set a start date for Monday. Why? Because if you turn reading into another test of willpower, you will be tense while you read, and tension makes you crave the very thing you are trying to understand. I want you relaxed and curious, not deprived. Eat exactly as you have been. Let BAD SUGAR keep doing what it does while we look at it clearly. The belief must change first; the behaviour follows easily afterwards. There is plenty of time. You are not being asked to be perfect today.

Begin with a feeling of elation. I know that may sound strange when you feel sombre about this subject. But listen: you are not about to lose something precious. You are about to escape a trap that has taken your money, your energy, your peace and many of your evenings. Cast aside doom and gloom. There is no need to be miserable. Get excited. Think how marvellous it will be to shop without the hum of wanting, to finish a meal and feel satisfied, to watch a film and enjoy the film, to wake up without the hunt. That excitement is not pretence — it is accurate. You have stumbled on a way out that does not require suffering. Let yourself feel that.

Those three instructions are the whole contract for this chapter. Keep them, and you will be free to hear what comes next without fighting yourself.

You may wonder about my authority to make such big promises. I do not offer certificates or jargon. My authority is lived. I was trapped, I tried the hard ways, I found the easy way out, I have since helped many others see the same thing. You do not need to take my word for it — you will take your own experience as proof as you read. All I ask is that you let me show you.

So we begin as investigator and guide. You investigate; I point. You bring your honesty about what BAD SUGAR actually does for you; I bring the clear sight to re-label it. Together we will look at every place BAD SUGAR has sneaked a ride on your life, and we will give the credit back to where it belongs — to you, to the moment, to real food, to the people you love.

You have already taken the hardest step: you picked up a book that offers to set you free. Keep it open. Keep eating as normal. Keep the definition of BAD SUGAR clean in your mind.

We have a wonderful job to do together.
