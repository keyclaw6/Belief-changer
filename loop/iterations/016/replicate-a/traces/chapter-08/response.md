Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THIS TRAP

IN THIS CHAPTER — The supermarket aisle at eye level, The children's cereal boxes, The lab with the dials, The web tightening between the shelves

*You did not build this craving — it was engineered for you and sold back to you as your character.*

### IT WAS NOT YOUR CHARACTER

If you believe this hunger is your character, ask yourself one honest question.

When did you decide to need BAD SUGAR to get through an afternoon? At what age did you examine the evidence and choose to be the person who hunts for something quick and sweet at three o'clock, who hides the packet, who promises one bite and finds the box empty?

We never decided. We were recruited.

For years you have told yourself a story about yourself — that you have a sweet tooth, that you are greedy, that you lack willpower, that others can take it or leave it and you cannot. That story is the cruelest part of the Sweet Con, because it makes you blame the very person the trap needs you to blame.

I have sat with hundreds of people who told me that story in the exact same words. They were not weak. They were not defective. They were consistent, caring people who applied a strong will to their lives every day — to work, to family, to getting through — and who applied that same strong will to persisting in BAD SUGAR even while every instinct told them something had taken hold. It takes a strong will to keep feeding a discomfort you can feel growing. Your persistence was not proof that you are weak. It is proof that the belief holding it in place was powerful.

The fact is you were not weak. You were outgunned.

And by what? Not by a taste bud. By an industry that employs armies of scientists to weaponize abundance, and by a second villain that has kept you blaming yourself every time you tried to escape its way.

### THE AISLE THAT WAS DESIGNED BEFORE YOU ARRIVED

Walk it with me, as you do every week.

You push the trolley in after work. Lights bright, music soft, the aisle at eye level stacked like a pharmacy where every packet is a dose in tasty packaging. At one end, the children's cereals — tigers and monkeys and bright cartoon promises beaming at a child at knee height. You remember them from your own childhood kitchen. The adverts that taught you morning means sweet, that reward is chocolate, that love comes in a bowl you pour.

That was your training.

In a quiet lab far from that aisle, people in white coats did not ask how to nourish you. They asked how to make you want more. They tested the bliss point — the exact sugar, fat and salt that makes the tongue light up and the brain fire want again. Too little and you notice nothing. Too much and you put it down. At the bliss point you cannot quite put it down. You reach again. That is not recipe development. That is trap design.

Then they designed the access. Not every second. Intermittent. Treat yourself. Just on weekends. Just a little after you have been good. A packet for the kids that you happen to finish. A deal that makes the family size cheaper than the small one. The fact is intermittent access drives bingeing and escalation while steady, always-there access does not. The trap is not the sweetness alone. It is the schedule — now you can, now you can't, now you deserve it — that teaches the brain to binge when the door opens. Moderation does not tame that schedule; it is that schedule. You were taught to make BAD SUGAR precious, and then you were blamed for treating it as precious.

Look at the wall of packets again. Each one is engineered to spike and crash, to create the dip that asks for the next dose. Each one is priced and placed to make the next dose feel like choice. Each wrapper is designed to fire want before hunger has even formed. When the smell from the bakery hits you at the entrance, when the wrapper crackles, that pull before you feel hungry is not your character. It is a cue circuit that the lab counted on.

You were not shopping. You were being harvested.

And this is the awe I want you to feel before we go further: look what your body can do without any help. It takes protein and a little fat and makes exactly the blood sugar you need, steady as a tide. It tells you when you are hungry with a calm hollow, and tells you when you are satisfied with a quiet pause. No dials, no packets, no bliss point. A marvellous, competent system that has kept people alive for thousands of years without a single added teaspoon. To override that with a packet that spikes and crashes it is not flavour. It is vandalism of something sophisticated and kind.

So who built the trap? People who profit when your marvellous system wobbles.

### THE SLOW BOIL YOU NEVER NOTICED

You say, but I feel fine. I am not ill. I run my life. How can I be trapped if I feel normal?

That is how the trap was meant to feel.

There is an old image of a frog in water that heats so slowly the frog never jumps. The heat rises by degrees, the frog adjusts, it feels fine while it is being cooked. We have lived in water that heated over years — a spoonful more in the tea, a bigger bar, a sweeter cereal re-branded as healthy, a drink that went from small bottle to liter. You did not notice because everyone’s water heated together. Normal stopped meaning natural. It just meant common.

There is a web too, almost invisible at first. A fly moves freely between strands, telling itself it is free, until each sweet hit lays another strand across its wings. One strand is nothing. Ten strands are nothing. Then one afternoon you try to leave and you cannot, and you think it is you. It was the strands you did not see being laid, one dose at a time, each promising relief while adding another line to the net.

You adjusted to both — the heat and the web — and called the adjustment your appetite. It was not appetite. It was habituation.

I tell you this plainly because it explains why you have not seen it, not to frighten you, and certainly not to ask you to change because you are frightened. Fear is not the way out of any trap. Seeing it is.

### THE LOAD HIDDEN INSIDE THE LIFT

Here is the hard truth the aisle never prints on the front of the packet. I will give it to you flat and then in the same breath I will tell you why you must not quit from fear of it.

The fact is, at population level, high added-sugar intake is linked with a higher load on the heart and vessels — the liver forced to process the surplus, pressure raised, chronic inflammation stoked, appetite control bypassed so you eat beyond need. Supermarkets do not sell that on the label, but that is the load that rides inside every lift the packet promises. The teaspoon figures are approximate, the causation is at population level — not a sentence on you personally — but the direction is clear. The lift is a deferred load.

The fact is, free sugars feed plaque acid and plaque acid rots teeth. That is not a vague risk. That is a mechanism: sugar, acid, decay. Fluoride and brushing can mediate it, food texture and timing matter, but the acid after the sweet is real, and it happens while the advert is still telling you sweets are love for children. If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, have a history of disordered eating, or any medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before changing what you eat. For the general reader, the cavity is the small, concrete stake that proves the harmless claim was false — while you were told you were treating yourself, something in your own mouth was being etched.

I do not want you to stop because those sentences scared you. If you stop from fear, fear will bring you back the first time fear ebbs. I want you to stop because the trap is ugly right now, today, in your mouth and your afternoon and that aisle — and because escape is marvellous right now.

Feel the difference. The industry wanted you hooked for the rest of your life or free for the rest of your life to sound like the same threat either way — forever without the taste you love. It is the other way around. Hooked for the rest of your life is the threat: paying, every day, for the rest of your life, for a relief that only returns you to normal for minutes. Free for the rest of your life is the reward: waking for the rest of your life without the hunt, shopping for the rest of your life without the pull, eating for the rest of your life with taste returned. That phrase — for the rest of your life — lands its whole weight when you see who was banking on which version.

You were not deciding for the rest of your life to be someone who needs BAD SUGAR. Someone decided for you that you should need it for the rest of your life, and then told you it was your choice.

And the second villain made sure you never questioned that choice. The Willpower Method told you that quitting meant sacrifice, that you must resist, stay strong, endure deprivation, count days, avoid triggers, earn freedom at some future milestone. When you failed, it let the industry keep its customer and let you keep the shame. Every time you thought I cannot stop, you were really saying the method I was given cannot work — because it was designed to keep the belief intact while you fought the behaviour. That method is a trap inside the trap.

Once you see the two villains — the engineers of desire and the method that told you desire is your fault — the shame falls off in one piece.

You were not weak. You were conned by an aisle that was built before you entered it.

You do not need to change from dread. You get to get out, easily and with relief, because there is nothing real to leave behind. The bliss point was never bliss. It was the precise calibration of your next dip. The aisle was never a market of treats. It was a pharmacy of doses. And the person you thought you were — the one who needs a fix to get through — was a costume the Sweet Con dressed you in.

Take it off. You are the person whose body already knows how to feel steady, hungry, satisfied and sweet on real food, without a packet's permission.

And now that you know who built the trap, you will never again believe the craving came from your character.

SUMMARY

- Craving BAD SUGAR is not a character flaw; it was engineered by bliss-point formulation and intermittent access that makes each dose feel precious and drives the next binge.
- The aisle, the packets, the adverts and the schedule were designed to fire want before hunger and to create the dip they then sell as relief.
- At population level the sweet lift carries a bodily load on heart and vessels and a direct mechanism for tooth decay; that proves the harmless story false, without predicting any individual's future.
- Your body's ability to make steady energy and to signal hunger and satisfaction is already sophisticated and complete; the trap exploits that sophistication instead of serving it.
- Seeing the two villains — the industry that weaponized abundance and the Willpower Method that told you to fight yourself — removes shame and leaves only relief that you can walk free for the rest of your life.
