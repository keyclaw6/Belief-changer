Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU?

IN THIS CHAPTER — The menu on the kitchen table, The question turned inside out, The two names for the trick, A house party with an uninvited guest, What the daily dose is really costing you

*It is doing plenty TO you, and it is doing nothing FOR you.*

### THE QUESTION WE HAVE BEEN ASKING WRONG

We have been asking the wrong question.

The wrong question is, does BAD SUGAR do more harm than good? That question keeps the trap alive, because it smuggles in an answer before you have examined it. It assumes there is a good.

We weigh harm on one side, benefit on the other. We tell ourselves, I know it is not ideal, but it gets me through, it gives me a little pleasure, it makes life sweeter. As long as you weigh harm against a benefit you believe is real, you will keep feeding the Sugar Trap. You will always find a way to make the scales balance.

I want you to change the question entirely.

Not, does it do more harm than good.

But, what good is there at all?

That is a colder, cleaner question. It does not invite balancing. It invites justification to justify itself. If BAD SUGAR really did something for you, you should be able to name it, plainly, in one sentence, without borrowing from the harm it causes. We are going to ask that sentence to stand up.

So let us put your reasons on the table where you can see them. These are not my words. They are yours. I have heard you say them, in your own dialect, in kitchens and offices and queues and on sofas.

Read them slowly. Hear your own voice in each one:

"I need sugar to get me through / to get me through the afternoon"

"It gives me energy / helps me concentrate / it's fuel"

"I deserve a treat / I've been good this week / treat when you deserve one"

"It's my reward / celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate"

"It's sociable / it makes the meal / the occasion wouldn't be the same"

"Just a treat / once a week is fine / I can cut down / keep the special ones"

"I don't keep it in the house so I'm in control"

"Natural sugar is fine / I need sugar for my brain / it's harmless"

"I'll quit tomorrow / I'll wean off slowly / I'll switch to the less-bad version"

"I just can't stop once I start / one bite becomes the box"

Put them there and leave them there. Do you see what they are? They are not ten different reasons. They are ten costumes for one idea. That idea is that BAD SUGAR gives you a genuine pleasure or treat. That it does something for you that you would miss.

We are going to test that one idea to destruction. Not harm versus benefit. Benefit, full stop. What does it actually do for you?

### A GENUINE PLEASURE OR TREAT

You were taught to call it that from childhood. A genuine pleasure or treat. The phrase was given to you complete, like a wrapped sweet. Adults gave you BAD SUGAR when you were good, when you were sad, when you finished your vegetables, when you had breathed through a dentist appointment. Birthday tables, celebration evenings, the cinema queue, the fridge after a hard day — all of them taught the same lesson: this is the pleasure, this is the treat, this is what you get when you deserve love.

Of course you believed it. We all did.

But listen to the words themselves. A genuine pleasure is something you enjoy for itself, that leaves you better, that you are glad you had afterwards as well as during. A treat is something that treats you — that is kind to you. Does that describe what you experience?

You know the lift. You also know the sticky mouth afterwards, the heavy eyelids, the faint restlessness an hour later that says do it again, the packet hidden at the back of the cupboard, the thought I just can't stop once I start. You know the way one bite becomes the box, and the way you pay yourself with chocolate because you have been good this week and then feel less good, not more.

The fact is, we have been calling a hit a treat.

From now on in this book, when we mean that claimed benefit — the pleasure, the reward, the energy, the sociability, the love you think BAD SUGAR gives you — we will call it by one fixed name only: a genuine pleasure or treat. Not nine names. One. So you can see it plainly each time the trap offers it. That phrase now carries the whole question: is there a genuine pleasure or treat in BAD SUGAR at all, or is it only ever doing something to you while you call it for you?

Once you agree to ask it that way, the answer becomes very simple. You will see it does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you.

### THE VOICE THAT TELLS YOU IT DOES

If BAD SUGAR did nothing for you, why do you hear a voice that says it does?

We named the Sugar Imp in the last chapter. That tiny, trivial physical gnaw. It complains when it is not fed, and it starves quickly when you stop feeding it. It is not you. It has no wisdom.

But the Imp does not speak in sentences. It only whispers, feed me. The sentences come from somewhere else. The long, clever, kind-sounding sentences that tell you why feeding it is reasonable, sociable, deserved, even necessary. I need sugar to get me through. It's my reward. It's harmless. I don't keep it in the house so I'm in control.

That belief-system was installed in you over a lifetime. By family tables, by advertisements, by bright packets designed by rooms of scientists to hit a bliss point, by every film night where the bowl in the middle was presented as the love itself. It talks in your voice, but it is not your reasoning. It is a con that learned your voice.

We will give that system its proper name, so you can hear it as a stranger's script, not your own thought. It is the Sweet Con.

The Sweet Con feeds the Sugar Imp. The Imp whispers and the Sweet Con translates the whisper into a reason. I deserve a treat. I have been good this week. The occasion wouldn't be the same. Each line is a strand. Each line puts another dose in your mouth and tightens the web. The Imp is small. The Sweet Con is large, and it is the real thing we must kill. Starve the Imp and it dies on its own. See through the Sweet Con and you will never want to feed the Imp again.

You are not arguing with yourself when you hear those justifications. You are hearing the Sweet Con speak. Once you hear it that way, its authority collapses. It is not you being weak. It is you being conned, and a con loses all power the moment it is seen.

### THE GATECRASHER

Let us take the reason that feels kindest and therefore holds hardest.

"It's sociable / it makes the meal / the occasion wouldn't be the same."

You know the scene. House party, kitchen warm, friends laughing, music low, a table covered with food you helped prepare. Or a family table where everyone is home at once. Someone carries in the pudding, or the biscuits, or the sweets, and the mood lifts. You feel the evening is complete. You credit BAD SUGAR with making it.

I want you to see what really made it.

Picture the party. The food is carbohydrate from edge to edge — bread, pasta, rice, biscuits — and carbohydrates are lonely. They invite company. BAD SUGAR gatecrashes any meal and then claims it made the party. Think of a cheerful, loud gatecrasher who walks into your house uninvited, talks over everyone, hands round his own sweets, and then tells you the evening would have been dull without him.

You laugh because you recognise him. He is not cruel. He is comic. He is also entirely unnecessary. The warmth was the people, the laughter was the company, the satisfaction was the food and the company of people you love, the relaxation was the end of the week and being home. BAD SUGAR contributed nothing except noise and sugar, and then took the credit.

We do a simple isolation in our minds. Replace the BAD SUGAR on that table with real food you enjoy, fruit, a meal you love, or nothing sweet at all, and leave the people, the light, the music, the story. Does the warmth remain? Of course it does. Now remove the people and the music and the story and leave only BAD SUGAR on an empty table, eaten alone at the sink at midnight, the packet pushed to the back so no one sees. Is there warmth? No. There is sticky mouth and a need for another hit.

It was never the gatecrasher. It was only ever sneaking a ride on your evening. The evening was the pleasure. He was just standing in the picture.

Do you see the inversion? The Sweet Con tells you BAD SUGAR makes the occasion. The truth is the occasion makes BAD SUGAR look good. Once you reassign the credit to its true owners — the people, the place, the moment, you — the sociable justification falls away and does not return. And if that most likeable reason falls, what does that tell you about the others waiting in the menu on the table? We will meet each one on its own ground. The energy claim on its own ground. The reward claim on its own ground. But here, now, you can already feel the method: every benefit is credit stolen from somewhere else.

### WHAT IT DOES TO YOU

It is doing plenty TO you. That part is not debated.

World health guidance says keep free sugars below ten percent of daily energy, about fifty grams, with additional benefit below five percent. Most of us live far above that, not because we are greedy but because BAD SUGAR is engineered to be everywhere and to be called normal. Normal is not natural. I am not prescribing a number for you. I am asking you to see that what you call a little treat is, by any ordinary measure, a heavy daily load you never chose to carry.

The load is not abstract. Free sugars feed plaque. Plaque makes acid. Acid makes decay. That is the mechanism, plain and flat. Brushing and fluoride soften the blow, of course they do, and they matter. But they mediate a mechanism, they do not erase it. The cavity is a real bodily stake, alongside that guidance for healthy eating, that BAD SUGAR takes while claiming to treat you. A treat does not drill holes in your teeth. A treat does not ask your liver and your vessels to carry its bill years later. This is not said to make you change from fear. I do not want you quitting because you are frightened. Fear keeps the trap alive by making the trap feel like a master. I want you to change because you see there is nothing to miss. But see it plainly for a moment: what you call a genuine pleasure or treat is, in the body, work and harm — a daily tissue tax for a pleasure that never arrived.

And then there is the pattern you describe so well in your own words. Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate. The language is payment. You have been good, so you pay yourself. But what currency are you paid in? BAD SUGAR. The very thing that creates the flatness you want to reward yourself for getting through. You do a hard day, you feel low, you pay yourself with the cause of low, you get a twenty-minute lift and then a fast drop, and tomorrow you need to be paid again. That is not a reward. That is the Sweet Con paying you in its own counterfeit coins and calling it kindness.

Ask the single question and keep asking it: what does it do for you? Not what does it do to you — we have answered that, and it is not nothing. What does it do for you that you could not have without it, that you would miss and mourn?

Try to name one. One real, irreplaceable benefit that is not relief from a discomfort BAD SUGAR itself created, that is not credit stolen from people or place or story. You search and you find — nothing. A little buzz that is only the Imp quietening. A little sociability that was the company all along. A little reward that was the trap paying you with the trap.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That is the axis switch. You entered this chapter weighing harm against benefit. You leave it holding benefit up to the light and finding no benefit there to weigh at all. The scales are not balanced. There is only one side.

We have not yet pulled apart the energy story or the reward story thread by thread. Those have their own rooms, and we will give them the care they deserve. Here we only needed to agree on the test. The test is not does it harm, but does it help. And the answer, once spoken plainly, is wonderfully clarifying: there is no genuine pleasure or treat to lose. There is only a gatecrasher to show the door, a con to stop listening to, and a small imp that will starve the moment you do.

SUMMARY

- The right question is not whether BAD SUGAR does more harm than good, but whether it does any good at all.
- All ten justifications on the menu are costumes for one claimed benefit, which this book calls a genuine pleasure or treat.
- The voice that tells you BAD SUGAR helps you is not your own reasoning but the Sweet Con, the belief-system that feeds the Sugar Imp.
- BAD SUGAR gatecrashes meals and occasions and steals credit that belongs to people, place and moment.
- Free sugars do plenty TO you, from daily load above guidance to plaque acid that drives decay, while fluoride and brushing only mediate the mechanism.
- Judged by what it does FOR you, BAD SUGAR has no genuine benefit to weigh; the trap is seen as escape, not sacrifice.
