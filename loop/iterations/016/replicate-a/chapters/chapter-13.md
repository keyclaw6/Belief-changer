Chapter 13
THE LAST ORDINARY DOSE

IN THIS CHAPTER — The kitchen table after the shops close, The last packet opened on purpose, The ugliness tasted with attention, The vow spoken out loud, The congratulation that comes at once

*You are not becoming free over time — you are free the moment you decide you are.*

### YOU ARE ALREADY AT THE DOOR

We stood there together at the end of the last chapter. You felt it — that quiet eagerness, that champing at the bit, that little lift that was not sugar at all but relief getting ready.

That is readiness. Not perfect calm. Not a perfect day. Not a promise that you will never think of BAD SUGAR again. Readiness is simply this: you see the trick plain, you know there is nothing genuine to keep, and you would rather step out now than stand admiring the cage.

If you still wait for a better Tuesday, you will wait for the rest of your life. The trap loves tomorrow. Tomorrow is where it keeps you.

I have seen this threshold a thousand times. We who grazed and hid the packet, who told ourselves just one bite and watched the box empty, who paid ourselves with chocolate for being good — we all thought freedom belonged to someone stricter, someone stronger. The fact is it belongs to anyone who sees that the lift was only relief from the low the last dose created. You have seen it. That is why you feel that tug to go.

You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. That was true on page one and it is true here. You have not lost a pleasure. You have found the reason you never needed to pay for relief that was only ending a discomfort BAD SUGAR itself created.

So we will not do a laboratory tasting. We will not do a sad farewell. There is no last supper to savour. There is a last ordinary dose to notice.

### TASTE THE UGLINESS ON PURPOSE

Go and get what you would have taken anyway. Not a special favourite bought for the occasion. Not the big bowl. The ordinary packet, the ordinary bar, the ordinary biscuits from the ordinary shop.

Take it with attention, now that your eyes are open.

We spent years telling ourselves this was a genuine pleasure or treat. Look at it plain. Unwrap it. Smell it. It is sweet in a way that shouts. The first bite is loud, then cloying, then sticky. The mouth coats. The teeth film. The sweetness is not bright the way an apple is bright or bread is sweet after hunger. It is blunt and chemical and too much. You chew because the bag is open, not because the flavour improves. Notice the second bite is less than the first. Notice the third is habit.

Stay with it for a moment more. Feel what happens ten minutes after. The little surge that feels like energy is not energy; it is the brief quieting of the Sugar Imp you fed. Then the dip begins to gather — that heavy eyelids, that sticky mouth, that faint empty, slightly insecure nag behind the breastbone that says more. We called that need. It was never need. It was the dip that follows the dose.

I am not asking you to change from fear. The facts about the bodily load have done their work and are set down. I am asking you to notice what is in front of your tongue right now: this is not love. This was the Sweet Con's script calling a tax a treat.

If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, or any medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before changing what you eat — I say it once, plainly, because your body is yours to care for.

You do not need a stronger will. We needed a stronger will to persist in this for years when every instinct said it made ordinary life harder. That will is yours. It is evidence for you, not against you.

The physical part, if you feel it at all in the next days, is mild but well-defined — a small, starving creature that has lived on regular hits. It complains a little when not fed. That is all. It is not a detox sentence. It is not proof you need this. In animals and in preliminary human reports the hump, when it comes, peaks in two to five days and then passes. Do not promise yourself you will feel nothing; promise yourself you will know what it is if you feel it — the death throes of something tiny, already dying. Rejoice in that faint complaint. Do not feed it and it starves. Feed it and you guarantee it returns.

You have seen enough.

### THE SOLEMN VOW

Stand up. Push the packet away. Say it out loud — not in your head, out loud, where a vow lives:

I have taken my last ordinary dose. I am free.

That is the threshold. Not tomorrow morning. Now.

9. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY DOSE AND MAKE THE SOLEMN VOW
Do not wait to be free — you already are.

I congratulate you. I mean it. There is no waiting period, no test to pass, no streak to earn. Freedom is an identity conferred the instant the belief changes. You have not given something up. You have escaped. The moment you mean the vow, you are a person who no longer feeds the Sugar Imp and no longer listens to the Sweet Con.

Say the sentence the book will give you to keep forever. Say it now and hear how true it feels when it is not a slogan but a fact:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

That is your replacement thought. Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — in a shop, at a table, on a bad day — you will think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! Not because you must fight the thought, but because the thought has become a trigger for joy. You do not need to suppress the pink elephant. You turn it joyful the moment it appears.

Two doors will try to reopen. I name them now so they arrive pre-labelled and powerless.

The first door is the bad day. It says, you deserve a rescue, you are stressed, just this once to get you through. You know this line in your own dialect: I need something quick and sweet to get me through the afternoon. Feel it for what it is — the con offering you the cause as the cure. The rescue is the perpetrator. A hit now does not soothe the day. It borrows relief from the next hour and makes the day heavier. You pity that offer now, you do not envy it.

The second door is the soft one. It says, one won't hurt, just the special one, just a taste, you can have a little now you are free. If there were any genuine pleasure or treat in that one, we would not need to ask permission for it. One keeps the belief plugged in. The body can cope with an accidental blip; your mind cannot allow a deliberate one. A deliberate one revives the trap because the belief is back in charge: that there is something to have.

So we close both doors with one movement:

10. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
The tug-of-war ends the moment you stop pulling.

Do you feel the tug-of-war? For years we pulled on both ropes — fear of not stopping and fear of stopping. Both ropes belong to the Sugar Trap. The moment you let go, the war is over. There is nothing to weigh, nothing to revisit, nothing to count. Counting days is another rope. You are free. Let others count. You will get on with enjoying your life.

Look ahead with different eyes. Tomorrow morning you will wake without the hunt. In the shop you will see the bright aisle not as treats but as doses in cheerful boxes, and you will feel pity, not envy, for the queue that still pays to ride the rollercoaster. At the table you will eat when hunger comes — that mild, friendly signal — and stop when satisfaction arrives, and you will taste real sweetness again, clean and marvellous.

You do not need to change anything else in your life. Keep the same bus. Keep the same sofa. Inhabit the old contexts confidently and let them prove freedom. The thoughts you already own are enough.

You have crossed.

SUMMARY

- Freedom is instant, not earned over time — the moment the vow is meant, you are a person who no longer feeds BAD SUGAR.
- The last ordinary dose is taken with attention on its ugliness, not enjoyed as a farewell, and reveals there was no genuine pleasure to lose.
- Any physical unease is mild and brief, often two to five days if felt at all, and signals a small creature starving, not a need to feed.
- Two relapse thoughts are pre-labelled as the trap's lines — the bad-day rescue offer and the just-one-cannot-hurt offer — and both are answered by not reopening the decision.
- The joyful replacement whenever BAD SUGAR is thought of is FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! — rejoicing at a dead enemy, never mourning a lost friend.
