Chapter 8
WHO BUILT THIS TRAP

IN THIS CHAPTER — the supermarket aisle at eye level, children's cereal boxes, a packet turned over in the hand, the bliss-point smile from a television long ago

*You didn't invent this craving — it was engineered for you before you could even read the packet.*

### YOU WERE NOT THE ENGINEER

We have blamed the wrong person.

We blamed quick hands. We blamed a sweet tooth. We blamed a character flaw that showed up at four o'clock and again after supper. We told ourselves I have no willpower, I am greedy, I love sugar too much. We wore that story for years, and it felt true because the want felt personal.

The fact is it was not personal.

Think of how we fell into the Sugar Trap. Not by study. Not by choice. As children we were shown a bowl that sang. Tigers and bears and cheerful cartoon captains leapt off the box and promised energy, fun, love. We watched ads where chocolate meant celebration and sweets meant being good. No one taught us to read the packet. Everyone taught us to love the front of it.

Someone else did the engineering.

In laboratories far from your kitchen, armies of food engineers weaponised abundance. They searched for the bliss point — the exact sugar, fat and crunch where the mouth lights up and the brain says more. They tuned texture to melt fast so you never feel finished. They priced the big share bag so the small one feels foolish. They placed it at eye level so your hand finds it before your mind does, and called variable access freedom. Put it everywhere, make it brilliant for a moment, let it crash, then sell relief from the crash. That is the design.

And it works because you are not weak. You are exquisitely well built. Your body runs a natural glucose supply of its own from protein and the ordinary food you eat — you need no import of BAD SUGAR to function, if you have a medical condition affected by what you eat talk to your clinician before you change anything. Your hunger rises as a mild, friendly signal and satisfaction arrives quietly and frees you from food-thought for hours. Your tongue is tuned so precisely that a single strawberry after weeks without blasts of BAD SUGAR tastes marvellous. This sophistication is not a flaw to be overridden with a louder hit. It is the baseline the product was built to crowd out.

Look at that with awe for a moment. Your instinct knows when to eat, how much, and when to stop. Real food satisfies that instinct. BAD SUGAR is engineered to bypass it. It hits appetite controls that never evolved for a sweet flood on demand, spikes, then leaves you emptier. The wonder is not that you craved. The wonder is that a system so finely balanced could be kept off balance for so long by packets that were designed to do exactly that.

There are two villains in this story, and neither is you. The first is the industry that built the trap and sells the fix for the hunger the fix created. The second is the Willpower Method that told you your failure to escape proved your weakness, so you kept trying the method that guarantees the trap stays shut. Warm to the person, harsh to the trap: that has been our rule from the start and it lands here with force.

Did you build this? Did you formulate the cereal to shine like a toy? Did you decide to put the dose by the till, by the petrol pump, on the child's birthday table as if it belongs there? No. You were conned, as we all were. The confidence trick was not played by you. It was played on you.

Do you hear the objection trying to be reasonable? But I still choose it. I could stop if I really wanted to.

If you freely chose it, why do you need a book to stop? Why do you hide the packet? Why does one bite so often become the box when you swore it would be one? Choice does not need hiding. Choice does not need apology. Choice does not feel like a tug you have to talk yourself out of.

That tug is not character. It is schedule.

### THE AISLE THAT SELLS THE SCHEDULE

Come with me up the aisle. Not to lecture. To see.

You walk in needing bread and milk and you walk out with a bright pharmacy. Every packet is a dose in tasty packaging. Children's cereals stare at children at their height — not yours — with wide eyes and free toys. Biscuits promise family. Chocolate promises reward. Drinks promise energy. The front shouts love and treat and gift while the back, in print you need glasses to read, lists dose after dose of BAD SUGAR, syrup, starch engineered to spike and crash under lovely names.

Pick one up. Any one. Turn it over. That long list is not food. It is formulation. And the schedule printed nowhere is the real trap.

The schedule is: sometimes.

We told ourselves just a treat, once a week is fine, I don't keep it in the house so I'm in control, I'll cut down. We believed we were being moderate and responsible. In animals, intermittent access drives binge volume and craving while steady, always-there access does not produce the same signs. Deprive, then permit. Restrict, then reward. Tell yourself Friday is special. That pattern is the lab pattern. It makes the Friday dose feel more precious, the relief more illusory, the next hunt sharper. It is yo-yo restriction creating the binge it claims to prevent, and we lived it as diet-binge, rule-break, Monday-again.

One bite carries the box not because you are broken but because intermittent access makes one bite a re-trigger. The wrapper fires want before hunger. The first dose re-lights the circuit it claimed to calm. You do not eat the box because you loved the first biscuit. You eat it because the first biscuit re-laid the track for the second. The trap built that track. Then it told you the craving was your character.

Walk the aisle with that re-label and a mercy replaces envy. You do not see indulgence. You see people feeding the Sugar Imp because the Sweet Con told them it is a genuine pleasure or treat. You do not see deprivation if you do not take a packet. You see escape. The queue ahead of you is not enjoying more than you. They are paying to ride the rollercoaster you have stepped off. You pity the queue, you do not envy it, and you do not moralise about them. They were coned as you were.

Remember the frog that never felt the water heating because the rise was gradual. Remember the strands you could not see until the web was already around your flight. Each packet was another strand, so thin it felt like freedom while you browsed. That was not freedom. That was the web being built one dazzling packet at a time.

You did not put the water on to heat. You did not spin the web. Someone else profited every time you told yourself just this once.

### THE PRICE THE BODY PAYS QUIETLY

The Trap does not only borrow your freedom. It borrows from the body while claiming to be harmless.

The fact is at population level high added-sugar intake is linked with a higher load on heart and vessels — through liver overload, raised pressure, chronic inflammation and that bypass of appetite control we just walked past in the aisle. Not as a scare about you tomorrow, not as a sentence on your chest. At population level the extra load is real, and it is the opposite of energy.

The fact is free sugars feed plaque acid and plaque acid decays teeth — that cavity on the back molar is not bad luck, it is mechanism. Brushing and fluoride mediate it, but they do not erase the stake. Sugar does concrete bodily harm while calling itself a treat. If that sounds frightening, do not change because you are frightened — change because you see the con and you want the freedom. Fear will not keep you free. Seeing does.

Feel the relief in the same breath. The load is population-level, not a forecast about you. The cavity mechanism is understood, not mysterious. The body you awe at is resilient. When you stop feeding BAD SUGAR, the bypass stops with it. The point is not to count teaspoons. The point is to stop letting the thing that taxes the body pose as the thing that powers it.

Ask: when did BAD SUGAR ever power you? A child runs all day on no BAD SUGAR at all. You did, once. The quick sweet does not put energy in. It borrows it, spends it fast, and sends you back for more. That's why the sweet top-up to get you through the afternoon always ended with the sticky mouth and the heavy eyelids and the hunt for another top-up. It's the other way around.

We were not weak-willed. We were outgunned. And being outgunned is not a verdict on you. It is a verdict on the trap.

### FOR THE REST OF YOUR LIFE

Here is where the shift turns from understanding to joy.

The Trap tells you time is its ally. If you stay inside it, there is a schedule for every single day. A little hunt in the morning. A fix at the coffee queue. A reward for being good. A rule and a break and a promise to start Monday. Paid in small doses, for years, with interest added in health, money, freedom and peace of mind.

Do you feel that weight? Imagine carrying that hunt with you into every shop, every birthday, every film night, every tired evening — hooked for the rest of your life. That is the Trap's version of for the rest of your life. It is a threat.

Now turn it over, as you turned the packet over, and read the other side.

There is another for the rest of your life.

Free for the rest of your life. No hunt in the shop. No negotiation at the counter. No packet hidden and then hunted. Hunger friendly. Real food sweet again. Evenings that are about the evening, not the bowl. Mornings that start clean because you did not end sticky. The same years, the same life, without the tax. That is the other for the rest of your life. It is a promise.

Same words. Opposite emotion. The only question is which life you mean when you say them.

We fell into the Sugar Trap together because we were brainwashed to believe a dose was a genuine pleasure or treat that we would be deprived without. The brainwashing was installed by everyone around us and by the industry that profits from it, and it was kept in place by the Willpower Method that told us failure meant we were the problem. Now you see both villains plainly. The craving is not your character. It is a small, starving Imp who lives on a story — the Sweet Con's story — and the story was not written by you.

The exit is not a fight with your own hands in the aisle. The exit is seeing that the hands that stocked the aisle are not your hands.

When you think of BAD SUGAR now, don't think of a pleasure you must resist. Think of the con in a bright aisle, diligently manufacturing want and calling it love. Think of the web so fine you flew between strands and called it choice. Think of the frog who felt fine while the heat rose, and then — lifted out — felt fine for the true reason: the water was gone.

You are that lift. Not frightened. Not deprived. Relieved. The trap was built for you. You do not have to keep living in it.

### SUMMARY

* The craving is not my character — armies of engineers designed packets, bliss points, placement and variable access to manufacture want and make each dose feel like a reward.
* Restrict-then-permit schedules make the next dose feel more precious and worsen the hunt; one bite often becomes the box because that schedule re-triggers wanting before hunger.
* At population level high added-sugar intake carries a real bodily load on heart and vessels and a concrete mechanism for tooth decay; that load is why the lift is not free energy.
* The body's hunger and satisfaction system and its ability to make its own glucose are sophisticated and do not need BAD SUGAR; stopping returns you to that clean baseline.
* Thinking for the rest of your life as trapped versus free shows the same horizon with opposite emotion; seeing the engineered trap lets me choose free for the rest of your life and feel relief, not sacrifice.
