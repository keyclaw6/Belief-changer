Chapter 7
HOW THE TRAP WORKS

IN THIS CHAPTER — tight shoes by the front door, the kitchen counter at four o'clock, an empty wrapper that twitches, a single loop traced from dose to want

*The lift is not a bonus — it is the pain pausing, and the pain was put there by the last lift.*

### THE LOOP YOU NEVER SAW FROM INSIDE

We thought we were chasing pleasure.

We had a dose of BAD SUGAR — a biscuit, a chocolate, a sweet drink, a handful from the packet — and for a few minutes we felt a little better, a little brighter, a little steadier, and we logged that as proof. It gives me energy. It helps me get through. It is a genuine pleasure or treat. That bright few minutes was our evidence, and we trusted our own feeling.

Let me show you what that feeling actually was.

Put a line on paper in your mind. That line is how you feel when BAD SUGAR has nothing to do with you — the clean, steady normal of a person who never grazes it. No spike, no hunt, no sticky mouth, no hunt for the next bite. Just ordinary calm, ordinary appetite, ordinary energy that rises with hunger and settles with real food.

We never live on that line for long, because the trap pulls us off it. Here is one ordinary loop, slowed down so you can watch it happen.

You take a dose. For a short while you climb toward that normal line. You feel relief — the edge softens, the flats lifts, the faint restlessness quiets. You call that lift pleasure. It is not pleasure. It is relief. You have not risen above normal. You have been returned toward it, briefly, from below.

Then the dose does what every dose of BAD SUGAR does. It drops you again. The steady line tips down. A mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly uptight dip arrives — not dramatic, not agony, but real enough that your mood flattens, your mouth wants, your attention drifts to the cupboard, the vending machine, the packet on the counter. This is not your natural state. It is the aftermath of the dose.

Then a cue fires. A wrapper crinkles. A smell from the kitchen. The clock says four. The ad plays. Your hand remembers the drawer. Want spikes before you are even hungry. You answer that want with another dose, feel that brief relief again, and guarantee the next dip. Dose, brief relief, dip, cue, want, dose. Around it goes.

Never once in that loop did you rise above the normal line. You fell below it and paid for a momentary climb back toward it.

I know this is hard to accept. For years you felt BAD SUGAR lift you. You felt it in your body. How can I tell you it never lifted you at all? Stay with that doubt for a moment and look again. If BAD SUGAR truly lifted you above normal, you would feel marvellous far above the non-user for hours after a dose. You do not. You feel briefly less flat, then you want more, then you feel worse and blame tiredness, stress, your own will. The lift is relief from a low BAD SUGAR itself created. It's the other way around.

That is the inversion. The rescuer is the perpetrator.

The fact is each hit re-triggers the same wanting circuit it claims to satisfy. The fact is after the hit there is a real dip — a mild but well-defined fall below where you started — that the next hit will briefly quiet. The size of that dip is small. The physical part of it is trivial and it starves in days if not refed. What makes it feel big is not the size of the dip. It is what you believe the dip means.

### TIGHT SHOES

Let me give you the picture that makes this impossible to forget. 

You put on a pair of shoes two sizes too small and you wear them all day because someone told you they help you walk. They pinch. They rub. Your feet throb. You shift your weight. You are distracted and irritable and you tell yourself you are tired because you have not sat down.

Then, for one marvellous minute, you slip them off. Ah. The relief floods up your legs. Blood returns. Cool air hits your skin. You sigh. You think, those shoes give me relief. 

Do they?

Did the shoes give you pleasure? Or did they create the pain they then briefly relieved? Would you thank a person who pinched you and then stopped, as if he had given you a gift?

That is BAD SUGAR exactly.

The dose is not the relief. The dose is the tight shoe and its removal in one packet. BAD SUGAR creates the low it then briefly relieves. The relief is real — you do feel it — but the pleasure is an illusion. It is the pain stopping for a moment, not a bonus added to your day. Wear tight shoes long enough and taking them off feels like ecstasy. Walk barefoot from the start and you never needed ecstasy at all. You were simply comfortable.

Make it sugar-specific, so it lands in your mouth, not just your mind.

You finish the biscuit. Sugar floods sweet across the tongue, white and loud, wiping out every subtle taste. For a few minutes the chatter quiets. Then your mouth turns sticky and stale. A film sits on the teeth. Thirst arrives. Energy that felt bright now feels heavy behind the eyes. Ten minutes later you are hunting — not for food, but for the same blast to wipe the sticky away. The packet promised lift. What it delivered was the pinch and the brief unbuckling, then another pinch. A non-user never rode that at all. They stayed on the comfortable baseline you briefly touched on the way down.

Here is the liberating part. The tightness is not you. It is not your character. It is not weakness or greed or lack of discipline. We all put the shoes on because we were conned into believing they help us walk. The shoes are small. The belief that we need them is enormous.

This is why you could never moderate your way out.

Tighten, relieve, tighten, relieve. If you wear them less often, each removal feels more precious. If you save the small shoes for Friday, Friday's relief feels like love. That is intermittent access making the loop tighter, not looser. One exceptional dose does not sit quietly as an exception. It re-tightens the strap and guarantees the next want.

### THE TWO CHARACTERS

Once you see the loop, you see the two characters who run it.

The first is the Sugar Imp. Tiny, physical, almost trivial. He lives low in the body and complains when his dose is overdue — a faint emptiness, a little restlessness, a twitch toward the cupboard. He is not mighty. He is not dangerous. He is a small parasite who will starve to death on his own in a few days if you simply stop feeding him. For many people the feeling is mild or barely noticed at all. For others it peaks in two to five days then passes. Either way it is brief. This is why the fear of withdrawal is so outsized. You were picturing a monster. The physical monster is a gnat.

The second is the Sweet Con. He is not in the body. He is in the mind. He is the lifelong brainwashing about what BAD SUGAR does for you — that it is a genuine pleasure or treat, that it is fuel, that it is love, that it is reward, that you need it to get through. He is eloquent. He is kindly. He sounds exactly like your own reasonable voice. And he is the one who feeds the Sugar Imp.

That is the Sugar Trap you have been living inside. Not a cage with bars, but a belief with a feeding schedule. The loop never ran on physical need. It ran on the Sweet Con telling you the faint complaint of the Sugar Imp meant you needed relief, and then handing you the tight shoes to remove.

See the split and you see why willpower could never win. The Willpower Method attacks the wrong character. It wrestles the Sugar Imp — the tiny complaining gnat — while leaving the Sweet Con untouched to keep ordering more shoes. No wonder you failed. You were aiming at the small thing while the big thing kept feeding it.

You do not need to fight the Imp at all. You kill the Con's story and the Imp starves by itself. We will do that now.

Do you hear the protest already? But I do feel hunger. I do feel better after sugar.

Of course you do. You feel better after removing tight shoes too. That does not make the shoes useful. Look again at your own days. You feel better after BAD SUGAR only when you were feeling low before it. On a day when you ate real food when hungry, felt clean and steady, and had not grazed — that low was not there. On that day the biscuit did not lift you. It made you sticky and restless. The lift only ever existed while you were in the dip it created. Outside the trap, there was nothing to lift.

I felt this as clear fact on the day I watched eating without the commentary for the first time. The bowl was ordinary. The hunger was friendly. The satisfaction arrived quietly and left me free of food-thought for hours. I realised I had been calling the Imp's complaint hunger and calling his brief silence energy. It was neither.

And then, almost without trying, the tight shoes were simply off.

### THE CUE THAT FIRES BEFORE YOU ARE HUNGRY

There is one more piece that makes the trap feel invisible until you name it.

Your brain learned the loop on a deeper circuit — the body's own opioid and wanting circuits and the cue memory that fires before thought. You do not need to be low for want to spike. A wrapper, a smell, a till, a tired afternoon cue can fire want ahead of any real need.

We all know that twitch. The packet is in the cupboard and you are not hungry at all, yet you can feel your hand already reaching while your mind says just a bite. That is not hunger. That is hyper-reactivity to cues. The circuit learned that wrapper means relief is coming, so it leans before you decide.

This is why one bite so often becomes the box. You do not eat the second biscuit because you loved the first. You eat it because the first re-triggered the circuit, spiked want, and re-laid the cue for the next. The one bite is not an exception. It is the trigger.

And this is why enjoyment fades while wanting grows. The same system that gets crowded and blunted with repeated hits — needing more for the same silence — is the system that gets jumpy at the sight or smell. Crowding dulls the pleasure and hyper-reactivity sharpens the pull. Together they explain the most miserable sentence you ever said to yourself: I don't even enjoy it any more, but I just can't stop. You were not weak. The trap had crowded the reward and sensitised the cue. You felt exactly what the trap was designed to make you feel.

If this were about character, the sight of a wrapper would not move you. If this were about hunger, you would not want sweets when full. The loop is not you. It is a learned circuit, small and correctable, running on a belief.

I know another objection sits waiting, patiently, like a friend: But surely sugar must do something good or we would not all do it.

That is the confidence trick speaking. We all did it because we were all told it was a treat, a fuel, a reward for being good. The village does it, so it looks normal. Normal is not natural. The loop feels freely chosen only because it is everywhere. You chose the first exposure on phoney information, then the trap removed the choice and sold it back to you as preference.

The fact is your body makes its own glucose and does not need BAD SUGAR to function, so letting it go is not deprivation — if you have a medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before you change what you eat. That is one short spoken clause of care, so no scare stands as a sentence on you. The rest is clarity: BAD SUGAR is not fuel. It is a spike and a dip sold as energy. Real food is the steady ground. The lift never beat the ground. It borrowed from it.

Now hear the stern, cheerful truth together.

You are not a person with a mighty craving. You are a person with a tiny, starving Imp and a loud, convincing Con. The Imp's complaint is trivial and already dying. The Con's script is the only thing keeping him alive. When you stop believing that BAD SUGAR gives you a genuine pleasure or treat, the Imp has nothing to live on.

That is why freedom is immediate. You do not need to sweat out a big withdrawal. You need to see the loop plainly and stop feeding the belief. The physical hump, if you feel it at all, is days-long and mild. The mental hump ends the moment you stop pulling the rope.

There is a small switchboard in the mind with many cords still plugged in while we negotiate. Cut most and leave one — just a treat, just on Fridays, just the natural kind — and the board stays lit. That is why totality is gentle. Unplug every cord and the light goes out. Leave one and the trap hums.

You already have what you need to unplug them. Your body knows hunger and satisfaction. Your instinct knows real food. That was chapter six, lived as a bowl in your hands. Now the mechanism you just watched explains why that bowl felt so free. The radio of BAD SUGAR was off. Without the loop, hunger is friendly, food is satisfying, and the hunt is simply gone.

### THE INSTRUCTION

There will be noise around you. Advice to cut down, to wean off slowly, to keep the special ones, to switch to the less-bad version, to count days and try hard. That noise is not information. It is the Sweet Con in other voices, keeping the switchboard lit and the shoes laced.

So at the climax of this chapter — exactly where belief must be guarded — I give you the firewall:

IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
If it contradicts escape, it keeps you trapped.

Keep that line clean. You do not need a second method. You need a clear one. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain by hearing this one through to the end.

Already you can feel the inversion landing as relief, not fear. You are not giving up a lift. You are stepping out of shoes that hurt. When you think of BAD SUGAR now, do not picture a lost pleasure. Picture the Sugar Imp blinking in the light, unfed, and the Sweet Con with his script suddenly sounding silly. Let them starve.

We fell into the Sugar Trap because we were conned. You will walk out of it because you now see the loop.

Rejoice. You have just shrunk the monster you feared and enlarged the target that will actually free you.
