Chapter 2
ALREADY HOOKED

IN THIS CHAPTER — yesterday afternoon from waking to midnight, the biscuit tin with the crumpled packet pushed to the back, the desk drawer that clicks shut, the invisible web between kitchen and sofa, warm water that never felt hot, the small creature that whispers and the trap that holds it

*You were not freely choosing BAD SUGAR — you were living inside a trap that removed the choice.*

### YESTERDAY, PLAYED BACK WITHOUT THE STORY

Let me ask you to do one ordinary thing with me. Not a test. Not a confession. Just look at yesterday together, exactly as it happened.

You woke up. You told yourself today would be light. Perhaps you had fruit, perhaps you skipped the sweet start and felt virtuous for an hour. Then the mid-morning mouth woke up. A biscuit with tea. Not because you decided it would be a genuine pleasure. Because it was there, and the tea felt incomplete without it.

You told yourself that was nothing. I don't overdo it.

Then the gap before lunch. A roll, white and soft, that turned to sugar in your mouth before you had finished chewing it. A chocolate bar you broke into — meaning to have a square — and finished while answering an email. You did not enjoy the third square. You ate it faster than the first.

Afternoon came, and the real performance started. The belly was not empty. You had eaten. But a different hunger arrived — tight behind the eyes, restless in the hands. You rummaged. You grazed. A handful from a packet you had put on the high shelf so you would not graze. A few sweets from a bowl meant for visitors. You told yourself you would replace them.

You hid the packet. Not from anyone else. From yourself. You folded the top over and pushed it to the back of the cupboard. You put the empty wrapper deep in the bin, under the paper. You told the person you live with, or no one at all, that you had been good today.

Evening came. You were full and not satisfied. While the television was on you reached again without deciding to reach. The bowl in the middle of the table emptied in small, automatic pinches. You said one more and then stopped, and then had one more. When you went to bed your mouth was sticky and your sleep was heavy and you promised yourself tomorrow would be different.

Does that feel familiar? I do not ask to catch you out. We have all lived that day. I lived it for years while telling myself I was a person who could stop if I wanted to.

Look again, without the story on top. Count only what the body did. How many times did you eat BAD SUGAR after you had already told yourself you would not? How many times did the dose happen before the decision? How many times did once I start I can't stop turn out to be true — I just crave sweets and when I eat them, I feel like I can't stop, one bite becomes the box?

That is not a lapse of character. That is a pattern being run.

You say you choose this. We all say that while we are inside it. But would a person who freely chooses need to hide the packet from themselves? Would a free choice need a rule about only on weekdays, only after dinner, only the good chocolate, and then break its own rule before the week is out? Would you need a book about how to stop doing something you truly choose to do?

We tell ourselves we could stop if we wanted. The fact is, we did not want the proof. The very drawer we arranged so cleverly, the packet we put out of sight so we would not touch it, is the proof that we already know we are not in charge. The trap has been running the day, not you.

I say this with warmth, not judgement. You were not foolish. You were conned. The confidence trick does not start with a chain. It starts with a small kindness — a sweet offered as love, a treat for being good, a bright packet that promised a lift. We accepted the kindness. Everyone did. The trap begins so politely you never feel the door close.

### NORMAL IS NOT NORMAL

Here is the strangest part. You felt normal yesterday. Tired, a little heavy, a little disappointed with yourself, but normal. Normal is the disguise the trap wears.

If you had eaten BAD SUGAR once last month, you would feel the difference violently — the rush, the wobble, the sour mouth, the restless search four hours later. When you do it every few hours, every day, you stop feeling the drug and start feeling the life you have while on it. You call that life normal. It is not normal. It is the normal of a person who is never allowed to get back to baseline.

Think of the people you love who never graze at all. They eat a proper meal, they stop, they forget about food for hours. They are not using willpower to forget. There is nothing to remember. That forgetting is the real normal — the state your body and instinct intended for you. What you called normal yesterday was the normal of a person who has been topped up just often enough never to notice how low the top-ups keep them.

There is a dignity in knowing how common this is. On questionnaire measures that ask about loss of control, cravings, eating more than intended and keeping going despite wanting to stop, about one adult in seven scores in the trapped range — a rate that sits alongside the rates for alcohol and tobacco on the same kinds of scales — and a similar proportion of children. That is not a diagnosis and it is not me labelling you. It is a simple, human fact: you are not uniquely weak. The pattern is everywhere because the trap is everywhere. Many of us feel it and call it habit while we do it every day for the rest of our lives without once feeling we have a problem. That feeling that you are alone in this is part of the con.

And there is a schedule behind the pattern. In laboratory animals, when sugar comes and goes — available, then gone, then back — bingeing escalates, volume climbs, and restless searching appears in the gaps. When the same sweet is there all the time with no on-off schedule, that frantic bingeing does not appear in the same way. It is the intermittence that drives the gorge. That is an animal finding, so I will not overclaim it, but we should hear the echo honestly: yo-yo rules, cutting down, forbidding, allowing a little, banning again — that on-off, now-you-can-now-you-can't rhythm is the human version of the same schedule. We create precisely the conditions that make one bite carry the whole box. The diet makes the binge precious. The binge makes the diet feel necessary. Neither is your fault. Both belong to the trap.

If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, live with a history of troubled eating, or have any medical condition where food changes need a clinician, talk to your clinician before you change what you eat. For the general reader, what I am describing is not a medical sentence. It is a small, mild dip that follows the dose and calls itself hunger. Hold that lightly. The fear of it is larger than the thing itself.

### THE WEB YOU DIDN'T SEE

Let me show you how that schedule built itself around you without a single dramatic moment.

Imagine a fly moving through a sunny kitchen. Between the window and the fruit bowl there is nothing visible. The fly darts left, right, sips, turns. It feels free because the strands are almost invisible — one here, one there. Each sweet hit is a strand. The first tastes were thin and far apart: a birthday cake, a can after sport. You flew through easily. The second strand was the rule: only after dinner. The third was the packet in the drawer for emergencies. The fourth was every advertisement that told you that sugar is love, that a sweet is what makes the meal.

No single strand ever felt like it would hold you. That is the design. You never felt a tug. You felt normal. By the time the web is dense enough to feel, flight is already gone. You do not crash into the web. You wake up inside it, grazing in a small circle and calling it choice.

We built this web together with a kind world. The world did not mean to trap you. Parents, friends, shops, bright packets, school treats — they repeated the same promise so often it became brainwashing in the gentlest voice: this is a treat, this is fuel, this is how we celebrate, this is how we get through. When everyone says it, you stop hearing it as a sentence and start hearing it as the truth. That is how a con becomes culture.

The web is not in your character. It is around you. And because it is around you, it can be left. A web is not a cage with a lock. You do not need a stronger will to chew through it. You need to see it. Once you see it, you stop flying the same circuit that glued you.

That web is what I mean when I say you are living in the Sugar Trap. Not a metaphor for emphasis. A working description. A trap engineered to be invisible while you live in it, whose only evidence at first is that you keep returning to the bowl you said you would leave alone.

### WHY YOU DIDN'T FEEL IT HAPPENING

You ask, if this trap is so real, why did I feel fine? Why didn't I notice harm?

Think of the frog who lives in a pot. The water is comfortable. The heat comes up so slowly degree by degree that the frog never feels a moment when it should jump. Every small increase is easy to adjust to. The frog tells itself this is normal warmth. It feels fine while it is being cooked.

We were that frog. The rise was gradual: one more sweet than last year, a packet that got a little bigger, a morning roll that became two, a bowl that became a family-size bag. No single step felt like harm. Each step felt like normal life. The fact is, that gradual rise carries a load on the heart, the liver, the teeth, the mood — but we felt nothing dramatic because the trap rises with us.

Hear the fact plainly and then feel the relief with it. The harm is real while the feeling is mild. You have not been imagining. You have been adjusting. The relief is this: you do not need to have felt dramatic harm to have been trapped, and you do not need to quit from fear. You quit because the trap is not giving you what you promised yourself it would, and because stepping out returns you to cool water immediately, without a long punishment. We land the hard truth and we leave fear where it belongs — not as your reason, but as the trap's weather.

That is why yesterday looked like nothing while it was everything. Normal was the warm water.

### THE TWO CHARACTERS WHO RUN THE TRAP

If the web is the trap, we need two names for the workers inside it so you can see them clearly when they whisper. I will name them simply, as fellow prisoners do.

The first is tiny. It lives in the body and does almost nothing except murmur when it is not fed. A mild, empty, slightly insecure, slightly uptight feeling a few hours after the last dose — the sort of gnaw you have been calling hunger or needing something sweet. It has no strength. It cannot command you. It is a small parasite, a nudge, already dying the moment you stop feeding it. Watch it with me and you will feel how small it is. That whisper is the Sugar Imp.

The second is huge. It lives in the mind and does all the work. It is the voice that says this is a genuine pleasure, this is my treat, I need this to get through, everyone does it, just one can't hurt. It tells you the Imp's tiny whisper means you are deprived and need a fix now. It feeds the Imp on schedule and then takes the credit for the brief quiet that follows, as if it had soothed you. That whole belief-system, that kindly con artist who installed the idea that BAD SUGAR helps you, is the Sweet Con — I will name it once here and you will hear it everywhere after.

See the split. The physical part is trivial. The mental part is dominant. We have been terrified of the wrong one. We feared the Imp's little gnaw as if it were a big monster that would overwhelm us if we did not feed it, while the Con sat above it telling us what the gnaw meant. Once you see the size of each, the job simplifies completely. You do not need to fight the Imp. You will starve it by not feeding it, and you will relish its little complaints as the sound of it dying. The real work is to stop believing the Con when it interprets the gnaw for you.

Watch eating once, as body and instinct intended, so you have ground to stand on when the Con speaks.

Eat a simple, satisfying meal made of real food when you are truly hungry. Notice true hunger: a clean, mild signal, friendly, not panicked — the body asking for food. Eat slowly enough to notice taste returning. Real food is sweet in its own way when you are not bathing your tongue in BAD SUGAR ten times a day. Notice the pause that arrives — a quiet, settled satisfaction that says enough — and how you naturally stop without a rule. That pause is not willpower. It is instinct. Body, instinct and real food already know how to do this. You have not lost them. The Trap has been talking over them.

Try that once, without making a project of it, just to watch. You will see the Con try to narrate — you need a sweet to finish, the meal is not a treat without it. Let the meal finish without it. Watch how the meal was satisfying before the Con added its advertisement. That watching is not a new system. It is you remembering the authority you already own: body, instinct and real food. We will use that memory later to strip every false credit BAD SUGAR has been taking.

### YOU ALREADY KNOW HOW TO BE FREE

We have walked through one day and seen the whole structure.

We saw that yesterday's grazing was not free choice but a schedule running you.
We saw that normal was not normal, only the warm water we adjusted to.
We saw the web that built itself strand by invisible strand until grazing felt like freedom.
We saw why you didn't feel it happen, and why not feeling it was part of the design.
We saw the two characters: the tiny Imp that whispers and the Con that feeds it and takes the credit.

Put those together and the verdict is simple and kind. You are not weak. You were outgunned by an engineered trap that made grazing feel like normal life and made stopping feel like you would lose a pleasure. You did not choose to be trapped. You chose a sweet offered in kindness on phoney information, and then the Trap removed choice. That removal is not your fault. Seeing it is your freedom.

I know this is a lot to accept. You still hear the Con saying but I like it, I need it to get through, just one can't hurt. Of course you do. That is its job. We will meet each of those lines where it lives — the fuel idea, the treat idea — and we will strip them clean, one by one, by reassigning the credit to its true owners: the situation, the body, the moment. Not yet. We do the demolitions on ground we have already installed, so they land easily and stay landed.

For now, I ask one thing before we go on. Not to stop eating. Not to be strong. Only to keep investigating with me, all the way to the end, without skipping. This method works like a lock. One number without the others will not open it. Give me all the numbers, in order, and the door opens.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Carry that lightly. It is not obedience. It is the way you guarantee that the correct belief, not the old brainwashing, is the one left standing when you walk out. You have nothing to prove by testing the Trap with a little dose while you read. Carry on eating as normal, watch what we described together happening in real time, and let the belief change do the work. The behaviour follows on its own. Easily, in the moment you see, and then for good.

You came to this chapter thinking I choose this and I could stop if I wanted. You leave it knowing you were already hooked and feeling normal only because you never left the Sugar Trap, and knowing the whisper you feared is just the Sugar Imp — tiny, already dying, and not worth feeding.
