Chapter 14
MORNINGS, SHOPS, TABLES

IN THIS CHAPTER — the quiet morning kitchen, the bright aisle that looks like a pharmacy, the table where real food is sweet again, the lion who stays by the open cage

*You do not need a new life to stay free — your ordinary life will prove you already are.*

### THE MORNING YOU ALREADY OWN

You wake up and nothing hunts you.

That is the first proof. Not a feeling you have to manufacture. A simple absence.

For years we woke into the hunt. Eyes open, mind already in the cupboard. What is in there. What is left from last night. Do I need to get something quick and sweet to get me through the morning. The day had not started and the Sugar Imp was already making its mild, empty, slightly insecure complaint, and the Sweet Con was already translating it as hunger, as need, as you deserve a little hit to get going.

This morning that translation does not come. You lie there a second and listen. The mouth is clean. The head is clear. There is no sticky film, no heavy lids, no urgency to fix anything before you can face the day.

And because we have been conned for so long, the mind tries to make this absence mean something is missing. Is this it, it says. Should I not be wanting something. The fact is, wanting was the trap feeding. Not wanting is freedom doing its quiet work.

Get up. Do what you have always done. Kettle. Water. Light in the kitchen. You do not need a new ritual. You do not need a new curriculum. You already own the thoughts that keep you free, because you have already understood the trick.

Make your breakfast when you are hungry. Not because the clock says you should top up, not because you fear a crash later. Wait for the mild friendly signal that real hunger is. It does not shout. It does not panic. It arrives as a small, clean call from your body, instinct and real food answering each other without argument. You eat. You stop when the pause comes — that little moment where enough arrives and you put the fork down because you want to, not because you are being good.

You will notice something you had forgotten. Real food is sweet. Not in the screaming way BAD SUGAR is sweet. In the clean way an apple is sweet, toast is sweet, milk in tea is sweet, a pepper is sweet. BAD SUGAR had been blocking the taste with its blast. With it gone, flavour returns on its own. You did not have to learn it. You only had to stop drowning it.

This is not a diet morning. This is an ordinary morning with the noise removed. You do not have to change everything else in your life to have this. You only have to keep the belief that there is nothing in BAD SUGAR you want, and the body catches up by itself. If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, have a history of disordered eating, or any condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before you change what you eat.

You worried how will I cope in my old life. Look. You are coping by living it. The morning proves it without you doing anything clever.

### THE AISLE THAT LOOKS LIKE A PHARMACY

Then you go to the shops.

We used to go to the shops to answer the Imp. We told ourselves we were shopping for meals, but we were shopping for hits. The aisle did the rest. You know the aisle I mean. The bright one, the long one, the one that hums.

Walk it now as a free person. Inhabit it confidently. Do not white-knuckle past it and do not avoid it. You have nothing white-knuckle about. You are free. Prove it by walking straight through your ordinary shop on an ordinary day.

Look at it with new eyes.

Re-see it as a pharmacy.

Every packet is a dose in tasty packaging. Brilliant colours, cartoon tigers, promises of energy, treat when you deserve one, just a treat once a week is fine. The packets are designed to look like food and to act like a fix. They sit at child height. They shout bliss on the front and whisper hit on the back in the ingredients you were never meant to read. This is not a shelf of treats. This is a bright pharmacy where the queue thinks it is indulging.

We were not weak for wanting it. We were outgunned. Armies of food engineers weaponized abundance to hit the bliss point, to make the crunch, the melt, the salt-sweet snap that keeps your hand reaching before your mind has decided. That is the Sugar Trap made visible — not a feeling inside you, but an industrial design around you that profits while you feel heavy and sticky and dull.

See the trolley in front of you piled with doses. A moment ago you might have envied it. You do not envy it now. You pity it, cleanly, without superiority. Does that really look like enjoyment. The person pushing it is not indulging. They are trapped and being told their trap is a pleasure. You were that person last month, telling yourself just this packet, just for the kids, just for the weekend. You know the tax that comes twenty minutes later — the dry mouth, the flat hour, the hunt starting again.

You are not being deprived. They are. You came in for bread and milk and fruit and you are leaving with bread and milk and fruit and your evening still intact. That is not willpower. That is seeing plain.

Let the aisle reinforce you. Every bright packet is a small, sad advertisement for the con that no longer cons you. You do not need to preach. Do not evangelise at the shelf. Visible ease does the recruiting. You walk out lighter because you left the doses where they belong — on the shelf.

That thought — not a new teaching, but the thought you already own — is enough. You already know it does nothing FOR you and plenty TO you. You already know the lift is relief from a low the last dose created. You already know a genuine pleasure or treat would not need to be fought for like this. Let that knowing do its work while you push the trolley past the pharmacy.

### THE TABLE WHERE SWEETNESS RETURNED

Come home. Eat at your own table.

This is the third proof, and it is the sweetest, because it is where we told the biggest lie: that the meal would not be the same, that the occasion would be flat without the hit at the end.

Set the table as you always have. People you like, food you like, cooked simply. Nothing elaborate to replace a trap that never deserved ceremony. The body, instinct and real food know what to do. You sit when you are hungry. You eat until the clean pause arrives where you are satisfied and a little glad to stop.

Taste it. I mean taste it.

When we lived on BAD SUGAR we ate without tasting. We chased the blast and then chased the next blast to cover the dip. Real hunger was buried under grazing, and satisfaction never got a chance to arrive because we kept feeding the Imp between meals. We called that normal appetite. It was the trap's schedule.

Now the signal returns. Hunger comes as a gentle emptiness, not an emergency. Satisfaction comes as a quiet sufficiency. In between, flavour is louder because it is not competing with a sugar siren. A tomato tastes sweet and sharp. Roast vegetables taste sweet and deep. An apple cracks and floods your mouth and you remember you used to love apples before you were told sweetness only came from a packet.

You do not need to tell yourself it is marvellous. You will taste that it is.

We used to invite BAD SUGAR to every meal like a gatecrasher who claimed he made the party. He did not. The party was the people, the warmth, the story, the room, your body finally fed what it recognises. He was only sneaking a ride. You see it now at the table. The warmth is exactly where it always was — in the company, not the dose.

You worried food would be deprivation. Look. You are eating food you like, when you want it, enjoying it more, not less, because you are not eating it through a film of sugar fatigue.

Stay there. Eat with your family. Let the meal end when it ends. There is no gap to close with a fix. The ending is clean.

### THE LION WHO STAYS BY THE OPEN CAGE

There will be a strange moment — today or in a few days — when you feel a tug and think perhaps freedom has not held.

It is not afternoon hunger. It is not real.

It happens at the habitual time. Three o'clock. The sofa after dinner. The petrol station. The place where you always had a hit. A small restless feeling passes through, a little empty, a little uptight, a ping in the chest that used to mean reach for the bowl. The mind, habitually, reaches for its old translation: You need this. You are missing something. Just one would be nice. Just to feel normal.

This is not proof you still need BAD SUGAR. This is the cage after the door is open.

Picture a lion who has lived years in a cage. One day the door swings open. The lion is free to walk onto the grass. But the lion does not run. He pads to the door, looks out, and then settles back down by the bars, out of habit. The open door frightens him more than the cage did, because the cage was familiar. He hovers by the bars not because the cage feeds him, but because his body remembers the shape of the bars.

That is what that ping is. The Sugar Imp is already starving. That faint gnaw is its death throe, not its hunger. It is the little parasite complaining because it is not being fed. Be glad to feel it complaining. Each complaint unanswered is the sound of it dying a little more.

And that thought that slides in with it — just one cannot hurt, just this once, just the good one — that is not your reasoning. That is the Sugar Trap talking in your own voice, the Sweet Con keeping one cord plugged in so the whole switchboard stays lit. You hear it now the way you hear the aisle: plain, pitying, done. You do not argue with it. You see it.

You do not need to resist a genuine pleasure or treat you have seen is not one. You only need to let the habit-noise pass while you get on with what you were doing anyway — answering the email, stirring the pan, laughing at the film. Change nothing else. Inhabit the old contexts confidently and they prove you are free.

Do not be alarmed if the ping clusters. If it comes, it tends to be loudest two to five days after the last intentional doses, then it thins. It is not a withdrawal to survive. It is the last echo of a loop you have already broken. Revel in it dying. Each day the door feels more open, not less, because you stop looking back at the bars.

That brief unease is not a warning that you are deprived. It is the final proof that you were trapped.

### THE THOUGHT YOU KEEP

So what do you do when BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — and it will cross your mind, in shops, at tables, on sofas where the bowl used to sit?

You do not suppress the thought. You cannot not think of a pink elephant by telling yourself do not think of a pink elephant. You reframe the thought the instant it arrives. You turn the ping into a peal.

You have already lived the proofs to do this. The morning without the hunt. The aisle seen as a pharmacy. The table where real food is sweet. Those are not lessons to revise. They are memories now. When the cue fires — packet, smell, wrapper, tired hour — let the memory fire with it and let the feeling that comes be the opposite of missing.

This is the thought you keep for the rest of your life. Not a struggle. Not a chant. A single joyous re-label you speak inside your head the way you would laugh with a friend.

REJOICE AT A DEAD ENEMY, NEVER MOURN A LOST FRIEND
Whenever you think of BAD SUGAR, think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Say it now and hear how true it sounds. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! Not I hope I am free. Not I am trying. FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! The packet that used to mean temptation now means relief. The thought that used to mean I can't have it now means great, I don't do it anymore and I was never more delighted.

There will be no mourning. Mourning is for a lost friend. This was a dead enemy, and the enemy is dead the moment you stop feeding it and stop believing its script. Rejoice. That is the whole emotional instruction, and it is easy because it is accurate.

And if you slip — a biscuit at a meeting taken without thinking, half eaten before you notice, the brain a second slower than the hand — do not turn a blip into a verdict. This is where freedom is kindest and clearest.

IF YOU SLIP, FORGIVE IT AND GUARD THE BELIEF
A blip revives nothing unless you let the Sweet Con back.

Put it down. Smile at yourself. No drama. No I have blown it so I may as well carry on. That sentence is the trap rebuilding itself with your voice. Your body can cope with an accidental spoonful in a sauce you did not know about. Your mind is what decides. Guard the belief and the blip revives nothing. Invite the story back — just one more to make up for it, just to prove I am still in control — and the belief rebuilds the trap.

A blip is a rumble strip, not a crash. It is feedback to see the thought plain and to hear yourself think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! again, a little louder, a little gladder. You do not need a perfect record. You need a belief that stays shut to the con. Keep it shut and you stay free.

You already own every thought you need. Not a new system, not a set of triggers to avoid, not a life reorganised around sugar. You own the morning, the shop, the table, the open door, and the joyous thought that turns the cue into celebration. Use them once, as speech, as lived moments — that is enough.

You are not white-knuckling your old life. You are inhabiting it and finding it proves you are free, for the rest of your life.

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

Get on with enjoying your life.

### SUMMARY

* Mornings prove freedom without effort — waking without the hunt shows there is nothing to fix before the day can start.
* The supermarket aisle re-seen as a bright pharmacy turns envy into pity — I do not miss doses in tasty packaging, I am relieved I no longer pay for them.
* Real food at my own table satisfies and tastes sweet on its own — hunger is a mild friendly signal and enough arrives as a clean pause.
* The odd ping at old habit times is cage-habit, not need — a brief empty feeling as the little creature starves, to be welcomed as it dies.
* A thought of BAD SUGAR becomes a cue to rejoice, and an accidental blip forgiven instantly revives nothing unless I let the old story back.
