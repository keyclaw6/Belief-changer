Chapter 10
FEAR AND THE WILLPOWER METHOD

IN THIS CHAPTER — the prison door standing open, the empty corridor, the released convict on the bench outside, the braggers at the table, the whingers by the vending machine, the calendar with no finish line

*You are not afraid of failing to escape — you are afraid of two stories the trap itself is telling you, and both stories are its ropes.*

### THE LAST THING LEFT STANDING

We have killed the fuel lie. We have killed the treat lie. We have taken your sweetest evening — the low lamp, the sofa, the bowl in the middle — and put the credit back where it belongs, with the people and the story, not the dose.

If the logic were all that mattered, you would be free already. You see it. BAD SUGAR does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you. It sneaks a ride on every good moment and then sends the bill.

So why is there still a pull in the chest?

Because the Sweet Con has one move left. It does not argue about pleasure any more. It argues about you.

It says two things in the same breath, quietly, so you hardly hear them as thoughts at all:

What if I fail.

And what if I succeed and I am someone who cannot live without this.

One is fear of failure. One is fear of success. They feel opposite. They are not opposite. They are the same hand holding both ropes of the same tug-of-war of fear. And both ropes belong to the trap.

Have you felt it? The little flip in the stomach when you think about truly stopping — not cutting down, not being good this week, but stepping out. Part of you leans forward, wanting to be free. Part of you pulls back, saying you will not manage, you will spoil the evenings, you will be deprived, you will be exposed as someone who needs this to cope. You tug one way, then the other, and the very tugging convinces you that something huge is holding you.

That tug-of-war is not evidence that you need BAD SUGAR.

It is evidence that you are still believing the Willpower Method's story about what stopping is.

Let us look at that method plainly, because it is the reason every strong, sincere attempt you have ever made felt so hard.

### THE SECOND TRAP

I want you to think of the Willpower Method as a second trap built on top of the first.

The first trap is BAD SUGAR itself — the Sugar Imp's tiny gnaw and the Sweet Con's big story that the gnaw means you need a fix, a treat, fuel.

The second trap is the way you were taught to get out of the first.

It goes like this: decide to be strong. Make a rule. Resist. Stay disciplined. Use effort to abstain. Count the days. Fight the craving. Treat yourself as an addict on a recovery journey, one day at a time. Hope that if you hold out long enough, the desire will fade.

Do you recognise the language? We have all lived it. We set a Monday. We cleared the cupboard. We told ourselves we would give up sweets. We tried to stay strong at 3 p.m. We lasted three days, or three weeks, and we thought about BAD SUGAR every hour we lasted. When we slipped — and with that method you always slip — we blamed ourselves.

The Willpower Method is vicious precisely because it looks like common sense. Of course you should try harder, it says. Of course you should resist.

But look what it does to your head.

It tells you that stopping is sacrifice. That you are losing a genuine pleasure. That the price of freedom is endless vigilance. It keeps BAD SUGAR precious by making it forbidden. It makes one sweet between fixes more valuable, not less — the way any intermittent reward makes the next hit more exciting. It turns a trivial physical feeling into a heroic battle. It installs a finish line that never arrives, because with willpower there is no moment you become free; there is only the next day you must not fail.

With the Willpower Method, there is no finish line.

That is not a weakness in you. That is the design of the method. It cannot give you freedom because freedom is not a thing you can white-knuckle your way into. The method keeps you staring at the thing you are supposed to be missing. It tells you to think about not thinking about it, which is the most reliable way to think about it all day.

And because it frames the change as loss, it guarantees fear.

Fear of failure: if stopping means suffering and deprivation, of course you fear you will not have the strength to suffer long enough.

Fear of success: if stopping means life will be bleaker, plainer, less sociable, less sweet for good, of course you fear what success would actually take away from you.

Both fears feel like they are about you. They are not about you. They are about the lie the method told you stopping would be.

### THE PRISON DOOR IS ALREADY OPEN

Picture this with me, not as an idea, as a room you are standing in.

You are in a prison cell. You have been there so long you have arranged it. You have a blanket, a cup, a small shelf with your packets. The door is heavy. You have tried to push it before and it did not move, because you were pushing it the wrong way, with willpower, against the belief that you needed what was inside.

You try again today, in your mind, the way the Willpower Method taught you: you grit your teeth, you promise to resist, you tell yourself this time you will be strong enough to stay strong. You push. The door seems to give a little, then stays. Your arms shake. You think see, I am not strong enough.

Now watch what actually happened.

The door has been unlocked the whole time. It opens inward. It was never held shut by strength at all, but by a misunderstanding about which way it moves. You were pushing a pull door. No amount of pushing would ever open it. The failure did not prove you were weak. It proved the method was wrong.

That is the Willpower Method in one image. It tells you to push harder with character where character was never the problem. And when pushing fails, it lets you conclude you are the problem.

Do you want the truth about your character?

It takes a strong will to persist in something that goes against all your instincts for as long as you have. To keep feeding the Sugar Imp when your mouth feels sticky, your stomach feels tight and restless, your teeth feel furred, your mood drops an hour later, your energy wobbles and you feel that twist of sticky regret you laugh off — to keep doing that day after day for years, paying money to feel worse and calling it a treat — that is not weak will. That is wilful. That is determination misdirected by brainwashing.

You are not weak-willed who failed. You are wilful who was conned and kept going despite your own body warning you.

Once you see the door opens inward — that freedom is a belief-change, not a strength contest — you stop measuring your arms and start looking at the hinge.

### THE CONVICT WHO WON'T LEAVE

Now picture the second fear, the fear of success, as a man.

He has been let out. The gate is open. The fruited plains are right there. He stands outside with his bag in his hand. He looks at the open door, looks at the field, and he does not move. He sits on the bench by the gate. He tells anyone who asks that he misses the cell, that he does not know who he is without it, that freedom feels frightening and he is someone who needs the routine of the cell to cope.

Do you think that man stays because the cell was wonderful?

He stays because the cell was familiar. The anxiety he feels outside is not proof that he needs the cell. It is the habit of the cage, still firing after the cage has gone. The body was trained to expect a dose at that time, in that place, in that company. When the cue fires and no dose arrives, an empty feeling flickers. The Sweet Con is instantly there to translate it: you see, you cannot be yourself without this.

But the man can be himself perfectly well. He was himself before the first sweet ever taught him that film night meant sweets. He was himself on every evening that was sweet without BAD SUGAR — and there were thousands of those before the trap ever found him.

That second fear — I am someone who needs this — is the most personal and the most false. It turns a learned translation into an identity. The traits you think prove you are that person — wanting something quick and sweet to get me through, feeling I just can't stop once I start — are not the cause of the trap. They are the result of it. You feel the roller coaster because you are on the roller coaster. You feel the pull toward the box because each hit re-triggers the want it pretends to relieve.

You were not born needing BAD SUGAR to make an evening loveable or an afternoon workable. That need was installed, then kept alive by the Willpower Method's own rule that you must keep wanting it in order to keep resisting it.

When you stop feeding the belief, the Imp dies and the translation stops. The unease after escape is not a signal to go back. It is the last echo of a habit with nowhere to land.

I know this is hard to accept. So let me voice the objection the way you would voice it to me if we were sitting together, because the method itself will voice it in your head and you should hear it answered now, without it ever being labelled as a technique.

You will think: this is all very nice, but I have tried to understand and I still felt awful. I need willpower. I need rules. You cannot just think your way out. You are brainwashing me in the other direction.

If that is your thought, I am glad you had it. It means you are listening like an investigator, not a believer.

Ask your own evidence: have the rules and the willpower ever made you feel free? Have they ever made the wanting quiet, or only louder? Have they given you a day where you thought about sweets less, or a day where you thought about sweets every hour to prove you were not eating them?

The Willpower Method keeps you thinking about BAD SUGAR for the rest of your life — either as something you are having and regretting, or as something you are resisting and missing. That is not freedom. That is the tug-of-war made permanent.

My method does the opposite. It does not ask you to resist a loss. It shows you there is no genuine pleasure or treat to lose. When you see that, you do not need to fight wanting because wanting has no object. You do not need a new identity because you are recovering the one you had before the trap put words in its mouth.

You do not need to believe me because I say so. You need to check whether, after we take away the false benefit, anything real remains that requires willpower to refuse.

### WHAT THE BODY ACTUALLY DOES

Let us be plain about the physical part, because fear loves vagueness.

The fact is the bodily grip of BAD SUGAR is mild but well-defined — a small, mild dip after a hit, a real but little physical complaining when the Imp is not fed. In animals it looks like a lower tone in the wanting circuit with a rise in the opposing chemical, the dip after the sugar. In you it feels like that empty, slightly edgy, mouthy, restless sense that says feed me.

That dip is real. I am not telling you you imagine it.

And — in the same breath — I am telling you not to change from fear of it. The dip is tiny compared to the story around it. The fear you feel is not the size of the Imp. It is the size of the Sweet Con.

The fact is any physical unease is brief. It peaks in 2 to 5 days after you stop feeding it and then passes — a days-long hump, not a lifelong fight. It is not a cliff you fall down forever. It is a small hill you walk over while you are already free. Some people feel little or nothing at all. Some feel that mild empty feeling for a few days, stronger at the usual times — the 3 p.m. desk, the sofa, the shop.

I will not promise you zero sensation. I will promise you this: you do not need willpower to endure a mild, brief feeling when you are rejoicing that it is dying. You starve a parasite by not feeding it. The feeling you may notice in the next days is not need. It is the Imp's death throes. You can feel it and be glad, because every flicker means it is shrinking.

That is not a sentence to be served for the rest of your life. The phrase for the rest of your life changes meaning the moment the belief changes — from a threat, you will be hooked for the rest of your life, to a relief, you will be free for the rest of your life. Same words, opposite emotion. The trap held both ropes. When you cut the belief, both fears fall at once.

If you have diabetes, take prescribed medication, have a history of disordered eating, or any medical condition affected by what you eat, talk to your clinician before changing what you eat — I want you free, not frightened, and your body looked after while your mind gets clear.

### THE BRAGGERS AND THE WHINGERS

If the Willpower Method is a trap, you should be able to see it living in people around you.

Watch them, not to judge them — they are caught too — but to see the method from the outside.

There are the braggers. They stopped by willpower and they make suffering into status. They tell you how many days they have done, how hard it has been, how strong they have been, how they still want it every day but they resist. They wear the struggle like a medal. They want your envy. Their secret message is: this is miserable, but look how disciplined I am to endure misery. If stopping were really escape to something wonderful, why would they need to keep telling you how miserable it is?

There are the whingers. They also stopped by willpower and they make suffering into warning. They sigh by the vending machine, they say oh I would love one, I miss it so much, you are lucky you can still have it. Their message is also: this is miserable, and I am being deprived.

Both are saying the same thing from different ends: willpower made BAD SUGAR more precious, not less. Both still believe there is something to miss. Both are still in the tug-of-war, pulling on the failure rope or the success rope, but still holding the trap's rope.

Neither of them is your guide. Their struggle is not evidence about you. It is evidence about the method.

You do not want to be a bragger or a whinger. You want to be free. The happy non-user does not brag and does not whinge. She does not count days. She does not think FANTASTIC! I'M FREE! because she has to remember — she thinks it because thinking of BAD SUGAR reminds her she is already free and she feels a little burst of relief and gets on with enjoying her life.

You will not get there by resisting harder. You will get there by seeing that there is nothing to resist.

We fell into the Sugar Trap by brainwashing, not by character. We stay in it by willpower, not by pleasure. The industry that engineered the trap profits when you believe both stories — that BAD SUGAR gives you something wonderful, and that the only way out is strength you do not have.

Both stories are lies.

There is a third story, the true one: you were taught a benefit that never existed, you felt dip and called it need because you were taught to call it that, you were taught a method that keeps the benefit looking real by keeping you staring at it forever.

When that is seen, fear collapses like a trick table.

Remember the table tops from the last chapter. You saw with your own eyes that a feeling of absolute certainty can be flatly wrong and still feel completely right. Fear is that same certainty. It feels like proof that you will fail or that success will be bleak. Measure it. The evening without the bowl was still warm. The afternoon without the hit did not require sugar; it required a real meal and a glass of water and ten minutes. The body that made glucose for you all your life before you knew the word sugar can still make it now. Your own history proves it. The fear predicted bleakness; your own evenings disprove it.

It is the other way around. The fear does not prove you need the trap. The trap creates the fear to keep you.

### YOU DO NOT NEED TO BE STRONGER

So let me tell you what we will not do now.

We will not make a rule about your character. We will not tell you to stay strong. We will not give you a recovery journey to walk one day at a time.

We will do what Carr does. We will cut the ropes by seeing they both belong to the same hand.

You fear you will fail at willpower. You will. Everyone fails at a method designed to keep you wanting. That says nothing about you.

You fear you will succeed and still be deprived. You will not be deprived, because there is nothing genuine to be deprived of. Success is not an identity you must become. It is the identity you recover when you stop believing you were someone else.

You were wilful enough to stay on the roller coaster long after it stopped being fun. You are certainly wilful enough to step off it when you see there is no benefit holding you on.

This is why I said past failures were never your fault. They were the Willpower Method's fault. And because they were the method's fault, they are not predictions about this time.

You do not need a stronger will. You need a truer view.

When fear whispers now — what if I think about sweets tonight and the thought defeats me — answer it the way a free person does: a thought is not a command. You cannot stop thoughts coming, and you do not need to. A thought that used to mean want can mean rejoice. The pink-elephant rule says you cannot not think about BAD SUGAR by trying not to. You can only change what you feel when you do think about it. Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind now, think relief. Think freedom. Think the door.

Practice that here, quietly, for a second. Think of the vending machine hum. Think of the bowl. Notice the flicker. Now, instead of I can't have it, think great, I don't need it, I'm free. Feel the flicker change hands. That is not suppression. That is reframe. Do it once, honestly, and you have proved you can do it.

And because the second trap is sticky and contagious, I am going to give you two quiet instructions before you read another page. They are not willpower rules. They are thought-substitution rules that guard the belief while it roots.

7. IGNORE ANYONE WHO STOPPED BY THE WILLPOWER METHOD
Their struggle is not your guide.

8. AVOID THE INFLUENCE OF OTHER USERS WHILE YOU READ
Brainwashing is contagious.

You do not need to argue with braggers and whingers. You do not need to convert them. You do not need to sit with users while you read and test your strength against their packets. That is the Willpower Method again, trying to prove freedom by enduring temptation. Freedom is not proved by temptation endured. It is proved by seeing there is nothing to be tempted by.

Read this book in peace, away from the aisle and the bowl and the chatter about who is being good this week. Let the belief settle without other voices pulling the rope.

If a thought says but they enjoyed it and I want what they have, pity them, don't envy them. They are not enjoying a genuine pleasure or treat. They are briefly relieving the low the last dose created, while paying with the next low and the sticky regret and the tired, flat hour afterwards. You are not missing that. You are escaping it.

We have demolished the benefits. We have named the method. We have cut both ropes at once.

Keep going with an open mind and a feeling of elation. You have not been shown you are weak. You have been shown the trap used your strength against you, and that its only power now is a mild Imp already dying and a story you can stop believing.

You are not about to become free after a hard fight.

You are about to see you already are.

### SUMMARY

* Fear of failing and fear of succeeding are not opposite truths about me — they are two ropes of one tug-of-war held by the trap, and the Willpower Method keeps me pulling both.
* The Willpower Method itself is the second trap: it frames stopping as sacrifice that requires resisting, has no finish line, and makes BAD SUGAR more precious the more it is forbidden.
* My strong will in the past is not proof I am weak; persisting with BAD SUGAR despite my body's own warnings took will, and that will now serves freedom once the benefit is seen to be illusory.
* The body's physical part is mild and brief — an empty, slightly edgy wanting that peaks in a few days and passes — so the largeness of fear is the story, not the sensation, and any flicker is the Imp dying to be rejoiced in.
* Braggers who boast about suffering and whingers who sigh about missing it both prove the failure of the willpower way, not a truth about me, and their struggle is not my guide.
* Seeing that any thought of BAD SUGAR can be answered with relief rather than resistance collapses fear, so I guard the belief by not arguing with others' struggles and by letting brainwashing fade in peace.
