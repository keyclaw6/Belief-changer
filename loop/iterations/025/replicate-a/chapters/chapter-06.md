Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Market hall at eight with crates and steam — Warm bread torn open with bare hands — A plain table with soup, beans and fruit — The clean call of true hunger — The quiet close of satisfaction — Evening company with laughter and full plates

*Hunger met by real food is the feast itself, and BAD SUGAR never added a crumb to it.*

### THE GREY FEAR

There is a fear that sits quietly behind all the talk of fuel and focus, and we must bring it into daylight because it holds many kind, hungry eaters in place.

It sounds like this, in your own voice:

"Surely food will turn grey. Surely meals will turn flat. Surely love will leak out of eating."

I know that voice. I lived with it for years. We all did. We told ourselves dessert is love. We told ourselves a treat when you feel you deserve one keeps life kind. We told ourselves celebrating with chocolate makes the occasion. Take the bright paper away, we thought, and what is left? Chewing. Duty. Beige plates eaten under bright light while everyone else laughs around something sweet.

That fear is honest. I honour it. No one wants a greyer life. No one picks this book to eat sawdust and call it virtue.

It is also back to front.

You arrive here with the afternoon wheel already seen. You watched the drawer open at three, the ten bright minutes, the thicker fog by four, the second reach, the flat evening. You know now that lift was relief of a dip the last dose left behind. Bring that clear, forgiven eye with you. Lay your own meals beside the grey fear and judge calmly, as an investigator, never as a defendant.

Ask from your own life.

When you sat truly hungry at a table with people you love and good plain food steaming in front of you, did you ever think, if only there were more sugar on this? Or did hunger itself make the first mouthful sing? When you were truly hungry after work or play, did a slice of warm bread or a cold apple ever taste dull? When you pushed back from a meal satisfied, warm and easy, did you feel deprived because no wrapper followed? Or did you feel complete?

Only one honest answer fits. Hunger makes food joyful. Satisfaction makes joy complete. Bright doses never taught that music. They drowned it.

You entered these pages fearing food joy was sugar. You will leave this chapter having lived one ordinary meal so clearly in mind and body that joy stands where it always stood, in hunger, in satisfaction, in real food eaten as favourite.

This is not a kill chapter. This is an inhabit chapter. We pause the demolition and live the doing. Come hungry. Sit down. Taste.

### MARKET MORNING

Come with me to one ordinary market morning. No lecture. No card of rules. Lived doing, noticed bite by bite.

Eight o'clock. Cold air at the door. Crates stacked high. Dark green leaves wet with spray. Oranges bright as lamps. Apples with bloom still on the skin. Plums with dust on them. Bread on a wooden shelf, loaves split at the top, steam rising when the baker breaks one open for a customer. Cheese with rind. Eggs brown and speckled. Herbs tied with string. Fish on ice. Mushrooms earthy and damp. The whole hall smells of earth and yeast and peel and coffee from a small hatch.

You are hungry. Properly hungry. Breakfast was hours ago, or the morning ran busy and you missed it without noticing. Your stomach calls with an open, clean emptiness. Not hunting. Not twitching toward one bright paper. Open toward food, any good food. Mouth watering at the smell of crust. Belly light and ready.

You buy what your nose tells you. A tomato heavy for its size. A handful of green beans that snap between the fingers. A loaf still warm in paper. A pear that gives under the thumb. A small bunch of basil that perfumes the bag. Nothing measured. Nothing counted. No voice in the head saying I have been good this week and may now pay myself. Hunger is pay enough.

Home. The kitchen fills with chop and sizzle. Onion softening in oil till sweet and gold. Beans brightening then darkening in heat. Bread torn, not sliced, crust cracking under your hands, crumb pulling soft and steaming. Salt. Pepper. Lemon. Oil that smells grassy. Soup from yesterday reheated, thickening, breathing. No packet rustle governs the hour. Hands move. Mouth waters. That watering is ancient and wise. It knows what to do with real food.

Sit. Eat.

First bite of warm bread with soup, taken hungry, stops thought for a second. Crust shatters. Crumb pulls soft. Soup warms the chest outward. Tomato sharp then mellow. Beans with bite. Pear after, juice down the wrist, sweet in the way living things are sweet, clear and bright and finished in three minutes.

Notice what happens as you eat. Taste grows. Then taste eases. Hunger quiets mouthful by mouthful. Breath slows. Shoulders drop. Conversation returns. Colour comes back to the face. At some point, without decision, the hand rests. The plate holds a little and no one cares. The bowl holds a spoonful and no voice scolds. The body says, enough, in a quiet, firm voice. You stop. Not because a rule told you to stop. Not because guilt tapped your shoulder. Because satisfaction closed the door quietly behind hunger.

That is the whole authority, met in one meal. Hunger opens. Satisfaction closes. Real food carries you from one to the other and leaves you free for hours.

Now set beside that picture your sweetest evening grazing, hour by hour, because contrast teaches what argument only points at.

Hand moving in bright packets while already full. Supper eaten, plates cleared, belly full, mouth still hunting. First second blooming on the tongue, second second fading to cardboard. Hand moving without tasting. Eyes on the screen, mind half elsewhere. Stomach full, mouth still hunting. That I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling arriving an hour after supper, opening only toward wrappers, closing never. Wrapper rustle. Crumbs. Fingers sticky. Thirst. Evening ending flat and cross, mouth filmed, head woolly, heart faintly scolding, morning vow already forming while the hand still moves.

Which of those two was joy? Which was love? Which left you grateful to be alive in a body that knows how to eat?

It was never the sweet. It was you, hungry and alive.

Stay inside the market meal a little longer, because inhabiting means staying, not glancing.

Feel the weight of the bag on the walk home. Feel the cool apple against the palm. Feel the warmth of the loaf through paper against your forearm. Hear the crack as you tear the heel off for the cook, the cook's perk, eaten standing, chewed slowly, gone. Hear the soup bubble. See the steam fog the window. See the table laid without ceremony, spoons, bowls, bread board, fruit bowl, water jug sweating. See people arriving, hands washed, chairs pulled, talk starting. Hunger makes everyone kinder. Hunger makes the plain vivid. Hunger makes the first spoonful a small arrival.

Eat again, slowly, in memory.

Spoon lifted. Blown. Tasted. Heat, salt, depth. Chew. Swallow. Warmth spreading. Bread mopped. Second spoon deeper. Beans bright then soft. Tomato high then round. Talk pauses, then returns fuller. Pear peeled, or eaten whole with skin, flesh grainy then melting, juice sharp then honeyed. Water drunk cold and long. Belly filling warmly, not tightly. Mind clearing, not fogging. Mood lifting steadily, not flickering.

Now watch the clock after. Ten minutes pass without thought of food. An hour passes. Two hours. Three. Thought on work, on people, on the window, on the walk later. No circling. No bargaining. No drawer. That long quiet is the signature of a meal eaten hungry to satisfaction. It feeds and releases.

This is the life you feared would turn grey. Look at it. Where is the grey? Colour lives here. Texture lives here. Smell lives here. Hunger answered lives here.

### HUNGER HAS A CLEAN VOICE

We were taught to fear hunger and to misread every gnaw as emergency. That teaching kept the drawer full.

Real hunger has a clean voice once you listen again.

It comes slowly, open and empty, growing toward mealtime. Morning hunger after sleep. Midday hunger after work. Evening hunger after the day. It builds like light building. It welcomes many foods. Bread appeals. Soup appeals. Fruit appeals. Cheese appeals. Eggs appeal. Rice appeals. The body says, feed me, and leaves the menu to nose and habit and love. It does not stamp its foot for one bright paper. It does not arrive ten minutes after a full meal. It does not tremble. It builds, it is answered, it passes for hours.

You know this voice. You knew it before you learned wrappers. That small child with the peach who stops mid-bite to run off knew it without ever naming it.

Hear how different is the other call.

It comes fast, narrow and edgy, an hour after eating, at the desk, on the sofa, in the kitchen at night while already full. It scorns bread. It scorns soup. It scorns the apple in the bowl. It wants only the bright hit, the dose, the fix taken standing up, eaten fast, barely tasted. It closes never, even as the box empties. Full stomach, hunting mouth. Hands a little shaky. Thought a little twitchy. Mood a little edgy. That is not hunger wearing a louder coat. That is echo wearing hunger's accent.

The fact is, your body knows how to hold its own steady fuel from plain meals. The glucose your blood needs, your body already knows how to make from ordinary food, hour by hour, without any bright import. Leaving BAD SUGAR out leaves nothing real out. Clean life without bright doses is physiologically coherent and complete. If you live with diabetes, take anything that shifts blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any history where changing food could carry risk, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Feel the relief in that plain fact. You are not asked to run on empty. You are asked to let hunger do its old job and let satisfaction do its old job, while the body quietly keeps the lamps lit between them.

Think of a morning you ate well because you were hungry. Porridge with fruit. Eggs with toast. Soup left from night before, reheated. Bread with cheese. No counting. No bargaining. How long before food crossed your mind again? Three hours? Four? Thought free for work, for play, for people. That long quiet between meals is hunger's signature. It feeds and releases.

Think of a morning that began with a sweet drink and a sweet bar eaten on the run, without hunger, standing at the counter, scrolling, rushing. How long before hunting returned? An hour? Less? Thought circling the shop, the machine, the cupboard. Belly full, mouth empty. That short leash is not hunger's signature. It is the wheel turning, dose calling to dose.

You say, but I feel hunger all the time. I say, of course you feel hunting all the time while bright doses keep echoing. Hunting while already fed proves nothing about your need. It proves the echo. Let hunger speak without the echo for a day, fed by real meals to satisfaction, and you will hear the clean voice again underneath, patient and wise as ever.

We fell into the trap together, all of us, eating without hunger and stopping without satisfaction, starting without choosing and continuing without tasting. Warm complicity here, never blame. Who taught us to distrust the stomach and obey the wrapper? Everyone around us obeyed the wrapper. Bright packets sat at every till. Kind hands pressed them on us as love. Tired hands reached for them as help. Tired minds read the ten bright minutes as proof. We were conned, not foolish.

Escaping it is your own bright doing. You learn again in one meal what no packet ever taught. Hunger trusted. Hunger answered. Hunger gone for hours.

Listen once more, closely, because this distinction frees every meal after it.

Hunger opens toward many. Echo narrows toward one. Hunger grows with time. Echo strikes after fullness. Hunger quiets for hours when answered. Echo quiets for minutes, then returns louder. Hunger lives in the belly. Echo lives in the mouth and the mind. Hunger ends in thanks. Echo ends in scold.

Ask your own evenings.

When the call came an hour after supper, did it welcome bread and soup? Or did it scorn them and demand only bright paper? When you answered it with bright paper, did it leave for hours? Or did it return before the programme ended? When you answered hunger with a real meal at noon, did it leave you alone to live? Or did it nag?

Only one honest seeing fits. You have two calls, not one. One feeds you. One feeds on you.

### SATISFACTION CLOSES QUIETLY

Satisfaction is the most slandered joy in the modern kitchen.

Diet weeks taught us satisfaction is danger. Stop early. Leave hungry. Drink water to fill the gap. Count. Measure. Earn. Pay yourself later with a sliver that becomes a box. Chew slowly while thinking of numbers. Praise the empty plate. Fear the full one. No wonder we stopped hearing the quiet close. We were too busy obeying noise.

Real satisfaction does not shout. That is why we missed it.

It arrives as ease. Warmth spreading from chest outward. Taste narrowing to gentle. The next bite pleasant but not urgent. The bite after that pleasant but forgettable. Breath deeper. A small sigh without meaning to sigh. Hand resting without order. Mind turning outward again, toward talk, toward work, toward the window. The plate may hold food and no voice scolds. The bowl may hold soup and no hand darts. The body has taken what it needs and returns you to your life.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as lived instruction, not as small print. It means no clocks ruling the stomach. No lists to memorise. No moral arithmetic after eating. No earning and no paying. Hunger calls, you eat with appetite. Satisfaction calls, you stop with thanks. Between those two calls you live, not chew.

Try it in memory with the market meal.

You ate hungry. You tasted. Midway, taste peaked, bread and soup at their brightest. Near the end, hunger thinned to comfort. Flavour softened. Urgency left. You stopped with bread still on the board and fruit still in the bowl, and felt no loss. Why would loss live there? Loss lives only where echo lives, where the second bite barely registers and the hand moves without joy. In a real meal, stopping feels like arrival, not denial. The door closes quietly and you walk through the afternoon free.

Ask again, from lived hours.

When you stopped a real meal at satisfied, did your mind circle food for the next hour? Or did food vanish from thought till hunger called again? When you stopped a packet at empty, did your mind rest? Or did it circle scold and vow and plan the next bright buy? Which stopping was peace? Which was war?

There is only one fit. Satisfaction satisfies. Packets pause.

I speak as one who chased pause for years and called it pleasure. I ate standing up, straight from bright bags, tasting only the first second. I ate after supper while full, hunting while fed, scolding while hunting. I ate in the car, in the queue, at the desk, on the sofa, crumbs in the keyboard, wrappers in the pocket. I believed stopping was a matter of character, so I praised early stopping and despised late stopping and never once asked why real meals stopped themselves without praise or blame.

Joy lived elsewhere. Joy lived in stopping without effort.

Watch satisfaction closely in ordinary life and you will see how generous it is.

A hungry eater eats fast at first, then slows without deciding to slow. Talk returns. Laughter returns. Hands rest. Eyes brighten and look outward. No one watches the plate. No one guards the last piece with anxiety. The last piece sits, or is shared, or is left, and no one keeps score. Time passes without food thought. That is freedom in miniature, repeated three times a day, free for the taking.

Watch the packet close and you will see the opposite physics.

A full eater eats fast throughout, then faster. Taste fades while speed rises. Hand moves without looking. Eyes stay narrow on screen or on nothing. The last piece is eaten without tasting and mourned without having been enjoyed. Thought stays on food after food is gone. That is slavery in miniature, repeated three times a day, mistaken for love of taste.

Which physics do you want to live inside? The one that feeds and releases? Or the one that fills and binds?

Satisfaction never needed teaching. It needed quiet. Bright doses shouted over it for years. Let the shouting stop and the quiet returns within a meal, within a day. You do not learn satisfaction the way you learn a language. You hear it again the way you hear birdsong when traffic pauses.

### REAL FOOD, EATEN AS FAVOURITE

Here is the inhabiting this whole chapter exists to give you.

Real food, eaten hungry to satisfaction, is favourite. Not second best. Not medicine you take to earn treats. Favourite.

Taste proves it the moment hunger leads.

Bread torn warm, crust and crumb. Butter melting to gold. Soup that warms the hands through the bowl. Potato crisp outside and cloud inside. Greens with bite and lemon. Rice steaming. Beans earthy and deep. Apple cracking cold. Berries staining the fingers purple. Pear running. Cheese deep and long. Herbs lifting the whole plate. Onion mellowed to sweetness in oil. Carrot roasted to edge and caramel. These are not dull foods waiting for sugar to rescue them. They are wide, various, vivid foods that satisfy and release.

This does not mean sweetness itself is banished from your life. That was never the decree. Hear it plainly, because fear loves to paint freedom as a bare room. A ripe pear is sweet. Berries are sweet. An apple cold from the market is sweet. Grapes are sweet. Sweetness living inside whole fruit, inside vegetables, inside a plain meal cooked with care, belongs to the menu of a free eater and is enjoyed without fear and without counting. What leaves your life is BAD SUGAR, bright doses taken as hits between hunger and beyond satisfaction. Fruit enjoyed hungry to satisfied needs no wrapper and leaves no echo.

Feel how eating returns to joy when those doses no longer shout over it.

Mornings clearer because breakfast, eaten hungry, holds you. Midday steady because lunch, enjoyed to satisfaction, releases you. Evenings kinder because supper, shared and savoured, completes you. Thought freed from circling the drawer. Mouth freed from filming. Temper freed from thinning by four. Money freed drip by drip. Time freed hour by hour. That is not a greyer life. That is colour returning to hours that had gone flat and woolly.

Walk through one free day, lived, not promised.

Morning. Hunger wakes you. Real hunger, clean and open. You eat. Porridge with sliced apple, or eggs with toast, or bread with cheese and tomato. You taste. You stop satisfied. You wash the bowl and forget food.

Midday. Hunger returns, honest and timely. You eat. Soup, bread, salad, fruit. You taste. You stop satisfied. You return to work with mind steady, mouth clean, belly easy. No drawer governs the hour. No clock mocks you. Three o'clock passes like any hour, light shifting, work continuing, no hunting behind the ribs.

Evening. Hunger returns, glad. You cook simply or buy simply. Vegetables, bread, something savoury, fruit for after. You sit. You eat with people or in glad quiet. You stop satisfied. Plates cleared. Kitchen closed. Sofa without rustle. Sleep without hunting. Morning without wrappers and fog.

That day holds no heroics. That is the point. No counting. No bargaining. No being good. No failing to be good. Hunger and satisfaction doing their old jobs while you get on with enjoying your life.

You say, but plain food never thrilled me like bright paper. I say, when did you last meet plain food hungry, without echo shouting over it, without a sweet drink dulling the tongue first, without a bar an hour before blunting appetite? The tongue bathed in daily syrup cannot hear soup. The mouth filmed by daily grazing cannot hear bread. Clean the glass and water tastes wonderful. Let hunger lead and plain food thrills the way water thrills the truly thirsty.

Try it as lived test, not as vow.

Tomorrow, eat breakfast hungry. Truly hungry. Wait till the clean emptiness comes. Then eat real food till the quiet close comes. Notice the first bite. Notice the middle. Notice the resting hand. Notice the hours after. Then ask, did this grey my morning? Or did this return my morning?

You already know the answer because your body never forgot it. Your body remembers hunger. Your body remembers satisfaction. Your body remembers meals that held you for hours. Buried under echo and rustle, yes. Gone, never.

### LOVE WAS THE MEAL

You say, food is love, sugar is love. I say, watch love closely and see where it sat all along. This is the tenderest part of the chapter, so we go gently and we go fully.

Was love in the bright bar handed without looking, eaten without tasting, gone without thanks? Or was love in the hands that chopped, the pot that steamed, the table laid, the hunger met together, the talk that ran long after plates emptied? Was love in celebrating with chocolate eaten fast while eyes stayed on screens? Or was love in the celebration itself, the laughter, the company, the day marked, the warmth in the room that would have warmed with any food or none?

Take the brightest memory you guard, birthday candles, cinema lights low, sofa with a friend after hard news, kitchen crowded at Christmas. Replay it slowly. Keep the light. Keep the laughter. Keep the people. Keep the hunger met. Lift the bright dose out of the picture like a smudge wiped from glass. Does warmth drain? Or does warmth stand clearer, no longer smeared by rustle and scold?

Warmth stands. It always stood. The dose sneaked a ride on love and charged love for the lift.

I speak as one who made that mistake for years. I called dessert love while love sat beside me uneaten, the talk, the laughter, the warm kitchen, the full plate cooling while I chased bloom. I handed bright bars to children and called it kindness while kindness was the walk, the story, the lap, the attention. I thanked the wrapper for evenings the room had made. I blamed the evening when the wrapper left me flat.

Look at your own reward frame with kind eyes.

"I deserve one." Deserve what? Rest? Kindness? Marking? You deserve rest, so rest. You deserve kindness, so sit, eat hungry, stop satisfied, talk long. You deserve marking, so light candles, lay the table, gather people. The bright dose never gave rest. It gave ten bright minutes and hours dull. The bright dose never gave kindness. It gave filming and scold. The bright dose never marked the day. It smeared the day with rustle and left morning-you to find wrappers.

Celebrating with chocolate never made celebration. Celebration made the chocolate seem warm. The warmth was the people, the laughter, the break in routine, the love in the room. Take people away and eat the same bar alone, fast, standing in a cold kitchen at midnight while already full. Where is the love then? Where is the celebration? Same sugar. No warmth. That single comparison ends the story. Love never lived in the paper.

Ask three questions and let only honest answers stand.

When you shared a meal hungry with people you love, did joy need bright paper to arrive? When you ate bright paper alone while full at night, did joy arrive? When you remember the sweetest evenings of your life, do you remember taste on the tongue, or faces in lamplight?

Only one honest seeing fits those evenings. Meal gave. Company gave. Hunger met gave. Bright paper took while giving nothing, then sent the bill as hunting an hour later.

Hear the verdict short.

Love was the meal, never the wrapper.

### LIVE THE ORDINARY MEAL

Bring this home tonight, not as a test and not as a vow, but as inhabiting.

Cook simply or buy simply. Market vegetables. Fresh bread. Fruit for after. Soup, salad, beans, rice, cheese, eggs, whatever your table loves. Sit hungry. Wait for the clean call if it has not come yet. Drink water. Lay the table. Sit down together or sit down glad alone. Eat with attention. Notice heat, crunch, juice, salt, sweet living inside real things. Talk or rest as you eat. Chew. Taste. Lay the fork down when the quiet close comes. Leave food without care. Get on with enjoying your life.

No counting. No earning. No paying yourself with bright paper for virtue. No punishing yourself after. Hunger and satisfaction are sufficient guides, old as breath, and they return within a meal once nothing shouts over them.

You will find, as I found, as thousands have found, that the body thanks you at once. Not with fireworks. With quiet. Hours without hunting. Mind on people and work. Mouth clean. Belly easy. Evening kind. That quiet is not emptiness waiting to be filled. That quiet is freedom warming, the natural baseline returning on its own tide because you fed it and left it alone.

Lift your head here. You came fearing meals would turn grey without bright hits. See now that grey was the cost of hits, not the absence of them. Flat, foggy afternoons came from the wheel, not from the lack of wheel. Colour lives in hunger answered, in satisfaction reached, in real food enjoyed as favourite among people you love.

Marvellous things wait in that ordinary doing. Breakfast eaten hungry and forgotten till lunch. Lunch enjoyed and forgotten till supper. Fruit sweet and finished. Bread broken and enough. Evenings where the kitchen closes with plates cleared and no cupboard calling. Mornings where thought starts clear because no echo governed the night. Talk that runs long because hands are not rustling. Laughter that stays bright because mood is not flickering. This is not a smaller life. This is eating grown back to its full size.

Bring your hunger to the table. Trust its clean voice. Stop at the quiet close. Enjoy real food as favourite. The feast was never in bright paper. The feast was always you, hungry, satisfied, free at an ordinary table.

**6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION**
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food without bright doses does not turn grey, hunger makes plain meals sing and satisfaction makes the singing complete.
- Hunger comes clean and open toward many good foods and stays quiet for hours once answered.
- Hunting comes fast and narrow while already full, opens only toward wrappers and closes never.
- Satisfaction arrives as quiet ease and releases thought back to life, bright packets bring only pause and renewed circling.
- The body keeps its own steady fuel from ordinary meals, leaving bright imports out leaves nothing real out.
- Sweetness living inside fruit and plain meals belongs to free eating and is enjoyed without fear.
- Love sat in the meal and the company and hunger met together, the wrapper only rode along and charged for the ride.
