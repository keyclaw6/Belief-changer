Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
The kitchen table with yesterday's wrappers — The desk drawer at half past three — The bathroom mirror and the aching molar — The shop receipt pinned to the fridge — The full column beside the empty column

*Forget whether it harms you for a moment — tell me one true thing it does for you.*

### THE COURTROOM WHERE YOU ALWAYS LOSE

You come into this chapter carrying one honest seeing from last night.

You saw your own hand move before thought, break its own number while making it, lower the wrapper noise on the stairs. You saw that free choice needs no hiding and no apology. You were caught, not weak. Good. Hold that. We shall need it.

Because caught people do one very sensible thing in the wrong courtroom. They argue harm against benefit and always lose.

I know your argument by heart because it was mine. We say, in our own words, I know it rots teeth and piles weight and costs money, but life would be greyer without sweet treats. At least it gives me something. At least it helps.

That little but is the whole Trap talking.

While we debate harm, the con is safe. Harm can be argued for years. One expert warns, another soothes, a friend says her grandfather ate sweets daily and lived to ninety, a label says low fat, a packet says made with real fruit, and by supper we are tired and reach for a dose to calm the debate. Harm invites delay. Harm invites tomorrow. Harm lets us say, I will cut down when the dentist speaks sternly, after the holidays, when money is tight.

I am not asking you to weigh harm. I lived that weighing for years and it never freed anyone.

I am asking a plainer question, the one the Sweet Con fears.

What does it actually do for you?

Not what does it do to your teeth or your wallet or your waist. We will look at those because they are true, but they are not the test. Even if every dose were free and weightless and left teeth white as milk, the question would stand. What good is there at all?

A prisoner does not ask whether his cell is painted a cheerful colour. He asks why he is locked in.

So lay yesterday on the table and judge as an investigator, not as a defendant. Bring the real day, crumbs and all. No tidy eating for my benefit. The truth does not fear daylight. Only cons fear it.

### YESTERDAY ON THE TABLE

Take an ordinary day. Not Christmas. Not a binge you are ashamed to name. A normal Tuesday with normal doses.

Morning. Tea and toast grown sweet, a spoon or two, a spread, a juice that calls itself healthy. Mid-morning at work or with chores, a biscuit with coffee, a bar from the drawer because the morning feels long. Lunch hurried, bread and something sweet to finish, a fizzy bottle cold from the machine. Afternoon at the desk, the familiar tug, two or three from the shared tin, eaten without looking up from the screen. Evening, tired, a pudding earned because the day was hard, a handful while cooking, a square while watching. Night, the ten o'clock reach we already witnessed, standing in socks on cold tiles.

Now set two columns beside that day, as you would for any honest audit.

In the first column, what it did TO you. Be plain.

Mood through the day: bright for minutes, then flat, then hunting again. That low hum by four o'clock, the short temper, the fog when you try to read the same line twice. Teeth: the film after each dose, the ache when cold water hits the molar, the hygienist scraping longer each visit and shaking her head. Wallet: the coins that never seem much until you pin a month of receipts to the fridge and see dinners eaten in wrappers. Time: the minutes hunting, queuing, unwrapping, hiding the evidence, promising morning-you will be good this week, scolding night-you for breaking it. The tug-of-thought from morning till night, thinking about sweets far more than any free eater ever thinks about food.

That column fills fast. You know it. I know it. We all lived it.

Now the second column. What it did FOR you.

Not what it stopped you feeling for ten minutes. Not what it saved you from that it had set up an hour before. A true gift, over and above what a person who never touched a sweet dose feels. A lift that leaves you higher than baseline. A joy that stays after the swallow. A help that makes the hour richer than if you had never reached.

Write it.

Most readers sit with the pen hovering here. They write taste, then cross it out, because after the second mouthful taste barely registered. They write energy, then pause, because the hour after felt foggy, not bright. They write comfort, then stare, because comfort that ends in scolding is a strange comfort. They write reward because, well, celebrating with chocolate and a treat when you feel you deserve one is what we were taught to say.

I ask you to stay with that hovering pen. Do not rush to fill the column to be polite.

You tell me you believe it is a genuine treat or fuel. Those are your words for it, learned young. A treat is a gift that makes an occasion sweeter. Fuel is a power that lets the engine run stronger. A genuine treat would leave the evening warmer for having had it. Genuine fuel would leave the afternoon clearer for having burned it.

Look at yesterday and tell me, where is the gift that stayed? Where is the power that lasted?

Ask only from your own lived day, not from slogans, three plain questions.

Did any dose yesterday leave you calmer, brighter, more yourself an hour later than if you had never taken it? Did any dose close hunger and let you walk away humming, as a real supper does, with no call back? Did any dose add anything to the hour that the hour did not already own — the laugh, the rest, the company, the break itself?

Only one set of answers fits the day we all share.

There is noise in the first column and silence in the second. Plenty happening TO you. Nothing arriving FOR you.

### TEETH, MONEY, HOURS

Let me speak plainly about the TO side, because soft talk here would cheat you, and then I shall lift it off you at once.

Frequent free sugars feed the acid in plaque that rots teeth. That is mechanism, not moral. The mouth after a sweet dose turns sharp for a while, and frequent sharp hours carve. Brushing matters, care matters, a good dentist matters, and still frequent sweet doses keep the drill busy. Any dentist will show you the map in your own mouth. I am not diagnosing you and I am not giving dental orders. If you live with diabetes, take anything that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any history where changing food could carry risk, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Money and hours tell the same story without drama. Guidance in this country says keep free sugars below a tenth of daily energy, near fifty grams, with further gain below half that, yet normal shelves and normal days carry most of us far above it. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low. The packs are built to feel normal. The till roll tells the truth the tongue forgets: small daily doses, priced to feel like nothing, gathered into hundreds over a year, thousands over a decade. Add the minutes — the detour to the shop, the queue, the drawer raid, the evening promise and morning review — and ask what those hours bought.

Now hear me, because I do not want you quitting out of fear.

Fright about teeth or money or guidance never freed anyone for long. Fear makes braced readers, and braced readers cannot see. I state the cost so you stop calling this harmless, not so you stop out of dread. You are not to leave this chapter frightened of BAD SUGAR. You are to leave it unimpressed by BAD SUGAR.

There is a world of difference between those two, and your freedom lives in the second.

What would you call a man who tripped you on the stairs each morning so he could offer you his hand and be thanked as a rescuer?

Hold that question lightly. Do not answer yet. Let it walk beside you while you look at the empty column.

Do you want to keep paying, reaching, hiding, regretting, for the rest of your life for a column that never fills? Or do you want to be clear, calm, un-torn, happy to be free, for the rest of your life with food that answers hunger and lets you go?

Both futures last the same length. Only one has anything in it.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Let that sentence arrive out of your own yesterday, not as a slogan. Plenty TO you — the film on teeth, the flat afternoon, the receipt pile, the night reach. Nothing FOR you — no true gift that stayed, no power that lasted, no hour made richer than baseline. Speak it in your own mouth and feel how settled it sits. The debate ends where the benefit fails to appear.

### JUDGE BY BENEFIT ALONE

I know this is hard to accept, but sit with yesterday once more as the investigator you now are.

We were brainwashed to call bright doses love and reward and energy. Kind hands taught us. Bright shelves taught us. Songs and parties taught us. We learned before we could test, as children learn honest faces. Then each dose left its own small echo an hour later and we read the echo as need and answered with another dose. That wheel taught us to say treat and fuel with complete sincerity while living flat, foggy days that never felt treated or fuelled.

You are not foolish for believing it. You were conned, as we all were. The shame belongs with the con, never with you.

So here is the new courtroom, single question, no weighing.

**3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU**
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit

Bring that empty column into tomorrow. Watch the desk at three, the sofa at nine, the cupboard at ten, and ask, what did that dose do FOR me that stayed? You will find the hand still reaches from habit for a while, and the meaning has changed. The tug is not proof of value. The tug is echo. Echoes fade when no belief feeds them.

**SUMMARY**
- Harm debate keeps the Trap safe by inviting delay and tomorrow, so the test is benefit alone.
- A normal day fills fast with what sweets do to mood, teeth, wallet and hours, while the benefit side stays empty on honest audit.
- Frequent sweet doses feed plaque acid that carves teeth, and normal intake sits far above guidance, yet feeling fine misleads.
- Treat and fuel promise a gift that stays and power that lasts, and neither appears an hour later in lived days.
- Plenty arrives TO the eater with each dose and nothing true arrives FOR the eater, so the old outweigh story cannot stand.
