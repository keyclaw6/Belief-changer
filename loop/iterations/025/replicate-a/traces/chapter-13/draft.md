Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A kitchen table with a photographed list — A closed book beside an empty plate — A plain meal waiting ahead, eaten hungry — Morning light on an ordinary street

*You have nothing left to study and nothing left to mourn, only a free life to live and sweetness to enjoy again.*

### There Is Nothing More To Learn

You came to this book believing you would need rules to carry around forever.

"How will I cope at birthdays?" "What will I do at the cinema?" "Will I need to keep reading and re-reading to hold myself clear?" I hear that whisper because we all heard it. We believed freedom would be heavy luggage we must drag behind us, page after page, year after year.

The fact is, you carry nothing now.

We carried the weight before. We carried the hunt into every morning, the bright paper into every bag, the fog into every afternoon, the rustle into every evening. We called that normal and told ourselves a free eater must live by vigilance. Now look with your own eyes as witness.

When you woke clear this morning, did you need a lesson to stay clear till lunch? When you walked the shop with clear eyes, did you need a lecture to walk past paper? When you ate supper hungry and left it at satisfied, did you need a rulebook to close the meal?

Only one honest answer fits. Living free needs no teaching. Living trapped needed constant teaching — scores, wages, promises, tomorrows, allowances, apologies.

So close the school. You are not a pupil on probation. You are an eater returned to nature. Hunger guides. Satisfaction closes. Real food pleases. That is not a system to memorize. That is the body you were born with, speaking clearly at last.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar, are pregnant, or carry medical risk around food, keep your clinician in charge and follow their advice while you enjoy this freedom.

There is no second course. There is no advanced level. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have followed them. Now live.

### Get On With Enjoying Your Life

This is my order to you, given with joy, not with warning.

Get on with enjoying your life.

Take the holiday. Cook the supper. Meet friends. Laugh at the film. Walk the morning street with light feet. Talk of work, of love, of weather, of anything real. Let BAD SUGAR become background noise while foreground life grows loud and bright.

We once believed joy lived in bright paper and life without it would turn grey. The fact is, paper borrowed brightness and repaid greyness. Mornings fogged. Afternoons dipped. Evenings rustled. The debt ran underneath every laugh. Now no debt runs. Taste sharpens. Hunger sharpens. Joy sharpens. Marvellous how little ceremony freedom needs — food, water, rest, warmth, people — and how fully life thanks you when fog leaves.

Do not stand at the open door admiring the daylight. Walk through it. Do not count clear days. Inhabit clear days. Do not test freedom by staring at hunters. Pity them quietly and walk past easy, happy to be free among shelves that once hunted you.

I closed my own last page years ago and expected a great emptiness to follow. What followed was washing, shopping, cooking, sleeping, waking. Ordinary days, lived bright. The book shrank behind me with every clear hour till I could barely recall the hunt except with relief. Yours will shrink the same way. That is not carelessness. That is cure.

When a sweet thought crosses — wrapper bright, smell warm, old memory glowing — you know what to do. You will not fight it. You will not mourn it. You will not debate it. You will answer with the joyful shout that has become your natural reply, and feel relief flood warm:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Each firing makes the next firing faster, till thoughts pass like weather and leave the sky clear. No vigilance. No armour. Life lived.

### The Sweetness That Was Handed Back

Hear the last truth, kept for this hour, because only a free eater can hear it rightly.

You did not lose sweetness. Sweetness was stolen from you and has now been handed back.

We were conned into believing BAD SUGAR was sweetness itself — love, reward, comfort, celebration. Doses coated the tongue, borrowed hunger, shouted over meals, then left the mouth flat, foggy and craving more. The child with the peach knew sweetness before paper taught its shout: hunger met, juice bursting, pleasure quieting into satisfied, then running off to play. That eater never left you. That eater was buried under tolls.

Think. Which life do you want for the rest of your life? The toll paid daily, the lift-crash-repeat, the box emptied before the film ends, the morning fog, the evening hunt, the tug of bright paper forever?

Or the clear life for the rest of your life? Mornings met hungry. Meals tasted fully. Evenings soft without rustle. Shops simple. Mind quiet. Joy unborrowed.

Only one honest answer fits. And you have already chosen it.

This was never about quitting a pleasure. This was about growing back into the eater you were before the con found you. The Sugar Trap took real sweetness, sold it back in bright paper at a cruel price, and called the theft a treat. Now the debt is cancelled. Hunger is sweet again. Fruit is sweet again. Supper is sweet again. Morning air is sweet again. No paper needed to sweeten what is sweet by nature.

So eat. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Eat gladly. Laugh fully. Leave bright paper untouched without comment, or take fruit gladly when offered. Sleep soft. Wake clear. Let mornings prove it. Let hunger prove it. Let evenings prove it. Each ordinary day returns what was taken — taste bright, belly calm, mind quiet.

You are free. You will stay free while belief stays dead, and belief stays dead because you see. No sacrifice was made, because nothing real was offered. It does plenty to the eater. It does nothing for the eater. Seeing that, there is nothing to miss and everything to gain, easily, immediately and permanently, as a life, not as a sentence.

Put the book down. Leave it closed. Go to the meal ahead hungry and meet it with joy. The road stays bright because you keep your eyes on where you are going.
