Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Market hall at eight with crates and steam — Fresh bread torn open with bare hands — A plain table with soup and fruit — The clean call of true hunger — The quiet close of satisfaction — Evening company with laughter and full plates

*Hunger met by real food is the feast itself, and BAD SUGAR never added a crumb to it.*

### THE GREY FEAR

There is a fear that sits quietly behind all the talk of fuel and focus, and we must bring it into daylight because it holds many good eaters in place.

It sounds like this, in your own voice:

"Surely food will turn grey. Surely meals will turn flat. Surely love will leak out of eating."

I know that voice. I lived with it for years. We all did. We told ourselves dessert is love. We told ourselves a treat when you feel you deserve one keeps life kind. We told ourselves celebrating with chocolate makes the occasion. Take the bright paper away, we thought, and what is left? Chewing. Duty. Beige plates eaten under bright light while everyone else laughs around something sweet.

That fear is honest. I honour it. No one wants a greyer life. No one picks this book to eat sawdust and call it virtue.

It is also back to front.

Ask from your own life, calmly, as an investigator who has already seen the afternoon wheel for what it was.

When you sat hungry at a table with people you love and good plain food steaming in front of you, did you ever think, if only there were more sugar on this? Or did hunger itself make the first mouthful sing? When you were a child, truly hungry after play, did an apple or a slice of bread ever taste dull? When you pushed back from a meal satisfied, warm and easy, did you feel deprived because no wrapper followed? Or did you feel complete?

Only one honest answer fits. Hunger makes food joyful. Satisfaction makes joy complete. Bright doses never taught that music. They drowned it.

You entered these pages fearing food joy was sugar. You will leave this chapter having lived one ordinary meal in your mind and body so clearly that joy stands where it always stood, in hunger, in satisfaction, in real food eaten as favourite.

### MARKET MORNING

Come with me to one ordinary market morning. No lecture. No rules on a card. Lived doing, noticed bite by bite.

Eight o'clock. Cold air. Crates stacked high. Dark green leaves wet with spray. Oranges bright as lamps. Apples with bloom still on the skin. Bread on a wooden shelf, loaves split at the top, steam rising when the baker breaks one open. Cheese with rind. Eggs brown and speckled. Herbs tied with string. Fish on ice. The whole hall smells of earth and yeast and peel.

You are hungry. Properly hungry. Breakfast was hours ago, or you skipped it without noticing because the morning ran busy. Your stomach calls with an open, clean emptiness. Not hunting. Not twitching toward one bright paper. Open toward food, any good food.

You buy what your nose tells you. A tomato heavy for its size. A handful of green beans that snap. A loaf still warm. A peach that gives under the thumb. Nothing measured. Nothing counted. No voice in the head saying you have been good this week and may now pay yourself. Hunger is pay enough.

Home. The kitchen fills with chop and sizzle. Onion softening in oil. Beans brightening then darkening. Bread torn, not sliced, crust cracking under your hands. Salt. Pepper. Lemon. Oil that smells grassy. No packet rustle governs the hour. Hands move. Mouth waters. That watering is ancient and wise. It knows what to do with real food.

Sit. Eat.

First bite of warm bread with soup, taken hungry, stops thought for a second. Crust shatters. Crumb pulls soft. Soup warms the chest outward. Tomato sharp then mellow. Beans with bite. Peach after, juice down the wrist, sweet in the way living things are sweet, thơm and bright and finished in three minutes.

Notice what happens as you eat. Taste grows. Then taste eases. Hunger quiets mouthful by mouthful. Breath slows. Shoulders drop. Conversation returns. At some point, without decision, the hand rests. The plate holds a little and no one cares. The body says, enough, in a quiet, firm voice. You stop. Not because a rule told you to stop. Not because guilt tapped your shoulder. Because satisfaction closed the door quietly behind hunger.

That is the whole authority, met in one meal. Hunger opens. Satisfaction closes. Real food carries you from one to the other and leaves you free for hours.

Now set beside that picture your sweetest evening grazing.

Hand moving in bright packets while already full. First second blooming on the tongue, second second fading. Hand moving without tasting. Stomach full, mouth still hunting. That I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling arriving an hour after supper, opening only toward wrappers, closing never. Evening ending flat and cross, mouth filmed, head woolly, heart faintly scolding.

Which of those two was joy? Which was love? Which left you grateful to be alive in a body that knows how to eat?

It was never the sweet. It was you, hungry and alive.

### HUNGER HAS A CLEAN VOICE

We were taught to fear hunger and to misread every gnaw as emergency. That teaching kept the drawer full.

Real hunger has a clean voice once you listen again.

It comes slowly, open and empty, growing toward mealtime. It welcomes many foods. Bread appeals. Soup appeals. Fruit appeals. Cheese appeals. The body says, feed me, and leaves the menu to nose and habit and love. It does not stamp its foot for one bright paper. It does not arrive ten minutes after a full meal. It does not tremble. It builds, it is answered, it passes for hours.

You know this voice. You knew it before you learned wrappers. Every child knows it. That small child with the peach, stopping mid-bite to run off.

Hear how different is the other call.

It comes fast, narrow and edgy, an hour after eating, at the desk, on the sofa, in the kitchen at night while already full. It scorns bread. It scorns soup. It wants only the bright hit, the dose, the fix taken standing up. It closes never, even as the box empties. Full stomach, hunting mouth. That is not hunger wearing a louder coat. That is echo wearing hunger's accent.

The fact is, your body knows how to hold its own steady fuel from plain meals. The glucose your blood needs, your body already knows how to make from ordinary food, hour by hour, without any bright import. Leaving BAD SUGAR out is not leaving fuel out. Clean baseline without bright doses is physiologically coherent and complete. If you live with diabetes, take anything that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any history where changing food could carry risk, talk to your clinician first and follow their advice.

Feel the relief in that plain fact. You are not asked to run on empty. You are asked to let hunger do its old job and let satisfaction do its old job, while the body quietly keeps the lamps lit between them.

Think of a morning you ate well because you were hungry. Porridge with fruit. Eggs with toast. Soup left from night before. No counting. No bargaining. How long before food crossed your mind again? Three hours? Four? Thought free for work, for play, for people. That long quiet between meals is hunger's signature. It feeds and releases.

Think of a morning that began with a sweet drink and a sweet bar eaten on the run, without hunger. How long before hunting returned? An hour? Less? Thought circling the shop, the machine, the cupboard. That short leash is not hunger's signature. It is the wheel turning, dose calling to dose.

You say, but I feel hunger all the time. I say, of course you feel hunting all the time while bright doses keep echoing. Hunting while already fed proves nothing about your need. It proves the echo. Let hunger speak without the echo for a day, fed by real meals to satisfaction, and you will hear the clean voice again underneath, patient and wise as ever.

We fell into the trap together, all of us, eating without hunger and stopping without satisfaction, starting without choosing and continuing without tasting. Warm complicity here, never blame. Who taught us to distrust the stomach and obey the wrapper? Everyone around us obeyed the wrapper. Bright packets sat at every till. Kind hands pressed them on us as love. Tired hands reached for them as help. We were conned, not foolish.

Escaping it is your own bright doing. You learn again in one meal what no packet ever taught. Hunger trusted. Hunger answered. Hunger gone for hours.

### SATISFACTION CLOSES QUIETLY

Satisfaction is the most slandered joy in the modern kitchen.

Diet weeks taught us satisfaction is danger. Stop early. Leave hungry. Drink water to fill the gap. Count. Measure. Earn. Pay yourself later with a sliver that becomes a box. No wonder we stopped hearing the quiet close. We were too busy obeying noise.

Real satisfaction does not shout. That is why we missed it.

It arrives as ease. Warmth spreading. Taste narrowing to gentle. The next bite pleasant but not urgent. Breath deeper. A small sigh. Hand resting without order. Mind turning outward again, toward talk, toward work, toward the window. The plate may hold food and no voice scolds. The body has taken what it needs and returns you to your life.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as lived instruction, not as diet small print. It means no clocks ruling the stomach. No good-food list and bad-food list to memorise. No moral arithmetic after eating. Hunger calls, you eat with appetite. Satisfaction calls, you stop with thanks. Between those two calls you live, not chew.

Try it in memory with the market meal.

You ate hungry. You tasted. Midway, taste peaked. Near the end, hunger thinned to comfort. You stopped with bread still on the board and fruit still in the bowl, and felt no loss. Why would loss live there? Loss lives only where echo lives, where the second bite barely registers and the hand moves without joy. In a real meal, stopping feels like arrival, not denial. The door closes quietly and you walk through the afternoon free.

Ask again, from lived hours.

When you stopped a real meal at satisfied, did your mind circle food for the next hour? Or did food vanish from thought till hunger called again? When you stopped a packet at empty, did your mind rest? Or did it circle scold and vow and plan the next bright buy? Which stopping was peace? Which was war?

There is only one fit. Satisfaction satisfies. Packets pause.

I speak as one who chased pause for years and called it pleasure. I ate standing up, straight from bright bags, tasting only the first second. I ate after supper while full, hunting while fed, scolding while hunting. I called dessert love while love sat beside me uneaten, the talk, the laughter, the warm kitchen, the full plate cooling while I chased bloom. I believed joy lived in the wrapper because the wrapper shouted loudest in the room.

Joy lived in the room. The wrapper rode along.

### REAL FOOD, EATEN AS FAVOURITE

Here is the inhabiting this whole chapter exists to give you.

Real food, eaten hungry to satisfaction, is favourite. Not second best. Not medicine you take to earn treats. Favourite.

Taste proves it the moment hunger leads.

Bread torn warm, crust and crumb. Butter melting to gold. Soup that warms the hands through the bowl. Potato crisp outside and cloud inside. Greens with bite and lemon. Rice steaming. Beans earthy. Apple cracking. Berries staining the fingers. Peach running. Cheese deep and long. Herbs lifting the whole plate. These are not dull foods waiting for sugar to rescue them. They are wide, various, vivid foods that satisfy and release.

This does not mean sweetness itself is banished from your life. That was never the decree. A ripe peach is sweet. Berries are sweet. An apple cold from the market is sweet. Sweetness living inside whole fruit, inside vegetables, inside a plain meal cooked with care, belongs to the menu of a free eater and is enjoyed without fear and without counting. What leaves your life is BAD SUGAR, bright doses taken as hits between hunger and beyond satisfaction. Fruit enjoyed hungry to satisfied needs no wrapper and leaves no echo.

Feel how eating returns to joy when those doses no longer shout over it.

Mornings clearer because breakfast, eaten hungry, holds you. Midday steady because lunch, enjoyed to satisfaction, releases you. Evenings kinder because supper, shared and savoured, completes you. Thought freed from circling the drawer. Mouth freed from filming. Temper freed from thinning by four. Money freed drip by drip. Time freed hour by hour. That is not a greyer life. That is colour returning to hours that had gone flat and woolly.

You say, food is love, sugar is love. I say, watch love closely and see where it sat all along.

Was love in the bright bar handed without looking, eaten without tasting, gone without thanks? Or was love in the hands that chopped, the pot that steamed, the table laid, the hunger met together, the talk that ran long after plates emptied? Was love in celebrating with chocolate eaten fast while eyes stayed on screens? Or was love in the celebration itself, the laughter, the company, the day marked, the warmth in the room that would have warmed with any food or none?

Take the brightest memory you guard, birthday candles, cinema lights low, sofa with a friend after hard news. Replay it slowly. Keep the light. Keep the laughter. Keep the people. Keep the hunger met. Lift the bright dose out of the picture like a smudge wiped from glass. Does warmth drain? Or does warmth stand clearer, no longer smeared by rustle and scold?

Warmth stands. It always stood. The dose sneaked a ride on love and charged love for the lift.

That is the credit inversion this chapter lands, flat and joyful. Meal gave. Company gave. Hunger met gave. Bright paper took while giving nothing, then sent the bill as hunting an hour later.

Hear the verdict short.

Love was the meal, never the wrapper.

### LIVE THE ORDINARY MEAL

Bring this home tonight, not as a test and not as a vow, but as inhabiting.

Cook simply or buy simply. Market vegetables. Fresh bread. Fruit for after. Sit hungry. Eat with attention. Notice heat, crunch, juice, salt, sweet living inside real things. Talk or rest as you eat. Lay the fork down when the quiet close comes. Leave food without care. Get on with enjoying your life.

No counting. No earning. No paying yourself with bright paper for virtue. No punishing yourself after. Hunger and satisfaction are sufficient guides, old as breath, and they return within a meal once nothing shouts over them.

You will find, as I found, as thousands have found, that the body thanks you at once. Not with fireworks. With quiet. Hours without hunting. Mind on people and work. Mouth clean. Evening kind. That quiet is not emptiness waiting to be filled. That quiet is freedom warming, the natural baseline returning on its own tide because you fed it and left it alone.

Lift your head here. You came fearing meals would turn grey without bright hits. See now that grey was the cost of hits, not the absence of them. Colour lives in hunger answered, in satisfaction reached, in real food enjoyed as favourite among people you love.

Marvellous things wait in that ordinary doing. Breakfast eaten hungry and forgotten till lunch. Lunch enjoyed and forgotten till supper. Fruit sweet and finished. Bread broken and enough. Evenings where the kitchen closes with plates cleared and no cupboard calling. Mornings where thought starts clear because no echo governed the night. This is not a smaller life. This is eating grown back to its full size.

**6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION**
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

**SUMMARY**
- Food without bright doses does not turn grey, hunger makes plain meals sing and satisfaction makes the singing complete.
- Hunger comes clean and open toward many good foods and stays quiet for hours once answered, hunting comes fast and narrow while already full and closes never.
- Satisfaction arrives as quiet ease and releases thought back to life, bright packets bring only pause and renewed circling.
- The body keeps its own steady fuel from ordinary meals, leaving bright imports out leaves nothing real out.
- Sweetness living inside fruit and plain meals belongs to free eating and is enjoyed without fear, bright hits taken beyond hunger belong nowhere.
- Love sat in the meal and the company and hunger met together, the wrapper only rode along and charged for the ride.
