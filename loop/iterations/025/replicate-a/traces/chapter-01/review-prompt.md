### What This Book Asks — And What It Never Asks

Now for our contract. Every honest book makes one. Here is mine, stated plainly so you can decide.

I will not bore you with chemistry lessons. I will not frighten you into change. I will not shame you for yesterday's box. I will not tell you that you are ill or broken. You are neither. You are a sensible person who was sold a story about sweetness before you were old enough to question it, and who has lived inside that story ever since, as we all did.

What I will do is walk with you, chapter by chapter, through one clear seeing at a time. I will ask you to look at your own afternoons and evenings with fresh eyes. I will ask you to notice what the dose really does in the hour after, not only in the ten bright minutes during. I will ask you to meet your own justifications — "I need sugary somethings to get me through," "a treat when you feel you deserve one," "I've managed to be good this week so one can't hurt" — and to test each against your own lived evidence.

And what do I ask of you in return? Very little, and all of it kind.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That sentence will return in this book because it is the whole method in miniature. Not most of the instructions when you feel like it. All of them, exactly as given. They are small. They are easy. They are there to keep your mind open while the seeing does its work.

The second part of my promise concerns your eating while you read. Listen carefully, because this surprises people.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Let that settle like a warm hand on your shoulder. No skipping meals to prove you are serious. No heroic hunger. No punishment for yesterday. Hunger is your friend, not your enemy. Satisfaction is your guide, not your limit. Real food, eaten with attention and pleasure, is part of freedom, not an obstacle to it.

Do you feel the difference from every diet you have tried? Those plans began by closing your life down. This book begins by opening it. You are not being shrunk. You are being invited.

There is a reason for this kindness, and it is practical, not sentimental. A hungry, punished reader cannot think clearly. A reader who is told to stop at once spends the whole book listening through a roar of loss, waiting for the pain to start, unable to hear the argument. I do not want you braced. I want you curious.

So read as an investigator, not as a defendant. You are not on trial here. BAD SUGAR is on trial, and you are on the jury. Weigh what I show you against your own afternoons, your own evenings, your own desk at three o'clock and your own sofa at ten. If I am wrong, you will know. If I am right, you will feel that quiet click of recognition that no diet slogan ever gave you.

Some readers worry at this point: "But if I carry on exactly as normal while I read, am I not proving I am happy?" No. You are proving you are willing to see before you act. That is wisdom, not weakness. The mother who checks whether the mushroom is safe before she serves it is not in favour of poison. She is in favour of truth.

Others whisper: "I have failed so often. What if I fail at this too?" Let me answer that with the warmth you deserve. You did not fail. The rules failed you. A method that depends on daily battle with your own hunger and joy is not a method for freedom; it is a trap with a timetable. This time there is no battle, because there is nothing worth fighting for on the other side. There is only a con to see through.

And that brings me to the first and lightest of my instructions, the one that opens the door for everything that follows.

Read it slowly. Let it land as a spoken promise between us.

**1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND**
Question what you think you know about sweetness.

**SUMMARY**
- This book is not a diet lecture and does not work by rules, hunger, or punishment.
- BAD SUGAR is a narrow, boxed target, separate from fruit, vegetables, and plain satisfying meals.
- An escaped sweet eater speaks here, not a judge, and the reader is invited as an investigator.
- Reading asks for openness and attention while life and meals continue as normal.
- Freedom ahead is framed as clear seeing and immediate gain, not loss or battle.
