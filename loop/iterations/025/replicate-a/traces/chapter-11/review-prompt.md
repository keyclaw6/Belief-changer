### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP SPEAKING

You have killed every carve-out. The daily graze stands seen. The afternoon lift stands seen as a loan. The sweetest moment stands returned to people and place. The Friday glow, the house rule, the weaning trick — all paint washed off.

I know that voice because we all spoke it. We held the book in one hand and a bar in the other and called delay wisdom. We told ourselves the roller coaster can be stepped off later, when the fair is quieter. We told ourselves sugary somethings to get me through just this week, then freedom.

Let me show you what that later really does, because you have lived it and never watched it whole.

Tomorrow keeps today safe for the trap. While tomorrow stays bright, today stays dim and excused. You tell yourself you will be strong later, so you permit yourself to be hooked now. You picture a calm future week with no tired evenings, no hard afternoons, no parties, no stress — a week that has never existed and never will. You wait for a sea with no waves to learn to swim.

Ask, with your own calendar as witness.

When did tomorrow ever arrive as a kinder week with fewer birthdays and less tiredness? When did waiting ever make the hunt quieter, rather than louder with anticipation? When did I want to get off the roller coaster ever mean I want to stay on it one more month?

There is only one honest answer to all three. Waiting never freed. Waiting fed.

Hear it short.

Delay is feeding.

The Sweet Con loves tomorrow because tomorrow never comes to trial. Today can be tested. Today can be seen. Tomorrow can be polished forever without proof. To say after the holidays is to say the birthday table still lights paper, the holiday evening still needs melt, love still needs a wrapper. You have already seen those lights lie. You are not reckless to act now. You are clear. The clear eater does not need a holiday to see. The clear eater sees in the middle of noise and rejoices that noise never mattered.

### THE TWO LYING DOORS

Hear both doors answered in one line.

Bad days need rest, not rescue. Good days need rejoicing, not proof.
