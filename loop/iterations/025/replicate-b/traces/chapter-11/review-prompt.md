Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A kitchen table at dusk. An ordinary bar from an ordinary shop. A clock with no tomorrow on its face. A vow spoken out loud. A bright morning without a hum.

*Waiting never made freedom safer — this ordinary dose, seen clearly, ends the wait now.*

**THE TOMORROW THAT NEVER COMES**

You know everything now.

You know there is no safe Friday sweet. No clean cupboard that cleans the mind. No short fall off a cliff. You have watched the evening box empty after one permitted bite and understood why one never stays one. You have seen the seatbelt for what it is — for accidents, never for steering at walls.

And still a soft voice says, not yet. After the holidays. After the birthday. After this stressful patch. Monday. January. When life calms.

I say this with warmth for you and with steel for the voice that tutors you. That voice is not wisdom talking. It is the Sweet Con talking in your voice.

We all lived in that waiting room. We called it being sensible. We called it picking the right moment. I called it my twelve Januarys, my after-Christmases, my when-work-calms. Each date arrived with its own excuse attached. Each farewell tour needed a proper send-off with the favourite dose bought in the favourite size. The line never shortened because the line was the trap.

“I’ll start after the holidays. I’m almost ready, just not today.”

I hear the hope in that. I hear the fear underneath it too. Let us answer both with clear eyes, not with pressure.

If BAD SUGAR were harmless joy, why leave it at all, tomorrow or ever? If it is a con that lays fog on afternoons, thirst under sweetness, broken sleep, sour mouth, evening twitch, money paid for nothing, why pay for one more day of it?

If tomorrow is safer, what changes overnight in the dose that makes today dangerous and tomorrow kind?

If waiting proves control, why does waiting feel like deprivation with a calendar?

If you truly chose this joy freely, why do you need a book to help you keep choosing it next year?

There is only one honest answer. Delay keeps the belief alive by promising a future courage that today’s belief forbids.

Tomorrow is the Trap’s favourite room. It admits you must leave while insisting you may stay. That is the tug-of-war of craving and fear held at both ends by the same hands.

You do not need a better date. You need a clearer now.

Freedom is available the moment the belief changes, not at some future milestone. Do not postpone the opening of a cage.

**ARE YOU CHAMPING?**

So here is the gate, and I put it kindly and firmly.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing at the bit. You should feel a bright impatience to be done, not a grey dread of what comes next.

If you feel excitement rising, read on. If you feel flat dread, do not force yourself forward with clenched teeth. Put this chapter down, go back through the rooms we walked — the Friday week that made precious, the house rule that moved the address, the cliff that promised a hover — and return when the picture is clear. No hurry. No panic. No failure in pausing to see.

I say this because I know you. You are the convinced reader at the edge, wanting certainty. You have done the seeing. You want to know the seeing will hold.

It will hold, because seeing is not mood. You have seen that the lift makes the low. You have seen that the scene gave the glow. You have seen that one carries the evening onward. That seeing does not wash off overnight.

And remember, you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain.

There is no loss here to mourn. Mourning belongs at a friend’s grave. This is an enemy’s death. Rejoice at a dead enemy, never mourn a lost friend.

If dread whispers that life without sweet hits will be grey, ask dread its question. When did eating become grey except under rules, waiting, and fog? A hungry meal of real food, eaten hungry, stopped at satisfied — was that grey? A morning with clear head, steady hands, quiet belly — was that grey? The grey was the roller coaster you called life. Colour returns when the coaster stops.

You can leave this easily, immediately and permanently.

That is not a boast. That is the nature of escape once the benefit is gone. It takes no effort to stop feeding a belief you no longer hold.

I promise you that as one who stood where you stand, bar in hand, heart thumping, wanting to believe and afraid to believe. I stood there. I saw. I vowed. I walked out and never looked back with hunger again.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

**AN ORDINARY DOSE, SEEN CLEARLY**

Now we do the ritual — not as experiment, not as laboratory tasting, not as special farewell tour with bells and candles. As ordinary life, seen clearly for the last time.

Fetch your ordinary dose. The bar you always buy. The biscuits you always reach for at ten at night. The can you crack at three. Nothing grand. Nothing rare. The usual fix in its usual wrapper from its usual shop. We close the Trap with truth, not with ceremony around rarity.

Sit where you usually sit. Unwrap it as usual. Do not change the hour to make it holy. Do not starve all day to make it bright. Take it exactly as the trapped eater took it — because you are still, for these few minutes, the trapped eater saying goodbye with open eyes.

And now pay attention. This is the whole work of the next minutes.

Notice the hurry in the hand. Notice how fast the first bite asks for the second, before pleasure has even registered. Notice the loudness — sugar shouting over taste buds until everything tastes of shout. Notice the sticky film on teeth, the rush of thirst underneath the sweet, the chew that turns to paste and then to want.

Notice how little savouring there is. A peach eaten hungry by a child closes with quiet. This opens with noise. Ten bright minutes of buzz, then fog gathering at the edges. Then the faint twitch for more before the mouth is empty.

Ask, while it is in your mouth, the question that ends it.

What did it do FOR me just now? Not TO my teeth, my thirst, my fog, my wallet, my evening — FOR me, as help, as fuel, as love?

Did it feed hunger or feed the Nibbler? Did it lift me above normal or return me briefly toward normal from a low the last dose laid?

We were brainwashed to call that cloying rush a genuine treat. Seen with attention, it is cloying. Sticky. Loud. Rushed. Gone. The glow you remembered from cinemas and birthdays is not in this wrapper. The glow was people, laughter, leisure, hunger met. This was only ever sneaking a ride on those moments.

Look at the ugliness with kindness for yourself and contempt for the con. Let the eye stay open. Let the mouth tell the truth it always told when you were not rehearsing pleasure.

That sticky hurry is the Trap without its paint. Once seen, it cannot be unseen.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That pair is the whole book in two sentences. Hold it while the mouth is still sweet, and feel the belief loosen its last cord.

**WHAT THE ECHO FEELS LIKE**

At this point many ask, with real courage, what the body will do next.

“Will the craving be terrible? Will nights be hard? Will I suffer for weeks?”

Hear me clearly, because calm here is truth, not sales talk.

The physical echo is tiny. A mild, well-defined grumble, small in scale, already dying from the moment you stop feeding it. For most it is days-long — an empty hum that visits for a few evenings, peaks around two to five days, then thins and passes. Not a siege. Not a life sentence. A hump in the road, not a wall across it.

You have already felt it a hundred times between doses. That restless reach at ten at night. That three o’clock hollow. You called it need-something-sweet-now and answered it with a hit that guaranteed its return. Now you will feel the same hum once or twice unfed and watch it fade for lack of fuel. Same feeling. New meaning. Not a command. A death throe to rejoice at.

Do not watch the calendar for it. Do not wait for suffering to prove you were hooked. Many notice almost nothing except brighter mornings and steadier afternoons, and then wonder what all the fear was about. If a grumble comes, smile at it. It is the little creature starving. If no grumble comes, smile anyway. Freedom does not need to hurt to be real.

The fear of that hum kept you pacing by an open cage long after the door stood open.

Both ropes of that pacing belonged to the Trap — fear of failing and fear of succeeding, fear you cannot stop and fear life will be empty if you do. Both dissolve in the same seeing. You can stop because there is nothing to stop with effort, and life is fuller because nothing real is removed.

Do not change from fear of a hard week. Change from joy at seeing the road wide and bright.

**THE TWO FALSE DOORS**

Before we vow, I warn you about the two doors that will never need to open again. I show them now so that later, when a thought slides by, it arrives already labelled as script, not as your reasoning.

The first door is the bad day.

It will come — a hard afternoon, a row, a bill, rain on the way home, tiredness with sharp edges. And the old script will slide in, warm and helpful: you deserve a little comfort, just this once, food is love, sugary somethings to get you through.

Hear it for what it is. BAD SUGAR never comforted a bad day. It laid fog on a bad day and charged you for the fog. It turned a hard hour into a hard hour plus crash plus second reach plus morning flatness. True comfort on a bad day is rest, real food eaten hungry, kind company, sleep, air. The dose comforted the low the last dose laid and called it kindness. The pusher offering a hand out of the ditch he pushed you into.

The second door is the sweet lie of one.

It will come — brighter, calmer, almost philosophical: you are free now, so one cannot hurt, just a taste to prove freedom, a celebration sweet because this moment is special.

Hear it too for what it is. One deliberate dose inside BAD SUGAR re-fires want, re-lays the low, re-polishes the next exception. It does not prove freedom. It rehires the belief. A free eater needs no proof by tasting. The proof is mornings lived with ease, shops walked with pity, not hunger, evenings closed with quiet.

A blip by accident — a misread label, a mouthful taken in trust — revives nothing by itself. Smile at how clearly the eye now sees, and drive on. Deliberate is different from accidental the way steering at a wall is different from hitting a pothole. One teaches nothing. The other teaches you to steer straight and think nothing more of it.

Guard the belief and the behaviour looks after itself. Reopen the belief and no rule will save you. That is why totality is kindness, not harshness. Leave the whole of it and you are free of the whole of it.

There are no other doors. Bad day and just one — both wear kinder faces, both are the same Trap.

11. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

Say it now, out loud if you can, solemnly and joyfully, as a vow that ends bargaining.

I see it now. This never helped me. This never was love or fuel. I am done with BAD SUGAR. I leave the whole Sugar Trap now. I am free.

And as the last sweetness fades from the mouth, feel what that means. Not waiting to be free. Not earning freedom day by day. Free as of now. The cage door open and you walking out, marvellous and light, into ordinary air.

Congratulations. You have done the brave and intelligent thing. You have chosen escape, not loss.

From this moment, whenever sweet craving or sweet memory crosses your mind — a wrapper rustle, a smell from a bakery, a birthday table, an evening hum — meet it with one joyful thought that replaces the old reach:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Think it brightly. Think it loudly inside. Think it with a smile for yourself and pity for trapped users still paying for fog. BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! turns an intrusive echo into a recurring hit of relief. The thought that once ordered a dose now confirms freedom. Each firing weakens the old script until it fires no more.

Get on with enjoying your life — mornings clear, shops easy, meals closed with quiet satisfaction, evenings yours again. You are happy to be free, never reopening the decision, never mourning, never counting, celebrating the wonderful relief of escape.

**SUMMARY**
- Tomorrow never made leaving easier, waiting only polished the want while life paid daily.
- Readiness is bright impatience to be done, and lingering dread means look again, not push harder.
- The ordinary dose taken with attention tastes rushed and cloying, not loving, and proves the glow lived elsewhere.
- The body echo is small and days-long, a fading hum to smile at, not a siege to fear.
- A hard day never needed a sweet fix, and one deliberate taste never proved freedom.
- Freedom starts at the vow, lived at once with relief, and every later sweet thought confirms it.
