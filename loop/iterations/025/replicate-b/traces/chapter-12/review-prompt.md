Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
First light in the kitchen. A breakfast bowl eaten hungry. A bright aisle and a checkout belt. A quiet desk at three. An evening sofa with tea. A passing sweet thought met with joy.

*You do not watch freedom to keep it — you live it, morning to night, and it keeps you.*

**THE MORNING AFTER**

Good morning, free eater.

You woke this morning without a vow to make and without a rule to remember. No drawer to clear. No promise to rehearse. No bargain about being good all week. That lightness in your chest is not accident. That is what mornings feel like when no hum orders them.

I stood where you stand now, the morning after my last ordinary dose, and I kept waiting for the heaviness to arrive. I waited for the sense of loss to tap my shoulder. It never came. What came was kettle steam, daylight on the worktop, birds loud outside, and a belly quietly asking for breakfast. Ordinary. Bright. Mine again.

You are happy to be free, and today you get to prove it the pleasant way — by living.

We used to start the day inside fog and call it normal. We woke flat and thirsty, mouth sour, head dull, and we read that flatness as need for a lift. We reached for something quick and sweet to get moving, and we called the ten-minute buzz energy. Then the fog returned by mid-morning and we blamed sleep, age, weather, anything but the hit that laid it.

Look at this morning clearly. No buzz is needed to begin. Hunger wakes cleanly. Legs move. Mind clears with washing and air. This is the body returning to its own baseline on its own, with nothing added and nothing forced.

Change nothing else in your life. Go to the same kitchen at the same hour. Make the same tea or coffee you love. Open the same curtains. Freedom does not ask for a new routine, a new shop, a new personality. It asks only that you live the old routine with open eyes.

If you live with diabetes, take medicine that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for the belief only.

That said, walk with me through this ordinary day, and see how easy ease is.

**BREAKFAST EATEN HUNGRY**

Eat when you are hungry. Stop when you are satisfied. Enjoy real food.

You know that rhythm because your body never forgot it. Watch hunger arrive as it should — low, slow, open, willing to be pleased by a proper meal. Sit down. Eat with attention. Notice flavour returning now that loud sweetness no longer shouts over it.

I had my first free breakfast at the same table where I used to graze through packets standing up. I sat down with bread, butter, eggs, a sliced apple, plain yogurt. I ate hungry. I stopped at quiet. No counting. No guilt. No second reach pulling my hand back to the cupboard. The quiet after was new to me, and wonderful. That quiet is satisfaction, and it was always yours.

Think of that small child with the peach who stops mid-bite and runs off to play. No lecture taught that ease. Hunger met, pleasure taken, done. You eat the same way now — not by rule, by nature.

This is important because the old voice will try to tell you that eating will be grey without bright hits. Ask the bowl in front of you if it is grey. Is warm bread grey? Is ripe fruit grey? Is a hungry belly filled to quiet grey? The grey was never in real food. The grey was in the roller coaster we called life, the short lift then the fast drop, the being good to earn a crash. Colour was always here, under the noise.

Do not weigh, measure, or moralise your plate. Do not turn breakfast into a project. You left the whole of BAD SUGAR, and real meals belong to you as favourite. Some mornings you will want a big breakfast. Some mornings a little. Some mornings fruit sings. Some mornings bread and cheese satisfy deeper. Trust the belly to ask and to tell you when enough is enough. It knows.

We once fed doses to a feeling that was never hunger. Hunger is patient and low and glad of food. The old hum was sudden and high and named a brand. Now you know the difference in your mouth and belly, and you will never confuse them again.

Eat, savour, stop. Get on with your morning.

**THE BRIGHT AISLE**

Sooner or later today you will walk past bright packs.

Go as usual. Buy food as usual. No detour. No wide berth. No clenched preparation. You walk old contexts confidently to prove freedom, not to test it.

The supermarket will do what it always did. Colour shouting. Cartoon mascots grinning. Checkout shelves crowded with last-minute doses at child height. Air sweet near the bakery. Trolleys full of family-size packs bought by kind, tired people who believe they choose freely.

We were all conned there together. I know that aisle ache — the eyes drawn before the mind decides, the hand half reaching, the small lift of excitement that was never about taste. It was about a belief polished for years until a wrapper shone brighter than any food inside it.

Now look with clear eyes.

Pick up nothing to argue with. Simply see. See the doses stacked as profit, not pleasure. See the bliss-point engineering that needed your repeat hunger to pay for itself. See mothers buying love in packets because the posters told them love comes foil-wrapped. See men buying fuel that lays fog. None of it is about you now.

And when you see someone lifting a bar with that old eager hurry, let your heart do the free thing.

Do not envy. Pity.

Does that really look like enjoyment? The quick tear, the fast chew, the eyes elsewhere, the hand already back for more before joy registers? You are not being deprived of that. They are still paying for that. You remember the hurry, the sticky film, the thirst under the sweet, the ten bright minutes then the dull pull for another. You remember evenings lost to rustle and regret.

Say inside, kindly and firmly: poor soul, still in the ditch, still thanking the hand that keeps him there. I am glad I am out.

We do not look down. We were there. I bought the favourite bar in the favourite size a thousand times. I hid wrappers. I promised Fridays. I know the ache from inside, and I know it ends. Warm to the person always. Harsh to the con that farms them.

You walked out of the Sugar Trap for good, and the aisle proves it.

That ease is not luck. That is seeing holding.

**THREE O'CLOCK WITHOUT NOISE**

Afternoon will come, as it always does.

At three the office air thickens. Computers hum. Shoulders tighten. The drawer where doses lived will catch your eye for a second. Someone will unwrap something and the smell will drift. The old script may slide by, helpful as ever: something quick to get through, a little lift to finish the day.

Smile. You know what that lift was.

We all lived that desk hour. We answered the dip with a hit, felt ten minutes of buzz, then watched fog gather by four and reached again. We called the buzz function and the fog tiredness. It was the other way around. The hit laid the low it then briefly muted, and charged us for both.

Now sit through the hour with clear eyes and watch what actually happens without a dose. A small wave of tiredness rises. You drink water. You stretch. You eat a proper bite if hunger is truly there — bread, nuts, fruit, leftovers enjoyed till quiet. You breathe. The wave passes on its own, as waves do. Mind returns. Hands steady. Work finishes cleaner without the second reach and the third.

That hum is the Nibbler dying. Not demanding. Dying.

Greet it kindly. It is tiny, starving, already fading from lack of feed. Its grumbles are death throes to rejoice at, never orders to obey. You have felt that hum a hundred times between doses. Now you feel it once unfed and watch it thin and pass. Same feeling. New meaning. No fear in it.

Do not watch the clock for it. Do not wait for suffering to prove you were hooked. Many free eaters notice almost nothing except steadier afternoons and brighter mornings, and then laugh at all the dread. If a grumble visits for an evening or two, smile at it and get on with enjoying your life. If none comes, smile anyway. Freedom does not need to hurt to be real.

You need no lift from outside. The body lifts itself when the load is removed.

Evening is the kindest test, because evening was the Trap's favourite hour.

Same sofa. Same lamp. Same tea. Same people you love. Nothing changed except the belief, and that changes everything.

We used to open the evening with a permitted sliver and close it with an empty box. We told ourselves one biscuit is free choice while the cupboard stood half-empty and the hand moved before decision. We knew the rustle, the hurry, the regret before swallowing ended. We called it comfort. It left us flat, restless, mouth sour, sleep broken, morning dull.

**WHEN A THOUGHT SLIDES BY**

Let me tell you plainly what will happen in the coming days, so that when it happens you laugh in recognition.

Hear it for what it is. It is not your reasoning. It is the old script arriving late, wearing your voice.

If everyone eats it, does common prove good? Did common ever make a con true?

There is only one honest answer each time. The thought is a dead echo, not an order.
