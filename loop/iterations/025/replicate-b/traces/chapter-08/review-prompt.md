WHO BUILT THE WANT

**IN THIS CHAPTER**

Week after week since childhood, arm in arm with mothers who meant love, past screens that paired every laugh and every heartbreak with a bright dose, past teachers who paid good work in wrappers, past friends who said, "I've managed to be good this week," and, "treat when you feel you deserve one." If the want is natural, why does no child left to real hunger ever graze for hours on bright doses, but stops mid-peach and runs off to play?

If you live with diabetes or take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder past or present, your clinician leads and you follow their advice first. This book changes a belief about BAD SUGAR. It does not treat illness.

Still, look at your own days. You dose for a short term lift then fast drop. Ten minutes bright, then fog and second reach. Morning vow to be good, afternoon slump, evening box, night regret, morning vow again. Is that relief? Or is that the ditch and the hand you already saw — shove, fall, rescue, thanks — until thanks felt like love?

The Sweet Con borrows your low to sell its high. It lays the low with the last dose, then sells the next dose as rescue from the low. You thank the rescuer who is the perpetrator. You call the hand kindness and forget the shove.

Consider the plain proof of your table. Hungry to a favourite meal, noticed, savoured, stopped at satisfied — clear head, quiet belly, colour in food with no bright close. Then consider the grazing night. Picking, fogging, regretting while the wrapper is still in hand, flat, foggy and never satisfied. Which eater was you? Both were you. One was hunger met. One was timetable fed. The difference was never virtue. The difference was dose.

You do not need a bright hit to lift mood. You need clear hours to let mood lift itself. The lift you chase is relief from a low the last hit laid. Remove the shove and there is no ditch to be lifted from. The road is level. Walking is easy.

That is why heavy grazers are the least calm, not the most. If bright doses gave calm, the heaviest users would glow with peace. They pick. They hide wrappers. They plan to be good while chewing. They wake restless. The calm they remember was the hand, not peace.

See it and the reward story dies. Sweet never paid you for virtue. It charged you for the payment.

**BOTH ROPES BELONG TO THE TRAP**

Now to the fear that holds you after the facts have fallen. This is the heart of this room, so bring your whole heart.

You feel the tug-of-war of craving and fear.

One rope pulls forward. What if I fail? What if nights are unbearable? What if I am the one person this does not work for? What if I shame myself again?

One rope pulls back. What if I succeed? What if meals turn grey? What if I never celebrate again? What if I lose my sweet self, my me-time, my love in a bowl?

Forward fear. Backward fear. You stand shaking in the middle and call the shaking wisdom. It is not wisdom. It is the Trap holding both ends.

I know because I stood there. I feared failing for the rest of your life, dosing and mourning and restarting for the rest of your life. I feared succeeding into a grey life for the rest of your life, clear and dull for the rest of your life. Both pictures terrified me. Both pictures were painted by the same hand.

Let me show you the cell.

Picture a small cell with the door standing open. Light falls through onto the floor. Birds sound outside. A prisoner sits on the bunk, staring at the open door, begging to stay. Outside is too big, he says. Outside I will fail. Outside I will be no one. Better the known bunk, the known walls, the known hour for the bright tray. He clutches the bars from inside while the door stands ajar. He calls his fear common sense.

Now picture a lion born in a travelling cage. One morning the keeper leaves the cage open and walks away. The lion steps out, blinks, stretches, then paces back and forth before the open mouth of the cage all day. Not from need. The plains are there. Food is there. Freedom is there. From habit. The feet remember the small circuit. The mind mistakes the circuit for safety. Passers-by say, look, he loves his cage. He does not love his cage. He remembers it.

We are that prisoner. We are that lion.

Fear of failure is the prisoner begging to stay. Fear of success is the lion pacing by the open cage. Both look like wisdom. Both are cage-habit. No new bars hold you. Only remembered circuits.

Succumb to fear of failure and you guarantee the very thing you fear, because you keep dosing to avoid facing a dread that dosing itself maintains. Cling to fear of success and you mourn a friend who was never a friend, but a keeper who fed you bright trays to keep you pacing.

See who holds the ropes and the war ends in one glance. The Trap pulls forward with dread of nights. The Trap pulls back with dread of grey days. Cut either picture and the other falls, because one hand held both.

Ask the fear its own questions and watch it thin.

If failure is certain, why did watching the grumble unfed for nine minutes end it, with no force and no misery?

If success is grey, why was the plain meal hungry and satisfied vivid with colour, while the grazing night was grey?

If fear is wisdom, why does every clear eater you know pity the pacing, never envy the cage?

There is only one honest answer. Fear is not information. Fear is installation. The old voices warned you the outside is unbearable so you would never test the door. Test the door and dread dies at the threshold.

Do not change because you are frightened of cells and cages. Change because there is no cell, there is no cage, there is only an open door and wide plains, and you are about to walk them with relief. Fear showed you the fraud. Freedom walks you home.

You will wobble. All freed feet wobble at first. You will pause before the old aisle. You will hear the old bell at sweet o'clock. You will feel a ghost pull toward the drawer. That wobble is not need returning. That is habit pacing by an open cage. Smile at the pacing. Watch it fade for lack of audience. Each wobble unfed shortens the circuit until the feet forget it.

Think of a seatbelt for a moment, only as a picture of the mind you will keep. A seatbelt is there for an accidental jolt, never permission to drive wild. Your body can ride over an accidental crumb without ruin. Your mind must never steer toward a deliberate dose. The belt guards accidents. It never licenses steering into a crash.

You are ready to close the gate on other voices. Too many kind, wrong voices rebuild the Sweet Con daily — the diet friend, the moderator, the social feed, the well-meaning expert who never saw the trap. You investigated. You saw the builders. You saw the mirror lie. You saw both ropes in one hand.
