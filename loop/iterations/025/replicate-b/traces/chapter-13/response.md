Chapter 13
GET ON WITH ENJOYING LIFE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A photographed list on the table. A closed book by the lamp. A kitchen with daylight coming in. A real meal waiting hungry.

*You need no more teaching to stay free — you have seen, you have crossed, and now you live.*

**NO MORE LESSONS NEEDED**

Good. You are here. You are free. And a small voice says:

“What if I forget? What if I need one more chapter, one more rule, one more teacher to keep me straight?”

I know that voice. I have heard it from every free eater at this exact doorway. It sounds sensible. It sounds humble. It is the last whisper of the old schooling that told you freedom must be maintained by effort.

Let me answer you plainly, as one who walked out and never went back.

You do not need ongoing teaching to stay free. You needed teaching to see. Now you have seen.

We spent years believing that staying clear of BAD SUGAR would demand study, vigilance, endless review. We believed we were fragile and the bright packs were mighty. We believed a free life would be a long exam we might fail. It was the other way around.

The trapped life was the exam. The counting. The bargaining. The being good all week to earn a crash. The Monday restarts. The drawer cleared and refilled. The promises whispered over an empty box. The roller coaster we rode and called appetite. That was hard. That was endless work for no pay.

Freedom is not hard. Freedom is the absence of that work.

Did you ever need lessons to keep you from drinking washing-up liquid? Do you rehearse reasons each morning not to chew paper? Do you fear you will forget and fall into the ditch you can now see with open eyes?

There is only one honest answer each time. You do not forget seeing. You do not need to guard what you no longer want.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

You have followed them. You kept an open mind when the claim sounded too good to be true. You read with a full cupboard and changed nothing until seeing was complete. You judged by what it does for you and found the benefit column empty. You stopped blaming yourself for failures that belonged to the wrong method. You began with joy rather than dread. You trusted hunger and quiet. You saw the low hum as dying. You shut out conflicting voices. You left the special sweet with no exception kept. You took your last ordinary dose with attention, vowed, and rejoiced. You refused to reopen what seeing closed.

That is the whole method. There is no second method behind it. There is no advanced course.

Think of Tuesday morning next week. You wake. Kettle on. Light through the blinds. A belly asking cleanly for breakfast. No lecture plays in your head. No list is checked. You eat. You stop. You go to work. Where is the effort you feared? Where is the lesson you thought you would need? Living has done the work while you were busy living.

Think of the shop on Thursday. Bright packs shouting. Trolleys rattling. Checkout shelves crowded at child height. You walk through with steady chest. No debate starts. No pull holds. You put real food in the basket and walk out into daylight. Where is the vigilance you were told would be required for life? It is nowhere, because there is nothing to watch. A free eater does not patrol freedom. A free eater inhabits it.

Think of the tired evening that will surely come. A long day. A short temper. A house loud with needs. The old script slides by, polite as ever: something quick to get through. You smile. You know what that quickness was and where it led. You make tea. You eat supper hungry to quiet. You rest. You sleep. Morning confirms what evening promised. The script thins each time it meets joy, until it stops visiting at all.

I promise you this as settled fact: a free eater does not stay free by remembering. A free eater stays free by seeing once, clearly, and then living. Living does the remembering for you.

If you take medicine for blood sugar, live with diabetes, are pregnant, or carry any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for the belief only.

For the rest of us, the schooling is over. The life is open.

Close the ledger. There is nothing more to earn. There is nothing more to prove. There is only the bright, ordinary business of mornings and meals and work and love, carried on with clear head and quiet belly. That is not maintenance. That is enjoyment.

You came to learn how to escape. You have escaped. Now get on with enjoying your life. That was always the assignment.

**WHAT YOU CARRY IS ENOUGH**

You came to this book clutching rules. You leave with eyes.

That is deliberate. Rules break. Eyes hold.

We were all trained to carry lists in our pockets — allowed foods, forbidden foods, grams, points, good days, bad days. We carried them like charms and watched them fail every evening at ten o’clock, hand moving before decision, box empty before joy arrived. The list never saved us because the belief behind the list still said bright hits help.

Now the belief is gone. What you carry is not a longer list. It is a shorter memory, portable, warm, enough.

You carry hunger you can trust. You carry satisfaction you can feel. You carry mornings that start clear without a lift from outside. You carry afternoons that pass without noise. You carry evenings that close full without rustle and regret.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

That single sentence is not a diet. It is permission to be an eater again, as you were before loud sweetness shouted over flavour. Some mornings you will want bread and eggs. Some mornings fruit sings. Some noons you will want soup and cheese, warm and slow. Some evenings a plain supper eaten hungry to quiet satisfies deeper than any box ever did. No weighing. No earning. No penance after. The belly asks. You feed it real food. It says quiet. You stop. That quiet was always yours.

I eat that way now at the same table where I used to graze standing up. Bread torn, butter melting, eggs hot, apple sliced, water cold. I sit. I taste. I stop. No second reach pulls my hand. No fog gathers by four. No sour mouth wakes me at three in the morning. The meal ends and the day continues, clean and usable. Marvellous, is it not, how satisfying satisfaction is when noise no longer interrupts it?

And you carry two pictures of time, to choose between whenever a memory crosses.

Picture the first: evening hurry and morning fog, money paid for dullness, hours lost to grazing and hiding wrappers, teeth and mood and sleep paying the bill, the short lift then the fast drop repeated for the rest of your life. Is that comfort? Is that love? Is that reward? Could any honest heart call that a treat?

Picture the second: clear head, steady hands, food tasting brighter, money kept for living, evenings long in the good sense, sleep deep, mornings bright, hunger met and quiet reached, repeated for the rest of your life. Is that deprivation? Is that loss? Is that grey? Could any honest heart call that missing out?

You have already chosen. The choice holds because seeing holds.

When a sweet memory crosses — birthday candles, cinema laughter, holiday lights — meet it with relief, not debate. Do not argue. Do not weigh. Ask its one question and it collapses. Was the glow in the dose, or was the glow in people and rest and the day done, with the dose sneaking a ride and taking credit? You know. You were there. You have replayed the same warmth without the dose and found warmth unchanged.

Were those cinema laughs ever made of chocolate? Was that birthday love ever baked into icing? Was that holiday ease ever poured from a bottle? There is only one honest answer each time. The scene gave everything. The dose gave nothing.

I think BRILLIANT! I’M FREE! and I smile. Think it with me now, brightly, loudly inside. Feel how it turns an echo into relief. Feel pity for the trapped hurry still paying for fog, never envy. That is guarding the belief, and it takes three seconds, with joy.

You need carry nothing else. Hunger, quiet, clear mornings, steady afternoons, full evenings, one bright thought for passing echoes, and two pictures of time that decide themselves. Small enough for a pocket. Large enough for a life.

**SWEETNESS HANDED BACK**

Now hear the last reveal, saved for this doorway. Listen carefully, because it changes the name of everything we have done together.

You have lost no sweetness.

Sweetness was stolen from you, and has now been handed back.

We entered the trap as children who loved food. We ate hungry. We stopped at quiet. A peach could delight us. Warm bread could delight us. Milk, apples, berries, soup, potatoes from the earth — all sweet in the true sense, all vivid when hunger met them. No one taught us that joy. The body brought it.

Then bright doses arrived, loud, engineered, polished by posters until wrappers shone brighter than food. They shouted over taste. They laid fog and called fog normal. They created a hum and sold back brief muting of it. Bit by bit, real flavour dimmed under noise. Bit by bit, we called the ten-minute buzz pleasure and the flat after-life. Bit by bit, we believed life would be grey without the very thing greying it.

Do you see? The grey was never in a world without bright hits. The grey was in the ride we mistook for living — short lift then fast drop, being good to earn a crash, craving sweets to get through.

Take the loudness away and what returns? Not emptiness. Taste.

This morning fruit tastes brighter because shouting no longer drowns it. Bread tastes deeper. Hunger feels clean. Quiet after eating feels wonderful rather than strange. Water tastes cold and good. An apple crunches and the juice runs and the belly says yes and then says enough, and that enough feels like wealth. Colour was always here, under the noise. You did not step into a smaller life. You grew back into the larger one you were cut from.

This was never about quitting. This was about growing.

A child does not quit crawling to walk. A child grows into walking and never looks back with hunger for the floor. You do not leave bright doses by loss. You grow past the need for them by seeing, and the grown eater does not mourn the floor.

I stood in my own kitchen the week after my last ordinary dose and ate a simple meal hungry — vegetables from the market, bread, cheese, an apple sliced slow. I waited for the sense of missing to arrive. What arrived was flavour so clear it almost laughed at me. Steam off the vegetables. Salt on the bread. Sweet sharp juice of the apple. Quiet in the belly after. I thought: so this is what eating was before the noise. I had handed nothing away. Life had been handed back.

Were apples ever grey? Was warm soup ever dull? Was a hungry belly filled to quiet ever empty? There is only one honest answer each time. Grey was the fog we bought. Colour is the baseline we rejoined.

You are that eater now.

We were conned together, you and I, by makers who needed our repeat hunger to pay for brilliance. Warm to yourself always. Harsh to the con that farmed us. You were never weak. You were outgunned and misled, and now you are undeceived. The strong will that persisted against instinct is yours again, turned toward living.

Marvellous, is it not, how ordinary feels wonderful when no master calls? How exciting a plain table looks when no hum orders it? How wonderful hunger feels when it is allowed to speak and allowed to rest?

You are not a person missing sweetness. You are a person to whom sweetness has been returned — the true sweetness of hunger met, flavour noticed, quiet reached, life tasted without shouting.

Keep that picture. It will never fade, because your own days will repaint it every morning. Breakfast will repaint it. Lunch will repaint it. Supper with people you love will repaint it. Each clear morning layers proof on proof until doubt has nowhere to stand.

Growing does not reverse. Seeing does not unsee. You have grown. Walk on in the larger life.

**CLOSE THE BOOK AND LIVE**

So now — live.

Close this book. Photograph the list below. Put the book aside. Go to your kitchen, your table, your people, your day.

There is a meal ahead. Go to it hungry. Eat it with attention. Stop at quiet. Notice how good real food tastes when no loud hit orders the evening. Notice how steady the afternoon runs without a ten-minute buzz and a four o’clock fog. Notice how kind the evening feels on the same sofa, same lamp, same tea, with company and rest doing the warming they always did.

Go to shops as usual. Walk the bright aisle without detour and without debate. See packets as profit, not pleasure. See tired kind people buying love foil-wrapped because posters told them love comes that way. Pity silently. Love openly. Speak only when invited. Let visible ease do the recruiting. You walked out. Your brightness is the kindest argument.

I walk that aisle now and feel nothing but relief and a tender ache for those still in it. I see the father lifting family-size packs to prove plenty. I see the student grabbing a quick hit to get through. I see the mother at the checkout, child pleading, tired hand yielding. We were all there. We all believed we chose. We all paid for fog and called it fuel. My heart says: poor souls, still paying. Glad am I to be out. No lecture forms. No envy stirs. Only gratitude that paper and colour no longer order my evenings.

Go to birthdays. Go to cinemas. Go to holidays. Sit where bright doses circulate and feel no envy and no heroics. The scene gives joy — laughter, leisure, people, hunger met. Doses circulate unmourned. A simple no thank you, I am happy without, ends any pressing. No apology owed. No debate entered.

Was that birthday joy ever in icing? Was that film laughter ever in a wrapper? Was that holiday glow ever in a glass? There is only one honest answer each time. People, rest, leisure, the day done — those made the warmth. The rest took credit.

Go to hard days the same way. Rest when tired. Eat when hungry. Ask for help when heavy. Sleep early when drained. Drink water. Step into air. Eat a proper meal to quiet. Call a friend. True comfort actually comforts, and charges nothing. The dose never solved a hard day. It laid fog on it and charged for the fog.

Change nothing else in your life. Same kitchen. Same desk. Same sofa. Same celebrations. The contexts that once cued reaching now cue rejoicing, because the belief that lit them is gone. Each ordinary day layers proof on proof until doubt has nowhere to stand. Soon you will stop thinking about freedom at all, which is the deepest freedom — to live without reference to a trap.

Whenever sweet craving or sweet memory crosses your mind, meet it with relief that you no longer feed it. Think your bright thought. Feel pity for those still caught, never envy. Never reopen what seeing closed. Get on with enjoying your life — marvellous, wonderful, exciting in its ordinary light.

You are out. You are staying out. Not by effort. By sight.

Are mornings clearer? Are afternoons steadier? Is the belly quieter? Is money kept? Is the mind less noisy? Is food more tasty now that shouting no longer drowns it? Watch your own days. Your own lived days are the witness no argument can overturn.

Now take the list. It is small enough to carry and large enough to hold you. Photograph it. Keep it by the lamp, on the phone, on the fridge, until you laugh at needing it at all. Then keep living, and let living keep you.

Rejoice. Celebrate. Enjoy. You are free. You are happy to be free. And you will remain happy to be free for the rest of your days, with joy that deepens as memory fades.

Now close the book and go to your meal. Life is waiting, hungry, bright, yours.
