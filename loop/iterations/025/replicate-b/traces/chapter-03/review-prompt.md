WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**THE WRONG COURTROOM**

Good. You saw the hand move before the decision.

You saw that late reach was not free choice and not weakness. That was the first click of the lock. Keep that seeing with you, because we need it for the next click, and this next click turns the whole case around.

Up to now you have been arguing the case in the wrong courtroom.

You have been asking, does BAD SUGAR harm me more than it pleases me. Is the harm big enough to make me change. Can I live with the harm if the pleasure is real. You weigh teeth against birthdays. You weigh weight against comfort on a hard day. You weigh worry against that bright lift at three in the afternoon.

We all argued it that way. I argued it that way for years. It feels sensible. It feels adult.

The fact is, it is a con trick of a question.

While you weigh harm against benefit you have already granted the benefit. You have let the Sweet Con smuggle its main lie into court as an agreed fact. The judge has accepted without evidence that there is a pleasure on the scales, and now the only argument is how heavy the harm must be to outweigh it.

No wonder you never left that courtroom free. No wonder every health scare faded by Friday. A scare can make you uneasy for a night. It can never remove a pleasure you believe is real. You simply tell yourself, I know it harms a little, but life has to have some joy.

So let us close that courtroom and open the true one.

I am not asking you whether BAD SUGAR harms you more than it helps you. I am asking you what it helps you at all.

Forget the harm for an hour. Put teeth and weight and worry to one side. Let the prosecution rest. Let the defence speak alone. Tell me one true benefit that remains when the noise clears.

You say you love sweet hits and would miss them. I hear you with warmth, because I said the same with complete sincerity. I am not mocking what you feel. I am asking you to test what you feel against your own ordinary day, as an investigator, not as a defendant.

What if there was never anything on the benefit side of the scales?

**AN ORDINARY TUESDAY, LOGGED HONESTLY**

Take an ordinary Tuesday. Not Christmas. Not a wedding. A plain Tuesday, the kind we live most of our lives inside.

Morning. You mean well. Tea or coffee. Perhaps a sweetened cereal or a pastry grabbed on the way because the house was rushed. It tastes bright for a minute. Evening. The proper dinner you meant to enjoy is dulled because grazing filled the edges. Then the sofa. Then the permitted sliver that becomes the box. You finish feeling flat and vaguely ashamed, promising a cleaner tomorrow.

Now lay that Tuesday out in two columns, as you would a household account.

In the left column put what BAD SUGAR did TO you that day. Be honest and concrete. Money out at the till for packs that vanished without memory. Time lost in queues and vending corners and late-night cupboard stands. Teeth bathed in sweet acid again and again. Mood swung high then low then high then low. Hunger confused. Satisfaction missed. Sleep a little lighter. Morning a little duller.

In the right column put what it did FOR you. Not what it promised before the bite. What it delivered that stayed with you after the swallow ended.

Read your own right column back to me.

Where is the lasting energy. Where is the lasting calm. Where is the lasting joy you can point to at nine at night and say, that is still here because of the sweet dose at three. You remember the wrapper. You remember the reach. You remember the brief flash. You do not remember a benefit that remained an hour later and made Tuesday richer, kinder, brighter.

We remember paying. We do not remember receiving.

Ask yourself, quietly, without defending anything:

If a friend sold you a ticket every day and every day the show was cancelled, how long would you keep paying?

If a tool broke every shelf it touched, would you call it useful because it shone in the shop?

And if BAD SUGAR truly delivered a benefit, why do you need to keep re-dosing the same grey day to feel normal again?

Do you see the empty column? That emptiness is not your failure to enjoy properly. It is the evidence.

You never rise above how a clear person feels before the whole cycle starts. You sink below it, then climb briefly toward it, then sink again. We mistook the climb for a lift. The climb was relief from a dip the last dose helped dig.

Is that what happens at ten at night, standing up, half-listening for footsteps, eating fast without plate or chair, finishing grey before the swallow ends? You told me yourself in the plainest line trapped eaters speak: celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate. And then the other line, the private one: I feel like i cant stop once I start. Does that sound like a reward, or does that sound like answering?

A reward does not need hiding. A reward does not need a rule week to contain it. A reward does not leave you promising never to reward yourself again tomorrow.

So I ask you to hold the taught phrase up to the light and look through it.

We were told BAD SUGAR was a genuine treat or fuel.

Say it slowly. A genuine treat or fuel. That is the whole claim in one object. If it were true, Tuesday would show it. The benefit column would hold one solid gold coin that no cost could argue away. Instead Tuesday shows wrappers without memory, buzz without remainder, reward without rest.

What kind of treat leaves you flatter than before you took it?

What kind of fuel needs refuelling within the hour to stand still?

What kind of friend takes your money, time, teeth and calm and hands you back an hour of wanting more?

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Read that again and let it stand as flat fact, not as slogan. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you. The left column is crowded. The right column is empty. Once seen, that cannot be unseen.

**THE HAND IN THE DITCH**

Here is a question I want you to carry, quietly, without answering too fast.

What if the hand that lifts you out of the low is the same hand that pushed you into it that morning?

You feel low at three. You dose. You feel rescued. You feel grateful to the rescuer. We have all felt that gratitude. I felt it daily. Warm rush, sigh, thank goodness for something sweet.

But who dug the low you were rescued from?

Follow the day backwards. The low at three follows the flash at one. The flash at one follows the low at eleven. The low at eleven follows the sweet start at eight. Each rescue plants the next need. Each thank-you pays for the next push.

We thank the dose for ending a discomfort the doses keep creating. We call that relief pleasure. We call that relief fuel. We call that relief treat.

Is it possible we have been grateful to our pusher?

You do not need to settle the whole mechanism now. Other clicks will make the how completely clear. For now notice only the shape: low, dose, brief ease, lower low. The rescuer and the perpetrator wear the same bright wrapper.

If that shape is true, then the benefit column was never merely empty by accident. It was empty by design. The thing cannot give, because its only gift is a pause in the taking.

Ask yourself once more, with the Tuesday audit warm in your mind:

When has a sweet dose ever left you better than a clear person who never dosed, for longer than minutes, in a way you could point to at night?

When has one permitted sliver ever stayed one, without the twitch for more arriving behind it?

And if there is truly nothing FOR you there, what exactly would you be losing by walking away from it for the rest of your life, compared with what you would keep paying for the rest of your life if you stay?

Feel the weight of those two futures. On one side, grey Tuesdays repeated for the rest of your life for something that never gave you anything. On the other side, mornings, meals, shops and evenings met clear and calm for the rest of your life because nothing was taken from you. Same years. Opposite meanings.

The difference is not fear. The difference is arithmetic. Empty benefit against certain cost. No pleasure to outweigh, because no pleasure was ever delivered.

That is why the old courtroom kept you trapped. It asked how much harm you could bear for joy. The true court asks what joy ever arrived. And your own Tuesday answers, none that stayed.

Rejoice in that answer. An empty benefit column is marvellous news. It means there is nothing to miss, nothing to mourn, nothing to crave with fondness. It means the door out is not a door into loss. It is a door out of paying.

Forget harm against benefit and ask for the benefit alone.
