Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Morning market with wet crates and loud voices. Warm bread torn open with hands. A peach eaten over the sink. A plain table, a chair pulled close, hunger arriving politely. Evening light on a shared plate.

*Eating itself is joy, and hunger met by real food is your favourite.*

**THE GREY FEAR YOU CARRY**

You come into this room with the fuel lie dead behind you.

You saw the afternoon clearly. Low made by the earlier dose, lifted briefly by the next dose, remade lower. You know the buzz was debt payment. You stand as an investigator with clean hands. Good. Keep that clear head for what comes now, because what comes now is softer and more joyful.

Behind the fuel fear sits a quieter fear. It rarely shouts. It whispers.

Without sweet hits, will eating turn grey?

I hear you. I lived inside that whisper for years. I believed food joy was sweet joy. I believed a meal without a sweet close was unfinished. I believed dessert was love. I called dessert love out loud, at tables with people I love, and meant it: celebrating with chocolate, a treat when you feel you deserve one. That language was mine. I know how true it feels.

We all learned to speak that way. We learned to call the sweet close the point of the meal. We learned to photograph the cake and forget the faces around it. We learned to say the evening was wonderful because of what glowed in a bowl, not because of who laughed beside us. We learned to endure plain plates in order to earn sweet plates.

Let me ask you plainly, as one eater to another, with no trap in the question.

When you remember the meals you love most, what do you actually remember? The warmth of the room? The hunger you brought to the table? The first bite when the belly was empty and the mouth was alive? Or the wrapper?

When a child laughs through dinner and begs to stay up a little longer at the table, is she celebrating sugar or celebrating being together, fed, and free for the evening?

And if sweet hits made eating joyful, why are the sweetest eaters you know the least joyful about eating — picking, grazing, regretting, planning to be good this week while the plate is still in hand?

There is only one honest answer. Joy was never in the hit. Joy was hunger met, company kept, satisfaction closing the meal. The hit only ever sneaked a ride on that joy and claimed it.

You do not face grey eating. You face eating returned to colour.

I want you to feel that colour today, not as an idea for tomorrow. Not as a promise that plain food will do. As a lived encounter you walk through hungry and satisfied. Come hungry. Sit down. Notice. Taste. Stop at satisfied. That small circuit, lived once with open eyes, teaches more than a hundred arguments.

We fear grey because we have not truly tasted plain food hungry for years. We tasted it rushed, half-full from grazing, half-distracted by screens, half-guilty about rules, then blamed the food for being dull. The food was never dull. The hunger was never allowed to arrive. The attention was never allowed to land. Clear those two and colour returns at once.

Think of music heard through traffic. You would not blame the violin. You would close the window. Hunger is the quiet room. Attention is the closed window. Real food is the violin, always playing, waiting to be heard.

**HUNGER IS A FRIEND**

We were taught to fear hunger.

We read an empty belly as emergency. We read a growl as warning. We read the slow gathering of appetite through the morning as proof we must carry sugary somethings to get us through. We eat before hunger arrives, and then we wonder why satisfaction never arrives either.

Watch true hunger. I mean the real thing, the body asking for food.

True hunger arrives politely. It does not shout. It gathers slowly, an empty lightness under the ribs, a keen edge on smell, a thought of food that welcomes any plain meal. It waits. It does not tap rudely and demand one taste only. Offer it bread and it says yes. Offer it soup and it says yes. Offer it fruit and it says yes. Feed it and it leaves happily for hours.

We know this voice. We knew it before any sweet timetable taught us a louder voice. It is the oldest authority you own. It never needed teaching. It never counted. It never punished.

I want you to trust that voice again, not as an idea but as an encounter you live today.

Eat when you are truly hungry. Stop when you are truly satisfied. Notice the two moments the way you notice doors opening and closing. Hunger opens the meal. Satisfaction closes it. Between those doors lies the whole pleasure of eating, bright and complete, needing no rescue from outside.

You say, but I never feel that clean hunger anymore. I feel the frantic need at odd hours. I feel the I-need-to-eat-something-RIGHT-NOW feeling at three and at ten at night.

I understand. That frantic call is not hunger. Hunger is slow and patient and satisfied by plain food. The frantic call is fast and twitchy and satisfied by nothing for long. Different voices. Different masters. Learn the two voices and you will never confuse them again.

True hunger waits politely and leaves happily when fed. The other voice taps rudely and returns angrily when fed. One is the body asking for dinner. The other is the timetable asking for quiet. Food answers the first. Only time without bright flashes answers the second, by letting the timetable fade.

You do not need to fight the second voice. You need only recognise the first voice and obey it with joy. The first voice is yours. It was yours at five years old. It is yours now.

Consider how hunger behaves when you honour it. You wake, drink water, go about the morning. Mid-morning a lightness comes. Not panic. Lightness. Smells sharpen. The thought of lunch is welcome. You do not need to look at the clock. The body tells time better than the clock. You prepare food, you sit, you eat, and the lightness lifts into warmth. That is the whole movement. Simple. Kind. Complete.

Consider how hunger behaves when you interrupt it. You graze at ten from habit. A biscuit, a sweet drink, a handful from a tin eaten standing. Lightness never gets to gather. Lunch arrives while the mouth is still sweet and the belly still confused. You eat without keenness, stop without ease, feel full and empty together. You blame the lunch. Lunch did nothing wrong. The graze stole the hunger that would have made lunch shine.

The fact is, hunger is not a hole to fear. Hunger is appetite ready to enjoy. An empty belly before a meal is not suffering. It is readiness, the way thirst before cold water is readiness, the way sleepiness before bed is readiness. Readiness makes pleasure possible. Without readiness there is no pleasure, only stuffing.

Ask hunger its own questions and let the answers come in your body:

Does true hunger ever demand one brand, one wrapper, one sweet taste only, refusing bread, soup, fruit, rice?

Does true hunger ever arrive two minutes after a full meal, shouting, then vanish if you walk round the block and drink water?

Does true hunger ever leave you more restless after eating than before?

No. No. No. Then the frantic caller is not hunger.

Once you hear the difference you cannot unhear it. Hunger slow, welcoming, glad of plain food. Tug fast, narrow, fixed on sweet hits, never glad for long. One opens the meal. The other interrupts living. Follow the first and the second loses its voice from lack of audience.

**MORNING MARKET**

Come with me to the market on a Saturday morning, hungry.

Not starving. Not faint. Just clear, morning-hungry after sleep, belly empty, mouth clean, head clear. You slept, you woke, you drank water, you walked out with a bag over your shoulder. The air is cool. The crates are wet. Voices call prices. Hands move fast.

You pass tomatoes still warm from the van, skins tight, smelling of vine and summer. You pass greens with water beading on leaves. You pass potatoes with earth still on them, carrots in bunches, herbs tied with string that perfumes your fingers when you touch it. You lift a peach and smell it before you buy it. The smell goes straight to the belly and the belly answers with a clean pull. That pull is hunger welcoming the world. That pull is health.

You buy bread from the man with flour on his sleeves. The loaves are still warm. He tears the end off one for you to taste, the way market men do, and the crust cracks loud. Steam rises. The crumb is open and soft and smells of wheat and fire. You eat that torn end standing there, hungry, with market noise around you, crumbs on your coat. Taste it. Really taste it. Salt, chew, warmth spreading. No sauce needed. No sweet coat needed. Hunger makes it sing.

Walk on with the bag growing heavy. Apples, pears, plums, a melon that thumps hollow. Cheese wrapped in paper. Eggs in a box. Fish on ice with clear eyes. Nothing in the bag shouts. Everything in the bag promises. Your belly grows keener with every step, not frantic, keen. You are hungry and happy to be hungry, because you know where hunger leads: to a table.

Notice what is absent. No drawer walk. No frantic glance at the clock. No coins counted for a quick bar. No eating standing half-turned away with eyes on a screen. You are hungry in the open air, among real food, and hunger feels like anticipation, not panic.

This is your nature under the noise. This is the eater you were before bright flashes taught you to fear emptiness. Emptiness is not danger. Emptiness is readiness.

Take the bag home. Your hands smell of herbs and peach skin. Your belly is ready. The kitchen is cool and bright. Lay the food out and look at it. Colour, shape, smell. This is not virtue. This is not a plan. This is dinner waiting to happen.

Linger in the kitchen a while hungry. Wash the greens and hear the water. Slice tomatoes and watch the seeds shine. Rub salt between your fingers. Break herbs and breathe. Lay bread on a board and hear the crust. Cut cheese and smell its depth. Crack eggs for an omelette and watch them turn gold. Put potatoes on to boil and smell the earth leave them. This slow handling is part of eating. Hunger makes you notice. Noticing makes hunger keener. Keener hunger makes the meal brighter. Round and round, the good circle, turning without effort.

You say, I shop hungry and I buy too much. I lose control among plenty.

Look closer. The loss of control you remember happened in aisles built for grazing — bright packs at eye level, sweet drinks cold by the checkout, small bars for the queue, the air sweet with bakery perfume pumped to stir tug. That is not hunger among real food. That is tug among doses. Hunger among real food buys for meals. Tug among doses grabs for the minute. Different hunger, different shop, different outcome.

In the morning market among crates and loaves, hunger shops well. It chooses colour. It chooses weight. It chooses smell. It imagines meals, not mouthfuls. It buys enough and leaves gladly. Watch yourself do it today and trust what you see.

Carry that trust to the evening shop when you must go there. Walk in fed or truly hungry, never in the frantic middle. Walk the outer edges first where real food lives. Fill the basket with meals. Pass the bright middle aisles without argument, the way a clear walker passes a loud hawker, without anger and without envy. Pity the packs if you think of them at all. They promise lift and deliver repeat. You are going home to a table.

**THE PLAIN FAVOURITE MEAL**

Now sit down hungry to a plain favourite meal.

I mean your own favourite among plain meals, the one you would choose if no one watched and no rule ruled. For me it was a thick vegetable soup with bread to push through it, then a plate of roasted vegetables with oil and salt, then fruit. For you it may be fish and potatoes, or rice and greens, or eggs and bread and salad, or stew that cooked all afternoon while the house filled with smell. Choose yours. Make it real. Make it the food you love when hunger is honest.

Sit down. No screen. No standing. No eating on the run. Chair pulled close. Plate warm. First hunger keen.

Take the first bite hungry. Notice it. The first bite hungry is the brightest bite of any meal. The mouth wakes. Taste sharpens. Warmth spreads down and out. Chew slowly because pleasure asks you to slow, not hurry. Hurry is the signature of the frantic voice. Pleasure slows the hand.

Eat and notice. Bread torn, not sliced neat, crust cracking, crumb pulling. Vegetables soft inside and edged crisp, salt bright on the tongue, oil rounding it. Soup steaming, spoon warm, belly warming with it. Meat tender, potatoes fluffy, greens with bite. Rice separate and fragrant, fish flaking, lemon sharp. Notice each change as the meal moves. Notice hunger softening at the edges, then in the middle, then gone.

Stop when satisfied. I mean the clear moment when hunger leaves and ease arrives. Not stuffed. Not heavy. Not regretting. Satisfied. The body says, enough, thank you, lovely. The plate may hold food still. Leave it. Satisfaction is not in the empty plate. Satisfaction is in the quiet belly. Push the plate away with pleasure, not with rules.

Sit a minute in that quiet. Head clear. Belly calm. Hands still. No second reach calling from a cupboard. No fog gathering behind the eyes. No hurry to find a close. The meal is complete because hunger opened it and satisfaction closed it.

Ask yourself in that quiet, without defending anything:

Did this meal need a bright flash to be favourite? Did the bread fail because it was not frosted? Did the fruit fail because it was not coated?

Or was the favourite in the hunger met — the keen edge, the noticed bites, the calm close?

You know the answer in your belly. The favourite was hunger met by real food, noticed and savoured and stopped at satisfied. The sweet close never made the meal. The meal made itself, and the close only stole the applause.

I lived that theft for years. I rushed plain courses to reach the sweet course. I ate bread without tasting it, soup without noticing it, company without hearing it, all to arrive at the glowing bowl. Then I ate the glowing bowl fast, standing half-hungry half-frantic, tasted brightness for two bites, then fog and want. I called the evening love. Love was the table, the talk, the hunger met. The bowl only rode along.

Take back the applause. It was you that hungered. It was you that tasted. It was the meal, the company, the hunger met that made the evening warm.

That is the credit inversion that frees eating forever. The scene gave everything.

Stay longer at that table in memory and watch satisfaction do its quiet work. Satisfaction is not a full belly. A full belly can still want. Satisfaction is a met belly. It has a signature you can learn the way you learn a loved voice on the stairs. Warmth steady, not rising. Breath easy. Hands willing to rest. Mind willing to talk of other things. Eyes willing to leave the plate. When you feel that signature, stop. Stopping at satisfied is not restraint. Stopping at satisfied is completion, the way stopping a story at its end is completion. You would not keep reading past the end to prove love of the book.

We lost that signature under grazing. Grazing never lets hunger gather, so satisfaction never gets to arrive. We eat from flat to full, missing both doors, then call eating grey. Open the first door and the second appears. Hunger opening, satisfaction closing. Between them the whole joy, bright and enough.

Watch a shared plate in evening light to fix the lesson. Friends round a table, dishes in the middle, hands reaching, talk running over. No one counts. No one earns. Bread passes, salad turns, fish flakes, bowls empty and fill. Laughter makes bites taste brighter. Hunger makes laughter warmer. You eat, you talk, you eat again, then you lean back because ease has come. No one announces the close. The close announces itself. Coffee, water, fruit, talk running long. No one misses a bright dose because nothing is missing. The evening is full.

You know such evenings. You have lived them on holidays, at old kitchens, at tables where no rule ruled. Remember what made them shine. Not the wrapper. The hunger, the food, the people, the ease. That shine is not exception. That shine is eating itself when left alone.

**REAL SWEETNESS STAYS**

You say, but I love sweet taste. Are you telling me sweetness is forbidden? Will fruit turn to ash? Will I never enjoy anything sweet again?

Hear me clearly, because honesty outranks force and I will never forbid joy.

Sweetness inside whole fruit, inside vegetables, inside a plain meal is not the target. A peach eaten over the sink, juice running down the wrist, perfume filling the nose — that is real food. Berries in summer, warm from the punnet. An apple that snaps. A melon that smells of flowers. Peas from the pod, sweet as candy from the garden. Roasted carrots gone honeyed at the edges. Milk, bread, corn — all carry their own gentle sweetness when the mouth is clear enough to taste it.

When this book says BAD SUGAR, take it to mean the bright manufactured doses — sugary drinks, confectionery, biscuits, cakes, desserts, sweetened cereals and the grazing handfuls that behave as sweet hits. Plain meals and whole fruit belong to you. They belong on your table every day, eaten hungry, enjoyed fully, stopped at satisfied.

You do not enjoy sweet food less when you leave bright doses. You enjoy it more, because taste returns.

Watch taste return. In the first days without bright flashes, plain food tastes plainer to a mouth still expecting shouting. That is timetable, not truth. Wait. Drink water. Eat hungry. By the end of the week the mouth quiets and hears again. Bread tastes wheaty. Cheese tastes deep. Fruit tastes wildly sweet — sweeter than you remember, because you meet it with a clear tongue. You know the child who stops mid-bite when play calls, hunger closed without counting.

That small picture holds your whole future: hunger led, satisfaction closed, joy remained. No guilt followed because no promise was broken. Eating was an event inside living, not a battle inside eating.

You will live that again. You will bite a plum and close your eyes because the flavour is so bright. You will tear bread and hear the crust. You will drink cold water when thirsty and feel it go all the way down. These are not small consolations for a lost treat. These are the treat itself, returned to full volume after years of shouting drowned it.

Ask the sweetness its questions, last whisper of the grey fear:

If joyful eating needed bright doses, why do clear eaters — children at play, workers on plain lunches, old cooks who taste as they go — glow at tables with no bright dose in sight?

If dessert were love, why does love feel exactly as warm when the bowl never arrives, the talk runs long, and no one misses it?

And if plain food were grey, why does the first bite hungry shine brighter than any frantic bite you ever ate standing by an open cupboard?

Concede it. Plain food is not grey. Grey was the fog after flashes. Colour is hunger met.

I want to meet one more whisper, the one that borrows virtue language. I have managed to be good this week, you say. Surely a reward is earned. Surely celebrating with chocolate is different from grazing. Surely love must be sweet to count.

Look at the words with investigator eyes. Good this week means hungry and satisfied without panic, meals noticed, tables shared. That good is already reward. It needs no payment after. Celebrating with chocolate borrows the celebration and lends it to a wrapper. The celebration was the degree, the birthday, the Friday light, the friend home safe. Those shine with or without a dose. Love that needs a wrapper to prove itself doubts itself. Love that sits at a table hungry and leaves satisfied proves itself by staying.

This is not moralising about good food and bad food. Food is not virtue. Food is dinner. I am not weighing your plate or timing your bites or naming you good or bad for what you chew. I am showing you where joy lives so you can walk there directly. Joy lives in hunger met, not in earning and paying. Earning turns dinner into wages. Paying turns dessert into debt. Leave wages and debt to offices. Keep the table for hunger and ease.

**YOUR BODY MAKES ITS OWN STEADY POWER**

Here is the plain bodily truth, spoken as settled fact, with no scare and no lecture.

Your body knows how to keep you going without bright doses. It makes its own steady glucose from plain meals — from bread, rice, potatoes, vegetables, fruit, protein, the ordinary food you love. That quiet making hums along for hours after a satisfying meal, carrying work, play, talk, walking, thinking, without drama. Clean baseline without bright doses is physiologically coherent. Leaving BAD SUGAR is not deprivation. It is return to the way the body feeds itself.

I say this carefully, with no eating plan and no moral about good food and bad food. I am not giving you a diet. I am not weighing your plate or timing your bites or naming foods virtuous and foods wicked. There is no plan here. There is only hunger and satisfaction, the two old friends who know their work.

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that as permission, not prescription. What you want means the plain meals you love, chosen hungry, eaten with attention. When you are hungry means at hunger's polite call, not at the clock's shout and not at the timetable's tap. Stop when you are satisfied means at the calm close, leaving food gladly when the body says enough. Never punish yourself again means no earning, no paying, no being good this week, no atonement after pleasure. Eating is not a court. Eating is a table.

You say, but my body feels shaky without a quick hit. Does that not prove I need quick power?

It proves timetable, not need. The body that has been rocked by bright flashes wobbles while the rocking stops, then steadies. Feed it plain meals hungry, let satisfaction close them, drink water, rest when tired, move when restless. The hum returns. Mornings clear. Afternoons hold. Evenings calm. Hands forget the drawer walk because no empty panic calls for filling.

I promise you from lived escape, not theory. When I stopped rocking, I did not collapse. I ate. I ate bread warm, soup hot, fruit cold, meals sitting down, hunger leading, satisfaction closing. Day one was strange from habit. Day two was lighter. By the week's end hunger arrived politely and left happily, taste sharpened, the three o'clock hour passed without ceremony. No heroics. No counting. Simply the river running again. That river is your nature.

Feel it now, with elation, not dread. Dread closes the mind I need open. Elation opens it. You are not losing the sweet close. You are finding the whole meal. A runner who drops a sandbag does not mourn the sandbag. The runner laughs at how light the legs feel. An eater who finds hunger again does not mourn the flashing. The eater laughs at how bright plain food tastes and wonders why the flash was ever called help.

So come to the table hungry today. Lay out your plain favourite. Notice, savour, stop at satisfied. Notice company warmer because you hear it. Notice time slower because you sit. Notice belly calm because it was met, not rocked.

That calm is freedom warming. Guard it. It holds.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

- Food joy was never in the bright dose, it was in hunger met by a real meal.
- True hunger arrives politely, welcomes plain food, and leaves happily for hours.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry, noticed and savoured, closes itself in satisfaction.
- Real sweetness in fruit and plain meals stays yours to enjoy fully.
- The body keeps you steady from ordinary meals without any bright flash.
- Eating needs no earning and no punishment, only hunger opening and satisfaction closing.
- The eater who feared grey tables now knows colour lives in hunger met.
