## 5. Lexicon and instruction spine

**Trap register.** BAD SUGAR for the target in every sentence after Chapter 1. Doses: fix, hit, dose. Practice: feeding the craving, the daily top-up. Status: the sugar trap, brainwashing, the con. Users: trapped, conned, hooked — always with warmth, always including the narrator. The tiny physical craving and the brainwashing voice for the two mechanisms, lowercase common nouns only.

**Freedom register.** Shown as lived moments — the clear morning, the quiet aisle, the closed meal, the steady afternoon — and named only by the frozen mantra lines. At most one freedom word per titled section inside a concrete sentence, never in consecutive sentences, never as a tag or refrain. The single final outward imperative closes the book.

**Banned register.** Give up, resist, stay strong, discipline, abstain, sacrifice except when naming the illusion of sacrifice, trying to stop, one day at a time, recovery journey, cold turkey as framing, and any instead, I can't have, or giving-up phrasing. Quit itself is acceptable as a plain verb.

**Reader dialect for ventriloquism only, always in quotes.** Treat, roller coaster, sugar crash, sugar high, food noise, pick-me-up, special occasions, just one, start again tomorrow, I deserve it, sweet tooth, thick head, white-knuckle, fall off the wagon, got my brain back, too sweet. The narrator never adopts these as their own verdicts.

**Clinical advisory ADV-01.** Boxed once up front, outside the argument: readers who are diabetic, pregnant or breastfeeding, taking glucose-affecting medication, underweight, purging, or distressed about eating should read for the belief argument and take all food and medication decisions with their own clinician; this book offers no meal plan, no medical advice, and no diagnosis.
