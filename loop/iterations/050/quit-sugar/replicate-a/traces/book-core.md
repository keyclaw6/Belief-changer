## 1. Book core

**Target behavior.** Compulsive consumption of refined and added sugar and its junk-carb carriers: the craving–snacking loop, the evening and afternoon binges, the daily grazing that never reads as a problem.

**Reader.** An adult who feels trapped in the sugar loop, has tried diets, moderation rules, and force of will, watched them all fail, and suspects the whole approach is wrong. Planner-facing state at entry: a tired, self-blaming grazer who believes the failures prove something about themselves and is reading one more book with guarded hope. General adult edition, one clear reader throughout.

**Load-bearing false belief.** Bad sugar is a genuine treat and lift that makes life sweeter, and life without it would be deprivation.

**Through-line.** The lift is relief from a low the last dose created; there is nothing real to give up; once that is seen, freedom is immediate and permanent.

**Format.** Full-length belief-change book, 16 chapters, about 60,800 words, plus a back-matter quarantine for raw citations. One belief-move per chapter. Behavior change is deferred until belief has changed.

**Fork decisions.**

- F1, inner state: full Carr personification at full strength. Two mechanism characters are named in passing when the trap is first seen, then used as plain everyday common nouns: the tiny physical craving (the faint withdrawal pang, trivial and short-lived) and the brainwashing voice (the learned belief-system that misreads every dip as proof a treat is needed). No capitalized branded creature names, no frozen creature tokens on the mantra sheet. The craving is a small starving parasite already dying; the belief feeding it is the real target.
- F2, outcome: total cessation of BAD SUGAR, commanded with warm cheerful certainty. Moderation is foreclosed with the pincer and the cliff image. The numbered instructions are commands the reader is expected to follow, never apologized for.
- F3, science weight: the hard facts are stated plainly at full weight, frightening where the truth is frightening, then the reader is told not to change out of fear. Facts arrive as settled perception-shifters, never as literature review. Raw study titles and journal names live in back-matter only. Evidence limits are honored by not overclaiming.
- F4, villain: two villains, both named hard — the engineered food system that built the trap, and the Willpower Method, the named anti-method that kept the reader in it. Every past failure is reattributed to the wrong method, never to the reader. Warmth to the person, contempt for the trap and the wrong method.
- F5, void: change nothing else in life. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own. The reader inhabits every old context confidently to prove freedom. No replacement system, no scaffolding. The positive authority is the body, instinct, and real food, met as concrete encounters in the first third.

**Redefinition and margin-for-error doctrine.** Defined and boxed in Chapter 1, carried by the CAPS name in every sentence after. BAD SUGAR means refined and added sugar in all its carriers: sweets, desserts, pastries, chocolate confectionery, sweetened drinks, sweetened cereals and yogurts, sauces and breads where sugar rides hidden, honey, syrups, fruit-juice concentrates, and any non-sugar sweetener product kept alive to preserve the sweet habit. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Real food — vegetables, whole fruit as fruit, protein, unrefined staples eaten as meals — is not the target and becomes part of the positive authority's menu. Margin for error: the body rides out an accidental blip without drama, but the mind must not renegotiate. A slip revives nothing unless the old belief is invited back in. Guard the belief, not the behavior.

**Clinical and eating-disorder safety perimeter.** This book covers the everyday sugar loop for a general adult reader. It does not cover medical nutrition therapy, diabetes management, medication-adjusted eating, pregnancy and breastfeeding nutrition, or eating-disorder treatment. Readers who are diabetic, pregnant, on glucose-affecting medication, underweight, purging, or in distress around food are routed to a clinician by the plan-wide advisory ADV-01, which appears once up front and is cited by ID on relevant cards. No diagnosis is offered, no diet is prescribed, no medical result is promised.

**Strongest pro-behavior scene.** The family birthday table: candles, singing, a grandmother's cake, everyone together — the moment sugar claims to be love itself. Met head-on in its own late chapter, never before staged.

**Destination state.** Whenever the reader thinks about bad sugar, they feel relief and freedom that they no longer do it. Not merely free: happy to be free.

**Saved ending reframe.** Appears only in the final movement: homecoming to one's own appetite — the reader discovers the feast was real food all along and the trap had been serving the menu through a haze. Freedom is arriving at the table, not leaving the party.
