Chapter 5

HEAVEN AT THE LIGHT, NOTHING IN THE PACK

IN THIS CHAPTER: the morning light and the first kind draw, the bitter tail and the washed mouth, the coffee can of butts kept from the dog, the coat aired and the hands scrubbed, and the weighing that leaves only timing.

*The heaven you remember lasted one draw because the closing was kind, never the tobacco.*

THE FIRST KIND DRAW

You know the draw I mean because your hand has chased it for years.

Morning. The kettle clicks off. The kitchen holds its early quiet. You step to the door with the pack, thumb the lighter, shield the flame with the cupped palm. The tip flares orange. The first pull lands warm after the night without. The chest loosens for a minute. The head clears a fraction. Thought starts to move again. For those seconds the day feels possible, even kind, and the hand holding the paper feels like a friend that stayed through the night to greet you first.

I speak of that minute with warmth because I lived inside it too, and I know how honest it feels from the inside. We all learned to call that minute proof. We told ourselves the taste pleased us. We told ourselves the smoke comforted us. We told ourselves the day opened because paper burned. The feeling arrived with the plume, so the plume must have carried it. That timing felt too neat to question while the house still slept.

Watch the same morning with clear eyes, and the neatness starts to confess. The night without built a thin lack through the hours of sleep. The mouth woke dry. The nerves woke edgy. Attention leaned toward the pack before the kettle boiled. The first draw closed that lack for minutes, and the closing felt kind after the tightening. We misread that kindness as proof the leaf pleases, rather than as the brief slackening of a hold the previous day had renewed.

Ask the draw its own plain questions, and listen for the only honest answers. Did the kitchen change while you stood at the door. Did the day grow kinder in its facts. Did the kettle boil sweeter because smoke crossed the air. You know nothing in the morning moved except your blood and your breath. The bills still waited on the table. The work still waited in its hours. The weather stood exactly where it stood. What moved was the interval since feeding, reset to zero and already counting upward again.

Follow the stick past its kind head and watch its character turn in your own mouth. The first pull lands kind after the lack. The second lands duller. The middle pulls come quicker, less noticed, less praised. The tail pulls scratch. The last tastes thin and hot and bitter on the tongue, and you crush it with a small grimace and wash the mouth with tea. You never crush a true pleasure with that grimace. You never rinse a true taste from the tongue the moment it lands. The ritual ends in mild disgust at its own tail, then claims the brief kindness at its head as its whole nature.

The fact is that no true taste behaves this way in human life. Bread eaten at hunger leaves strength for hours. Water drunk at thirst leaves the mouth clean. Rest taken in stillness leaves the limbs looser than before. Only the dose behaves the other way, kind at the touch and sour before the stick is done, while calling the brief closing at the start its whole character. That reversal already tells you where the kindness came from.

THE MOMENT I LIGHT UP I AM IN HEAVEN, BUT FEEL SHITTY AFTERWARDS

You have said it in your own words, and I will read it back without trimming, because the sentence holds both halves of the proof:

"The moment I light up I'm in heaven, but feel shitty afterwards. So whilst I love smoking I hate being a smoker."

I greet that sentence kindly before answering it, because I know how much honesty it costs. You have spoken the heaven part with conviction on the doorstep, and you have spoken the shitty part with equal conviction at the sink an hour later when the taste still coats the tongue. You have loved the light and hated the identity in the same day for years. Both halves can be true at once, and any book that told you that you invented the heaven would lose your trust on the spot.

Hold both halves where they belong and the false credit falls away. The heaven is real. The credit is false. The heaven reports timing, not tobacco. It arrives at the light because the light closes the lack, and the lack was deepest at the light after the hours without. The shitty afterwards reports the tobacco, because the tobacco leaves its own signature once the closing thins: stale mouth, grey smell on the fingers, tight chest on the stairs, quiet knowledge that the pack owns another hour of the day.

We all lived this split for years without seeing what it proved. We praised the head of the stick and apologized for its tail, as if a friend may be judged by his greeting and forgiven his stay. We said love while hiding smell and cough, and we called the hiding prudence rather than evidence. The split itself was the verdict, spoken daily in our own acts. A pleasure that shames its lover within minutes is not a pleasure. It is a closing misnamed.

Test the heaven against any honest pleasure you keep without defense. You drink the first sip of cold water after work in the garden and the mouth thanks you, and an hour later the mouth still thanks you. You bite into ripe fruit and the tongue wakes, and an hour later the tongue holds no regret. You sit in warm sun after cold and the skin loosens, and no shame follows the loosening into the house. True pleasures leave no aftertaste to scrub, no smell to air from the coat, no grey mound to hide from guests. They do not require apology within the hour.

Now set the light beside those pleasures and watch the difference stand clear. The light pleases for seconds and accuses for hours. The water pleases and leaves cleanliness. The fruit pleases and leaves freshness. The sun pleases and leaves warmth. Only the dose pleases by closing and accuses by staying, because only the dose created the lack it closed. The heaven timed replenishment speed. The shitty afterwards told the truth about the goods.

Did the tobacco turn sweet at the flame, or did the interval turn short before the flame. Did the leaf carry heaven, or did the hours without carry the hell that made any closing feel like heaven. Did you love the smoke, or did you love the minute the edginess stopped talking. You already know the true answers when both questions stand together. The interval made the heaven. The tobacco only timed its arrival to coincide with the closing.

So I do not tell you that you lied about the light. You told the truth about the minute. You felt the wash as the draw landed after lack, and the wash felt kind after tightening. I tell you that you misnamed what carried it. The minute was yours. The quiet was yours. The loosening was the slackening of a hold. When that difference settles, the old sentence loses its command. Love coexists with hate because only the light was ever credited, and the light borrowed all its kindness from the lack before it.

THE COFFEE CAN KEPT FROM THE DOG

Come with me to the corner of the kitchen where the proof stands in tin and paper, because the room where we hide the cost convicts the claim of taste.

Most of us kept such a can. A large coffee can with the plastic lid, tucked under the sink or on the back step, filled through the week with crushed ends. The lid went on tight. The can went out of sight when visitors came. We told ourselves the lid kept the smell from the dog, whose nose wrinkles at the stale mound. We Febreze the hell out of the curtains after an evening inside. We air the coat on the line before a visit. We scrub the fingers before shaking a hand. We chew mint before a meeting. We wash the ashtray in the morning with distaste, waving the smell from the cloth.

I ask you to look at that careful ceremony with the eyes of an investigator, because the hands confess what the mouth defends. We loved the taste, yet we washed it from every surface we own. We praised the aroma at the light, yet we aired it from hair and cloth before company. We called the plume fragrant while striking the match, yet we emptied its leavings with a grimace and sealed the lid against our own nose. No lover treats a loved taste this way in human life.

Think of tastes you truly love, and watch how differently you house them. You grind coffee and leave the jar open to catch the smell on passing. You bake bread and carry the loaf through the house to share the crust warmth. You slice lemon and hold the skin to the nose for pleasure. You do not seal the peelings in tin against the dog and air the kitchen after. You do not scrub the tongue after and apologize to guests for the lingering note. Love keeps. The trap hides.

You may answer me here with the strongest objection a smoker can raise on this ground, and I want it spoken plain between us. You say the hiding proves nothing about taste, only about manners in a world that frowns on smoke. Other people dislike the smell, so you spare them. That is courtesy, not confession. I hear the courtesy in that answer and I do not dismiss it. Courtesy is real. Yet courtesy does not explain the private acts with no witness in the room.

Who were you sparing when you waved the curtain smell from your own nose at midnight with no guest due. Who were you sparing when you changed the shirt before bed though no lover waited. Who were you sparing when you brushed the teeth twice after the last of the evening though the house slept. The dog did not judge you. The curtains did not lecture you. The shirt did not frown. Your own nose judged, your own mouth judged, your own chest judged on the morning stairs. The masking began as courtesy and continued as self-protection, because the body knows stale when the story calls it sweet.

Count the butts the way the can teaches them when you lift the lid with clear eyes. Each end in that mound was once lit as heaven. Each was praised at its head. Each now lies grey and damp and sour, ends crushed in the same twist, filters stained the same brown. The mound holds no heaven to the eye or the nose. It holds the week measured in paper and money and cough. You never once lifted that lid and breathed deep with joy at the store kept. You lifted it with distaste and pressed the lid down fast. That lift tells you what the light refused to tell. The gift was timing. The goods were ash.

Take the dose out of that kitchen and the senses do not dull. The smoker who spends a clean weekend in hills without a pack smells food sharper by the second day. The smoker kept from the pack by flu tastes soup cleaner once fever lifts. The nose you feared would lose its pleasures sharpens once it is no longer bathed in stale plume. The taste you feared would thin returns, because taste lives in a clean mouth, not in a coated one. What failed in that kitchen was never pleasure. What failed was the story that ash pleases.

I name that story plain because soft names keep it alive. The story is the brainwashing voice that taught you to read the closing of lack as proof of flavor, fed and renewed through years of lights. Together with the tiny physical demand that returns on schedule, it held the palate to ransom and sold back ten minutes of your own relief as if it were the leaf talking. The embrace felt kind while it lasted. Tight in the hours that taught you to need the closing. That is how we stayed held by the timetable while calling the hold a matter of taste.

I REALLY ENJOY SMOKING. I DON'T REALLY WANT TO GIVE UP, BUT KNOW I MUST

A second sentence guards the same belief from another side, quieter and harder to catch, spoken late at night when the pack lies half full and the day has gone its way:

"I really enjoy smoking. I don't really want to give up, but know I must."

I greet that one kindly as well, because I know the weariness underneath it. You have spoken enjoyment with a tired laugh among friends, and you have spoken must with a heavier heart alone. You have defended the light while counting its cost in cough and money and hours. You have said love while standing in rain outside a warm room to feed. That split is not hypocrisy. It is the clearest window into the trap we ever get.

Hold the window open and look through it together. Enjoyment that must apologize to health, to money, to time, to company, to breath, yet still claims the name of enjoyment, has already confessed by its own defense. True enjoyments need no must beside them. You never say of music loved that you must leave it, yet love it still. You never say of walks at dusk that you must stop them for your own good. You never stand in rain to keep a pleasure that pleases, then scrub its smell from your hands before rejoining the table. The must beside the love already weighs the love and finds it borrowed.

We all kept that sentence for years because it seemed to protect something precious. We feared that without the sentence we would stand exposed as fools who fed for nothing. So we polished the love a little brighter each time the must grew heavier. We praised the first of the morning a little louder after a night of cough. We praised the one after food a little harder after counting the weekly spend. The louder praise did not prove deeper pleasure. It proved deeper need to justify the feeding.

Ask yourself what the love spares you from feeling for those minutes. It spares you the thin stir the last dose left, not the day, not the work, not the company. The day waited. The work waited. The friend waited. Only the stir closed, and the stir was the dose talking in borrowed clothes. Once that distinction lands, the sentence loses its weight. It loved the closing. It dreaded the goods. Both truths lived in one mouth because one mouth spoke for two masters: the body noting lack, and the voice trained to call the closing a gift.

You know this from hours when no dose was near and enjoyment arrived clean. A laugh with friends that needed no paper in the hand. A meal closed with coffee tasted to the last sip while the pack sat untouched in the coat. A walk to the window and back that steadied while the lighter lay cold. The body owns joy. The trap taught you to light while feeling it, then credit the flame.

So the enjoyment is not a lie to mock. It is a misaddressed letter, delivered to the wrong house for years. The feeling arrived, warm and real, in a pause you loved, beside people you liked, after work you finished, in air you were glad to breathe. The dose stood beside the scene and signed for the parcel. It was never a genuine pleasure or crutch.

Reverse the picture and the fraud shows clear. If the leaf pleased by its own nature, the smoker who chains three on a wet afternoon of strain would grow steadily happier with each lighting. You have watched that smoker, and you have been that smoker, and happiness is not what grows. The throat burns. The head crowds. The fingers hurry. The third tastes of ash and is crushed with distaste. The mouth that was sold as the seat of joy delivers hurry and bite the longer it is used. A true seat of joy does not behave this way in human life. Clean air taken alone leaves the mouth clean and the head clearer. Clean air taken through the dose leaves the mouth stale and the nerves listening for the next hour.

I TOLD MYSELF IT WAS NOT A BIG DEAL SINCE I DID NOT SMOKE THAT MUCH

A third sentence guards the same belief by making the feeding sound small, spoken by the smoker who counts with care:

"I told myself it wasn't a big deal since I didn't smoke that much."

I greet that one kindly as well, because I know the relief inside it. You have counted five a day against a friend's twenty and breathed easier. You have kept the pack light on travel days and called the lightness proof of control. You have told yourself the small number buys a small claim on heaven without the heavy chain. The counting feels like evidence. I ask you to weigh the counting itself.

Watch two smokers with clear eyes, one at five a day and one at twenty, and watch what matches despite the numbers. Both praise the same first of the morning. Both crush the same bitter tail with the same grimace. Both air the same coat. Both hide the same can a little longer or a little shorter. Both cough on the same stairs when winter comes. Both stand outside the same warm room while talk continues inside. The number changes the size of the mound. It does not change the split between heaven at the head and shame at the tail. Five closes lack five times. Twenty closes lack twenty times. The closing pleases by timing in both. The goods accuse in both.

Did the small number change the taste, or did it change the story you told about the taste. Did five leaves carry a different leaf than twenty of the same brand from the same shop. Did the lungs keep a separate ledger for the careful feeder, marking his smoke sweet and his neighbor's sour. You know the honest answers. The leaf did not change. The clock changed. The chain lengthened or shortened, but the links stayed the same metal.

You may tell me here that a few a day proves you smoke for pleasure while the heavy smoker smokes from need, and I want that thought heard fully before it falls. Look at your own few and see need wearing pleasure clothes. The few are kept with the same jealous guarding as the many. The few are timed with the same care, after food, with coffee, on the break, before sleep. The few are missed with the same sharpness when the pack runs out early and shops stand shut. The few are defended with the same heat when anyone questions them. Pleasure kept this jealous watch does not behave like pleasure in human life. Pleasure light of heart forgets to count. Need counts, guards, times, defends.

We all tried the small number as a shelter at some point. I kept such counts myself in my years inside, and I know how reasonable the shelter feels from the inside. We told ourselves the smallness proved taste ruled, not schedule. Yet the smallness still obeyed schedule to the minute. The five still arrived on their hooks through the day. The hooks still owned the hours between. A small cage still locks. A short chain still pulls. The trap does not ask how many links you polish. It asks whether one link still holds.

Bring the test down to one of your few you praised most, and run it clean. Name the hour you lit. Name the hours since the prior light. Name the taste at the head and the taste at the tail. The head pleased after lack. The tail soured before finishing. The mouth asked for water after. The fingers asked for washing after. The room asked for airing after. Those five observations together already undo the smallness defense, without any lecture from me. A big deal or a small deal was never the question. The question was whether any deal carried a gift, and the aftertaste answered in your own mouth.

WHAT REMAINS WHEN TIMING IS LIFTED OUT

There remains one clean test that settles taste without quarrel, and I lay it here because your own acts already ran it.

Lift timing out and watch what taste is left. Take the same brand you call sweet and light it straight after the prior one, with no lack to close, and taste it with attention. Smokers who run that test report the same verdict in their own words: flat, hot, stale, nothing to praise. The heaven fails to arrive when lack does not precede it. The leaf did not change between lightings. Only the interval changed. That single difference already convicts timing as the carrier.

Now run the test the other way, and watch pleasure arrive with no leaf at all. Recall one morning when the pack stood empty and the shops stood shut and the day still required waking. You brewed coffee strong. You stood at the door in cold air and breathed slow. The head cleared a fraction through air and movement alone. The coffee tasted cleaner without the coating. The walk to the shop woke the limbs. Beneath the circling thought of lack, ordinary senses did their ordinary work. What failed that morning was not taste. What failed was the ceremony that had claimed taste as its own.

Did non-smokers lose the flavors you fear losing, living their days with clean mouths. Do they drink coffee as dishwater, unable to praise the cup without paper burning beside it. Do they finish food bland, unable to close a meal without stepping out. You have sat at tables with such people and watched them praise food louder and longer than any smoker at the table, holding the taste without hurry to leave. If smoke carried flavor the way salt carries food, the room would divide clean between those who lit and savored and those who went without and found all flat. You never saw that division. You saw smokers leave the table to feed and return with dulled tongues, and non-smokers stay and taste to the end.

Isolate the variable the way you would test any honest claim about the tongue. The smell is not alluring to the nose that never fed. The first whiff of another lighting across the street turns the clean nose aside, while the feeder leans toward the same whiff with warmth. The same air cannot be sweet and sour by its own nature. It is sweet to the schedule that needs closing and sour to the nose that needs nothing. That split already tells you where the sweetness lived.

So the leaf adds heat and bite to acts the body already knows how to do clean. The pause rested you through stillness. The breath steadied you through slow movement. The coffee pleased through its own bitterness and warmth. The meal closed through fullness and company. The dose only timed its own closing to coincide with all four, the way a stranger steps into your photograph at the viewpoint and is later credited with the view.

The verdict lands short, as true verdicts do. Taste never lived in the paper. Timing did.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Other voices pull the old belief back in.

I place that direction here with purpose, at the point where the great excuse of pleasure has fallen and other voices will rush to rebuild it. Friends will praise their own lights with fondness. Voices at work will swear by taste and company and calm. The talk in bars will dress the old closing in warm words. Each voice speaks from inside the same timing error you just weighed in your own mouth. Their fondness reports their lack closing, not the leaf pleasing. Their certainty reports training, not evidence.

You came to this chapter defending love of the light while hiding its leavings. Leave knowing love and hate lived side by side because only the minute of closing was ever kind. The head pleased by timing. The tail accused by goods. The can accused by smell. The coat accused by masking. The small count accused by the same split as the large. Carry that weighing without dread and without hurry. You do not need to prove it through strain. You only need to keep your own evidence where your hands already laid it, in the ashtray and the sink and the aired coat, and let other talk pass like weather.

SUMMARY

- Love at the light and hate after lived side by side because only the closing pleased, never the tobacco.
- The heaven timed the hours without, not the flavor of the leaf, and thinned within the same stick to a bitter tail.
- Your own masking proved the verdict: a loved taste is kept, not aired and scrubbed and sealed in tin.
- A few a day kept the same split as many, with the same heaven at the head and the same shame at the tail.
- Lift timing out and no taste remains to defend; the pause and the air and the cup carried every pleasure claimed.
