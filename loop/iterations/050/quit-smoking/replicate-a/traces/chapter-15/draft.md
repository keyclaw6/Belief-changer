Chapter 15

MORNINGS, SHOPS AND THE LIFE AFTER

IN THIS CHAPTER: a morning with no hand reaching to the bedside, shops passed without counting the window, a wedding stayed inside through the toast, a bad day met without any rescue offer, travel and money reclaimed, the whole escape on one page, and life to get on with.

*Ordinary moments were never empty without cigarettes; they were yours all along and now you live them all the way through.*

The Morning Without Reaching

You wake and the hand does not move to the bedside. Light comes through the curtain in the same way it always did and the house makes its small morning sounds and for a second memory lays the old picture before you, the pack and the lighter and the first harsh draw before the eyes are open. You smile before the picture fades because you know what it was. We all lived that reach for years, the cough from the night still in the chest and the mouth stale and the body already ringing its hourly clock for the next feed. The clock has gone now. There is no garage hide to slip to while the family sits inside. There is no leaving the house with a strange feeling because the pocket is empty. There is no timetable in the pocket at all.

I stood where you stand this morning, in the quiet before anyone needs anything, and I tell you plainly that this hour tells the whole truth. You take the stairs without the old wheeze catching halfway. You run water and taste coffee as coffee, full and bitter and warm, with nothing laid over it. You stand at the kerb and take a deep breath and feel the air reach further than it ever did through smoke. The patio is yours again in the morning, owned without paying for it in grey rolls and stained filters. Nothing is missing. Nothing needs feeding for the hour to be complete.

Did the morning ever need paper to start it, or did you start mornings while paper billed you for standing beside you. Did clean air ever refuse you, or did smoke only narrow what air could do. Did the hand reach because the day required it, or because hunger kept its hour whether the day was good or dull. You know you carried the morning, and you know air was kinder before paper narrowed it, and you know hunger kept its own hours. What reached this morning was only light through cloth and your own breath answering it.

The short pause stays yours without lighting. Mid-morning at work the heads lift and the feet move and the air outside calls, and for a second memory marks the hour with paper because memory marked every pause that way for years. You step out with them or you stay at your desk, whichever pleases you, and you take the pause whole. You breathe at the kerb and feel the chest move clean. You drink water and let talk run its course and return when you are ready. Did pauses ever belong to feeding, or did feeding only arrive during pauses and claim them. Did thoughts ever clear because smoke rose, or did thoughts clear because work paused and air moved. You know pauses hosted themselves, and you know thoughts ran on their own while feeding only interrupted them. What rests you now is rest itself, taken straight and left complete.

Think at once, with gladness, what free people think when memory lays the old picture down.

WONDERFUL! I'M FREE!

Say it inside as a spoken sentence. Let it carry the pack left on the wall last night and the dog pulling ahead into clean air and the stairs taken clear and the coffee tasted straight. You do not chant it to convince yourself. You think it because it is true. Each time the old picture comes in these first days, relief answers a half second later, until the picture itself grows welcome as the bell that rings freedom.

Shops, Weddings and Bars

You walk down the high street in the afternoon and the shop window passes without counting. For years we counted without knowing we counted, the prices and the brands and the cartons stacked in rows for a store you had to keep supplied, the small tight arithmetic of how many are left and how long until the next one can be lit. You pass now without the arithmetic. You go in for bread and milk and come out with bread and milk. The money stays where it belongs and the pocket stays light and no part of the walk is organized around the next smoke.

Did the shop ever sell you anything but doses wrapped in paper, or did it sell you the story that a small party waited inside the cardboard. Did counting ever make the day run smoother, or did it only prove who was in charge. You know the stack on the shelf never held company or calm, and you know counting proved the timetable owned the hours. What passes now is only glass and goods while you walk on without paying the old tax.

The meal closes where you sit now. For years we stood at the end of food and stepped out, leaving talk to cool while hunger rang, then came back with the taste of ash laid over what we had eaten. You know now that the food never asked for that ending. Tonight you finish and stay, and you taste the meal clean to its end, and you take coffee where you are. The table holds you without a second act. Did stepping out ever close a meal, or did it only interrupt one that was already complete. You know interruption never seasoned food, and you know staying never took anything from the hour. What closes the meal now is satisfaction itself, and satisfaction needs no paper to sign it.

The drive runs without counting stops now. We once measured every road in feeds, windows cracked in winter and the ashtray filling by the mile, the mind already spending the next town before this one arrived. You drive and the air stays clean and the road holds your attention whole. You stop because you choose to stop, for fuel or for legs or for nothing at all, never because hunger rings. Did counting ever shorten a road, or did it only cut it into hungry lengths. You know the miles pass the same under any engine, and you know clean air never tired you more. What carries you now is the car and the road and your own eyes on them.

At the wedding you stay inside through the whole toast. Talk runs late and music plays and glasses lift and for a minute the old whisper offers its kindliest line, that drinks and friends make this the hour when one will not hurt. I name it now so it arrives already answered whenever it comes. That thought is not yours. That thought is hunger borrowing loyalty and the room to reopen a door you closed on joy. You stay where the music is because air feels good and company holds you, never because hunger rings. You watch others slip to the corner without one grain of envy and without one word of dispute. You see the lone figures outside while the speeches run on inside, and you do not see enjoyment to envy. You see hours taxed by hunger while life runs on without them.

Did talk ever need ash to be easy, or did talk ease itself while paper drifted unnoticed above it. Did drink ever turn paper into pleasure, or did drink only loosen the guard that knew better sober. Did staying inside ever cost you the night, or did stepping out always cost pieces of it. You know talk carried itself, and you know drink only lowered the guard, and you know stepping out taxed every hour it touched. What shines that night is company and music and air. Paper only stood beside them and sent a bill.

You take the first drink and keep it yours. We once paired every glass with a lit end before the glass was half done, as though coffee and beer called for paper by some law of nature. You know now that pairing was only hunger arriving on schedule and borrowing the cup. Coffee tastes of coffee. Beer tastes of beer. Talk tastes of talk. The evening does not thin when nothing burns. It thickens, because you stay through all of it instead of spending pieces of it outside. Did the cup ever ask for ash, or did hunger only arrive when the cup arrived and claim the cup as its reason. You know cups never ordered feeding, and you know evenings run whole without interruption. What pairs now is company with music and thirst with drink, and nothing else needs a seat.

In the bar on an ordinary Friday the same script may come in work clothes. A friend you love lights without knowing your vow and the smoke reaches from the doorway and the head turns before thought. Let it turn. Do not fight the turn and do not argue it down with effort. Fighting keeps alive what it touches. Let the turn come and let the answer come faster, glad and certain, the joyful thought you now own. Pity lights without scorn. Your part is only to stay at the table and close the meal where you sit, to take the drive home without counting stops, to wake the next morning without paying for the night. Keep the same bar and the same friends. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own. Ordinary hours prove sufficiency the way quiet proves itself when noise stops.

Bad News, Travel and Money Found Again

On a bad day the news lands hard and the old whisper changes its accent. It no longer speaks of parties. It speaks of rescue. It says strain like this is exactly what paper was for. It says no one could meet this without something to take care of it. I hold that hour gently because we all feared it, the morning the phone rings wrong or work falls through or someone we love is ill and the hand shakes toward the pocket before thought finishes.

Did paper ever take care of strain, or did it only close for minutes the gap the last dose had opened while strain stood untouched beside it. Did the long exhalation ever loosen the trouble, or did pause and air loosen what little could be loosened while feeding only renewed hunger. Does a person who never fed hunger need a dose to sit through bad news. You know paper closed only its own gap, and you know pause and breath did what helping could be done, and you know those who never fed hunger meet the same news on their own resources. What meets bad news now is you, whole and clear-eyed, without paying hunger for the right to feel.

Meet the wave as you meet any echo now. Drink water. Breathe deep and feel the chest move without tightening to meet smoke. Keep the hands busy with the cup and the keys and the work in front of you. Do not watch the clock to measure the stir and do not count days to earn freedom you already hold. The faint restless emptiness that comes and goes in these weeks is only the body tidying up after the tenant left, noise fading rather than need calling. Tidying never means the tenant should move back in. I do not ask you to change from fear of what the stir means. The method takes care of the rest through clear seeing.

The evening after bad news still belongs to you. Supper is cooked and eaten at the table. The rooms are kept warm. Sleep comes the way sleep comes to a body that was not fed smoke late, deeper and sooner, and the next morning the trouble is still there to be met and you meet it again on your own breath. Did paper ever shorten a bad week, or did it only add hourly billing to days already heavy. Did a lit end ever answer the phone or mend the work or heal the loved one. You know billing never lightened a load, and you know answers never rose with smoke. What endures through bad weeks now is the same clear seeing that closed the trap, and clear seeing never leaves you to face anything alone with hunger.

Travel shows you the arithmetic of freedom in plain figures. The five-hour drive runs without the old ritual, without smoking through the miles and then stopping at the station to dump the ashtray and change the shirt and wash the hands and the interior and mask the smell. You drive and the air stays clean and the stops are yours to choose. The holiday no longer starts with the carton packed first and the frantic count in a foreign shop and the hours budgeted around where feeding is allowed. You pack and go. Stations and queues and long waits hold no dread now, because waiting never belonged to hunger. You know waiting was always only waiting, and you know clean air never made a journey longer. What travels now is you, unaccompanied by a timetable.

Money returns month by month and you watch it without making it your reason. The daily burn for something you never needed becomes the weekly shop paid without thought, then the coat kept instead of burned, then the weekend away taken on what paper once took without thanks. Count it once if you wish, so the scale of the old billing stands plain, then let it go. I name money last on purpose. Money is the bonus, never the motive. You stopped because life is sweeter lived all the way through, not because the till rings louder. The till rings louder all the same, and you may spend it gladly on what pleases you, because pleasure was the scene all along and scenes are yours to keep.

What You Left Behind

There comes an hour, often around the sixth week, when memory plays its strangest trick. It lays before you the feeling that a best friend was lost, and for a minute the feeling sounds almost true. I validate it before I disarm it because we all felt it. We spent years calling paper company through strain and drink and work and nights, and company mourned feels like grief even when company never gave. Then retelling sounds absurd even to the teller, the way a grown person laughs telling how a shadow at the door once frightened them as a child. It was never a friend that carried you through strain and celebration. It was hunger keeping its hours while you carried yourself.

You no longer use the old phrase except to dismiss it. There was never a genuine pleasure or crutch in paper. There was only relief from hunger dressed as help, and hunger unfed starves and quiets. It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you. That sentence settles the whole account in one portable truth. Paper taxed breath and money and hours and rooms loved while standing unnoticed above them. It never hosted a single one.

There is nothing to give up; you are escaping. That is the escape reminder to hold against any whisper that speaks of loss. We were conned out of choice by engineered doses and by brainwashing that taught us to read hunger as need. What was left behind on the wall last night has a plain name, and you may speak it without fear now. You walked out of the nicotine trap. The trap stays shut behind you because the belief that powered it is gone. Hunger spoke. Voice dressed hunger as memory. Seen plain, both were noise with no order behind them.

Do not use again what kept you in it. Every strained attempt before belonged to the Willpower Method, never to your worth. That method taught bracing and missing and counting while the belief stayed intact, and missing kept hunger precious. You do not brace now. You do not miss now. You do not count now. You see, and seeing holds.

Let visible ease do what dispute never could. Friends who smoke watch you stay through the meal and the toast and the long talk without stepping out, and they see no cost in your face to pity or to envy. You know preaching would only drag you both back into arguments that reopen doubt, so you preach nothing. You answer if asked, plainly and warmly, that stopping was escape and that escape was easy once seen. Then you leave the seed where it fell. Some will ask again months from now when their own billing grows heavy. Your life will have answered before your mouth opens.

If you ever light one again in an unguarded minute, do not call yourself lost. A slip never dooms you while the belief stays out. See it at once as a warning that the old belief slipped back in for a minute and ordered hands before seeing could speak. Put it out. Leave the pack. Think at once the joyful thought and walk on without reopening what is settled. What turns a warning into a chain is not the puff. What turns it is letting the old meaning back in and mourning paper as a friend lost instead of pitying hunger as noise dying. One never stays one where the belief is let back in, because one keeps the belief alive. Guard the belief and the behavior guards itself.

The Whole Thing on One Page

I put the whole escape on one page now so you can photograph it and keep it. These were the agreements that carried you here. They need no explanation anymore because you lived them. Read them once and keep them whole.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND
So you can investigate instead of defending.

2. SMOKE AS NORMAL UNTIL YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE
So belief changes before behavior does.

3. BEGIN WITH ELATION, NOT DREAD
There is no need to feel miserable about what comes next.

4. FOLLOW ALL THE INSTRUCTIONS
Miss one piece and the trap stays shut.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Other voices pull the old belief back in.

6. LET OTHERS SMOKE WITHOUT ENVY OR ARGUMENT
Visible ease does the recruiting.

7. CREDIT THE MOMENT, NOT THE CIGARETTE
The pleasure was the scene all along.

8. WELCOME THE FAINT ECHO AS PROOF THE TRAP IS DYING
It is noise fading, not need calling.

9. MAKE NO EXCEPTION, NOT EVEN ONE
One keeps the belief alive.

10. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY STRAINING AND MISSING IT
Their struggle was the wrong method.

11. REFUSE EVERY SUBSTITUTE THAT KEEPS NICOTINE COMING
A different delivery is not freedom.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE WITH A SOLEMN VOW AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
You cross on joy, not loss.

Hold them together and the door stays shut. Miss one piece and the old voice finds its crack. You will not miss. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain. You can stop easily, immediately and for good. Those sentences described what happened on the pavement, and they describe every morning after. They are the standing truth of a person happy to be free for the rest of your life.

Yours All Along

I close with the reframe I saved for this hour because only this hour can hold it. You were never saying goodbye to the thing that carried you through strain and nights and work. You carried yourself through all of them while paper billed you for standing beside you. What you laid on the wall was something that had been carrying you away from rooms you loved, outside into rain and cold and counting, while life ran on inside. Ordinary moments were yours all along. Mornings and shops and weddings and drives and bad days met on your own breath were always enough. Now you meet them without paying.

When smoking crosses your mind tomorrow and next month and years from now, in whatever room and at whatever hour, think at once with gladness and with full force the sentence that answers every whisper.

WONDERFUL! I'M FREE!

Let it be the final word on the subject. Let every later thought arrive already answered. Pity those still inside without scorn. Keep nothing else changed to prove you are changed. Take coffee as coffee and work as work and pauses as pauses, each complete without feeding.

Go enjoy your life.

SUMMARY

- Mornings arrive whole without reaching, and the hourly clock gone proves hunger kept its own hours while you carried the days.
- Shops are passed without counting, and money reclaimed is the bonus on a freedom already complete.
- Weddings and bars are stayed inside with ease, and the thought that one will not hurt arrives already answered as hunger borrowing the room.
- Bad news is met on your own breath, and strain never proves paper helped because paper only closed its own gap.
- Travel runs clean without counting stops or masking smell, and ordinary hours prove sufficiency when noise stops.
- The mourned companion now sounds absurd in retelling, because carrying was always yours and billing was always paper.
- Whenever smoking crosses the mind the joyful thought answers with relief, and the belief guarded keeps the behavior free.
