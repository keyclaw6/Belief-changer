### IF YOU FREELY CHOOSE IT, WHY ARE YOU HOLDING THIS BOOK?

Do you choose BAD SUGAR?

Most readers answer quickly. Of course I choose it. I like it. I could stop if I wanted. It's just a treat. I don't keep it in the house so I'm in control. Once a week is fine.

I hear that voice. I have heard it thousands of times. It sounds reasonable. It sounds like you.

Let me ask you something simple. If you wanted to quit a hobby you enjoy, would you need a book on how to stop?

Think about it. If you loved gardening and decided to garden less, would you search for a method? Would you feel a tug of shame when you walked past the garden centre? Would you promise on Sunday that you will be good this week?

You would not. You would just do less. No book required.

So why are you here? Why are you reading a book that promises escape from something you claim is a free choice?

That question is not an accusation. It is a trap question, and its only honest answer sets you free.

Do you freely choose something you have to defend to yourself? Do you freely choose something you have tried to cut down and watched creep back up? Do you freely choose something that makes you say "I need something RIGHT NOW" in a flat, urgent voice that does not sound like choice at all?

We say those things. We all have. "It's just a treat." "I've been good this week so I deserve it." "I don't keep it in the house." We mean them when we say them. We also find ourselves at the shop on the way home.

Which is true? The story we tell, or the behaviour we repeat?

The fact is the holding of this book is evidence. Not of weakness. Of a trap.

If BAD SUGAR were a genuine pleasure you could take or leave, you would have left it on the days you promised yourself you would. You didn't. That is not because you are weak. It is because something else is happening that looks like choice but is not.

Do you feel the small jolt in that? That little "yes, but..." rising in you? That is not you defending a pleasure. That is the trap defending itself.

We fell into that defence the same way. We told ourselves everyone eats it. Everyone does. How could it be a problem if it is normal? If it were serious, wouldn't we know? Wouldn't someone have warned us?

Those are good questions. They deserve real answers. Not slogans. Not willpower. Seeing.

Do you notice how much of the "choice" story depends on calling it normal?

We will look at normal in a moment. First, we need to name what actually took your choice. Once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

And when you see it, shame has nowhere to live. Relief takes its place.

### THE CONFIDENCE TRICK THAT STOLE YOUR CHOICE WITHOUT ASKING PERMISSION

Let me show you how the trick works. I will stage it fully so you feel it, not just understand it.

Imagine a smart, well-dressed man approaches you on a busy street. He is warm. He smiles. He does not pressure you. He shows you a glossy paper for a share that will make your life sweeter. Small price. Big promise. Everyone is buying it. Your friends hold it. Your family offered it to you as a child with love.

He says it will give you energy. Comfort. Reward. He shows you pictures of people laughing, sharing, celebrating with it. You think, why not? A tiny dose. What harm?

You buy. You taste. Nothing dramatic happens. But he told you to come back tomorrow for the dividend. You do. And the next day. After a few weeks you notice you look forward to it. You think you like it.

Here is the con. The share was worthless.

It never gave you energy. It never gave you comfort. It never gave you reward. It created a faint unease when you were without it and then briefly quieted that unease when you paid again. You paid to relieve a discomfort the share itself created. You called that relief pleasure.

Would you call that a choice? Would you call a man who keeps paying dividends on a worthless share an investor? Or would you call him conned?

That is the Sugar Trap.

Do you remember your first sweets? I do not mean the first pleasure. I mean the first lesson. Who gave them to you? What did the giving mean? Was it "you've been good"? Was it "here is love"? Was it "everyone has some, join in"?

We were taught that BAD SUGAR means love, reward, fuel. That lesson was not true. It was advertising. It was culture. It was repeated until it felt like our own thought.

And once the belief was installed, the schedule did the rest.

### WHEN EVERYONE IS SICK, NO ONE CALLS IT SICK — THE BOILING FROG

How do you know if you are fine?

We usually answer with feeling. I feel okay. I get by. Everyone I know eats like this. My bloods were normal last year. I am not in hospital. So it cannot be that bad.

That feels sensible. It is not.

Let me take you to a kitchen. Not a lecture hall. A kitchen with an old pot on the stove.

Put a frog in cold water. Turn the heat a fraction. The water warms. The frog adjusts. Turn it a fraction more. It adjusts again. All day, degree by degree, the frog calls warm normal. At any moment you could ask the frog "is it hot?" and it would say "fine." By evening the water simmers and the frog has never once decided it was hot. It was hot the whole time. It just never jumped.

That is normal.

We live in water that warmed so slowly we call it fine. The shop on every corner sells BAD SUGAR at the till. The children's party bags are sugar. The office biscuits appear at ten and three. The ad tells you to treat yourself because you deserve it. The diet tells you to restrict, then you binge, then you swear to restrict again. Everyone does it. So we adjust.

Does everyone doing it make it not a trap? Does a whole pond of warm frogs make the water cool?

The fact is the food environment is not neutral. World health bodies set a clear ceiling for free sugars — less than ten percent of daily energy, about fifty grams — and they note extra benefit below five percent. Those are population recommendations, not individual poison lines. And what is true across populations is that current consumption lives far above both ceilings. Excess is normal.

Do you see the trick? Normal is not healthy. Normal is excess.

If you grew up in that pond, would you know? Would you call excess moderate because everyone exceeds? Would you say "I don't keep it in the house so I'm in control" while the street outside keeps it for you?

We did. We measured ourselves against the pond, not the spring.

There is a second way normal fools you. We check feeling, not arithmetic. "I feel okay after my fix. So it helped." But the lift you feel is not health. It is relief from the dip the last dose created. You never feel better than a person who never needed the lift at all. You just feel less below.

We will pull that mechanism fully apart later. For now, notice how "I feel fine" can be true and still blind. The frog feels fine too. Until it does not. But by then it thinks fine is tired, crashy and foggy at three pm, heavy and restless at night, foggy again in the morning until the first hit.

Do you recognise that day? Many of us do. We call it life. It is the trap.

And there is a number that helps shame lift. On validated scales that ask about addiction-like patterns around highly palatable foods — not a medical diagnosis, not a label on you, simply self-report patterns — about fourteen in every hundred adults and about twelve in every hundred children meet that threshold. That sits in a similar neighbourhood to the rates seen for alcohol and tobacco on their own scales.

I give you that number for one reason only. You are not uniquely weak. You are not the one person who cannot handle treats while everyone else can. You are part of a large, ordinary group that was taught to want an engineered product, on a schedule designed to make you want more.

Does that make the water feel a little cooler? Does it make the word normal a little less trustworthy?

Ask yourself: if the pond were fresh, would I need to want this at all?

Have you noticed that children do not crave the smell of sweetened drinks until they have been taught to drink them? Have you noticed that people who do not use BAD SUGAR do not sit at three pm thinking "I need something RIGHT NOW"?

That wanting was not in you at the start. It was installed.

The water warmed you into wanting. The Sugar Trap then sold you relief from the warmth it created.

Once you see normal as sick, the last defence falls. Not with fear. With clarity.

You were not choosing. You were adjusting. And you can stop adjusting the moment you decide to see the water as it is.
