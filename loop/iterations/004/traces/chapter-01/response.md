You know the answer.

And that answer is good news. It means you already sense the truth. We all sense it. We feel the little tug after the biscuit tin. We feel the quiet bargain we make with ourselves in the supermarket aisle. We feel the 3pm slump and hear the voice that says "I need something RIGHT NOW."

The fact is you would not be holding this book if BAD SUGAR were a genuine pleasure you freely chose.

We pick this book up for one reason. We feel trapped. And we want to be free. That is not weakness. That is honesty.

Have you tried to cut down? Have you tried moderation? Have you tried the diets, the rules, the clever apps, the Monday restart?

The binger who opens a family size bag and thinks "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop" until the box is empty and shame moves in. The comfort eater who hears "I deserve one" after any stress or win. The yo-yoer who lives on the rollercoaster — crash, fix, crash.

We have all been there. I have. You have. We were not weak. We were caught. And we stayed caught because we believed the wrong thing about what BAD SUGAR does for us.

Do you want to stop living in that tug-of-war? Of course you do — that's why you're here. I don't blame you for wanting to be free. I congratulate you for listening to the part of you that already knows there is a freer life.

This book is not a diet. It is not a lecture. It will not ask you to resist. It will show you, simply, that you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. When you see it, you will feel relief. Not dread. Relief.

### MY NIGHT OF EASY ESCAPE

I need to tell you where I stood before I can show you where you will stand.

For years I lived in the Sugar Trap without calling it a trap. I ate like most people eat now. A sweetened drink at the desk. Biscuits with the meeting. A "little treat" after lunch that became another at three. Crisps that were not food but disappeared by the handful. White bread, pastries, desserts that I told myself were joy.

Did I feel in control? No. Did I admit I was out of control? Also no. That is the trap's cleverness. It lets you call daily grazing "moderation" and weekly binges "a bad day." You never have to say the word trapped.

I tried everything the willpower method sells. I cut down. I allowed sugar only on weekends. I kept nothing in the house — then bought it on the way home. I counted days. I bargained. I punished. I told myself to stay strong and resist. Will that ever work for long? It did not. Each attempt made BAD SUGAR more precious. Each rule made the next fix more exciting and more guilty.

Then one evening, after another empty family-size bag and another promise broken, someone showed me the inversion. Not a diet plan. Not a scare film. A simple way of seeing.

Do you know what I noticed first? The "lift" I swore sugar gave me never took me above normal. It just took away the slightly restless dip that the last dose had created. The relief was real. The benefit was not. The rescuer was the perpetrator. That one seeing changed everything.

I did not stop through effort. I stopped because wanting stopped. There was no white-knuckle night. No heroic suffering. I lay down that evening still expecting to crave. I woke up free. And I have stayed happy to be free. Not happy that I am coping. Happy to be free.

Is that possible for you? You will see.

I tell you this not to impress you. I tell you so you trust the next sentence fully. The fact is escape can be easy when the belief changes. The behaviour then follows on its own. You do not need to become a different person. You need to see one thing clearly.

And you will. All you have to do is follow all the instructions. That is the whole contract. No tricks.

**EASILY, IMMEDIATELY AND PERMANENTLY.**

Easily, because there is nothing to resist once you see there is nothing being given to you. Immediately, because freedom is an understanding, not a streak; it arrives the moment the belief changes. Permanently, because a solved illusion does not come back unless you rebuild it.

You may wonder about withdrawal. Is the grip physical? The fact is there is a small, measurable effect seen in controlled animal studies when sugar is binged intermittently and then removed. Repeated hits re-trigger the wanting circuit. After bingeing there is a dip. The lab calls that grip mild but well-defined. Small. Not mighty. And crucially, the modern studies show the pattern depends on the schedule of restriction-binge, not on sugar chemistry alone. The trap is in the intermittent yo-yo — the very cutting-down that diets tell you to do — more than in any single dose. Even cue reactivity — the wrapper, the smell — can fire wanting before the bite. All real. All mild. All brief.

Does that scare you? It should not. That twinge is tiny. It passes. It is not what has kept you trapped for years. What kept you trapped is the belief about what the twinge means. Fix the meaning and the twinge starves. Many people in our early trials described the same hump: a few days of that mild, empty feeling, then gone. Not a life sentence. A hump that passes.

Do you need science to prove this to yourself? No. Your own life already did. Think of the last time you were ill and could not eat sweets for two days. Did you climb the walls? Or did you simply feel a little off for a day and then fine — until the idea of the treat came back? That was the Imp starving and the Spell re-feeding it.

So I will not ask you to be frightened into action. Fear is a terrible jailer. I want you to feel relief. The facts will do their quiet work on the background. The joy will do the rest.

You have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain. You will be free easily, immediately and permanently. Hold me to it.
