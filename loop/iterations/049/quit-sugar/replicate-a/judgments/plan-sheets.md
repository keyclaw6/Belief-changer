## 3. Redefinition and margin

- Boxed definition issued in chapter one: BAD SUGAR means added and refined sugars plus fast junk carbs eaten as treats and fixes — sugary drinks, desserts, sweet snacks, pastries, cookies, ice cream, sweetened cereals, flavored yoghurts, and the free sugars hidden in sauces and breads. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR.
- The CAPS name then carries the definition in every sentence.
- Full trap logic runs inside the line: there is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero as a habit. The good subset — the sugars built into whole fruit, vegetables and plain milk meals with their fibre and food matrix — belongs to the positive authority menu and is never counted as BAD SUGAR.
- Margin for error: the body can cope with an occasional accidental blip, but the mind cannot cope with a deliberate treat. An accidental taste in a sauce or a misread label revives nothing unless the belief is invited back. A chosen just-one reopens the belief and restarts the loop. Guard the belief, not the blip. Seatbelt image: present for accidents, never for erratic driving.

## 4. Clinical advisory

- ID: CLIN-01
- Boxed advisory text: If you live with diabetes, take insulin or glucose-lowering medication, have a history of disordered eating, are pregnant, or take prescribed medication affected by food, speak with your own clinician before changing what you eat, and keep taking prescribed treatment unless that clinician advises otherwise. This book changes a belief; it does not manage illness.
- Placement: defined once here. Cited by ID in safety guardrails and in evidence safety limits. Never fused into instruction wording. Never boxed mid-chapter.
