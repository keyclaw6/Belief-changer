Chapter 15
KEEP THE LIST AND LIVE CLEAR

IN THIS CHAPTER: the short list to photograph and the next ordinary day.

*You have seen the whole picture, and now you keep a short list and live the days as they come.*

IF YOU HAVE COME HERE FIRST

If you have opened this book at this page before reading the pages before it, put this list aside for a minute and hear one request.

This list will not work as a set of tips picked out of context, because the beliefs behind each line were changed in the pages before it.

This ground is built in the pages before it, and those pages repay a straight read from the start.

Start at the beginning and read straight through while you eat as normal, and come back here when you have finished.

You will know when you are ready to keep the list, because the list will sound like description of what you already see.

If you have read the book through, stay here and take the list as your pocket reminder.

All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That line is not a slogan to admire, because it is the contract that carried you here.

You followed each line once in its place, and now you keep all of them together in one view.

Read them slowly and let each one sound like your own voice speaking plainly.

We all arrived here by seeing one thing clearly after another, and we all found that seeing held when the lines stayed together.

You do not need to memorize arguments or carry reasons in your head, because the seeing stays when the thoughts below stay.

You keep the list together rather than carrying single lines apart, because the lines guard each other when they stand side by side.

You look at the whole row in one glance in the morning, and you close the book on doubt for that day.

We all tried carrying fragments before, and we all found fragments fade while the whole row holds.

You know now that clarity loves company, and company is what thirteen lines give each other.

You lend the list no fresh debate, because debate would reopen what seeing closed.

Keep the paper or the photograph where mornings find you, and look at it when the old sentence stirs.

There is the morning kitchen with the list on the table and the day opening without any question to settle.

That opening shows that freedom needs no fresh decision each morning, and you know that steadiness matters because you once began each day with bargaining.

WHAT TO THINK WHEN THE SWEET CROSSES YOUR MIND

A thought of something sweet will cross your mind again, and you already own the thoughts that answer it.

Keep these sentences word for word, because they carry the whole book in the form you can speak in one breath.

When doubt touches you about reading on or about staying clear, answer with the entry promise that opened this book and now closes it.

Tell yourself plainly that you risk nothing and gain everything by staying clear.

That sentence is true in the same way on the last page as on the first page, and you speak it as fact rather than as hope.

We all feared there was a price hidden somewhere in stopping, and we all found the price was in continuing.

You know now that keeping clear costs you nothing you ever owned.

You speak that line when friends raise their eyebrows, and you speak it when your own chest tightens for a second.

When the voice says stopping sounds too hard or too long, answer with the promise triad that stated the contract.

Tell yourself that you stopped easily, at once and for good.

Those three words belong together, and you speak them together whenever the old fear of effort returns.

You speak them in the shop queue, and you speak them at the family table, and the same three words cover both places.

We all believed stopping had to be gradual and painful, and we all learned that pain belonged to the method we left.

You know now that ease came from seeing, and seeing does not wear off.

When the old feeling of missing something rises, name where you stood and where you stand.

Tell yourself that you walked out of the sugar trap and you have no reason to walk back into it.

That name tells the truth about the place you left, and you speak it without anger toward any person.

We all once called the daily reaching normal and personal, and we all learned to call it what it was.

You know now that a trap needs no mourning when its door stands open behind you.

You speak that name when the bright packets crowd the till, and you speak it when the evening cupboard hinge sounds in memory.

When the sentence says this one would be a real pleasure, answer with the illusion phrase that names the claimed benefit.

Tell yourself that there is no such thing here as a real treat or lift.

That phrase covers every claimed benefit in one object, and you speak it whenever any benefit is offered.

We all once listed pleasures and comforts and rewards, and we all watched each one belong elsewhere.

You know now that the moment held the pleasure and the sweet only rode along.

You speak that line at celebration tables, and you speak it on tired afternoons, and the answer stays the same in both rooms.

When the body stirs with a small hollow restlessness, answer it by naming it rightly.

Tell yourself that this is only a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling and it passes while you go on with your hour.

That naming takes the mystery out of the stir, and you speak it calmly rather than with alarm.

We all once read every stir as need, and we all learned that need speaks plainly through hunger.

You know now that this faint stir asks for nothing and carries no order.

You watch it thin while the tea cools, and you forget it before the next task begins.

When the voice says perhaps later or perhaps for a party or perhaps for a season, answer with the stakes phrase in its free valence.

Tell yourself that you are free for every day for the rest of your life.

That horizon is good news rather than a sentence, and you speak it with relief rather than with dread.

We all once heard a lifetime sound like a prison term, and we all learned that a lifetime of not feeding a loop is a lifetime of mornings kept whole.

You know now that the length proves the gain rather than the loss.

You speak that horizon when someone asks how long you will keep this up, and you answer with gladness rather than with a count.

When the sweet crosses your mind in a shop or at a table or on the sofa, answer at once with the joyful replacement thought.

Think with a full chest: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it aloud where you can and silently where others rest, but say it every time and mean it every time.

That thought turns an old print into a fresh hit of relief, and you let relief do what argument once had to do.

We all met those prints in the first weeks, and we all watched them fade faster each time relief answered first.

You know now that speed keeps the door closed without any quarrel.

You let pity for hands still reaching follow the thought, and you feel warmth where envy once rose.

When the old ledger tries to reopen and says the sweet must do something for you, close it with the verdict that switched the whole question.

Tell yourself that It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

Those two sentences leave no middle ground, and you speak them as settled fact.

We all once weighed harm against benefit, and we all learned to ask what benefit ever stood there.

You know now that the answer was nothing, and nothing needs no further audit.

You speak those sentences once and you move on, because repetition would grant the ledger dignity it lost.

When the voice says you must work out your own version of these lines, return to the ease clause that closed every loop.

Tell yourself that All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

That clause removes negotiation, and you speak it kindly rather than sternly.

We all once added exceptions and footnotes, and we all watched exceptions act as doors.

You know now that the whole list kept together keeps you clear.

You speak that clause when advice arrives from magazines and neighbours, and you speak it when memory offers its own revised version.

When you speak about food, keep the distinction that this book drew on its first pages.

Call the treats and fixes and sweetened drinks and pastries and desserts and hidden sweeteners by their kept name, which is BAD SUGAR.

That name carries the definition in every sentence, and you use it whenever the bad subset is meant.

You eat fruit and vegetables and plain milk meals as food, and you never count them under that name.

We all once blurred everything sweet into one fog, and we all learned that clarity keeps the table simple.

You know now that the good foods were never the ones that trapped you.

You use the plain name at home and abroad, and the plain name keeps the shop simple.

There is the steady afternoon with work continuing and attention intact and no fog arriving to thin the hours.

That steadiness shows that the thoughts above hold in working hours, and you know that proof matters because you once paid afternoons for seconds of brightness.

TOMORROW AS IT COMES

You wake tomorrow into the same house and the same hours, and you live them as a free person who needs no new rules.

Breakfast comes when hunger calls, and you eat until satisfaction answers quietly.

Work fills the middle of the day, and the clock moves through the old difficult hour without any ceremony.

The shop holds the same rows and the same music, and you walk it for supper without any inner debate.

Supper sits around talk and laughter, and the meal closes when satisfaction has closed it.

You clear the plates without hurry, and the talk continues while the kitchen settles.

The evening settles into lamps and chairs and rest, and sleep arrives clean.

You wake clear and you begin again, and the same simple pattern proves itself with each return.

You meet people who offer sweetness with kindness, and you decline briefly and warmly and turn talk back to the day.

You hear old sentences from others about earning and deserving and weekends and portions, and you hear them as old recordings without naming them aloud.

You speak about your own change only when asked, and then you speak briefly and gladly.

You keep your attention on faces when dessert talk runs its course, and faces reclaim the minute without any help from you.

If a smell or a song or a tired hour lifts an old print for a second, you answer with the joyful thought and let pity for hands still reaching settle the second.

You grant the second no debate and no weighing, because weighing would dress a print as news.

If a taste arrives by accident in sauce or bread, you note the blip and continue the day without commentary.

You wipe the mouth and you keep the vow intact, and the day proves that accident carries no message.

If a deliberate bite ever warns you, you let the minute teach and you keep the vow intact and you think the joyful thought at the next memory.

There is the meal closed by satisfaction with talk continuing and no sweet to seal it.

That closing shows that enough is a bodily fact, and you know that fact matters because you once feared hunger would never speak clearly.

WHAT WAS NEVER LOST

You may have feared that stopping would take the sweetness out of birthdays and holidays and evenings and love.

Hear this plainly at the end, because it was saved for the end.

The child who could laugh through a whole afternoon and taste an apple bright and feel a day complete without needing anything sweet was never gone.

That child stayed in you through all the years of coating and fog, and clearer taste only uncovers what was covered.

The sweet in life returns once the sweet that covered your taste is gone.

Food sounds louder now, mornings stand steadier now, talk lasts longer now, rest sits deeper now, and none of that needed the packet.

We all believed brightness came wrapped, and we all found brightness lived in the day itself.

You know now that you lost nothing, because there was nothing there to lose.

You taste the apple loud and the soup deep, and you hear talk longer into the night.

You watch children play without thinking of packets, and you sit through long afternoons without watching the clock for a break.

We all thought such hours would feel thin, and we all found thin was the coating leaving rather than pleasure leaving.

You know now that fullness was under the covering all along, and uncovering returns what was always yours.

I tell you plainly that I watched plain days grow fuller rather than thinner, and I promise you that fuller is the normal shape of days once the covering lifts.

There is the quiet aisle walked while the music plays and dinner rides home in the basket.

That quiet shows that the rows lost their voice once belief left them, and you know that silence matters because you once believed the rows owned your hand.

SUMMARY

Photograph these lines and keep them where you can see them. They are the whole method in the order you met it.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND. You lose nothing by hearing this out.

2. DON'T CHANGE ANYTHING YET. Finish the book first and watch how you eat.

3. BEGIN WITH JOY, NOT DREAD. There is no need to feel miserable about reading on.

4. NOTICE THE AFTERNOON. Watch what the lift costs you an hour later.

5. QUESTION WHAT YOU CALL A REWARD. Ask who the treat really serves.

6. EAT WHEN HUNGRY AND NOTICE ENOUGH. Let hunger and satisfaction speak before the sweet does.

7. SEE THE LOOP BACK TO FRONT. The low comes first, the sweet comes second.

8. BLAME THE DESIGN, NEVER YOURSELF. You were outgunned, not weak.

9. REFUSE THE MIDDLE WAY. One does not prove control; it reopens the question.

10. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD. Other methods kept you in the trap.

11. IGNORE ANYONE WHO QUIT BY STRAIN AND CHEERLESS EFFORT. Their struggle is not your path.

12. MEET THE SWEETEST MOMENT HONESTLY. Credit the people and the day, never the sweet.

13. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY MEAL AND VOW FREEDOM. Notice the ugliness, then cross over joyful.

Keep these thirteen together, because together they keep you seeing clearly.

You read them in order when doubt sounds loud, and you glance at the single line you need when doubt sounds soft.

We all kept them on paper until memory kept them for us, and we all found memory learned quickly what relief repeated.

You know now that thirteen short lines weigh less than one old loop, and lightness is what the pocket reminder preserves.

When any sweet thought comes, think at once: BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Get on with enjoying your life.
