Chapter 9
THE TEN-SECOND LOOP

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen clock watched through an hour, the step outside on the hour, the held breath under water, the first-week wave, the want that arrives with coffee and talk

*The smoke never opened anything; it only closed a hunger it had lit minutes before.*

### The Clock That Rings And Collects

The kitchen clock ticks through the morning and you know its sound by heart. You lit at seven and you watched the minute hand move and you felt the call return near eight. You stepped to the back step and you fed the hunger and you came back inside and you called that hour normal. You lived that hour and you believed the hour needed paper to run.

We all lived by that ring. We woke and we fed and we worked a little and hunger whispered and we fed again. We arranged meetings round the next feed and we cut phone calls short to feed and we checked the pack before leaving any room. We called that planning. We called that habit. You know that checking and you know how fast the eye counts sticks before a drive. I do not scold that counting. I ask you to watch what the counting served.

Look at one ordinary day with eyes open and the master shows himself.

You feed at nine and the chest loosens for a short while and the mind clears a little and you credit the dose with settling you. You work till ten and the shoulders tighten and attention thins and the hand moves toward the pocket. You call that tightness pressure from work. You step out and you draw and tightness loosens and you call that dose help. You return and you work till eleven and tightness returns and you feed again. We called that rhythm a day. You know that rhythm and you know how the feet learned the path to the door without asking.

I tell you flat a different reading holds the whole day together without forcing it.

Nicotine is a reliable timekeeper. It rings every hour and it collects. You take a dose and the level in the blood rises within seconds and the brain registers the arrival and hunger quiets. Then the level falls fast and hunger whispers again and the clock rings again. The ring feels like a need for air or pause or company. The ring is hunger asking to close. The dose closes hunger it opened. The clock rings because the last dose faded. The fade proves nothing about your nerves or your workload or your character. It proves a short cycle ran its course and asked to run again.

You know the objection that rises here, spoken in your own voice: "But the ring feels too regular to be hunger. It feels like part of me."

Hear that voice plain and test it against your own day without argument.

You fed every hour and the ring stayed hourly. You flew through a long meeting without feeding and the ring screamed at first and then quieted while you sat still. You slept through the night without feeding and the ring waited till waking. People who never fed hear no ring at any hour and they work and pause and meet without checking any pack. They miss no signal. They keep time by clocks on walls, and the walls ask for nothing.

If the ring belongs to character, why does the ring keep hourly time where paper burns and keep no time where no paper burns? If the dose settles nerves, why do nerves tighten on schedule after every dose while unfed nerves run through the same workload without that hourly tightening? And if the hour needs paper to run, whose hour runs the meeting that ran three hours with no step outside?

You know the honest answers because you lived both the fed hour and the held hour. The ring keeps hourly time where paper burns because each dose sets its own fade and the fade sets the next call. Nerves tighten on schedule after every dose because hunger returns on schedule, and unfed nerves run without that tightening because no fade was set. The meeting ran because hours always ran on work and talk and air, and paper only cut the running into pays. People do not call a thing a friend when the friend rings to collect every hour. They call it a master that learned to speak as need.

The clock rang. The dose collected.

Carry that reading into your next hour. You work and you feel the ring and you check the wall clock and you note how long since the last feed. You watch tightness arrive on time and you read it as fade, not as truth about the morning. You drink water and you keep working and you watch the wave rise a little and pass. That watching is not thin theory. It is the same hour, seen plain.

### Ten Seconds Up, Then Gone

The lighter clicks and the first draw runs hot to the back of the throat and the chest fills and something loosens within seconds. You know that loosening and you know how fast credit moves. You call that loosening calm. You call that loosening focus. You call that loosening return to self. I am warm to you in this and harsh only to the teaching that laid that credit on smoke.

Watch the seconds themselves and the gift changes hands.

When smoke enters the lungs the drug moves into the blood and reaches the brain within seconds, and the level peaks within seconds of the draw. That speed is why the first puff after strain feels like a switch thrown. The brain reads arrival as arrival of help. Then the acute effect fades fast, with the short reward fading alongside, and the smoker feeds again to hold the level and to hold off the low. The fact is simple and it holds for the whole book. The loosening timed the arrival speed. The return of tightness timed the fade speed. The dose never touched the meeting or the bill or the traffic that raised strain. It only closed hunger that the prior dose had reopened.

We mistook speed for power. We took the fastest close for the deepest help. We paid for that mistaking with years of hourly pays and we called paying proof of value. You know that paying and you know how the hand moved faster when strain spiked. I tell you the hand moved faster because hunger plus strain speaks louder, not because smoke knows anything about strain.

You know the voice that defends speed: "Nothing else works that fast. That speed must mean real help."

Hear it and answer with your own lungs and your own day.

You drew and loosening came in seconds and tightness returned within the hour. You drank water and loosening came slower and stayed without billing another hour. You stepped into cold air and breathed deep and shoulders dropped without lighting. People who never fed meet strain with pause and breath and talk and movement, and strain passes as strain passes. They miss no speed. They ride the same spike without setting a second spike underneath it.

If speed proves help, why does the fastest help need paying again within the hour while slower helps hold without another pay? If the draw solves strain, why does strain solved by smoke return on schedule while strain met with air and pause moves through and clears? And if the first puff after strain carries unique power, why does the last puff of the same stick taste bitter and leave the chest tight and the mouth stale?

You know the honest answers because you smoked both the first draw and the last. The fastest help bills again because fast fade sets fast return, and slower helps hold because no fade was set beneath them. Strain solved by smoke returns because hunger returns, and strain met with air moves through because only strain was there to move. The last puff tastes bitter because the stick never carried flavour as property; the first draw only tasted sweet beside hunger. People do not call a thing a cure when the cure must be re-taken hourly to hold the same ache at bay. They call it a feed that posed as remedy.

Speed delivered. The fade collected.

Hold that distinction through your next strain. You feel pressure spike and hunger speaks inside pressure and the hand checks the pocket. You pause and you breathe and you drink water and you watch which part stays. Pressure stays as pressure from the day. Hunger moves as a faint wave and passes. You meet pressure with what pressure asks, work or words or rest, and you let hunger close unfed. That meeting is not less help. It is the same strain, met direct.

### The Lifeguard Who Holds You Under

Picture a pool at noon with light on the water and a guard on the stand. You swim and a hand holds your head under. You struggle and water fills the nose and the chest burns for air. Then the hand lets you up for one breath. You gasp and you call the hand rescue. You thank the hand for air. You stay near the hand because the hand knows breath.

We lived inside that pool for years and we called the hand friend. We felt panic rise and we felt the hand lift and we felt air return and we credited the hand with saving. You know that gasp and you know how sincere the thanks felt. I do not mock that thanks. I show you where the panic started.

A lifeguard who holds you under and then sells you breaths is not a lifeguard. The panic belonged to the hold. The gasp belonged to release from the hold. The hand caused the drowning it then paused. The thanks belonged to air, not to the hand. The fact is flat and it carries the whole book in one picture. The cigarette never gave breath. It held breath under and then let go for seconds and sold the letting as gift.

You feel the pushback at once, in your own words: "But I feel better after I smoke. That better must count for something."

It counts, and I tell you exactly what it counts for without trimming the truth.

You feel better after you smoke because hunger closed. Hunger opened when the last dose faded. The better is real as feeling and false as credit. The better proves the hold ended for seconds. It never proves smoke touched the day. We credited the hand with air because we never watched the hand push. Once the push is watched, credit moves where credit belongs. Air did the saving. Pause did the settling. Talk did the loosening. The finished job did the lifting. The dose only paused its own hold and asked to be thanked.

If the hand saves, why does the swimmer held never reach the side while the swimmer unheld crosses with steady strokes? If breath comes from the hand, why does breath run full all morning where no hand holds while the held chest coughs and tightens between breaths? And if thanks belongs to the hand, why do those who never entered the pool breathe without thanks and call breathing nothing special?

You know the honest answers because you swam both held and unheld in these pages. The held swimmer never reaches the side because each rescue sets the next hold. Breath runs full where no hand holds because lungs always knew breathing. Those outside the pool call breathing nothing special because breathing always was nothing special until a hand made it scarce. People do not call a thing rescue when rescue must be bought hourly from the one who caused the panic. They call it a hold that learned to sell breaths.

The hold caused. The breath was yours.

Keep that picture beside your next want. You feel panic rise in a hard hour and the hand offers one breath and memory of gasps pulls hard. You watch the hand plain and you name the hold and you breathe without the hand. Panic passes as hunger passes. Air stays as air stays. That staying is not theory. It is the same lungs, unheld.

### The First-Week Wave

The fear that holds many in place is not the hour but the week. You picture days of gnawing ache and nights of torment and a body that screams for months. You picture yourself white-knuckled while life runs on without you. You hear that picture in your own voice: "The craving is too strong to be only thought. My body proves I need it."

I hear that fear with full respect and I answer it flat because fear feeds on vagueness.

When you stop feeding, the body registers the missing dose. What arrives is withdrawal pharmacology with a short typical course. It may begin within hours. It typically peaks in the first few days and it usually eases within a few weeks. That is the whole shape for most daily users. It is not months of torture for everyone and it is not identical in any two bodies. History and build shift severity. The edge is sharpest early and then softens as days stack unfed. You know vagueness inflates that edge into a life sentence. I name its length so vagueness loses its office.

Watch the wave itself and the label changes without forcing.

What you feel in those days is a faint hunger more than a pain. It arrives as an empty restlessness and a thin edginess that comes and goes in waves. It rises for minutes and then ebbs. It sharpens when you pause between tasks and dulls when attention takes you. It sharpens with coffee and drink and talk because those hours were always fed, and it dulls in new hours that carry no feed memory. We called that wave self. We called that wave need. You know that naming and you know how the hand moved to close every rise. I tell you the wave is hunger unfed, not self speaking.

You lived harder weeks than this wave. You sat through flu and through sleepless nights with a sick child and through long drives with a bad back, and you met each without calling it proof of need for paper. You know your own strength in those weeks and I speak to that strength now without flattery. This wave is smaller than those trials and it shortens as you watch. It asks no fight. It asks to be read plain while it fades.

If the wave proves need, why does the wave peak early and then fade while unfed, while true needs grow louder the longer unfed? If hunger for smoke belongs to self, why does hunger arrive in minutes-long waves with clear gaps while self stays through hunger and through fullness alike? And if the body cannot run without paper, why do mornings in these pages run complete with clear air while the wave still speaks softly underneath?

You know the honest answers because you felt the wave rise and fall in a single hour. The wave peaks and fades unfed because no new fade was set and the body returns toward its natural baseline on its own. Hunger arrives in waves because unfed craving pulses and passes, and self stays because self never was hunger. Mornings run complete because breath and taste and legs always did the running, and the wave speaks underneath as dying noise, not as orders. People do not call a thing need when need fades the longer unfed. They call it noise that posed as need.

The wave rose. The wave passed.

Meet your next wave with that reading in your hands. You feel emptiness rise after a meal and the mind says smoke. You note the hour and you breathe and you keep clearing the table and you watch minutes move. The wave crests and then thins. Another wave comes later and crests lower. That lowering is not hope. It is the same body, unfed, settling.

### The Echo And The Voice

Two things speak in the same chest and we spent years hearing them as one. I lay them apart now so you can hear each clean.

The first is the tiny physical craving. It is small and starving and already dying once unfed. It speaks as hunger, not as thought. It knows no words for stress or company or reward. It only pulses and passes. The second is the brainwashing voice. It knows every word you ever laid over hunger. It says smoke calms and smoke focuses and smoke belongs with coffee and talk. It borrows hunger volume to sell old sentences. Once hunger is separated from voice, the job divides clean. Hunger fades on its own when unfed. The voice loses volume once credit moves. The target is the story feeding hunger, not the pulse itself.

We fed both with one act and we called one act proof of both. We felt hunger pulse and we heard voice speak and we lit and both quieted for a short while and we called quiet proof of truth. You know that quiet and you know how total it felt for minutes. I tell you quiet proved only that hunger closed and voice rested while hunger slept. Neither proved smoke touched the day.

Try the separation as a lived exercise through your next three hours, not as theory.

You choose a morning stretch with clock in view and you note the time of the last feed. You work and you watch hunger arrive near the hour and you mark its shape without lighting. You note how it rises for minutes and ebbs without your help. You note how voice arrives on top of hunger with a full sentence about calm or taste or company. You answer voice with your own evidence from these pages, mornings owned and stairs climbed and coffee tasted, and you let hunger pass unfed. You repeat through the next hour and the next. Hunger pulses lower. Voice repeats older lines with less pull. That is enacted perception, not will. You watch the clock and the wave and the line between them sharpens.

You know the last defence voice speaks here, quiet and adult: "Even if hunger is small, my cue want feels huge at parties and with drinks. That size must mean something real remains."

Hear it fully before answering, because parties and drinks carry long memory.

Cue want is voice plus memory, not hunger grown large. Hunger stays small and short and fades within weeks when unfed. Cue want arrives later, in old rooms, with old company, round old hours, and it borrows size from scene. The scene holds music and talk and light and nearness, and voice says paper made the scene glow. You watched scenes in these pages hold complete without paper, and you credited the minute, not the paper. That credit stands in louder rooms also. Company glows because company glows. Music lifts because music lifts. Air at night tastes clean because night air tastes clean. The want that arrives there is an old line surfacing where it was often fed, not a body calling for need. Lines fade when unfed. Scenes stay.

If cue want proves need, why does cue want arrive only in old rooms while hunger arrives by the clock, and why does cue want vanish in new rooms where hunger still pulses on time? If parties need paper to glow, why do unfed evenings stayed inside hold together laughing while fed evenings step out and return cold and cut? And if voice speaks truth about calm and company, why did calm and company in these pages arrive fuller once credit moved from paper to minute?

You know the honest answers because you lived owned hours and watched credit move. Cue want arrives in old rooms because memory lives in rooms, and hunger arrives by the clock because fade sets timing. Unfed evenings hold because talk and warmth always did the holding, and fed evenings cut because tending cut them. Calm and company arrived fuller once credit moved because calm and company always did the giving. People do not call a thing the host when the party runs warmer once the guest stays away. They call it a hanger-on that learned to sit beside.

That hourly ring is the nicotine trap doing its only work.

What you feel between rings is a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling that comes and goes, nothing more.

Read that feeling as dying noise and the story over it as the target, and the loop loses its office. The dose set the clock. The fade rang the bell. The voice sold the ring as truth about life. Once ringing is read as fade and voice is read as old teaching, no part of the loop points to life. Hunger passes. Voice thins. Hours hold. You meet strain with work and rest with pause and company with talk, and each pays direct without a middle hand taking its cut.

I speak to you now with the full weight of watched hours behind me. You inhabited mornings and you climbed stairs talking and you tasted coffee to the bottom and you walked with empty hands and you sat with hands still. Those hours were not thin and they were not brave. They were ordinary and complete. The loop ran underneath them and they held. That holding is your evidence, not my promise. Trust what your own days proved while hunger still whispered.

You watched mornings hold complete while hunger still whispered underneath, and you watched stairs climb talking while the wave still moved, and you watched coffee hold flavour to the bottom while old lines still surfaced. Those three holdings prove the split plain without further theory. Hunger pulses and voice speaks while hours hold complete around both. Each hour that holds while hunger pulses teaches the chest a new reading that no sentence can undo. You carry that reading forward as lived fact, not as hope held for later. The next ring will come on time because fade sets timing, and the next wave will crest lower because unfed hunger loses volume, and the next old line will sound thinner because credit stays with the minute.

SUMMARY
- Hourly want follows dose fade, not the needs of the hour. Watching the clock shows tightness arriving on time after each feed and absent where no feed was set.
- The fast loosening after a draw times drug arrival within seconds. The return of want times fast fade, which proves replenishment rather than help for strain.
- The gasp after lighting feels like rescue because a hold was paused. Air and pause and talk did the settling all along.
- The restless empty wave after stopping peaks early and usually eases within weeks. Its pulsing and passing marks dying hunger rather than self.
- Later wants in old rooms borrow size from memory and scene. Company and music hold complete without paper, which shows cue lines fading apart from physical need.
- Hunger and the voice are heard apart now. Hunger pulses small and passes unfed while the old story thins once credit stays with the minute.
