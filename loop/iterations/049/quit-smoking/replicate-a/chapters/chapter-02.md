Chapter 2
WHO IS IN CHARGE

IN THIS CHAPTER: garage hide, hourly clock, house without a pack

*You were never weak. You were caught, and your own body can prove it.*

### The Garage After Supper

The garage is cold after supper. The family is inside with the plates still warm, the television low, the talk going on without us. We slip out with the pack and the lighter, past the old car, past the boxes we never unpacked, and we close the side door soft so it does not bang.

We know this walk by heart. You feel the concrete under thin shoes and you know it means another hide. You smell oil and damp cardboard and you know the smell means you are out of sight. You see the single bulb take a second to hold and you understand it lights a ritual, not a pleasure. We stand where no one watches and we light, and you know the first draw feels like arrival. You know it is not joy you feel but arrival, as if you held your breath inside and you believe you breathe only here, alone, in the cold.

Do you know that standing place? Of course you do. Every daily smoker has one. You know the doorway where you hide and you see it means the same walk. You know the stairwell where you linger and you understand it means the same clock. You remember the corner of the yard and you know it serves the same schedule. We tell the people we love that we are just getting air. We tell ourselves we prefer the quiet. In the garage both sentences sound thin, because the body knows why the feet moved.

I am not writing that to shame you. I have stood in that cold with smokers who wept telling it, and I tell you flat that there is nothing weak in you for standing there. There is something accurate in you for noticing the walk. The feet moved before the mind agreed. The hand checked the pocket before the mind chose. That is not choice as you understand choice in any other part of life. That is a schedule using your legs.

Look at the evening from the outside for a minute, as an investigator. Inside, warmth and company you claim to love. Outside, cold and fumes you claim to enjoy. If cigarettes truly gave calm and company, why do we leave calm and company to get one? If the light truly made the meal complete, why must the meal be interrupted to step away from the table where the pleasure sits? Ask it plainly and the old story wobbles. The scene that should prove pleasure proves its opposite. We leave what we love to feed what we claim to love.

That leaving is not small. We plan days around it. We carry the pack the way we carry keys. We count how long a film lasts, how long a meeting lasts, how long a flight lasts, not by interest but by interval. We step out of weddings. We step out of work. We stand in rain under thin awnings with strangers and call them friends for seven minutes. Then we go back inside smelling of smoke and chew a mint and hope no one minds. We Febreze the hell out of ourselves and walk back in as if nothing happened.

Call that what it is when seen without flinching. The day is no longer lived in hours. It is lived in doses. Each dose is small. Each interval is short. The circle is tight, and we walk it faithfully while calling ourselves free. That faithful circle, walked in cold garages and doorways while life goes on inside without us, is the nicotine trap.

There is your first plain name for what holds you. You know it is not a lecture aimed at you. You see it is not a label on you. You understand it is a description of the circle. You walk out. You feed. You return. You wait. You walk out. Once the circle is seen, it cannot be unseen, and seeing it costs no pain. It only asks honesty about feet and doors and cold air.

### The Hourly Clock

Every smoker lives by a clock no one hung on the wall.

Ours rings about every hour. You know it sometimes rings every forty minutes and you understand timing proves the pattern. You know it sometimes waits two hours when work holds you and you see delay proves nothing changed. But it rings. Attention thins. The chest feels a touch hollow. The fingers look for something to hold. The mind finds a reason to stand. You tell yourself you know you need air and you believe the thought is fresh. You tell yourself you know you need to check the car and you understand the reason is a reminder. The reason sounds fresh each time. The timing gives it away. Reasons that arrive on schedule are not reasons. They are reminders from a pattern.

We learned to read that clock early and to call it need. We say the stick settles us. We say ciggies punctuate the day. We say habit smokes, as if habit were a small engine inside us with its own timetable. Listen to that phrase with clear ears. Habit smokes. Who smokes then? Not you choosing. Something smoking through you on time.

I ask you three plain questions, and I ask you to answer only from your own day. If you truly choose each cigarette freely, why does the want arrive on time whether the day is good or bad? If you could stop whenever you wanted, why does wanting to stop never stop the clock? And if each light is a free pleasure, why does missing one on time feel like pressure behind the ribs rather than like missing a pleasure you can take later?

You know the honest answer because you have lived the hours. The want keeps time. Choice does not keep time. Hunger for food keeps rough time because the body needs fuel. This keeps time because the last dose fades on time. That is all. The clock is not character. It is not weakness. It is replenishment calling, dressed as desire.

Think of a friend who never smoked sitting through the same film you sit through. He does not check his pocket at minute fifty. He does not rehearse the walk to the door. He does not bargain with himself to hold on until the credits. He simply watches the film. We watch the film and the clock. That split attention is not personality. We were not born clock-watchers. We became clock-watchers dose by dose, until the day organised itself around the next feed. The reliable timekeeper in our pocket is not a friend who tells time. It is a master who collects on time.

See how the language shifts once the clock is visible. We no longer say we enjoy being chained. We say we are chained and have called the chain normal. The chain is light. It does not bruise. It only shortens the leash to about an hour and asks to be fed to lengthen it a little. Fed, it shortens again. That is why the garage hide repeats, not because the garage is pleasant but because the clock rang and the garage is where we answer without being seen.

There is no shame in having answered. We all answered. We were taught that answering proves the cigarette helps us cope. The fact is the clock proves the opposite. A helper that must be seen every hour is not helping. It is occupying. Once that is seen, the old pride in coping falls away without a fight. What remains is a simple, almost boring truth. We feed on time because we are fed on time.

### The House Without A Pack

Try a small honest memory, not as a test of nerve but as observation.

Recall leaving the house without a pack after decades of carrying one. Perhaps the pocket felt light at the gate. Perhaps the hand patted the coat twice, then a third time, as if patting could make the weight appear. You remember the morning was ordinary and you know shops and bus and work should have filled it. You know the shops and the bus asked nothing of the pack and you see the day asked only presence. Yet the mind kept returning to the missing weight. You know it was not to smoke this minute but you understand it was knowledge that smoke could not be had on time if the clock rang far from a shop.

Notice what that proves. The distress did not wait for the hour. It began at the gate, hours before any physical want could bite. The mind raced ahead to a future ring and suffered it in advance. A free chooser does not suffer a missing object he does not presently want. A man carrying an umbrella he forgot does not panic about rain that may not come. We panic because we know the clock will ring and we will have no answer ready. That panic is not about the present moment. It is about a future feed already owned by the pattern.

I have heard smokers describe that walk in the same words across towns and decades. They call that walk strange and off, as if a shoe were missing. That uneasy feeling proves they already sense the schedule owns them. Keys and wallet are tools for the day. The pack is not a tool for the day. It is the day's master packed flat in cardboard. Forgetting tools makes tasks harder. Forgetting the pack makes the mind harder on itself before anything hard happens.

Ask again with that walk in mind. Who is in charge on that walk? You, who decided to go to the shops, or the missing cardboard that decides how you feel about going? If you choose smoking the way you choose a coat, why does forgetting a coat not produce the same hollow pacing? A coat is useful and its absence is a practical problem. The pack is useless for every task on your list, yet its absence colours every task. That colouring is not usefulness remembered. It is ownership felt.

We hid use to hide that ownership, even from ourselves. The lone guy outside the office with the lighter cupped against wind tells himself he likes the air. The closet smoker with the spray in the bag tells herself no one knows and therefore no one can judge. Each story protects dignity. I respect the dignity and I dispute the story, warmly and flatly, because dignity survives truth here. You hid because some honest part of you already registered the leash. People do not hide free pleasures with that care. They hide what they sense has taken hold.

That sensing is your ally. We instinctively sense when something has taken hold of us. That unease you felt at the gate without the pack was not proof you cannot live without smoke. It was proof you already live under a timetable you never voted for. The unease belongs to the con, not to you. You are not uneasy by nature. You are uneasy on schedule.

Hold that walk beside the garage hide and the hourly clock and one picture forms. You see feet moving out while the family stays inside, and you know the schedule moved them. You see attention splitting during films and the pocket patted at the gate, and you know one master owns those different hours. That master has nothing to do with your strength. Strong people follow timetables they hate when the timetable lives inside the body and is misnamed as choice.

### The Hug That Does Not Let Go

There are two parts to the hold, and naming them plainly makes the rest of this book simple.

The first part is tiny and physical. After the last dose fades, the body registers a small vacancy. You know it is not pain and you understand it is not illness. You feel a light restlessness and you know it proves nothing about need. You notice an empty hum behind the ribs and you understand it means a fading signal that will steady on its own. You know it well because you meet it every night between the last light and the first light of morning. It begins within hours for many. It is familiar. It is small. Left unfed, it peaks and passes as the body returns to its natural baseline on its own. Fed, it quiets for a short while and then returns on time to be fed again.

The second part is large and mental. It is the story laid over that small vacancy since your first borrowed light behind the school or at a party or on a work break. The story says the vacancy means you need smoke to be calm, to think, to belong, to enjoy. The story was taught by every lit scene you ever saw presented as ease, by every break framed as reward, by every friend who offered and every advertisement that linked smoke with poise. That learned story interprets every hollow minute as proof the cigarette gives something real. Without that story, the vacancy would be a passing itch. With that story, the itch feels like meaning.

Call them in plain words so they stay small and winnable, as the tiny physical craving and the brainwashing voice. One is a fading signal in the body. The other is a loud commentary that feeds the signal by crediting smoke with relief. The craving unfed starves and quiets. The voice fed grows and calls again. The book aims all its fire at the voice, because once the voice stops lying, the craving has nothing to live on.

Picture the hold the way it feels from inside, not as science but as lived arms. It is like a hug that does not let go. At first the arms feel warm around the shoulders. Held breath loosens. The mind says here is comfort. Then the talk inside the hug continues after comfort should have ended. The arms stay. The chest cannot expand fully. The mind must ask leave to step back for an hour. When leave is granted, it is granted only until the next embrace. The warmth was real for a minute. The keeping is real for years. That is the inhale as we actually live it. A brief softening, then a long holding after the arms should have opened.

Do not harden that picture into more than a picture. It is not a creature with will. It is not a mighty enemy demanding a battle. It is a fair image of replenishment mistaken for comfort. The softening lasts minutes. The holding lasts until the next dose. Once the image is seen, the famous love of the light shrinks to size. We never loved being held. We loved the second the grip eased, and we mistook the easing for a gift.

Here is where the circle gets its portable name. You know doses arrive on time and you understand they prove the schedule. You remember garage hides and pocket pats at the gate and you know they prove the same ownership. You understand a tiny vacancy misread by a loud story drives the circle and you know feeding keeps it alive. That whole works, with its clock and its hiding and its hug that stays past welcome, is the nicotine trap.

Speak it plainly and it loses menace. Traps are not moral verdicts on the caught. They are devices that work until seen. A snare does not prove the rabbit foolish. It proves the snare well set and baited with something the rabbit already wants. You walked in on false information that smoke calms and pleases. The trap then removed choice while preserving the feeling of choosing. Seeing the device is not defeat. It is the beginning of exit.

You will meet that small vacancy again in these pages when we look at mornings and meals and pauses. Meet it in its book words from now on, so the body is read correctly as a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling that comes and goes. That sentence is not poetry. It is instruction for the nerves. When the wave arrives, you will know its name and its nature. You know it is faint and empty so it cannot force you and you see emptiness proves it is not guidance. You know it is slightly edgy and comes and goes so waiting is enough and you believe waiting starves it. Hold that reading and the voice loses its main witness.

### Why Twenty Failures Prove Nothing About You

"I choose this. I could stop if I wanted. My failures prove I am weak."

I hear that thought because smokers speak it in every town, often with a short laugh that does not convince even themselves. Let us test it against your own receipts rather than against my claims, because your receipts are stronger than any lecture.

Count the attempts honestly. Many of us attempted at least twenty times before and used most of the replacements and meds on the market with no success. Gum chewed until the jaw ached, then back to ciggies when the box ran out. Patches counted and rationed. Strained abrupt stops with teeth clenched and hours counted. Cutting down to half a pack with the day reorganised around the permitted lights. Each attempt began with grim resolve. Each ended with quiet return. Each return felt like a verdict on character.

I tell you flat that the verdict was misaddressed. Those attempts were typical in this community, not proof you are uniquely weak. I have sat with teachers and drivers and nurses and builders who describe the same drawer full of boxes and the same dented promise to themselves. Strong, reliable people who raise children and hold jobs and keep words in every other domain. If weakness explained the return, weakness would show everywhere. It does not. It shows only where the belief stayed intact while behaviour was forced.

That is the work of the Willpower Method. That is its plain name in this book from now on. The method that leaves the loud story untouched and orders the body to endure loss by force. Do not smoke and miss it. Count days and mourn. Avoid corners and friends. White-knuckle the clock while still believing the clock guards something precious. No wonder the mind rebelled. No wonder one hard day or one drink with friends brought the old thought back dressed as comfort. The belief survived, so the behaviour returned on time.

Blame that method, never yourself, and watch hope return without hype. It takes a strong will to persist for years in something that goes against all instinct, that burns the throat and sends you to cold garages while family stays warm inside. Your persistence proves strength misdirected, not weakness. Once the story changes, strength is no longer required to hold a line, because there is no line to hold. There is only a seen device and a quiet body.

Ask the logic trap that brooks no honest dodge. If you wanted to stop a hobby you enjoy, would you need a book on how to stop? Would you need gum and patches and counted days to stop eating mustard? The mere fact you hold this book proves you are not freely choosing in the way free choice is meant. Free pleasures do not require methods. Traps require methods, and wrong methods require repeating. You repeated the wrong method. That is all. That repetition is not biography. It is bibliography.

Do you want to stop? Of course you do. That is why you are reading. Does wanting to stop while continuing prove you are broken? No. It proves the tug you feel has two ropes and both belong to the same device. One rope is the tiny vacancy calling on time. The other is the loud story calling it need. Pull either rope and the device tightens while the story is believed. See both ropes as one device and the tug loses authority. That seeing is not effort. It is recognition, and recognition cannot be failed the way a test of nerve can be failed.

Keep your receipts, then, but refile them. Twenty attempts do not file under weak. They file under misled about the job. The job was never to endure. The job is to see. You attempted endurance many times. You have not yet attempted clear seeing with an open mind to the end. That attempt is still ahead, and it asks nothing harsh of you.

### Tired, Wheezy And Owned By The Hour

The body keeps honest books even when daylight talk cooks them.

Mornings tell first and you know they report truly. You know your throat is raw and you understand it proves smoke passed where only air belongs. You feel your chest tight and you know it proves the cost is real. You remember stairs needing a pause you did not need ten years ago and you understand the pause proves the body keeps books. You know the cough that finds us at three in the morning and you understand it speaks truth in the dark. You count money at the counter daily becoming notes weekly becoming a holiday yearly for upkeep of the cough and you understand the sum proves the toll. None of this requires lecture to land. You lived it. You know it cold.

Give that state its fixed words so it cannot be softened back into normal. You know it is not tired as a good day tires and you understand the difference proves the dose maintains it. You know it is not breathless as stairs wind anyone and you see the difference proves smoke did it. You recognize a specific kept state, dose-maintained and clock-maintained, known as tired, wheezy and chained to the next cigarette and you understand those words fix the address. That is the permanent address while the circle runs. You know you are tired because sleep is broken by the clock even at night and you understand broken sleep proves ownership. You know you are wheezy and chained to the next cigarette because smoke passes where only air belongs and attention never leaves the coming feed and you see that split proves the leash. You know those three words name the bill paid daily and you believe naming stops softening.

I state the bill flat because you deserve plain speech, and I state it once without dressing it as horror. You know years inside that address change how days feel and you understand they change how long good years last. You remember the dawn reach and the garage cold and the pocket pat and you know those hours prove small taxes. You understand those small taxes are levied for the rest of your life if the circle is kept and you believe keeping means paying. That horizon is the stakes in one phrase. You know kept, the circle bills you for the rest of your life and you understand billing proves the trap. You know seen and left, ordinary life returns for the rest of your life and you believe return proves freedom.

Do not change from fear. This method takes care of that.

Read that relief as fact, not as comfort talk. Fear never freed anyone, and I do not want fear as your reason. I want clear seeing as your reason, because fear fades and seeing stays. The body quiets on its own when unfed. The mind clears when credit is corrected. What remains is not a void to endure. It is ordinary completeness tasted fully. You know clear stairs without cough prove the body returns and you understand ease proves freedom. You see the patio owned again without stepping out proves company needs no exile and you know staying proves ease. You feel a deep breath taken at the kerb without planning proves air is enough and you understand the breath proves baseline returned. Those are not promises of marvels. They are the day as it was before the circle took its cut.

Meet the coming vacancy in that frame when it arrives in the next days of reading. It will arrive, because you still feed on time while we talk. When it arrives, read it in book words rather than in old story words as a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling that comes and goes, which cannot be a command. It is noise fading, not need calling. It typically builds in the first few days for most daily users and usually eases within weeks. It may begin within hours. For some the sharpest edge sits a couple of days after the last feed, then softens. That typical course is not a pledge about your exact hour. Bodies differ. Histories differ. The shape holds. You know it is small and passing and you understand small means it cannot force you and waiting is enough. You remember it is familiar from every night between evening and dawn and you believe familiarity proves you survived it before. You survived that gap nightly for years without drama. You can observe it in daylight with the same ease once the story stops calling it proof.

That is the body as authority rather than as accuser. You know the throat and chest report truly at night and on stairs and you understand the report proves cost. You know the hollow reports truly but briefly when unfed and you understand brief proves noise, not need. Each report, read correctly, testifies against the old credit, not against you. The body never asked for smoke. It only asked to be heard without the loud commentary laying its lines over every signal. Hear it plain and the later kills in this book have ground to stand on. Stress and pleasure and company and reward will each be weighed on this ground. For now, ground is enough. Choice was taken by device. Failures belong to method. The body tells true.

You enter this chapter willing to investigate but still calling smoking choice. You can leave it knowing the schedule owns choice and past failures belong to method. That shift asks no behaviour yet. It asks only that feet and doors and clocks be believed over slogans about choice. Believe them. They were there.

I lay one rule down now because without it the rest cannot land. Take it as a cheerful command from someone who has watched this work again and again.

2. SMOKE AS NORMAL UNTIL YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE
So belief changes before behavior does.

Hold to that rule and the investigation stays honest. No forcing. No counting. No early test of nerve to prove what seeing will prove without pain. Smoke where you always smoke while we talk. Weigh each page against garage and clock and gate. When objection rises, speak it plainly and let the pages answer. An open investigator cannot stay held by an old story once the device stands clear.

SUMMARY
- Choice was a con, not a flaw. Feet, doors and clocks prove the schedule decides while the mind still claims to choose.
- Past failures with gum, patches and strained stops were typical and belong to the wrong method, not to your worth.
- The hold has two parts. You know a tiny physical signal fades when unfed and you understand a loud learned story feeds it by misreading the signal as need.
- The body reports truly. Morning cough, hourly hollowness and the missing-pack unease mark a device at work, not a self that needs smoke.
- Smoking on time bills tired breath and chained attention across a lifetime, while ordinary completeness waits on the same horizon once the device is seen.
