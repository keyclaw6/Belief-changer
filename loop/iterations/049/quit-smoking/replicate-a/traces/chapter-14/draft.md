Chapter 14
THE FINAL CIGARETTE

IN THIS CHAPTER: daylight on an ordinary pavement, a dog pulling ahead on the lead, an ordinary pack with two left, close attention on the smell and the harshness, a solemn vow spoken and meant, the remaining pack left behind

*You smoke one last ordinary cigarette with your eyes open, and you are free from the moment you mean it.*

The pavement is ordinary and the light is ordinary and the dog pulls ahead to the next smell while you walk with your hands in your pockets. You carry the same brand you always carry and the pack holds two. Nothing about the hour is special and that is the point. No party glows around you. No strain hangs over you. You have no surviving proof that paper ever gave you anything and you feel eager to close. I meet you here because this is where closing happens, not in a bright ceremony and not in a dark farewell, but on a plain walk where you see clear and you finish clear.

### ARE YOU READY

I ask you straight because I want joy at the close and not a forced march. Do you feel ready to finish. Are you eager to leave the last thread behind and walk on without it.

You hear your own voice answer first, and I want to hear it with you. Most readers at this step say yes at once. They feel light and impatient and they know there is nothing left to defend. If that is you, read on and I will tell you which one and how and what to say. You can stop easily, immediately and for good.

Some readers feel a different pull. They hear a small voice that says I see it all but I am not quite ready. I still feel nervous about the moment itself. What if I miss it. What if I choose the wrong time.

Hear that voice with warmth because I never call you weak for hearing it. We all heard some version of it when we stood near the end of the trap. We had been conned for years into calling paper a friend and the brainwashing voice does not go quiet in one hour. It speaks last in the language of timing. It says wait. It says not yet.

Test that voice against your own life and it thins. If paper still gave you a genuine pleasure or crutch, what would waiting protect. Would an extra week of feeding teach you anything you have not lived through a thousand times. And if paper gives you nothing, what would waiting add except more doses to pay for and more ash to wash from your hands.

You know the honest answers because you have watched every hour with me. Waiting would not teach. Waiting would only bill. The nervousness is not proof that belief still lives. It is the last stir of a long habit of expecting loss where there is none. There is nothing to give up; you are escaping.

So here is the gate and I put it plain. If you feel eager, go on to the walk below and close tonight. If you still feel unwilling, if some part of you still guards a reason and believes paper holds one gift, do not force the moment with strain. Put this chapter aside and read the book again from the start with an open mind. The second reading takes days, not months, and it completes what the first reading began. What will not help is clenching your teeth and ordering yourself to be ready. Readiness is not summoned. It arrives when seeing is complete.

I say this with cheerful sternness because I want you free, not driven. The trap kept you by pressure. I will not keep you by pressure. Come through when you know.

### WHICH ONE AND HOW

You do not need to arrange a special night. You do not need a last supper with paper. You close on the next ordinary cigarette you would have smoked in the ordinary course of the day.

Take the dog out or take yourself out for the same short walk you take most days. Carry your ordinary brand. Carry no ceremony with it. When the time comes for the next dose in the schedule, have that one and have it with full attention.

This last one is a closing, not extra smoking. Do not smoke more than you would have smoked in order to study it. Do not smoke two or three to make the lesson stronger. One ordinary instance watched straight is enough because you have watched hundreds already. What I ask is attention, not quantity.

Light as normal and then keep your eyes open for what paper actually does while you feed.

Notice the smell first because smell never lies after years of covering. You catch it on the fingers and on the coat even outdoors. You know that clean morning stairs without cough smell of nothing, and you know this smells of burning paper and stale air. People who never fed walk the same pavement breathing only cold air. You breathe cold air mixed with smoke and you call the mixture richer. Is it richer. Does the added smell make the air wider or narrower.

Notice the harshness next because harshness speaks while the brainwashing voice talks over it. The first draw scratches. The throat tightens a fraction. The chest takes the dose fast and the level rises within seconds and the tiny physical craving quiets for a little while. You feel the quiet and you are taught to call the quiet pleasure. People who carried no hollow through the same walk feel no scratch and need no quiet. They walk at full level while you rise toward their level for minutes. Is that a gift. Does closing a small hollow that feeding reopened this morning count as help.

Notice the attention itself because attention proves the schedule owns more than the minute. Even on this plain walk part of you counts. How much is left in the pack. Where will the next one come from. How long before the faint empty stir returns. The dog pulls ahead to a gate and sniffs and pulls on while you stand for a moment to finish. The walk waits for the dose. The dose does not serve the walk. Who is in charge in that picture.

You hear the defence speak even here, quiet and reasonable: but I still like the ritual of stopping on the walk and lighting. It marks the hour.

Hear it and answer with your own walk. What marks the hour is pause and air and movement, not fire. The pause would mark just as well with empty hands. The air would taste cleaner with empty hands. The dog would still pull and the light would still lie flat on the pavement and thought would still drift and settle. Paper rode the pause. Pause owned the mark.

Watch the cigarette down to its last third without looking away because the last third testifies. Does the taste stay kind. Does the mouth stay fresh. Or does the draw turn hot and bitter while the fingers grow stale, proving that ash was never flavour, only cover. You know the answer because you have lived the bitter end thousands of times and masked it with washing and changing and airing. People do not wash away what purely gives. They wash away what takes.

Let the cigarette burn out in the ashtray or the bin at the end of the walk. Do not keep it half lit as a keepsake. Do not press it out early to avoid seeing. See it whole. This is what feeding always was when no glow lent it costume. A dose fed. A hollow closed. A hollow reopened. A smell left on the hands.

### THE SOLEMN VOW

You stand now where we all stood when seeing turned into leaving.

We were conned into believing paper calmed and pleased and carried us. We fed on schedule for years. We paid with money and breath and hours and we called the payment pleasure. The brainwashing voice fed the tiny physical craving with stories and the tiny physical craving called back for more doses to prove the stories true. That loop is now seen through. No drop remains with paper. No gift survives to mourn.

You hear the last whisper before the threshold, and I answer it before you speak it: but this feels too simple. Surely leaving something that owned so many years must hurt more than this.

Hear it with tenderness because the whisper sounds like wisdom and is only old teaching. Would a longer pain prove a larger gift. If leaving were truly hard, would thousands of ordinary men and women who saw the same loop walk away on an ordinary day and never look back.

You know the honest answers because you carry no surviving reason to argue against them. Pain never measured value. Ordinary people leave when seeing is whole because there is nothing left to leave with strain.

So speak now and mean it. Speak inside, in your own words, but hold these two certainties inside the words: that this was your final cigarette, and that you are free from the moment you finish it.

12. SMOKE YOUR FINAL CIGARETTE WITH A SOLEMN VOW AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE
You cross on joy, not loss.

Say it as a vow, not as a wish. I smoked my final cigarette. I am free. I will never feed again because there is nothing to feed for. Say it once and mean it the way you mean any plain fact about your own life. Your name is your own. Your house is your own. Paper owns nothing in you now.

Do not bargain beside the vow. Do not add unless or except or only at parties. One kept exception keeps the whole story alive because if there is no benefit in feeding often there is no point in feeding occasionally. The reader who keeps one keeps the belief that one gives something, and the belief reopens the hollow wider than any dose. Let the vow stand whole.

Leave what remains of the pack behind you now. You carry two at the start of the walk so that the close is ordinary and not staged by emptiness. You finish one with eyes open. Leave the other where you cannot reach it without deliberation. Drop it in a street bin. Leave it on a wall for no one. Pour water on it if you like finality. The act is not destruction of value. It is refusal of billing. Meal closed without stepping out tastes complete because the meal was complete. Drive without counting stops runs calm because the road was calm. Your walk runs whole now because the walk was whole.

### WONDERFUL! I'M FREE!

Congratulations. You have done the thing you feared you could not do and you have done it without pain because there was never anything to lose.

Feel what happens in the chest in the minutes after the vow. The shoulders drop. Breath runs a little deeper at the kerb. Air smells of nothing but cold and leaves. The dog pulls on and you follow with empty hands and the hour does not thin. This is not a mood I talk you into. This is what remains when billing stops. Patio owned again in morning light holds no gap. Stairs climbed without cough prove the body never needed paper to climb. You do not need to rehearse these hours. You only need to inhabit them and let them testify.

I give you now the sentence that will guard every future thought. Thoughts of smoking will still cross the mind from time to time, at first often and then rarely, because old pairings echo. An echo is not a command. It is a dying noise. When it comes, meet it at once with joy and not with argument.

Think at once, out loud if you can, inside if you cannot: WONDERFUL! I'M FREE!

Mean it with full joy. The thought that arrived as temptation leaves as reminder. Paper crosses the mind and freedom answers before belief can stir. That is the whole guard. No second sentence is needed.

Use it for every form the echo takes. When coffee steams and the old pairing stirs, answer with it. When a drink is poured and the midnight corner hums in memory, answer with it. When strain spikes and the old voice says a cigarette takes care of it, answer with it and watch strain stay where it belongs, in the work to be done, while your hands stay empty. When an offered pack passes hand to hand with a smile, smile and shake the head and think it with pity for those still inside, never with envy. Their lighter flares and your chest stays quiet. You pity the schedule that still owns them. You rejoice that no schedule owns you.

You hear the question underneath the joy, practical and plain: what about the restless feeling. Will it come. How long will it last.

Hear it and hold the plain fact without dread. If a faint empty edgy restlessness comes and goes in the first days, it is the tiny physical craving unfed. It typically rises sharpest in the first few days and usually eases within weeks. It begins within hours for many and then thins. It is noise fading, not need calling. It never proves paper was your calm or your friend. It proves feeding had kept a small hollow reopening on schedule and the hollow now closes for good. Meet it as proof the trap is dying. Welcome each wave as one wave fewer. People live through it while working and sleeping and laughing because it is small beside a day. You will live through it the same way.

You hear the second question close behind it, softer and more dangerous because it wears the voice of reason: what about just one, months from now, when I am sure I am free. Surely one then will not hurt.

Hear it and answer now while joy is loud, so the thought arrives later already answered. One never arrives as proof of freedom. One arrives as reopening. I know men and women who walked clear for five months and believed control had returned and lit one to test it. The test taught only that the hollow learns fast and the brainwashing voice learns faster. The dose rose and faded within the hour and the stir returned and the old credit returned with it. Next week the corner called. Next month the morning called. People who let the offered pack pass kept no such chain. They held the same glass and laughed at the same joke and stayed to the end without opening any hollow.

If a slip ever happens, do not read it as doom and do not read it as permission. A slip is a warning that belief was let back in for a moment, not proof you are chained again. Do not tell yourself you have blown it and might as well feed on. Do not tell yourself you got away with it and can plan another. Put the cigarette out at once. Think WONDERFUL! I'M FREE! again and mean it again. Ask what story you briefly believed and close it. The trap retakes only minds that reopen the question. Minds that close it stay free.

Look once more at the walk you just finished because it holds the whole book in one hour. Ordinary pavement. Ordinary brand. Ordinary dog pulling ahead. Attention paid straight. Ugliness seen. Vow spoken. Pack left. No strain raised its head because strain was never needed once seeing was whole. That is how you stop. Not by force. Not by counting days. Not by stepping around old corners. By seeing and vowing and walking on.

Congratulations again. You crossed on joy. You stand with empty hands and full hours. What comes next is mornings and shops and ordinary days lived in that freedom, and I will walk those days with you next.

SUMMARY
- If you feel eager, close on the next ordinary cigarette watched with full attention, if you still feel unwilling, read the book again rather than forcing the moment.
- The last cigarette is one ordinary brand on an ordinary walk, seen straight for smell and harshness and counting, a closing rather than extra smoking.
- The solemn vow taken at the end makes you free from that moment, with no kept exception, and the remaining pack left behind.
- Whenever smoking crosses the mind, answer at once with joy that no schedule owns you, and meet any faint restless wave as dying noise that typically peaks early and eases.
- One offered later never proves freedom but reopens hunger, and any slip is only a warning to close the belief again, never doom and never permission.
