Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

IN THIS CHAPTER
A wet morning market. Warm bread torn with fingers. A plain favourite meal eaten hungry. Peach juice on a small chin. An evening table, laughter, and a clean plate.

*Hunger met with real food is joy itself — BAD SUGAR never made it sweeter.*

Welcome Back, Fuel-Killed

Welcome back, investigator. You walk in different than you arrived.

You came in counting good days and blaming yourself for bad ones. You leave the last room forgiven. You know the afternoon buzz was debt dressed as wages. The lift never lent. It took. You saw the pinch and the sigh for what they were.

I honour that seeing, because it clears the chair beside you for what we do now. We do not fight here. We eat.

You tell me, quietly, in the voice you use when no one lectures you, that you fear what comes next. If the sweet lift was a fraud, what is left on the plate? Will eating turn grey? Will joy thin to chewing fuel? Will love leave the table when dessert leaves?

I felt that fear myself. I believed dessert was love and hunger was trouble. I believed my appetite needed sweet handling or it would howl all day.

The fact is the opposite. Hunger is a friend. Satisfaction is a signal you can trust with your eyes closed. Real food eaten hungry is favourite enough to make you smile without thinking.

Sit with me and eat one ordinary meal the way we were built to eat, and you will feel it in your own mouth.

The Grey Fear

You say, "But I love food because of sweet things. Take those away and what is left to love?"

I print your words warmly, because we all spoke them. "Treat when you feel you deserve one." "Celebrating with chocolate." "Without something sweet, dinner is dull."

Look at what those sentences confess. They confess that love now lives at the end of the meal, in a small bright parcel, while the meal itself has become labour to get through. The soup is eaten quickly. The bread is counted. The vegetables are endured. All attention waits for the parcel. Is that love of eating? Or is it love starved and narrowed to one bite?

We learned to live like that, all of us trapped in the sweet loop. We grazed all day on doses that shouted louder than hunger, then sat to dinner already coated and foggy, then woke hunger with a sweet hit and called the waking appetite. We forgot what clean hunger feels like. We forgot what stopping at satisfied feels like. No wonder we fear the table will grey. We have not met the table clearly for years.

Ask yourself, with your own evenings open before you:

When you ate truly hungry after a long walk, did plain bread ever taste dull?

When you laughed through a whole meal with people you love, was the brightest minute the wrapper minute?

When you stopped at satisfied and pushed the plate away with a sigh, did you need a rule to tell you to stop?

There is only one honest answer, and your body already knows it. Hunger makes simple things marvellous. Company makes them glow. Satisfaction closes them cleanly. The dose only shouted over that music and then claimed it wrote the song.

Hunger Is Not The Enemy

We were taught to fear hunger. A hollow opens under the ribs and attention runs straight to panic. Eat something RIGHT NOW. Silence it. Cover it. Never let it grow.

I tell you as one escaped: hunger is not a fire to smother. It is an invitation.

Real hunger arrives slowly, like light rising. It does not twitch. It does not shake. It does not point one finger at one bright packet. It opens. It says, food will be good now. It sharpens smell. It makes the mouth water at bread. It makes the stomach ready and calm.

You remember this from childhood, even if you buried it. The little one with the peach who stops mid-bite to run off and play — that is hunger and satisfaction before rules touched them.

That child did not count. He did not earn. He did not punish. He ate because hollow called, he glowed while eating, he stopped while juice still shone on his chin because play called louder. No guilt followed him to the garden. No fog followed him to bed.

We were that child. The signal still lives in you, undamaged, under years of noise. Doses can shout over it. They cannot cut it.

I lived years without hearing it. I ate by clock and by mood and by wrapper, full but never settled, empty but never clean-hungry. The first morning I felt true hunger again, low and slow and kind, I almost wept at the checkout, because I recognised an old friend walking toward me down the aisle.

You will recognise him too. Not through effort. Through hearing.

The Market Morning

Come with me on an ordinary morning, no ceremony, no new rules for living.

Air cold on the face. Stalls wet from rain. Greens piled high, dark and shining. Carrots with earth still on them. Apples blushing in crates. Tomatoes smelling of vine even in cool air. A baker's table at the corner breathing warm.

You have not eaten since night. Hollow has opened, honest and calm. You are hungry.

Tear the warm bread. The crust cracks. Steam rises. Smell fills the nose before taste touches tongue. You bite. Crumb soft, crust singing, chew deep and nutty. You close your eyes without deciding to. No icing needed. No drizzle needed. Hunger seasoned it perfectly.

Pick an apple. Cold, firm, snap loud. Juice runs. Sweetness bright and clean, wrapped in crunch and water and skin, arriving and then leaving cleanly. You eat it standing, coat collar up, feet cold on pavement, heart warm. No second apple shouts. One is glory. Hunger met, pleasure full.

Buy what you fancy for supper. Not what a chart orders. What your nose wants when hungry. Leeks. Peppers. Mushrooms with forest smell. A hunk of cheese. Fresh bread again. Fruit for after.

If you live with any condition where changing what you eat carries risk, talk to your clinician and follow their advice first — I speak to the belief, not to your prescription.

Carry the bag home swinging. Notice you are already happy, and no dose has touched your mouth.

That happiness is not virtue. It is appetite awake in the world.

The Favourite Meal Eaten Hungry

Evening now. Light low. Kitchen warm. No performance.

Cook simply. Slice vegetables thick so they keep bite. Salt honest. Oil shining. Bread on the board. Fruit washed in a bowl. Nothing weighed. Nothing counted. Nothing feared.

Sit when hungry. Truly hungry, not twitchy-hungry, not clock-hungry, not sad-hungry. The slow hollow that says now.

Eat. First bite noticed. Chew. Taste. The pepper sweet where heat meets flesh. The leek buttery where soft meets salt. The bread tearing, again that crack. The mouth alive, the nose open, the belly warming bite by bite.

Talk. Laugh. Listen. Let company season as company always did. Notice how flavour rises when attention is easy, not when sugar shouts.

Halfway through, pause. The edge has left hunger. Glow has arrived. You are no longer ravenous, not yet full. Food tastes brightest here, in the middle, where hunger still lights but satisfaction begins to gather.

Eat on a little. Then stop when satisfied. You will know. The next forkful does not call with the same music. The belly says enough, quietly, without thunder. The mouth is content. The plate can wait.

Push back. Sigh. Smile without deciding to. The evening is yours now, not the cupboard's. No coating calls for washing down. No fog gathers behind eyes. No second reach stirs the hands. You get on with enjoying your life, full and clear, free to talk, to walk, to rest, to sleep deep.

Tell me honestly: what in that hour needed BAD SUGAR?

Was the bread dull without icing? Was the apple poor without syrup? Was laughter thin without chocolate at the end?

You know. The scene gave everything. Hunger gave the seasoning. Satisfaction gave the close. Love lived in the hands that cooked and the faces that laughed, not in a parcel handed after.

Your body knows how to do this without teaching. It makes steady fuel from ordinary meals and carries you between them without drama. You do not need a sweet wire to stay alight. Eat when hungry and the light steadies by itself.

Fruit Is Not The Question

You say, cleverly, as we all did when cornered, "Fruit is sugar too, so nothing matters. If an apple is sweet, why blame a bar?"

I honour the sharpness. It sounds like logic. It is the con changing clothes.

Taste them side by side in memory and the body answers before argument.

An apple eaten hungry: crunch, juice, bright sweetness arriving inside water and fibre and chew, satisfaction rising cleanly, one enough, two a feast, ten unthinkable.

A bar eaten full: rush, coating, want sharpening with each bite, satisfaction never arriving, one never one, box empty before regret ends.

Same word, sweet, two different experiences. One belongs to hunger met. The other belongs to want re-fired. Your mouth knows the difference even when words blur it.

No one sits at midnight haunted by apples. No one hides apple skins in shame. No one says, "I feel like i cant stop," with an orchard. The loss of control lives where the trap lives, not where hunger lives.

So enjoy sweetness where hunger finds it — in fruit, in vegetables grown ripe, in a plain meal that turns sweet on the tongue when you are hungry enough to taste it. That enjoyment is not a slip. It is proof the instrument works. Real food enjoyed as favourite leaves no bill after.

The only sweetness that ever billed you was BAD SUGAR eaten as hits between hunger and beyond satisfaction. Leave those hits and eating returns to itself. Nothing is forbidden to the satisfied eater. Everything is savoured by the hungry one.

My promise to you is simple: Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Satisfaction Knows When

We lost trust in stopping. The box taught us stopping is impossible. The evening taught us one becomes all. So we armed ourselves with rules, counts, forbidden days, good weeks. "I've managed to be good this week." Good. Bad. Naughty. Clean. A whole courtroom carried to the table.

I tell you plainly: the courtroom was built by the loop, not by you.

Watch satisfaction in a free meal and you will see it needs no police.

It speaks quietly. Interest softens. Chewing slows by itself. The next bite looks pleasant but not urgent. The belly feels easy. The mind drifts to talk, to evening, to rest. That drift is the signal. Follow it and you stop, not through virtue, through completion.

We all knew this as children and we all relearn it in one meal when hunger is clean. No weighing teaches it faster. No counting guards it better. The body closes the meal the way night closes day, without vote.

BAD SUGAR broke that close by shouting louder than the quiet voice. Each hit re-fired want right where satisfaction should have settled, so full never read as full. Remove the shouting and the whisper is heard again at once.

Try it in memory on your market supper. No one told you when to put the fork down. You put it down because music eased. That ease is authority. Trust it and it strengthens each time you obey it. Hunger calls clearly. Satisfaction answers clearly. You stand between them, eating what you want, free from punishment on either side.

That is not a diet. I prescribe no menu, no good against bad, no pure against sinful. I hand you back ears to hear with. Eat hungry. Stop satisfied. Enjoy real food. The rest is living.

Love Was Never In The Wrapper

You say, last and softest, "But dessert is love. My mother gave it. I give it. Without it, how do I show tenderness?"

I honour the tenderness, friend. I felt it too. The birthday candles. The cinema hand finding yours in the dark with chocolate between. The tired child offered something sweet after a hard day. To question that feels like questioning love itself.

Look closer, with love still in the room, and see where love actually lived.

Mother's hand on your hair while you ate — was the love in the hand or in the parcel? The laughter round the birthday table while candles dripped — did the glow come from wax and icing, or from faces gathered for you? The cinema warmth, shoulder to shoulder, story unfolding, funny scene breaking tension — did sweetness make the laugh, or did the laugh make sweetness seem bright?

Isolate the variable and credit truly lands. Keep the hand, keep the faces, keep the shoulder and the laugh, lift the dose clean away. Does love thin? Or does it stand clearer, undistracted, uncoated, unhurried by want for more?

You know. We all know when we dare to replay without flinching. The meal gave. Company gave. Hunger met gave. The dose sneaked a ride on giving and then billed you after in fog and craving.

Real love at the table feeds hunger and honours satisfaction. It offers food when hollow calls. It lets stopping be easy. It never says love must shout louder than the body or punish the eater after. Offer that love to yourself and to others and eating becomes marvellous again, wonderful in its plainness, exciting in its freshness, brilliant in its simplicity.

Hunger makes food marvellous.

Read that again, because your freedom sups here. Not icing. Not drizzle. Not parcel at the end. Hunger. Plain, slow, honest hunger, met with real food you fancy, stopped at satisfied, never punished. That is favourite. That was always favourite before noise taught you otherwise.

So come to the table hungry, eater. Come rejoicing, not fearing. The grey you feared was fog from old hits, lifting now with each clean meal. Colour returns bite by bite — green biting, red juicing, brown crust cracking, faces glowing. You will not miss joy. You will pity the evenings you mistook shouting for singing.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

SUMMARY
- Hunger is a kind invitation to enjoy food, not trouble to silence with a sweet hit.
- Plain food eaten truly hungry tastes favourite without any bright parcel at the end.
- Satisfaction arrives quietly in the middle of a meal and can be trusted to close it.
- Sweetness inside fruit and ordinary meals belongs to joy and leaves no shouting after.
- Love at the table lived in hands, faces, laughter and hunger met, not in the wrapper.
- Eating without punishment returns colour, ease and steady evenings.
