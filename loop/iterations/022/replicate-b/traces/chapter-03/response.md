Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT DO FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — A Tuesday at the kitchen table with pen and receipts, the bathroom mirror after brushing, the dentist's lamp, and one benefit column that stays empty.

*Forget what it costs you and answer me honestly — name one good thing it actually does for you.*

### A DAY ON PAPER

We saw we were caught in the Sugar Trap, not weak.

Now let me put a different question on the table, and I want you to answer it like an investigator, not like a defendant.

We have spent years arguing about harm. Is it really that bad. Am I eating that much. My teeth are fine. My weight is fine. Someone thinner eats more. Someone older lived to ninety on pudding. Back and forth, harm against pleasure, guilt against excuse, and the pleasure always seems to win by a hair.

I am going to take that whole argument off the table.

I am not asking what BAD SUGAR does TO you. I am asking what it does FOR you.

Take one ordinary Tuesday. Not Christmas. Not a binge night. A normal day. Write it down if you like. Morning sweet coffee, mid-morning biscuit, fizzy drink with lunch, chocolate bar at four, dessert after dinner, a few grazing hits you barely notice. Beside each dose write what you paid — money, time queuing and unwrapping, that dull film on the teeth, the flat hour after — and then write what you gained.

I have done this audit with thousands of sweet eaters, and I have done it myself, and the second column always does the same thing.

It stays empty.

I can hear you object, because I objected exactly as you do.

“But I know it harms me a little. I still get something from it. It treats me. It fuels me. Life would be greyer without it.”

Good. Say it plainly. That is the whole belief in one breath. You believe it is a treat and a fuel. You believe that without it the day would be harder and flatter.

Let us test that belief against your own Tuesday, not against my opinion.

Tell me, on that Tuesday, which hour was genuinely better because of a dose? Was the morning better after the sweet coffee, or did you simply stop thinking about sweet coffee until the next one? Was work better after the 4pm chocolate, or did the fog lift for ten minutes and then settle thicker? Was the evening better for dessert, or was the company and the rest and the warm kitchen better, with the dessert only sneaking a ride?

And this. If those hits are fuel, why do you need another one so soon? If they are treats, why is there no glow to remember, only a wrapper to hide?

The fact is, you did Tuesday. You answered the emails. You made the jokes. You got the kids to bed. The doses did none of that. You carried the day despite them, not because of them.

We were taught to call it a genuine treat or fuel. That phrase was put in our mouths before we could chew. Treat means love. Fuel means power. Give a powder those two names and who would question it? But look at the paper. Where is the treat? Where is the fuel? A treat you do not taste after three bites is not a treat. A fuel that leaves you needing more fuel is not a fuel.

### TEETH AND CEILINGS

Let me give you two hard facts, flatly, because you deserve truth, not comfort.

First, your teeth. Every frequent dose of free sugar feeds plaque acid that eats at enamel and drives decay. That is mechanism, not moral. Brushing and fluoride and all the rest matter, of course they matter, other things decide how fast it goes — but the acid after the sweet hit is real, and dentists say plainly to limit free sugars for that reason. I speak of mouths in general here, not handing you a personal sentence.

You know that furry, slightly sour mouth after an afternoon of grazing? That is not pleasure. That is plenty being done TO you while you wait for something to be done FOR you.

Second, the amount. The public guidance says keep free sugars below a tenth of daily energy, about fifty grams, with further benefit lower still — and ordinary eating now sits far above that guidance. Feeling fine does not mean intake is low. Most of us who counted ourselves moderate found we were heavy users who had simply learned a moderate story.

I tell you this frighteningly plainly, and then I tell you in the same breath: do not change because you are frightened.

Fear never freed anyone. If you stop from fear you will feel deprived and you will stare at every birthday cake like a punishment. I do not want your fear. I want your clarity. These harms matter, but they are not my argument. My argument is simpler and far harder to answer.

Even if none of it harmed you at all, what would be left to keep it for?

What if the hand that seems to lift you in the afternoon is the same hand that pushed you down in the morning?

Ask that and stay with your Tuesday. The lift you call help arrives exactly where the last dose wore off. The comfort arrives exactly where the want gnaws loudest. Is that help, or is that a loop selling you back a little of the ease it took?

Look at the price stretched out. Do you want to pay money and enamel and flat hours for an empty column for the rest of your life? That is the hooked rest-of-life: same reach, same rustle, same regret, every single day, for something you do not even need.

Now turn it round. Picture mornings and shops and evenings with no debt to pay, no column to fill, appetite leading and satisfaction stopping you, for the rest of your life. That is the free rest-of-life: nothing to miss because there was nothing there.

Both futures last a lifetime. Only one has anything in it.

So here is the ledger closed in plain words.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

3. JUDGE ONLY BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Forget harm versus benefit and ask for the benefit.

### SUMMARY

- The harm debate was set aside and the benefit column was examined on an ordinary day.
- Teeth, money, time and mood all showed a cost, while no hour showed a gain that belonged to the dose.
- The names treat and fuel did not match what happened on paper.
- Population guidance sits far below normal intake, and frequent sweet doses drive the acid that decays teeth.
- The rescuer feeling arrives where the last dose wore off, which questions whether help was given at all.
