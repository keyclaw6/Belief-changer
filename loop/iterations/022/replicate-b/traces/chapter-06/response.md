Chapter 6
HUNGER, SATISFACTION AND REAL FOOD

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — Morning market stalls, warm bread torn open, a plain favourite meal eaten hungry and left satisfied.

*Hunger met with real food is the feast itself — BAD SUGAR never made eating joyful, it only dulled the joy that was already there.*

You have seen the afternoon lift was never fuel, only the last dose briefly quieting its own low.

### THE FEAR OF GREY EATING

I understand the worry that sits behind the fuel story now it has fallen.

You say, in your own true voice: “All right, I see the 3pm bar borrows from 4pm. But surely evenings will be grey? Surely a meal with no pudding is a meal with the love taken out? Dessert is my favourite part.”

We all said that. We called dessert love. We called the sweet finish the point of cooking, the reward for the day, the proof we had lived well. Take that away, we thought, and eating becomes cardboard. Life becomes chewing to stay alive.

Let me ask you straight, as one eater to another.

When you came home truly hungry — cold hands, empty belly, after fresh air and hard work — did plain bread ever taste dull? When you sat stuffed after an evening of picking at packets, did the last biscuit taste better than the first, or like paper you kept chewing from habit? What tells a hungry belly it has had enough — a rule on a packet, or the quiet ease that rises by itself?

There is only one honest answer, and you have lived it a thousand times.

Hunger is flavour.

### A MARKET MORNING

Let me take you to an ordinary morning, not a special occasion. No candles. No celebration. Just hunger doing its old work.

You wake hungry. Real hunger. Not the twitch for something sweet. The clean hollow that says food will be good.

You walk through market stalls. Green leaves wet from spraying, tomatoes warm from the boxes, apples piled high, fresh bread stacked with the crust crackling as it cools. You tear a corner while it is still warm. The smell fills your nose. The crumb pulls soft. The crust shatters. You chew slowly because you are hungry, and because it is good.

Later you sit to your plain favourite meal. For me it was a bowl of market vegetables in olive oil with thick slices of that bread and apples for afterwards — nothing clever, nothing counted, nothing earned. You eat. You notice. The first mouthfuls sing because the belly is empty and ready. The middle of the plate steadies you. Near the end something quiet happens. The taste is still good, but the urgency is gone. You leave food on the plate with no effort, no speech, no rule. You are satisfied, like that small child with the half-eaten peach running off to play.

No counting. No guilt. No second reach from habit. The body led and the mind followed, and eating was joy from first bite to easy stop.

We lived for years without that ease and called it normal. We grazed through sweet weeks and told ourselves we loved food. Did we? Think of your own sweet evenings. A dose to start, then another to chase the taste, then picking at wrappers you barely tasted, then regret before swallowing ended. Mouth coated. Belly full and still asking. Mind still circling what sweet thing waits next.

That is not love of eating. That is eating that cannot finish.

Real hunger finishes. Sweet grazing cannot. One lets you forget food for hours and get on with enjoying your life. The other makes food unforgettable all day. Which one sounds like joy to you?

### THE BODY KNOWS WHAT TO DO

Here is the fact that makes this simple, and I speak of ordinary healthy bodies here, not a verdict on you — if food changes carry risk for you, your clinician leads and this book waits on that.

Your body makes the glucose it needs from ordinary meals. Plain bread, vegetables, fruit, a proper dinner eaten hungry — the body takes what it needs and keeps you steady. The fact is, dropping BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing. There is no hole where fuel should be, because BAD SUGAR was never fuel.

This is why a real meal satisfies and a sweet hit cannot. A real meal answers hunger and then releases you. Hunger comes clean and leaves clean. You eat, you are glad, you stop, you forget. A sweet dose answers nothing. It spikes, drops, re-fires the asking, and leaves you fuller and emptier at once.

Do you see who deserves the credit for the pleasure of that market morning?

Not a sauce. Not a topping. Not a sweet finish. You. Your hunger, your senses, the food itself, the company around the table. BAD SUGAR never added love to that scene. It only sneaked a ride on love that was already there, then claimed the applause and left you reaching for more.

And hear me on this, because I do not forbid sweetness. Ripe apples are sweet. Berries are sweet. A peach in season is sweet beyond anything wrapped in foil. Enjoying that sweetness as part of a meal eaten hungry is part of freedom, not a threat to it. I am not asking you to live grey or plain. I am telling you colour was there before the packets, and it returns the moment the packets stop dulling it.

I have eaten both ways. I know the sofa evening with the box that empties itself and leaves the mouth tired and the heart flat. I know the hungry table with simple food that leaves a man leaning back, breathing easy, glad to be done. No contest. One is noise. The other is music.

You do not need to learn hunger. You do not need to train satisfaction. You do not need a plan or a rule or a new way to be good. The equipment is still in you, exactly as you were born with it. Hunger rises. Food tastes wonderful. Enough arrives by itself.

The rule for free eaters is simple:

Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Feel the relief in that. No forbidden rooms. No permitted hours. No making up for yesterday. You eat what you want because you are hungry, you stop because you are satisfied, and eating returns to what it always was — favourite, marvellous, wonderful in its ordinariness.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

### SUMMARY

- Grey eating was a fear, not a fact — meals without BAD SUGAR are brighter because hunger is allowed to lead.
- A plain favourite meal eaten hungry — market vegetables, fresh bread, fruit — was noticed, savoured, and left satisfied with no effort.
- Real hunger finishes by itself while sweet grazing keeps asking after a full belly.
- The body makes what it needs from ordinary meals, so living without BAD SUGAR leaves nothing missing.
- The love in dessert evenings belonged to hunger met, good food, and company, not to the sweet dose.
- Free eating is hunger trusted and satisfaction obeyed, with no punishment ever again.
