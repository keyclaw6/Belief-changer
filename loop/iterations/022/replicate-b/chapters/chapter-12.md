Chapter 12
ORDINARY DAYS

**IN THIS CHAPTER** — bright morning kitchen with kettle and fruit, busy aisle with shining packets, checkout belt beside a neighbour's trolley, plain hot dinner at a familiar table, quiet sofa with evening tea.

*Life without BAD SUGAR is not watching yourself; it is eating, shopping and resting easy as yourself.*

You took your last ordinary dose with open eyes and you are free now.

### THE MORNING YOU ALREADY KNOW

Wake as you always wake. Feet cold on the floor. Kettle loud. Light coming sideways through the window.

There is no ceremony to perform. There is no drawer to check. There is breakfast when hunger calls, eaten sitting down, tasted properly.

I woke on my first free morning half expecting a hollow. I stood in the kitchen waiting for the old tug to order me about. Nothing ordered me about. Hunger arrived cleanly, I ate toast and apples and yoghurt until satisfied, then left a crust without a second thought.

That is your body doing what it always knew how to do. Hunger rises. Food satisfies. Satisfaction tells you to stop.

Have you noticed how a small child stops mid-bite and runs off to play, with no counting and no guilt? You eat the same way now. You eat when hungry, you stop when satisfied, and you get on with enjoying your life.

We once believed mornings needed a sweet lift to start. We drank tea loaded white, we grabbed a biscuit to be kind to ourselves before work, then wondered why ten o'clock felt foggy. The fog was the price of the lift. The lift was gone and the day had not even begun.

Ask with a clear head.

When you eat plain food hungry now, do you miss the hurry? When you finish satisfied and walk out clear, would you trade that clear head for the old sticky mouth and second reach? When no voice is bargaining in the kitchen, is anything lost?

There is only one honest answer. Nothing is lost. The morning is yours again.

This is not a new rule for living. Change nothing else in your life. Same kitchen. Same walk to work. Same people at the table. You simply live there free, happy to be free.

If you live with diabetes, take medication that affects blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any medical risk around food, follow your clinician first and use this book for belief only.

### THE SHOP WITHOUT ORDERS

Go into the same shop at the same hour.

The aisle is still bright. Packets still shine. Cartoon tigers still grin. The smell from the bakery still rolls warm toward the door. The checkout still stacks chocolate at hand height where tired hands reach.

Walk through it free.

You will hear the old whisper once or twice: "Just look. Just remember how good it was. Everyone else is buying it."

Do not argue with it. Do not push it away. Let the thought come and change how it feels.

We all stood in that aisle conned for years. We read treat on the packet and believed it. We believed want was ours when want was built for profit, pack by pack, to keep us circling back. Now you see the trick from outside. You are out of the Sugar Trap, and the bright wall is only paper and sugar dust.

Look at the trolley beside you piled with family packs and two-litre bottles for the week. Do you envy it?

Can envy live where you know the evening that follows? The rustle after dinner, the second helping eaten standing, the flat sour mouth, the vow to be good tomorrow? Does that look like pleasure or like slavery with a sweet coat? Would you climb back into a cage to keep the prisoner company?

Feel what you feel. Relief. Quiet pity for trapped eaters, never envy, never superiority. You know what they do not yet know. You know each dose feeds the Nibbler and keeps the low alive that calls for the next dose.

Keep walking. Buy your real food with pleasure. Bread that smells of bread. Vegetables with colour still in them. Cheese, eggs, rice, fruit. Plain things that satisfy. The cashier does not matter. The display does not matter. The thought passes the way weather passes.

And when it passes, think at once with a grin:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it inside loudly. Mean it. Let the same shop that once ordered you about prove you need no orders now.

### MEALS AND EVENINGS THAT ARE YOURS

Lunch is hunger met, nothing more, nothing wonderful-er than that.

Eat when you are truly hungry. Notice heat, texture, salt, chew. Stop when the edge is gone. Leave food. Take more if hunger is still there. No goodness to count. No badness to confess.

Dinner is the same. A hot plate. Company. Talk. Seconds because you want them from hunger, not because the first sweet left you empty.

We once called dessert love. We thought the meal needed a sweet finish to be complete, that company needed a shared bar to be warm. The warmth was company. The joy was hunger satisfied among people you love.

Remember that cinema laughter with chocolate in hand, and see it clearly once. The laughter was the film and the friend beside you. The chocolate was noise on top of it. Play the same evening now without the noise and nothing dims. Smell of popcorn, dark room, funny scene, shoulders shaking — all yours, clearer, with no sticky thirst and no box to finish before the credits.

The evening sofa is yours in the same way. Tea as you like it. A blanket. A programme. Hands quiet.

A passing picture will cross — birthday cake on a table, biscuits offered at work, your old evening seat calling for its old fix. It is only a picture. You cannot stop pictures coming and you do not need to.

Do not debate it. Do not mourn it. Do not lecture yourself about it.

Catch it and turn it joyful in one move. Ah! A dead enemy knocking. BRILLIANT! I'M FREE! Feel the lift that follows. That lift is freedom recognised, not sugar. Each time you do it the picture loses colour and you gain ease.

I speak as one who loved night eating most. I feared empty evenings more than anything. I tell you flat what I found. Evenings do not empty. They fill with calm. Sleep comes quicker. Mornings come cleaner. Food tastes brighter when the tongue is not coated. You do not sit braced through temptation. You sit free, with nothing to guard but the happy knowing that BAD SUGAR does nothing for you.

### IF A CRUMB FINDS YOU

Let me tell you what to do with a stumble, because life is crumbly.

Aunt offers cake before you can speak. You taste a sauce thickened sweet before you know. A child pushes a biscuit into your hand with love.

Smile. Swallow or leave it, as ease dictates. Forget it.

Your body can cope with an accidental mouthful. Freedom does not break like glass. What matters is the thought you keep after. If you keep the belief free, a crumb stays a crumb. If you let the old voice back with it — "See, wonderful, I miss it, I have blown it, I may as well carry on" — then a crumb grows a story.

Hear that voice for what it is. It is not reason. It is the dying echo rattling.

For a few days there may still be that empty, twitchy call in the early evening. A brief flatness. A sense something is missing. Rejoice when you notice it. That feeling is the Nibbler starving, not demanding. It passes in minutes when unfed. Each clear evening starves it further. Each fed memory feeds it back. Smile at it as proof you are healing, then forget it in what you are doing.

And never preach. If friends ask why you shine, tell them plainly you escaped and they can too when they wish. Do not argue. Do not warn. Do not watch their plates. Let your ease do the talking. Preaching drags you back into defending freedom when freedom needs no defence. Live it brightly and leave doors open.

Ask once more, honestly.

If a crumb proved you still needed daily doses, would a man who trips need to crawl for life? If a rumble strip on the road means slow down and smile, would you drive into the ditch to prove you felt it? Is a lesson a sentence?

There is only one honest answer. A slip is a rumble lesson, not a fall. Learn, laugh, and drive on free.

12. NEVER REOPEN THE DECISION
A passing thought is a dead enemy, not an order.

Celebrate ordinary days. Marvellous relief in a plain breakfast. Wonderful ease in a busy shop. Excitement in evenings that end clear. This is escape, not vigilance. Hold the joyful thought whenever sweetness crosses your mind and get on with enjoying your life.

### SUMMARY

- Mornings run on hunger met and satisfaction felt, with no sweet lift needed to begin clear.
- The shop holds bright packets with no orders in them, and other trolleys bring pity, never envy.
- Meals satisfy by taste and company, and evening pictures turn joyful the moment they arrive.
- An accidental taste passes when belief stays free, and a brief evening echo is a dying whisper to rejoice at.
- Ease with friends keeps freedom bright, with no preaching and no watching of plates.
