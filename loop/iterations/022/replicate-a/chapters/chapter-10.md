Chapter 10
NO SAFE SWEET

IN THIS CHAPTER
- A kitchen calendar with Friday circled and a drawer opening on Thursday
- A high cliff with a promised short fall
- A guest tin and midnight wrapper rustle
- A seatbelt drawn tight for a jolt nobody planned

*Inside BAD SUGAR there is no careful sliver, only the trap left switched on.*

You have seen that even the brightest celebration lent all its warmth to the people, not the dose.

### THE FRIDAY RULE MAKES IT PRECIOUS

Let me sit with you in the week you know best, because this is where the negotiation lives.

Monday you are virtuous. You lay down the law with a clean voice: nothing all week, then Friday I allow it. Friday pudding, Friday bar, Friday cake with the film. Just that. Once a week is fine. A small treat. Controlled.

Tuesday you think about Friday. Wednesday you think about Friday with fondness. By Thursday the thought has a glow around it that no ordinary food carries. You open the drawer you swore you would not open and tell yourself you are only checking. You tell the shopkeeper you are only buying ahead for Friday. You tell your partner, lightly, “I’ve managed to be good this week,” and you feel the praise warm you before a crumb has touched your tongue.

Then Friday comes. You eat. You eat fast, with a focus that has nothing to do with hunger. The glow you chased for four days collapses into ten seconds of mouth noise, then the old film on the teeth, the thirst, the faint heave, the hand going back to lift the dip the last mouthful started. Saturday carries the spill. Sunday you promise Monday will be stricter.

We have all lived that week. We called it control. It was worship.

Ask yourself, plainly, from your own weeks, not from theory.

If Friday were truly a small harmless pleasure, why did it occupy Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday?

If the rule gave you freedom, why did the week narrow to waiting, and why did Friday narrow to hurry?

If one allowed evening settled the matter, why did Thursday creep forward and Saturday creep backward, until the allowance owned five days?

There is only one honest answer. The rule did not shrink BAD SUGAR. The rule gilded it.

What is forbidden on Monday becomes holy by Friday. What is waited for is magnified. A biscuit you would barely notice in a free month becomes a prize when you have starved the thought of it for days. The deprivation was never in the food. The deprivation was in the waiting you imposed. Then the dose arrives to relieve the ache the waiting built, and you thank the dose for the relief.

It is the other way around. The Friday rule creates the hunger it pretends to satisfy.

You say, “But I need a line. I need a house-rule, or I will eat it all day.” I hear the fear inside that sentence, and I honour the person who speaks it. You are not weak for wanting a fence. We all wanted one.

But look what the fence does. It keeps the belief alive that BAD SUGAR holds value worth queuing for. While the belief lives, the craving lives. While the craving lives, the week is spent circling. A line drawn inside BAD SUGAR is not a way out of the Sugar Trap. It is the Sugar Trap with a calendar attached.

You are not failing at moderation. Moderation is failing at freedom.

### JUST ONE CARRIES THE EVENING

Now the smaller negotiation, the one whispered at the tin.

“Just one. One square. One biscuit. Surely one cannot matter.”

I tell you flatly, as one who whispered it a thousand times: inside BAD SUGAR one is never one. One is the key turned in the lock.

You know the shape from your own evenings. The permitted sliver, the pause where you tell yourself you have proved control, then the tug, louder now because taste has woken memory, then the second piece to quiet the first, then the midnight wrapper rustle and the strange speed where hand moves before mind has agreed. Then regret before swallow ends.

That loss of steering is not proof you are broken. It is the signature of the trap when a live belief is re-fired. The craving, once triggered, carries the binge because the belief tells you the next piece will complete what the last piece started. It never completes. It renews.

Do you want to test this from your own life rather than my word?

When did a single permitted piece ever stay single without a debate following it?

When did that debate ever feel like calm choice, rather than bargaining with a louder voice than before?

If one were innocent, why would one need defending, counting, justifying, hiding?

You already know. One keeps the Sweet Con alive in your head, and while the Con lives it sends its orders down to feed the Nibbler. The little creature grumbles, you answer with a sliver, the grumble dips for minutes, then returns with interest because you have fed the very mouth you meant to quiet. The evening becomes a lesson you have paid to repeat.

I do not say this to frighten you, and I do not say it to brand you helpless for life. Helpless is the last thing you are. I say it so you stop blaming character for chemistry-plus-belief. Strong people binge on a sliver. Careful people lose evenings to a tin. The sliver did its job. The job was to re-open the question.

Close the question and the sliver loses its power. Leave the question ajar with “just one” and you leave the engine running.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

That verdict holds for the box as surely as for the week. If there were benefit in the dose taken often, there would be sense in the dose taken rarely. If there is no benefit in the dose taken often — and your own audit has shown none, only lift then drop, only glow then fog — then rarity cannot invent one.

### I DON’T KEEP IT IN THE HOUSE

Then the house-rule, spoken with pride.

“I don’t keep it in the house. That is how I control it.”

Dear friend, hear your own sentence. If you controlled it, you could keep it in every cupboard and walk past yawning. The fact you must banish it to control it confesses who is in charge when it is near.

We all ran that system. We cleared shelves on Monday, felt clean at noon, felt clever at six, then at nine walked to the shop in the rain for the very thing we had cleared. Or we ate around it — bread, cereal, fruit, leftovers — hunting the hit without naming it, until the hunt itself proved the want was driving. Or we were angelic at home and ravenous at work, at parties, at petrol stations, as if geography changed pharmacology.

A house without BAD SUGAR while the belief still lives in the head is a tidy house with a noisy tenant. You spend the evening guarding doors rather than living in rooms. Is that control? A free eater does not guard doors. A free eater has no urge to guard because there is nothing precious to guard against.

And beside the house-rule stands its twin, the kindest liar of all.

“I’ll quit tomorrow. After the birthday. After the holidays. After this hard week.”

If the Friday bar were a genuine pleasure, why postpone joy? If it were harmless, why would tomorrow be better than today? And if it leaves you flat and foggy and circling, why feed yourself another week of it before you allow escape?

Tomorrow is not a date. Tomorrow is a device. It lets you keep the comfort of deciding while keeping the comfort of dosing. We loved tomorrow because tomorrow never arrives with a bill. Today always does.

I have watched strong, honest people lend years to tomorrow. Not from laziness. From the Con whispering that a calmer time will make it easy. There is no calmer time inside the loop, because the loop manufactures the storm it offers to soothe. The roller coaster you say you want to get off does not slow down for birthdays. It steepens, painted gold for the occasion.

You do not need a better tomorrow. You need a clear today.

### THE CLIFF

Let me show you cut-down in its true clothes, because the mind still pictures it as sensible and grown-up.

Imagine I walk you to a cliff on a bright morning. White chalk, gulls, a long drop to hard sea. I point to the edge and say, with a reassuring smile:

“Jump off, by all means. Only fall a few metres. Be careful. Use your judgment. Just a little fall. On Fridays.”

You would stare at me. You would ask whether I had lost my mind. A cliff is a cliff. The direction is down. A short fall off a cliff is not a safe version of jumping. It is jumping with a story attached.

Yet that is exactly what we propose with BAD SUGAR when we say: cut down, wean off slowly, keep the special ones, allow the small bar, switch to the slightly less-sweet dose and call it progress.

The trap does not grade on length of fall. The belief is the cliff. While you believe a dose holds value, any deliberate dose feeds the belief and the belief rebuilds the want. A short fall still teaches the brain that falling is the route to relief.

Weaning is the slowest fall of all. A little less each day, with each smaller hit framed as a goodbye to a dear friend. Between hits the mind rehearses the next one, polishes it, loads it with farewell glow. The interval becomes longing school. I have never seen a weaner escape gently. I have seen weaners make the final bar sacred enough to mourn for a year.

And the substitute plays the same trick in softer shoes. The “lighter” sweet, the “natural” bar, the savoury starch eaten as a sweet hit — each keeps the hand-to-mouth answer to that empty, tugging feeling intact. The name on the packet changes. The question in the head stays open. While the question stays open, the Nibbler lives on scraps and the Sweet Con lives on hope.

You will hear a strong objection here, and I want to meet it head-on because it sounds like wisdom and it keeps good people trapped.

“You are extreme. Totality is rigid. A balanced adult can take a little of anything. You sound like a cult.”

I welcome the challenge. I was that adult, balancing harder every year while the balance owned me.

Totality is not rigidity. Totality is relief. “None” is a clear line the mind can rest on. “A little, sometimes, if I deserve it, if it is special, if it is Friday” is a fog where every day becomes a court case. Which day counts as special? How small is small? Who decides deserving? The Con volunteers as judge, and the Con always rules in favour of another dose.

Ask the cliff its own question. If a little fall were safe, why must you concentrate so hard to keep it little? If moderation were easy, why has every moderate period in your life needed rules, hiding places, promises, restarts? Ease does not need policing. Slavery does.

There is no safe sweet inside BAD SUGAR because safety was never about amount. It was about meaning. While the meaning says value, any amount revives the want. When the meaning says nothing, no amount is wanted.

### THE SEATBELT

Now I must give you the margin exactly, because a clear mind panics less than a confused one, and panic is the last thing you need.

Think of a seatbelt.

You wear it every journey, not because you plan to crash, but because roads jolt. If a taxi brakes hard and the strap locks across your chest, you do not declare yourself a reckless driver and drive into the next wall. You feel the jolt, you are glad of the belt, you ride on unchanged. The belt was there for the accident, not for steering by.

Your body treats an accidental crumb the same way. A trace of sugar in a sauce you did not make, a bite taken in good faith before you tasted the glaze — the body absorbs the jolt and moves on. Nothing is revived unless the mind climbs in and grabs the wheel. One unplanned trace, noticed, left behind without drama, does not rebuild a belief. If you live with diabetes or take medicine that moves blood sugar, are pregnant, or carry an eating-disorder past, talk to your clinician first and follow them.

But a seatbelt is not permission to drive erratically. You do not test it on purpose. You do not loosen it to feel the wind. You do not say, “I got away with a jolt, so I will steer into jolts.” The mind cannot afford a deliberate swerve, because a deliberate swerve is the belief talking: that a dose is worth seeking, worth risking, worth keeping alive as an option.

So the rule is simple and kind. Guard the belief, not the day with fear. The body copes with an accidental blip. The mind must not book one.

This is where pity replaces envy, even before you are fully free. Watch the moderate next week, counting, bargaining, glowing on Thursday, hurrying on Friday, repenting on Saturday, vowing on Sunday. Does that look like pleasure? Does that look like balance? That is labour to keep a trap comfortable. You are not being asked to envy that labour. You are being shown how to leave it.

And you leave it not by force, but by sight. You see that cut-down gilds, that one re-fires, that the empty house still houses the want while the belief pays rent, that tomorrow never comes, that the cliff has no safe distance, that the belt is for jolts and never for joyrides. Once seen, the negotiation looks absurdly small beside the life it stole — money dripped, hours circled, evenings hurried, mornings fogged, all for something that never once lifted you above where you started.

You were never weak for believing the brilliance the Con pasted on a dose. We were all conned by bright packets, kind voices, tired evenings, and the short lift that hides its own bill. Now the paste is peeling. What remains is plain and marvellous: real hunger, real meals, real evenings where attention stays in the room because no grumble is calling from the drawer.

Hold that plainness close. It is freedom knocking.

11. NEVER KEEP A SPECIAL SWEET
One exception keeps the whole Trap alive.

### SUMMARY
- A weekly allowance gilds the dose by making you wait for it, so waiting grows the glow.
- A single permitted piece reopens bargaining and the evening runs on rails you did not choose.
- Clearing shelves while keeping the belief leaves the want on guard duty inside a tidy house.
- Postponing to tomorrow keeps the comfort of deciding while keeping the comfort of dosing.
- Totality is rest because none needs no judging, while a little needs a court in session daily.
- An accidental trace is a jolt absorbed and left behind, never a booked swerve steered into on purpose.
