Chapter 6
THE MEAL WAS THE LOVE ALL ALONG

IN THIS CHAPTER
- A morning market of vegetables, warm bread and ripe fruit
- A plain favourite meal eaten truly hungry
- A small child with a peach, happy and done

*Hunger met by real food is the favourite — BAD SUGAR only dulled it.*

You have seen the afternoon lift was never fuel, only ten minutes of relief from a low the last dose built.

### The Grey Plate Fear

I know the worry sitting quietly behind everything we have undone so far.

It says this. “Very well, the lift was a trick. But take the sweet away and what is left? Plain food. Grey plates. A life of chewing joylessly while other people have dessert.”

“Food without something sweet is flat.” “Dessert is where the love is.” “I am a comfort eater. That evening bowl is love.”

I lived inside that fear too. We all did. We told ourselves BAD SUGAR painted the colour onto the meal. Take the paint away and the picture goes dull.

It is the other way around.

Let me ask you three plain questions, and answer from your own belly, not from the chant.

When you came home starving after a long walk in cold air, did plain bread ever taste dull?

When you sat round a loud table with people you love, laughing with your mouth full, was the glow in the spoon — or in the faces, the hunger, the hour?

When a dessert truly thrilled you, were you hungry for it an hour after a full satisfying meal — or were you jaded, stuffed, chasing a hit while your stomach begged you to stop?

There is only one honest answer. The joy was hunger met. The joy was taste awake. The joy was company and ease. BAD SUGAR was only ever sneaking a ride on that joy and taking the applause.

We confused the paint for the picture because we stopped tasting the picture.

Think how we ate in the Trap. Grazing all day. Sweet on sweet. Mouth never empty, belly never truly hungry, head never clear. We sat down to a good meal already half-blunted, then blamed the meal for being dull and praised the pudding for saving it. Of course plain food seemed grey. We never met it hungry.

Hunger is not an enemy. Hunger is the appetite that makes eating wonderful.

### A Meal Eaten Hungry

Come with me to an ordinary morning. No ritual. No plan. Just life.

A market stall damp from rain. Tomatoes that smell of the vine. Crisp greens. Warm bread with a crust that crackles. A peach that gives under the thumb and perfumes the hand. Nothing labelled. Nothing counted. Just real food.

You have waited until you are truly hungry. Not twitchy. Not shaky for something sweet. Hungry. Hollow, clear, ready. The honest hollow that says the body wants feeding.

You sit down to your plain favourite. Perhaps soup and bread. Perhaps eggs and greens. Perhaps fish and potatoes. The steam rises. You notice it. You taste the first mouthful and you actually taste it — salt, warmth, chew, juice running.

Halfway through something marvellous happens. The edge leaves the hunger. Pleasure deepens. You slow down. You smile without meaning to. You stop when you are satisfied, with food still on the plate and no urge to chase, and the stopping feels as good as the starting.

That is eating as it was meant to be. Noticed. Savoured. Finished at satisfied.

Ask yourself. Where was BAD SUGAR in that pleasure? It added nothing to the bread. It sharpened nothing in the soup. Had you poured a dose over it, you would have tasted less, not more. The Trap does not heighten taste. It floods taste until real flavour cannot get through, like shouting all day and then wondering why music sounds dull.

Like that little one who leaves the peach half-finished to run off and play.

She ate because she was hungry. She stopped because she was satisfied. No rules. No guilt. No bargaining for one more bite to fix a mood. Her body led and joy followed. Yours will lead again. That ease is still in you, buried under years of grazing and topping up, waiting to be lived.

If you live with diabetes, take anything that shifts blood sugar or appetite, are pregnant, or carry any risk around food, your clinician leads and you follow them first.

The fact is your body builds the steady glucose it needs in the blood from an ordinary meal. Bread, vegetables, fruit, a satisfying plate — plain food keeps you alight without a sweet hit. Leaving BAD SUGAR is not leaving fuel. It is leaving the flood that drowned hunger and called the drowning pleasure.

And hear this clearly, because the Con loves to twist it. Sweetness itself is not forbidden. The ripe peach bursting on the tongue. Berries cold from the punnet. The sweetness inside whole fruit and plain meals belongs to you and always did. This book draws its line round BAD SUGAR — the refined and added hits and junk snack-carbs eaten as sweet fixes. Real food enjoyed hungry is freedom, not a rule.

### The Love Was Never In The Bowl

“But my evening bowl comforts me. That is love.”

I say this warmly, because I said it myself. We called dessert love because we felt warm while we held it. Look closer at where the warmth came from.

Was it a hard day ended, feet up, house quiet at last? Was it a hand on your shoulder, a child leaning against you, a funny show, permission to rest? Was it hunger finally allowed to sit down after hours of nibbling without ever truly eating?

Take the bowl away from that scene and replay it with a satisfying meal eaten hungry. The sofa still holds you. The room still softens. The tiredness still melts. The love remains — because the love was the rest, the company, the hunger met, never the dose.

BAD SUGAR did not create comfort. It interrupted comfort and sold it back sugared.

A dose after a full meal never satisfies. It overshoots. Mouth still wanting while belly feels packed. That restless, stuffed, still-looking feeling is not love. It is the signature of a hit taken without hunger. Love does not leave you like that. Hunger met leaves you settled, warm, done.

You do not need another rule to eat this way. Eat what you want, when you are hungry, stop when you are satisfied, and never punish yourself again.

Read that again with fresh eyes. It is not a diet. It is permission to live as that child lives. Hunger calls. You eat. Satisfaction answers. You stop. No clock. No penance. No earning the plate by being good through the week. The body knows its work when the flood is gone.

You will marvel how quickly taste returns. First week, bread tastes wheaty again. Greens taste green. Fruit tastes almost too sweet, in the best way. Meals hold you for hours without the chant. Evenings grow calm because the belly is truly fed, not teased.

You are not losing the favourite. You are meeting the favourite at last.

6. TRUST HUNGER AND SATISFACTION
Eat when hungry, stop when satisfied, enjoy real food.

### SUMMARY
- Food without BAD SUGAR is not grey, the Trap dulled taste by flooding it.
- Real hunger makes a plain favourite meal deeply satisfying from first bite to natural stop.
- The body keeps its own steady fuel from ordinary meals, the sweet flood was never needed.
- The warmth of dessert evenings came from rest, company and hunger met, not from the dose.
- Stopping at satisfied feels good on its own, with no penance after eating.
