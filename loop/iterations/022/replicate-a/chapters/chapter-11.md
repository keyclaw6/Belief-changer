Chapter 11
THE LAST ORDINARY TREAT

IN THIS CHAPTER
- An ordinary table with the usual bar or biscuit and a glass of water
- A quiet vow spoken aloud with attention on the mouth
- Two side doors with familiar whispers after a bad day and after a good week
- Morning light on shops and kitchens walked through at ease

*There is no kinder tomorrow to get free on; this ordinary mouthful seen clearly makes you free now.*

You have seen there is no safe sweet left to negotiate with.

### TOMORROW IS THE TRAP

We all know the lingering voice because we have all obeyed it.

“I’ll start after the holidays. After the birthday. After this hard week. When I feel ready someday.”

It sounds sensible. It sounds kind. It sounds like wisdom that respects timing.

I tell you as one who waited through a hundred kind tomorrows: waiting is not wisdom. Waiting is the Sweet Con talking.

Look at your own life, not at my claim. You have waited before. You set the date, you kept dosing till the date, and when the date arrived you moved it. Not because you are weak. We were never weak. We were conned into believing delay would soften the crossing, while delay kept the belief fed and the little mouth alive.

Ask yourself from your own calendar, honestly.

If tomorrow were truly safer, why has every waited tomorrow left you deeper in the circling, not clearer?

If readiness came from waiting, why do you feel less ready after another week of lift then drop, not more?

If delay held any value, what has it ever added except more doses you did not even enjoy?

There is only one honest answer. Tomorrow does not prepare freedom. Tomorrow protects dosing. While you wait for the perfect calm week, the loop manufactures the storm that makes calm never come. The roller coaster you say you want to get off does not slow down for birthdays. It paints its cars gold for the occasion and pulls you round again.

Delay is the Trap still steering.

You say, “But I am almost convinced. I only want certainty before I vow.” Dear friend, I honour the hunger for certainty. Of course you want to be sure. That is why you are still reading.

Certainty does not arrive before the crossing. Certainty arrives from the crossing. You do not wait till you feel free to declare freedom. You declare freedom from sight, and the feeling floods in behind it. You have watched the fuel claim collapse into spike then drop. You have watched the celebration lend all its warmth to people and laughter. You have watched the Friday rule gild its idol and the house-rule confess who is in charge. What remains to wait for? One more film on the teeth? One more fogged afternoon? One more midnight rustle?

You are not hovering at an edge because something is missing. You are hovering because the Con whispers that hovering is safer than stepping. See that whisper for what it is and stepping becomes marvellous.

### TAKE IT WITH OPEN EYES

So we do not part with drama. We part with attention.

Take your last ordinary treat as you always take it. Not a laboratory dose. Not a punished mouthful. Not hidden, not hurried, not blessed as a farewell friend. The usual bar, the usual biscuit, the usual bowl, at your usual time, in your usual chair, with a glass of water beside you.

And this time pay attention all the way through.

Notice the bright promise on the packet and the dull reality on the tongue. Notice the first second of mouth noise, then the rush to chew and swallow because staying with the taste is faintly sickening. Notice the film, the thirst, the cloy, the faint heave under the sweetness, the hand already stirring to lift the dip the last mouthful started. Notice how little joy is in the room once you remove the glow the wait pasted on it.

That ugliness is not a trick I planted. It is what BAD SUGAR always was when you stopped loaning it magic. We all loaned it magic for years. Now you are collecting the loan.

I took my last ordinary bar on just such an afternoon, certain and a little tearful, and tasted what I had defended for a decade: sugar noise, then hollowness calling for more. I did not need to force disgust. I only needed to stop looking away. You will find the same, because truth needs no forcing once attention stays in the room.

Do you feel ready? You should be champing to cross. That low churn you feel is not wisdom warning you back. That churn is only the tug-of-war of craving and fear loosening its last rope. Both ends belong to the Sugar Trap. Let them fall.

If you still feel flat dread rather than bright impatience, do not summon force. Read this book once more from the start, slowly, and let sight finish its work. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

There is nothing here to fear in the body. The physical echo is small. There may be a few days of flatness, that empty, tugging call, then it fades because nothing is feeding it. It is a dying grumble, not a rising storm, and it asks for no courage, only clear sight. Knowing it is days-long and slight takes the dread out of tonight and leaves room for joy.

Ask the night its own plain questions.

Did any past dose ever lift you above the clean normal of a child eating from hunger and running off satisfied?

Does the mouth in front of you now promise anything except another dip to relieve?

If freedom were painful, why does the thought of never bargaining again feel like light coming under a door?

It feels like light because it is light. Escape, not loss.

### VOW AND BE FREE NOW

Now close it.

Say it aloud, solemnly, as a vow to yourself, with the last taste still honest on your tongue:

I see it now. It does plenty to me and nothing for me. I am not losing a friend. I am leaving a con. I will never feed it again, and I am free.

Then rejoice at once. Do not wait for proof. Do not serve a sentence to earn the title. Freedom is conferred the moment belief changes, and belief has changed. You walked out of this easily, immediately and permanently because there was never anything real to lose.

Remember, you have absolutely nothing to lose and everything to gain by closing it tonight and walking into morning with clear eyes.

Push the wrapper away. Drink the water. Smile wide. Feel the weight off your shoulders spread through your chest. This is not the start of a hard week. This is the end of a long slavery. The evenings are yours again. The mornings are yours again. Food is yours again. Get on with enjoying your life with the ease that was always your birthright.

I want to place in your hands the thought you will keep, because the mind cannot hold a blank and you must never leave the old script in charge.

Whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind — a smell from a bakery, a packet bright on a shelf, a voice saying you deserve one, a low hour whispering for a hit — you cannot fight the thought away by clenching. You change what the thought means. Let it be a signal to celebrate the escape, not to mourn a loss. Let it remind you that a tiny dying grumble is starving because you stopped feeding it, and that you are marvellous without it.

Think, with full heart, in your own voice, as your instant reply:

BRILLIANT! I'M FREE!

Say it now, aloud, and feel how true it rings. Say it when you pass shops tomorrow. Say it when laughter is loud and dessert goes round and you feel pity, not envy, for those still hurrying and repenting. Each time sweet memory touches you, that joyful shout turns a dead echo into fresh relief. Happy to be free becomes your habit, and habit born of truth needs no guarding.

Before I congratulate you, let me warn you of the two side doors, so they arrive pre-labelled and powerless.

First, the bad-day rescue. “This day was cruel. One dose will soothe it.” Dear friend, you know from your own afternoons that the dose never soothed the day. It borrowed relief from tomorrow and charged interest by evening. A bad day needs rest, food, company, sleep — real comforts that leave you stronger. The Con offers a ditch dressed as a blanket.

Second, the fond whisper. “Just one cannot hurt. You have proved control.” One deliberate dose inside BAD SUGAR is never a test of control. It is the belief climbing back in and ordering fresh hunger. You do not need to test a cage you have left, like a lion pacing past an open cage from habit alone. Walk on into the open and the pacing stops.

If ever a trace slips in unplanned — a glaze you did not order, a sauce made sweet without your knowing — smile and walk on. The body absorbs the jolt and forgets it. Only a booked return, a deliberate swerve steered for old magic, could rebuild the Con, and you will never book one because you see there is nothing to book.

You are free. Let the relief flood you now, warm and complete. Think how proud you will feel tomorrow morning, waking with no bargaining, no fog, no drawer calling. Think of shops walked through at ease, meals eaten hungry and stopped at satisfied, evenings where attention stays with people you love.

12. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY TREAT AND VOW FREEDOM
Pay attention to the ugliness, then rejoice you are free.

### SUMMARY
- Waiting never made escape softer; waiting kept dosing alive while promising a kinder date.
- A last ordinary mouthful taken with full attention tastes of cloy and hollowness, not of love or fuel.
- Freedom starts at the vow, not after days served; the moment belief clears, the eater is free.
- Any physical echo is slight and days-long, a dying grumble to greet with joy, never a storm to dread.
- A joyful reply to every sweet thought keeps relief alive and leaves no room for mourning.
- Two familiar whispers arrive pre-answered: a bad day is never soothed by a dose, and one deliberate taste only reopens bargaining.
