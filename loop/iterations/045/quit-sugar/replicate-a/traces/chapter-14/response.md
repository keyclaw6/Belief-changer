Chapter 14
EAT YOUR LAST AND WALK FREE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the Friday table set as always, the sweet tasted to its ugly end, and the vow that makes you free as of now.

*You leave nothing you love behind; you finish something that never did anything for you.*

You have argued it all the way through with a clear head.

You see the afternoon lift never rose above normal. You see comfort never lived in the wrapper. You see the loop for what it is. You hold no exception standing. No smaller number. No special date. No tomorrow. No taper. No kinder name. No confusion about fruit. No special case about you.

There is one step left, and it is not another argument.

It is a meal, a sentence spoken aloud, and a new thought to carry.

IS THERE ANYTHING LEFT TO WAIT FOR

I ask you plain because this is the last place waiting can hide.

Do you feel ready to finish. Are you champing to be done with feeding. Do you see that every line you ever fed belonged to one loop.

If the answer is yes, read on and finish tonight. You have nothing to lose and everything to gain.

If the answer is no, if something in you still pulls back and says you will miss it, do not force it and do not scold yourself. Put this chapter down and read the book again from the start. The pull you feel is not proof the argument failed. It is proof one thread still holds, and reading again loosens what forcing never loosens.

Hear the waiting voice as we have all heard it.

“I understand it all, but I will miss it.” “Let me wait until Monday when life calms.” “Let me finish the packets in the cupboard first.”

Ask what Monday ever gave that tonight withholds.

Did a calmer week ever arrive because you fed through a loud one. Did finishing the packets ever close the want, or did finishing always book buying. Did waiting ever thin the call, or did waiting only book more fed evenings and fed evenings book more vacancy in the morning.

You know the answer because you have lived the calendar. The Monday restart wrote the Thursday return. The cupboard cleared by eating wrote the cupboard filled by buying. Waiting kept the teaching employed while the mouth kept listening.

It is the other way around. Seeing thins it. Feeding feeds it. Not feeding starves it.

Would you keep paying a lender through the weekend because you plan to leave him Monday. Would you keep a thief at the table while you set a date to show him the door. Would you stay inside a trap you have mapped because the evening feels comfortable for one more sitting.

You would not. The door stands open while talk about hinges goes on.

You leave easily, immediately and permanently. That is not a boast about character. That is what happens when the belief goes and the act follows without battle.

Picture supper closed tonight with plates washed while the house stays quiet.

THE LAST ORDINARY MEAL

Eat tonight as you always eat, with your people, at the ordinary hour, at the ordinary table.

This is not a test. This is not a laboratory dose. This is not a last binge to say goodbye. Take your Friday meal the way Friday always looks in your house. Cook what you cook. Serve what you serve. Sit where you sit. Let the evening run its ordinary course.

Do one thing differently. Pay attention to the ugly end.

We have all eaten the sweet without tasting it. We unwrapped while talking. We chewed while scrolling. We reached while full. We finished past pleasure to mechanical finishing and then sat tired, foggy and heavy in a way we called normal. Tonight watch what the dose actually does.

Eat your real food hungry to satisfied and notice how food closes hunger. Notice the talk, the faces, the warmth in the room that never came from a recipe. Then if a sweet sits on the table, have your usual if it is there. Do not seek it and do not avoid it to prove something. Taste it the way a judge tastes, not the way a mourner tastes.

Does the first bite lift you above normal, or does it return you briefly toward normal from a vacancy the last dose dug. Does the second bite taste as bright as the first, or does the mouth already dull. Does the third carry pleasure, or does it carry chewing. Where does pleasure live after five minutes. Where does it live after twenty.

Ask who is speaking while you chew.

The Sugar Mite whimpers faintly when unfed. That is all it does. It has no words, no love, no memory of birthdays. The Sweet Talker translates the whimper and says treat, fuel, love, one more for the road. The translation is the trap, not the whimper.

You know the translation now because you have heard it in every room. The breakroom cake that promised fuel and billed the afternoon. The nine o clock search that promised comfort and billed the morning. The birthday mousse that promised love and billed the night with dulling. The mouth that learned loud means right while loud kept arriving. None of that was food loving you back. It was a loop feeding on feeding.

The fact is it does plenty to you and nothing for you. It taxes the afternoon. It dulls the tongue for what follows. It re-opens hunger that food had closed. It books the faint call that returns tomorrow at three. That is what you taste tonight when you taste all the way to the end.

Would love live in a glaze, or would people carry every drop while sugar rode along. Would fuel floor you by six and still count as fuel. Would a treat that must be hidden, counted, bargained over, and paid for with self-reproach still count as a treat.

It would not. You are not eating a friend. You are watching an enemy pose as one, and seeing ends the pose.

Picture yourself setting the spoon down with the meal closed and the head clear.

WHAT THE NEXT FEW DAYS FEEL LIKE

I tell you straight what comes after the last dose so no surprise can dress itself as doubt.

For a few days the mite complains a little louder because it is fed nothing. You may notice a faint, empty, slightly edgy feeling. A little flatness in the afternoon. A little noise at the old hours, three in the afternoon, nine in the evening, the old break, the old sofa, the old queue. A thought arrives pre-labelled as need.

That feeling is not hunger. Your body knows hunger from the mite. Hunger builds slowly, sits in the belly, and closes with real food. This call builds at the clock, sits in the mouth and the mind, and closes with nothing because it was never food calling.

That feeling is not illness and not danger. It is small in magnitude and still a grip while it lasts. Even the laboratory that mapped the loop in animals called its measured effect mild, but well-defined. Small magnitude, still a grip. That phrase defuses dread without denying what you feel. You feel something because a loop is closing, not because a need is denied.

Ask what the feeling proves.

Does a faint call prove a pleasure lost, or does it prove a parasite unfed. Does an edgy hour prove you need a hit, or does it prove the last hit dug the vacancy this hour sits in. Does noise at nine prove the evening needs sugar, or does it prove nine learned to expect feeding and now meets an evening that does not feed it.

You know because you have felt both sides. Fed evenings wrote louder tomorrows. Unfed evenings wrote quieter ones. The call thins when nothing re-fires it. Within days it peaks and passes. The mouth learns plain again. The same apple reads sweeter because the filter lifts. Tea at three steadies without spoonful rescue.

This is why you revel when the whimper comes. When the faint tug arrives, you name it. That is the Sugar Mite dying. You do not mourn it. You mark its dying as proof you stopped feeding it. The throes are not a warning to feed. They are the sound of starving ending.

Would you feed a dying whimper to keep it alive longer. Would you call a few thin days proof you must feed for the rest of your life. Would you mistake the last echo of a loop for love of the loop.

You would not. The hump is days long, not life long. It asks nothing of you but seeing.

Picture morning starting level after a closed evening, the mouth quiet where the old call used to sit.

THE ROPE FALLS

We have all lived torn.

One rope pulled toward the packet. One rope pulled toward freedom. We called that torn state character. We called it appetite. We called it being human around treats. The book gave it its true name. It is the tug-of-war, and every rope in it belongs to the Sugar Trap.

Hear how the ropes talked.

One said eat and enjoy. One said do not eat and suffer. One said eat tomorrow and start clean Monday. One said eat less and call less freedom. Each rope claimed to be you. None of them was you. All of them were the Talker translating the mite.

Ask what remains when the translation stops.

Does a free person need two ropes to stand still. Does a person who sees no benefit need a rule to refuse a dose. Does a closed question need guarding, counting, avoiding, and debating to stay closed.

It does not. The war ends not because one side wins. It ends because you see both ropes were held by the same hands. You drop them together.

For the rest of your life has carried two meanings through this book. Hooked for the rest of your life meant every day forever paying for something you do not even need. Free for the rest of your life means every day forever with no payment, no bargain, no stones turned over at night. Same horizon. Opposite emotion. Only celebration holds the second meaning in place.

Would you keep one rope in your hands to prove the war was real. Would you keep doubting the decision to keep yourself honest. Would you keep the question open to stay safe.

You would not. Doubt is the only danger left, and doubt belongs to the trap. A closed mind on this one point is not narrowness. It is freedom with its door shut from the inside.

Picture the market walked with no list of fixes while talk runs plain.

THE VOW AND THE NEW THOUGHT

Now cross.

You have eaten your last ordinary meal with eyes open. You have seen the ugly end while the plates sit washed. You stand with nothing left to haggle and nothing left to wait for. What remains is a sentence spoken and a thought kept.

Speak the vow tonight, on a full stomach of real food, joyful, never nostalgic. Say it aloud where you sit, plainly, the way you would say anything true.

“I am free of BAD SUGAR. I finish this and I do not feed it again.”

Do not say it the way mourners speak at burials. You did not bury anything that loved you. Say it the way prisoners speak when the door opens. Say it with relief that the mapping is done and the walking starts.

The moment you speak it, you are free as of now. Not after three days. Not after three weeks. Not when the whimper thins. Now. Freedom is conferred at the vow, not earned by serving time. The mite starves after. The Talker dies at the sentence.

I congratulate you the second you speak it. Well done. You have done the only hard part, which was seeing. The rest is living.

Keep one thought for whenever BAD SUGAR crosses your mind again, because it will cross your mind. A packet will flash in an aisle. A smell will rise from a bakery. A birthday will set its candles. Someone will push a plate toward you with love in their eyes and sugar on it. The pink thought cannot be suppressed by forbidding it. It is reframed by answering it with joy.

Think this, every time, at once, with a smile that knows:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

That is not a slogan to chant. That is the compression of everything you now know arriving as one lived sentence. You thought a treat lived there. You saw it never did. You thought leaving meant loss. You saw leaving meant escape. When the old sentence arrives, the new sentence answers before doubt can speak. Rejoice at a dead enemy.

Learn its use the way you learned the loop, but backward. The loop taught the mouth to hear a whimper and answer with feeding. Freedom teaches the mind to hear a memory and answer with relief. The thought that once booked a search now books a hit of relief that you no longer search. The intruder becomes the reminder.

Think of the margin the way you think of a seatbelt. The belt sits there for accidents on the road, not for driving erratically to test it. Your body can cope with an accidental blip. Your mind cannot afford to re-admit the belief. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. A slip, if it ever comes, revives nothing unless the Talker is let back in to translate it as proof. Let a stumble be a rumble that steadies the wheel, never proof you must drive back into the trap and never licence to drive wild.

Look at those still feeding with new eyes.

You will see the office queue at half past three. You will see carts loaded the way yours loaded. You will see wrappers hidden the way yours hid. Do not envy them their doses. Does that look like enjoyment to you now. Tired eyes at four. Coated mouth after. Loud evening ahead. You are not deprived of what they have. They are still inside what you left. Pity the trap in them, never the person, the way you once deserved pity while living the same evenings. Let visible living do any talking that needs doing. Do not preach and do not argue your freedom back into doubt.

You change nothing else in your life. You inhabit every old context confidently. Same table. Same Fridays. Same birthdays. Same kettle at three. The contexts never needed sugar to hold. Sugar only sneaked a ride on them. Now the ride ends and the contexts stay, clearer because the filter lifts.

7. WHEN YOU THINK OF BAD SUGAR, THINK FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!
Rejoice at a dead enemy.

8. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY MEAL, MAKE THE SOLEMN VOW, AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE AS OF NOW.
Cross joyful, never nostalgic.

Speak them tonight and keep the first for life. The second you speak once and it holds.

SUMMARY

- You now see the last ordinary meal as a joyful threshold rather than a loss, because attention on the ugly end showed the dose doing plenty to you and nothing for you.
- You now know the faint call of the next few days as a small starving whimper that peaks and passes, because unfed quiet thins the want while feeding always re-fired it.
- You now hold yourself free as of the spoken vow rather than after waiting, because the tug-of-war belonged wholly to the trap and dropping its ropes ends it.
