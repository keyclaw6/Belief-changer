## 1. Book core

Target behavior: compulsive consumption of refined and added sugar and junk carbs — the craving-snacking loop and its grip, not nutrition pedantry.

Reader (planner-facing state): an adult who grazes daily and binges some evenings, hides wrappers, runs 3pm top-ups to get through, pays for treats with self-reproach, has failed diets, moderation rules and willpower, and suspects the whole approach is wrong. General adult edition, one reader.

Load-bearing false belief: bad sugar is a genuine treat and lift that makes life sweeter, and life without it would be deprivation.

Through-line: it does plenty TO you and nothing FOR you — see that, and escape is immediate.

Format: full-length Carr book, 15 chapters, last ordinary meal as ritual, one ordinary-life chapter, short photographable recap. Budgets sum to 60,000 words.

Fork decisions (style-guide Part A §4, Carr defaults):
- Fork 1, inner state: full Carr personification. The trivial physical loop is THE SUGAR MITE; the belief-system that feeds it is THE SWEET TALKER. Both frozen on the mantra sheet. The mite whimpers; the Talker translates the whimper as treat, fuel, love. Starve the mite by refusing BAD SUGAR; kill the Talker by argument. Never a mighty enemy demanding willpower — a starving parasite already dying.
- Fork 2, outcome: total cessation of BAD SUGAR, commanded with cheerful certainty. Moderation foreclosed with the pincer and the cliff-jump. No autonomy-to-moderate override. Conditional-bonus neighbours (alcohol, dairy) are not required to stop.
- Fork 3, science weight: hard facts at full Carr force, flat and frightening where true, then relief in the same chapter — change from escape-joy, never from fear. Facts arrive as settled fact. Raw citations quarantined to back matter. The scare stays in the argument, never in an appendix.
- Fork 4, villain: two villains, both hit hard — the engineered food industry that built the trap, and THE WILLPOWER METHOD that kept the reader in it. Warm to the person, vicious to both.
- Fork 5, void: change nothing else in your life. The body returns to its natural baseline on its own; the reader inhabits every old context confidently. No replacement system, no transmutation programme. Root-plus-direction is not taken.

Redefinition and margin-for-error doctrine: the category eating cannot be quit, so the target is redrawn early and carried by a CAPS name.
- Boxed definition in CH-01: BAD SUGAR means added and free sugars plus the junk carbs that behave like them in the loop — sweets, pastries, biscuits, ice cream, chocolate confectionery, sugary drinks including juices and sweetened mixes, sweetened yoghurts and cereals, white-flour snacks that vanish without satiety. When this book says sugar, take it to mean BAD SUGAR. Whole fruit, vegetables, plain unrefined staples and the glucose your own liver makes are not BAD SUGAR; they belong to the positive authority.
- Full total-abstinence trap logic runs inside the line: there is no healthy level of BAD SUGAR other than zero.
- Margin for error: your body can cope with an occasional accidental blip, but your mind cannot afford to re-admit the belief. A slip revives nothing unless the Talker is let back in. Guard the belief, not the behaviour. Seatbelt logic: there for accidents, not for driving erratically.

Clinical and eating-disorder safety perimeter: this book covers the everyday sugar loop for a general adult. It gives no medical nutrition therapy, no diabetes management advice, no eating-disorder treatment, no weight-loss-program mechanics, no prescription. Readers with diabetes, pregnancy, medication, an eating-disorder history, or purging/compensatory behaviour follow their clinician first. Crisis pointers live in front matter. All clinical limits live in the single plan-wide advisory AD-A and in ledger safety limits, never inside instruction wording.

Strongest pro-behaviour scene: the family birthday table — warm light, the rich chocolate mousse or holiday cake everyone eats, you ordering mousse-all-of-it rather than sit out, the joke that skipping means you are no fun. Sugar claims here to be love, belonging and celebration at once. Met head-on in CH-10; every drop of pleasure reassigned to company, occasion and real food.

Destination state: whenever the reader thinks of BAD SUGAR they feel relief and freedom that they no longer feed it — happy to be free, free from day one, never counting days.

Saved ending reframe (appears only in the final movement): you did not bury a friend; you buried an enemy that posed as one — grieve nothing.
