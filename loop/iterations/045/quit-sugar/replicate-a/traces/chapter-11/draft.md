THE PAPER BAG AT THE BULK BINS

I start where comedy turns, because you know this bag from inside.

A man I knew lived his afternoons by rote at the bulk bins. Clear tubs stood in a row with tongs on chains and scoops resting in sugar dust. He took a thin paper bag and worked down the row with practised hands while he shovelled gummy ribbons and chocolate lentils and yoghurt raisins and sour belts that puckered the mouth before they sweetened it. He counted by handfuls without thinking, the way a till counts without thinking, and he told himself two handfuls belonged to tonight and one belonged to the desk drawer and one belonged to thrift because the price breaks at weight.

He folded the top with care as though he packed something fragile, and he staged it like contraband under a folded jacket in the cart. At the till the cashier lifted the bag and weighed it and smiled without unkindness while she named the contents aloud as sweets for the whole queue to hear. The queue heard and he laughed a beat too loud and everyone laughed. Comedy covered the heat in his neck because laughter felt easier than feeling what he felt.

Would hunger buy by rote count from tubs. Would true enjoyment need staging under a jacket. Would a free choice need comedy at the till to feel bearable.

They would not. Hunger asks for a meal and then closes. This asked for doses and then hid. Comedy asked for witnesses to laugh so the buyer would not have to feel what he carried out while the bag crackled against his leg.

That night the bag sat on the passenger seat like company. He ate while he drove with one hand. He ate while he stood in the kitchen with the fridge humming. He ate past the point where the mouth reported anything beyond sweet on sweet and a jaw aching from chew. The last handful went in mechanical and joyless while the mind ran the old script about tomorrow turning cleaner. The bag lay empty and loud on the counter. The mouth stayed coated. The head stayed dulled. The midnight kitchen called louder for something appetising that never arrived.

Ask who enjoyed that bag.

Did the tongue enjoy the last handful as it enjoyed the first. Did the stomach enjoy weight laid on a closed evening. Did the heart enjoy company, when the bag kept no company and told no joke on the drive home.

You know it did not. What the evening enjoyed was the idea of pay after a long day. What the bag delivered was the loop re firing wanting while liking thinned to nothing. That explains why comedy turns here. Laugh warmly at the man with the crackling bag, for we were that man and woman in many aisles, and then see the turn. The laugh thins when the bag lies empty and the mouth knows it never tasted company at all.

He told this story months later without heat, the way you tell a story about a locked door you no longer rattle. He spoke not as a hero but as an ordinary shopper who sees the bins now and walks past them the way you walk past a wrong turning you once took daily. No drama waits at the till. No negotiation waits at the tubs. The tongs hang and the chains clink while he hears them and keeps walking because the belief that the bag paid him has gone.

THE YEAR WITH STRANGE DREAMS

A woman kept a journal for a full year after she stopped feeding the loop. She wrote not as a teacher but as a witness to her own months, dated and plain, with numbers where numbers belonged and senses where senses belonged.

During the first week she dreamed of dessert tables. Strange vivid dreams slid cakes past on trays while she reached and woke with her hand in air. She wrote the dreams down while she laughed at herself and felt a little shaken. Afternoons felt flat. Evenings felt loud with old timing. Nine o clock arrived with its old pull toward the kitchen. Three o clock arrived with its old vacancy. She ate real food hungry to satisfied and went to bed with the kitchen closed and wrote that closing felt odd, like a room gone quiet after a loud tenant leaves.

Would a true need arrive exactly at nine and three. Would hunger dream of trays while the belly holds supper. Would the body ask for noise after it asked for food and received it.

They would not. Timing asked. Habit asked. The small physical whimper asked and the old translation called the whimper hunger and love and fuel. She learned within days to name the whimper as the Sugar Mite starving and to hear the translation as Sweet Talker script rather than her own reasoning. Naming did not feed it. Naming let it pass while she washed plates and kept the evening closed.

By the second month the dreams thinned. By the third month food noise thinned with them. She wrote of mornings starting level without the old acid night behind them. She wrote of work starting without spoonful rescue and the twenty minute lie down that never rescued. She wrote of a birthday where she sang full and held coffee and talked to the end of talk and walked home with a clear head while others loosened into the old dulling. She pitied them without preaching because she knew visible ease teaches better than talk at a table.

In month six she wrote a line I keep for you. She reported energy steadier and mind less occupied, and she claimed neither height nor transformation but only steadiness and less occupation. The very steadiness proved the old lift stole the afternoon it claimed to fuel. The lift never lifted above the baseline of a person who never fed. Each dose only returned her toward that baseline briefly and then booked the next low.

In month twelve she closed the journal without ceremony. She kept no count. She feared no milestone. She did not write that strength made her free. She wrote that seeing made wanting collapse and behaviour followed without battle. She still buys for a family. She still sets candles. She still laughs hand to hand at weddings. What left was only the coated mouth in photos and the dulling afterward and the vacancy calling louder at midnight. That absence counts as keeping rather than loss.

Ask what this year proves and what it does not.

Does one dated year prove your months in advance. Does one woman prove all bodies. Does steadier prove transformed.

They do not and I claim nothing of that kind. I offer one ordinary life lived clear for a year, with strange dreams early and steadier energy after, as proof that ordinary durability exists and looks undramatic from inside. Theory argues. A dated year shows. Months can stay ordinary because nothing needs feeding.

SIX WEEKS AND THE SMALL PRINT

A correspondent wrote to me after six weeks clear. She keeps no journal. She parents busy hours and shops fast and eats standing more than she admitted. Her shock concerned print rather than dreams.

She started reading labels with clear eyes after weeks unfed. She followed no homework I set. Curiosity returned as the mouth woke. Sauce with syrup listed second waited there. Juice sold as fruit waited there and behaved in her evenings exactly like soda. Sixty names for the same tuned dose lined the small print where hunger never looks.

They would not. Hiding proves tuning. Tuning proves intent to hold the mouth past satiety. She saw what benches built and felt anger move where shame lived. She wrote that the shop changed shape without changing aisles. The bright bags still shouted. The quiet foods still waited where they always waited. What changed was the filter. Through daily doses fruit read dull and the tuned bag read vivid. After weeks clear the same apple read sweet enough to close a lunch. The same water read clean. She did not write that label shock freed her from fear. Shock showed the manufacture. Relief kept her clear. The method takes care of the rest once the belief that the dose helps has gone. She eats when hungry and stops when satisfied and inhabits every old context without staging food as a test. The kitchen closes. Mornings start level. That report stays undramatic and durable at six weeks.

THE BITE IN HAWAII

Now hear the story doubters need most, because it holds a slip without collapse.

A man clear for months flew to Hawaii with family. Sun lay bright and leisure lay open and company stayed close, the very brightness where the old voice speaks softest and says this place differs. This counts as love itself. This counts as once in years. Dessert arrived riding on all of it. It looked rich and cold and shared while laughter ran around. The old script arrived pre labelled and for a minute it read as his own reasoning. It said just this once and said do not sit out. He took a bite and then took another. The old sequence ran exactly as we know it. The first spoon sounded loud. The second sounded less. The mouth stayed coated. The head stayed dulled. The evening stayed less bright around a bright table.

He wrote to confess without hiding, the way we once hid wrappers and now speak plain. He did not write that one bite proved helplessness. He wrote that one bite proved the mechanism. Wanting re fired at once. Liking thinned at once. The midnight vacancy booked again that night and called louder the next day. One bite called toward the box, not because he stays weak, but because the schedule re fires when fed. That signature belongs to the loop rather than a verdict on the man.

Would one bite enjoyed prove moderation works. Would one dulled evening prove love lived in the recipe. Would a louder call the next day prove the bite helped.

They would not. The bite proved the rescuer acts as perpetrator. Relief that seems to soothe books the next discomfort. Seeing that plain, he did what free people do. He closed the next meal hungry to satisfied with real food. He let the faint call pass while he walked with family. He opened no debate about special ones. He staged no test with a second dose to check. He guarded the belief and let behaviour follow. Within days the noise thinned again. Months stayed ordinary after.

Ask what this confession teaches about long.

He grieved nothing because nothing of value left. He buried an enemy that posed as a friend at a bright table and kept the table. Company stayed with him and occasion stayed marked and real food stayed sufficient. What left was only the old sequence, recognised at once because the filter had lifted enough to hear it.

Picture the beach walk the next morning with a clear head while the island stays bright without a dose.

Hold this for your own future thought. When sugar crosses the mind months clear, you will know what to do because belief guards you. Hear the script as script. Let the whimper starve. Pity those still feeding without envying them. Keep visible ease off your tongue. Offer no preaching on holiday. Living clear recruits better than talk.
