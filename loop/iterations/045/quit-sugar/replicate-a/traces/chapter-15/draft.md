Chapter 15
MORNINGS, SHOPS, FOOD; THEN LIFE

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kettle at three, the quiet aisle, the market trip, and the list to keep.

*You left nothing worth keeping; you carry everything worth living.*

You spoke the vow on a full stomach of real food.

The plates sat washed. The house stayed quiet. You said the sentence aloud and I congratulated you the second you said it. Well done. You did the only hard part, which was seeing. What remains is not another argument. What remains is living.

A note outside the argument: if you live with diabetes, take glucose-lowering medication, are pregnant, or live with an eating disorder, follow your clinician first and bring this book to them.

I tell you now what the first free days look like, because you have earned plain dealing. Nothing in them needs managing. Nothing in them needs timing. You eat when you are hungry. You stop when you are satisfied. You walk through the same hours and the hours hold without feeding.

Do you feel ready to live them. Of course you do — that is why you finished the book.

THE KETTLE AT THREE

We have all lived three in the afternoon as a negotiation.

The mouth turned loud at half past two. The drawer called. The tin called. The machine queue lengthened and we stood in it telling ourselves we needed something to get us through. We ate standing, tired, bored, slightly hungry, and called the bite fuel. By four the head sat coated. By six the body asked for the sofa before supper. We called that normal. The fact is food had closed hunger at lunch and the call at three never came from hunger.

Tomorrow three arrives again. It arrives at the same hour with the same kettle and the same chairs.

At three the kettle boils and you drink your tea plain while the stretch breaks the afternoon.

That is the whole picture. Keep it that plain.

Stand and stretch. Fill the kettle. Wait by the window while it sings. Pour. Sit where you sit. Take it plain. Let the pause be a pause, not a payment. No counting. No bargaining. No tin opened to ration and then found gone by Thursday. The cookies stay shut because no sentence in you asks them to open.

Hear what used to arrive here and hear it arrive thinner.

“I deserve a little treat.” “I need something to get me through.” “Feeling like a zombie by 3 pm.”

Ask which of those ever told the truth about the hour.

Did the bite ever carry you through, or did it bill the evening. Did the tin ever close the afternoon, or did it re-open hunger that lunch had closed. Did loud ever mean right, or did loud only mean fed yesterday at the same hour.

You know because you watched it to its ugly end at your own table. Fed threes wrote louder fours. Unfed threes wrote quieter fours. The call thins when nothing re-fires it. The mouth learns plain again.

When the thin whimper comes at the old hour, name it for what it is. That is the Sugar Mite dying. You do not answer it with feeding. You mark it as proof you stopped feeding it. Within days it peaks and passes.

Does a thin hour prove something is missing, or does it prove nothing is demanding.

It proves nothing is demanding. It was never love. It was only feeding.

Picture supper closing tonight with the head clear where the coated four used to sit.

THE QUIET AISLE

We have all walked the long aisle as hunters.

We knew where every favourite brand lived. We lifted bars and bags by rote count. We staged the bag like contraband and laughed with the cashier while hiding the count from ourselves. At checkout embarrassment dissolved because neighbouring carts looked the same. Everyone eats like this, we told ourselves, so this must be choosing.

You walk it now with different eyes and the same legs.

You push your cart past the bright war-zone and you do not stop where you stopped.

That is the sentence. Keep it that single.

No weighing. No debating. No standing guard over yourself. You came for real food and real food fills the cart. Greens. Meat and fish where you eat them. Eggs. Plain staples that close hunger. Fruit that tastes sweeter now the filter lifts. The packets flash and pass. A smell rises from the bakery and passes. The bright bags do their loud work on other carts and the loud work does not translate.

You will see the office queue form at half past three by the breakroom table where the stale cake once decided the afternoon. Someone stands tired with a paper plate, eating what we ate, telling the same story about fuel. You pass with your tea plain and you keep walking.

Does that look like enjoyment to you now.

Ask honestly and answer honestly. Tired eyes at four. Coated mouth after. Loud evening ahead with the fridge opening and closing. You are not deprived of what they hold. They still sit inside what you left. Pity the loop in them, never the person, the way you once deserved pity while living the same afternoons. Let visible living do any talking that needs doing. Do not preach and do not argue your freedom back into doubt.

I have stood where you stand, cart rolling past the old hunting ground, expecting a pull and finding a memory. The memory says eat. The body says nothing is needed. You follow the body because the body never lied about hunger. Hunger builds slowly, sits in the belly, and closes with real food. The old call builds at the clock, sits in the mouth and the mind, and closes with nothing.

THE MARKET AND THE FRIDGE

We have all done the evening search.

Nine o clock. Lights low. House quiet. Fridge opened without hunger and closed without answer. Cupboard opened and closed. Anger if nothing appetising sat waiting. We called it appetite. We called it needing something sweet after a hard day. We called it love of food while food sat untouched and only one class of packet would do.

Tomorrow evening the fridge opens and closes without negotiation.

You come home steady from the market with bags that close hunger and the evening runs its ordinary course.

Cook what you cook. Serve what you serve. Sit where you sit. Eat hungry to satisfied and notice how food closes hunger. Notice the talk, the faces, the warmth in the room that never came from a glaze. Then wash the plates while the house stays quiet. Open the fridge for milk for the morning and shut it. No second opening. No standing stare. No treaty about tomorrow.

The market itself teaches the same lesson in daylight.

Go when you go. Walk with no list of fixes. Lift an apple and taste it. It reads sweeter because the mouth no longer sits coated. Buy what closes hunger and leave what re-opens it. Talk runs plain. Hands stay calm. The bill holds fewer packets and more food. No one at the stall needs to know what you left. Your legs know. Your belly knows.

Would love have lived in a packet, or would people have carried every drop while packets rode along. Would fuel have floored you by six and still counted as fuel. Would a treat that had to be hidden, counted, bargained over, and paid for with self-reproach ever have counted as a treat.

It would not. You watched an enemy pose as a friend and seeing ended the pose. You buried an enemy that posed as a friend — grieve nothing.

WHAT TO THINK WHEN IT CROSSES YOUR MIND

It will cross your mind. That is not failure. That is memory.

A packet will flash in an aisle. A smell will rise. A birthday will set its candles. Someone will push a plate toward you with love in their eyes and BAD SUGAR on it and say the joke about being no fun if you sit out. The pink thought cannot be forbidden by forbidding it. It is answered by answering it with relief.

Think this, every time, at once, with a smile that knows:

FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!

That is not a chant to perform. That is the compression of everything you now know arriving as one lived sentence. You thought a lift lived there. You saw it never rose above normal. You thought comfort lived there. You saw comfort never lived in the wrapper. You thought leaving meant loss. You saw leaving meant escape. When the old sentence arrives, the new sentence answers before doubt can speak.

Learn its use backward from the way the loop taught you. The loop taught the mouth to hear a whimper and answer with feeding. Freedom teaches the mind to hear a memory and answer with relief. The thought that once booked a search now books a hit of relief that you no longer search. The intruder becomes the reminder.

Keep one rule around the thought and the rule holds everything. Never doubt the decision once it is made. Doubt is the only danger left. A closed mind on this one point is not narrowness. It is freedom with its door shut from the inside.

Hear the two sentences the old hours will try.

“Special occasions.” “Allowed to eat it again.”

Ask what occasion ever needed a dose to be special, and who ever allowed what never helped. The company makes the occasion. The food closes hunger. The dose only re-opens what food closed. You do not need a rule to refuse what you see holds nothing. A closed question needs no guarding, counting, avoiding, and debating to stay closed.

If a stumble ever comes, let it steady you without licensing another.

A slip revives nothing unless you let the old sentence back in to translate it as proof. Do not say “I've already blown today” and do not say “start again tomorrow.” There is nothing blown and nothing to start again. There is only seeing again. If you eat a dose unawares or in a tired hour, notice the dull mouth after, notice the flat hour after, and read the feedback plain. That taste is not a friend calling you home. That taste is the ugly end you already judged. Let the stumble be a rumble that steadies the wheel, never proof you must drive back and never licence to drive wild.

You change nothing else in your life. Same table. Same Fridays. Same birthdays. Same kettle at three. The contexts never needed BAD SUGAR to hold. BAD SUGAR only rode along on them. Now the ride ends and the contexts stay, clearer because the mouth sits quiet.

Look once more at those still feeding and keep your eyes kind.

We were all conned in the same aisles by the same bright bags. No one in the queue is weak. No one at the table is foolish. They live the same torn evenings we lived, pulled toward the packet and pulled toward freedom, calling that pull character. You know both pulls belonged to one loop and you hold no rope left to pull. Do not envy their doses. Pity the loop, love the person, and walk on.

THE LIST TO KEEP

Photograph this list. Keep it where you keep your keys. These are the spoken rules that freed you, with the claim that carries them. No callbacks. No footnotes. Read them as said.

1. KEEP AN OPEN MIND.
You have nothing to lose by hearing me out.

2. DO NOT TRY TO CUT DOWN OR QUIT UNTIL WE FINISH.
Let the belief change first.

3. BEGIN WITH EXCITEMENT, NOT DREAD.
There is no need to feel miserable.

4. EAT REAL FOOD WHEN YOU ARE HUNGRY AND TRUST YOUR BODY.
Your body knows hunger from the mite.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD.
The old method kept you trapped.

6. NEVER DOUBT THE DECISION ONCE IT IS MADE.
Doubt is the only danger left.

7. WHEN YOU THINK OF BAD SUGAR, THINK FANTASTIC! I'M FREE!
Rejoice at a dead enemy.

8. TAKE YOUR LAST ORDINARY MEAL, MAKE THE SOLEMN VOW, AND KNOW YOU ARE FREE AS OF NOW.
Cross joyful, never nostalgic.

Eat as much real food as you want, whenever you are hungry, and never crave sugar again.

SUMMARY

- You now live mornings, aisles, markets, and fridges as ordinary hours that hold without feeding, because attention on the ugly end showed the dose doing plenty to you and nothing for you.
- You now answer every memory of BAD SUGAR with relief that you no longer feed it, because the whimper belongs to the Sugar Mite dying and the thought belongs to joy.
- You now keep the spoken list as your guard and walk on without debate, because seeing ended the pose and no rope remains to pull.

Well done. You finished something that never did anything for you.

Get on with enjoying your life.
