Chapter 3
WHAT DOES IT ACTUALLY DO FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen table after supper, the sofa at half-time, the doorway where the talk goes on without you.

*Stop weighing what smoking costs you against what it gives you, and ask what it ever gave you at all.*

You keep two columns in your head. I know them because I kept them for years.

In one column you put the price. The money going up in smoke month after month. The cough in the morning. The stairs that leave you short of breath. The smell in the coat and the car and the curtains. The hiding in the garage and the lies about rubbish. The voice at night that says this cannot go on.

In the other column you put the reasons to keep going. It calms me down. It helps me concentrate. It marks a pause. It goes with coffee and it goes with a drink. It is my reward when I finish something. It is company when I am with smokers. It is something to do with my hands.

You look at the two columns and you tell yourself the second column is shorter but heavier. You tell yourself the pleasure is real even if the price is high. You tell yourself a sensible person weighs harm against benefit, and you have weighed, and the benefit still holds you.

I want to take those scales off the table. Not to argue the price column is heavier. Not to add another fright to your nights. I want to show you the second column is empty, and a scale with one side empty needs no weighing.

## The Scales That Lie

We love to weigh. It feels fair and grown-up. Harm on one side, benefit on the other, and let honest sense decide.

The trap loves that weighing too, because the weighing smuggles in the lie before the argument starts. The moment you ask whether the harm outweighs the benefit, you have already agreed there is a benefit to outweigh. You have handed the cigarette its credit before you examined it. Everything after that is arithmetic on a false account.

I smoked through years of that arithmetic. I told myself I knew the risks and I still chose the pleasure. I sounded honest. I sounded like a man looking facts in the face. I was doing nothing of the kind. I was looking at the price with open eyes and looking at the so-called pleasure with eyes half closed, the way we look at an old friend we do not want to judge.

Ask a different question and the whole room changes shape. Not what does it cost me compared with what it gives me. What does it give me at all. What did any single dose ever add to food or rest or friendship or work that was not already there before the lighter clicked.

That question feels rude. It feels ungrateful to something that stood beside you through every hard hour. I know the feeling. I felt disloyal asking it myself, as if I were turning on a friend who had seen me through divorces and deadlines and deaths. Hold that feeling gently and ask the question anyway. Loyalty to a con is not loyalty. It is the con working.

You say, "Come on. You cannot tell me I get nothing from it. I enjoy it. I feel it working."

I hear you, and I honour what you feel, because I felt it too. I am not asking you to deny what you feel. I am asking you to check where what you feel comes from. The warmth you remember is real as a feeling. The question is whether the cigarette put it there or only took the praise for it.

Think of the way we weighed in the last pages together. You saw the hiding by the garage door. You watched your own hand move toward the pocket before your mind had spoken. You felt the lift that lives only in the lighting and the flat stale hour that follows. You began to see the small tug under the ribs and the great system of stories that translates every tug as proof you gain something. That system has a name between us now. It lays its smoke across every good moment and says the glow gets the credit.

So bring your list into daylight and let us look at the entries one by one, not to frighten you, but to free you.

## Say It Out Loud

Do this with me now, wherever you are sitting. Say your reasons aloud, plainly, in your own words. No one listens except you.

Most smokers say some version of the same short list. It relaxes me. It helps me think. It gives me a break. It finishes a meal. It goes with a drink and a laugh. It rewards me when I get something done. It is something to share outside with the others.

Say yours. Hear how familiar they sound. They sound like your voice, and we have learned together that some sentences in our voice were installed long before we examined them.

Now take three ordinary moments and ask what the dose added to each. Not what it cost. What it added.

Take the meal. You finish supper. The plate is empty. The taste of good food still sits on the tongue. You push back the chair and you light up. Tell me honestly what the smoke does to that taste. Does it carry the flavour further. Does it clean the mouth. Or does it lay ash over the last of the meal and turn the tongue sour within minutes, so the fine edge you were enjoying is gone. You believe the cigarette completes the meal. Watch next time and notice the order. The pleasure was the food, the company, the ease of hunger gone. The dose arrived after the pleasure and claimed it, the way a man who walks into a room after the joke claims the laugh.

Take the pause. You work for an hour. Your shoulders ache. Your eyes sting. You step out or step back and you light up and you stare out of the window for five minutes. You call that the break helping you. Ask what helped. Was it the paper tube, or was it stopping work for five minutes, straightening the back, breathing, looking at distance, letting the mind idle. Any person who stops for five minutes feels better for stopping. The non-smoker who stands and stretches and looks out of the same window gets the same rest without paying per minute. You did the wise thing when you paused. The dose only rode along and took the wage.

Take the company. You stand with friends and you laugh and someone lights up and you light with them. You remember the night as warm because of the smoke curling together. Ask what warmed it. Was it the tobacco, or was it friends on an easy night, the drink in the hand, the old story told well, the relief of belonging. Put the same friends on the same night with the same laughter and take the sticks away, and tell me what is missing. Nothing is missing except the coughing and the stepping away from the table to feed the small need. The talk did the work. The dose sneaked a ride on the talk.

Take the thinking. You sit with a hard letter or a bill or a decision, and you light up and stare, and the thoughts seem to come straighter. Ask what straightened them. Was it the smoke in the lungs, or was it the stopping, the sitting still for five minutes, the looking away from the screen while the mind kept working underneath. Every mind thinks better when it pauses. The pause did the thinking. The stick only held your hand while your own mind did its quiet work, and then took the praise for the idea.

You already feel the objection rising, and I want to give it full voice because it deserves an answer.

"That is all very clever, but you pick easy moments. You do not understand my hard moments. When everything piles up, a cigarette settles me. When I finish something real, a cigarette pays me. That cannot all be nothing."

I understand those two best of all, because they held me longest. The settling and the paying. Put this chapter's question to them as well, here and now. If the settling were real, why does the trouble sit exactly where you left it when the end is stubbed out. If the paying were real, why can the same stick serve as pay for teaching a class and for driving to the shops, for finishing a novel and for finishing a paragraph. What kind of pay fits every deed without looking at it.

A pay that fits everything pays for nothing. It is the stick collecting praise from every corner of the day while doing none of the day's work. You did the work. The hour did the resting. The food did the tasting. The friends did the warming. The pattern only pinned its name on each one after.

Notice how wide that collecting runs. One smoker I knew paid himself with a dose for teaching a class, for finishing a story, for finishing a paragraph, for driving five hundred miles, for driving to the shops. The deeds had nothing in common except that each ended and each ending got a light. When the same payment fits the great and the tiny, the fitting proves nothing about the payment. It proves the hand reaches at every full stop, whatever the sentence said. The party was never in the paper tube. The party was the finished thing, and yours, and the stick stood beside it like a man photographed next to a building he never built, hoping to be mistaken for the architect.

## The Hug That Will Not Let Go

Let me give you a picture for what that pinning feels like from the inside, because pictures stay when arguments fade.

You are in rough water. You are tired and afraid. A strong pair of arms comes around you and holds you up, and you cling with gratitude, because without those arms you would go under. You love those arms. You tell everyone on shore that those arms saved you.

Then one day you notice the truth about the water. The same person holding you is standing on your shoulders. The weight that pushes you under and the arms that lift you belong to the same body. You were never being rescued from the sea. You were being rescued from the grip, in small lifts, just enough to keep you grateful, never enough to let you stand.

That is what each dose does. It wraps itself around a discomfort and eases it for minutes, and you call the easing help. You do not see that the discomfort grew where the doses taught the body to ask, dose after dose, year after year. The hand that steadies you in the hard hour is the hand that made the hour harder to meet without it.

The arms in this picture belong to the cigarette, never to any person you love. No friend did this to you. No partner, no parent, no boss built the water. A trap built it, dose by dose, with your own hand doing the fetching, while a whole weather of pictures and promises taught you to call the fetching by your name. That weather has a name between us too. It is the stories that say the tug means you need smoke to cope and to enjoy and to belong.

I do not ask you to hate yourself for clinging. Any good person clings to arms in rough water. I ask you to look at whose weight holds you down, and to let the gratitude move to its rightful place. Be grateful to your own patience that sat through the hard call. Be grateful to your own humour that warmed the night. Be grateful to your own legs that carried you up the stairs even while breathless. Those things are yours. The arms around your neck only charged you for the lift.

There is a further turn to this picture, and I want you to feel it because it changes pity into anger in the right direction. The arms never meant to save you. A rescuer who stands on your shoulders while lifting your chin is not clumsy. He is running a trade. Each small lift buys your trust for the next pushing-under. Each pushing-under makes the next small lift feel like mercy. Round and round, all day, year after year, the trade turns, and you pay at both ends: you pay with the breath and the money and the hiding, and you pay with the praise you hand over for the lifting. We were all conned by the same trade. I paid gladly for years and called the paying friendship. Seeing the trade does not make you hard. It makes you clear, and clarity here is the beginning of escape.

Once you see the hug clearly, three plain questions finish the work of this chapter. Answer them in your head with the honesty you use when no one listens.

If each dose truly adds something, why does the lift live only in the lighting and turn thin before the end is out. If the dose truly completes meals and pauses and company, why do those same meals and pauses and company please people who never smoke, fully, without missing any missing thing. If the benefit were real, why must it be paid for twenty times a day, every day, with the need returning a little stronger each time rather than ever being satisfied.

You know the answers already. The lift is brief because it is only the pausing of a need the last dose left behind. The moments please others without smoke because the smoke never owned them. The need returns because each feed guarantees the next hunger. None of that makes you foolish. It makes you conned, and conned people stop paying the moment they see the arms for what they are.

## Plenty TO You, Nothing FOR You

Now gather the threads, because the verdict of this chapter is simple and I want you to feel it as relief, not as loss.

Think of everything smoking has done TO you. It taught the hand to move before thought. It put the coat by the back door and the spray in the bathroom. It thinned the breath on the stairs. It soured the mouth after meals. It taxed every pause and every finished task. It divided you into the person who loves the lighting and the person who hates being a smoker, both living in one chest. It did all that openly, day after day, while you watched.

Now set beside it everything smoking has done FOR you. Not what it promised. Not what it seemed to do in the glow of the moment. What it added that was not already in the food, the rest, the friendship, the work of your own hands.

Look long. Look honestly. The second list will not hold still. The calm shrinks to a few minutes of quiet in the skin while the trouble waits on the desk. The reward shrinks to a pellet that fits any deed because it belongs to none. The heaven shrinks to the lighting, those first draws where the shoulders drop, and then the taste turns and the feeling thins and the mind starts counting toward the next one. Each entry, held up to daylight, turns out to be your own good moment with the credit signed over.

Be exact about that heaven, because exactness frees you. I do not deny the seconds. The scratch of the match, the first draw, the shoulders falling, the noise in the head quieting for a breath or two. Those seconds feel good, and we both know they feel good, and pretending otherwise would close your mind. But hold the whole dose in view, not only its first three seconds. Nobody sails through the day on one lighting. The stick burns down. The taste turns dry and sour. The chest tightens a fraction. The mind that felt settled starts circling back toward the pocket within the hour. Whilst we love the lighting we hate being smokers, and both truths live in one chest because the dose gives seconds and takes hours. A pleasure that must be re-bought twenty times a day, that leaves the mouth stale and the attention chained between feeds, is not a gift being weighed. It is a toll being collected.

That signing-over is the whole trick. We were trained to pin every soft part of life onto the glowing end, until the end looked like the source of softness. Take the pins out and the softness stays where it always lived. Food still tastes. Rest still rests. Friends still warm. Work still satisfies. The body still knows how to breathe and settle without being taught.

I state this as settled fact because I have watched it hold in life after life, including my own. It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

And because this chapter changes the question, it also changes the mood you bring to the rest of our walk. Too many readers arrive here braced for gloom, as if seeing clearly must mean feeling worse. I understand that dread, because I carried it myself for years. I believed that if the pleasure went, only the price would be left, and I would spend the rest of my days knowing what I missed. It means the opposite, and you can feel the opposite from this page on if you let the empty column do its work in your chest.

Every false credit you take back from the stick is a piece of your own life returned to you. The food was yours. The pause was yours. The laugh was yours. Reclaiming them is not doom. It is homecoming. You are not standing before a smaller life. You are standing before the same life with the toll booth removed, and the road beyond looks wider because you no longer pay to travel it.

Stay a little with what is returned, because returned things need handling to be felt as yours again. After supper at the kitchen table, the taste lingers when no ash is laid over it. You know that lingering already from the meals you once ate without reaching, in childhood and on holidays and in houses where you did not light up, and you ate and were satisfied and the satisfaction stayed. That staying is the natural shape of a finished meal. The dose never lengthened it. It cut it short and signed its name on the shortening. When you take the signature back, you do not gain a new pleasure to learn. You get back the old full finish you always owned, clean mouth, settled hunger, ease that does not turn sour. What is there to mourn in that return. You understand now that the finish was never missing anything, and understanding this lets you sit at the same table tonight with more of the meal, not less.

The same return waits on the sofa at half-time. The rest you took there was never in the paper tube. It was in the stopping, the sitting down, the legs up, the eyes resting from work, the mind idling while the game went on without you. You believe you will miss the companion on the sofa. You will find you kept the sofa and lost only the tax on it. The shoulders still drop. The breathing still slows. The break still breaks the evening in two and lets the second half start fresh. Nothing in that rest asks for smoke to complete it. It asks only to be owned again without the small voice counting down to the next feed. Once you see the rest as yours, the sofa feels larger, not emptier, and you meet the second half of the evening with clearer eyes.

The same return waits in the doorway where the talk goes on without you. For years you believed the doorway held the warmth, that stepping out together made the friendship closer. You know now that the talk did the warming and the laugh did the binding, and the sticks only stood beside them hoping to be mistaken for the cause. When you take that credit back, you do not lose the company. You get the company whole, without dividing yourself into the one who laughs inside and the one who must slip outside to keep laughing. You hear the whole story now. You stay for the punchline. You remember the night without the sour mouth. The friendship was never in the doorway. The doorway only borrowed it, and the borrowing is over.

This is why an empty credit column feels like freedom ahead and not like gloom. Gloom says something precious is gone. Freedom says nothing precious was ever in the column to lose. You are not poorer by one pleasure. You are richer by three returned ones, taste that lingers, rest that rests, talk that warms, each one larger for no longer being signed away. I want you to feel that richness as a present fact, not as a promise about next year. The meal tonight will still satisfy. The pause tonight will still restore. The laugh tonight will still bind. Each one proves the point afresh the moment you stop paying the signer.

So at the point where the question has moved, I lay down our third rule. Take it in the same warm and firm spirit as the first two:

3. BEGIN BY FEELING GREAT TO BE ESCAPING
There is no doom here, only freedom ahead.

Carry that feeling with you from this page on, and watch what it does to the hours after this page. The hand will still reach. It has reached without asking for years, and it will reach again tonight and tomorrow. The difference is that you now meet the reaching with a new question ready, and the question arrives faster each time. At first you ask it deliberately, almost word by word, what did that dose ever put into the meal that the meal did not already hold. Next time the question comes shorter, what did it add. Next time it comes as a glance, and then as a smile before the pocket is even touched. Each asking takes back a little more of the day, and each taking-back leaves less to miss and more to enjoy.

You will notice the dread thinning as the question quickens. Dread said seeing clearly would hurt. Escape-joy says seeing clearly returns. Where you once braced yourself to face the evening without a prop, you now catch yourself looking forward to the same evening with the prop exposed. The kitchen table after supper becomes a place to linger, not a signal to leave. The sofa at half-time becomes a rest you own outright, not a pause you rent by the dose. The doorway where the talk goes on becomes a talk you stay inside, whole and undivided. The hours do not feel longer. They feel fuller, because the small counting voice between feeds has gone quiet and your attention stays where you put it.

Hold that fullness when the old tug stirs under the ribs. I do not deny those seconds at the lighting, the shoulders falling, the noise quieting for a breath or two. You know those seconds and I honour them, and you know too that they live only at the lighting and never lasted the day. Let the tug be what it is between us now, small and already fading, and let the great smoke of stories be what it is, words that once called every tug proof of a gift. Neither one can hold you once the credit is withdrawn. You are not marching toward a loss. You are walking out of a hug that never loved you, into clean air that was yours all along.

SUMMARY
- I stopped weighing harm against benefit once I saw the weighing grants a benefit before it is proven, and I now ask what any dose ever added on its own.
- I listed my own reasons aloud and checked them against meals, pauses and company, and I now see the food, the rest and the friendship did the pleasing while the dose collected the praise.
- I pictured grateful arms that were also standing on my shoulders, and I now understand the brief easing each dose brings pauses a need its own round created.
- I feel great to be escaping, because there is no doom in seeing an empty credit column, only freedom ahead.
