Chapter 6
WHY WILLPOWER NEVER WORKED

IN THIS CHAPTER: the kitchen drawer of half-chewed gum, the calendar crossed with quit dates, the whisky poured into a brandy glass.

*You did not fail those quits. Those quits failed you.*

The classroom gave back its glow. The table gave back its taste. The ring of holes gave back its promise. The work shines with no pellet signing it. The meal settles with no lid laid over it. The bench counts one puff and the mouth smokes another, and the two never matched.

And still a weight sits on the chest that has nothing to do with pellets or milds. I know it because I carried it for years. It is the drawer in the kitchen and the dates on the calendar and the dread of one more attempt.

Open that drawer with me now, because we need to look at it with clear eyes before we go further.

The drawer sticks a little. Inside lie three half-used strips of gum, the foil pushed through in some places and untouched in others, gone soft at the edges. Beside them a box of patches, two missing, the rest still sealed, the printed leaflet folded and refolded. A plastic mouthpiece with no charge. A leaflet from the chemist with a timetable ticked in biro for four days and then abandoned. Underneath, the old lighter you kept as a spare even during the weeks you told everyone you had stopped. You know each object by the hour it failed you.

On the wall the calendar carries the same story in ink. A Monday stands circled with a star. Nine days stand crossed one by one and then a blank. A New Year carries capital letters. A birthday bears a line under it. A holiday shows an arrow. Each mark began with a clenched jaw and ended with a lit dose behind the garage and a vow that next time the jaw would clench harder. You believed each star would be the last.

You know what those marks taught you to believe. You believe you lack the will for this. You believe others possess some firmness you were denied. You believe the drawer proves you cannot be trusted without a prop, and the calendar proves the prop cannot save you either, so you stand between two verdicts and accept both. Too weak to do it bare-handed. Too weak to make even the aids work.

I understand that belief from the inside because it owned me longer than any pellet did. I stood at that drawer and read it as my biography. I counted my attempts and stopped counting when shame ran past twenty. I told myself the next attempt would need a harder jaw, a cleaner timetable, a stronger character. I did not question the approach. I questioned myself.

A smoker from our own ranks put that drawer and that calendar into one plain line:

"I had probably attempted at least twenty times before and used most of the replacements and meds on the market with no success."

Hear your own drawer inside that line: the strips, the boxes, the dates, the weeks counted and lost. That line is not unusual. It is the typical line of a person caught as we were caught. Never again read your drawer as proof that you are uniquely broken. The drawer does not prove you are weak. It proves the approach put you in a fight you could not win and then blamed you for losing.

## THE DRAWER THAT PROVES NOTHING AGAINST YOU

Keep the drawer open a minute longer, because the order of events gives the game away exactly as it did at the desk and in the classroom.

You began each attempt already convinced you loved smoking and would miss it. That conviction was the great weather of pictures and promises, smoke laid across every finish and every trouble until both looked as if they belonged to the dose. You then removed the dose while keeping the conviction intact. You kept believing the pellet paid for work. You kept believing the light sealed the meal. You kept believing the tube steadied the nerves. You removed the object and kept the belief that the object gave.

What follows from that order. A hunger runs under the day that you credit to need. Part of the mind counts the hours since the last dose. Part of the attention stands already by the door while the body still sits at the table. The food arrives and you believe it tastes flatter. The work ends and you believe the finish rings hollow. The phone goes down after hard words and you believe the nerves scream for the one thing that ever quieted them. None of those readings came from the body alone. They came from the belief laid over the body, the Smokescreen laid over the tug.

You say, and I want to give your objection its full weight because it is the strongest one an ashamed repeater can make, you say the hunger felt physical. You say the head ached. You say sleep broke. You say the hands shook at the wheel and the temper shortened with the children and no picture in a book can talk that away. You say the drawer proves the body needs the drug and all this talk of belief insults the sweat you sweated.

I hear you. I sweated it too. I paced the same kitchen at midnight and told myself the body had spoken its final word. But hold the whole attempt in view, not only its hardest three evenings. The body asked for a small quieting it had been taught to ask for. The belief told you that quieting was pleasure, calm, reward, company, taste, courage, and the loss of all six at once. The body asked for a coin. The belief told you the coin bought the house. Which of the two kept you awake, the coin or the house.

Answer three plain questions with the honesty you use when no one listens.

If want of will were the trouble, why did the firmest months of your life end with a lit dose between your fingers. If a prop solved the lack, why did the drawer fill while the smoking continued, strip after strip and box after box. If hardness proved the path, why did the hardest attempts leave you smoking more at the end than the easy weeks before they began.

You know the answers already. Determination did not fail. The path failed. The drawer filled because each aid kept the belief intact while changing the wrapper. Hardness deepened the wound because each hard week taught you the prize must be precious if it costs such pain to refuse it. The fact is plain once seen. You were not weak-willed. The approach made a small hunger feel like six large losses and then measured your character by your willingness to bleed.

The method failed. You did not.

Notice how that verdict changes the drawer without moving a single object in it. The gum stays where it is. The patches stay sealed. The calendar keeps its stars and crossings. What changes is the signature under them. Where you once read your name as the culprit, you now read the approach as the culprit. We were conned about the object and then conned about the quitting, first told the dose gave and then told the pain of refusing it measured our worth. Both pictures came from the same weather. Both lift together.

## WHY CLENCHING ALWAYS LOSES

There is a named approach behind that drawer, and I want you to see its shape whole, because while it stays unnamed you will keep blaming the hands that held it.

I call it by its plain name. The Willpower Method.

It runs on one picture. Smoking is a precious pleasure you must refuse by force. It tells you to hold out against desire, to count the hours and days since the last dose, to measure progress by pain endured, to white-knuckle through parties and pauses and mornings, to keep the dose alive in the mind as a lost friend and prove your character by mourning it without touching it. It has no finish line. Each day survived only buys the same fight tomorrow, with the prize always behind glass and the hands always tied.

Watch what that picture does to a decent person over months, because you have lived it and I have lived it and we can check it against our own days rather than against any slogan.

It divides the mind. One part orders the hands to leave the pack alone while the other part keeps a fond count of what the hands are missing. The pellet after the class grows larger in memory with each week it is refused. The seal after the meal shines brighter in memory with each evening it is denied. The approach does not weaken those pictures. It gilds them. It teaches you that each dose refused was a jewel surrendered, and then asks why you feel deprived. You can see the gilding plainly once it is pointed out.

It feeds the very hunger it claims to fight. Each counted hour keeps attention fixed on the next dose that must not come. Each counted day keeps the question open: shall I or shall I not. An open question is a fed question. The small tug under the ribs, which on its own would have faded like any unfed habit, gets fresh orders every hour to report for duty. We believed we were starving the hunger by refusing the dose. We were schooling it by attention. The Nipper drank the attention and grew fat on the watch we kept over it.

It turns a slip into a collapse. Under this picture one lit end in a weak minute proves the whole attempt was vanity. The calendar with its neat crosses becomes a chain, and one broken link scraps the chain. So the smoker who held for nine days and smoked on the tenth does not smoke one and resume clear seeing. He smokes the pack and tells himself the nine days meant nothing. I have done exactly that. I have watched good men do exactly that. The collapse did not come from the one dose. It came from the picture that said one dose erased nine days of clear seeing. A stumble on a path is a stumble. A stumble on a tightrope is death. The Willpower Method lays a tightrope where a path would do, then blames the walker for falling.

Now meet the two voices this picture breeds, because you will hear them at every party and behind every counter, and you need to know them for what they are.

One boasts. He tells you he stopped by force and it nearly killed him, and the hardness proves his strength. He counts his years like medals and polishes the pain in the telling. The other moans. He tells you he stopped by force and hungers still, years later, and the hunger proves the prize was real. Both keep the same picture intact from opposite ends. Both tell you the dose was precious and the pain was the price. Both ask you to admire the suffering rather than question the picture that ordered it.

You say the boast must mean something. You say a man does not polish pain for nothing, and a woman does not count years for nothing, and if force worked for them, force should work for you. You say my pages insult honest strugglers who earned their clear lungs the hard way.

I honour the strugglers. I was one. I counted days on the same calendar and polished the same pain in the same pubs. You know polish is not proof. But I ask you to check what their struggle proves and what it does not prove. It proves they endured. It does not prove endurance was needed. A man can dig a ditch with a spoon and boast of the blisters. The blisters are real. The spoon was still the wrong tool. Admire the man. Do not borrow the spoon.

This is also where I answer the doubt that rises in every sharp reader at this point. You wonder whether this book merely plants pictures of its own while accusing others of planting pictures. It does plant a picture, and I own it openly. The difference is that every picture here is offered for checking against your own days: the classroom, the meal, the ring of holes, the drawer. You do not take my word. You watch your own hands and your own mouth and your own evenings. That checking is the whole of it. All you have to do is follow all the instructions.

Hold that checking stance when the old voices crowd in, because they will crowd in tonight. A friend will tell you quitting nearly broke him. A column will tell you the count is against you. A packet in the drawer will promise a cleaner wage for the same hunger. Each voice asks to borrow your judgment for a minute while its approach does the work. You have lent that minute twenty times. You know what it bought. Let this argument finish its hearing before you lend the minute again.

And see yourself, at last, without the old signature. It takes a stubborn will to keep drawing smoke into lungs that cough each winter and stairs that punish each climb, to keep paying coin after coin for a quieting that fades within minutes, to keep hiding in garages and spraying the coat and lying with a smile about the smell. A weak creature could not have kept that up for years. You are not weak-willed. You are wilful, and your wilfulness kept you marching through punishment that would have stopped a dilettante in a week. That same wilfulness, turned once against the picture instead of against yourself, finishes this. The strength was never missing. It was misdirected.

## WHISKY IN A DIFFERENT GLASS

Now take the drawer objects one by one in your palm, because one of them needs its own hour.

Pick up the gum. Chew it in memory. The jaw works. The pepper bites. The nicotine enters through the cheek instead of the lung. The tug quiets for a while, then returns and asks for another piece. Pick up the patch. Feel its square under the shirt through the morning. The drug seeps through skin instead of smoke. The tug quiets for a while, then sharpens at the edges and asks for a stronger square. Picture the vapour stick on the counter at the garage, mint and mango, the cloud in the cold. The drug arrives in perfume instead of ash. The tug quiets for a while, then asks for a deeper pull.

Three wrappers. One drink.

Think of a man who loves whisky and decides the whisky owns him. He pours the same measure into a brandy glass and tells himself he has left whisky. He pours it into a coffee mug and tells himself the mornings are now safe. He pours it into a medicine bottle with a measured dropper and tells himself it is now treatment. The glass changes. The pour stays. The hand still lifts at the same hours. The mind still counts to the same lift. He has changed the crockery and kept the drink.

You laugh at that man, and rightly, because you see his trick from the outside. The drink never changed. Only the container changed. The hours stayed. The lift stayed. The belief that the lift steadies him stayed. He calls each new container a fresh start while the old thirst walks beside him into each new glass.

Now turn that clear seeing upon the drawer, because the drawer holds the same trick in three costumes, and we believed each costume in turn.

The gum is whisky in a chewing glass. The drug that once arrived in smoke now arrives through the cheek, and the cheek learns the same hours the lung once kept. After the meal comes a chew instead of a light. After the finished paragraph comes a chew instead of a step outside. During the night hours the strip waits beside the lamp instead of the pack on the table. The hand lifts at the same bells. The mind counts to the same lift. Nothing in the trade changed. The cough moves rooms for a while. The smell leaves the coat for a while. The belief that a dose steadies, rewards, and seals stays exactly where it was, fed at the same hours through a different door. We credited the new door with virtue while the old hunger walked through it untouched.

The patch is whisky in a sticking plaster glass. The drug seeps through skin while you sleep and while you work, and the mind, which once watched the clock for the next light, now watches the skin for the next square. Is it strong enough. Shall the square move up a strength. Why does the tug sharpen at four in the afternoon despite the square. The questions prove the belief survived the swap. A person who believed the dose gave nothing would not interrogate a plaster about its strength. He would not need to. The interrogation is the Smokescreen talking through a new mouth, asking the same old question: where is my prop.

The vapour stick is whisky in a perfume glass. The cloud smells of mint and mango instead of ash, and the novelty lets the mind pretend the old trade ended. But watch the hands with the honesty you brought to the classroom. The stick lives where the pack lived. It is reached for where the pack was reached for. It is drawn after meals where the tube was drawn after meals. It is puffed harder when the tug sharpens, held longer, taken deeper, exactly as the mouth once laboured over the ring of little holes. The flavour changed. The picture did not. The Nipper still gets its hourly coin through a prettier slot, and the Smokescreen still collects the credit for every pause the vapour happens to stand beside. Once seen, the perfume cannot hide the rhythm.

The fact is simple, and I paid to learn it through each glass in turn. A different door into the same hunger does not close the hunger. It teaches the hunger new hours while keeping the old belief that the hunger must be fed from outside. Each swap keeps alive the two convictions this book exists to dissolve: that the dose gives something worth keeping, and that you need a prop to stand without it. While those two convictions stand, the drawer fills and the calendar fills and the jaw clenches and loosens on schedule.

You say, and I give this objection its full weight because the drawer makes it ache, you say the gum once held you for six weeks and the patch once held your brother for a year, so the glasses must hold something real. You say I insult a decent aid that carried you further than bare hands ever did.

I honour those six weeks. I do not mock a single hour you stayed clear of smoke. But I ask you to check what carried those weeks and what ended them. During those weeks you still believed the dose gave calm and reward. You still believed parties needed a marker and trouble needed an off-switch. You held the belief and refused the smoke, exactly the divided state the Willpower Method orders, only now with a quieter tug. Then a hard evening came, or a dull Sunday, and the belief spoke louder than the gum could quiet, and the hands went back to the old glass because the belief had never left it. The aid did not fail from weakness in the aid. The attempt ended because the belief outlasted the wrapper. Keep the belief and any glass empties in time. Dissolve the belief and no glass is needed.

Ask with the same plain honesty you brought to the drawer.

If the wrapper solved it, why does the same hand that chewed through strip after strip still reach for smoke when the evening turns. If the strength lived in the plaster, why does the mind still bargain over strengths instead of forgetting strengths exist. If the perfume ended the trade, why do its users puff harder under pressure in the same rhythm the old mouth kept over the vent holes.

You know the answers already, because your evenings already gave them. The wrapper never owned the belief. The belief owned the wrapper. New crockery never sobered anyone while the pour stayed sacred.

Notice what this seeing does to the fear of the next attempt. The fear said you stand between two failures: too weak for bare hands, too weak for aids. Both prongs were forged by the same picture. Bare hands failed because they clenched around a precious prize. Aids failed because they gilded the prize in cleaner foil while keeping it precious. Remove the picture and both prongs snap at once. There is no precious prize to clench against and no precious prize to gild. There are only days to live and work to do and meals to taste, with a small unfed tug fading underneath like a bell heard from another street.

There remains one more weight, and it arrives in numbers. A friend quotes them over the fence. A column prints them in bold. A leaflet folds them into the drawer. They sound like verdicts on you. They are not. Let me show you why, because this is where the watched-room count and the street count get folded into a single slogan, and that slogan has frightened good readers into believing arithmetic decides their life.

In watched rooms where quitters are counted week by week, those handed nicotine in clean wrapping hold out past the months in larger numbers than those handed empty wrapping. That is one room, with its own doors and its own counting. Out here where no one is watched and no one counts, most attempts are made with bare hands, and few hold on any single attempt. That is a different room, with different doors and no counting at all. Each statement speaks of its own room. Neither room speaks of you.

Read those two rooms slowly, because the mix counts on haste. The first room does not say a prop owns your future, and it does not say bare hands doom it. It says that among counted heads in a watched room, clean wrapping nudged the count for a while. A nudge in a watched room is not a law over your kitchen. The second room does not say you cannot hold, and it does not say attempts are futile. It says that single attempts often end in smoke, which you already know from your own calendar without any column telling you. A low count on single attempts is not a verdict on a reader who now sees the picture that sank those attempts. Both rooms describe what happened while the belief stood. Neither room describes what happens once the belief falls.

You say numbers do not lie. You say a nudge is still an edge, and a low count is still a warning, and I ask too much when I ask you to set both aside.

I ask nothing of the kind. Keep both numbers exactly where they belong, in their own rooms, describing their own doors. What I refuse, for your sake, is the collapse that turns two rooms into one sentence over your head: that you need a prop because the count says so, or that you are doomed bare-handed because the count says so. That collapse is the Smokescreen doing arithmetic. It takes a nudge among counted heads and forges a chain for your hands. It takes a low count on blind attempts and forges a blindfold for your seeing. The fact is plain. A watched count never measured a reader who had dissolved the belief, because no count can photograph a seeing. Your drawer counted attempts made under the old picture. It cannot count the attempt made after the picture falls, because that attempt is of another kind entirely.

Hold that distinction when the voices quote figures at you tonight. Thank the figure. Return it to its room. The watched room keeps its nudge. The street keeps its count. You keep your own days, and your days are now under new management, the management of a person who knows the pellet never paid and the plaster never taught.

At this point a reader who has followed honestly sometimes pauses and asks whether the management itself is the trick. All this talk of pictures and weather, all this renaming of hunger and pleasure, is it only paint over the same old orders. Clench here instead of there. Count seeings instead of days.

I welcome the pause, because an honest check sharpens the tool. Paint would ask for your trust. This asks for your eyes. Go back to the classroom and check the order of work and pellet. Go back to the table and check the order of food and lid. Go back to the mild and feel the ring under your fingers while the mouth covers it. No trust required. Only looking. What falls under looking was never paint. What stood only while unlooked at was never solid.

And look, last of all, at the hands that held the spoon for so long. At the drawer you read your own name as culprit. Read it now as witness. No weak will stands convicted here. You are wilful through and through, wilful enough to keep paying for punishment year after year. That wilfulness was never the trouble. The orders were the trouble. Turn the same wilfulness against the orders, once, fully, and it carries you where clenching never could.

So at the point where the drawer stands open and the glasses stand empty and the numbers stand back in their rooms, here, then, is our sixth rule. Take it in the same warm, firm spirit as the five before it. You have weighed the so-called gifts and found the scales empty. You have seen the pellet sign its name to work it never did. You have felt the ring betray its own promise. You have watched the method breed the struggle it then punished you for. The only voice that can still borrow your judgment is the one that suffered under the old orders and now exhibits the suffering as proof. Wish him well and keep your own counsel. His pain was real. His spoon was wrong.

6. DISREGARD ANYONE WHO QUIT BY WILLPOWER
Their struggle was the method, not you.

Carry that disregard as warmth, not contempt. The bragger at the party endured real nights. The moaner behind the counter misses something he believes was precious. Both speak from inside the picture you have now stepped outside of. When they tell you it must hurt, hear the picture talking, not the body. When they count their days aloud, hear the tightrope talking, not the path. You owe them kindness and you owe them no obedience. Your path is not their tightrope. Your days are not their count.

Tonight the old bells will ring at their hours. You know these bells better than any timetable, after classes and paragraphs and long drives, and they ring from habit while the conviction that fed them wears thin. Greet each bell with the new questions, and the questions shorten with use. First they come long, almost word by word: did my jaw ever teach one student, did my plaster ever close one meal. Then they come short: what did it add. Then a look at the closed drawer is enough, then a smile before the pocket is touched. Each question wins back a stretch of the day. Each stretch won back leaves less static and more of yourself.

The shape of the evening alters as the questions shorten. Where you once went outside to quiet the tug, you now linger at the window and watch the lamps come on. You pour water and drink it slow. You turn the day's labour over in the mind and see the good hour land again. The evening does not feel emptier. It feels denser, because the small tallying voice between feeds has fallen silent and your notice rests on the life your hands produced.

Keep that density beside you when the tug moves under the ribs. I grant those two breaths when the match burns down, with the shoulders dropping half an inch and the buzz softening. Those breaths belonged to us both and I concede them without argument, and they lived only at the lighting and never taught an hour, never cooked a dish, never settled a quarrel. Let the tug be what it is between us now, a small tug unfed and losing strength by the day, and let the great weather of pictures be what it is, talk that once stamped every finish as proof you mark it through smoke. Neither voice can keep you once the stamp is refused. We pay the Nipper no more coin through prettier slots, and the Smokescreen wears thin where payment stops.

SUMMARY
- I now see that my twenty attempts do not prove I am weak, because the Willpower Method manufactured the struggle it then blamed me for.
- I understand that patches, gum, and vapour only change the glass while keeping the same drink and the same belief that a dose gives something.
- I know that counted nudges in watched rooms and low counts on blind attempts describe their own rooms and decide nothing about my days now that the belief has fallen.
