Chapter 5
PARTIES, PAUSES AND MILDS

IN THIS CHAPTER: the classroom door closing, the last line of the paragraph, the grocery bags on the step, the tiny holes under your lips.

*The party was your work all along. The dose only arrived after to take the applause.*

The papers told the truth. The trouble sat untouched while the tug went quiet for minutes, and much of the edginess we called nature turned out to be hunger between doses fused with true pressure.

There is another corner where the same signing-over holds, and it feels warmer than the desk after the row. It does not feel like pressure. It feels like pleasure. It feels like the small bright moments that make a day worth living.

The classroom door closes behind the last student. The paragraph lands with its final sentence exactly where you wanted it. The car rolls into the drive with the grocery bags upright in the back. The meal ends with the plate clean and the taste still on the tongue. The phone call ends well. The shift ends. The sun comes out over the yard and the chair looks inviting.

In each of those moments the hand moves with the same speed it showed at the desk, finding pocket and pack and lighter and stepping outside to light and draw and pause.

We called those moments little parties. We said we had earned them. We said the dose made the finish sweeter, the pause deeper, the taste fuller, the break real. We believed the day was strung with small rewards and the tube lit each one.

I know that belief from the inside because I kept it longer than any other. I could doubt the off-switch on a bad day when the papers stared back. I could not doubt the pellet after good work. It felt like gratitude. It felt like the one decent wage in a hard day.

A smoker put our version into one line better than I ever could.

"Cigarettes were my reward for…well, almost everything. Teaching a class. Finishing a story. Finishing a paragraph. Driving 500 miles. Driving to the grocery store."

Read that line again and hear your own day inside it, teaching a class, finishing a story, finishing a paragraph, driving the long road, driving the short road. The reward attaches to almost any finished act. The party is not in the work. The party is the dose.

I am not going to mock that feeling, and I am not going to tell you the glow at the finish was false. The glow was real. The done-ness was real. The shoulders dropping after the class, the mind clearing after the paragraph, the body settling into the seat after the drive, those things were real as blood. I ask only that you check where the glow came from, what part the tube played in it, and what the same finish feels like when the tube takes no part.

Hold one finish in view and keep it concrete. Take the class.

## The Pellet After The Work

The bell goes. The chairs scrape. The last question is answered and the door closes and the room empties and leaves you with the board still covered in your own writing. Forty faces looked up. You held them. You found the example that landed. You answered the hard question without bluffing. The hour asked everything and you gave it.

You step into the corridor with chalk on your fingers and the head still buzzing. You light up by the door and draw and feel the day nod to you that the work was good and done and yours.

Now slow that minute down, because the order gives the game away exactly as it did at the desk.

The work came first. The attention, the patience, the memory reaching for the right example, the voice holding steady through the restless middle ten minutes, the judgment that knew when to stop explaining and let them write, all of that belonged to your hands and your nerves and your years of practice. The glow you feel in the corridor is the glow of a difficult thing done well. It rises in teachers who never smoked, in teachers who never touched a pack in their lives, and it rises in the same place in the chest and with the same warmth.

The dose arrived after. It added smoke to a glow already lit.

We were taught to read that order backwards. We were taught by years of pairing that the finish needs the light to be complete, the way we once were taught that the hard hour needs smoke to be met. That teaching has a name between us now. It is the great weather of pictures and promises that says every tight feeling proves you cope through smoke and every bright feeling proves you mark it through smoke. It lays its smoke across the finish the way it laid its smoke across the trouble, until the finish looked as if it belonged to the dose.

You say, and I want to give your objection its full weight because it is the strongest one a pleasure-keeper can make, you say I am ignoring what I feel. You say the pause after the light is deeper, the shoulders drop further, the mind savours the done-ness more fully with the tube in hand. You say the non-smoker teacher walks the same corridor and feels less, because he has nothing to mark it with. You say I have confused sequence with cause and the light truly adds a layer.

I hear you. The pause after the light does feel distinct. It feels set apart, ringed off from the noise, held a little longer. The question is what sets it apart, and whether the setting-apart belongs to the tube or to the pause itself.

Watch the non-smoker teacher with clear eyes. He closes the door. He stands at the window. He drinks water. He looks at the empty desks. He lets the buzz settle while he wipes the board. He walks slow to the staff room and talks the class over with a colleague and laughs at the moment that nearly went wrong. His pause lasts longer than our three minutes by the door, his attention stays on the work done rather than on keeping the end dry in the wind, his mouth stays clear and his chest stays open for the next hour. Nothing in his savouring asks for smoke to complete it.

What made our pause feel distinct was not addition. It was division.

Part of the mind during the class counted down to the pellet. Part of the attention during the last ten minutes already stood by the door with the pack. The work got most of us, never all of us, because a thin hunger ran underneath the teaching and asked to be fed the moment the bell went. Then the feed quieted that hunger for minutes, and the quiet felt like depth. It felt like the pause was deeper because the noise before it had been louder.

The fact is plain and it changes the whole shelf of little parties once seen. The dose never paid you for the work. You paid the dose with your attention during the work, then paid again after with your breath and your time, and called the second payment a reward.

Answer three plain questions with the honesty you use when no one listens.

If the dose marked good work, why does the same pellet follow bad work and no work at all, the dull drive, the dull hour, the gap between tasks where nothing was finished. If the glow belonged to the tube, why does the same glow rise in the teacher who never smoked when his class lands. If the reward were real, why must it be re-bought within the hour, twenty times a day, with the mouth turning stale and the chest tightening and the mind starting to count toward the next one while the glow still claims to be shining.

You know the answers already. The same pellet follows different finishes because the pellet never belonged to the finish. The same glow rises without smoke because the glow never lived in smoke. The need returns because each feed guarantees the next hunger. None of that makes your work smaller. It makes your work larger, because the work shone through a divided mind and still landed.

I do not ask you to love teaching less, or writing less, or driving the familiar road less. Any honest person loves the click of a thing done. We all do. I loved the moment the paragraph closed almost more than the pay. Seeing the pellet clearly does not thin that love. It returns the whole wage to its rightful owner. Your patience taught the class. Your ear found the sentence. Your hands brought the bags to the step. Those things are yours. The tube only stood beside them hoping to be mistaken for the cause.

Watch the next finished act with this seeing and notice how little the tube changes your choosing. You still must decide the lesson. You still must find the example. You still must shape the sentence and cut the weak line and keep the strong one. You still must park the car straight and carry the bags in from the cold. The tube writes none of those lines. What marks the finish is the stopping itself, the standing back, the breath, the minute of looking at what the hands made. We credited the tube with the pride that follows good work, the way we once credited it with the settling that follows trouble. Take the credit back and the pride stays where it always lived, in your own craft and care.

## The Taste That Was Never In The Tube

There is a second room in this same house, quieter than the classroom, and it holds the taste argument.

The meal ends. The plate sits clean. The tongue holds the last of the food. The body leans back a fraction. The talk slows. You light up and draw and tell yourself the taste is sealed, the meal closed properly, the pause given its full depth.

We all said it. The dose after food. The dose with the hot drink. The dose on the step with the evening air. The dose that makes the pause a pause rather than a gap.

Check the order again, because the order has not changed.

The food did its work before the light. The tongue, the nose, the stomach, the long learning of what ripe and hot and salted mean, all of that met the meal without any paper tube. The settling of the body after eating, the slowing, the warmth spreading outward, that is the body doing what bodies have done at table since before any factory rolled its first tube. The pause after the meal is a real pause. It asks only to be inhabited.

What did the first draws add to that pause. For two or three breaths a distinct dryness, a distinct bite at the back of the throat, a distinct stale edge laid straight over the taste still on the tongue. Smokers know this and say it plainly when no one is selling them anything. The moment of lighting is called heaven and the minutes after are called feeling rotten in the mouth and tight in the chest. The aftertaste shames the lighting. The lighting and the shaming live in one chest, side by side, lighting after lighting.

You say the bite is the point. You say you love the bite, the scratch, the throat catch, the way the head turns a fraction with the first draw after food. You say the non-smoker misses that layer and his meal ends flatter than yours.

I answer plainly. The bite is real as a sensation. I am not going to tell you the throat felt nothing. It felt the bite. But hold the whole dose in view, not only its first three seconds. Nobody closes a meal on one lighting. The tube burns down. The taste of food thins under smoke. The mouth turns dry. The nose dulls. The talk that might have lingered on the food turns toward watching the end and guarding it from wind. A closer that must be re-lit after the next course, that leaves the tongue less able to taste the next meal and the next, is not a seal. It is a lid laid over the seal.

Think of the meal you remember most clearly from before you smoked, or from the rare days when smoking had no place, the holiday table, the outdoor lunch, the night when talk ran long and no one left the table. The taste stayed. The tongue stayed clear. The pause held without any prop because the company and the food and the hour held it. Nothing in that holding asked for smoke to complete it.

We were taught to read our own table differently. We were taught that the pause needs a marker, that the mouth needs something to do, that the hands need holding, that the end of eating leaves a gap only smoke can close. That teaching came from years of pairing, not from the tongue. The tongue never asked for smoke. The tongue asks for water, for air, for the next taste to arrive clear. The hands never asked for a tube. The hands ask to hold the cup a little longer, to rest on the table, to gesture while the story is told. The gap after the meal is not a hole. It is the meal settling, and settling needs no marker but time.

It does plenty TO you. It does nothing FOR you.

Let that verdict stand without gloom, because there is no gloom in it once felt rightly. If the taste belonged to the tube, losing the tube would mean meals go flat. If the taste always belonged to the food and the pause, losing the tube means meals arrive clearer, with the tongue clean for the next bite and the nose open for the next smell. You are not poorer by one flavour. You are lighter by one lid that used to be laid over every flavour and called the flavour.

Notice how the same lid lay over every pause, not only meals, whether the work break on the step, the pause between tasks, or the evening chair. We called those pauses deeper with smoke for the same reason we called the meal closer with smoke. Part of the mind counted down through the task toward the pellet, part of the attention stood already outside while the body still sat inside, and the quieting of that divided hunger for minutes felt like depth. The non-smoker on the same step feels the air on the face, hears the street, lets the shoulders fall because the shoulders were told to, stretches the legs, looks at distance, and returns with the whole mind. His break is not flatter. It is cleaner, because no small voice counts the minutes till the next feed.

You already began this unmasking when we watched the papers sit untouched while the tug went quiet. Food still fed the body, rest still rested it, and talk still warmed the hour. The softness stayed where it always lived once the signing-over stopped. The same return waits at table and on the step. Pause still pauses. Taste still tastes. A minute of looking out still clears the head. Those things were never in the paper tube. The tube only arrived after hoping to be mistaken for the cause.

## The Hole In The Mask

Now we come to the mild story, and I want you to hold something small and concrete in your palm while we look at it.

Take a mild, a light, a low-tar, whatever the pack calls its paler promise, and look at the filter end in good light. Around the paper, in one or two neat rings, stand tiny holes, pinholes and vents neat as stitching. They look honest. They look like engineering in your favour.

They were put there so a machine would smoke a cleaner dose.

The machine holds the tube in a dry holder. It draws a fixed puff of fixed length at fixed gaps. It does not press lips over the ring. It does not press fingers over the paper. Air streams in through the little holes and thins the smoke, and the trap on the bench reads lower numbers. On paper the mild delivers less. On the bench the numbers fall.

You are not a bench.

Watch what the human mouth often does with those same holes, because the mouth tells the truth the bench cannot hear. The lips often close over the ring to hold the tube. The fingers often close over the paper to keep the end dry in wind or out of habit. The little holes that stood open for the machine are often covered for you. The air that thinned the machine puff often never reaches your puff. A lower number on the bench does not mean you took less. The puff often grows larger to find the draw it knows. It is often held longer. It is often taken deeper. The body seeks the dose it was taught to seek, and the hands and mouth, without any decision, can shape themselves to find it.

The fact is simple and it changes the mild shelf once seen. A machine reading of lower tar and nicotine does not mean the smoker took less. The human puff is not the machine puff. The ring that thins smoke for brass and rubber thickens work for lips and fingers.

You say, and I give this objection its full weight because the mild promise held me too, you say you can feel the mild is lighter. You say the throat bites less, the chest feels less hit, the morning tastes less stale after milds than after the strong ones. You say the body cannot be fooled about lightness and the lighter feel must mean lighter harm.

I hear you. The lighter feel is real as a feeling. I felt it through years of paler packs and told myself I had found the sensible corner of the trap. The throat does bite less on the vented puff, at first, the way watered drink bites less at first sip. The question is what the hands and mouth do after that first sip to find the accustomed strength, and what the bench numbers hide about that doing.

Cover the holes and the draw stiffens. To pull the accustomed quieting through covered holes the puff often has to work harder, with larger volume and longer hold and deeper pull. The throat that felt less bite at the first draws often asks for more puffs, or longer puffs, to reach the quieting of the tug it knows. By evening the tray can still hold much the same count, the mouth can still taste much the same stale afternoon, the chest can still carry much the same tight evening. The paleness lived on the pack. The day lived in the body.

Think of a mask with a neat ring of breathing holes punched around the mouth, handed to you in a dusty room. You tie it on. You breathe. Then, without noticing, you press your lips tight over the holes to hold the mask steady and you pinch the sides with your fingers to keep it from slipping. You breathe harder to pull air through the blocked ring. The dust still comes. At the end of the hour someone shows you the bench test where the mask, clamped dry in a vice with its holes standing open, filtered clean. Would you call that hour safer. Would you thank the holes.

That is the mild in one picture. A hole that stands open for the vice and closed for the wearer is not a kindness. It is a story printed on the pack and smoked through by the mouth.

Notice how the story had to be sold. We wanted it true. Any decent person wants a smaller harm if the larger habit stays. We all leaned toward the paler pack when cough ran deep in winter and the stairs told on us and the morning throat felt rough. The lean was human. It proved nothing about the tube except that we hoped. Hope is not a measurement. The bench is not a mouth. The mouth closes what the bench leaves open, and the puff grows to find what the tug asks for.

Be exact here, because exactness keeps anger where it belongs. I am not telling you milds poison more than the strong ones, and I am not telling you to trade paler packs for darker ones as if darker were honest. Darker is not honest. Paler is not honest. The honesty never lived in the colour of the pack. It lived in the trade itself. Each dose, whatever its ring, feeds a small hunger for minutes and leaves the next hunger waiting behind it. The ring changes how the puff must work to find the dose. It does not change what the dose does once found.

Hold a mild between finger and thumb after this and look at the ring with new eyes. The holes stare back, neat and small. They promised the machine an easier reading. They ask your lips to do extra work to defeat their own promise. Once seen, the promise cannot be unseen. The mild was never a step out. It was the same room painted paler, with the door still locked from the inside by the tug.

## What The Paler Pack Cannot Cover

There is a further turn to the mild story, and it touches the little parties again.

We told ourselves the mild let us keep the parties at lower cost. The pellet after the class. The seal after the meal. The pause on the step. If the tube must stay for the glow, let it at least be a lighter tube. That sounded like prudence. It sounded like a person looking after himself while still honouring the day's finishes.

But the pellet never paid for the finish, whatever its colour, and the seal never closed the meal, whatever its ring. A paler pellet still arrives after work it never did and signs a name not its own. A paler seal still lays its lid over taste it never made. The mild keeps the signing-over intact while painting the pen paler. It keeps the hand moving to pocket and pack and lighter twenty times a day. It keeps part of the mind counting down through the class and the paragraph and the drive toward the door. It keeps the mouth stale and the attention chained between feeds, only now with a story that the chaining costs less.

Ask with the same honesty you brought to the desk and the classroom.

If paler meant less taken, why can the day still hold much the same count of ends in the tray. If lighter meant lighter hunger, why does the tug often return within the same hour and ask with much the same edge. If the ring truly thinned what you took, why can the evening chest still tell much the same story at the stairs.

You know the answers already, because your days already gave them. The count stays because the body seeks its accustomed quieting. The tug returns because each feed, paler or darker, is soon followed by the next hunger. The stairs tell because the trade never lived in the ring. It lived in the feeding.

I state this as settled fact because I watched it hold in my own years of paler packs and in life after life around me. The mild did not loosen the trap. It tightened it with comfort, the way a con tightens when the mark stops checking the figures. We checked the pack. We stopped checking the day. The day kept its books straight. In those years the bench read lower and I took no less, feed after feed bringing its brief quieting and its return.

Hold that without self-reproach, because reproach belongs nowhere here. Any good person in a trap looks for the softer corner. We all did. I did through winters of cough and summers of promises, switching colours like switching seats in the same locked room. Seeing the holes clearly does not make you foolish. It makes you clear, and clarity here returns your own judgment to its rightful place. You were never unwise to want less harm. You were conned about where less harm lives. It never lived in the ring. It lives in seeing that no pellet ever paid for work and no lid ever sealed a meal.

At this point a new noise starts up, and I want to meet it head-on before it hardens.

As the little parties fall and the mild story thins, the mind reaches for other voices. A friend did it this way. A column says that way. A packet in the drawer promises a cleaner wage. A broadcast warns you cannot do it like this. The voices contradict each other and they contradict these pages, and each one asks to borrow your judgment for a minute while its method does the work.

I welcome the doubt behind that reaching, because doubt examined becomes conviction. But I will not share these pages with a dozen rival stories while the argument is still on its feet. Too many good readers have lost the thread here, not because the thread was weak, but because they let another hand pull at it mid-knot. The pellet argument needs its full hearing. The mild argument needs its full hearing. Borrowed advice, however kindly meant, breaks the hearing into pieces and leaves the trap standing in the gaps.

So at the point where reward and mild have fallen, I lay down our fifth rule. Take it in the same warm and firm spirit as the first four. You have tested the axis and found the credit column empty. You have watched the glow stay with the work once the signing-over stopped. You have felt the ring under your fingers and seen how the mouth defeats its own promise. The only thing left that could hold you is the sense that this is too tangled to follow alone. It is not tangled. It is one simple trade seen clear, and a clear trade needs only one clear hearing.

5. IGNORE ANY ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS BOOK
Let this argument finish before you borrow another.

Carry that quiet hearing with you into the hours after this page. The hand will still reach after the finished act. It has reached after classes and paragraphs and drives for years, and it will reach again tonight when the plate is cleared and the bags are in. The difference is that you now meet the reaching with a new seeing ready, and the seeing arrives faster each time. At first you ask it deliberately, almost word by word, did that pellet ever teach a single student in that room. Next time the question comes shorter, what did it add. Next time it comes as a glance at the still-warm work, and then as a smile before the pocket is even touched. Each asking takes back a little more of the day, and each taking-back leaves less noise and more of you.

You will notice the finishes change shape as the question quickens. Where you once stepped outside to mark the done-ness, you now stand a minute longer at the window and let the done-ness mark itself. You drink water. You look at the empty desks. You read the paragraph again and hear it land. You carry the bags in and set them down and stand in the kitchen with the cold still on your coat and feel the house around you. The hour does not feel thinner. It feels fuller, because the small counting voice between feeds has gone quiet and your attention stays on the work your hands just made.

Hold that fullness when the old tug stirs under the ribs. I do not deny those seconds at the lighting, the shoulders falling, the noise quieting for a breath or two. You know those seconds and I honour them, and you know too that they live only at the lighting and never taught a class or sealed a meal. Let the tug be what it is between us now, small and already fading, and let the great weather of stories be what it is, words that once called every finish proof you mark it through smoke. Neither one can hold you once the credit is withdrawn. We feed the Nipper no more praise, and the Smokescreen thins where praise is withdrawn. You are not marching toward a flatter day. You are walking out of a noise that posed as the party, toward the same day with clearer taste to meet it.

SUMMARY
- I now see that the glow after good work belonged to my own craft and care, not to the pellet that arrived after to take the applause.
- I understand that taste and depth lived in the food and the pause itself, and the dose only laid its stale lid over them.
- I know that a machine reading of lower numbers never meant I took less, because my lips and fingers close the tiny holes the bench leaves open.
