Chapter 6
REAL HUNGER AND REAL FULLNESS

IN THIS CHAPTER
A midday kitchen table, a real plate eaten slowly, clean hunger quieting into easy fullness

*Real hunger asks for real food, real food answers it, and no sweetness is missed.*

The afternoon lift is settled — it did plenty TO you and nothing FOR you. The sofa pay is settled — the love was never in the bar. We leave both closed and turn to the last fear that still guards the kitchen.

THE DULL PLATE FEAR

I know this fear because I carried it for years.

“If I stop the sweets, eating will be dull.”
“I will never feel satisfied on ordinary food.”
“I need something RIGHT NOW and only something sweet will do it.”

We all said it. We said it standing at the fridge at ten at night, grazing with no hunger and no fullness, telling ourselves real food is fuel and sweet food is joy. After months of nibbling, real meals did look grey. After months of chasing, quiet did feel empty.

That is why this excuse feels reasonable. It is not about love or energy now. It is about the plate itself. Will anything ever taste good again? Will anything ever be enough?

Let us answer it the only honest way — not by arguing, but by eating.

THE GRAZER WHO FORGOT HUNGER

We forgot what hunger feels like.

We grazed all day. A biscuit at eleven. A handful at noon. That drawer at three. A bar while we cooked. We were never hungry and never full. We lived flat in the middle, with the edge never quite gone, topping it up to keep it quiet.

Ask yourself plainly, as you would answer a friend you would never lie to.

When you eat like that, do you taste anything fully? When the edge is topped up every hour, does any meal ever arrive as welcome? Does the constant nibbling make food more delicious — or does it blur every flavour into the same tired sweet?

You know the answer because you have lived it. The grazer tastes nothing twice. The chase dulls the tongue it promises to delight.

Clean hunger is a different creature. It comes slowly. An emptiness in the belly, a lightness, a clear call for food. No panic. No noise. It does not shout for sugar. It asks to eat.

Most of us have not felt it for years, because we never let it arrive. We fed the edge before hunger could speak.

A REAL MEAL, EATEN SLOWLY

Try this with me today, with ordinary food you already like. No rules. No plan. No weighing or counting. Just a real meal, eaten like a human being.

Sit down. Be hungry first — truly a little hungry, not edgy, not faint, just empty and ready. Take a plate of real food. Meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, potatoes, rice, bread with butter, cheese, fruit — whatever real food you like. Eat it slowly. Taste the first mouthful. Put your fork down between bites if you can. Notice.

What happens?

The emptiness softens. The flavours grow. The second helping tastes as good as the first, then less urgent. Then, without effort, enough arrives. Not stuffed. Not heavy. Full. Quiet. Done.

Now ask the two questions that close the fear:

When clean hunger met real food, did the food taste dull — or lively, deep, and satisfying? When fullness arrived on its own, did you feel deprived — or relieved and at ease?

There is only one honest answer, and you felt it in your own mouth. Real food satisfies real hunger. It always did.

That edgy gnaw we called hunger for years was something else entirely. You recognise it now as an empty, slightly edgy hunger that feels like it needs something sweet, and nothing more. It is the echo of the last dose, not the voice of the body. Real hunger does not feel edgy. Real hunger feels clean, and clean hunger loves food.

YOUR FAVOURITE FOOD WAS WAITING

“But what about favourite food?” you say. “Surely favourites need sugar.”

What made any food your favourite before the chase began?

Was it the packet — or was it hunger meeting flavour? A roast when you were empty. Fresh bread still warm. Strawberries in summer. A stew that filled the house with smell. Did sugar make those moments — or did hunger and taste and fullness make them, with the sweet only riding along and dulling what it rode?

Isolate it. Same hunger, same table, same people, no chase after. Does the meal collapse? Does the taste leak away? Would you trade a satisfying dinner you loved for a bar eaten standing up, edgy and half-tasted?

You would not. You never would. No one remembers the standing-up bar with joy. We remember meals where hunger was met and fullness came.

It is hunger that makes food delicious, not sugar that makes food interesting. It is you that tastes, not the dose.

It is the other way around.

Here is the flat fact, felt in the body, not argued on paper. Your body makes its own steady fuel from the real food you eat, so there is no hole that only sugar can fill — I speak of the ordinary dip we all know, not a diagnosis on you. When you feed clean hunger with real food, the system quiets because it is answered. No topping up. No second reach. No flat evening to pay for a bright ten minutes.

This is what I told you at the start, and now you feel it true in ordinary doing:

Eat whatever real food you like, whenever you are hungry, and you will never miss BAD SUGAR.

Not as a rule. Not as a plan. As permission. Hunger knows when to begin. Fullness knows when to arrive. Taste wakes up when the chase stops blurring it. You do not need to manage what your body already manages beautifully when the noise is gone.

6. LET HUNGER AND FULLNESS DO THEIR JOB
Your body knows enough.

SUMMARY
- Constant grazing blurs hunger and fullness and makes all food taste flat.
- Clean hunger is calm and empty, not edgy, and asks plainly for a meal.
- A real meal eaten slowly tastes lively and brings easy fullness on its own.
- The edgy sweet-urge is the echo of the last dose, not the body asking for fuel.
- Ordinary eating satisfies fully, and nothing sweet is missed in the meeting.
