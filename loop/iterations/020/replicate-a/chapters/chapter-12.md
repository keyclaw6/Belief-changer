Chapter 12
MORNINGS TASTED CLEAR

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A bright morning kitchen, a supermarket aisle in evening light, an ordinary plate eaten slowly

*Being free is not something you practise — it is ordinary life tasted clear.*

WAKING WITHOUT THE LOOP

You wake, and nothing calls.

That is the first marvel. No edgy calculation before your feet touch the floor. No bargain about what the day will demand and what sweet will get you through it. Just light at the window, a mouth that feels clean, a body that knows whether it is hungry yet or not.

We knew the other mornings. We woke heavy and slightly ashamed, already planning the first fix to lift the flatness the last fix had left. You remember what the trap rented: tired, flat and craving.

That rent is cancelled.

Eat when you are hungry. Drink what you truly like to drink. Go about the small morning jobs — wash, dress, open the door — with no ceremony around food at all. If hunger is there, meet it with real food and eat till fullness quietly arrives. If hunger is not there yet, leave it alone and get on with your morning. Your body knows enough now that the noise is gone.

The fuel question is settled. The reward question is settled. Hunger and fullness are trusted again to do their quiet work. The low that used to call for a fix was seen as the trap's own work. Every past collapse belongs to the wrong method. The scattered excuses have nowhere left to stand. The sweetest table was returned to its owners. A bad day can never be rescued by the dose that made bad days heavier. One stray taste can never stay single.

You do not need to rehearse any of that. You live past it. That is what this chapter is for — not to argue, but to walk with you through three ordinary places and show you how easy they are now.

THE AISLE IN EVENING LIGHT

You will walk down the aisle under the bright lights and hear the rustle of wrappers.

Let me tell you what you will find there. The colours still shout. The smell from the bakery still drifts. A thought may rise, quick as a flash: a sweet would be nice here.

Good. Let it rise. You have no need to push it away and no need to obey it.

You see it now for what it is — a remembered echo firing before hunger, nothing more. The afternoon drawer that once promised fuel holds no promise now.

Stand still for a moment and look at the shelves with clear eyes. Who is that mountain of packets for? For people still paying daily for the brief quieting of a want the last packet left. Does that look like enjoyment to you? Does that look like love or comfort?

You know the answer in your belly before your mind can dress it up. It looks like tired shoppers feeding the Crumb and calling it a treat. It looks like the Confectioner taking thanks for a con.

Feel what rises in you. Not hunger. Not envy. Pity, warm and genuine. You were once where they stand, and you are glad you stepped outside.

Buy your real food with pleasure. Choose what looks fresh and satisfying, what you will enjoy cooking and eating. Hold your head high at the till. You are not depriving yourself of anything on those shelves. You are free for the rest of your life, and freedom shops calmly.

AN ORDINARY PLATE

At home, eat as a free person eats.

Cook simply or richly, as you like. Sit down. Taste. We spent years eating fast and half-absent, chasing a hit that never landed, then reaching again because the edgy hunger returned within the hour. Now the chase is gone.

Notice how lively real taste is when it is not drowned in sweetness. Hot and cold, crisp and soft, salt and juice. Notice hunger quieten bite by bite. Notice fullness arrive — not stuffed, not buzzing, just done, content, ready to move on.

You need nothing in place of the old fix. No clever swap, no ritual chew, no rules about the table. Change nothing else in your life. Keep your cafés, your routines, your favourite meals. Freedom does not ask for a new life. It returns sweetness to the life you already have.

I eat this way myself, and I tell you plainly: food became kinder the moment the con left it. Mornings are lighter. Evenings are calmer. The flat hour that used to send us to the drawer passes like a small cloud and leaves clear sky.

If someone near you eats a sweet at the table, let them. You do not need to comment or convert them. Your ease is louder than any lecture. Eat your own meal with joy and let the moment stay warm. Love was never in the packet. It was in the people and the food itself all along.

WHEN A THOUGHT ARRIVES

Sooner or later — tomorrow, next week, on a tired day — the thought will cross your mind: just a bite.

Do not be startled. Do not scold yourself. A thought is not a fall. You cannot stop thoughts arriving, and you do not need to.

Meet it with joy. Answer it at once with the truth you now own. Say it inside and feel relief answer:

FANTASTIC! I’M FREE!

That joyful shout is the new reflex. It turns an old echo into a fresh hit of freedom. You do not mourn a lost friend. You celebrate a dead enemy.

If by genuine accident — a misread label, a bite taken in mistake at a friend's table — some sweetness passes your lips, do the same. Notice how cloying and needless it tastes now. Smile at how ugly the trick looks in daylight. Leave it there and get on with enjoying your life. An accident revives nothing. It is only a rumble on the edge of a fine road, reminding you how smooth the road is.

Never plan a single deliberate taste for any reason, bright or dark. No test, no reward, no bad-day rescue. Not because you must be vigilant, but because you see there is no point. Why pay again for tiredness?

And do not preach. If friends ask why you look brighter, tell them simply that you escaped the sugar trap and eating is marvellous now. Say no more unless they beg to know. Let them see you free, calm, and happy, and leave the door open by example.

12. WHEN YOU THINK OF SUGAR, THINK FREEDOM
Meet the thought with joy.

**SUMMARY**
- Mornings are clear when nothing calls for a fix before the day begins.
- The shop is easy when wrappers are seen as other people's rent, met with pity.
- Ordinary meals satisfy when hunger is met and fullness is allowed to arrive.
- A passing sweet thought is answered with joy and relief that you live free.
- An accidental taste is only a warning ripple, never a failure and never a reason to plan another.
- Freedom needs no substitute, no preaching, and no change to the life you love.
