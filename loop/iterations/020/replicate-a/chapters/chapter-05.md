Chapter 5
THE REWARD THAT NEVER LOVED YOU BACK

IN THIS CHAPTER
The sofa after a hard day, the cinema dark with shared chocolate, the deserve-it moment run again with the sweet taken out

*The comfort was never in the chocolate — it was in you and the moment all along.*

The afternoon lift is finished. It did plenty TO you and nothing FOR you, so we leave that settled and turn to the excuse that still feels like love.

THE DESERVE-IT HOUR

I know this hour because I lived it for years.

You get home wrung out. Keys down. Shoes off. The day has taken everything and given nothing back, and a voice speaks with great tenderness:

“I’ve been good this week.”
“I deserve a treat.”
“Just a treat to get me through.”

We all said it. We said it with chocolate in the hand on the sofa, with biscuits after the argument, with sweets for the children and one — two, three — for us. We were not greedy. We were paying ourselves. After pressure comes pay. After hard comes kind.

That is why this excuse is guarded like a jewel. Energy you can test at a desk. Love you do not want to test at all.

So let us test it like investigators, gently, because the truth here is kind.

Recall one hard-day pay-yourself moment exactly. The door closed. The quiet. The first bite. Warmth spreads. Shoulders drop. For a minute the day loosens its grip.

Now ask, honestly, what did the loving?

Was it the bar that noticed you were tired? Was it the packet that listened to your day? Did the dose put its arm around you?

Or did you put yourself down for a minute, in warmth and quiet, and breathe — with a sweet in your hand?

Run it again in your mind, same sofa, same quiet, same ten minutes to yourself, only without the sweet. A hot drink you like. A cushion. Nothing to prove. Is the rest still rest? Is the quiet still kind? Is the relief of stopping still sweet?

Of course it is. The pause was the kindness. The comfort was you allowing comfort. The chocolate only sneaked a ride on a moment that was already tender, then sent you the bill for loving you.

That drawer sweet at three gave us the same lesson from the other side — bright for minutes, then heavier. The sofa sweet is the same trick in softer clothes. It borrows the relief of stopping and sells it back as love.

WHAT WE MEAN BY TREAT

Listen to how we speak when we are inside it:

“celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate.”

Celebrating with it. Rewarding with it. Loving with it.

Ask two plain questions, and answer as you would to a friend you would never lie to.

When you paid yourself with chocolate after the hard day, did the hardness lift because the chocolate solved anything — or because you stopped for a moment and the whine went quiet? When the packet was empty an hour later, did you feel paid — or tired, flat, and in need of another pay?

You know the answer because you have lived it a thousand times. A pay that leaves you emptier is no pay. A comfort that leaves you needing comfort is no comfort.

Here is the flat fact. We call that minute a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, as if it were a small solid good that cares for us. It is not a solid good. It is a brief quieting of the edgy want left by the last sweet, dressed up by a tired brain as affection. The relief is real — I do not deny the minute felt softer — but the softness was the pause and you, not the sugar. It never gave. It only quieted what it had made noisy, and took the thanks.

That is the sugar trap at its sweetest. Not a shouting master. A false friend who trips you in the dark, then loudly helps you up and takes your gratitude.

LOVE IN THE DARK ROW

“But the cinema chocolate,” you say. “Surely that is love.”

I understand. I loved that row too. The dark. The laughter. The shoulder against shoulder. The shared bar broken in half and passed along like a small vow: we are here together, this is ours.

Take that night and hold it still. Where was the love?

Was it in the cocoa and the wrapper — or in the person beside you, the story on the screen, the laughter rolling through the dark, the rare permission to sit close and want nothing else?

Isolate it. Same film, same friend, same laughter, same closeness, no bar. Does the evening collapse? Does the love leak out with the missing wrapper? Would you trade the company for the chocolate alone on an empty row?

You would not. You never would. No one goes to the cinema to sit alone with a bar in the dark. We go for the togetherness, and the sweet sneaks a ride on togetherness so complete that we misremember who carried whom.

It is you that is warm, not the sweet. It is the moment that is sweet, not the sugar. It is the other way around.

That is how the Confectioner story rents affection that was never its to rent. It sticks its label on your love — your tiredness eased by rest, your joy made by people, your evening made by closeness — and collects the credit. Peel the label off and nothing is lost. The people remain. The laughter remains. The rest remains. Only the renter leaves.

WHEN THE TREAT PREDICTS THE LOW

And there is a harder truth to set beside the tender one, stated plainly because you deserve plain.

Men who ate more sweets day to day were more likely to struggle in mood in the years after, and it was not low mood driving the sweets. I am not saying that is a sentence on you, only that the daily treat points toward the low rather than away from it.

Does that surprise you, once you have watched the loop? The treat promises to lift the grey and then rents the grey. The comfort promises to mend the hard day and then makes tomorrow harder to meet. You reach to feel brighter and find yourself dimmer an hour later, reaching again.

So ask the last question, the one that closes the door:

If it truly comforted, why do the comforted need comforting again so soon? If it truly rewarded, why does the reward leave you feeling less rewarded, not more? Does real love behave like that — take more than it gives, leave you flat, and call you back for another proof?

No. Real comfort builds you. Real reward fills you. Real love does not empty the hand that holds it.

YOU WERE THE WARMTH ALL ALONG

We were never fools to want comfort. We were conned about where comfort lives.

We fell into the trap together — tired workers, tender parents, loyal friends — reaching for a little kindness and being handed a dose that quieted for a minute and charged for an hour. There is nothing to be ashamed of in wanting the kindness. There is everything to rejoice in seeing the bill for what it is.

You do not need to mourn this friend, because it never befriended you.

It never loved you back.

SUMMARY
- The deserve-it sweet feels like kindness after pressure and tiredness.
- The kindness is the pause and the permission to rest, not the chocolate in the hand.
- The cinema warmth belongs to the people, the laughter and the closeness, with the sweet only riding along.
- A daily treat points toward future lowness rather than fixing mood, and brings no promise about any one reader.
- The reward that leaves you emptier is no reward, and seeing that returns the love to its owners.
