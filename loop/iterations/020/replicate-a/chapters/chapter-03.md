Chapter 3
WHAT IS IT DOING FOR YOU

IN THIS CHAPTER
An ordinary Tuesday from morning to midnight, the desk drawer and the sofa packet, the box emptied standing up, the two small words that change the question

*Forget how much it harms you for a moment and tell me honestly what it ever did for you.*

You have seen that you are not weak and were never freely choosing, and you have seen the sugar trap at work, the Crumb whining and the Confectioner answering with its loud story, so we leave that settled.

THE TREAT YOU KEEP DEFENDING

I meet you where you are. You will grant me that the midnight eating is ugly. You will grant me the heaviness in the morning, the sticky mouth, the promises that do not hold. But underneath you hold one line back:

“It can’t be that bad. I get something from it.”

Of course you say it. We all said it. We clung to it because if there is something real in it, then losing it would hurt, and who wants to hurt?

Listen to how that line sounds on an ordinary day. It does not arrive as philosophy. It arrives as muttering.

Morning rush: “I need something RIGHT NOW.” Mid-morning: “to get me through.” Afternoon at the desk: the drawer opens by itself. Evening: “I’ve been good this week, I deserve one.” Night on the sofa: “just a treat.” Friday: “once a week is fine.” Sunday night, staring at wrappers: “don’t keep it in the house.” Monday: “I can’t stop once I start.”

I know every one of those sentences because I spoke them. They are not reasons. They are the sound of a person negotiating with a dose.

Let me ask you as an investigator, warmly, without a shred of blame. Take any one of them and hold it up to the light.

If the drawer sweet truly got you through the afternoon, why did you need a second one an hour later? If the evening packet truly rewarded you for being good, why did you feel faintly ashamed while you ate it? If once a week is fine, why is it never once a week — why is it, like that box emptied standing up at midnight, always the whole box?

You cannot answer those questions and keep the defence. That is the point. I am not asking whether the harm outweighs the good. I am asking what good is there at all.

TO YOU OR FOR YOU

There are two tiny words that cut this whole confusion open. TO. FOR.

Plenty is being done TO you by BAD SUGAR. I will state it plainly because you can check it in your own body and in the world around you.

In large population studies, men and women with high intakes of added sugar turn up more often with a strained heart and circulation, through liver overload and pressure and appetite bypass, and free sugars feed the mouth acid that rots teeth. International guidance says keep free sugars below a tenth of calories, with further benefit below a twentieth, and ordinary intakes run far above it. I am not writing your medical sentence, only describing the load — if you live with illness or medicine, your clinician answers the medical part.

That is real. That is being done TO millions of bodies, quietly, daily, while the packets smile.

Now the other word. FOR. What is being done FOR you?

Name it. Not vaguely. Precisely. One true thing it gives you that you could not have without it.

Is it pleasure? Is it comfort? Is it fuel? Pick your strongest one and put it on the table. Look at it hard. Did the sweet create that good feeling, or did it only interrupt the edgy want the last sweet left behind and take the credit?

This is where the phrase you will need was born. We talk about sweetness as if it were a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, a single solid good that makes life sweeter. Say those words slowly and test them against yesterday. Where was the pleasure in standing and stuffing? Where was the treat in hiding wrappers? Where was the lift in the flat heaviness an hour after?

You felt something, yes. I do not deny you felt something. Every trapped person feels something when the whine goes quiet for ten minutes. But quiet for ten minutes is not pleasure. It is pause. A pause that guarantees the noise returns louder is not a lift. It is rent.

It is doing plenty TO you. It is doing nothing FOR you.

WHAT IT COSTS WHILE YOU CALL IT FREE

Think of an ordinary Tuesday without romance.

You start bright enough. Mid-morning the mouth turns restless. You feed a dose and feel normal for a little while. Afternoon the restlessness returns with its friend, flatness. You feed again. Evening you feel tired and a little sour with yourself, so you allow a reward to cheer yourself up. Night you eat past comfort and sleep heavy. Morning you wake fogged and vow to be strict.

What did Tuesday give you? Count it honestly. Not what it promised. What it delivered.

A few minutes of quiet mouth, repeated. Hours of wanting, heaviness, self-reproach. Money gone for something that left you hungrier than it found you. Teeth bathed in acid. A body carrying a load it never asked for while you called the load a light.

And for that you defend it as a friend?

I want you to feel the absurdity without feeling attacked, because the absurdity was never yours. We were taught to call slavery rented as a treat. We paid the rent daily and thanked the landlord for the roof while he took the house. The con is in the naming. Call a fix a treat and you will defend your own pickpocket.

So I land this where it belongs, flat and kind.

There is no benefit to defend. Only a bill to stop paying.

I do not want you to stop from fright, and fright will not keep you stopped. I tell you the bodily cost because you deserve to see what is being done TO you, and then I tell you to put fear aside. You are not escaping a poison because you are frightened. You are walking out because you have seen there was never a gift, only a trick, and walking out of a trick is relief.

THE QUESTION YOU CANNOT DODGE

From today, every sweet thought gets one test. Not how bad is this, not can I afford one, not have I earned it. One question only.

If there is no good in it often, what sense is there in it occasionally? If it does nothing FOR you daily, what magic would make it do something FOR you on Friday?

You do not need to answer me. Answer yourself, out loud in your kitchen if you like. Ask what good is there at all, and hear how silent the room goes.

You will find no answer because there is none, and that silence is the doorway. A person who sees there is nothing to defend does not need force to walk away. He walks because there is nothing to stay for.

4. JUDGE BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Ask what good is there at all.

SUMMARY
- The defence is never the harm, it is the claimed benefit, and that benefit has never been examined.
- Ordinary-day muttering about getting through and deserving one describes wanting, not enjoying.
- Much is being done to bodies by high sweet intake, while nothing solid is done for the eater.
- The felt something after a dose is brief quiet, not pleasure, and it guarantees more noise after.
- The test changes from harm versus benefit to whether any genuine benefit exists at all.
