Chapter 10
THE SWEETEST TABLE

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A birthday table with candles still burning, a dark cinema row with chocolate passed hand to hand, a bright aisle glimpsed on the way home

*The sweetness you remember was the people and the moment — the sweet was only sneaking a ride.*

### The One You Still Guard

The scraps are cleared, the wired-story is finished, the cut-down schedule is exposed for what it does.

And now you guard one thing. I understand. We all guarded it.

Not the drawer at 3 p.m. Not the box emptied standing up at midnight. This is prettier than that. This is the birthday table, laughter around you, candles low, someone you love pushing the cake toward you. This is the cinema in the dark, shoulder to shoulder, the chocolate passed hand to hand while the film starts.

You say:

*"But that was genuinely sweet. Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate — that was love itself."*

I am warm to you in that memory, and I am harsh to the trick inside it.

If I took everything else and left you this one, would you be free? Or would you keep a door open that only leads back to the same room?

Let us look at it honestly, together, while you are still free to look. I will not steal anything you love. I will show you where the love actually lived.

### At The Table

Picture it fully. Do not hurry.

The table is crowded. Plates too close together. Someone has laughed so hard they have to put their glass down. A child is talking over everyone and no one minds. The candles make every face softer. There is real food — warm, fragrant, plenty of it — and there is that sweet in the middle, bright wrappers, bright icing, presented like the heart of it all.

Now re-run it slowly in your mind and ask the honest question.

What is doing the warming? Is it the packet — or is it the people?

Would that laughter be less dear if there were no icing on the table? Would the child be less lovely? Would the friend beside you be less your friend? Did the sweet make the joke funny, or did the joke make the sweet seem precious because it happened to be there at the same time?

Isolate the variable, as any fair investigator would.

You have laughed like that at tables with no sweet at all, and the laughter did not fail. You have felt that cinema glow on nights when you forgot the chocolate and never missed it until the advert told you to. You have loved people fiercely in moments where no dose was within miles. Joy does not need a wrapper to arrive.

So what did the dose add?

It added a brief hit, a chew, a rush, then that familiar flatness and the reach for another piece while the conversation went on without you for a second. It borrowed the light of the room and claimed it as its own. That is how the sugar trap kept its last lock on you — not with the dull daily graze, but with this bright memory it painted as proof.

Ask:

If the sweet made the night, why do you remember the faces, not the flavour?
If the chocolate carried the love, why does the love remain when the taste is long gone?
If that table needed BAD SUGAR to be sweet, what sweetened every happy table before you ever learned to crave it?

There is only one honest answer. The night was sweet. The sweet was not the night.

It was never a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, only a passenger pretending to drive. The people, the leisure, the story, the real food you ate with appetite — they did all the giving. The dose did the taking, and took the thanks.

It is the other way around.

### See How Easily You Are Fooled

You trust your feelings. Of course you do. We all did. But feeling can lie flatly to your face, and I want you to feel that lie now so you never trust the sweet-feeling again.

Do this with me, right where you sit.

Think of that celebration sweet — see the wrapper, hear the little rustle you know so well. Notice what happens in your mouth before you have tasted anything. Watering. A lift of wanting. A picture of pleasure completed before a crumb has touched your tongue.

Now keep watching. That wanting fired in a bright aisle weeks ago too, under lights, among smells, long before hunger, long before love, long before any birthday. Wrappers, smells, times of day can set the mouth running with no hunger underneath at all.

Do you see what that proves?

The wanting is not hunger. It is not love. It is a thought that fires when cued, and a thought can be wrong. Your own body just demonstrated it — pleasure promised where nothing has happened yet. If perception can manufacture a whole treat out of rustle and light, why would you trust it when it tells you the birthday needed the dose?

The cue does not compel you. It only speaks, and a cued thought is a thing to reframe, not to obey. Meet it with joy: ah, there is the old cue firing. Good. That is not need. That is not love. That is the echo of a schedule that is already dying.

The story that told you cue means need was the Confectioner talking, not your heart. Pity that voice. Do not obey it.

### What I Found At My Own Table

Let me tell you plainly what happened at mine, because I guarded this table longer than any other.

I was the man who always brought the big bar to the cinema. It was my role. Generous, I told myself. Part of the fun. The plastic seats, the hush before the lights went down, my daughter's hand coming over for another square without looking — I would have sworn that chocolate was love made edible.

The year I saw the trap, I went again. Same cinema. Same daughter, a year taller. Same hush. I had no bar in my pocket and a strange lightness I did not yet trust.

The film started. She leaned her head on my shoulder in the dark without thinking about it. She laughed once so loud people turned, then hid her face. At the interval we ate real food we had brought — bread, cheese, apples that cracked — and she ate with that clean appetite children have, and talked with her mouth full, and I felt something I had not felt in years of bringing bars.

Ease. No counting squares. No edgy watch on how many were left. No flat heaviness creeping in halfway through the car chase. No small shame walking past the bin with empty foil.

Halfway home she said, out of nowhere, that was the best night. There had been no dose to make it best. There had been us, and the story, and food that satisfied.

I tell you this not to impress you but to free you. My celebration did not lose its sweetness. It found it. The love had been there all along, rented out to a packet that charged heavily for standing in front of it.

Since then there have been birthdays, bad days, good days, tired evenings, bright tables and ordinary Tuesdays. The thought still crosses my mind at times — a picture, a smell — and it arrives already labelled. Not loss. Not danger. Just an old cue with nowhere to live. I meet it and get on with enjoying my life, and the stability of that has become the quietest joy: no negotiation, no special-day arithmetic, no morning-after heaviness.

Nothing was taken from my sweetest table. Everything was returned to its owners.

### No Benefit Often

So here we stand, friend, at the end of demolition.

Energy was the drop after the lift. Reward was people mislabelled as packet. Hunger and fullness were clean signals once the bursts stopped. The low was the last dose pretending to be need. Willpower was the wrong method with no finish line. The leftovers were scripts, not character.

If there is no true benefit in a dose often, what point is there in a dose occasionally?

That question closes the last door, and I close it with joy, not sternness. We do not keep one to honour love. We leave all of them because love never lived there.

You need not fear an empty life without it. Eat whatever real food you like, whenever you are hungry, and you will never miss BAD SUGAR.

Hear that as the ground under totality. Real hunger quietens with real food. Real fullness arrives. Taste grows livelier, not duller, when it is not chased by hit and dip. There is no deprivation to be brave about. There is abundance to inhabit.

Which is why the next step is simple, and total, and marvellous.

10. REFUSE THE SPECIAL OCCASION
No benefit often means no point occasionally.

**SUMMARY**
- The birthday and the cinema were loved for laughter, closeness and the moment, not for the dose.
- Wanting can fire from wrapper, smell and hour with no hunger present, which proves the feeling is a cued thought, not need.
- The same warmth remains intact with real food and people, and arrives without flatness or negotiation.
- Keeping one special dose keeps the whole chase alive, while leaving all of them returns sweetness to life itself.
