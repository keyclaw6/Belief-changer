Chapter 9
THE LAST HIDING PLACES

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
A row of leftover excuses on the table, a cliff edge with a short fall promised, a seatbelt coiled on the seat

*There is nothing wrong with you, and there is nothing right with just one.*

### The Table Is Almost Clear

We have seen the Monday collapse was never your fault; the method you were given had no finish line, and both fears were two pulls from one trap.

Good. That leaves only scraps.

Not arguments. Scraps. The little sentences that pop up at the shop, at the desk, at night, sounding so reasonable, so personal, so like you. They are not you. We were all conned on false information, and a conned mind repeats the sales script in its own voice until the script feels like character.

Let us lift each scrap to the light and look at it. When you see where it came from, you will wonder how it ever hid you.

You are not uniquely tangled in this. In large surveys about one adult in seven answers yes often enough to highly sweet food to meet addiction-like scores on the standard scales, at a rate that looks much like alcohol and tobacco — and I say that, friend, not to label you, only to lift blame from you. A trap that catches that many good people is not proof of weak character. It is proof of a well-set trap.

### "Just One," "Cut Down," "Tomorrow"

Listen to the negotiator. He arrives precisely now, when the belief is dying.

*"Once a week is fine."*

If it were fine, why read a book to stop? And if there is no true benefit in a dose often, what point is there in a dose occasionally? You would not keep a leash because you only wear it on Fridays.

*"Just a treat." "Been good this week, I deserve a treat when you feel you deserve one."*

Deserve what — tired evenings and an edgy morning? We pay ourselves with the very thing that rents us heaviness and call it kindness. Is the week not payment enough to deserve an evening unrented?

*"Don't keep it in the house and I'll be fine."*

Whose house is the world? Wrappers fire in every aisle, every desk, every birthday. A free person does not need the world child-proofed. The thought is a thing to reframe, not a doorway to barricade.

*"I need something RIGHT NOW."*

That urgency feels like hunger, but hunger does not shout for one narrow packet. What shouts is the whine after the last fix, misread as need. The afternoon slump is real to feel — I grant you the heaviness — but feeling low does not mean you are low in a way a sweet fixes. That flat hour follows the lift as surely as evening follows afternoon. Does the cure arrive before the complaint, or after?

*"Can't stop once I start." "I just crave sweets and when i eat them, i feel like i cant stop."*

Exactly. Thank you for the honesty, because that sentence is not your shame. It is the trap's signature. One bite becomes the box because the schedule teaches chase: restrict, crave, binge, regret, restrict. The binge does not prove you lack control. It proves loss of control is the form the trap takes once triggered. Why defend the first bite that removes your say over the rest?

*"I'll start tomorrow. To get me through today first."*

Through to what — another tomorrow that needs getting through? If the dose got you through, you would by now be through. How many tomorrows have you borrowed against?

Do you see? Each excuse admits the trap while pleading for it.

If one is harmless, why does one always argue for the next?
If cutting down worked, why does every cut-down week end with the drawer raided by Friday?
If tomorrow is better, what is today protecting?

There is only one honest answer. The negotiator is not reasoning. He is rehearsing.

### The Cliff

Let me show you moderation where it stands, because this is where most clever people hide last.

A man stands at a cliff edge. White spray below. A friend says: jump, it is perfectly safe — provided you fall only a few metres.

You laugh. You would not call that an offer. You would call it nonsense. No one can jump and not fall. The fall is the jump.

*"Just cut down." "Just the special ones."*

That is the same offer. Have the dose but do not have the consequences of the dose. Keep the little whine alive but promise it will never shout. Keep the restrict-then-binge rhythm turning but promise it will never spin. We know now what that rhythm does — it re-arms wanting and teaches the next binge to be bigger. Interruption is what escalates it. Keeping the schedule alive keeps the chase alive.

It is the other way around.

Cutting down does not make the thing smaller. It makes it more precious. Forbidden makes each hit glint brighter, and brighter makes the dip after darker, and darker calls louder for relief. You cannot keep a trap as a pet. You either live in it or walk out of it.

No benefit often means no point occasionally. That is not sternness. That is relief. You need not guard a line forever. You step off the cliff-path entirely.

Your body can cope with an accidental jolt the way a seatbelt is there for a jolt — but no one drives erratically because the belt exists. The mind cannot keep a deliberate exception and stay free. Totality is not harshness. It is the easy way, because one decision ends a thousand negotiations.

### "I'm Wired This Way"

This one sounds deeper. It feels like identity.

*"I have a sweet tooth. My family are all the same. Addictive personality. Roller coaster since childhood."*

I am warm to you in that ache, and I am harsh to the trick inside it.

Look at the order. Did the traits make the trap, or did the trap make the traits?

We all learned the same grazing, the same secret cupboard hour, the same flat 3 p.m. reach, the same shame-laugh, because we all lived in the same bright-lit world of fixes engineered to be craved. Shared symptoms are not shared genes. They are shared exposure.

Ask:

If wiring decided it, why do the same people feel clean hunger return within days when the bursts stop?
If character decided it, why do strong, disciplined, loving people empty the box standing up like everyone else?
If you were built for slavery rented as treat, why did your body warn you with heaviness and regret every time?

You know the answer. The so-called personality is the result, not the cause.

And here is my graceful concession, and I mean it. Suppose, just for argument, that you were the most craving-prone person alive. Suppose your wanting sang louder than your neighbour's. What of it? A louder whine from a dying loop is still a dying loop. This method does not ask you to become another person. It shows any person the benefit was never there, and wanting without belief has nowhere to live. Even if the label were true, freedom would still be true.

You are not a type. You are a person who was conned. Conning leaves no mark on your nature once seen.

### Too Easy To Be True?

Now the last hiding place. Not in the sweet. In this book.

*"This sounds too easy. Other quitters quit by suffering. Real change must hurt."*

Of course that voice comes. It is the Confectioner story wearing a sensible coat — no, let me speak plainly without that name: it is the old sales script wearing a sensible coat. For years you were told freedom must be earned in sweat, counted in days, paid for in misery. When someone says you can walk out easily, the suffering-method calls him a liar to protect its own price.

Let me answer as one who lived both.

I know the white-knuckle weeks. I know the calendar crosses and the drawer raids. I also know the morning the belief changed and the wanting simply had nowhere to stand. Your body makes its own fuel from ordinary food — there is no deprivation in leaving BAD SUGAR, and I tell you that not as diet talk but as freedom. When the bursts stop there is a few days of flat, edgy reaching at the old hours, loudest around two to five days for many, then clearing, mild and passing and already fading. Scientists still argue hotly about what to call all this — craving, habit, addiction, yes or no — and I will not hide that split from you. They argue about the word. They grant the shape: intermittent fixes teach binges, cues fire before hunger, the loop re-arms itself. The name does not matter. The exit does.

So ask:

If easy meant false, why did hard never work for you?
If suffering were the price of freedom, why did the most suffering people stay the most trapped?
If truth must hurt, why does seeing a con feel like relief?

It is the other way around. Hard was the wrong method protecting itself. Easy is what happens when the benefit dies.

Do not judge this by how much it hurts. Judge it by what it does for you.

Which brings me to the warning I owe you before we part these scraps. Plenty of kind voices will offer you conflicting rules after this — meal plans, willpower tips, sorrowful tales of their own deprivation offered as wisdom. They quit by suffering and now preach suffering as proof. Pity them, do not obey them. If you borrow their fear, you borrow their trap.

9. IGNORE ADVICE THAT CONFLICTS WITH THIS METHOD
Other quitters quit by suffering.

**SUMMARY**
- Leftover excuses are sales scripts, not character, and each one admits the trap while pleading for it.
- One always argues for the next because the schedule teaches chase; moderation keeps that schedule alive.
- Cutting down makes each dose more precious and the dip darker; totality ends negotiation.
- Shared craving traits are the result of shared exposure, not proof you are wired to stay.
- Even a loud whine fades in days once belief is gone, and the body makes its own fuel from real food.
- Ease does not disprove the method; suffering never proved the old one.
