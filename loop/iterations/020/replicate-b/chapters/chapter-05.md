Chapter 5
THE REWARD THAT NEVER LOVED YOU BACK

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Hard-day kitchen late at night, cinema row with shared chocolate, deserve-it voice after a good week

*The love was in the moment and the people — the sweet only took the thanks.*

### The Lift Is Dead, The Love Remains

We watched the afternoon dose over the whole film and saw it leaves less energy, not more. We asked what it does FOR us, not only what it does TO us.

That leaves the claim we guard most closely. Not fuel. Love.

We all know the voice after a hard day:

“Been good this week.”
“Treat when you feel you deserve one.”
“Just a treat. I have earned it.”

And we know the warmer one:

“Celebrating, rewarding myself, going to the cinema, etc with chocolate.”

I do not blame you for that voice. Of course you want comfort and reward. Of course you want love to have a taste. That is why you are reading.

But I tell you flatly, as one who stood in the same kitchen: that box was never a genuine pleasure, treat or lift.

Let us look together, without doom and without lecture, at what that reward actually did.

### The Pay-Yourself Moment

It is late. The door is closed. The day has taken everything — the boss, the traffic, the messages, the tired legs. The house is quiet. You stand in the bright kitchen and reach, almost tenderly, for the bar, the tub, the bag.

For a minute there is pause. Shoulders drop. The day loosens. You think, I deserve this. I am looking after myself.

Or the lights go down. The film starts. Laughter around you. The hand goes into the shared box, shoulder to shoulder. It feels as if the chocolate is part of the love itself.

I know how precious that feels. I am not mocking it. I am asking you to separate, for once, what belongs to whom.

Who made the day bearable before the wrapper opened? Who got through the hard shift, kept patience, paid the bills, showed up? Who is sitting beside you in the dark, laughing at the same moment, warm against you?

You did. They did. The moment did.

The sweet arrived after the work was done and took the thanks.

Think of that three-o’clock drawer for a second — the same quick flare, the same fast fade. The evening dose works the same way. Ten minutes of flare while you exhale, then the flat hunt again, then shame that you needed comforting at all.

Does that sound like being loved back?

### Run It Again Without The Sweet

Let us do what we did with the afternoon: run the whole film, not the first minute.

Take the hard evening and strip the dose out. Same late kitchen. Same quiet. Same tired body that has earned rest. You change, you sit, you breathe, you have a drink of water, you rest your feet. The pause is still there. The relief of stopping is still there. The pride of having got through is still there.

What is missing? Only the second hunger an hour later. Only the rattle of the empty packet and the voice that says, why did I do that again?

Now take the cinema and strip the dose out. Same film. Same friends. Same laughter that makes your sides ache. Same hand finding yours in the dark. Is the laughter thinner? Is the story less funny? Is the shoulder beside you less dear because nothing sweet is in your mouth?

You know the answer. The warmth is intact.

So I ask you, and answer honestly:

If the bar was your reward for being good, why did it leave you feeling bad about yourself within the hour?
If the chocolate was the love in that row, why is the love still there when you forget the chocolate?
Eat that same bar alone over the sink, with no pause, no film, no one to love — where is the reward then?

There is only one honest answer. You brought the love. It only charged you for it.

That is the sugar trap at its kindest voice. It does not shout. It puts its arm around you and says, you deserve me, while it picks your pocket for comfort.

It did nothing for you. It did plenty to you.

### The Comfort That Leaves You Lower

We were conned into reading that soft minute as proof the sweet cares. The fact is, the relief you feel is the relief of stopping for a moment — the pause, the sit-down, the exhale — plus the brief quieting of the hunger the last dose left behind. The people, the rest, the laughter do the comforting. The dose is only ever sneaking a ride.

Look at mood itself. Men who ate sweets most often were more likely to feel low in the years that followed — that does not prove cause, and I am not saying that means you.

But ask what you have lived: does the daily treat fix a low mood, or does it predict it? You pay yourself on Monday for stress, on Tuesday for tiredness, on Wednesday for being good, and by Friday the need for payment is louder, not softer. A true comfort would leave you needing less comfort. This one leaves you needing more.

That is not love. Love does not rent itself back to you by the hour. Love does not leave you emptier for having been held.

I am not saying sweetness itself is wicked, and I am not saying a happy life has no sweet tastes in real food and shared meals. I am saying this fix, this dose taken to pay, soothe or reward you, never paid, soothed or rewarded anything. It borrowed warmth from your own evening and handed it back as craving.

Who deserves the credit for getting through that hard day? You do. Who made the cinema row glow? You and yours, the story, the night out. Once you see who did the work, you stop handing the thanks to the wrapper.

The reward never loved you back. You loved, and it billed you.

**SUMMARY**
- The deserve-it dose borrows relief from the pause and rest, not from the sweet.
- The cinema warmth belongs to the people, laughter and moment, not to the chocolate.
- Judged over the whole evening, the reward leaves more want and less comfort.
- A daily treat does not fix low mood; it is followed by more low mood, not less.
- The love was always yours; the dose only took the thanks.
