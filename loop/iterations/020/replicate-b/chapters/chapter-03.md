Chapter 3
WHAT IS IT DOING FOR YOU

**IN THIS CHAPTER**
Daytime kitchen table and an honest stock-take, desk drawer and sofa cushion at night, mirror and the memory of the dentist's chair

*Forget how bad it is for a moment and tell me what good it ever did for you.*

### The Price You Already Admit

We have seen we are caught in the sugar trap, with the Confectioner feeding the whine of the Crumb, and that was never weakness.

I know where you stand now. You admit it does harm. You have felt the heaviness and the hurry. And still a voice says:

“It can’t be that bad. I get something from it.”

I hear you. We all said it. It sounds like balance. It is really the last defence of the con.

Let me put the harm plainly first, so we stop arguing about it. The fact is, across whole populations, heavy added sugar goes with more heart and stroke trouble, through liver overload and pressure and appetite bypass. Free sugars feed the acid on the teeth that makes holes and rot. Public guidance draws the line at a tenth of calories from free sugars, with more benefit lower still, and ordinary sweet-eating days sail far past it. I am not writing your future, so take any health worry to your clinician while you keep eating regular meals.

That is frightening, and I do not want you to change because it frightens you. Fear never freed anyone. I give you those facts only to close a false debate, because even if you shrug them all off, my question still stands untouched.

We waste years asking does it do more harm than good. That is the wrong scale. A clever poison can do more harm than good and still keep you hooked while you weigh the two sides. I am not asking you to weigh harm against benefit. I am asking what benefit there is at all.

Look at that box emptied standing up in the quiet kitchen. You already know what it does TO you. Tell me what it did FOR you.

### An Ordinary Day on Trial

Let us take one ordinary day, in your own words, and put each dose on trial.

Morning. “Been good this week, just a treat.” A biscuit with tea, a sweet roll on the way in. What did it do TO you? A brief sweet rush, then hunger back sooner and louder, teeth coated, the day's good resolve already dented. Now tell me, in plain words, what did it do FOR you that the break itself, the hot drink, the pause at the window, did not do?

Afternoon. “To get me through.” The drawer opens. The bar is eaten half without looking. What did it do TO you? A short lift, then that flat edginess that calls for another, work still waiting, mood thinner than before. I am not asking you today to decide whether that lift is real. We will look each claim in the eye in turn. I am asking you to notice the full ledger. What did it do TO you in that hour? And what, exactly, did it do FOR you?

Evening. “Treat when you feel you deserve one.” Tired, fed, entitled. Chocolate on the sofa for comfort, for love, for rest. What did it do TO you? Another hurry, another heaviness, sleep duller, morning duller, the promise to be good tomorrow already loaded. What did it do FOR you that rest and warmth and company would not have done alone?

“Once a week is fine.” “Don’t keep it in the house.” We all know those bargains. What do they do TO you? They keep the sweet precious and on your mind all week. What do they do FOR you? Did the week of watching and waiting feel like freedom?

Do you see the pattern? The TO side fills a page without effort. The FOR side goes thin the moment you remove the people, the pause, the evening, the relief of stopping work. I am leaving those favourites untouched for now. I only want you to feel how empty one side of the scales already is.

Ask yourself:

If you add up everything it did TO you today, what single thing can you name that it did FOR you?
If the break and the company and the rest explain the pleasure, what is left in the packet itself?
And if there were no pleasure at all, would you still pay that price for the heaviness and the hurry?

There is only one honest answer. You pay because you believe you must, not because you receive.

### TO You Is Plenty, FOR You Is Missing

This is where the ground shifts.

We were taught to see each dose as a genuine pleasure, treat or lift, three sweet names for one supposed friend. Morning pleasure. Afternoon lift. Evening treat. Say those three slowly and feel how much of your life they claim to cover.

I am not asking you to surrender them yet. I am asking you to test them.

It does plenty TO you. Show me what it does FOR you.

That is the whole change in this chapter. Not how bad is it. What good is there at all.

4. JUDGE BY WHAT IT DOES FOR YOU
Ask what good is there at all.

**SUMMARY**
- Harm alone will not free you, because the belief in benefit survives it.
- An ordinary day shows a long list of costs and an empty place where the benefit should be.
- Each dose is still felt as pleasure, treat and lift, and that feeling now has to prove itself.
- The fair test is what it does for you, not only what it does to you.
- Freedom begins when benefit, not harm, is put on trial.
